## A lived scene

Map connections: `W03`, `P07` (learning), `M06` (learning commons), `E04` (less credential-driven learning), `T04` (attention and prestige). See the [possibility map](../foundations/possibility-map.md).

Someone arrives wanting to understand why the sea glows. The welcome room offers a conversation, a walk, or simply somewhere to sit. There is no admissions interview. At lunch, a researcher and a visitor compare sketches with a dancer exploring rhythm. Later, one joins a field trip; another spends the afternoon staring into a shallow tank.

A neighboring workshop is preparing an exploration instrument. The project may take years. Someone who loses interest can leave with their curiosity intact and livelihood unaffected.

## Assumptions, values, and proposal

- **Speculative assumptions:** Material security enables extended learning without employment credentials. AI supports research and teaching; autonomous systems assist laboratories, fieldwork, and exploration. These assumed capabilities and their reliability require evidence before implementation.
- **Desired values:** Curiosity, intellectual honesty, embodied understanding, hospitality, accessible participation, and freedom to change direction. Being knowledgeable does not establish greater entitlement to a good life.
- **Proposed design:** An inhabited learning commons with public entry, quiet study, workshops, gardens, residences, and progressively controlled specialist facilities. Learning is a possible way of living rather than a compulsory identity.

## Purpose and people

The University welcomes long-term inquiry, tentative questions, practical making, and the pleasure of understanding. Children, experts, late beginners, disabled learners, and artists participate without following one educational ladder. Human teachers matter through judgment and relationships; AI tutors offer additional support.

Open participation does not imply unrestricted use of hazardous equipment. Demonstrated readiness can govern a particular activity without becoming a ranking of human worth. People uninterested in study remain welcome in public gardens, kitchens, performances, and ordinary social life.

## Spatial organization and adjacencies

Arrival leads to a welcome room and a public court with food, seating, displays, and easy routes onward. A visible workshop and small demonstration room invite participation without forcing a newcomer into a seminar. Quieter paths lead toward a library, individual study rooms, and small conversation spaces. A movement studio, music room, and garden support kinds of knowing that are not limited to verbal argument.

Specialist laboratories have explicit access thresholds, preparation, storage, maintenance, and appropriate service routes. Residences connect to everyday amenities without passage through academic events. An exploration studio links simulation, instrument making, and remote observation; rocket launches or dangerous tests require a separate, justified site.

A continuous step-free route joins the public program. Physical signs, recognizable landmarks, resting spaces, accessible equipment choices, and quiet alternatives allow arrival without an account or wearable device. Remote participation expands access but is not presumed to replace accessible physical presence.

## Visible and invisible infrastructure

Visitors can see developing questions, failed experiments, instrument repair, and uncertain results, without exposing personal data or hazardous processes. Technical infrastructure includes power, networking, environmental controls, material handling, storage, cleaning, and waste treatment. Requirements depend on the disciplines chosen.

Knowledge also has maintenance costs: preserving records, checking provenance, correcting errors, translating explanations, and keeping equipment usable. AI-supported work identifies assistance and retains enough records for appropriate scrutiny. No architectural openness can substitute for sound evidence or independent criticism.

## Governance, access, refusal, and exit

Access to ordinary learning spaces and essential support does not depend on credentials, publications, or service hours. Limited instruments and specialist mentorship need transparent allocation, safety requirements, and appeals. A recurring public process reviews whose questions receive resources and which groups face practical barriers.

Participants can decline data collection, assessment, public presentation, or a project invitation. Leaving a group does not remove material security or ownership of personal records. Long-term projects need explicit commitments and handover practices so that freedom to leave does not hide preventable harm to collaborators. Expertise informs decisions within its scope; it does not confer unrestricted authority over the community.

## Dependencies and real tensions

The University depends on energy, instruments, supplies, care, accessible housing, maintenance, and trustworthy information. Some inquiries consume scarce land, materials, attention, or shared environmental capacity. Their appeal does not automatically justify those costs.

Deep expertise can become a closed social circle. Open access can overload mentors or desirable facilities. Patient inquiry conflicts with demands for exciting demonstrations. A failure branch produces an informal prestige economy: newcomers perform intelligence, fashionable projects absorb resources, and care or repair becomes socially invisible. Watch for opaque invitations, humiliating correction, and the claim that exceptional talent excuses harmful conduct.

## Partial and broad automation

With partial automation, concentrate on accessible libraries, shared workshops, field observation, supported teaching, and a limited set of maintainable instruments. Fund labor and access rather than relying on unpaid enthusiasm. With broad automation, experimentation and exploration may expand, including remote and eventual space-based work where feasible. Interpretation, research ethics, resource decisions, and the value of a question remain open human and collective concerns.

## Material life: public knowledge becomes usable things

**September 7 addition:** AI design interpretation of [Ren's regenerative-material direction](../archive/004-ren-regenerative-materials.txt), developed in [Regenerative material civilization](../foundations/regenerative-material-civilization.md).

A learner arrives with a broken observation instrument. Its public design record includes editable drawings, material requirements, interfaces, assembly and disassembly instructions, and the history of earlier repairs. She discovers why a clip failed, asks another group about their revision, and makes a replacement. A suitable process might be 3D printing, machining, or using an existing part. A downloadable file does not make every material or manufacturing process interchangeable.

The knowledge commons preserves authorship, license terms, version histories, reported failures, test conditions, and AI assistance. Records distinguish a promising prototype from a design evaluated for a particular use. Public availability does not establish suitability or grant access to hazardous equipment. Learners can challenge a design, improve it, and publish a compatible alternative; standards remain open to revision rather than becoming one issuer's permanent control over making. People may also keep and care for personally meaningful instruments.

A material library joins the digital one. Returned apparatus reaches an inspection bench, a repair workshop, and a store of reusable parts. Separated materials go to processes capable of handling them; rejected or contaminated stock has an explicit destination. Students can investigate what actually survives each transformation, including energy use, losses, and delayed availability. Recovering a useful part is itself a discovery worth sharing.

In the broad-autonomy branch, a research group may propose robots that help expand production facilities. Its proposal must show supplies, recovery throughput, usable inventory, energy, sites, and a stopping condition. More capacity could advance shared inquiry or multiply wasteful experiments and concentrated power. Open knowledge supports collective understanding; it does not by itself settle which physical interventions others must live with.
