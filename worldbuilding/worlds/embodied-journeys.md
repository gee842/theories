# Feeling the future: six embodied journeys

**Edition 05 · AI-authored scene and engineering-concept guide**, developed from [Ren's request](../archive/008-ren-embodied-worlds-and-ar-megastructure.txt) and the [independent edition-four review](../artifacts/friends-worldbook/reviews/2026-09-07-v4/README.md). The request makes two corrections: the examples need embodied experience and visible environmental/engineering imagination, and the AR world belongs in a megabuilding built for that purpose. This revision makes dependable provision, practiced cultural feedback, material transformation, and life after AR arrival visible; it also expands the existing expressive-community seed. These are directions for architecture, visual storytelling, and later prototypes. The scenes below are proposals, not quotations, measured outcomes, or qualified schematics.

The reader should be able to picture arriving, place a hand somewhere, move with another person, remain long enough for a relationship to happen, and understand a little of what makes the experience possible. A wide image establishes a place; closer views and a coherent sequence let someone inhabit it. A section then reveals what the image depends on. Keep the [technical](../foundations/automation-to-shared-abundance.md), [generative](../foundations/generative-worldmaking.md), and [material](../foundations/regenerative-material-civilization.md) accounts joined through each case.

Ren's [edition-five authorization](../archive/009-ren-edition-five-upgrade.txt) asks us to fix the review and update the project files. The specific characters, institutions, rituals, and geometry in these scenes are proposed implementations of that direction.

The guide preserves the six world IDs, including the newly developed `W04`. It adds journey and visual direction without replacing their full cultural briefs. The House's [edition-03](02-house-of-worlds-edition-03.md) and [edition-04](02-house-of-worlds-edition-04.md) formulations remain preserved; its current brief is a purpose-built AR megastructure with a common spatial reference across views.

## 1. Living Garden · W01

You step from the sheltered tram stop into a path that follows moving water. Breakfast is already happening somewhere beyond the trees. A child waves from a shallow observation basin; your friend has kept a place at a long table. You feel the cool rim of a shared metal cup, hear bowls being set down, and notice the garden opens onto people's homes rather than closing at a ticket gate. Later, rain begins. The sound moves across leaves and roofs while everyone shifts further under the shelter. You have no appointment. The weather becomes the afternoon's event.

| Frame | What the reader sees and feels |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival | Eye-level view from transit through a sheltered opening; the path and a distant occupied table promise somewhere to go. |
| First contact | A hand on a seat or cup, wet leaves near the path, another person making room; use a close view. |
| Inhabitation | The same companions eating, watching water insects, or speaking under a tree; show ordinary dwellings at the edge. |
| Changing condition | Rain reaches the courtyard while the continuous sheltered route remains occupied; water has a visible direction. |
| Material continuity | The cup enters a real return shelf; a nearby section shows dirty receiving, washing, inspection, and clean storage. |
| Remaining or leaving | A resident turns toward a private doorway while friends continue into the public garden; neither route is a backstage exit. |

**A freedom the reader can follow.** Carry Mira, in a teal shirt with an orange bag, through a changed commitment and the unchanged conditions of her day. She pauses a habitat-planning project, arranges a handover, has breakfast beside home, and retains the same care and household services. Later she borrows a telescope for a freely chosen evening of inquiry. The instrument library has an available model and a reservation process for equipment already in use; it asks for no commercial rationale or proof of contribution. A quiet return home completes the sequence. The full brief distinguishes the durable access arrangement from the productive capacity that supports it.

The scene may also reveal how provision survives a fault: reserve clean cups and an agreed nearby washing service bridge a kitchen repair. A willing qualified team and measured machine assistance do the remaining work. The explanation records the service and human effort actually required. An unexplained robot in the corner cannot certify that labor has disappeared.

**What the section must reveal.** Show the dwelling threshold, sheltered public path, planted basin, drainage route, kitchen, and reachable utility/service edge. The basin's intended water role must be explicit; its attractive appearance does not make it drinking-water treatment. The breakfast service includes food deliveries, preparation, clean/dirty separation, equipment maintenance, and washing demand. Garden machines reach their work areas without using private interiors as service corridors. Existing industrial methods and more capable automation support this system; the proposed scene's actual service performance needs its own account.

The culture comes through the affordances: a novice can learn the watershed by returning to the same place, neighbors share meals without scheduling a civic performance, and a long-kept tree can anchor several generations. The next image should show something close enough to touch as well as the breadth of the garden. Full brief: [Living Garden](01-living-garden.md).

## 2. Open University · W03

You arrive with a small question: why does the sea sometimes glow? A welcome table leads you toward a dark observation room. Your eyes adjust. Someone beside you turns a prepared demonstration vessel and a faint blue pattern appears. You compare what you saw with a drawing, then walk into a workshop where another group is repairing a marine instrument. One person has been here for years; another came this morning. By dusk you are helping arrange an observation, and the sea outside seems different because you now notice a question in it.

| Frame | What the reader sees and feels |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival | A public court offers a view into a demonstration room and workshop; a newcomer can watch before joining. |
| First contact | Close view of hands around a prepared observation and faces adjusting to dim light; the phenomenon is specific. |
| Inhabitation | The same people compare sketches and observations at a table with instrument parts beside them. |
| Making | At a repair bench, someone aligns a replaceable component with its public design record; show the tool and physical fit. |
| Underlying system | A section connects observation, preparation, controlled facilities, repair, clean storage, and service delivery. |
| A changed departure | Evening conversation beside water; the original newcomer points something out to a friend, continuing the inquiry outside. |

**What the section must reveal.** A university for this question has specific spaces: controlled lighting for observation, prepared demonstration equipment, suitable handling and cleaning, an instrument workshop, record stations, stores, and an external field connection. It is not an undefined room in which all science happens. Specialist work retains appropriate entry conditions; a public window and ordinary conversation table let people follow it. Knowledge includes measurements, uncertainties, method, and repair history. AI can help a learner find a path through those records while a person or group carries the continuity of inquiry.

The visual contrast is between the small physical action and the new reality it makes perceptible. A giant library alone cannot communicate it. The generative result is a question, an instrument, or an explanation that someone else can take further. Full brief: [Open University](03-open-university.md).

## 3. Exploration Community · W05

You hear conversation before seeing the spacecraft. The approach enters a public gallery high along the integration hall. Below, a large assembly moves slowly within its working zone; above it, the roof seems to disappear into structure and light. A friend shows you an instrument their group helped develop. Years later, or after a different mission's long journey, you sit together in the listening hall as its observations arrive. An unfamiliar ridge becomes visible. The image is small compared with the building, but the room falls quiet around it.

| Frame | What the reader sees and feels |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival | A person-sized threshold opens to the astonishing scale of the integration hall, viewed from a public gallery. |
| First contact | Close view of an educational instrument model or sample housing on a table, with the actual distant vehicle beyond. |
| Inhabitation | Friends, builders, and visitors compare mission questions while a separate handling operation continues below. |
| Technical section | Public gallery, assembly volume, separated operation zones, freight access, testing/preparation, and component stores occupy real space. |
| Discovery | An eye-level view among listeners as actual mission observations appear, with the reporting delay and provenance legible. |
| Continuing life | People leave for supper or remain with the new image; public annotations begin tomorrow's questions. |

**What the section must reveal.** Separate spacecraft integration and public learning from hazardous tests and launch activity. A regional diagram locates a distinct launch site; a civic courtyard does not become a launch exclusion area simply because a rocket looks impressive there. Show the assembly hall's handling space, service access, instrument preparation, inspection, storage, and maintenance without supplying operational rocket design. A returned fixture can be inspected and used by the next mission. Material sent into space leaves the terrestrial circulating stock unless it actually returns.

The cultural link is patience and shared purpose. Tools, data, failures, and explanations pass between groups and generations; a mission's worth includes what other people can understand through it. Avoid compressing years of construction and travel into one supposedly simultaneous afternoon. Images should distinguish assembly, departure, and received discovery as separate events. Full brief: [Exploration Community](05-exploration-community.md).

## 4. City of Shared Quests · W06

You pass under the canal arch and hear a rehearsal break into laughter. A friend who plays a mapmaker lends you a weathered coat from the public wardrobe. You spend the afternoon following a mystery through streets whose stories have accumulated for years. At the water's edge, people turn toward an imagined creature appearing in a shared media layer; physical lanterns and musicians carry the same event for others. Later, you return the coat for cleaning and eat with a relative who has never played. The day mattered because of the people who made it with you.

| Frame | What the reader sees and feels |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival | A lived canal street with doors, workshops, meals, and an unfolding event, seen from the pedestrian approach. |
| First contact | A friend fastens the borrowed coat; show fabric, hands, public storage, and affectionate conversation. |
| Inhabitation | A small group works out a clue using a real map table and street feature, rather than only reading a quest screen. |
| Shared wonder | From within the gathering, people look toward a common event above the canal; spectators and ordinary passersby remain present. |
| Next life of the season | Costume return, cleaning, mending, and a scenery module entering repair; the backstage has a usable physical route. |
| Ordinary morning | The same street next day: residents breakfast, new contributors discuss a story, and a nonparticipant crosses toward transit. |

**The inhabitants revise their world.** Add a connected episode at the map table. The successful Iron Heron guild receives first authoring choice and the best rehearsal times after winning, and its advantage reinforces itself. Ivo shows sketches that have never entered the season; a veteran notices the missing newcomers. They trial a separation between competitive honors and the allocation of shared authoring time, with supported new coauthors and rotating slots. The next canal scene includes Ivo's imagined creature and the veteran guild's music. The same people and recognizable sketch join the conversation to its consequence. Follow-up asks whose access actually changed.

Only quest authorship and rehearsal allocations were affected. Food, home, care, civic standing, and an ordinary life never depended on game results. This episode should make bounded rivalry and reflective worldmaking visible, rather than imply that a benevolent game designer grants necessities after reviewing performance.

**What the section must reveal.** Housing, kitchens, care, transit, and utilities form a real town beneath its persistent fiction. Workshops, wardrobes, material stores, and performance preparation are ordinary civic capacities. Optional media needs a chosen display mechanism, tracking, common event coordinates, and boundaries between recorded or public performance and private life. A visible creature does not become a physical obstacle unless a corresponding object exists. Permanent terrain and actual access remain intelligible independently of the story.

Here material security enables play, and public creative means let residents coauthor the town instead of consuming a finished attraction. Show guild relationships and long-lived narrative alongside unremarkable domestic life. An empty fantasy skyline or a close-up of a points counter would miss the world. Full brief: [City of Shared Quests](06-city-of-shared-quests.md).

## 5. House of Worlds · W02

You enter a building whose mass is made of three broad stacked world halls above a logistics base. A real forest path leads to a portal lift in the left passenger core; the cabin rises while its leaves seem to become a field of sky. The doors open onto a curved promenade above an apparent sea of clouds. Your hand finds the rail exactly where you see it. A friend sits on the amber bench ahead. Beyond it, musicians gather around a physical table for the First Arrival: the imagined vessel that brings the next season's weather. Its lights answer their shared rhythm. This is where architecture, machines, and shared perception become one experienced place with a continuing culture.

| Frame | What the reader sees and feels |
| --- | --- |
| Scale and purpose | Three broad stacked world halls over a logistics base; in the principal approach and section the passenger core is on the left, service core on the right, with amber technical bands between halls. |
| Departure | Within the forest realm, follow the same traveler and friend along a real curved path toward the lift. |
| Passage | Inside the actual cabin, a world transition accompanies ordinary vertical travel; the geometry remains consistent. |
| Arrival | Doorway view opens directly onto the cloud harbor; light, sound, horizon, companions, and contact surfaces continue coherently. |
| Sustained inhabitation | Keep the broad curved walkway, rail, amber bench, and physical music table. Newcomers and familiar players answer a repeated phrase while others listen; the First Arrival vessel remains an experienced distant event. |
| Matching physical view | Identical camera and anchors reveal the enclosed world hall, terrain deck, shared props, and environmental/tracking apparatus. |

The middle hall is the cloud-harbor reference. A quiet adjoining edge lets a visitor rest or remove the device and reach ordinary circulation without crossing the event. Paired scenes should keep the same contact geometry and support structure; a dense column forest in one view and a broad clear hall in another do not establish one building. These concepts still require a shared model and engineering study to resolve their geometry.

**What the section must reveal.** Large programmable volumes, physical paths and props, separated passenger/freight lifts, environmental zones, acoustic separation, tracking, compute/cooling, service decks, making studios, and generous component stores. Trace a curved terrain module from the closed middle hall to staging at the right service core, down to receiving/inspection in the logistics base, into repair or storage, and back for checked installation. Keep the left passenger route distinct. Show destinations for rejected material rather than drawing a perfect loop. The megabuilding is designed for AR world inhabitation. Refuge and ordinary access enable sustained use of that medium. The main visual language and the cloud-harbor geometry are proposed choices, not settled structural design.

The full visual-replacement branch requires an explicitly chosen occlusive display path; ordinary transparent glasses are not assumed to erase the building. Maintain corresponding walkway, rail, bench, people, and camera positions between experienced and physical views. The lift moves bodies; distant scenery changes perceived surroundings; terrain modules move between checked configurations while their zones are closed. A sky that looks boundless remains supported by a finite occupied hall. Full brief: [House of Worlds](02-house-of-worlds.md).

Place the display assumption beside the first immersive image and any standalone paired view: the proposal uses video-mediated or effective active-occlusion displays, with unresolved performance and form factor. The distant vessel and sky are experienced imagery; the rail, table, floor, and companions have real shared positions. This local explanation should accompany the image without replacing the embodied scene.

## 6. Expressive Commons · W04

You cross a dim threshold into a broad movement hall. Loose fabric catches warm light, a low note continues, and people answer it through movement. A friend offers a hand and waits. Sal sits at the cushioned edge simply listening; someone else is laughing after losing the rhythm. Later you step through an acoustic threshold into a quiet room and close the door. Expression, absorbed presence, and solitude all belong to this community without becoming tasks.

| Frame | What the reader sees and feels |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival | A person-sized threshold opens to a warm movement hall; belongings, water, rest, and a quieter parallel route make it usable. |
| Contact | An offered hand, real footing, seated participation, the texture of fabric, and an invitation someone can decline. |
| Inhabitation | The same people share an unfinished musical phrase, answering with small gestures or simply listening; no stage or score is needed. |
| A different rhythm | One resident enters an acoustically separated quiet room with a view and a door they control. |
| Supporting section | Hall, buffers, instruments, garment care, rigging access, service delivery, and private rooms occupy distinct spaces. |
| Ordinary continuation | Supper, a quiet walk, or home; the day does not need to culminate in a work, recording, or performance. |

**What sustains the experience.** Shared provision supplies ordinary life and creative means; specialized room time and instruments have explicit allocation. Necessary maintenance and care retain an account of willing human work and measured machine assistance. A return route shows garment washing, mending, storage, and next use. Responsive sound and light support stated interactions rather than secretly optimizing emotional engagement. Sound, floor, rigging, accessibility, and ventilation remain design questions. Full brief: [Expressive Commons](04-expressive-commons.md).

Give this case a different atmosphere from the learning commons: texture, darkness and warm light, weight, sound, pauses, intensity, privacy. A person sitting alone is already inhabiting the world. They do not need to be shown as preparing for productive participation.

## How to make the next artifact

Choose a small cast and carry them through a continuous route. Start with a scale view, then draw at eye or seated height close enough for contact and expressions to matter. Keep direction, daylight or authored lighting, clothing, anchors, and material identity coherent between frames. A different scenic image on every page does not establish a journey by itself.

Give each world both **experienced scenes and a supporting section or service sequence**. Label engineering drawings as proposed, with a distinction between physical structure, moving machinery, environmental effects, and perceptual appearance. Use captions to connect one concrete feature to its purpose; reserve uncertainty detail for the source brief where it does not need to crowd the image.

Make the foundations observable across the atlas. Productive recursion needs added equipment visibly helping produce a later addition, with outside inputs, maintenance, commissioning, and improved knowledge included. Dependable freedom needs a person's changed commitment with ordinary provision still available. Material transformation needs an identifiable part through successive useful lives: in the edition-five proposal an indigo-endplate steel bay moves from a garden pavilion through disassembly and qualification into a music room. Circulating that bay differs from building several simultaneous new rooms, which may require additional stock. A neighboring mature-tree decision shows why the power to reconfigure does not settle whose environment may be changed.

Use source images as observed evidence of the illustration, not as evidence that a future system exists. Captions should follow what the actual image contains and identify proposals locally when an isolated image might imply a current display capability or resolved engineering. “Concept section” is the appropriate label until a model and qualified design justify more.

Before adding polish, ask whether the reader can locate their body, see what another person is doing, imagine spending time there, and understand at least one real enabling system. Also ask whether the artifact preserves the causal origin: existing knowledge and automation directed into productive capacity, common means, and further generative worlds. The next step can be a storyboard or a rough 3D model; neither should be described as built or tested before it exists.
