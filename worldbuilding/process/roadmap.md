## Keep the whole argument connected

Ren's [technical-foundation correction](../archive/007-ren-technical-foundation-and-next-version.txt) restores the reason these worlds are practical directions to pursue. Follow **existing knowledge and useful automation → capacity that helps build capacity → conditional compounding → generous common provision and creative tools → less compelled human work → people building further generative systems**. Technical possibility, material inheritance, and cultural direction need to remain legible together in future revisions. Free association is one capability the resulting systems enable.

The technical foundation and edition-five visual plan now include both feedback loops: productive equipment contributing to further equipment, and operating knowledge improving what the network can do. The next measured technical study can run alongside a world study: choose a useful service, map its equipment and upstream inputs, identify a repeatable production cell, and show how its output could equip another installation. Use the [industrial](../research/2026-09-07-industrial-automation-evidence.md) and [AI](../research/2026-09-07-ai-engineering-evidence.md) cases as starting points with preserved boundaries. Measure completed useful output, interventions, maintenance, commissioning, and the dependency constraining the next addition. Then connect that output to who can use the service and what their actual freedom to create becomes.

## Next substantive cycle: a world, its culture, and one dependency

The courtyard was the initial suggested starting place. Ren's [correction](../archive/006-ren-meta-level-and-worlds.txt) restores equally substantial exploration, MMORPG-like life, and experiential AR directions. The next study can begin with any of these bounded questions; the choice should follow what we most want to learn.

| Study | What it should reveal | First concrete output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [House of Worlds](../worlds/02-house-of-worlds.md): purpose-built megastructure and portal-linked halls | How can dedicated structure, real passenger movement, sensory systems, and physical anchors make different shared realms inhabitable? | A common three-hall reference, forest-to-cloud-harbor passage, First Arrival music gathering, and physical/experienced views with left passenger and right service cores |
| [Exploration Community](../worlds/05-exploration-community.md): spacecraft hall and listening garden | How can an intergenerational mission become a shared culture, public knowledge, and an ordinary place to live? | Public-gallery/assembly-hall section and a first-image evening scene; launch site shown as a separate regional relationship |
| [City of Shared Quests](../worlds/06-city-of-shared-quests.md): canal, guild house, and ordinary route home | What makes a persistent shared story welcoming to new coauthors, with room for people whose lives are elsewhere? | Festival/ordinary-morning blockout, newcomer exclusion from authorship and the revised rule, and a panel or costume’s next life |
| [Living Garden](../worlds/01-living-garden.md): courtyard and watershed | How do ecological knowledge, shared provision, and everyday relationships shape a place together? | Mira’s home–provision–instrument-library route, a tree-preserving footprint decision, and a courtyard section with water, maintenance, and returns |
| [Expressive Commons](../worlds/04-expressive-commons.md): movement room and quiet edge | How can embodied feeling, privacy, and rest have material support without an obligation to perform? | Arrival–movement–rest sequence, sound-separated routes, and a reused pavilion bay in a music room |
| [Open University](../worlds/03-open-university.md): arrival and public workshop | How does understanding become a way of inhabiting reality and a resource for another person's inquiry? | Learning-to-making sequence and the public knowledge/material-recovery connection |

1. **Write and show a day and a cultural change.** Follow several inhabitants, including someone who wants little involvement. Include a disagreement, a moment of care, and an unremarkable pleasure. Then trace how one shared experience changes a habit, relationship, story, or future undertaking. Make the positive ambition tangible without assuming every resident embodies it perfectly. Use arrival, close interaction, sustained inhabitation, and departure to identify a sequence of images; include reachable surfaces and what people actually do together.
2. **Select one important dependency.** For example: how this district secures water, food, energy, repair, and access to housing when resident employment is optional. Bound the inquiry to the systems necessary for this district; do not imply local self-sufficiency where it has not been justified.
3. **Investigate that dependency.** Use dated primary evidence for real capability claims. Map the remaining human work, external suppliers, inputs, failure modes, and institutional access arrangements. Document what changes under weaker automation.
   Where it involves production, trace how new equipment can help produce or maintain further equipment. Distinguish additional hardware from commissioned useful capacity, and a bounded working method from coverage of every task. Give the engineering program a next action, not only an uncertainty label.
4. **Draw the relationships.** Produce an adjacency plan and a section before architectural styling. Show routes for residents, visitors, care, maintenance, and material flows. Match the same major volumes, lift cores, paths, physical anchors, and structural vocabulary across the section and inhabited views. For the dedicated AR House, show world halls and service levels as a coordinated architecture, with experienced apparent extent clearly distinguished from actual occupied geometry. Establish dimensions only when they help a specific design question, marking their basis.
5. **Build a small 3D study if it helps.** Model the chosen threshold or public place, its neighboring uses, and the relevant service interface. A portal arrival, spacecraft gallery, quest square, courtyard, or university workshop can each anchor a bounded study. Preserve editable geometry and a viewable export.
6. **Walk through contrasting conditions.** A festival, an ordinary quiet day, maintenance disruption, and a person refusing automated guidance. Inspect what can be reached, understood, maintained, and contested.
7. **Revise the world and map.** Record what the spatial study revealed, which assumptions changed, and what to test next.

The cycle is complete when one place is easier to imagine, one important dependency is better understood, and the relation between the culture and its environment has become more concrete. A successful visual gives readers a lived possibility and helps expose a question; it does not validate a causal theory.

## Material continuity: two connected studies

Ren’s [2026-09-07 addition](../archive/004-ren-regenerative-materials.txt) gives the first spatial cycle a concrete material question. Follow one shared cup through use, return, washing, inspection, and further use, with a damaged-item route and an actual proposed recovery destination. Compare the shared service with a long-kept personal cup and other locally plausible alternatives. Use the [materials evidence note](../research/2026-09-07-material-circulation.md) to define what needs measurement; do not assign environmental savings without a service comparison.

At architectural scale, the edition-five proposal follows one indigo-endplate structural bay from a garden pavilion through disassembly, inspection, qualification, and storage into a music room. A next measured study should test that proposed sequence and the additional layers or stock it needs. Show service layers, handling access, rejected parts, and occupant continuity. These two studies can inhabit the same Living Garden project and connect to the House of Worlds’ reversible spaces and Open University’s public knowledge library.

The initial outputs can be diagrams, an annotated plan/section, and scenes. Proceed to a 3D study when the geometry will resolve a named question. Distinguish rapid reconfiguration of existing stock from adding simultaneous productive capacity; both need explicit inputs and bottlenecks.

## Develop the possibility network

The [graph](../foundations/possibility-map.md) connects productive expansion and shared creative means with generative conditions, authorship, revisable understanding, and feedback between people and worlds. Extend it as evidence and worlds accumulate. The expressive/embodied branch now has a full brief under `W04`. Useful future branches include ordinary neighborhoods, inter-community encounters, and mixed societies with uneven access to automation. Develop exploration beyond its terrestrial brief only after specifying what a later mission or habitat requires.

Follow constructive feedback as carefully as institutional failure: a game-world festival starts an astronomy guild; the guild joins the exploration community; public instrument knowledge returns to the university; new discoveries reshape another festival. The sequence should show specific people, tools, choices, and learning. It need not make every activity serve a larger project.

Add cultural detail alongside institutions: language, humor, festivals, childhood, relationships, informal prestige, aging, grief, stories, and habits of repair. A culture should have internal differences and a history; it should not read as a charter enacted perfectly by every inhabitant.

Compare worlds that share principles but choose different arrangements. Also compare similar-looking architecture under different power relations: a garden can be welcoming, paternalistic, exclusionary, or collectively shaped. Its appearance alone does not decide its society.

## Research as needed

The [research agenda](../foundations/research-agenda.md) prioritizes technical, institutional, and cultural uncertainties. Preserve a creative version of a world while testing its dependencies; record weaker and slower variants rather than forcing every uncertainty into one optimistic estimate.

If numerical growth modeling becomes useful, first specify the production network, units, external inputs, bottlenecks, and evidence for rates. A curve labeled exponential is not an industrial model.
