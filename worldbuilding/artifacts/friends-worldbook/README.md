# Worlds we can inhabit

7 September 2026 · **Edition 05: The systems and lives of a generative civilization** · A worldbuilding project by Ren, developed with AI.

**Edition five is complete and reviewed.** The [illustrated atlas](output/pdf/worlds-we-can-inhabit-v5.pdf) has 34 pages and 25 distinct source images, each placed once, including eight new illustrations. It brings the technical, social, generative and material argument into the same reading experience as the imagined worlds. Earlier editions remain preserved below.

## What this edition develops

The opening shows two connected feedback loops: useful production equipping further productive capacity, and observations becoming tested public knowledge that improves later operation. Ren’s thesis—that existing knowledge and AI make full automation an engineering undertaking—is explicit. Bounded existing capabilities support the starting point; the proposed integration program is not presented as an already demonstrated autonomous supply network or a measured growth forecast.

Shared freedom becomes a lived episode. Mira can pause a garden-planning commitment while her home, food and care continue, then use a shared telescope to begin another interest. A service interruption makes maintenance, reserve provision and the acceptable arrangements for remaining human attention visible. Productive capacity and durable common access must be designed together.

The generative principles now precede the worlds. Later, a quest guild notices how its rewards prevent newcomers from authoring stories and tests a different access rule. People learn to revise the conditions that shape their behavior. An expressive commons adds a distinct atmosphere for movement, feeling, listening and rest.

The House of Worlds has coordinated concepts for three broad stacked halls, opposing passenger and service cores, and a recurring First Arrival musical gathering. The physical/experienced pair retains its reachable anchors. A route diagram follows a visitor and a tactile module; the display assumptions are explained beside the experienced scenes. These views share an architectural concept rather than a resolved geometric model.

Material continuity follows the same indigo-marked steel frame from a garden pavilion through disassembly and inspection to a public music room. A second episode shows people revising construction around a mature tree. The material loop distinguishes reusing an existing stock from adding simultaneous capacity, and material recovery from ecological renewal.

## Read and share

The [PDF](output/pdf/worlds-we-can-inhabit-v5.pdf) is the visual reading experience. Its text is selectable, with bookmarks and clickable external sources. It is not a tagged PDF.

## Images, editable sources and review

All eight new illustrations used the built-in image-generation tool. Their exact prompts, generation history, selected original hashes and inspection notes are preserved in the [AR record](assets-v5/ar/README.md), [social record](assets-v5/social/README.md), and [material record](assets-v5/materials/README.md). All selected source PNGs are 1536×1024 and remain unchanged. The PDF uses complete uncropped frames, serialized as high-quality JPEG streams at those same dimensions.

Each source image is placed once. The AR pair deliberately returns to the First Arrival gathering to compare physical and experienced space; this is an explanatory comparison, not an accidental repeated opening image. Images and cutaways are concepts, not validated engineering designs or views exported from a common 3D model.

Editable [content](content-v5.json) and [layout builder](build-v5.py), the [image placement manifest](image-placements-v5.json), [export checks](qa-report-v5.json), and the [edition-five review](reviews/2026-09-07-v5/README.md) keep the result inspectable. The reader builder preserves full source copy while adding authored descriptions. Every page was visually inspected; the final export, companion copy, package links and unchanged earlier PDF were verified. Two fresh reviewers checked first-reader understanding and fidelity to the sources.
