01 / AN ORDINARY DAY
Your place here
is already yours.
You wake in a home opening onto a garden. Breakfast is available at the
neighborhood kitchen. You can join a table, carry a cup outside, or stay home.
Along the path, someone is tending a patch of moss. Children are testing a
floating path. A neighbor who once organized everything now spends long
afternoons reading. Her home remains hers to inhabit.
The city is a garden with rooms inside it: places for friendship, solitude, care,
invention, and seasons. Machines maintain the systems that keep it livable.
Residents can take part because they want to.
You have no plan for today. You still belong.

02 / THE CONDITIONS OF FREEDOM
What makes this
possible?
Imagine autonomous intelligence and robots reliably operating enough of the
industrial network to provide and maintain the means of living. Some machines
help build factories that make more machines.
01
A technological premise
We grant this capability to explore a possible future. This project does not
establish complete autonomy today or predict an arrival date.
02
A durable material claim
Everyone can access housing, food, care, learning, and creative tools. The
claim survives unemployment, unpopularity, and disagreement with those
administering the system.
03
A meaningful way to leave
People can leave a community without losing their means of living.
Communities can challenge the systems serving them.
Productive capacity and the right to share in it have to be built
together.

03 / OPEN UNIVERSITY
Many ways to
spend a life.
At the Open University, a question about the sea can become an afternoon of
observation or a project lasting years. You can learn an instrument, study
perception, leave, return, and begin again. Understanding changes what you
notice.
Elsewhere, people gather around dance, sensation, costume, and music.
Exploration communities build observatories and imagine journeys beyond Earth.
Gardeners devote themselves to a small piece of ground.
These lives overlap. You can be absorbed in astronomy for a season and
friendship for the next. Some people have no great project. They cook, swim, fall
in love, watch the weather. A generous civilization makes room for them too.
Belonging never requires an impressive answer to what you are becoming.

04 / HOUSE OF WORLDS
Realities we
make together.
Your friends meet in a building whose rooms become places they imagine
together. An elevator opens onto a forest performance, a floating archive, or a
birthday beneath an invented sky. Augmented reality layers shared stories onto
physical rooms; light, sound, surfaces, and adaptable assemblies deepen the
experience.
An ordinary path remains available through the building. You can remove the
glasses, find a quiet room, or leave. A responsive environment must also
understand a refusal.
Some rooms change constantly. Others stay familiar for years. People need both
the freedom to transform a place and the freedom to recognize it when they
return.

05 / MATTER HAS MANY LIVES
A cup with
a next life.
You return the cup near the library. It is washed, inspected, and made available
again. Someone else keeps a beloved cup at home for decades.
A PROPOSED LOOP / RECOVERY IS PART OF THE DESIGN
Use
Return
Clean +
inspect
Use again
When needed: repair, assess, recover material, and make again.
Energy and process inputs enter. Losses and residues need destinations.
The shared service includes cleaning, collection, repair, transport, and eventual
recovery. Its value depends on those processes working well. Durable metal alone
does not guarantee a good outcome. [1]
Public design libraries hold instructions, compatible connections, and lessons from
earlier failures. Fabrication might involve printing, machining, forming, or reusing
an intact part.
Care, repair, reuse, and remaking can give an object many useful lives. Energy helps
materials take new forms; losses still need to be counted. The aim is to keep useful
possibilities available across generations.

06 / ARCHITECTURE WITH A FUTURE
A building's
next life.
A pavilion is being taken apart. Robots lift its members into a material yard, where
each part is inspected for possible use elsewhere. Its history matters: what it
carried, how it weathered, what was repaired.
Buildings were designed with their later lives in mind. Connections are accessible.
Services can be replaced. Useful components have somewhere to go when a room
changes. A shared library of parts supports many architectures.
Recoverability has limits. Structural reuse needs assessment; making more robots
and buildings at the same time can tie up additional material. Circulation does not
make expansion limitless. [2, 3] Living systems need care of their own. Reusing steel
does not restore a watershed.
Freedom includes preserving the capacity to change our surroundings
again.

07 / LIVING WITH OTHER PEOPLE
The machines
are ready.
Are we?
The neighborhood proposes turning a square into a wetland. Children want
floating paths. An artist imagines mist and light. Someone worries about
nesting birds. An older resident would lose the place where she meets her
friends.
The machines could begin quickly. Agreement takes longer.
Greater ability to build also gives someone greater ability to erase a place,
exhaust shared capacity, or impose a vision. Technical capability cannot settle
who may authorize a change. Some experiments can begin small; some
consequences cannot be undone by rearranging materials.
People still disagree, seek recognition, and enjoy winning. There are fierce
games, rival festivals, and demanding competitions. Their rewards cannot buy
control over someone else's necessities.
This culture practices hearing objections, protecting a refusal, and repairing
mistakes. Hope lives partly in noticing when a beautiful plan has harmed
someone.

08 / AN INVITATION
Come imagine
it with us.
This is a worldbuilding project about possible lives. Its technical assumptions,
institutions, customs, and places are open to examination. An attractive picture is
the beginning of that work.
01
Where would you want to spend a day?
02
What would you want to keep from your life now?
03
What would you refuse - and who might be left out?
One place to begin is a Living Garden courtyard: homes, a shared kitchen, a quiet
place, a cup's return journey, and a change residents disagree about. A spatial
study and an explorable scene could let the details challenge the vision.
We share the world in which our different futures have to live.

ABOUT THIS INVITATION
A possible world.
An open project.
This booklet introduces a worldbuilding project by Ren, developed with AI. It brings
together secure existence, plural ways of living, public knowledge, and materials
designed for continuing use and transformation.
The places and institutions are proposals. Broad autonomous provision is a scenario
assumption. The images are generated concepts, not architectural plans or evidence
that the depicted systems work.
The project develops through lived scenes, research, spatial studies, and eventual
explorable worlds. Disagreement and a closer look at the details are welcome.
