# Independent evidence and argument review — Edition 04

Review completed on 7 September 2026. Reviewed [Worlds we can inhabit, Edition 04](../../output/pdf/worlds-we-can-inhabit-v4.pdf).

Read all 25 pages, including rendered illustrations, and checked the ten cited primary URLs. No previous editions or reviews consulted, and no other reviewers consulted before or during this independent verdict. The PDF and its source files were not changed. This file preserves the completed independent review.

**Verdict:** This is a convincing speculative atlas with responsibly bounded technical references. I found no material factual error in the seven evidence summaries. Its principal weakness is the compressed causal bridge from productive machinery to dependable shared abundance.

1. **Page 3 overstates what the evidence establishes.** “Useful pieces” is justified; “a practical development path” is stronger than the cited evidence supports. The sources demonstrate capabilities in separate systems, but do not establish their integration into an expanding autonomous supply network. Gemini’s agent experiments cover eight tasks, retain planning, success-detection, and action failures, and include additional training for one robot platform. PI explicitly describes substantial remaining research and human intervention. These are real starting points; connecting them remains part of the proposed research program. The existing “construction thesis” qualification is good, but this one phrase weakens it. A bounded improvement would identify one next integration test—such as a productive cell operating, maintaining itself with documented assistance, and supplying equipment for another cell. [Gemini report](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03342v3), [PI partner report](https://www.pi.website/blog/partner).

2. **Pages 3 and 22 leave the social conversion mechanism implicit.** The arrow from “Further productive capacity” to “Abundant shared means” contains decisions about ownership, entitlement, allocation, and who performs remaining essential work. “Shared access can remove the need to sell labor merely to survive” is a defensible conditional proposal, but shared access to workshops alone does not establish dependable access to food, housing, care, energy, and external inputs. Page 22’s protection of necessities from competitive winners supplies a valuable principle. The argument would become more concrete with a short account of how necessities remain available during shortages and how maintainers or system operators remain accountable. This needs an institutional example, rather than a complete constitution.

3. **Pages 3 and 21 acknowledge resource costs without carrying them through the expansion story.** Energy, inspection, storage, and ecological renewal are explicitly acknowledged—there is no claim of costless recycling. The remaining gap is explaining that preserving material value and expanding the total stock are different operations. The cited IEA report explains how growth and stock-turnover delays limit scrap availability, and how contamination can constrain secondary steel quality. The atlas would benefit from one sentence connecting expansion to additional primary inputs, infrastructure, and ecological limits, alongside reuse. A full numerical lifecycle assessment is unnecessary for this edition. [IEA roadmap, especially pp. 64–66](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/eb0c8ec1-3665-4959-97d0-187ceca189a8/Iron_and_Steel_Technology_Roadmap.pdf).

4. **Page 22 offers coherent generative principles, with an optional opportunity to show them governing a difficult choice.** Rest, quiet lives, continuity, plural purposes, bounded rivalry, and revisability prevent “generative” from becoming a demand for constant productivity. The phrase 生生不息 is explicitly developed as this project’s interpretation, rather than presented as an exhaustive historical definition. What remains abstract is a conflict between worthy beginnings—for example, preserving a mature garden versus constructing another world hall. Showing inhabitants negotiate one such choice would demonstrate the principles’ practical force.

**AR assessment, pages 13–20 and 25:** The central account is technically coherent as a proposal. It distinguishes physical distance from apparent distance and explicitly requires an occlusive or passthrough display for substantial visual replacement. That correctly avoids assuming ordinary additive transparent optics erase buildings. Shared spatial anchors already support multiuser experiences, although Google describes its Cloud Anchors as best suited to room-scale use; this does not establish the atlas’s city or megastructure scale. Tracking, display comfort, synchronization, and physical reconfiguration remain development questions, appropriately covered by the proposal framing. No demand for an already engineered megastructure is warranted. Bringing the display assumption forward from page 25 to the first House of Worlds page would make its status clearer while reading. [Google ARCore documentation](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/cloud-anchors), [Stanford group’s occlusion-display research](https://www.computationalimaging.org/publications/factored-occlusion-ar-display-ieee-vr-2020/).

The three materials references are relevant further reading, with no apparent misrepresentation:

- **UNEP, 2021:** Reuse benefits depend on number of uses, washing, energy mix, and local conditions. It supports conditional lifecycle thinking, rather than a universal rule that reuse always wins. [Report](https://www.lifecycleinitiative.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/UNEP-D002-Beverage-Cups-Report_lowres.pdf).
- **JRC, 2025:** The actual PDF supports assessment, documentation, storage, design, and installation requirements for reclaimed steel. **Publication record: 21 November 2025.** Its scope is guidance toward design rules. [Report](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC144410/JRC144410_01.pdf), [publication record](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC144410). The browser’s initial direct-PDF request returned an internal error; the actual cited PDF was subsequently retrieved successfully from the same primary URL and inspected, alongside the JRC publication record.
- **IEA, 2020:** Relevant to material efficiency, primary production, scrap availability, and steelmaking energy. Its future quantities are scenario projections, which the atlas does not incorrectly present as observations. [Report](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/eb0c8ec1-3665-4959-97d0-187ceca189a8/Iron_and_Steel_Technology_Roadmap.pdf).

The strongest achievement is that the imagined freedom has a visible maintenance culture. The garden, university, spacecraft community, quest city, and world halls all include service spaces, repair, shared learning, and ordinary social life. Pages 19 and 25 make the visual proposals’ status unusually clear. Preserve those strengths; concentrate any argument revision on the three transitions above.
