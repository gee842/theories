# Independent spatial review of Edition 04

Reviewed 7 September 2026. This records the completed independent review, before coordination with other reviewers. All 25 pages were inspected visually and their captions read. No earlier reviews or project QA were consulted.

Source: [Worlds we can inhabit / Edition 04](../../output/pdf/worlds-we-can-inhabit-v4.pdf)

**Independent verdict: strong architectural storytelling, with no major failure within its stated concept-art scope.** The main remaining weaknesses concern interpretation and continuity, rather than a need for construction drawings.

## Prioritized findings

1. **P2 — Put the proposed-display qualification beside the complete visual replacement (pp. 17–19; qualification currently p. 25).** People appear to wear ordinary-looking clear glasses while the enclosed building disappears into an immense sky. Page 19’s reference to a “proposed display system” helps; page 25 explicitly explains occlusion/passthrough and the limits of transparent glasses. A reader who sees an isolated spread can still infer that the depicted glasses already deliver this effect. Add one short local sentence or image note identifying these as experienced views through a proposed occlusive/passthrough display. The floating world itself is appropriate concept imagery.

2. **P3 — “Working section” sounds more resolved than these illustrations are (pp. 6, 12, 20; also p. 15).** The cutaways convincingly allocate space to maintenance, storage, fabrication, utilities, and movement. They do not establish complete circulation or service connections. On p. 20, for example, the lift banks, freight area, technical layers, and world halls are visible, but the route by which a replacement setting reaches a hall is not traceable. “Concept section” would match the evidence more precisely; a few named connections would strengthen the explanatory value. This is not a request for engineering detail.

3. **P3 — The House of Worlds has consistent motifs, but cannot be read as one building’s coordinated views (pp. 16, 19, 20).** Page 16 shows a staggered, open-sided composition; p. 19 shows a dense longitudinal column grid; p. 20 shows three broad, largely uninterrupted stacked arenas. Dark cores, monumental spans, and technical layers tie them together, but the actual spatial organization changes. Page 25 already acknowledges that the views do not come from one resolved model, so this is disclosed. If geographic continuity matters, carry one recognizable hall, core, or doorway through these views, or put the existing conceptual-view qualification earlier.

4. **P3 — The House’s inhabitation remains primarily sightseeing (pp. 17–19).** Arrival and awe are effective, and the seating makes lingering credible. Compared with the garden’s meals and water play, the university’s instrument making, and the quest city’s collective authorship, this world gives less evidence of an ongoing shared activity. One small gathering doing something specific in the cloud harbor would substantiate the proposed traditions and continued inhabitation.

## What works especially well

- **Embodiment:** the foreground hand on the garden wall (p. 5), manipulable instruments (p. 8), quest object and nearby companions (p. 14), and lift threshold (p. 17) make the viewer’s body consequential.
- **Ordinary life:** pp. 4–7 and 9 mix learning, eating, gardening, rest, and conversation. Older people and wheelchair users participate in the scenes instead of serving only as scale figures.
- **Architecture supporting experience:** pp. 6, 12, and 15 give maintenance its own spatial presence. Page 12’s public audience, visible assembly chamber, workshops, and lower logistics level communicate useful program relationships.
- **AR explanation:** p. 19 is the strongest explanatory image. The paired foreground bodies, curved bench, walkway, and railing remain recognizably aligned; the illustration successfully distinguishes tactile space from perceived scenery.
- **Scope honesty:** p. 25 explicitly distinguishes shared design vocabulary from resolved geometry and visualized cutaways from engineering feasibility. That is the correct standard for this atlas.

Construction feasibility remains unestablished, as the book says. The images nevertheless communicate intelligible architectural proposals; their unresolved engineering does not invalidate their conceptual fidelity.
