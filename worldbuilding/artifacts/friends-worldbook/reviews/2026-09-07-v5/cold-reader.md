## What a friend can understand without earlier context

**The causal premise is understandable.** Pages 2–4 distinguish a production loop from a knowledge loop: equipment can help equip further production, while tested improvements can travel between facilities. Outside inputs, maintenance, commissioning and reliability constrain this process. I did not read this as a claim that a closed, fully autonomous economy already exists or has a measured doubling time.

**The future direction is clear.** The ambition is to connect existing capabilities into more dependable automation and make the resulting capacity available for generous lives. The atlas offers several directions for living, rather than one mandatory life plan. Pages 4 and 32 offer a construction direction, although they do not constitute a costed transition plan.

**Human freedom is concrete.** Mira's page 5 is particularly effective: she leaves a group, keeps her home and care, obtains food, and gains access to a new interest. Pages 6–7 and 21 repeatedly make rest, private life and refusal legitimate. The scope of the essentials promise under a human-work shortfall needs the clarification below.

**The places feel inhabited.** Food, conversations, instruments, maintenance, recurring gatherings and ordinary returns give the places more substance than destination images alone. Pages 17–20 show authorship and institutions; pages 22–27 explain the distinction between physical footing and experienced space. The university and garden remain especially easy to imagine living in.

**This differs from attractive scenery for identifiable reasons.** A resident can change association without losing subsistence (p5), a community can revise access to creating stories (p20), one material stock can support successive settings (p29), and people can choose to preserve an existing place (p31). Those are changes in social and material conditions. The argument would survive removing the decorative style of the images.

## Findings and concrete corrections

### 1. Page 6: distinguish essential coverage from optional postponement

**Priority: substantive content clarification. Status: pending at handoff; lead reviewer has accepted a correction.**

The page promises dependable home, food, care and mobility, then says that if needed voluntary human attention cannot be secured, people revise the service or its timing. A first-time reader cannot tell whether essential care may simply be deferred. The broken cup washer is a useful example of reserve coverage, but it does not by itself settle the harder case of an essential need that still requires a person.

This is an unresolved design condition in an openly proposed arrangement, not evidence that the author's current factual claim is false. The correction should state that reserve and partner coverage protect essentials, optional expansion can wait, and an uncovered essential need is a failure of the provision promise requiring repair. It should preserve the refusal of compelled labor. That makes the intended priority visible without pretending the staffing problem has been empirically solved.

### 2. Page 26: the route strip sent the visitor toward the service core

**Priority: diagram consistency. Status: corrected and visually verified in the refreshed page 26 render.**

The first render showed passenger core → middle hall → service core. That contradicted the separate passenger and service paths in the prose and page 27. The refreshed strip now converges on the hall from the two sides. It communicates the intended relation cleanly.

### 3. Page 3: the two feedback loops need visible two-way coupling

**Priority: diagram clarity. Status: corrected and visually verified in the refreshed page 3 render.**

The first version showed only a downward connection from production to knowledge, although its adjacent label also said tested knowledge improves production. The refreshed version supplies both directions. The two-loop explanation now agrees with the graphic.

### 4. Page 27: the checking label and return route were visually tangled

**Priority: minor diagram readability. Status: corrected and visually verified in the refreshed page 27 render.**

The original visitor-return diagonal passed through the region of the setting-check label, and the module arrow pointed into empty space beside the music table. The refreshed diagram separates the return path, adds a tactile-setting box and places the module exchange beside it. This resolves the earlier ambiguity. A small optional refinement is to use two visibly separated legend entries or color samples; the current legend reads as one run of words. This does not prevent comprehension.

### 5. Page 24: listener location does not match the image

**Priority: minor caption accuracy. Status: pending at handoff; lead reviewer plans to correct it.**

The caption places another listener on the ochre bench, but the two visible bench occupants are playing instruments. There is a seated listener in a wheelchair and a person paused by the rail. Refer to the person pausing by the rail, or simply say another person listens nearby. No image regeneration is needed.

### 6. Pages 2 and 20: two avoidable first-reader stumbling points

**Priority: minor wording. Status: pending at handoff; lead reviewer has accepted both corrections.**

- Page 2 introduces “cell” before defining it. Say “small production unit” at first use, then use “cell” if useful. A friend should not have to infer an industrial term during the central premise.
- Page 20 says the guild “keeps its honors and trials a separate, open route.” “Trials” is being used as a verb, but the reader first parses “honors and trials” as a pair of nouns. Replace “trials” with “tests.”

The known cover edition change and page 25 pose/framing qualification were not treated as new findings.

## What remains open without being a defect in a concept atlas

The text does not demonstrate full autonomous supply, current AI sufficiency for all tasks, an industrial growth rate, structural spans, display performance, recovery yields, or a complete institutional design. Pages 3–4, 6, 25, 27, 30 and 34 generally mark these boundaries plainly. Demanding those demonstrations as a condition for presenting this proposal would apply the wrong standard to this artifact.

There is still more social agreement than resistance in the episodes. The guild accepts revision, and the neighbors can alter a construction proposal together. A later edition could strengthen institutional credibility with one unresolved disagreement or a scarce workshop-slot decision that shows who decides, how a newcomer can challenge it, and what happens when agreement fails. That would add depth; its absence does not make the stated freedom or central premise unintelligible.
