# Material lives and collective judgment — Edition 05

Two AI-generated conceptual illustrations for the fifth edition of *Worlds we can inhabit*, created 2026-09-07 with the built-in `image_gen.imagegen` tool in separate calls. Both selected PNGs are 1536 × 1024 pixels, copied byte-for-byte into the project. The generated originals remain preserved. No image editing or resizing was performed.

Exact submitted prompts are in [prompts.json](prompts.json). [generation-metadata.json](generation-metadata.json) records the original paths, dimensions, byte sizes, checksums, method, and inspection status.

## Beam lives

[beam-lives.png](beam-lives.png) follows an identifiable pale structural portal across three separated temporal panels: a garden tea pavilion; disassembly and inspection in a work area; and a later public music room. Indigo connector plates and the upper-left triangular plate with a circular opening recur in all three views. The center panel includes a visible overhead hoist supporting the crossmember, a robotic manipulator, a person inspecting recovered components, stacked timber layers, and a separate small tray of seals and fasteners. The final room holds a cellist, a percussionist, and an intimate audience.

The sequence shows the proposal of preserving useful members while changing the architectural layers around them. It does not establish an exact bill of parts, structural qualification for reuse, a robot work procedure, or a quantified recovery rate. The recurring member's markings make the narrative identity clear; generated weathering and connection details are not a precise inspection record. The requested seated or cradled columns became upright staged members in the generated middle view. This does not obscure the intended sequence, and the image is accepted as a concept narrative.

## Garden choice

[garden-choice.png](garden-choice.png) places six neighbors around a detailed physical model of the garden in which they are meeting. A model tree and its visible circular root-area boundary correspond to the large mature tree immediately behind them. One participant moves an indigo-jointed building bay; removed modules rest in a tray at the table edge. The reduced proposed structure stands away from the tree's protected area. A wheelchair user and a quieter listener take part at the same table. Idle robotic equipment and stacked components remain in the background beyond the tree gathering space.

The image supports a caption about revising a proposal to preserve a loved place while retaining the means to build. It shows deliberation and a revised arrangement; the exact earlier footprint and the group's history require the accompanying narrative. It is an illustrative decision, not a claim about any actual consultation or construction process.

## Source connections and visual inspection

The episodes develop [Regenerative material civilization](../../../../foundations/regenerative-material-civilization.md), especially a structural bay's successive uses, freedom to maintain a place, and the difference between technical capacity and authority to transform. They respond to the [Edition 04 full review](../../reviews/2026-09-07-v4/README.md), particularly its material-inheritance and collective-worldmaking findings. The garden palette and architectural vocabulary follow the existing [garden walk](../../assets-v4/garden-university/garden-walk.png), which was visually inspected before prompting. No image reference was supplied to the generation calls; both illustrations are new scenes.

Both images were inspected at their full source dimensions. No text or logos were generated. Panel divisions, recognizable component motifs, scale, people, principal surfaces, the model's tree boundary, the unused modules, and equipment placement are legible. The images are accepted for the proposed narrative role; they are not evidence of engineering readiness.
