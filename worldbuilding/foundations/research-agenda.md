# Research agenda for believable futures

Status: AI research planning, started 2026-09-05; expanded 2026-09-07. Proposed method and questions, open to revision.

The creative center of this project is life after material survival no longer depends on employment. Its practical starting point is existing AI, robotics, and industrial knowledge directed toward systems that produce further capacity and common provision. Research should explain how that construction can proceed and make its consequences concrete: buildings, relationships, institutions, learning, play, and ways of becoming. Preserve Ren's strong engineering thesis and investigate what supports it, what remains unproven, and what can be attempted next.

Three bounded external evidence notes now exist: [industrial automation](../research/2026-09-07-industrial-automation-evidence.md), [AI engineering](../research/2026-09-07-ai-engineering-evidence.md), and [materials](../research/2026-09-07-material-circulation.md). They distinguish operating deployments, reported results, and research demonstrations from their broader implications. They support a concrete development program; they are not an exhaustive capability audit, independent inspection of all reported operations, or validation of autonomous industrial reproduction.

The [technical foundation](automation-to-shared-abundance.md) joins the evidence to the argument: capacity can contribute to further capacity; usable output can compound under specified conditions; common access can turn provision into freedom from compulsory labor; and shared tools let people build further generative systems. The [source reading](../research/2026-09-07-abundance-source-reading.md) establishes where Ren develops that thesis. Historical sources establish provenance, not present-day performance. The sufficiency of today's available AI for every required task remains an open evidence question rather than either an established fact or a reason to omit the engineering program.

## Separate kinds of statement

Every major dependency in a world should have both a **kind** and a **review status**. This lets us preserve the strength of a desired future without representing a preference, an assumption, and a measured result as interchangeable.

| Kind | What it means here | What would justify it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desirable | A value or condition the world aims to protect. | An explicit project commitment and attention to tensions with other commitments. Desirability does not establish feasibility. |
| Technically conceivable | A mechanism worth investigating under stated conditions. | A coherent account of its inputs, operation, constraints, and dependencies. It may still be unbuilt or fail in practice. |
| Engineering thesis | A claim that an objective can be pursued through an identified program of construction, integration, learning, and testing. | Working component evidence and a dependency account, with remaining research and engineering problems explicit. This status alone does not prove current all-task sufficiency. |
| Demonstrated within bounds | A result observed in a specified setting. | A dated primary source, task and environment definition, evaluation method, duration, and limitations. A demonstration supports only its observed scope. |
| Deployable at scale | A system can deliver a specified service repeatedly under the conditions of a proposed setting. | Evidence about reliability, recovery, maintenance, supply, cost, energy, staffing, local constraints, and integration. Component demonstrations alone are insufficient. |
| Forecast | A claim about what will happen, where, and on what timescale. | An explicit forecasting method, assumptions, uncertainty, and a reason for the scope. A scenario is not a forecast merely because it has a date. |
| Scenario assumption | A condition temporarily granted so that its consequences can be explored. | A clear statement, a list of dependent features, and an alternate branch where it is weaker or absent. No empirical proof is implied. |

Review statuses are `proposed`, `assumed-for-scenario`, `needs-evidence`, `supported-within-bounds`, `contested`, and `revised-or-retired`. These are working labels rather than a ladder of inevitable progress. A normative commitment may remain a commitment without becoming an empirical claim.

## First investigations

### 1. Trace one useful productive loop

Begin with a bounded service, such as making, deploying, and repairing accessible modular rooms. Draw a process map from incoming materials and energy to usable rooms, maintenance, waste, and replacement equipment. Mark every step as assumed automated, human operated, imported, or unknown. Include the equipment that makes the equipment.

Start from the actual demonstrated processes and learning methods in the industrial and AI evidence notes. Identify which can plausibly serve the chosen loop, then distinguish a transferable method from a locally validated operation. Design an initial repeatable cell and the next installation it could help equip. Record what additional output makes possible and which dependency still limits expansion.

The first artifact should be a dependency map and a table of missing evidence. It should expose mining and refining, specialized machine tools, fabs and electronics, chemicals and process consumables, metrology and inspection, batteries or other power systems, transportation, construction, and maintenance where they actually enter the selected process. We do not need to solve the global economy to identify the loop's unresolved interfaces.

Then seek dated primary technical evidence for the most consequential unknowns. Record operating conditions and failures alongside successes. Observable questions include output quality, useful operating time, maintenance demand, human intervention, yield, and whether added equipment increases net service capacity after replacing worn components.

This changes the fictional industrial model: a locally complete loop, a federation with specialized supply nodes, or partial automation supported by external production. It should not become a single percentage of “automation” that hides a critical missing component.

### 2. Make one neighborhood's material floor accountable

Choose an illustrative population and climate explicitly as design parameters. Build a rough service budget for food, housing, water, sanitation, care, energy, mobility, communication, repair, and discretionary construction. Record units, source quality, ranges, imports, storage, and unresolved inputs. Later estimates should be traceable to sources rather than presented as decorative precision.

Describe both ordinary operation and a disruption: a supplier fails, energy production drops, a machine fleet requires repair, or more residents arrive. Show who notices, what service changes, who decides, how residents appeal, and what continues automatically. Land and material limits should be visible alongside financial accounts.

The observable design test is whether a resident keeps an adequate life while refusing a community role or criticizing its administrators. A tabletop exercise can expose contradictions, but it cannot by itself prove institutional reliability. If the design needs compulsory contribution to sustain essentials, record that dependency and choose a partial branch or revise provision.

### 3. Prototype one portal as a physical experience

Start at room or small-building scale: one arrival space, two contrasting destinations, and an ordinary route through them. Produce a plan and a simple 3D blockout with dimensions labeled provisional. Show the building with and without its experience layer. Construction and safety claims require later qualified review in the relevant jurisdiction.

Test journeys for someone using a wheelchair, someone without a headset, a person sensitive to sensory overload, a newcomer who does not share the local language, and someone leaving during a power or network interruption. Define assistance requests without assuming everyone wants personalization. Separate chosen fiction from navigation and factual system information.

The next observable result is whether participants can orient, choose an experience, refuse it, and leave. Simulated journeys are an early design check; participant testing requires actual people and accessible arrangements. If the experience depends on obscuring physical reality or makes ordinary transit harder, change the experience before enlarging the building.

### 4. Compare different lives under the same material conditions

Write parallel ordinary-day scenes for a committed builder, a caregiver, a lifelong learner, a person seeking sensory expression, someone content to rest, a dissenter, and a newcomer. Use the same infrastructure in each scene. Ask what each can initiate, refuse, understand, and change, and whom they still depend on.

Add one shared disagreement, such as whether to transform a familiar courtyard. Follow the resulting conversation, delay, experiment, decision, and repair if the decision causes harm. Make room for fatigue, grief, jealousy, and affection without assuming either universal harmony or inevitable collapse.

This produces character studies and institutional questions, not behavioral predictions. Later research can look for bounded evidence concerning security, voluntary participation, education, commons institutions, and time use. No existing community should be treated as a complete empirical proxy for a fully automated civilization.

## Keep several technology branches available

| Branch | Granted material conditions | Creative consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: Extensive autonomy | The essential production and maintenance network can operate with very little necessary human work. Its dependencies and ecological costs still exist. | Explore voluntary pursuits, ownership, freedom to rest, plural communities, and ambitious construction subject to material and civic limits. |
| B: Partial autonomy | Routine services are largely automated; difficult manipulation, repair, care tasks, or exceptional conditions still require people. | Show how essential human activity is shared, compensated, or supported without claiming that no necessary work remains. Preserve freedom through institutional provision and honest limits. |
| C: Uneven deployment | Highly automated places coexist with less automated regions and depend on shared supply networks. | Explore reciprocity, migration, unequal access, external labor, and whether one settlement's comfort shifts burdens elsewhere. Avoid treating the beautiful enclave as the whole world. |
| D: Slower physical change | Intelligence improves faster than machinery, energy, materials, construction, or coordination. | Favor retrofits, durable places, modular expansion, and vivid cultural change within existing cities. Research and AR may progress while physical rebuilding remains deliberate. |

These are alternative settings and sensitivities, not a predicted sequence. Each world brief should name its branch or define its own, state what it grants, and identify which parts of its culture survive if a strong assumption is relaxed.

## Questions to retain beyond the first prototypes

- **Coordination and deployment:** Who can commit resources, authorize construction, verify a system, halt operation, and restore it? How do independent communities interoperate without requiring a single controller or technical dependency?
- **Ownership and allocation:** Can an entitlement survive a change of operator, institutional conflict, migration, or shortage? How are scarce sites, attention, ecological capacity, and discretionary projects allocated without converting prestige into authority over necessities?
- **Maintenance and ecology:** What wears out; what must be replaced; where do imports come from; and who lives with extraction, heat, waste, or water demand? Who can challenge a locally attractive project that harms a neighbor?
- **Human and cultural variation:** Which people experience new freedom as relief, disorientation, opportunity, or loss? How do childhood, disability, care, intimacy, disagreement, and the wish for an ordinary life alter the picture?
- **Artificial participants:** If a setting gives an AI a sustained point of view or a capacity to suffer, what obligations follow? Keep that fictional premise distinct from a claim that a current system is conscious.
- **Transition:** What minimum material and institutional foothold could preserve these principles before extensive automation arrives? Keep this as a connected inquiry while the main creative work explores life after the transition.

## Completion standard for a research pass

A useful pass resolves a bounded question, cites what it actually inspected, identifies remaining uncertainty, and changes an artifact: a dependency, an institution, a spatial decision, a character's day, or a scenario branch. It need not settle the future.

The next practical sequence is one production dependency map grounded in the checked cases, one repeatable-cell/next-installation account, one generous shared-service sketch, one portal or other world blockout, and contrasting ordinary-day scenes. These can develop together. Test the technical-to-social chain as a connected program: useful output, further capacity, dependable common access, less compelled human work, and new shared undertakings. Free association is one outcome to enable, not a substitute for these systems. Research supplies constraints and new possibilities; the worlds give research a reason to ask particular questions.
