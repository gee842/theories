# Demonstration status — 1 October 2026

The main page now contains an **end-to-end planned construction sequence** using the new carrier-oriented Blender kit: empty terrain, arriving fleet, foundations and first frame, repeated material transport and assembly, enclosure and departure. This is an authored planning/motion model, not a fully autonomous or physically verified construction run. The [controlling plan](IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md) defines the remaining visual, architectural and engineering acceptance criteria.

| Current work | Evidence and remaining boundary |
|---|---|

Persistent part missions, phase/scrub/follow controls, and departure states exist. The guided tour follows one complete beam delivery at a slower pace. Detailed meshes use visible-instance batches; the empty scene was inspected at one draw call/two terrain triangles. Interior traversal, receiving-tool reach, forces and service remain unqualified. |
| Carrier-oriented Blender kit | Editable building and four machine-role libraries share a geometry contract. The candidate is 18 × 12 m, twenty 3.2 m storeys, with 18 initial robots. Stairs now have actual shaft openings, landings and delivered headers; a ground entrance connects to the core. Dedicated half-beams retain full-size end hardware. Door leaves stay closed in transport and open during installation. These are geometric/design results, not qualified egress or weather performance. The proposed 350 kg payload capacity is not qualified. |
| Loaded carrier dynamics | A 92.928 kg beam rises 3.2 m with bounded actuators and ideal point grips. Separately, [finite jaws hold that load for five seconds](production/physics/carrier/FINITE-GRIP.md) without equality grips. A loaded contact-safe step, pickup, frame attachment and receiving cycle remain unproved. Rejected contact/controller candidates are retained; the all-upward-reaction criterion is being distinguished from actual bidirectional clamp retention. A column-mass ballast case fails capture; heavier catalogue handling remains unresolved. |

Connection capacity, local floors/landings, foundations and partial-stage stability remain unqualified. |
| Duration and regenerative lifecycle | The current slow-travel assumption yields roughly 100 planned days; it is not a construction forecast. Service, commissioning, full dismantling and recomposition remain incomplete. |

## Earlier workcell evidence

The earlier 36 × 24 m tower study is retained at `workcell.html`. Its active-floor trajectory is recorded from MuJoCo and its frame snapshots are exported from OpenSeesPy. It is a **partial physical placement cycle within an otherwise planned tower**. The table below describes that separate study and its supporting benches, not the current carrier kit. The procedural laboratory remains at `lab.html` for small mechanisms, custody and material accounting.

The next main deliverable is to bring the **continuous empty-site-to-completed-building scene** through the plan's visual and architectural acceptance review, while integrating loaded locomotion and supported transfers. Blender assets, site execution and dynamics develop together. The existing beam, clamp and pin experiments support those actions; matching two-ended connector geometry is no longer the main product milestone.

The mounted pin retains its narrow axial evidence. Finite grips, loaded travel, continuous ground-to-frame transitions, full reversible connections and concurrent structural stages still need engineering work within the shared site sequence. The old crane throughput arithmetic is not a forecast for the newly preferred climbing-carrier system.

Ground work remains schematic: the current pile-section placements do not yet model joined segments advancing together during driving, soil response or pile installation torque. A visible foundation task label does not establish that process. Complete deconstruction/recomposition and commissioning remain outstanding.

The wider sequence is documented in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md). No deployment or construction recommendation follows from these simulation results.
