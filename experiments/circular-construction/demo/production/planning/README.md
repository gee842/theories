# Throughput and logistics feasibility

Run from the demo directory:

```sh
python3 production/planning/study.py
```

The standard-library script executes only the authored `tower`, `joint` and `yard` geometry functions with primitive collectors. It does not need Blender, execute the build entrypoint, regenerate assets, or simulate physics. It hashes the source and reads canonical MuJoCo validation metrics. `throughput-study.json` is the machine-readable result. Embedded assertions check extraction consistency; they are not construction validation.

**The existing evidence does not establish a full building erected in days.** The 24 s MuJoCo episode starts with a 638.6 kg beam already near its seats; its validation explicitly says `completeFastenedCycle: false`. The separate 199.396 s climb test advances 3.6 m on ideal rail locks. Neither test includes ground pickup, crane travel and return, all fasteners, horizontal transfer, or a complete floor/building construction sequence.

The actual Blender asset depicts 700 column segments, 1159 installed horizontal beams and one lifted beam, 286 deck cassettes, 480 façade modules and 144 wall/lintel cassettes over 16 core storeys. It depicts a partial completion state: decks stop at floor 12, façade at 8 and core at 16. The 36 yard beams and 12 yard decks are inventory, not installed components.

A hypothetical completed 20-storey version extrapolates the same module pattern: 700 columns, 1160 horizontal beams, 320 diagonals (including temporary transverse braces), 480 decks, 1200 façade modules, 180 core wall/lintel cassettes and 80 assumed assembled stair flights/landings. This is **4120 individual crane lifts**. Stair modularization and lifting every brace individually are planning assumptions; bundling may lower hook counts but adds handling and distribution work. The new `ArchitecturalFinish` hierarchy—pale fins, terrace assemblies, soffits, planters and landscaped forecourt—is visual design geometry **outside this lift and mass catalogue**. Its material schedule, module boundaries and attachment loads must be engineered and added before using the result as a whole-building estimate. Roof, MEP, fire protection and fit-out also add scope.

For a single crane working 10 h/day at 65% productive utilization, assumed complete lift cycles of 180–300 s imply **31.7–52.8 days** for these lifts alone. These cycle assumptions are sensitivity cases, not measured crane performance. A 7-day target requires 39.8 s average per lift and at least 5–8 independent cranes at 180–300 s; a 3-day target requires 17.0 s and 11–18 cranes. Such crane counts are arithmetic lower bounds; the illustrated site has one crane and its shared airspace and installation sequence may make those parallel rates impossible. Even assigning the local 24 s beam episode to every catalogue lift gives 4.23 workdays, but that calculation is physically incomplete and is included only to expose why replay timing cannot support a construction schedule.

Steel profile-only payloads derived from dimensions and assumed 7850 kg/m³ density are 616–711 kg per typical horizontal beam and 776–921 kg per column segment. The active demo beam has a different section and an explicitly simulated 638.6 kg mass. Main core wall panels contain **7.95–10.21 tonnes of concrete alone** at assumed 2400 kg/m³, before frames and inserts. The deck's solid 5.89×5.89×0.09 m geometry would weigh 7.49 tonnes at that density, but its `Deck` material has no engineered layup or density: treat this as a conditional proxy, not a specified deck weight. Crane capacity must be checked at operating radius for these payloads; no load chart is present.

The illustrated crane center is (24,−12) m and the long jib extends 45 m from it. The furthest tower corner needs a 48.37 m radius and the furthest staged beam endpoint about 53.46 m. Assuming slewing does not resolve the reach deficit; crane location/jib/yard layout needs revision, followed by a capacity check. The existing local hoist model is not a complete tower-crane reach or travel model.

One robot pair climbing once per storey and serving its 58 horizontal beams consumes at least 1591.4 s/storey in the local arithmetic model, excluding lateral transfer. Twenty-storey ascent alone extrapolates to 66.5 min; only one storey was tested. If every beam task required a fresh 199.4 s climb while maintaining a 24 s crane takt, at least ten pairs (20 robots) would be required to hide climb time. Batching by floor reduces climb overhead; it does not establish access to every beam end. The JSON also records robot labor lower bounds for calendar targets, but those cover beam-end local episodes only and are unsuitable as total fleet specifications.

A defensible quick-build claim needs a revised crane/yard plan and load chart, validated whole-hook cycles, an engineered module bill of materials, rails and transfer paths, complete reversible connector fastening, precedence and airspace scheduling, delivery/fabrication rates and fault/weather allowances. The current work supports local mechanisms and a quantitative feasibility screen, not demonstrated autonomous full construction.
