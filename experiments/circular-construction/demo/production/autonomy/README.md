# Empty-site autonomous construction process

Revised 1 October 2026. The [implementation plan](../../IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md) sets the main deliverable: empty flat land, arriving robots and cargo, ground preparation and foundations, the first stable frame, a swarm of loaded climbing carriers and assemblers, the complete declared building, and all construction machines departing. This specification describes the work needed to show that entire sequence. It remains **specified, not implemented or simulated end to end**.

Climbing material carriers are the default vertical supply method. Ground manipulators build the first stable bay before any machine can climb it. A lift, hoist or crane is optional: if selected, its own delivery, support, robotic assembly, commissioning, extension, dismantling and departure must be visible tasks. No installed yard, rails, foundation, dock or lifting plant exists at time zero.

Robots arrive assembled and charged with secured materials and tools. Their onboard or mobile handling equipment unloads the first supplies; deployed service equipment follows before bootstrap reserves run out. Qualified factory supply is the upstream boundary. A driver, rigger, installer or recovery technician cannot be an unstated site prerequisite. Proposed machine roles describe responsibilities, not validated hardware or available products.

## Material transport and custody

The repeated carrier cycle is **pickup → secure → approach → attach → loaded climb/traverse → supported handoff → install and verify → release → return**. Every trip keeps the same robot and component identities. Incoming material, waiting, route conflicts, payload restraints, passing positions, energy and empty returns belong to the process. Floor, roof, facade and service modules each need an explicit handling method; their appearance in a final architectural model is not an installation event.

Each asset has a custody record naming its transport restraints, gripper, cradle, temporary support or accepted structural connection. Sending and receiving machines overlap support during transfer. The carrier remains supporting through A13 placement, A14 fastening and A15 structural-stage acceptance, and releases only in A29. Reaching a pose or expiring a timer is insufficient. First-frame deliveries use ground routes; a climbing route cannot be required to build its own first support.

A physical custody record includes asset identity, configuration revision, participating machines, retained supports, measured forces and motion, acceptance thresholds, evidence time and transfer outcome. Thresholds belong to the engineered member, machine and joint. Existing local prototype values are not universal field limits. Planning runs can record assumed support, but must distinguish it from physically verified transfer.

On failure, restraints preserve support within the declared operating envelope. A stopped motor alone is not a demonstrated holding mechanism. Recovery establishes backup support before disturbing a failed robot or part. If a fault cannot be recovered autonomously, the run remains in a supported abort and is not a completed autonomous trial.

## Sequence, repetition and completion

The initial chain is arrival and registration (A02–A03), self-unloading (A08), ground survey/deployment (A04/A06), foundations (A05), service and storage (A07/A09), ground delivery (A10), and the first stable frame (A11). Ground preparation and independent deployment may overlap when their work zones permit it. Optional lifting equipment must not introduce a circular bootstrap dependency.

Subsequent construction repeats A10 pickup, A12 loaded travel, A13 supported placement, A14 fastening, A15 stage acceptance and A29 release/return. A12 also deploys and repositions the construction robots: receivers must visibly climb into their supported work positions before a carrier arrives. A11 supplies progressive climbing interfaces before use. Floors/core/roof (A16), enclosure/terraces (A17) and services/interiors (A18) instantiate their own delivery and connection cycles. These aggregate task families do not authorize whole floors to appear at once. Several eligible instances run concurrently with route reservations and combined structural loads; service A20 and recovery readiness A21 remain available throughout.

Construction completion covers A02–A22 and A29, with A01 qualified supply upstream. It requires the declared building inventory and inspections, temporary-work recovery, final access closure and site finish, and departure of the last machine. A19 inspections apply to installed stages; A22 includes inspection of final access closures, changed support states and ground finishes before final acceptance and the last machine’s departure. The plan must preserve access until the last required installation, inspection and descent. A failed-recovery scenario may abort; a nominal run need not invent a failure merely to exercise the A21 task family.

Later deconstruction is a distinct mission (A23–A28): a recovery fleet arrives, inspects and supports the building, isolates systems, removes components, sorts and qualifies them for reuse, records repair/reprocessing losses, and recovers plant and foundations with declared site residuals. The kit must support this from the outset, while the first main implementation milestone remains the complete construction demonstration.

The type-level dependency graph expresses precedence, not task duration, resource availability or a proven schedule. Instance-level dependencies must include previous-stage acceptance, exact component identity, route occupancy, temporary supports and module-specific prerequisites. Connecting the existing climb trace at arbitrary heights or replaying a beam action under different loads is not validated execution.

## Development and evidence

Build the continuous site sequence, Blender assets, loaded carrier/handoff mechanics and Three.js experience in parallel. An early full planning run may use explicitly provisional motion and support assumptions; it must still show coherent inventory, assembly order, paths, concurrent robots and departure. Physical evidence then attaches to those same actions. The complete scene must not be postponed behind a series of disconnected joint benchmarks.

Engineering acceptance requires appropriate nominal and failure cases for the actual mechanisms: capture, support transfer, collision clearance, actuator limits, staged structural loads, sensing, power and communications. Limits must be tied to the selected payload, geometry and hardware. Whole-process physical verification additionally requires bootstrap, scheduling, service reserves and final egress. Passing the task graph validator establishes none of these.

Existing evidence is narrowly credited: [beam workcell](../physics/mujoco/README.md), [unloaded climb prototype](../physics/climb-prototype/README.md), [mounted pin experiment](../physics/integrated-fastener/README.md), [connector experiment](../physics/connector-prototype/README.md), [structural snapshots](../structure/opensees/README.md), [earlier crane throughput arithmetic](../planning/README.md) and [material ledger](../../src/material/README.md). The unloaded climb does not establish loaded carrying capacity. Earlier hoist seating does not demonstrate climbing delivery. The one-crane schedule is historical evidence for a different logistics architecture.
