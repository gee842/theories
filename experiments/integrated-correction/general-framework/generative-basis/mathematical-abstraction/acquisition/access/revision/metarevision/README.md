## The logical retention comparison is separate

The four histories are00,01,10,11. Each of16 encoders maps them into one binary memory cell. Each of two future questions has one of four decoders from that cell to a bit. All256 encoder/decoder designs are checked.

No design answers both coordinate questions exactly for every history. The maximum total is six correct answers among eight history/question pairs. Preserving the first coordinate is exact for the first question and loses distinctions needed by the second; preserving the second reverses that advantage. Any other exact joint encoding would require four distinct retained states, not two. The counts assign neither a future-query probability nor a causal resource reward.

This proves no universal exact solution on this finite domain under the complete one-bit information bound. It does not exclude useful average-case, minimax, competitive or constrained-domain policies. All accessible historical clues must count toward the bound; resource balances or clocks cannot carry extra uncounted bits.

The14 six-reading signatures of the preceding restricted behavioral class are also pairwise distinct; only12 distinct five-reading signatures occur. This preserves that positive restricted-class result alongside the general delayed-disagreement argument in the proof note. A finite program may describe an infinite class, but finite observations do not determine a unique unrestricted continuation.
