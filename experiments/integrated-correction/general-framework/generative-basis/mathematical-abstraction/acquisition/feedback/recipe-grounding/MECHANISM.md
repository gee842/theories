## What is constructed

The earlier reader supplied a reliable physical-bit-to-acquired-bit mapping.
Here the raw transducer is an unknown unary Boolean map `f`, and both decoder
rows initially lack connections. Two actual reference commands impose jig
settings 0 and 1. The same transducer exposes a raw result at each setting;
the issued command and contact binding qualify the acquired reference record.
The constructor binds row `g[raw]` to that acquired record's issued setting.
It preserves a contrary previous binding and marks conflict instead of
overwriting it. Neither the constructor nor the later selector reads `f`.

If `f(0) ≠ f(1)`, the two acquired constraints occupy different rows, and

\[
g(f(0))=0,\qquad g(f(1))=1.
\]

Because the addressed domain is exactly Boolean, these equations establish
`g(f(x))=x` for every addressed input. If the raw results coincide, the two
constraints demand different outputs from one row; no deterministic decoder
on that raw alphabet can satisfy both. Identity and inversion therefore work
without privileging raw polarity, while constants fail. The four possible
complete decoder tables follow from independently assigning the two output
rows. This is completeness of the declared grammar, not a claim that four
recipes exhaust physical sensing architectures. The fixed bound-reference
schedule itself reaches inverse or partial/conflicted wiring, not all four
complete tables. The pure formal helper overwrites a colliding temporary row
and rejects duplicate replies; the native binder instead preserves the first
row and records conflict. Their accepted decoder result agrees, not every
invalid intermediate wire state.

The native constructor applies each encountered constraint directly; it does
not enumerate completed recipes, receive an accuracy reward, or draw a
successful decoder label. This is evidence-constrained wiring formation and
selection, not population selection or a proof of superior reproduction.
