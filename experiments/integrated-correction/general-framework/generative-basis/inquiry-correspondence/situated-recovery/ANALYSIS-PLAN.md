# Existing human recovery data: analysis decisions

8 October 2026. Written after reading the published qualitative findings, dataset README and author Fig. 2 extraction code, but before opening participant outcome arrays. This is an exploratory secondary analysis, not a prospective preregistration or independent experimental replication.

Reuse: the completed situated prediction/revision account and its six-study evidence synthesis. Delta: bind participant-level physical observations to a specific question about retained response and its finite continuation. A scalar sufficient observer construction is not assumed to be a full psychological model.

Data: all eight spontaneous-recovery and all eight evoked-recovery participants from Heald, Lengyel and Wolpert (2021), Dryad DOI 10.5061/dryad.m63xsj42r, also deposited at Zenodo record 5516490. Obtain original bytes and compare against deposited digests. No selection by performance. The memory-updating experiment is a separate 24-person study and is not part of this analysis.

Extraction: follow the author code: normalize adaptation by the sign of the first FieldA trial's first FieldConstants component; retain recorded finite Adaptation values. Reconcile missed trials with the author code and README before interpreting any mismatch. Expected recorded channel observations: 167 per spontaneous participant and 165 per evoked participant. The first 17 are at trials 5:10:105 and 120:10:170; subsequent spontaneous observations are trials 191:340, evoked observations 191:192 and 195:340. Verify this schedule against TrialData.

Primary descriptive outcomes: participant mean over final 50 channel trials (291:340), with and without subtracting each participant's five baseline channel measurements (5:10:45). Report both groups and all participants. Report participant-resampling 95% bootstrap intervals for group means and their difference (20,000 draws, seed 20261008); do not treat trials as independent participants. An exact two-sided allocation permutation comparison (8 of 16; absolute mean difference) may be reported as a group association under exchangeability, not proof of randomized causal identification. No multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory claim is planned.

Secondary outcomes: mean of first 10 uninterrupted terminal channel trials (191:200 spontaneous, 195:204 evoked), final 50 minus early 10, and raw versus baseline-corrected conclusions. Show individual heterogeneity, including weak or opposite responses. Do not interpret failure to reach zero during 150 trials as a theorem about an infinite-time limit.

Exploratory forecast comparison: within each person's uninterrupted terminal channel segment, fit on all but the last 50 measurements and predict those 50 without updating from held-out outcomes. Compare zero-target exponential decay, exponential relaxation to a fitted level, constant calibration mean and last-calibration-observation persistence. Fit squared loss with q in [0,1], using deterministic bounded optimization and explicit endpoint candidates. Models are descriptive curves for expressed adaptation, not direct readings of a hidden memory or a refit of the published COIN model. Report participant RMSE and aggregate paired differences; no fitted curve is automatically the true mechanism. Near-unit decay can imitate a nonzero finite plateau, which limits identification.

Revisions: a failed schedule, unsupported measurement mapping or inconsistent bytes stops interpretation pending correction. A simple forecast matching or beating the richer one is retained. Published COIN findings are credited; this analysis does not claim novelty for contextual inference. No new theorem or proof rerun is needed for empirical estimates; the current Lean results retain their hypotheses.

## Extraction clarification before group analysis

The first file's actual pre-terminal channel trial numbers differ from the nominal x-coordinates in the author's plotting script (for example, the second channel observation is trial 19, not 15). Retain the author's observation order and sign normalization, but use actual TrialNumber and BlockName for all temporal interpretation. Baseline consists of the five finite Adaptation observations in the Null block, not an exact 5:10:45 time mask. The fixed terminal segments and final 50 remain unchanged if verified. This is a recorded correction to the schedule assumption, not removal of an adverse outcome.

The table is stored as a MATLAB MCOS object. A dataset-specific reader must reconcile common fields across the serialized table copies, validate trial numbers, field types and lengths, and preserve the originals. Supplementary force-based reconstruction will compare the README's slope rule to provided Adaptation. Any discrepancy is reported, not silently calibrated away; the author-provided values remain the primary outcome used by the published plotting code.
