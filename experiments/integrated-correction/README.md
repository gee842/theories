# Selection and the reproduction of correction

5 October 2026. A bounded mathematical extension of the situated alignment and coordination programme.

**Scope clarification:** the [general framework](general-framework/README.md) concerns the coupled development of practices, participants, relations, and the conditions of their continuation and revision. The financial studies below are one specialization. Their fee mechanisms, payoff-based selection, and paid correction services do not define the broader theory.

**Claim tested by this specialization:** selection can sustain or undermine the capacities required for useful correction. A corrective practice's immediate usefulness, its ability to pay for continued operation, and its ability to persist under the prevailing selection process are different properties. This project establishes an explicit model in which they come together, and conditions under which they come apart.

Three specialist agents developed the mathematics, audited the integration, and challenged the proof and prior-art claims. The coordinating agent implemented and verified the model. These are AI research and review roles, not independent human peer review.

The model joins paid evidence and execution capacity, actual output, funding, and selection of a funding practice. It proves an exact financing window, coexisting persistence and depletion regimes, and a finite capacity-only initiation under stated premises. It does not establish the political survival of the collection/access rules, empirical validity, originality, or a general solution to AI alignment.

The result supplements, and does not supersede, the [situated correction framework](../evaluator-succession/SITUATED-CORRECTION.md), [renewal research](../../_process/research/2026-10-04-integrated-evolution/README.md), or the [v7.3 manuscript](../../drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.3/README.md). Existing experiments and manuscripts were not altered for this work.

The [constitutional extension](constitutional-extension/README.md) addresses part of the remaining gap. It introduces paid access verification, custodians who can claim payment without maintaining it, and conditional payment that changes their returns. Both practices and both operating stocks are endogenous. The extension proves joint participation and maintenance conditions, recovery and loss regions, and why finite infrastructure alone cannot repair some failures. Settlement, evidence and assignment primitives, and the limited strategy repertoire, remain explicit.

The [systems analysis](systems-dynamics/README.md) maps a basin slice, identifies mixed saddle states and local stability boundaries, and tests a feedback from verification shortfall to rule revision. It separates these checked dynamics from the proposed broader framework of situated, plural and changing goals. A feasible, locally stable target can be reached by one rule-response speed and missed by another through an amplifying loop across levels.

The [bounded rule-revision study](viability-control/README.md) constructs a controller that preserves separate operating conditions while its target changes. It proves a sufficient invariant region, compares eight rules under the same actuator limit, and tests whether apparent performance survives the reintroduction of a rare alternative. The result is conditional on a specified model, declared constraints and available governance; it does not establish a general theory of goal formation or empirical alignment.

The [paid-governance extension](governance-closure/README.md) brings observation and execution costs into the same fee ledger, models maintained sensing and actuation, and tolerates arbitrary minority reports and commands within an authenticated interface. Its preservation theorem and failure controls distinguish selected service delivery from neutral truthful composition. Initial majority, calibration, settlement and command-path assumptions remain explicit; unrestricted capture is not solved.

To reproduce the numerical audit, run `verify_model.py` with Python, NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib. The exact versions, source hash, parameter comparisons and trajectories are saved in `results/`. This is an illustrative mathematical audit with no empirical fitting.
