Learning rules, research and assumptions

Direct behavior: q′ = (c q + y)/(c + 1), c′ = c + 1, with y = 1 for constructive behavior and 0 for defense. Fresh favorable explanation: q′ = (c q + r)/(c + r), with confidence and credibility unchanged. Externally verified reports change credibility separately, halfway toward reliable or misleading under an illustrative rule.

Chang et al. (2010) motivates prior-plus-experience trust learning; Siegel et al. (2018) distinguishes impressions and uncertainty; Schweitzer et al. (2006) studies how deception affects trust recovery. None calibrates these fictional people or proves these particular equations.

No forgetting, inferred volatility, severity weighting, context transfer, or dependence correction is included. “Fresh” and “verified” are supplied interpretations. Growing evidence weight can be inappropriate when a partner changes. Multiple distinct explanations can accumulate influence. No inference of motives, safety guarantee, maturity score, or obligation to remain engaged follows.

Repair can take another try

This example starts with two people whose different histories shape the same feedback exchange: Alex expects attack, while Maya expects dismissal. Under explicitly chosen rules, supporting experiences gradually build evidence for a different response, while defensive exchanges can set that learning back. Explore the same episode at six levels: Raw Lean shows the actual definitions and proofs; Abstraction shows why retained histories and their smaller evidence-tally model agree; Two people shows the exchanges, thresholds, lapses, and retries; Evidence & repair separates a confirmed task error from an unsupported personal verdict; Team sketches shared repair practices; and Society sketches institutional memory and wider consequences. The conclusion is conditional: an early lapse can restart the defensive loop, another clarifying attempt can reopen repair, and accumulated experience can help an interaction recover from a later lapse. These are checked consequences of supplied toy rules, not validated psychological laws or a guarantee that clarification is safe or effective; the team and society levels remain proposals.
