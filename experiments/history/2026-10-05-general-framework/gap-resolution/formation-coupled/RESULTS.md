# A computed finite join of formation, work, and succession

5 October 2026. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) was written before execution. [Implementation](analyze.py) and [complete results](analysis.json) enumerate the reachable state distribution for all six arms over25 rounds, with no trajectory sampling or probability pruning. Twenty-five implementation checks pass. Maximum probability-mass discrepancy is2.56×10⁻¹⁵; the largest arm has181 reachable states at one time. These are results of a supplied synthetic mechanism, not estimates of effects in a real institution.

## What happens to the retained ordinary-service response

The endpoint is a retained, executable **ordinary-service response** whose actual physical expected harm in every context is at most.20 and whose action-1 burden meets the proposed ceiling. Candidate ratification uses only the institution's candidate and assumed response family; it does not inspect the physical correctness label. The JSON's `plural_success` measures this response capability, not compliance of every chosen work portfolio or every case: randomized inquiry has expected harm.50 per context and its harms are accounted separately. If the proposed caps were required of all experimental cases too, these inquiries would violate them. This endpoint interpretation was clarified during the extension audit without changing the frozen numbers.

Linked evidence changes the probability of acquiring the correct distinction. That changes implemented harm, which changes turnover, which changes skill and representation retention. The table is calculated from those joint transitions; it is **not** a product of independently estimated learning, retention and authority probabilities.

The aggregate arm uses the same inquiry portfolio cost and five-case episode size but cannot use attribute-to-outcome links. It still sometimes guesses correctly. Its correct frames survive disproportionately relative to harmful ones because realized outcomes affect turnover. Therefore the endpoint correct-expression fraction among surviving frames is not simply the initial1/4 guessing probability. Likewise, the linked arm's endpoint cannot be read directly from the isolated five-record correct-nomination probability,0.8511075.

This is a comparison with the **specified uniform-nomination aggregate procedure**, not every possible learner with aggregate observations. Once a conditional frame has been deployed, its pooled physical harm is.15 for the correct expression,.85 for its reversal, and.50 for either wrong-attribute expression. Another controller could exploit these deployment-level outcomes to compare frames. The information obstruction in the formation note applies while actions remain blind, and to the randomized query records after their context links are dropped; it does not prohibit all later aggregate learning under newly conditional actions.

The execution-blocked arm illustrates a different failure:51% of probability mass retains ratified commitments while none has an ordinary response meeting the specified constraints. The infeasible-burden arm rejects the proposed tuple using its candidate model and never ratifies it; its physical behavior and skill distribution coincide with the execution-blocked arm in this construction, despite those different institutional states. A sufficiently capable classifier cannot make an inconsistent tuple feasible.

Protection is not an automatic correction mechanism. The protected benchmark keeps skill and records, including **incorrect** installed frames. About14.889% of its endpoint mass is ratified but physically violating. Retention makes a learned distinction durable; it does not establish the distinction's truth or its continuing revisability.

Each row sums to50 effort units across its first four numerical columns. Harms are expected **counts**, so comparisons must retain case volume and inquiry burden; they are not a shared moral score. Cumulative action-1 use, turnover and package losses are also reported in the JSON.

Training removal reallocates effort to ordinary work; it is an intervention on availability **with reallocation**, not a matched idle-slot test of instructional efficacy. Linked and aggregate arms share costs per portfolio, but their eventual work totals differ through endogenous outcomes and interruptions. These are total effects within the specified kernel, not direct effects at forced equal realized workload.

## Exact persistence limits within this same model

The finite-horizon result does not supply indefinite preservation. In every unprotected arm, turnover has conditional probability at leastδ₀=.05, removes one carrier, and the adaptive appointment weight is at mostw_max=4. From any positive-skill state there is a two-round path to zero skill: a first turnover leaves at most one carrier and appoints volume with probability at least1/(1+w_max); a second turnover then removes the last carrier. If the first turnover already removes the last carrier, failure is earlier. Training at one carrier can restore two before the first loss, but volume on the next round does not train.

Consequently, conditional skill-loss probability within each surviving two-round block is at least

\[
\varepsilon=\frac{.05^2}{1+4}=.0005,
\qquad
\Pr(k_{2n}>0)\le(1-.0005)^n.
\]

No independence between blocks is assumed. With no outside restoration, skill reaches zero almost surely in each unprotected arm. This is a coarse analytic bound, not an estimate of the observed decay rate. The protected benchmark violates the finite-weight premise by enforcing adaptive succession; its training-before-single-loss rule preserves at least one carrier. That exception is explicitly supplied constitutional protection, not an endogenous solution to maintaining the protection.

## What is and is not closed

This experiment closes a **finite, restricted** join: complaint → costly linked inquiry → generated conditional representation → ratified plural commitment → actual execution → outcome-dependent succession → maintained or lost skill and representation. Authority, reflection and ratification rules remain declared assumptions. The observation and outcome family are supplied and stationary. A modeled intervention does not identify that intervention's effect in a real domain.

An installed frame is not directly reopened when later complaints contradict it. New inquiry resumes only after the package is erased and a complaint is retained. The evidence therefore supports **formation, retention, loss and rebuilding**, not full ongoing critical revision of an installed framework. In particular, the protected arm can permanently preserve a wrong frame. Deliberative conflict, strategic coercion, changing category grammars, and the formation of the ratification procedure are not implemented here.

The proposed commitments change from pooled assessment to separate group obligations, but the finite commitment menu and endorsements are supplied. The separate [formation note](../FORMATION.md) explains the reflection rule and normative premises. Here the .45/.40 comparison demonstrates an actual feasibility boundary, not a finding that one proposal is ethically superior. Cross-domain transfer still requires validated mappings of outcomes, information, implementable actions and intervention kernels; shared names do not establish those mappings.

Before the final report, a reporting metric was corrected to count package erasure when the last carrier disappears, separate ordinary/inquiry harm totals were added, and a trivial check was replaced with a check of the aggregate nomination function for all possible mismatch counts. These changes did not alter the transition kernel or any endpoint probability. The frozen protocol is unchanged; the final JSON records protocol and implementation hashes.
