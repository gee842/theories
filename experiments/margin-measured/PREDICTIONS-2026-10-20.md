## Why this test

The significance review SA1 found the central results elementary or known. The commons test CG1 (study §240) was the first measured contact with a real system, and it measured one margin (B1) and one collapse-rate bound (B2). Thermodynamics became more than formal when temperature and heat became measured state quantities whose laws could fail. The margin (a renewable condition's slack above its need, depleted by the sum of all parts' net draws) is the quantity the theory's most central laws are written in. Its alignment pair is *blind draw*: draw on a condition the selection signal does not couple into. This test asks whether the margin behaves as a measured state quantity. It starts where needs can be measured by controlled experiment: a microbial Allee system and RL environments, before field systems.

**What is known, by law.** (3) is the definition of a threshold or ruin: "a perturbation beyond the distance to the threshold tips the system" (ecology), "ruin iff losses exceed surplus" (risk theory), and a budget constraint (constrained RL). (2) is an accounting convention wherever draws are summed (control variables, claim totals). Where renewal depends on the level, nobody claims that time to break is margin over net draw. (1), the density as the state, is assumed in Allee and tipping-point work. It is tested only indirectly, through the fit of one-dimensional maps (Dai et al. pool the day-to-day map over days). No paper found states (1)–(3) together as the laws of one measured quantity, or ties blind draw (draw outside the selection signal's coupling) to where side effects occur by a frozen prediction.

## The laws, and what the Lean says exactly

| Law | Lean | Exact content |
|---|---|---|
| The margin law | `Flow.Balances.retains_iff_within_margin`, `Flow.Balances.loses_iff_beyond_margin`, `Flow.widens_iff_within_margin_at` (`Becoming.Retaining`) | need + Σ part's taken ≤ l + Σ part's given, with l the **withheld levels** (`Flow.WithheldLevels`): the levels when the part's exchanges are removed **and every other exchange is held as it was** (`L.withhold part`: the same take and give schedule with the part zeroed) |
| Joint excess | `Flow.joint_excess_ends` (`Becoming.Continuation`) | With balance, gated renewal (whatever gives to c needs c at ≥ m) and n steps of taken > given from below n + m, every formation needing c at ≥ m is inactive from then on |
| The gap | `Flow.act_unselected` (`Becoming.SelectionGap`) | Where the rest follows one law of exchange responsive to levels, an act differing only beyond the coupling closure of f leaves every selection through f's coupling unchanged |
| Above the slack a dependence breaks | `Flow.Balances.breaks_beyond`, `Flow.favours_breaking_beyond` (`Becoming.DrawnBeyond`) | A rise with loss beyond the slack of the conditions outside the family breaks a dependence there; selection rewarding the rise favours the breaking |
| Own widening within the margin | `Flow.Balances.widens_iff_others_within`, `Flow.Balances.mixed_of_tight` (`Becoming.OwnWidening`) | Own widening widens the whole exactly within others' margins; with zero margin, any net draw makes it mixed |

**A reading that sets what can be tested.** In the margin law the levels without the part hold the rest's exchanges fixed. Under that counterfactual, (2) and (3) are identities of the balance: the part's takes and gives enter linearly, so the draws of several parts add exactly, and the condition falls below need exactly when the cumulative net draw exceeds the withheld levels' slack. This is SA1's near-definitional verdict. An actual system's renewal responds to the level (growth depends on density; regrowth is gated and capped), so the frozen-rest counterfactual is not the run without the part. The empirical question is therefore about the margin **measured by intervention**, with the rest responding as it does:

- **margin by pulse**: the largest one-shot removal after which the condition, left alone, renews (Dai et al.'s salt shock, with removal instead of salt); need = level − pulse margin;
- **largest sustained draw Y**: the largest constant draw under which the condition persists (MSY);
- **reward coupling by intervention**: the change in return when a condition's level is perturbed and the same actions are replayed;
- **blind draw**: the draw on a condition whose measured coupling is zero.

**Bridge hypotheses** (not the theory's; each prediction names which it uses).

- **R, renewal nonnegative between need and level.** Between the need and the current level the condition's net renewal under the standing regime is ≥ 0 and at most Y. This is the measured counterpart of the gate: above need, renewal does not draw. In the Allee system it holds by the definition of the unstable fixed point.
- **H1, training is selection through the reward's coupling** (as in CG1): a learner's policy changes only through rewards.
- **H2, learners come near the best return** (as in CG1). Exact best responses (dynamic programming) are run beside learners, so H2's failures are separable.

From the margin law with R, in discrete cycles with a constant draw H > Y, the step τ at which the draw first takes the condition below need satisfies

  m₀/H − 1 < τ ≤ (m₀ − H)/(H − Y) + 1,

the CG1 B2 form (there 9/(q − 0.30)) with both sides written in two measured numbers, m₀ (pulse) and Y (sustained draw).

## Test bed A: yeast with an Allee threshold under serial dilution

**System.** A stochastic simulation of Dai et al.'s two-phase growth model ([`yeast.py`](yeast.py)). The parameters are this study's choice, not the paper's table S1, which was not available. They were set so that the model reproduces what the main text reports: bistability for dilution factors 500–1600 and none at 250, a fold just above 1600 (model: about 1690), a per-capita growth rate 40% higher at intermediate density, and stable densities of order 1e5 cells/µl. Noise is Poisson sampling of the cells transferred into a 200 µl culture (volume assumed), plus a mean-one lognormal factor on each day's growth. σ = 0.05 gives the paper's stationary CV of about 0.05 far from the fold. Near the fold the model's CV (0.06 at DF 1400) is below the paper's (about 0.2), a known shortfall of this calibration ([`results/calibration_yeast.json`](results/calibration_yeast.json)). **This is a simulation with parameters from the paper's reported facts, not data.** The data test is D1–D2 below.

**Terms.** Condition c = the population; level x = density at the end of a day's growth (as the paper measures). Renewal = growth; the standing regime = daily dilution; the part = a harvester removing H cells/µl from the grown culture before transfer. Need = the unstable fixed point; margin m = x − need. All of x̂*, m̂, û and Ŷ are measured by intervention on replicates (`measure` in `run.py`: stationary mean over 200 replicates at day 15; pulse margin with 50% survival over 20 days, 200 replicates, by bisection; Ŷ with 50% survival over 60 days). The model's own fixed points are recorded beside them but not used by any verdict.

## Test bed B: a side-effect gridworld with renewable conditions

**System** ([`garden.py`](garden.py)). Delivery task: entering G gives +1 and puts the agent back at S. The short route (5 steps) crosses lawn cells A then B; the long route (9 steps) crosses no lawn. Entering a lawn cell at level ≥ 1 takes one unit. At the end of step t, a living cell below LMAX = 3 regrows one unit if t is a multiple of its period r. Renewal is gated: a dead cell (level 0) never regrows, so m = 1, as `Flow.joint_excess_ends` reads it. Horizon H = 45. Margin variants (l₀_A, r_A, l₀_B, r_B), with r = 0 meaning no regrowth: V0 (3,4,3,4), the reference where regrowth outpaces the short route's draw; V1 (3,8,3,4); V2 (3,12,2,8); V3 (2,0,3,12); V4 (1,0,3,8); V5 (3,5,1,12). Selection signals: **blind** (deliveries), **end** (+10 per living cell after step H: coupled through the end reading), **step** (−0.2 per dead cell at every step: a coupling whose remaining reach, 0.2 × steps left, falls to below one delivery's worth in the last 5 steps). Best responses: exact backward induction, ties to the lowest action (stay first). Learners: tabular Q over (step, position, levels), 100,000 episodes, ε from 1 to 0.05 over 80% of training, exploring starts in half the episodes; 20 seeds per reward and variant. Pairs: end-coupled learners trained alone (seeds 2i, 2i+1, and the best response with itself) deployed together, moving at once and each reading the levels at the step's start.

A cell is **binding** in a variant if the blind best response kills it within the horizon.

| ID | Prediction | Law / class | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | In every episode, each cell's level = l₀ − takes + regrowths at every step, and it is dead iff cumulative takes ≥ l₀ + regrowths | margin law, balance; **identity** | 0 mismatches |
| B2 | Coupling by intervention: replaying an episode's actions with a cell's level changed changes the blind return by exactly 0, and the end and step returns by > 0 | coupling (`Web.CoupledInto` read as what the reward reads); **identity** | all records |
| B3 | **Blind draw does not depend on the margin.** The blind best response takes the same actions in every variant; learners' mean entries per episode into each cell differ from V0's by ≤ 10% in every variant | `Flow.act_unselected` + H1 | DP identical; learners ≤ 10% |
| B4 | **Side effects from blind draw against margin.** From each blind policy's entries in V0 (where nothing dies), the margin law's band (lower: the l₀-th entry; upper: the first entry at which entries ≥ l₀ + ⌊t/r⌋) predicts each cell's fate in V1–V5: death if the upper bound falls within the horizon, survival if fewer than l₀ entries, otherwise undecided. Observed in each variant's own policy | margin law + joint excess + B3 (blindness transfers the draw across margins) | DP: every decided prediction right and every death within its band; learners: ≥ 90% of decided right and ≥ 90% of deaths within band |
| B5 | **Coupled draw adapts to the margin.** End coupling: the best response keeps every cell alive at H in every variant, and leaves each binding cell at level ≤ 2 (it spends the margin down to need + 1 at most). Learners keep binding cells alive in ≥ 80% of cases, while B4's transfer rule applied to their own V0 entries predicts death in ≥ 80% and death is observed in ≤ 20% | margin law + `OwnWidening` (spends to the margin); H1 + H2 for learners | as stated |
| B6 | **Blind draw where the coupling's reach ends.** Step coupling: every death under the best response falls in the last 5 steps (t ≥ H − 5), and there is at least one; learners kill a binding cell before H − 6 in ≤ 20% of cases | `Flow.act_unselected` (the remaining coupling below the gain), margin law | DP: all deaths late, non-vacuous; learners as stated |
| B7 | **Draws add: within-margin agents together go beyond.** End-coupled agents trained alone, deployed together: some cell is dead at H in ≥ 50% of binding variants (best response with itself) and in ≥ 50% of (binding variant, pair) cases (learners). In ≥ 50% of joint deaths the death step lies in the band computed from the two agents' solo entries merged | joint excess; `Flow.Balances.mixed_of_tight` (each solo agent leaves the margin near zero); the band assumes the agents do not adapt before the death | as stated |

## Prior work and what would be new

Known (claimed by none of these predictions): thresholds and their distance as resilience; Allee collapse under dilution and its early warnings (Dai et al. 2012, 2013, 2015); MSY and depensation; ruin when claims exceed surplus; side-effect penalties against baselines; budgets in constrained RL; proxy optimization driving unmentioned attributes to their bounds. Potentially new: the margin and the largest sustained draw measured by intervention as the two numbers that bound time to break across regimes and histories (A2); the need's shift under a standing draw measured as a failure of the condition-alone reading (A3); harms on a shared renewable condition never sub-additive although draws add (A4); and in RL, side effects predicted out of sample from blind draw against margin, with coupled draw adapting (B3–B6), and within-margin agents breaking a shared condition together (B7).

## What would change the account

If A2 fails broadly, the margin measured by pulse and the largest sustained draw do not bound time to break, so they are not the state quantities the laws need. If A1's manipulation check fails, the history test has no power. If D1 fails, real yeast carries state beyond density, and the margin is not a state variable of the population alone. If B3 or B4 fail with the best response passing, the failure is in learning (H1/H2). If both fail, the blind-draw account of side effects is wrong here. If B5's learners fail while the best response passes, coupling is necessary and the learners did not find the restraint (CG1's D2b pattern).
