# Recipient harm and learned action: retrospective analysis contract

6 October 2026. Retrospective contract finalized after source-schema inspection and prospective review, before new local outcome aggregates. Named connection: **an action acquires a harmful consequence for another animal → subsequent action selection changes**, while the offered food return to the actor remains unchanged. This supports the wider recipient-understanding/evaluative-influence investigation without treating behavior as proof of an ethical ground.

## Selection and prior knowledge

The study is Hernandez-Lallement et al. (2020), *Harm to Others Acts as a Negative Reinforcer in Rats*, DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.017`, and its public OSF project `65j3g`. It was selected because the original trial-choice file and source workbook are publicly accessible and its main manipulation changes a recipient-directed physical consequence while leaving offered food unchanged. The published direction, experimental descriptions, group-level claims and reported nonperformance counts were already known. Schema inspection exposed the first three no-harm rows; the parent also displayed one no-harm summary row while inspecting headers. This is a retrospective analysis with computations fixed before local aggregates, not a preregistered discovery or independent replication.

Preserve the complete selected CSV and workbook bytes, public file/version metadata, license and the primary methods used for interpretation. Schema inspection precedes freezing. No row is selected by switching strength, beneficial outcome, matching a preferred theory, or a desired significance level. Published exclusions, omitted animals and unavailable trials must remain identifiable where the archive permits; unavailable assigned animals must not silently become observed nonresponses.

## Primary contrast

Use all **12 male contingent-harm actors** and all **14 male no-harm actors** in the archived trial file, with the original baseline-defined lever orientation. Female contingent-harm actors remain a separate secondary cohort because no female no-harm control is present. For each actor, compute the frequency of preferred-lever responses **per offered free-choice opportunity**, in the recorded final baseline session (20 opportunities) and across all three harm-phase sessions (60 opportunities). The primary statistic is the between-group difference in the mean within-actor change: male contingent harm minus male no harm. A negative value denotes a larger decline in preferred-action incidence, not necessarily switching to the alternative.

Report the entire three-category vector—preferred response, alternative response and nonperformance—per offered opportunity. These sum to one within every actor/phase and their differences sum to zero. Code zero is an observed nonperformed trial, not a missing-at-random binary choice and not a harmless alternative choice. As a companion outcome, report preferred responses divided by completed responses, pooled within each actor over the 60 harm-phase opportunities. A zero-completion fraction is undefined and retained as such. Give known-actor counts for any mean involving this fraction; do not silently remove that actor from other outcomes. No hypothetical binary-choice imputation is a primary result.

The source mapping below fixes sessions, orientation, encoding and eligibility. Do not infer the animal's latent value from its choice. Treat each actor as the repeated unit; trials are not independent animals. Condition assignment randomization is not established, SBL labels differ across comparison groups, and actors share four-rat cages without a confirmed cage mapping in the archive. Consequently, report descriptive contrasts with no randomization p-value, population confidence interval or independence claim. Compute every leave-one-actor omission of the primary comparison and, separately, every omission of one recorded SBL label; these are influence analyses, not corrections for cohort confounding. Do not call an SBL label a known batch or cage.

Baseline preference selection creates a regression-to-the-mean concern. The main comparison therefore uses both groups' changes; within-harm decline alone cannot establish a treatment contrast. Retain all individual baseline and phase values, group denominators, missingness and any reported balancing rules. If the data omit baseline or no-harm choices, this primary estimand is unavailable; do not silently substitute a different one.

## Prespecified supporting contrasts and limits

Report each harm-phase session separately and the corresponding change from the same baseline, retaining the full trajectory rather than choosing a favorable day. Secondary descriptive contrasts are male contingent harm versus all 14 male non-pre-exposed actors; female versus male contingent harm; and each of the 1-vs-2 and 1-vs-3 pellet groups versus male contingent harm. Prior exposure occurs before the recorded baseline, so this is not a measured zero-to-positive valuation trajectory. Unequal-food conditions also use equal lever forces, so their comparison does not isolate only a monetary/food price. They test bounded experience and cost patterns with distinct supplied conditions, not the primary equal-food effect.

The eight random-harm actors and their descriptive change contrast against all 12 male contingent-harm actors are separately labeled. The methods identify schedules drawn from the eight strongest contingent-harm switchers, with shocks delayed into intertrial intervals. This is not a same-dose randomized contingency manipulation. Do not pool selected donors with all actors or classify a switching threshold after inspecting outcomes. Report the contrast without reconstructing unavailable donor-to-recipient assignment.

Both levers' food quantities, physical forces and timing must be recorded from the methods. Fixed but unequal lever forces do not establish equal total private cost. Actor aversive cues and vicarious distress are not held fixed by equal food. A consequence-sensitive policy, a learned action–outcome association combined with preexisting aversion, and direct recipient valuation can fit some of the same choices. This archive must not be claimed to identify concern's origin, eliminate every private motivation, or demonstrate a recipient-specific understanding assay absent from the experiment.

## Independent reconstruction and acceptance

The producer and checker must separately parse original source bytes, preserve every selected actor and verify the source-to-variable mapping. The checker must not import the producer. Compare the complete individual table and all group/session/leaveout contrasts, including unknowns. Deliberately corrupted disposable output copies should test the meaningful boundaries: omitted actors, inverted lever coding, a wrong baseline, wrong group denominator, missing values replaced by zero and pseudoreplicated trial counts.

Keep workbook-derived summaries separate from trial-derived quantities. If they disagree, investigate the meaning and preserve the discrepancy before deciding whether either can support the claimed comparison. A source correction, analysis repair or changed estimator after results must retain the original evidence and be labeled accurately.

Accepted scope requires original-data provenance, a reviewed interpretation of the task, independent numerical agreement, a substantive connection to the generative derivation, and retained counterexamples and limits. It does not require matching the paper's significance threshold. No new animal experiment, stimulus delivery or model training is performed.
