# Finite support handoff: semantic contract

7 October 2026. Written before the first Lean check. This fixes the proposed result scope; failed attempts will not be repaired by silently changing it.

One local process contains loose material, a first support assembly, a second support assembly, a work assembly, the actual connections, a count of constructed products, remaining operational support and recorded failed attempts. These are differentiated organizations and relations in one history, not independent subjects. Assembly recipes and their effects are supplied local laws. This is not a refinement of the native `FormedSelection` chemistry or a biological implementation.

Each support assembly and the work assembly occupies one material unit. Each successful work attempt converts one loose unit into one constructed product. Dismantling returns the assembly's material; products are retained. Every event consumes exactly one operational-support unit, including connections, dismantling and unsuccessful attempts. These numerical costs and capacities are local inputs, not generative primitives. An activity has a declared finite target of three products and can finish only after actual production has reached it.

The first support assembly is formed before the work assembly; forming the work assembly connects them. A second support assembly must be separately formed and connected. A work attempt names the connection actually used. A missing or disconnected support, or missing loose material, produces no product and increments the failed-attempt count. It is physically permitted when the active unfinished work assembly has operational support. Dismantling the first assembly while work is unfinished is also physically permitted.

Required histories, from no formed assemblies and six material units:

1. With fourteen support units, form the first support and work assembly, produce one unit through the first connection, form and connect the second support, produce one unit through it, dismantle the first support, produce the final unit through the second, finish and dismantle the second support.
2. Dismantle the first support early, then actually attempt work through it. Show the failed operation. Later construction and connection of the second support can recover completion, while that earlier occurrence remains in the same history. Do not label temporary infeasibility by itself as harm.
3. Produce all three units through the first support, finish, and dismantle it without ever building a second. This is a real finite completion and ending.
4. With seven support units, the third history is possible, but the declared handoff word is not. This is an insufficiency claim about that word, not an unproved global minimum or a prohibition on ending.

General conservation and support accounting must accompany the witnesses. Actual histories use `Generative.Trace`. The existing `PurposeCorrection` occurrence interface may show that later success does not remove an earlier failed operation from the same recorded mathematical history. This is an analyst's occurrence description, not a new internal memory implementation.

No theorem says that dependency establishes a moral obligation, that the work's continuation is always good, that producing more is always better, or that ending requires replacement. Interpretation under Ren's positive 生生 direction belongs to the accompanying practical argument, including finite endings, self-protection, burdens and conflicting scopes.

## Review-added comparison, before final verification

The event law has no wall-clock demand during assembly or disconnection. The root and counterreview therefore require one further actual history: from the reached prepared state, dismantle the first support, assemble and connect the second, and only then perform the remaining two work attempts. Check that it completes with no failed attempt. This preserves the original laws and the direct-exit failure witness; it prevents a false necessity claim that replacement must always precede removal. The earlier failure is specifically an actual attempt on the already removed support, not a structural gap by itself.
