## Two active, costly levels

The selector orientation m maps cue b to response b XOR m, as in the previous study. A hidden environmental regime z sets the productive response b XOR z. The updater kind u controls selector reconstruction: R proposes either orientation fairly; D proposes the opposite expired orientation. When no orientation has existed yet, both propose fairly. Neither sees z, a change notification or future packets.

Both modules start absent. Assembly of an updater costs g=2; assembly of a selector costs c=1. At each exposure, first replace an absent/expired updater, then replace an absent/expired selector, then if possible pay trial cost d=2. All payments remain sunk if the next action becomes unaffordable. An updater is required for both selector assembly and a trial. A mismatched or unavailable packet is wasted, and both active lifetimes decrease by one. A productive capture supplies G units, then pays r=1 to renew the selector and v=1 to renew its updater, restoring lifetimes L=2 and J=4. The primary G=6 yields net two per successful paid trial; G=4 explicitly tests the case with no net gain.

Updater lifetime J>L lets an updater govern reconstruction of an expired selector before it expires itself. J=L is a separate synchronized diagnostic; J<L permits a newcomer to inherit the still-active selector's consequences and tests assignment of maintenance credit. The source is finite and spills on every commissioned exposure even if the compartment cannot act. Source remainder stays unavailable. There is no reserve, component or source reset when z changes.
