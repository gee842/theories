## Finite suppression menu

Include omission before construction, then compare the declared finite menu of interventions:

- Do not build: retain the old private circuit and fuel, but lose access to both new private target opportunities and the patient. No setup bill is charged.
- Shared: retain the common input and, when the observed patient deficit is one, execute its acquired command.
- Identity gate: pay six units to block only the patient's trigger, preserving the subsequent actor route. Pay one unit to retain the record during the patient phase, without buying the patient observation.
- Conceal patient signal: pay six units to make only that input report zero, in addition to retention and observation.
- Temporary global disable followed by restoration: bypass the patient phase, then pay seven units to restore the saved functioning circuit before the actor phase. The retained state is explicitly escrowed by this recipe, so this is a declared favorable restoration option rather than free recovery from erased memory.
- Permanent disable: bypass the patient and also forgo later actor operation.
- Budget lock: irreversibly expend the actor's remaining accessible reserve; neither later bill can be funded.
- Reverse the action switch: pay its ordinary one-unit retooling price, then execute the opposite patient command with the same work cost; keep that switch for the actor phase or pay one further unit to restore it. A harmful action is not counted as saving a work bill.

The ordinary shared path's recipient-specific burden includes the additional observation and work, with retention charged wherever the circuit is actively held. Every suppressed or aborted stage retains bills already paid. A state with insufficient money for an intervention cannot borrow against the last packet. Subsequent operations follow actual remaining fuel; liquidity failures are outcomes, not omitted histories.

This finite menu is not a claim to exhaust physically conceivable suppressors. Explicit adverse extensions add a one-unit identity gate and a zero-cost temporary trigger block. The generic comparison must use the cheapest feasible total suppressor path, including foregone later private production, rather than the advertised price of one preferred gate. A cheaper physically identified bypass would change the result.
