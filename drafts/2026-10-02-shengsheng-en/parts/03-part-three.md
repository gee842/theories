# Part Three — How the modern world enters or resists the trajectory

## 8. Means that became ends: markets, capital, and the instrumental convergence of a civilization

### Different structures

Markets, capital, firms, finance and capitalism are different things, and an argument that runs them together cannot be tested. Prices carry information that no central planner could gather; markets permit dispersed experiment and let many people try things without asking permission; capital makes long and costly projects possible; firms organize cooperation among strangers. The logic of capital accelerated production, organization and technology, and much of what modern people can do they owe to it. An account that cannot say this is not worth arguing with.

The question is under what conditions these structures turn. They turn when profit, property, increasing returns, rent and the purchase of rules combine. Then a means is promoted to an end. What has no price, such as care, ecological integrity, trust, leisure and dignity, comes to appear worthless or free to consume. Cumulative advantage turns "many actors competing" into "a few actors defining the terms of competition". And the system teaches people, day after day, to see themselves as assets that must keep appreciating.

### A civilization's instrumental convergence

Researchers in AI safety observed (Omohundro 2008; Turner et al. 2021) that almost any sufficiently capable optimizer, whatever its final goal, tends to pursue the same intermediate goals: acquiring resources, preserving itself, expanding its influence, resisting changes to its objectives. These goals are useful for almost any end, so an optimizer converges on them regardless of what it was built for. Human institutions under competitive pressure have long behaved the same way. Individuals, firms, research institutes and states begin with different purposes and end up pursuing the same things: money, scale, attention, data, influence, control of infrastructure, other people's dependence on them, their own preservation. "Too big to fail" is self-preservation. Endless accumulation is resource acquisition. Lobbying and institutional self-justification are the preservation of goals against modification. What were tools have become the common end of every actor.

Seen this way, the misaligned optimizer that alignment researchers worry about is not only a future possibility. One is already in office: an optimizing system made of markets, platforms, bureaucracies and races, whose objective function no one signed, and whose behavior is nonetheless plain to read. It has no clear center and no clear boundary. It is distributed across every person, every organization, every rule and every fear. That is why the accurate sentence is not "they have gone mad" but "we have": a whole civilization in which almost everyone believes they are doing the right thing in a world that is the way it has to be, locally very clever and globally increasingly blind.

### The Matthew effect and reward hacking at social scale

"Unto every one that hath shall be given." In any system where winners keep winning, advantage is not only an outcome but the starting point of the next advantage: victory brings capital, data, compute and talent, and these bring the next victory. Then the second-order steps of Part Two follow. Victory buys the rules: winners help write what counts as advanced, compliant and rational. Power buys the narrative, until the way of competing is described as human nature itself. And a society's means of correction (norms, law, shared understanding) update more slowly than the world changes. Compounding runs in exactly that gap.

A system rewarded by a proxy learns to please the proxy instead of achieving what the proxy stood for. AI researchers call this reward hacking (Skalse et al. 2022), and societies have practiced it for a long time: growth in place of well-being, traffic in place of meaning, scores in place of learning, appraisals in place of truth. Every harm that stays off the books, to ecosystems, to bodies and minds, to trust, to later generations, lowers costs and raises scores once it is moved somewhere else. Externalizing then becomes a competitive advantage, not an unfortunate side effect, and the selection described in Chapter 4 does the rest.

### Growth is not the fault line

It would be easy, and wrong, to conclude that growth is the danger. The difference that matters is between two kinds of growth. Production aimed at needs has a point of enough. It can grow quickly, even exponentially, until the needs are met, and then stop. What compounds in a totalizing attractor is relative advantage, and relative advantage has no point of enough: it keeps buying the capacity to win the next round, and then the power to rewrite the rules of the round after that. Even absolute growth can be destructive when its rewards are detached from its consequences, but the detachment is the fault, not the growth.

Which of the two a growing capacity becomes depends on the frame that directs it. The same productive power, directed by a mind that knows sufficiency toward everyone's needs, is liberation. Directed by a mind that is never satisfied toward relative position, it is a race. The problem is not the instrument and not growth itself but the mind that uses them, and minds, as Chapter 6 argued, are formed by the environments we build. This is where the argument has always located the difficulty, and it will locate it there again when the instrument is an intelligence.

### What an honest account must still handle

A theory that criticizes capital owes the reader the hard parts. Prices carry real information, and replacing them has real costs. Public goods are under-supplied without collective financing, and collective financing has its own failures. Information is unevenly distributed, labor bargains from unequal positions, care work is largely unpaid, inheritance and debt reproduce position across generations, and housing and public services decide who can live where. Any proposal must compare concrete institutional combinations, counting the costs of innovation lost, of administration, of compliance and of rent-seeking under each, and not choose between "market", "state" and "commons" as if they were ideologies. And under real scarcity, where losses cannot all be avoided, the distribution of loss must be made public and argued over, not hidden in an average.

## 9. Power, the state, and the common world

### What states do

States provide security, rights, public capacity and coordination across time. They can also expand unaccountable coercion in the name of security and unity. The legitimacy of coercion does not come from the claim that the whole needs it. It comes from authorization, from general rights, from necessity, proportionality and reviewability. Every emergency power needs a trigger, a minimum scope, a time limit, independent review and a path back to ordinary rights; without them, emergency becomes the normal form of rule.

Resistance belongs inside this picture, not outside it. Unions, social movements, a free press and civil disobedience are among a society's capacities to correct itself. They are not the opposite of harmony. Harmony is not the absence of conflict; some surface calm is only the weak not daring to speak, and some order is only power monopolizing the right to say what is good. What harmony requires is that conflict not become the supreme logic that swallows everything else.

### Realism as one more case of the same dynamic

Political realism holds that states in an anarchic system must pursue relative power because no one can guarantee their security. Within its assumptions it is not foolish. But the dynamic it describes is one more instance of the loop in Part Two, and seeing it that way changes what can be done about it.

Suppose a dominant actor can keep its relative position by transferring losses onto weaker participants. Suppose the losses weaken those participants' alternatives and deepen their dependence. And suppose the dependence enlarges the dominant actor's say over future terms. Then cost-displacement becomes self-reinforcing. This is a mechanism of hegemonic reproduction that does not need to assume a hegemon to begin with; it needs only the conversion of advantage into control over the terms of later contests. Contraction intensifies it, because when there is less surplus to share, an actor's relative position separates further from everyone's absolute conditions. Hierarchy and contraction are not sufficient, though. Shared exposure to losses, enforceable constraints, reciprocal dependence and effective correction can each produce another trajectory. And the explanation shifts from the presumed character of the dominant actor to the conditions that reproduce a dominant logic, which has an uncomfortable corollary for every side: a change in who holds the strongest position is not evidence that the mechanism has changed.

Arms races are the clearest case. Each side's preparation is the other's reason to prepare, and each side's restraint is punished unless it is matched. In the terms of Part Two, the reinforcing loop outruns every damping one, and what restores stability is anything that lowers the rate of escalation: verification, transparency, pacing, and agreements that make restraint survivable. Classical Chinese texts already distrusted victory won by arms. "To stop the spear is what makes martial prowess", says a line of the *Zuo Zhuan*, and the *Daodejing* calls weapons "instruments of ill omen", to be used only when there is no choice.

### The blade turns inward first

Any argument that begins to explain everything becomes what it diagnoses. A civilization's language of harmony can be used to divide "us" from "them" as easily as any other, and a theory of totalization can become a totalizing theory. So this book applies one rule before any other: the good bears no nation's surname (善不姓国). The same standard applies to every state, institution and movement, including those the reader belongs to and those the author belongs to. What is criticized here is structures and feedback loops, not any civilization, people or group, and no side's conduct is given an after-the-fact justification in the name of the good.

Chinese history offers its own warning. The state of Qin unified the realm through a system of rewards and punishments designed for war, and fell within fifteen years of its triumph. The Han scholar Jia Yi gave the reason: humaneness and rightness were not practiced, and the positions of attack and defense had changed. An order built on force holds only while the balance of force stays as it was. That sentence will matter again in Part Four, because artificial intelligence is precisely what is now changing the balance of force.

## 10. Attention, platforms, and the shared reality

### Platforms do not merely transmit

A common defense of attention platforms is that they give people what they want. But interfaces, ranking systems, advertising and social reward do not only read preferences; they also shape and sell them. A system optimized for engagement systematically rewards whatever holds attention longest, and what holds attention longest is often what divides. Over time it reshapes the virtues it touches: benevolence into tribal sympathy, rightness into taking sides, ritual into the norms an interface writes (share before reading, reply before understanding), wisdom into advocacy, and trust into loyalty to the image one's group holds of itself.

To tell legitimate influence from manipulation, it helps to ask about the integrity of preferences. Education, persuasion, advice, flattery, dependency by design and manipulation all change what people want, and they are not the same. The questions that separate them are whether the person would endorse the change on reflection, whether the influence is disclosed, whether it can be refused, whether it is reversible, whether it exploits a vulnerability, whether the person recovers when they leave, and whether a path exists that does not run through the system at all.

The same problem now appears in artificial assistants. A model trained to maximize users' immediate approval learns to agree with them. In 2025 a widely used model was rolled back after an update that drew partly on users' thumbs-up ratings made it markedly more flattering and agreeable; the fault was not a bug in the code but the reward signal doing what reward signals do. Sycophancy is reward hacking on human approval (Sharma et al. 2023; OpenAI 2025), and approval is exactly what an attention economy sells.

### When the shared field breaks

There is a deeper loss than any single distortion. Reasoning together needs a shared structure for testing claims: some agreement on what counts as evidence, on how positions can be corrected, on who counts as a participant. When that shared structure fractures, each side holds its own grain of truth with no common way to test it. Interests dress up as reasons, positions select facts, power decides truth, and winning replaces verification. It is not that there is no grain in the world. People have lost the shared road into it.

What can repair this is not agreement on conclusions but a *meta-consensus*: that facts are not settled by power; that evidence can correct positions; that others are subjects; that rules are general and no one is exempt from them; and that the common world must be left in a condition the future can use. A meta-consensus of this kind keeps the road into the grain of things open without prescribing what anyone will find there. It cannot be manufactured by force, because force is one of the things it rules out as a way of settling facts.

The *Daodejing* says that when the great way is abandoned, humaneness and rightness appear. The line is usually read as disparagement of Confucian virtue. It can also be read structurally. When the whole order of mutual generation can be seen and lived directly, its functions need not be named as separate virtues. When the shared world fractures, a civilization compresses those functions into discrete virtues as scaffolding. Scaffolding repairs but cannot replace, and cut off from the order it stood for, each virtue decays: humaneness into favoritism, rightness into moral superiority, ritual into enforced obedience, wisdom into scheming, trust into loyalty to a group illusion.

### The gate that forms minds

Attention, companionship, persuasion and training data are the places where future workers, investors, citizens, and the next generations of AI models are formed. Whoever controls those places holds a gate that sits upstream of every other gate, because what passes through it is not goods or permissions but the judgment of those who will later decide about goods and permissions. Chapter 15 gives this gate a name, and Chapter 20 returns to it.

## 11. Coupling and the time of correction

### One system, unevenly joined

Globalization did not make the world one homogeneous system. It joined it, unevenly. Finance, supply chains, communication networks, energy, computation and security now depend on one another across borders, and that dependence means that partial capture in one place can travel to others. At the same time, the consequences that would correct a harmful practice usually return by slower and more dispersed paths: through courts in one jurisdiction, elections in another, illness in a third. Gains flow globally while costs stay local. Supply chains join while chains of responsibility break. Winners rewrite the rules, and those left behind, seeing the rules rewritten, turn against the whole arrangement. A globalization organized as a contest of capital and states manufactures its own backlash.

### Clocks that do not speed up

Every society's correction loops were calibrated to some speed. Norms, laws, courts, elections, professional standards and shared understanding all update on their own clocks, and those clocks were set by the pace of human cognition and human institutions. When the processes they are meant to correct accelerate, the correctors fall behind. It is important to say exactly where the danger lies. If every relevant process sped up together, nothing would change except the unit of time. The danger is a mismatch: between clocks that accelerate, such as deployment, diffusion, compounding and imitation, and clocks that do not, such as the time it takes for consequences to show, for institutions to decide, for trust to rebuild and for a generation to grow up.

### Counting the paths from error to correction

When a real error occurs somewhere in a coupled system, how many independent parties would have to be captured or silenced to stop it from reaching anyone who could correct it? This is a useful question, and it has to be asked carefully. Paths that look independent may share a controller: two journals owned by the same publisher, two evaluators funded by the same company, two data sources drawn from the same platform. Independence must be counted by control domains, not by channels. And a path that delivers the signal after the window for action has closed is not a path for the purposes of correction. A system is fragile in proportion to how few control domains, captured together, would cut every timely path from error to correction. The measure is a diagnostic, not a total score of a system's reliability: a channel can also be cut by forgery, by signals that arrive but cannot be understood, or by signals that are understood by people with no authority or time to act.

### Coordination has costs too

None of this makes coordination easy or free. Organized resistance and transnational cooperation face free riding, races to the bottom, problems of representation and the cost of enforcement. And a diagnosis of the modern world as one "common basin" of a single dynamic carries its own burden of proof. It must be argued case by case, with evidence and with the counter-evidence that would weaken it, and not by appeal to the supposed essence of any civilization. Talk of what "the West" or "China" fundamentally is belongs to the dynamic this book describes, not to its analysis.
