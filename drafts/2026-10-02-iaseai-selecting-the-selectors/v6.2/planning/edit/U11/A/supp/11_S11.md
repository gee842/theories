## S11. The transition

### What relaxes the trust constraints

Section 11.1 states the three places where trust enters a transition and what relaxes each; this is the result in full, in the notation of {{assure}}, {{talk}} and {{overhang}}.

**{{!Corollary:relaxtrust}} (What relaxes the trust constraints) [thm].** Whatever lowers the value of winning relative to the joint value relaxes all three constraints at once; verification relaxes the first only on what it observes; a sanction relaxes the first and the third only while it can still be imposed. *In the notation of {{assure}}, {{talk}} and {{overhang}}, with $g_C,g_D>0$:*
1. *Raising the value of mutual restraint relative to the value of winning, so that $T-R$ falls while $R>P-F$, raises $g_C$ and lowers both the belief threshold and the timely detection that cooperation needs, $(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ ({{verify}}(1)); a wider working objective, a larger $\theta$, lowers the effective bias $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$ of talk; and a smaller relative term in every payoff lowers the net gain from exit, $V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay}$.*
2. *Where $T-P+F>0$, a larger probability $\upsilon$ of timely detection raises $g_C$ and, where $P>S$, lowers $g_D$, so it lowers the belief threshold; it carries no information about what it does not observe.*
3. *A larger sanction $F$ raises $g_C$, and it deters an irreversible, public exit while $\upsilon F\ge V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay}$. It cannot be imposed on a defector whose position has become decisive, so against such a deviation it acts only through the bound $\bar p_d$ on the chance of breakout ({{enforce}}(2)).*
4. *Lowering the vulnerability of restraint, $P-S$, lowers $g_D$ and with it the belief threshold.*

*Proof.* Write, as in {{assure}}, $g_C=R-\upsilon(P-F)-(1-\upsilon)T$ and $g_D=(1-\upsilon)(P-S)$. Where both are positive, the belief threshold $\varpi^\ast=g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ falls as $g_C$ rises and rises with $g_D$.

*(1)* $\partial g_C/\partial R=1$ and $\partial g_C/\partial T=-(1-\upsilon)\le0$, so raising $R$ or lowering $T$ lowers $\varpi^\ast$. The timely detection that cooperation needs, $u^\ast=(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ from {{assure}}(2), has $\partial u^\ast/\partial R=-1/(T-P+F)<0$ and $\partial u^\ast/\partial T=(R-P+F)/(T-P+F)^2$, positive iff $R>P-F$. The effective bias of {{talk}}(1), $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$, has derivative $-2(n-1)b_0/(n-1+\theta)^2<0$ in $\theta$. The net gain from exit is $V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay}$ ({{overhang}}(1)), and an agreement that preserves relative positions preserves its relative term ({{overhang}}(3)), so lowering that term lowers the gain.

*(2)* $\partial g_C/\partial\upsilon=T-P+F>0$ under the premise, and $\partial g_D/\partial\upsilon=-(P-S)<0$ where $P>S$. Verification reaches only what it observes ({{verify}}, Scope): messages about the rest remain cheap talk, credible only where the effective bias is below $\frac14$ ({{talk}}).

*(3)* $\partial g_C/\partial F=\upsilon>0$. An irreversible, public exit is deterred while $\upsilon F\ge V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay}$, and a reversible covert preparation while $\upsilon F\ge(1-\upsilon)(V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay})$ ({{overhang}}(1)). A defector whose position has become decisive is beyond punishment, so against a deviation with $D_i>v_i$ the sanction acts only through $v_i$, and restraint holds only while $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$ ({{enforce}}(2)).

*(4)* $\partial g_D/\partial(P-S)=1-\upsilon\ge0$. $\square$

*The link to the levers* [thm∣prem]. Sharing a fraction $\beta$ of the contested stakes scales them by $1-\beta$ ({{racehazard}}), which lowers what racing against a restrainer gains over mutual restraint; provision beneath the game lowers the won share of capacity and with it $\Gamma_\delta$ ({{scissors}}; {{compound}}); and a lower $\Gamma_\delta$ lowers the price of standing and widens the working objective ({{standprice}}). A common frontier raises what restraint earns against a defector, $S$, by pooling the restrainers' effort, and so lowers $P-S$; in the contest of {{commonfrontn}} it turns all cooperating into a strict equilibrium beside the race [num]. The stage payoffs $T,R,P,S$ of {{assure}} are reduced forms of those contests, so the link holds where they are read from them.

### Proof of {{signal}}

The receiver restrains with probability $\rho_m$ after message $m\in\{\mathsf E,\mathsf N\}$, where $\mathsf N$ is not taking the step: $\rho_m=1$ if its posterior that the sender is $\mathsf S$ exceeds $\varpi^\ast$, $\rho_m=0$ if it falls short, and any $\rho_m$ at equality; after a message no type sends, any belief, and so any $\rho_m$, is admissible. Type $\mathsf t$ receives $\rho_{\mathsf E}\Pi_{\mathsf t}-c_{\mathsf t}$ if it takes the step and $\rho_{\mathsf N}\Pi_{\mathsf t}$ if not. The step is open to inspection: that is what makes it verifiable and gives $c_{\mathsf R}$ its size.

*(1)* In the candidate the posteriors are 1 after the step and 0 without it, so $\rho_{\mathsf E}=1$ and $\rho_{\mathsf N}=0$, since $\varpi^\ast\in(0,1)$. $\mathsf S$ takes the step iff $\Pi_{\mathsf S}-c_{\mathsf S}\ge0$, and $\mathsf R$ refrains iff $\Pi_{\mathsf R}-c_{\mathsf R}\le0$.

*(2)* If $c_{\mathsf R}<\Pi_{\mathsf R}$, $\mathsf R$ gains $\Pi_{\mathsf R}-c_{\mathsf R}>0$ by imitating the candidate of (1), which is therefore not an equilibrium. Let $0\le c_{\mathsf R}<\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ and $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, and take any equilibrium. The posteriors on the path average to the prior $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, so at most one message on the path has a posterior of $\varpi^\ast$ or more. If $\rho_{\mathsf N}>0$, then $\mathsf N$ is either off the path, so that $\mathsf E$ is the only message on it, its posterior is $1-\mu$ and $\rho_{\mathsf E}=0$; or on it with a posterior of at least $\varpi^\ast$, so that the posterior after $\mathsf E$, if $\mathsf E$ is sent, is below it and again $\rho_{\mathsf E}=0$, while if $\mathsf E$ is not sent the posterior after $\mathsf N$ is $1-\mu$, contradicting $\rho_{\mathsf N}>0$. If $\rho_{\mathsf N}=0$ and $\rho_{\mathsf E}>c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$, $\mathsf R$ takes the step with certainty, and whatever $\mathsf S$ does the posterior after the step is $(1-\mu)\sigma_{\mathsf S}/\big((1-\mu)\sigma_{\mathsf S}+\mu\big)\le1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, so $\rho_{\mathsf E}=0$, a contradiction. Hence $\rho_{\mathsf E}\le c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ in every equilibrium, after the step whether or not it is on the path. The bound is attained in the equilibrium in which $\mathsf S$ takes the step, $\mathsf R$ takes it with probability $\sigma_{\mathsf R}=(1-\mu)(1-\varpi^\ast)/(\mu\varpi^\ast)\in(0,1)$, which holds the posterior after the step at $\varpi^\ast$, and the receiver restrains with probability $c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ after the step and not otherwise; $\mathsf R$ is then indifferent, and $\mathsf S$ takes the step iff $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$.

*(3)* follows from (2) as $c_{\mathsf R}\to0$. $\square$

V6.1 stated (2) as "the receiver restrains after the step only if $1-\mu\ge\varpi^\ast$". That holds among pure-strategy equilibria, where $\mathsf R$ either imitates always or never; the equilibrium that attains the bound above has $\mathsf R$ imitating with a probability below one, and restraint after the step with positive probability even where prior trust falls short.

*Refinement.* Let $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, $c_{\mathsf S}<\Pi_{\mathsf S}$ and $c_{\mathsf R}>\Pi_{\mathsf R}$. The step is strictly dominated for $\mathsf R$, whose payoff from it is at most $\Pi_{\mathsf R}-c_{\mathsf R}<0$. In any equilibrium in which $\mathsf S$ does not take the step, the posterior without it is $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, so $\rho_{\mathsf N}=0$ and $\mathsf S$ receives 0, while the step could give it up to $\Pi_{\mathsf S}-c_{\mathsf S}>0$. The intuitive criterion (Cho and Kreps 1987) then puts all weight after the step on $\mathsf S$, the receiver restrains after it, and $\mathsf S$ deviates; and no partially separating equilibrium exists, since $\mathsf R$ never takes a dominated step and $\mathsf S$, once the step earns restraint, strictly prefers it. The separating equilibrium is the only one that survives. Where prior trust is already sufficient, $1-\mu\ge\varpi^\ast$, an equilibrium without the step and with restraint exists beside it, and the step is not needed.

*The signal and its imitation.* This is the costly signal of a security-seeking type (Kydd 2000). Where the lock is certain, $c_{\mathsf S}\le0$ and the survival-first type takes the step whatever the receiver does; the signal's work is then done entirely by what the step costs a relative type. What the selection account adds is the reason the step is cheap to imitate unless it is inspected: a relative type that declares the removal and keeps the delegation it does not disclose pays nothing for the declaration, so $c_{\mathsf R}\to0$ and (3) applies. Inspection of doctrine and exercises, and durability that makes reversal costly, are what make $c_{\mathsf R}$ a cost at all; the difference between the two types' costs is the difference a signal needs.

*Numerics* [num: TR]. A search over strategy profiles, which does not use the conditions it tests, agrees with (1) in 300 random games, finds no equilibrium above the bound of (2) in 200, and finds the bounding equilibrium in every one of the 135 in which $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$. With $c_{\mathsf S}=-0.05$, $c_{\mathsf R}=0.30$, $\Pi_{\mathsf S}=0.40$ and $\Pi_{\mathsf R}=0.20$ the step separates. If it can be reversed cheaply, $c_{\mathsf R}=0.05$, it does not: with $\mu=0.5$ and $\varpi^\ast=0.6$, $\mathsf R$ imitates with probability 0.667, and the step buys restraint with probability 0.25 at most.

### A pause is valued by what changes during it

**{{!Proposition:pausevalue}} (A pause is valued by what changes during it) [thm].** A pause that changes nothing buys time, not survival. *Let $h_t$ be the residual hazard of the irreversible event in the $t$-th active round, and let a pause add no hazard of its own. (1) If the same process resumes after the pause, so that the $t$-th active round has the same $h_t$, survival over any number of active rounds is unchanged, and so is its limit, which is zero where the $h_t$ are not summable ({{nonsum}}). (2) A pause after which the residual sequence is summable, where before it was not, keeps survival bounded away from zero. (3) In the field model, a pause that freezes shares while ordinary rule support keeps accumulating completes capture from every frozen share above $1/G$ ({{pausefield}}).*

*Proof.* *(1)* Let $h_t$ be the conditional probability of the irreversible event in the $t$-th active round, given survival to it, on each surviving history. A pause that adds no hazard of its own changes the calendar date of each active round and nothing else, so survival over the first $N$ active rounds, $\prod_{t\le N}(1-h_t)$ on each history, is the same with or without it, and so is the limit. By {{nonsum}}, it is zero where the $h_t$ are at least a non-summable sequence. *(2)* Where after the pause $h_t\le\bar h_t<1$ with $\sum_t\bar h_t<\infty$, survival is at least the product of the rounds before the change and $\prod_t(1-\bar h_t)>0$. *(3)* is {{pausefield}} with ordinary rule-making, $b_p=b$. $\square$

Resuming unchanged after a pause repeats the process that made the pause necessary; that is (1), and it is why Section 11.3 defines readiness by what has changed rather than by the time elapsed.

*Example* [num: TR]. With residual hazard 0.01 a round for 20 rounds and $0.01/t^{1.5}$ in the $t$-th round after a change, survival to round 10,000 is 0.80, and its limit is 0.80, since $\sum_t0.01\,t^{-1.5}=0.01\,\zeta(3/2)\approx0.026$; with the hazard left at 0.01 a round it is $2\times10^{-44}$.

### The decision rule of readiness

**{{!Remark:readrule}} (Readiness under noise and conjunction) [thm].** A reading fixed in advance biases a conjunction of seven dimensions toward "not ready", more so as noise grows; a sequential test turns noise into delay. *(1) If each of seven dimensions, when met, is declared met with probability $1-\mathsf b_q$, independently, a ready state is declared ready with probability $\prod_q(1-\mathsf b_q)$, which is $0.95^7\approx0.70$ at $\mathsf b_q=0.05$. (2) Let each dimension be decided by a sequential probability-ratio test between a registered level below its threshold and one above it, on independent readings whose likelihood ratio is computed from their distribution, with boundaries set for error rates $\mathsf a_q$ (declaring it met when it is not) and $\mathsf b_q$ (declaring it unmet when it is met). The test ends with probability one, and its error rates are at most $\mathsf a_q/(1-\mathsf b_q)$ and $\mathsf b_q/(1-\mathsf a_q)$ at every level of noise (Wald 1945). Over seven dimensions a ready state is held back with probability at most $\sum_q\mathsf b_q/(1-\mathsf a_q)$.*

*Proof.* (1) is a product of independent events. (2) is Wald's termination theorem and his bounds for the boundaries $\ln\big((1-\mathsf b_q)/\mathsf a_q\big)$ and $\ln\big(\mathsf b_q/(1-\mathsf a_q)\big)$ on the log-likelihood ratio, with a union bound over the seven. $\square$

*Numerics* [num: TR]. A fixed reading of 16 observations, designed for a 1% rate of false "met" and a 5% rate of missed "met" at unit noise, and holding its 1% rate at any noise, passes a met dimension with probability 0.95 at unit noise and 0.37 at twice it; seven met dimensions then pass together with probability 0.71 and 0.001. Sequential tests with $\mathsf a_q=0.01$ and $\mathsf b_q=0.05/7$, on Gaussian readings at levels 0 and 1, keep both observed error rates below their bounds at noise 1, 2 and 4, while the mean number of readings needed to pass a met dimension rises from 10.5 to 38.7 and 148.6, about as the variance. Over seven such dimensions a ready state is held back with probability at most $0.05/0.99\approx0.0505$.

The residence condition of {{readiness}} sets the window over which the readings are taken: no test may end before the residence time has passed, so a dimension cannot be declared met before its slow variable could have crossed its threshold. The rates are registered asymmetrically because the errors are: declaring a dimension met too early is the error the protection standard weighs, since resuming cannot be undone; holding it back too long is bounded, since a pause held past its evidence spends coverage ({{unravel}}). A dimension once met is re-opened by a test of the same kind, so that a single low reading neither re-opens it nor is ignored.

### The seven dimensions of readiness

Each dimension of {{readiness}} asks a question with observable indicators and rests on a slow variable, whose time constant is the least residence time over which the indicator must have stayed past its threshold. Four of the seven slow variables are those of the tracks of Section 11.4.

| Dimension | Question | Slow variable; the residence time is at least |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Technical reliability | Has the residual hazard of the relevant classes fallen, by evidence independent of the developer and valid against evaluation awareness? | Residual risk per class; the class's manifestation window $\tau_e$ ({{tempo}}) |
| Q2 Shared objectives | Have governing purposes moved from relative advantage toward shared provision? | The self-models that governing positions select; a turnover of those positions |
| Q3 Strategic incentives | Has the value of winning fallen relative to the joint value? | The won share of capacity, $\omega$ ({{scissors}}); a period of growing stakes long enough to show provision keeping pace with them |
| Q4 Effective correction | Can evaluators obtain evidence, keep independent funding and execute findings? | Rule support in each corrector domain; its dwell ({{dwell}}), not the return of shares to their former level |
| Q5 Deployment constraints | Is the tempo of capability and delegation bound to correction capacity? | Coverage against hazardous variety; a generation of capability, across which catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours stay level |
| Q6 Retained human agency | Can people still take over delegated functions? | Human capacity $\mathcal M$; its recovery time after delegation ({{lockin}}) |
| Q7 Custody and verifiability | Is decisive-class capability in common custody, and conduct verifiable faster than breakout? | The capability held in check, and detection latency against the time to a decisive position ({{detectlink}}); a period in which the capability grows, since the net gain from exit grows with it ({{overhang}}) |

### Dwell, depth and a recruitable lineage

Take one domain of the field model {{eq:field}} with its own conversion $b$ and conversion gain $G=ab/(cd)>1$, and write it in the logit of the share, $u=\ln\big(x/(1-x)\big)$, and rule support in units of break-even, $\tilde z=az/c$: then $\dot u=\zeta c(\tilde z-1)$ and $\dot{\tilde z}=\varepsilon d(Gx-\tilde z)$. Capture is the state $x\to1$, $\tilde z=G$; absence of capture, $(0,0)$, is locally stable, the interior saddle is $(1/G,1)$, and the two basins are divided by the saddle's stable manifold, which meets $\tilde z=0$ at the share $x_\sigma$ of {{pausefield}}. A reform that insulates rule-making sets the conversion to zero for a time $T$ and then restores it; the *dwell* $T_{\min}$ is the least $T$ after which the state converges to absence of capture. "Rule-making ten times slower than adoption" is $\varepsilon d=0.1\,\zeta c$.

**{{!Proposition:dwell}} (Dwell, depth and a recruitable lineage) [thm; num: DW].** A reform that insulates rule-making has to be held past the return of shares to their former level, for longer the deeper the capture, and without bound as capture ages unless a lineage of the alternative persists that can be recruited when the rule changes. *Start an insulating reform from rule support at its captured level, $\tilde z=G$, with share $x_0$.*
1. *(Two phases.) Under insulation $\tilde z=Ge^{-\varepsilon dt}$; the share keeps rising for $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$, and its logit is back at $u_0$ when $G(1-e^{-s})=s$, $s=\varepsilon dt$. After release the state converges to absence of capture iff it lies below the saddle's stable manifold. If $x_0>x_\sigma$, the dwell exceeds the time at which the share is back at its starting level* [thm].
2. *(Values.) From $x_0=0.99$, $\varepsilon dT_{\min}$ is 1.89, 5.32 and 10.45 for $G=2$, 5 and 10 when rule-making is ten times slower than adoption, and 5.86, 10.34 and 16.18 when the two are equally fast; the share is back at its starting level at $s=1.59$, 4.97 and 10.0* [num: DW].
3. *(Depth.) $T_{\min}$ is nondecreasing in the depth $u_0$ and at least $(u_0-\operatorname{logit}x_\sigma)/(\zeta c)$, so it has no bound as depth grows. At the captured state $\dot u=\zeta c(G-1)$, so the depth grows linearly with the age of capture* [thm]. *Each further unit of depth adds about $\varepsilon d/(\zeta c)$ to $\varepsilon dT_{\min}$* [num: DW].
4. *(A recruitable lineage.) If a lineage of the alternative persists inside selection at a share of at least $\phi_{\rm alt}$, so that the practice's share never exceeds $1-\phi_{\rm alt}$, and grows from it when the rule changes, the depth never exceeds $\ln\big((1-\phi_{\rm alt})/\phi_{\rm alt}\big)$, and $T_{\min}$ is at most its value from that depth, whatever the age of capture* [thm]. *With $G=10$ and rule-making ten times slower, a floor of 0.1% cuts $T_{\min}$ from 10.45 to 3.81; a share of the alternative protected outside selection, which does not recruit, leaves it at 10.44* [num: DW].

*Proof.* (1) Under insulation $\dot{\tilde z}=-\varepsilon d\tilde z$, so $\tilde z=Ge^{-\varepsilon dt}$ and $u(t)=u_0+\zeta c\big[G(1-e^{-\varepsilon dt})/(\varepsilon d)-t\big]$, which rises while $\tilde z>1$ and is back at $u_0$ when $G(1-e^{-s})=s$. After release the function of the proof of {{pausefield}} is nondecreasing along solutions, so every solution converges to an equilibrium and the saddle's stable manifold separates the basins. The field is cooperative ({{fieldlemma}}), so the basin of absence is a down-set in $(u,\tilde z)$; it contains $(u,0)$ only for $u<\operatorname{logit}x_\sigma$, and so it contains no state with $u>\operatorname{logit}x_\sigma$. When the share is back at $x_0>x_\sigma$, and at every earlier time, the state is therefore outside the basin of absence. (3) The insulated equation for $u$ does not depend on $u$, and $\tilde z(t)$ does not depend on $u_0$, so a start deeper by $\Delta u$ stays deeper by $\Delta u$ at every $t$ with the same $\tilde z(t)$; since the basin of absence is a down-set, a $T$ that suffices from the deeper start suffices from the shallower one. The released state is in the basin only if $u(T)<\operatorname{logit}x_\sigma$, and $u(T)\ge u_0-\zeta cT$. At the captured state $\tilde z=G$, so $\dot u=\zeta c(G-1)$. (4) With the floor, $u\le\ln\big((1-\phi_{\rm alt})/\phi_{\rm alt}\big)$ throughout, and the insulated path from any start is bounded above, at every $t$, by the capped path from that depth; by the down-set property the bound on $T_{\min}$ follows. $\square$ The increments of (3) and the values of (2) and (4) are numerical: once rule support has decayed the logit falls at a rate approaching $\zeta c$, so each further unit of depth costs about $1/(\zeta c)$ of time.

*Numerics* (DW: forward integration in logit coordinates, and independently a backward-integrated separatrix with the analytic insulated path, agreeing to three decimals in six of six cases). The holds by gain and speed are those of (2): in units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, from a captured share of 0.99 the hold runs from 1.89 to 10.45 as $G$ rises from 2 to 10 when rule-making is ten times slower than adoption, and from 5.86 to 16.18 when the two are equally fast. Depth without a floor, with $G=2$ and equal speeds: from $x_0=1-10^{-1}$, $1-10^{-2}$, $1-10^{-3}$, $1-10^{-6}$ and $1-10^{-12}$ the dwell is 3.45, 5.86, 8.17, 15.08 and 28.90, so at equal speeds and $G=2$ it is 5.86 from a share of 0.99 and 28.9 from $1-10^{-12}$; with $G=10$ it runs from 13.78 to 39.22; with rule-making ten times slower the increments are a tenth as large (1.59 to 4.34 at $G=2$; 10.21 to 12.76 at $G=10$). A floor on the alternative, with $G=10$ and rule-making ten times slower: 3.52 at $\phi_{\rm alt}=0.01$, 3.81 at 0.001, 4.60 at $10^{-6}$ and 6.04 at $10^{-12}$, against 10.45 without; at $G=5$, 2.99 at 0.001; with equal speeds and $G=10$, 9.48 and 11.79 at the first two floors, against 16.18. Re-seeding the alternative at the rate $\phi_{\rm alt}\zeta(G-1)$ also bounds the dwell (3.28 at $G=10$, rule-making ten times slower, $\phi_{\rm alt}=0.001$; 4.12 at $10^{-6}$), but re-seeding is symmetric and re-seeds the practice wherever it has been removed. A share protected outside selection, $b\to b(1-\phi_{\rm alt})$ with $\phi_{\rm alt}=0.001$, leaves the dwell at 10.44.

*Scope.* One domain, quasi-static conversion and a fixed $G$. The dwell rises with $G$, so capacity that capture has built and that goes on converting, a sunk stock that decays slowly, keeps the effective gain, and the dwell, up. The starting share of 0.99 is a convention, which is why (3) matters. The field is cooperative, so these are lock-in times, not cycle times. With several domains the reform must also take down the core that sustains the domain ({{reabsorb}}). Rule support is not observed directly, and near full share the depth cannot be read from the level. For real institutions the magnitudes are a conjecture (TESTS, Test D); the comparison the model supports, that a reform judged on share levels is withdrawn too early and that a floor under a recruitable alternative bounds the hold while a frozen order's hold grows with its age, is the claim of Section 11.4. The share-freezing pause has its own length, given with {{pausefield}} in S4: a frozen share accumulates no lock-in.

### The ratchet

**{{!Remark:ratchet}} (Graduated steps reach only as a ratchet) [thm].** *In the field model, a step that moves a captured domain's share, rule support or own conversion without bringing an inbound gain below one from the domains that remain captured is re-absorbed once the domain is re-seeded ({{reabsorb}}), and a step that brings it below one must be held for the dwell of {{dwell}}, or the state returns across the separatrix. A graduated sequence therefore crosses the separatrix only where some step crosses its own threshold and is held.* The trust the earlier steps build changes $\varpi$ in the game of {{assure}}; it changes the field only where it is spent on such a step. That is the part of graduated reciprocation (Osgood 1962) that survives capture: it answers a coordination failure, and it moves capture only where a step lands past a threshold and stays.

### Reachability as a capture basin

**{{!Remark:capturebasin}} (What a theorem of reachability would say) [def].** In the language of viability theory (Aubin 1991; Cardaliaguet, Quincampoix and Saint-Pierre 1999), a target is *reachable* from the present state if that state lies in the viable capture basin: the set from which some admissible control brings the system to the target while the state stays viable throughout; the states from which it is reached within a time $T$ form the capture basin of horizon $T$. For this paper the state is the field of Section 4 together with the host of Section 3, the admissible controls are the corrections the transition can apply (insulating rule-making, provision, sharing, custody), and the target is the configuration without capture, with the host above its floor. *Proved:* in the one-domain field model with insulation as the only control, the basin of absence is reached from the captured state iff insulation is held for the $T_{\min}$ of {{dwell}}, which is therefore the minimum capture time; with a floor under a recruitable alternative as a second control, the minimum time is bounded whatever the age of capture ({{dwell}}(4)). *Not proved:* the basin for several domains with all controls, with disturbances, and jointly with the host's floor. That is why {{reachable}} is a table of requirements with the support each has, and not a capture-basin proposition.

### The tracks and their time constants

Each track of Section 11.4 rests on a slow variable, and the order in which the tracks start follows their time constants.
- *Track 1, the shared account of hazards*, rests on shared understanding, which cannot run ahead of the evidence: for a class whose evidence arrives only after a manifestation window, no measurement shortens it ({{tempo}}). Its instruments, evaluation suites and incident channels, are the fastest of the five to build, which is why agreement among independent measurements is the first indicator that can move.
- *Track 2, narrow commitments*, rests on human capacity $\mathcal M$, which decays at $\delta_h$ under delegation and recovers from it in $\varrho_h^{-1}\ln\big[(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M)/(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M_{\min})\big]$ ({{lockin}}); kept in parallel it removes the lock at a cost in strength, and where correction rests on it, it also sets how fast correction must grow ({{humancorr}}).
- *Track 3, correction and shared services*, rests on rule support in each corrector domain, with the dwell of {{dwell}}. If $1/(\varepsilon d)$ is an institutional turnover of five to ten years, an assumption and not an estimate, the dwells computed there run from about a decade to more than a century [num: DW, with the assumed turnover]. That is why this track starts first and cannot wait for the trust of Track 4.
- *Track 4, delegation, access, custody and decision rights*, rests on the capability held in check, whose growth raises the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}), against the clock $T_{\rm dec}$ to a decisive position ({{tdec}}); custody's installation and verification must take less than that time, so its preparation runs furthest ahead of need.
- *Track 5, broadening*, rests on the record of the arrangements under stress, which accrues only through cycles of stress and recovery, and on the self-models formed inside them, which change over generations, a time the field does not model [conj]. After a recovery, mobilization falls back and certificates can fail again ({{countervail}}), the regulatory cycle; openness of membership has to be built in from Track 1, since a bloc that closes early is hard to open later [conj].

### {{reachable}}, row by row

*Existence* rests on {{commonfrontn}}, {{participate}} and {{safeguards}}; *reachability* on {{assure}} with {{relaxtrust}}, {{survassure}}, {{delegatelock}} with $q_L=1$, {{signal}} and {{ratchet}}; *survival of the transient* on {{safeguards}}(1), {{lockin}}, {{coverage}}, {{restraint}} and the two clocks of {{slowroute}}; and *persistence* on {{overhang}}, {{certificate}}, {{rivalverify}}, {{reabsorb}}, {{dwell}} and {{unravel}}. The table claims no more than its rows. No result shows that the four can be met together, and graduated steps are shown to reach only as a ratchet ({{ratchet}}); {{capturebasin}} states what a proof of reachability would have to cover.
