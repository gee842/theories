## S2. The conditions of winning

### Proof of {{loopgain}}

*Steady state.* At equal standing every $p_i=1/n$, so each stock satisfies $X^{j\ast}=(1-d_j)X^{j\ast}+\kappa_j\Omega/n+k_{0j}$, that is, $X^{j\ast}=(\kappa_j\Omega/n+k_{0j})/d_j$.

*Linearization.* Write $x_i^j=X_i^j-X^{j\ast}$ and split it into its mean over carriers, $\bar x^j$, and the deviation $y_i^j=x_i^j-\bar x^j$, with $\sum_iy_i^j=0$. At the symmetric point $\partial p_i/\partial\sigma_i=(n-1)/n^2$ and $\partial p_i/\partial\sigma_k=-1/n^2$, so $dp_i=\frac1n\big(d\sigma_i-\frac1n\sum_kd\sigma_k\big)=\frac1n\sum_jv_jy_i^j$, where $v_j=\partial\sigma/\partial X^j\ge0$ at $X^\ast$; the common parts cancel. Hence
$$\bar x^{j\prime}=(1-d_j)\bar x^j,\qquad y_i^{\prime}=\mathcal Jy_i,\qquad \mathcal J=\mathcal D+\tfrac1n\mathbf u\mathbf v^\top,$$
with $\mathcal D=\operatorname{diag}(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})$, $\mathbf u=(u_j)$, $u_j=(1-\beta)\Omega\kappa_j$, and $\mathbf v=(v_j)$. The mean deviations decay since $0<d_j\le1$. Every carrier's deviation vector evolves by the same matrix $\mathcal J$, a rank-one perturbation of a diagonal matrix: every stock is fed by the one win share, which is why the channels add.

*Spectral radius.* $\mathcal J\ge0$ because $d_j+\delta_{f,j}\le1$ and $\mathbf u,\mathbf v\ge0$. If $u_jv_j=0$ for all $j$, the spectral radius of $\mathcal J$ is $\max_j(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})<1$, and $\Gamma=0$. Otherwise an eigenvalue $\Lambda$ of $\mathcal J$ that is not a diagonal entry of $\mathcal D$ satisfies the secular equation
$${{!eq:secular}}\qquad 1=\frac1n\sum_j\frac{u_jv_j}{\Lambda-(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})}.$$
For real $\Lambda$ above every diagonal entry of the channels with $u_jv_j>0$, the right side decreases strictly from $+\infty$ to $0$, so there is exactly one such root $\Lambda^\ast$; by Perron–Frobenius the spectral radius of the nonnegative matrix $\mathcal J$ is the larger of $\Lambda^\ast$ and the diagonal entries of the channels with $u_jv_j=0$, which are below one. So the spectral radius is below one iff $\Lambda^\ast<1$, that is, iff the right side at $\Lambda=1$ is below one: $\frac1n\sum_ju_jv_j/(d_j+\delta_{f,j})<1$.

*Simplification.* Since $r_j=v_jX^{j\ast}$, $\frac1nu_jv_j=(1-\beta)\frac{\kappa_j\Omega}{n}\frac{r_jd_j}{\kappa_j\Omega/n+k_{0j}}=(1-\beta)r_j\omega_jd_j$. So the condition is $\Gamma<1$, which proves (1); for $\Gamma>1$, the deviations $y_1=\mathbf z$, $y_2=-\mathbf z$ along the Perron vector $\mathbf z$ grow by $\Lambda^\ast>1$ a period. Part (2) is read off $\Gamma$.

*The price of standing.* Let an actor's relative net payoff today be $y$, reinvested so that its stock deviations rise by $\kappa_jy$. From the next period its relative deviation evolves by $\mathcal J$, and its winnings exceed the mean by $(1-\beta)\Omega\,dp=\frac{(1-\beta)\Omega}{n}\mathbf v^\top x_t$ per period. The present value is $\frac\delta n\mathbf v^\top\sum_{t\ge0}(\delta\mathcal J)^t\mathbf u\,y$. The series converges iff the spectral radius of $\delta\mathcal J$ is below one, which by the secular argument applied to $\delta\mathcal J$ holds iff $\frac\delta n\sum_ju_jv_j/(1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j}))<1$, that is, iff $\Gamma_\delta<1$. There, with $A=I-\delta\mathcal D$, Sherman–Morrison gives $(A-\frac\delta n\mathbf u\mathbf v^\top)^{-1}\mathbf u=A^{-1}\mathbf u/(1-\frac\delta n\mathbf v^\top A^{-1}\mathbf u)$, so the present value is $\frac{\Gamma_\delta}{1-\Gamma_\delta}y$ with $\Gamma_\delta=\frac\delta n\sum_ju_jv_j/(1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j}))$, which is the stated form; it grows without bound as $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$. Finally $\delta(d_j+\delta_{f,j})<1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})$ for $\delta<1$, so $\Gamma_\delta<\Gamma$, and $\Gamma_\delta\to\Gamma$ as $\delta\to1$.

*Beyond the domain.* The Perron root of $\delta\mathcal J$ is $\delta\Lambda^\ast$, so $\delta\Lambda^\ast<1$ iff $\Gamma_\delta<1$. Take one channel (with several, the same argument runs along the Perron direction). The map {{eq:stocks}} is increasing in a carrier's own stock, so a positive relative perturbation $\varepsilon$ stays positive; write it $y_{t+1}=f(y_t)$ with $f(0)=0$, $f'(0)=\Lambda^\ast$, write the actor's extra winnings $w(y)$ with $w'(0)>0$, and $PV(\varepsilon)=\sum_{t\ge1}\delta^tw(y_t)$. Fix $\eta>0$ small enough for the estimates below on $|y|\le\eta$.
- *If $\delta\Lambda^\ast<1$:* fix $\Lambda'$ with $\Lambda^\ast<\Lambda'<1/\delta$. While $|y_t|\le\eta$, $|y_t|\le C\Lambda'^t\varepsilon$ and the error of linearization is $O(y_t^2)$; after $T=\ln(\eta/\varepsilon)/\ln\Lambda'$ periods the tail is at most $\delta^T\sup|w|/(1-\delta)=O(\varepsilon^{\ln(1/\delta)/\ln\Lambda'})=o(\varepsilon)$, since the exponent exceeds one. So $PV(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\to\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$: the first-order price holds on the whole domain $\Gamma_\delta<1$, including $\Gamma>1>\Gamma_\delta$, where equal standing is unstable and discounting still wins; there the convergence is slow, of order $\varepsilon^{-\ln(\delta\Lambda^\ast)/\ln\Lambda^\ast}$.
- *If $\delta\Lambda^\ast>1$:* choose $\Lambda''<\Lambda^\ast$ with $\delta\Lambda''>1$. For $t\le T=\ln(\eta/\varepsilon)/\ln\Lambda''$, $y_t\ge c\Lambda''^t\varepsilon$, so $PV(\varepsilon)\ge c'\sum_{t\le T}(\delta\Lambda'')^t\varepsilon\ge c''\eta\,\delta^T=c'''\varepsilon^{\ln(1/\delta)/\ln\Lambda''}$, an exponent below one, and $PV(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\to\infty$. Conversely, with $\Lambda'>\Lambda^\ast$ and $T'=\ln(\eta/\varepsilon)/\ln\Lambda'$, the sum up to $T'$ is at most of order $\varepsilon(\delta\Lambda')^{T'}=\eta\,\delta^{T'}$ and the tail at most $\delta^{T'}\sup|w|/(1-\delta)$, both $O(\varepsilon^{\ln(1/\delta)/\ln\Lambda'})$, so $PV(\varepsilon)\to0$: the value of standing is continuous at equal standing.
- *If $\delta\Lambda^\ast=1$:* the same bound gives $PV(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\ge c\ln(1/\varepsilon)$.

The value of standing is therefore continuous at equal standing with infinite slope when $\Gamma_\delta\ge1$. The printed multiplier $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$, negative there, means nothing, and no finite horizon recovers a first-order price, since the price over a horizon $T$ grows without bound in $T$.

*Proof of (4).*
- *Rank one survives.* In a symmetric Markov equilibrium the equilibrium win share is $p^{\rm eq}_i(X)=P(X_i;\{X_k\}_{k\ne i})$, symmetric in the others' stocks. Linearize at equal standing: $dp_i=\sum_ja_j\,dX_i^j+\sum_jb_j\sum_{k\ne i}dX_k^j$. Since $\sum_ip_i\equiv1$, perturbing one carrier's stock $j$ alone gives $a_j+(n-1)b_j=0$. Hence $dp_i=\sum_ja_j\frac n{n-1}\big(dX_i^j-\overline{dX^j}\big)$: the equilibrium share responds to relative deviations only.
- *Same dynamics.* The deviation dynamics are therefore exactly $\mathcal D+\frac1n\mathbf u\mathbf v^{\rm eq\top}$, with $v^{\rm eq}_j=a_jn^2/(n-1)$ and $r^{\rm eq}_j=v^{\rm eq}_jX^{j\ast}$, which is the definition in the statement, scaled as $r_j=v_jX^{j\ast}$ is. Mean deviations still decay at $1-d_j$, because $\sum p\equiv1$. By premise $\mathbf v^{\rm eq}\ge0$, so the Perron argument of (1) applies unchanged, and (2) is read off. If some $v^{\rm eq}_j<0$, a catch-up stronger than the structural advantage, $\mathcal J$ is no longer nonnegative and $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$ alone does not decide.
- *Price.* Apply the envelope theorem to the actor's value, differentiable at equal standing by premise. Its own intensity is optimal, so the intensity's response drops out at first order in every period, and the derivative of the value follows the state with the actor's own intensity held as a number and the rivals' at their Markov policies. A relative shift (own stocks up by $\varepsilon$, each other carrier's down by $\varepsilon/(n-1)$) leaves totals unchanged, since $\sum p\equiv1$ whatever the actor does, so it stays a relative shift, with $dp_i=(r^{\rm pr}_j/(nX^{j\ast}))\,y^j$. The present value is then the Sherman–Morrison expression of (3) with $\mathbf v^{\rm pr}$ in place of $\mathbf v$, under the same condition $\Gamma^{\rm pr}_\delta<1$, plus $-\ell\sum_{k\ne i}\partial e_k/\partial X_i$ per period when intensity $e$ imposes a shared loss $\ell$ per unit: a lead that makes rivals spend less is worth $\ell$ per unit of their reduction. $\square$

*Numerics.* Block LG: across 4,000 random two-channel systems the spectral radius of the numerical Jacobian agrees with the criterion in every case (four within $10^{-3}$ of the boundary set aside); nonlinear simulations from a $10^{-4}$ perturbation grow or decay as $\Gamma$ is above or below one, including a case with $r=0.8$ in which a reflexive channel lifts $\Gamma$ to 1.1; the present value of a perturbation in the nonlinear model matches $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ to a relative error below $2\times10^{-6}$, and converges to it in twenty systems with $\Gamma\ge1>\Gamma_\delta$. Block CH: two reviewers' independent scripts, with one to three stocks entering strength by logarithm, linear and square-root forms, find no mismatch in 2,996 and 1,489 random systems, and the price on three mixed stocks matches to $7\times10^{-7}$. Block PD: in an outside reviewer's boundary case ($n=2$, $\kappa\Omega=1$, $r=2$, $d=0.2$, $\delta=0.9$, so $\Gamma=2$, $\Gamma_\delta=9/7$ and $\Lambda^\ast=1.2$) the present value is positive at every $\varepsilon$, while the printed multiplier is $-4.5$; the fitted exponent of $PV(\varepsilon)$ is 0.5628, 0.5758, 0.5776, 0.5778 for $\varepsilon=10^{-2},\dots,10^{-8}$, against the predicted $\ln(1/0.9)/\ln1.2=0.5779$. Inside the domain but above $\Gamma=1$ ($\delta=0.8$, $\Gamma_\delta=0.889$), $PV/\varepsilon$ rises from 7.163 to 7.987 as $\varepsilon$ falls from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-12}$, converging to the formula's 8.000 at the predicted rate $\varepsilon^{0.224}$; at $\delta\Lambda^\ast=1$ it grows by a constant 8.42 every two decades, a logarithm. Block MP: {{eqgain}}.

*Bounds.*
- $\Gamma$ is evaluated at equal standing. Off parity the reflexive channel's gain is higher: the return to influence over correctors grows with the square of share ({{influence}}), and the conversion that selection cultivates is proportional to $\sum_kp_k^{1+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$ ({{corrrate}}(4)). With $\eta=1$, $\xi_v=0$ and four carriers, a leader share of 0.6 gives $\sum_kp_k^3=0.223$ against $1/16$ at parity, about 3.6 times [num: arithmetic of {{corrrate}}(4)].
- The certificate of {{certificate}}(2), a row sum of conversion gains below one, has the same form, but is evaluated at a captured state, not at equal standing; the two are of one form, not one theorem.

**{{!Remark:tdec}} (Time to decisiveness) [thm; num].** In the linearized single-channel loop a relative gap grows by the factor
$$\Lambda^\ast=1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$$
a period; with several channels, by the Perron root $\Lambda^\ast$ of {{eq:secular}}, which is not a function of $\Gamma$ alone. **$\Gamma$ decides the sign of a gap's growth; the turnover of the stocks, through $\Lambda^\ast$, its speed.** A gap grows from $\varepsilon_0$ to a decisive level $\bar\Delta$ in about $T_{\rm dec}=\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast$ periods.
- From 1% to 50% at $\Gamma=3$, $d_K=0.2$, $\delta_f=0$: 11.6 periods in the linearization, 13 in the nonlinear model. At $\Gamma=1.2$, a channel turning over at $d_K+\delta_f=0.5$ needs 41 periods and one turning over at 0.02 needs 980. With two channels at $\Gamma=1.2$, turnovers $(0.3,0.02)$ give $\Lambda^\ast=1.0088$ and $(0.1,0.1)$ give $1.0200$; $(0.5,0.02)$ gives $1.0092$ and 427 periods [num: TD].
- With diffusion the nonlinear gap can settle short of decisive: at $\Gamma=1.5$, $d_K=\delta_f=0.05$, $r=3$, a 1% gap has not reached 50% after 10,000 periods, where the linearization predicts 80 [num: TD].

This is the channel through which a larger loop gain raises the breakout probability $p_d$ of Section 5.2. Where punishment is triggered by detection and acts before decisiveness, $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$: the probability that a breakout is not detected and answered within the time $T_{\rm dec}$ it needs to become decisive ({{detectlink}}). In the interfaces of S13 it is $\Lambda^\ast$, not $\Gamma$, that sets $T_{\rm dec}$. *Proof.* With one channel, $y'=(1-d-\delta_f)y+(1-\beta)\kappa\Omega\frac{r}{nX^\ast}y$, and $(1-\beta)\kappa\Omega r/(nX^\ast)=(1-\beta)r\omega d=(d+\delta_f)\Gamma$. V6.1's factor $1+d_K(\Gamma-1)$ omitted $\delta_f$: at $d_K=0.2$, $\delta_f=0.1$, $\Gamma=4/3$ it gives 1.067 where the exact factor is 1.100 [num: TD]. $\square$

**{{!Remark:eqgain}} (Structural and equilibrium gain) [thm; num].** {{loopgain}}(4) keeps the form of the loop gain when rivals respond; this remark gives its numbers. The contest is two-actor: each round is a Tullock lottery with chosen effort, and the winner converts its prize into capacity by {{eq:stocks}}; equilibria are symmetric Markov-perfect, computed by value iteration with a global best-response gap at parity below $10^{-16}$ [num: MP]. That a lead discourages the follower is the discouragement effect of dynamic contests (Harris and Vickers 1987; Konrad and Kovenock 2009); what is added here is its effect on the loop gain and on the price of standing.
1. *Two elasticities.* Stability is decided by $r^{\rm eq}$, which counts every actor's response to a shift of standing; the price by $r^{\rm pr}$, which holds the actor's own effort at its equilibrium level. They differ by the actor's own response, which the envelope theorem removes from the price and not from the dynamics. So the price is not $\Gamma^{\rm eq}_\delta/(1-\Gamma^{\rm eq}_\delta)$, which is negative or infinite wherever $\Gamma^{\rm eq}_\delta\ge1$ while the computed price stays finite [thm]. In the lottery the expected relative deviation at equal standing evolves by $1+d(\Gamma^{\rm eq}-1)$, matched to five digits; with small jumps ($d=0.02$) and no shared loss the computed price equals $\Gamma^{\rm pr}_\delta/(1-\Gamma^{\rm pr}_\delta)$ to four digits in five cases. With others' strategies held fixed instead, the exact price at the paper's jumps equals $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$: what follows comes from the rivals' response, not from the size of the jumps [num: MP].
2. *A static factor.* In a Tullock contest among $n$ with effort, a carrier's equilibrium payoff $\Omega w_i^2$ responds to its weight $\alpha_i$, with its own effort held and its rivals' responding, as $2\Omega(n-1)^2/(\alpha n^3)$, against $\Omega(n-1)/(n^2\alpha)$ with all efforts held: $r^{\rm pr}/r=2(n-1)/n$, which is 1, 1.333, 1.5, 1.667 and 1.8 for $n=2,3,4,6,10$ [thm; num: MP]. A two-period game whose second-period effort responds to capacity gives the same factor [num: MP]. It is a within-period response of rivals and vanishes at $n=2$; the amplification below, at $n=2$, is dynamic, from the convexity of the value in the state. Neither is "the" factor.
3. *Magnitudes* ($n=2$, $d=0.1$, $\delta=1/1.1$, so that $\Gamma_\delta=\Gamma/2$) [num: MP]:

| Structural $\Gamma$ | $r^{\rm eq}/r$ | $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$ | Computed price ÷ structural price | Envelope formula with measured $r^{\rm pr}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 1.09 | 0.22 | 1.05 | within 0.2% |
| 0.3 | 1.18 | 0.35 | 1.10 | within 0.5% |
| 0.4 | 1.41 | 0.56 | 1.24 | within 2.3% |
| 0.45 | 2.48 | 1.11 | 1.77 | not applicable |
| 0.6 | 4.72 | 2.83 | 2.66 | not applicable |
| 0.8 | 5.09 | 4.08 | 2.79 | not applicable |

Where $\Gamma^{\rm eq}>1$ the equilibrium leaves parity and the first-order price of {{loopgain}}(4) no longer predicts the computed one (0.64 against 0.51 at a structural gain of 0.45). With a shared loss of intensity ($\ell=0.5$, $\Gamma=0.3$) the computed price is 0.2201 and the envelope formula, with the rivals' shared-loss term, gives 0.2221. At larger jumps ($d=0.2$) the computed price at parity is 1.12 times its structural value at $\Gamma=0.3$, 2.4 to 2.6 times at $\Gamma=0.6$, 2.19 times at $\Gamma=0.9$, and 4.75 times at $\Gamma=0.6$ with a shared loss; at a structural $\Gamma=0.6$ the equilibrium gain is 1.96, and the spread of standing after 60 periods is 0.198 against 0.120 under the structural lottery. With more patience ($\delta=0.97$, $r=1.5$, $\Gamma=0.6$, $d=0.2$) the factor reaches 7.87; with larger jumps ($r=1$) $r^{\rm eq}/r$ reaches 5.41 at $\Gamma=0.9$ [num: MP].
4. *Multiplicity* [num: MP; held for outside review]. Where the contest runs in many small rounds, value iteration started from zero and from a step at parity converges to different fixed points. At $\Gamma=0.2$, $d=0.05$, the first gives the smooth equilibrium (price 0.1169, 1.05 times the structural value) and the second a winner-take-all one (price 1.392 on a grid of 3,000 cells and 1.410 on 6,000, about 12.5 times the structural value), both converged to $10^{-11}$; at $\Gamma=0.3$ the two prices are 0.1969 and 1.551. At $d=0.1$ the step start returns to the smooth equilibrium. The same structural gain then admits an equilibrium in which a lead is decisive and one in which it is not, and the belief that a lead is decisive selects the equilibrium in which it is. What needs outside review: that the winner-take-all fixed point is an equilibrium of the continuous-state game and not an artefact of the grid; the size of round and the gain at which it appears; and whether it exists in the deterministic version of {{eq:stocks}}. If it survives that review, it gives formal content to the sense in which Ψ is partly made true (Section 3.8) [conj].
5. *Catch-up* [num: MP; held for outside review]. With a flow cost of standing below the top strong enough that a laggard tries harder ($L_{\rm sub}=100$), $r^{\rm eq}/r$ falls to 0.993 at $\Gamma=0.3$, so the strategic correction changes sign there, and to 3.42 at $\Gamma=0.6$, where it does not. Which contests in a given field are of the discouraging and which of the catching-up kind is an empirical question.
6. *Off parity.* With $r=1$, where the structural price is the same at every state, the laggard's price at a share of 0.25 is 57% of the structural value at $\Gamma=0.3$ and 25% at $\Gamma=0.6$; the leader's at 0.75 is 153% at $\Gamma=0.3$. At the larger jumps the laggard's price at a quarter share is 0.065 against 1.62 at parity, and negative ($-0.039$) with a shared loss [num: MP]. The laggard stops contesting standing: the exclusion of {{compound}}(2), seen from the laggard's side.

The form of {{loopgain}} survives strategic response; its number does not. The structural gain is a benchmark from which the equilibrium gain departs upward where leads discourage and downward where catching up is rewarded.

**{{!Remark:delayosc}} (A countervailing channel with delay) [thm; num].** Let one channel be reinforcing and one countervailing, so that in $\mathcal J=\mathcal D+\frac1n\mathbf u\mathbf v^\top$ the products $g_j=u_jv_j/n$ have opposite signs: a stock that success provokes and that lowers the provoker's strength, such as the mobilization of those harmed against a leader, or correction that arrives late. $\mathcal J$ is then not nonnegative, the criterion of {{loopgain}}(1) does not apply, and a net gain $\sum_jg_j/(d_j+\delta_{f,j})$ below one does not ensure stability. With two channels the characteristic polynomial is $(\Lambda-\mathcal D_1)(\Lambda-\mathcal D_2)-g_1(\Lambda-\mathcal D_2)-g_2(\Lambda-\mathcal D_1)$; when its roots are complex, their modulus is $\sqrt{\det\mathcal J}=\sqrt{\mathcal D_1\mathcal D_2+g_1\mathcal D_2+g_2\mathcal D_1}$. Take $g_1=0.5$ with $\mathcal D_1=0.6$, and $g_2=-0.05$ with $\mathcal D_2=0.95$: the net gain is $1.25-1=0.25$, the roots are $1\pm0.1225i$, of modulus $\sqrt{1.015}=1.0075$, and deviations oscillate with growing amplitude and a period of 51.6 rounds, reaching 292 times a unit start after 600 [num: CH]. A balancing loop that answers late overshoots. Correction therefore cannot be subtracted from $\Gamma$, and the regulatory cycle of {{countervail}} can diverge as well as lock in. What a balancing channel does to the loop is a question of its delay, not only of its gain.

**{{!Proposition:chansign}} (Which levers lower the loop gain) [thm].** *Let stock $j$ act through strength $h_j(X^j)$, so that $r_j=h_j'(X^{j\ast})X^{j\ast}$, and let $\xi_{r,j}=d\ln r_j/d\ln X^j$ at equal standing be the level elasticity of decisiveness. Ratio channels ($h=r\ln X$, as capacity in a Tullock contest) have $\xi_r=0$; linear level channels ($h=aX$, as rule support in the field model of Section 4) have $\xi_r=1$.*
1. *Sharing $\beta$ and diffusion $\delta_{f,j}$ lower $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma_\delta$ in every channel.*
2. *A faster rate of decay or correction $d_j$ changes $\Gamma$ with the sign of $\delta_{f,j}/(d_j+\delta_{f,j})-\xi_{r,j}$, and $\Gamma_\delta$ with the sign of $(1-\delta+\delta\delta_{f,j})/(1-\delta+\delta d_j+\delta\delta_{f,j})-\xi_{r,j}$. Correction therefore lowers both gains wherever $\xi_{r,j}\ge1$, as in level channels. In ratio channels it leaves $\Gamma$ unchanged without diffusion, raises it with diffusion, and always raises $\Gamma_\delta$: a stock that turns over faster is made more of recent winnings.*
3. *Provision not won, $k_{0j}$, lowers the gain through stock $j$ iff $\xi_{r,j}<1$. In a linear level channel it adds the same to everyone and changes nothing; where decisiveness rises faster than in proportion to the stock ($\xi_{r,j}>1$) it raises the gain.*
4. *The convertible share $\kappa_j$ raises the gain through stock $j$ iff $1+(\xi_{r,j}-1)\omega_j>0$: through ratio channels only when $k_{0j}>0$, and through level channels always.*

*Proof.* At equal standing $X^\ast=c/d$ with $c=\kappa\Omega/n+k_0$, and $\omega=(\kappa\Omega/n)/(dX^\ast)$, so each channel's term of {{eq:gamma}} is $(1-\beta)r\omega\frac d{d+\delta_f}=(1-\beta)\frac{\kappa\Omega}n\cdot\frac{r(X^\ast)}{X^\ast}\cdot\frac1{d+\delta_f}$, and in $\Gamma_\delta$ the last factor is $\delta/(1-\delta+\delta d+\delta\delta_f)$. (1) $\beta$ enters only through $1-\beta$ (the mean inflow $\kappa\Omega/n$ does not depend on it), and $\delta_f$ only through the last factor. (2) $d\ln(r/X^\ast)/d\ln X^\ast=\xi_r-1$ and $d\ln X^\ast/d\ln d=-1$, so the term's elasticity in $d$ is $1-\xi_r-d/(d+\delta_f)=\delta_f/(d+\delta_f)-\xi_r$, and in $\Gamma_\delta$ it is $1-\xi_r-\delta d/(1-\delta+\delta d+\delta\delta_f)$, the stated sign; both thresholds are below one when $d>0$. (3) $k_0$ moves only $X^\ast$, and $r/X^\ast$ falls in $X^\ast$ iff $\xi_r<1$. (4) The term's elasticity in $\kappa$ is $1+(\xi_r-1)\,d\ln X^\ast/d\ln\kappa=1+(\xi_r-1)\omega$. $\square$

*Check (CH).* The growth factor of the relative mode, simulated in the nonlinear model for five channel forms (logarithmic, linear, $X^{0.5}$, $X^2$, $\ln(1+X)$), with and without diffusion, agrees in sign with the rule in every case, and $\Gamma$ read off it equals {{eq:gamma}} to $10^{-3}$. Logarithmic channel: $\Gamma=0.8571\to0.9643$ when $d$ rises from 0.2 to 0.3 with $\delta_f=0.1$, and unchanged at 1.2857 without diffusion. Linear channel: $1.125\to0.750$. Raising $k_0$ from 0.1 to 0.2: the logarithmic channel's gain falls from 1.2857 to 1.0000, the linear channel's stays at 1.125, the $X^2$ channel's rises from 1.75 to 2.25. A ratio channel's $\Gamma_\delta$ is 0.853, 1.157 and 1.409 at $d=0.1,0.2,0.4$ ($\delta=0.9$) while its $\Gamma$ is unchanged. An independent spectral computation ($n=2$, $\kappa\Omega=1$, $k_0=0.2$, $\delta_f=0.1$): raising $d$ from 0.1 to 0.6 takes a ratio channel's gain from 0.71 to 1.22 and its spectral radius from 0.943 to 1.157, so equal standing becomes unstable, while it takes a level channel's gain from 3.0 to 0.86 and its radius from 1.40 to 0.90 [num: CH].

The proposition is why the levers of Section 2.2 attach to channels: provision to capacity, correction and non-appropriability to rule support, sharing and diffusion to all. The field model's correction rate in $G_{ij}=a_ib_{ij}/(c_id_i)$ is of the level kind; the depreciation of capacity in {{eq:stocks}} is of the ratio kind. A shared symbol $d$ is not a shared mechanism, and the tables of Section 7.1 and S7 split the row: correction of rule support lowers the gain and the drift of the working objective; faster turnover of capacity leaves the gain unchanged without diffusion and raises it with diffusion, raises the discounted gain and so narrows the working objective, and, where $\Gamma>1$, opens leads faster, since the gap factor of {{tdec}} rises with $d$. Provision acts through capacity.

**{{!Remark:gammarange}} (What $\Gamma>1$ asks, at illustrative values) [num].** $\Gamma$ is not estimated for any channel. For a single ratio channel with nothing shared and no provision ($\beta=0$, $\omega=1$), {{eq:gamma}} gives $\Gamma=rd/(d+\delta_f)$, so leads compound iff $r>1+\delta_f/d$. At the diffusion rate used to illustrate what a lead buys (0.1 a month; {{lead}}) and depreciation of 0.02 to 0.05 a month, that requires decisiveness between 3 and 6; at diffusion of 0.05 a month, between 2 and 3.5; where diffusion is blocked, $r>1$ suffices [num: CH]. Provision raises each threshold by the factor $1/\omega$, and sharing by $1/(1-\beta)$; in contests where a lead discourages the follower, strategic response lowers it ({{eqgain}}). A channel of rule support is of the level kind: its decisiveness is $aZ^\ast$, which correction lowers ({{chansign}}), so no bound of this form applies to it, and its gain is higher at the present concentration of standing than at parity (Bounds above). These values are not observations; they show which parameter carries the inequality in each channel. What can be observed is not $\Gamma$ but the ratio of conduct's response to shared harm and to private cost, $1+\theta$, which provision raises in ratio channels; Test Γ (Section 7.4) registers that observable.

### What the loop gain unifies, and what it does not

| Result | A comparative static of $\Gamma$ or $\Gamma_\delta$? |
|---|---|
| Compounding ({{compound}}) | Yes: the single-channel case |
| Which levers lower the gain ({{chansign}}) | Yes: comparative statics of {{eq:gamma}}, channel by channel |
| Rivals who respond ({{loopgain}}(4), {{eqgain}}) | The same form, with $r^{\rm eq}$ for stability and $r^{\rm pr}$ for the price; the magnitudes are not functions of the structural $\Gamma$ |
| The feedback benchmark ({{bench}}) | Of the same form, not a case: a cross-coupled pair through which success and control feed each other, where {{loopgain}} is a rank-one perturbation through the single win share; both are stable iff a loop gain is below one |
| Influence over correctors and certificates ({{influence}}, {{certificate}}) | Of the same form, evaluated at a captured state |
| Working objective, visibility of the host, levies, reneging ({{standprice}}, {{twochan}}) | Yes, through the working self-model: through $\Gamma_\delta$ with a fixed prize, and through the ratio of relative to common return when the prize grows with capacity |
| Talk, between two parties and among three or more ({{talk}}) | Yes, through the working self-model: under a common self-model the effective bias between sender and receiver rises as the self-model narrows |
| The breakout probability and the decisive value in the enforcement condition | Partly: $\Gamma$ decides whether a gap grows and $\Lambda^\ast$ how fast ({{tdec}}); whether a position beyond sanction exists, and detection lags, do not depend on it |
| A countervailing channel with delay ({{delayosc}}) | No: the deviation map is not nonnegative, and stability is not decided by a net gain |
| Tempo and coverage ({{tempo}}, {{coverage}}) | No: they concern the absolute growth of capability |

This is why the core speaks of interconnected conditions with one structural loop gain at their core, and not of a single pressure from which every signature follows.

### Proof of {{compound}}

(1) With one stock and $\sigma_i=r\ln K_i$, $r_K=r$ and $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$. The condition $\Gamma>1$ reads $(1-\beta)r\kappa_c\Omega d_K>(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)(d_K+\delta_f)$; with $S^\ast=(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)/d_K$ this is $\kappa_c\Omega((1-\beta)r-1)>nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$, and $\Gamma<1$ is the reverse. With $k_0=\delta_f=0$ it is $(1-\beta)r>1$, independent of $\kappa_c$. At equality $\Gamma=1$, the linearization is neutral, and neither {{loopgain}} nor this proposition decides.

(2) *The laggard's inflow.* With the others at $\bar K$ and the laggard at $K$, the mean is $\bar X=(K+(n-1)\bar K)/n$, so diffusion adds $-\delta_f(K-\bar X)=\delta_f\frac{n-1}n(\bar K-K)$ to the laggard's inflow; V6.1 dropped this term. Adding the contest inflow, provision and depreciation gives $\phi$.

*(i)* At the cap $\phi(\bar K)=\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0-d_K\bar K=d_K(K^\ast-\bar K)$. For $\bar K<K^\ast$ this is positive, so by continuity $\phi>0$ just below the cap and a laggard there returns. At $\bar K=K^\ast$, $\phi(K^\ast)=0$, and the sign just below the cap is that of $-\phi'(K^\ast)$, with
$$\phi'(K^\ast)=\frac{(1-\beta)\kappa_c\Omega\,r(n-1)}{n^2K^\ast}-d_K-\delta_f\frac{n-1}n=d_K\Big[\Gamma_0\frac{n-1}n-1\Big]-\delta_f\frac{n-1}n,$$
using $\kappa_c\Omega/n=d_K\omega K^\ast$. This gives (i). The equality of the cap is the interesting boundary: without diffusion, for $1<\Gamma<n/(n-1)$ equal standing is unstable without a cap, yet a cap at exactly $K^\ast$ holds a laggard, because the cap stops the leaders from pulling away.

*(ii)* With the others held, the laggard's capacity follows the scalar map $K'=\min\{K+\phi(K),\bar K\}$, so it reaches the cap from $K$ iff $\phi>0$ on $(K,\bar K)$, and from every start iff $\phi>0$ on $(0,\bar K)$. As $K\to0^+$ with $r>1$ the contest term is $O(K^r)=o(K)$, so $\phi(K)=\phi(0^+)-d_KK+o(K)$ with $\phi(0^+)=\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0+\delta_f(n-1)\bar K/n$; if this is zero, $\phi<0$ near zero and laggards below a threshold are excluded. If it is positive, exclusion needs $\phi$ to dip below zero somewhere, which $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0\ge d_K\bar K$ rules out: $\phi$ is at least the linear function $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0+\delta_f\frac{n-1}n(\bar K-K)-d_KK$, which decreases in $K$ and is non-negative at $\bar K$, hence positive on $(0,\bar K)$.

*Leaders held.* A leader's inflow at the cap is at least $\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0-d_K\bar K-\delta_f\bar K/n$, since its win share is at least $1/n$ and its loss to diffusion is $\delta_f(\bar K-K)/n\le\delta_f\bar K/n$; this is non-negative iff $\bar K\le(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)/(nd_K+\delta_f)$, the Scope's bound. $\square$ At $\bar K=K^\ast$ with diffusion the bound fails, and the leaders are held only locally: with the laggard a small $x$ below the cap, a leader's net inflow is $(x/n)(d_K\Gamma_0-\delta_f)$ to first order, so for $\delta_f>d_K\Gamma_0$ the leaders leave the cap with the laggard. The local statements of (i) were therefore also checked in simulations in which every carrier moves under the cap, and agree in every case computed (below).

*Growth variants.* (a) *Fixed stakes, growing capacity.* Without depreciation, total capacity $S$ grows by $\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0$ a period, and the share deviation has multiplier $[S(1-\delta_f)+\kappa_c\Omega(1-\beta)r]/(S+\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$. Equal standing is therefore unstable at a date iff $\kappa_c\Omega((1-\beta)r-1)>\delta_fS+nk_0$ with $S$ the current total, a condition that changes with time. (b) *Stakes proportional to capacity.* With $\Omega_t=gS_t$, $k_0=0$ and depreciation $d_K$, total capacity grows by the factor $G=1-d_K+\kappa_cg$ a period, and the share deviation has multiplier $m=\big[(1-d_K-\delta_f)+\kappa_cg(1-\beta)r\big]/G$. So equal shares are unstable iff $\kappa_cg((1-\beta)r-1)>\delta_f$, a condition constant in time, and without diffusion iff $(1-\beta)r>1$. That is the condition under which the working objective narrows when the prize is proportional to capacity ({{standprice}}): without diffusion, narrowing and instability coincided in 24 of 24 simulated cases [num: SP]. With diffusion the two thresholds part: equal shares become unstable only at $(1-\beta)r>1+\delta_f/(\kappa_cg)$, while the objective narrows from a lower threshold, derived in S3; at $r=1.17$, $\delta_f=0.03$ the objective narrows while equal shares are stable [num: SP].

*Numerics (CE).*
- *Cap equality.* Ten cases of the local condition at $\bar K=K^\ast$, simulated with every carrier moving under the cap, each predicted and observed in agreement. Among them, $n=4$, $r=2$: with $\delta_f=0.3$ the laggard returns, with $\delta_f=0.1$ it falls to a stalled $0.48K^\ast$, with $\delta_f=0$ it is excluded; with provision $k_0=0.05$ and $\Gamma_0(n-1)/n=1.026$ it falls away and stalls at $0.41K^\ast$. An outside reviewer's case ($n=2$, $r=3$, cap at $K^\ast=2.5$, no sharing, provision or diffusion): from $(2.499,2.5)$ the laggard falls to $2.4\times10^{-32}$. Over 400 random settings the criterion at $\bar K=K^\ast$ predicts the simulated outcome in 400; without diffusion, a laggard at a cap equal to $K^\ast$ returns iff $\Gamma<n/(n-1)$ in four further cases.
- *Strict caps.* Caps at $0.98K^\ast$ and $0.9K^\ast$ at $\Gamma=3$ restore a laggard just below; over 400 random settings a cap at $0.97K^\ast$ is restored in 400.
- *Global criterion.* $\phi>0$ on $(0,\bar K)$ agrees with simulation from 1% of the cap in 444 of 446 random settings with the leaders provably held; in the two others the exclusion threshold (0.7% and 0.2% of the cap) lies below the start, so the criterion, which speaks of every start, is right.
- *Diffusion as a source.* The same reviewer's case $n=2$, $r=2$, $d_K=0.2$, $\delta_f=0.6$, cap $K^\ast=2.5$, no sharing or provision: $\phi$ stays positive on $(0,\bar K)$, its minimum about $10^{-4}$, and $(0.01,2.5)$ goes to $(2.5,2.5)$. *Not a guarantee:* with $\beta=k_0=\delta_f=0$, $r=2$, a laggard starting at 0.3 of a cap at $0.9K^\ast$ is excluded; with $\delta_f=0.05$ alone it falls and stalls at 0.16 of the cap, since $\phi(0^+)=0.056>0$ but $\phi$ dips to $-0.013$.
- *Each inflow suffices alone.* $n=5$, $r=1.5$, cap 0.8: with none of the three, laggards starting at 0.05 and 0.2 are excluded; with $\beta=0.1$, $k_0=0.05$ or $\delta_f=0.15$ alone, all return.
- *A cap below $K^\ast$ (SM).* $n=5$, $r=1.5$, a cap of 1.5 below $K^\ast=2$, and a laggard starting at 0.1: with neither sharing nor provision the laggard is excluded; with $\beta=0.02$ or $k_0=0.002$ it stalls near 0.06; with $\beta=0.2$ or $k_0=0.03$ all reach the cap. The exact condition predicts each case.

**{{!Remark:bench}} (A local feedback benchmark) [thm].** With $u$ a small deviation in a carrier's success and $w$ a deviation in its favourable control over the rules, let $u'=(1-d_u)u+aw$ and $w'=bu+(1-d_w)w$, with cross-gains $a,b\ge0$ and decays $0<d_u,d_w\le1$. The pair is stable exactly when $ab<d_ud_w$, that is, when the loop gain $(a/d_u)(b/d_w)$ is below one. This is the stability condition of Richardson's arms-race model with control over rules in place of armaments (Richardson 1960). It is of the same form as {{loopgain}}, a loop gain below one, but not a case of it: here success and control feed each other directly across a pair, there every stock is fed through the single win share. Its use is that each coefficient names a channel that can be measured. Instability does not prove capture or harm, and stability does not prove that an excursion stays within a safety constraint.

*Proof.* The characteristic polynomial is $(\Lambda-1+d_u)(\Lambda-1+d_w)-ab=0$, with roots $1-\frac{d_u+d_w}2\pm\sqrt{\frac{(d_u-d_w)^2}4+ab}$. The larger root is below one iff $ab<d_ud_w$; then the square root is below $(d_u+d_w)/2$, so the smaller root exceeds $1-(d_u+d_w)\ge-1$. $\square$ The statement is an algebraic identity; block FB keeps it as a regression check against numerically computed spectral radii [num: FB].
