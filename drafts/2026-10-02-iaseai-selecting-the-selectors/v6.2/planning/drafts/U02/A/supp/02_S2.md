## S2. The conditions of winning

### Proof of {{loopgain}}

*Steady state.* At equal standing every $p_i=1/n$, so each stock satisfies $X^{j\ast}=(1-d_j)X^{j\ast}+\kappa_j\Omega/n+k_{0j}$, that is, $X^{j\ast}=(\kappa_j\Omega/n+k_{0j})/d_j$.

*Linearization.* Write $x_i^j=X_i^j-X^{j\ast}$ and split it into its mean over carriers, $\bar x^j$, and the deviation $y_i^j=x_i^j-\bar x^j$, with $\sum_iy_i^j=0$. At the symmetric point $\partial p_i/\partial\sigma_i=(n-1)/n^2$ and $\partial p_i/\partial\sigma_k=-1/n^2$, so $dp_i=\frac1n\big(d\sigma_i-\frac1n\sum_kd\sigma_k\big)=\frac1n\sum_jv_jy_i^j$, where $v_j=\partial\sigma/\partial X^j\ge0$ at $X^\ast$; the common parts cancel. Hence
$$\bar x^{j\prime}=(1-d_j)\bar x^j,\qquad y_i^{\prime}=\mathcal Jy_i,\qquad \mathcal J=\mathcal D+\tfrac1n\mathbf u\mathbf v^\top,$$
with $\mathcal D=\operatorname{diag}(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})$ and $u_j=(1-\beta)\Omega\kappa_j$. The mean deviations decay since $0<d_j\le1$. Every carrier's deviation vector evolves by the same matrix $\mathcal J$, a rank-one perturbation of a diagonal: this is the formal content of "the channels add". A stock fed by an input chosen under another rule, such as lobbying bought from profits by a separate decision, contributes a term not of this form, and the additivity of (1) does not cover it.

*Spectral radius.* $\mathcal J\ge0$ because $d_j+\delta_{f,j}\le1$ and $\mathbf u,\mathbf v\ge0$. If $u_jv_j=0$ for all $j$, then the spectral radius of $\mathcal J$ is $\max_j(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})<1$, and $\Gamma=0$. Otherwise an eigenvalue $\Lambda$ of $\mathcal J$ that is not a diagonal entry of $\mathcal D$ satisfies the secular equation
$${{!eq:secular}}\qquad 1=\frac1n\sum_j\frac{u_jv_j}{\Lambda-(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})}.$$
For real $\Lambda>\max_j\mathcal D_{jj}$ the right side decreases strictly from $+\infty$ to $0$, so there is exactly one such root $\Lambda^\ast$, and by Perron–Frobenius it is the spectral radius of the nonnegative matrix $\mathcal J$. Then $\Lambda^\ast<1$ iff the right side at $\Lambda=1$ is below one, that is, iff $\frac1n\sum_ju_jv_j/(d_j+\delta_{f,j})<1$.

*Simplification.* Since $r_j=v_jX^{j\ast}$, $\frac1nu_jv_j=(1-\beta)\frac{\kappa_j\Omega}{n}\frac{r_jd_j}{\kappa_j\Omega/n+k_{0j}}=(1-\beta)r_j\omega_jd_j$. So the condition is $\Gamma<1$, which proves (1); for $\Gamma>1$, the deviations $y_1=w$, $y_2=-w$ along the Perron vector $w$ grow by $\Lambda^\ast>1$ a period. Part (2) is read off $\Gamma$.

*The price of standing, and its domain.* Let an actor's relative net payoff today be $y$, converted so that its stock deviations rise by $\kappa_jy$. From the next period its relative deviation evolves by $\mathcal J$, and its winnings exceed the mean by $(1-\beta)\Omega\,dp=\frac{(1-\beta)\Omega}{n}\mathbf v^\top x_t$ per period. The present value is $\frac\delta n\mathbf v^\top\sum_{t\ge0}(\delta\mathcal J)^t\mathbf u\,y$. The series converges iff the spectral radius of $\delta\mathcal J$ is below one; the secular argument applied to $\delta\mathcal J$ shows that this holds iff $\frac\delta n\sum_ju_jv_j/(1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j}))<1$, that is, iff $\Gamma_\delta<1$. There, with $A=I-\delta\mathcal D$, Sherman–Morrison gives $(A-\frac\delta n\mathbf u\mathbf v^\top)^{-1}\mathbf u=A^{-1}\mathbf u/(1-\frac\delta n\mathbf v^\top A^{-1}\mathbf u)$, so the present value is $\frac{\Gamma_\delta}{1-\Gamma_\delta}y$ with $\Gamma_\delta=\frac\delta n\sum_ju_jv_j/(1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j}))$, which is the stated form, and it grows without bound as $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$. Finally $\delta(d_j+\delta_{f,j})<1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})$ for $\delta<1$, so $\Gamma_\delta<\Gamma$, and $\Gamma_\delta\to\Gamma$ as $\delta\to1$.

*Beyond the first order: the cusp.* The two signs of $\Gamma_\delta-1$ are the two signs of $\delta\Lambda^\ast-1$, by the same secular argument. Write the nonlinear relative deviation along the Perron direction as $y_{t+1}=f(y_t)$ with $f(0)=0$ and $f'(0)=\Lambda^\ast$, the actor's extra winnings as $w(y)$ with $w'(0)>0$, and $PV(\varepsilon)=\sum_{t\ge1}\delta^tw(y_t)$ for a relative perturbation $\varepsilon$.
- If $\delta\Lambda^\ast<1$, fix $\Lambda'$ with $\Lambda^\ast<\Lambda'<1/\delta$. While $|y_t|\le\eta$ we have $|y_t|\le C\Lambda'^t\varepsilon$ and a linearization error $O(y_t^2)$; after $T=\ln(\eta/\varepsilon)/\ln\Lambda'$ periods the tail is at most $\delta^T\sup|w|/(1-\delta)=O(\varepsilon^{\ln(1/\delta)/\ln\Lambda'})=o(\varepsilon)$, since the exponent exceeds one. So $PV(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\to\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$: the first-order price holds on the whole domain $\Gamma_\delta<1$, including $\Gamma>1>\Gamma_\delta$, where equal standing is unstable and discounting still wins; there the convergence is slow, of order $\varepsilon^{-\ln(\delta\Lambda^\ast)/\ln\Lambda^\ast}$.
- If $\delta\Lambda^\ast>1$, choose $\Lambda''<\Lambda^\ast$ with $\delta\Lambda''>1$. For $t\le T=\ln(\eta/\varepsilon)/\ln\Lambda''$ we have $y_t\ge c\Lambda''^t\varepsilon$, so $PV(\varepsilon)\ge c'\varepsilon\sum_{t\le T}(\delta\Lambda'')^t\ge c''\eta\,\delta^T=c'''\varepsilon^{a}$ with $a=\ln(1/\delta)/\ln\Lambda''<1$. The value of standing is continuous at equal standing, positive on both sides, and its slope $PV/\varepsilon\asymp\varepsilon^{a-1}$ diverges: no finite first-order weight represents the actor, and a finite horizon gives a price that grows without bound in the horizon. The reading of (9) in Section 3.5 "where the loop gain is high" is therefore the limit $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$, and the printed formula is not to be evaluated beyond it, where it is negative and meaningless.
- If $\delta\Lambda^\ast=1$, the same bound gives $PV(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\ge c\ln(1/\varepsilon)$.

*Rivals who respond, (4).* In a symmetric Markov equilibrium the equilibrium win share is $p_i^{\rm eq}=P(X_i;\{X_k\}_{k\ne i})$, symmetric in the others' stocks. Linearize at equal standing: $dp_i=\sum_ja_j\,dX_i^j+\sum_jb_j\sum_{k\ne i}dX_k^j$. Since $\sum_ip_i\equiv1$ whatever the stocks, perturbing one carrier's stock $j$ alone gives $a_j+(n-1)b_j=0$, so $dp_i=\sum_ja_j\frac n{n-1}(dX_i^j-\overline{dX^j})$: the equilibrium share responds to relative deviations only, and the mean deviations still decay at $1-d_j$. The deviation dynamics are therefore again $\mathcal D+\frac1n\mathbf u\mathbf v^{\mathrm{eq}\top}$ with $v^{\rm eq}_j=a_jn^2/(n-1)$ and $r^{\rm eq}_j=v^{\rm eq}_jX^{j\ast}$; with $\mathbf v^{\rm eq}\ge0$, which is the premise that the equilibrium win share rises with each own stock, the Perron argument of (1) applies unchanged, and (2) is read off. If some $v^{\rm eq}_j<0$, catch-up in that channel outweighing the structural advantage, $\mathcal J$ is no longer nonnegative and $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$ alone does not decide the sign. For the price, apply the envelope theorem to the actor's equilibrium value, differentiable at equal standing by premise: its own intensity is optimal, so the response of its own intensity drops out at first order, and the derivative of the value follows the state with the actor's own intensities held as numbers and the rivals' at their Markov policies. A relative shift (own stocks $+\varepsilon$, each other's $-\varepsilon/(n-1)$) leaves totals unchanged and stays relative, with $dp_i=r^{\rm pr}_jy^j/(nX^{j\ast})$. The present value is then the Sherman–Morrison expression of (3) with $\mathbf v^{\rm pr}$ in place of $\mathbf v$, plus $-\ell\sum_{k\ne i}\partial e_k/\partial X_i$ per period when intensities carry a shared loss $\ell$: a lead that makes rivals spend less is worth $\ell$ per unit of their reduction. $\square$

*Numerics.* Across 4,000 random two-channel systems the numerical Jacobian's spectral radius agrees with the criterion in every case, and nonlinear simulations from a $10^{-4}$ perturbation grow or decay as $\Gamma$ is above or below one, including a case with $r=0.8$ in which a reflexive channel lifts $\Gamma$ to $1.1$; the present value of a perturbation in the nonlinear model matches $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ to a relative error below $2\times10^{-6}$ on the domain, and converges to it in twenty systems with $\Gamma\ge1>\Gamma_\delta$ [num: LG]. Two reviewers' independent scripts, with one to three stocks entering strength by logarithm, linear and square-root forms, found no mismatch in 2,996 and 1,500 random systems (S15). *The domain and the cusp* [num: PD; m02 D, f1 C]: in the two-carrier case $\kappa\Omega=1$, $d=0.2$, $r=2$, $\delta=0.9$ ($\Gamma=2$, $\Gamma_\delta=9/7$, $\Lambda^\ast=1.2$), the present value of a relative perturbation stays positive and scales as $\varepsilon^{a}$ with fitted exponents $0.5628$, $0.5758$, $0.5776$, $0.5778$ over $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ to $10^{-8}$ against the predicted $\ln(1/0.9)/\ln1.2=0.5779$; the local exponent of the price per unit runs $-0.4442$ to $-0.4225$ against $-0.4221$. Inside the domain but above $\Gamma=1$ ($\delta=0.8$, $\Gamma_\delta=0.889$) the price per unit is $7.163$, $7.702$, $7.894$, $7.962$, $7.987$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$ to $10^{-12}$, converging to the formula's $8.000$ at the predicted rate $\varepsilon^{0.224}$; at the boundary $\delta\Lambda^\ast=1$ it grows by $8.42$ per two decades of $\varepsilon$, logarithmically. *Rivals who respond* [num: MP]: {{eqgain}} below.

*Bounds.* The result is local and holds for reinforcing channels: a countervailing channel is not a negative term of $\Gamma$, and with delay it oscillates ({{delayosc}}). $\Gamma$ is evaluated at equal standing; the reflexive channel's gain is higher at the present concentration of standing, since cultivated conversion scales with a high moment of shares (Proposition {{#corrrate}}(4); S13). The prize is fixed; S3 lets it grow. The certificate of Proposition {{#certificate}}(2), a row sum of conversion gains below one, has the same form, but is evaluated at a captured state, not at equal standing; the two are of one form, not one theorem.

**{{!Remark:tdec}} (Time to decisiveness) [thm; num].** In the linearized loop a relative gap grows by the Perron root $\Lambda^\ast$ of {{eq:secular}} a period. With one channel, $\mathcal J=1-d_K-\delta_f+(1-\beta)r\omega d_K=1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$, since $\frac1nuv=(1-\beta)r\omega d_K$ and $\Gamma=(1-\beta)r\omega d_K/(d_K+\delta_f)$; V6.1's factor $1+d_K(\Gamma-1)$ omitted the diffusion term. A gap therefore grows from $\varepsilon_0$ to a decisive level $\bar\Delta$ in about $T_{\rm dec}=\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast$ periods. **$\Gamma$ sets the sign of growth; the turnover of the stocks sets its speed.** At $\Gamma=3$, $d_K=0.2$, $\delta_f=0$, a gap grows from 1% to 50% in 11.6 periods by the linear estimate and 13 in the nonlinear model; at the same $\Gamma=1.2$, a channel with turnover $d_K+\delta_f=0.5$ needs 41 periods and one with turnover $0.02$ needs 980 [num: TD; m03 C, f3 C]. With several channels $\Lambda^\ast$ is not a function of $\Gamma$: at $\Gamma=1.2$, turnovers $(0.3,0.02)$ give $\Lambda^\ast=1.0088$ and $(0.1,0.1)$ give $1.0200$; $(0.5,0.02)$ gives $1.0092$ and 427 periods [num: TD]. The simulated factors $1.40000$, $1.05000$, $1.10000$, $1.03000$ equal $1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$ exactly, where the old formula gives $1.025$ and $1.067$ for the second and third [num: TD]. With diffusion the nonlinear gap can settle short of decisive: at $\Gamma=1.5$, $d_K=\delta_f=0.05$, $r=3$ it never reaches 50% [num: TD]. This is the channel through which a larger loop gain raises the breakout probability of Section 5.2: $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, the probability that a breakout is not detected and answered within the time it needs to become decisive ({{>detectlink}}). The interface (S13) reads "$T_{\rm dec}$ set by $\Lambda^\ast$", not by $\Gamma$.

**{{!Remark:eqgain}} (Two gains when rivals respond) [thm∣prem; num].** Once intensities respond to the state, "one loop gain" splits into two numbers of the same closed-loop form. *Stability* is decided by the equilibrium gain $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$, built from $r^{\rm eq}_j$ with every intensity responding; the expected relative deviation at equal standing evolves to first order by $1+(d+\delta_f)(\Gamma^{\rm eq}-1)$. *The price of standing* is $\Gamma^{\rm pr}_\delta/(1-\Gamma^{\rm pr}_\delta)$, built from $r^{\rm pr}_j$ with the actor's own intensity held and the rivals responding, plus the shared-loss term of (4). Both reduce to the structural $\Gamma$ when intensities are fixed. The two elasticities differ by the own-response term, which the envelope theorem removes from the price and not from the dynamics; the price is not $\Gamma^{\rm eq}_\delta/(1-\Gamma^{\rm eq}_\delta)$, which is negative or infinite wherever $\Gamma^{\rm eq}_\delta\ge1$ while the equilibrium price stays finite.

*Magnitudes* [num: MP; m01, f2]. Two carriers, a Tullock lottery with chosen intensity, each round's winnings converted into capacity, $d=0.1$, $\delta=1/1.1$ (so $\Gamma_\delta=\Gamma/2$), a symmetric Markov-perfect equilibrium by value iteration with a best-response gap at parity at most $10^{-16}$:

| 0.2 | 0.1164 | 0.1166 | 0.1111 | 1.09 |
| 0.3 | 0.1941 | 0.1950 | 0.1765 | 1.18 |
| 0.4 | 0.3099 | 0.3170 | 0.2500 | 1.41 |
| 0.45 | 0.513 | 0.643 | 0.290 | 2.48 ($\Gamma^{\rm eq}=1.11$) |
| 0.6 | 1.142 | — | 0.429 | 4.72 |
| 0.8 | 1.861 | — | 0.667 | 5.09 |

Three cases appear. In the *smooth* equilibrium, up to a structural gain of about $0.4$, the envelope formula with $r^{\rm pr}$ reproduces the equilibrium price to within about $2\%$, and the structural price understates it by $5$–$24\%$. Above about $0.45$ the equilibrium is *amplified*: $r^{\rm eq}$ is $2.5$ to $5$ times $r$, equal standing is unstable although $\Gamma<1$, and the price is $1.8$ to $3.2$ times the structural value; the first-order formula with $r^{\rm pr}$ no longer tracks it. With smaller rounds ($d=0.02$) and no shared loss the equilibrium price equals the closed-loop form in $\Gamma^{\rm pr}_\delta$ to four digits in five cases ($0.0319/0.0319$, $0.0491/0.0491$, $0.0492/0.0492$, $0.0768/0.0768$, $0.1078/0.1079$), and the expected-deviation multiplier equals $1+d(\Gamma^{\rm eq}-1)$ to five digits; at the paper's rounds ($d=0.2$) the amplification is $\times1.12$ at $\Gamma=0.3$, $\times2.4$–$2.6$ at $\Gamma=0.6$, $\times4.75$ with a shared loss $\ell=0.5$, and $\times2.19$ at $\Gamma=0.9$, with $\Gamma^{\rm eq}=1.96$ at a structural $0.6$ and the spread of standing after 60 periods $0.198$ against $0.120$ under the structural lottery. With shared loss ($\ell=0.5$, $\Gamma=0.3$) the price is $0.2201$ against the envelope's $0.2221$ once the rivals' shared-loss term is included. In a static $n$-player Tullock contest with effort, $r^{\rm pr}/r=2(n-1)/n$ exactly: $1.000$, $1.333$, $1.500$, $1.667$, $1.800$ for $n=2,3,4,6,10$. The two amplifications are different mechanisms: the static factor vanishes at $n=2$ and is a within-period rival response; the Markov amplification at $n=2$ is dynamic, the convexity of the value in the state. The third case, *multiplicity*, is below.

*Off parity.* Where the structural price is the same at every state ($r=1$), the laggard's price at a share of $0.25$ is $57\%$ of the structural value at $\Gamma=0.3$ and $25\%$ at $\Gamma=0.6$, while the leader's at $0.75$ is $153\%$; with a shared loss ($r=1.5$, $\Gamma=0.6$, $\ell=0.5$) the laggard's price at $0.25$ is negative, $-0.039$ [num: MP]. The laggard stops contesting standing, which is the exclusion of {{compound}}(2) seen from the laggard's side.

*Catch-up* [num: MP; held for outside review]. A strong concave cost of standing below the top ($L_{\rm sub}=100$) gives $r^{\rm eq}/r=0.993<1$ at $\Gamma=0.3$, and the laggard tries harder than the leader. The sign of the strategic correction is therefore a property of the contest class: discouragement contests amplify, catch-up contests damp, and which class a given contest belongs to is an empirical question adjacent to crux C5.

*Multiplicity* [num: MP; held for outside review]. Value iteration from $V=0$ and from a step at parity converge to different fixed points when rounds are fine. At $\Gamma=0.2$, $d=0.05$: from $V=0$ a smooth equilibrium with price $0.1169$; from the step a winner-take-all equilibrium with price $1.392$ on 3,000 cells and $1.410$ on 6,000 ($12.5$ and $12.7$ times the structural value), both converged to $10^{-11}$. At $\Gamma=0.3$, $d=0.05$: $0.1969$ and $1.551$. At $d=0.1$ the step start returns to the smooth equilibrium: the winner-take-all branch needs fine rounds, and a smaller gain shrinks the region in which it appears. If it survives outside review as a genuine equilibrium of the continuous-state game, the belief that a lead is decisive selects the equilibrium in which it is; Section 3.8's "Ψ3 is partly made true" would then have this as its formal content, and the generative answer is the one the paper already gives: lower the gain, by sharing, provision, diffusion or closing the reflexive channel, until one equilibrium remains.

**{{!Remark:delayosc}} (A balancing channel with delay oscillates) [thm; num].** Let two stocks be fed by the same relative payoff, one reinforcing with gain $0.5$ and turnover $0.4$ (so $1-d_1=0.6$), one countervailing with gain $-0.05$ and turnover $0.05$ ($1-d_2=0.95$): mobilization of those harmed, or correction that arrives late. The net DC gain is $0.5/0.4-0.05/0.05=0.25<1$, yet the deviation matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1.1&0.5\\-0.05&0.9\end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues $1\pm0.1225i$, modulus $1.0075$, period $51.6$: a growing oscillation, with $|y|=292$ after 600 periods from a unit start [num: CH; m03 D]. The criterion of {{loopgain}}(1) needs every channel reinforcing ($\mathcal J\ge0$); correction is not a negative term of $\Gamma$, and the regulatory cycle of {{countervail}} can diverge. What a balancing channel does to the loop is a question of its delay, not only of its gain.

**{{!Proposition:chansign}} (Which levers lower the loop gain) [thm].** *Let stock $j$ act through strength $h_j(X^j)$, with $r_j=h_j'(X^{j\ast})X^{j\ast}$ and level elasticity $\xi_{r,j}=d\ln r_j/d\ln X^j$ at equal standing. Ratio channels ($h=r\ln X$, as capacity in a Tullock contest) have $\xi_r=0$; linear level channels ($h=aX$, as rule support in the field model of Section 4) have $\xi_r=1$.*
1. *Sharing $\beta$ and diffusion $\delta_{f,j}$ lower $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma_\delta$ in every channel.*
2. *A faster decay or correction rate $d_j$ changes $\Gamma$ with the sign of $\delta_{f,j}/(d_j+\delta_{f,j})-\xi_{r,j}$, and $\Gamma_\delta$ with the sign of $(1-\delta+\delta\delta_{f,j})/(1-\delta+\delta d_j+\delta\delta_{f,j})-\xi_{r,j}$. Correction therefore lowers the gain of level channels. In ratio channels it leaves $\Gamma$ unchanged without diffusion, raises it with diffusion, and always raises $\Gamma_\delta$: a stock that turns over faster is made more of recent winnings.*
3. *Provision not won, $k_{0j}$, lowers the gain through stock $j$ iff $\xi_{r,j}<1$. In a linear level channel it adds the same to everyone and changes nothing.*
4. *The convertible share $\kappa_j$ raises the gain through a ratio channel only when $k_{0j}>0$, and through a level channel always.*

*Proof.* At equal standing $X^{j\ast}=c_j/d_j$ with $c_j=\kappa_j\Omega/n+k_{0j}$, and $\omega_j=(\kappa_j\Omega/n)/(d_jX^{j\ast})$, so the $j$th term of {{eq:gamma}} is $(1-\beta)\frac{\kappa_j\Omega}{n}\cdot\frac{r_j(X^{j\ast})}{X^{j\ast}}\cdot\frac1{d_j+\delta_{f,j}}$. (1) is read off this form and off $\Gamma_\delta$. (2): by the chain rule $dr_j/dd_j=(r_j\xi_{r,j}/X^{j\ast})(-X^{j\ast}/d_j)=-r_j\xi_{r,j}/d_j$, so $\partial_{d_j}\big[r_jd_j/(d_j+\delta_{f,j})\big]=\frac{r_j}{d_j+\delta_{f,j}}\big[\frac{\delta_{f,j}}{d_j+\delta_{f,j}}-\xi_{r,j}\big]$; the $\Gamma_\delta$ case is the same computation on $\delta d_j/(1-\delta+\delta d_j+\delta\delta_{f,j})$, whose own derivative in $d_j$ is positive even at $\delta_{f,j}=0$. (3): $k_{0j}$ moves only $X^{j\ast}$, and $\partial_{X^\ast}(r_j/X^\ast)$ has the sign of $\xi_{r,j}-1$. (4): for a ratio channel $\omega_j$ rises with $\kappa_j$ iff $k_{0j}>0$; for a level channel $r_j\omega_j=a\kappa_j\Omega/(nd_j)$. $\square$

*Checks* [num: CH; m03 A–B, f3 B]. The relative-mode growth factor is simulated in the nonlinear model for five channel forms (logarithmic, linear, $X^{0.5}$, $X^2$, $\ln(1+X)$), with and without diffusion, and every sign agrees with the rule. Logarithmic channel: $\Gamma$ unchanged at $1.2857$ when $d$ rises from $0.2$ to $0.3$ without diffusion, and $0.8571\to0.9643$ with $\delta_f=0.1$. Linear channel: $1.125\to0.750$. Provision $k_0$ from $0.1$ to $0.2$: the logarithmic channel's gain falls $1.2857\to1.0000$, the linear channel's stays at $1.125$, the $X^2$ channel's rises $1.75\to2.25$. Ratio channel, $\Gamma_\delta$ at $\delta=0.9$: $0.853$, $1.157$, $1.409$ at $d=0.1,0.2,0.4$ with $\Gamma$ unchanged. By numerical spectral radius of the nonlinear map ($n=2$, $\kappa\Omega=1$, $k_0=0.2$, $\delta_f=0.1$): a ratio channel at $d=0.1,0.2,0.3,0.6$ has $\Gamma=0.71,0.95,1.07,1.22$ and radius $0.943,0.986,1.029,1.157$, stable, stable, unstable, unstable; a level channel ($a=1.2$) has $\Gamma=3.0,2.0,1.5,0.86$ and radius $1.40,1.30,1.20,0.90$: correction stabilizes it. *Consequences.* The §2 box and Table 7.1 split the correction row into correction of rule support (a level channel: ↓) and turnover of capacity (a ratio channel: 0 without diffusion, ↑ with it; working objective ↑ through $\Gamma_\delta$; leads open faster when $\Gamma>1$, since the gap factor $1+(d+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$ rises with $d$); the provision row says "through capacity". Provision is the remedy for compounding through capacity; the remedy for the reflexive channel is correction and $\chi\to0$ (Section 10.2). Test Γ (Section 7.4) is re-signed accordingly: provision lowers $\Gamma_\delta$ in ratio channels and so raises $1+\theta$.

### What the loop gain unifies, and what it does not

| Result | A comparative static of $\Gamma$ or $\Gamma_\delta$? |
|---|---|
| Compounding ({{compound}}) | Yes: the single-channel case |
| Channel signs ({{chansign}}) | Yes: comparative statics of {{eq:gamma}} |
| Time to decisiveness ({{tdec}}) | Partly: $\Gamma$ sets whether a gap grows; the speed is the Perron root $\Lambda^\ast$, not a function of $\Gamma$ |
| The equilibrium gain ({{eqgain}}) | Of the same form, with $r^{\rm eq}$ and $r^{\rm pr}$ in place of $r$; its size is not a function of $\Gamma$ |
| The feedback benchmark ({{bench}}) | Of the same form, not the same theorem: a cross-coupled pair without self-gain |
| Influence over correctors and certificates (Propositions {{#influence}}, {{#certificate}}) | Of the same form, evaluated at a captured state |
| Working objective, visibility of the host, levies, reneging (Propositions {{#twochan}}, {{#standprice}}) | Yes, through $\Gamma_\delta$ set against the common gain $\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta$ |
| Talk between two parties (Proposition {{#talk}}(1)) | Yes, through $\theta$ |
| The breakout probability and the decisive value in the enforcement condition | Partly: $\Gamma$ sets whether a lead grows and $\Lambda^\ast$ how fast; whether a position beyond sanction exists, and detection lags, do not depend on it |
| Tempo and coverage (Proposition {{#tempo}}, Theorem {{#coverage}}) | No: they concern the absolute growth of capability |
| Talk among three or more (Proposition {{#talk}}(2)) | No: its direction is not monotone |

This is why the core speaks of interconnected conditions with one structural loop gain at their core, and not of a single pressure from which every signature follows.

### Proof of {{compound}}

(1) With one stock and $\sigma_i=r\ln K_i$, $r_K=r$ and $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$. The condition $\Gamma<1$ reads $(1-\beta)r\kappa_c\Omega d_K<(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)(d_K+\delta_f)$; with $S^\ast=(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)/d_K$ this is $\kappa_c\Omega((1-\beta)r-1)<nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$, and the reversed inequality gives $\Gamma>1$. With $k_0=\delta_f=0$ it is $(1-\beta)r<1$, independent of $\kappa_c$. Equality is $\Gamma=1$, a neutral linearization, which {{loopgain}} does not decide either.

(2) With the others at $\bar K$ and the laggard at $K$, the mean is $\bar X=(K+(n-1)\bar K)/n$, so the laggard's diffusion inflow is $-\delta_f(K-\bar X)=\delta_f\frac{n-1}n(\bar K-K)$, a term V6.1 dropped; adding the contest inflow, provision and depreciation gives $\phi$. *Strict cap.* $\phi(\bar K)=\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0-d_K\bar K=d_K(K^\ast-\bar K)$, positive for $\bar K<K^\ast$, so by continuity $\phi>0$ just below the cap and a laggard just below returns. *Cap at $K^\ast$.* Then $\phi(K^\ast)=0$, and the sign just below is that of $-\phi'(K^\ast)$, with
$$\phi'(K^\ast)=\frac{(1-\beta)\kappa_c\Omega\,r(n-1)}{n^2K^\ast}-d_K-\delta_f\frac{n-1}n=d_K\Big[(1-\beta)r\omega\frac{n-1}n-1\Big]-\delta_f\frac{n-1}n,$$
using $\kappa_c\Omega/(nK^\ast)=d_K\omega$. If $\phi'(K^\ast)>0$ then $\phi<0$ just below the cap and the laggard leaves; if $\phi'(K^\ast)<0$ it returns. Without diffusion the criterion is $\Gamma\frac{n-1}n\gtrless1$. The equality of the cap is the interesting boundary: for $1<\Gamma\le n/(n-1)$ without diffusion, equal standing is unstable without a cap, yet a cap at exactly $K^\ast$ holds a laggard, because the cap stops the leaders from pulling away. *Global recovery.* With the others held, the laggard's capacity is a scalar map $K'=\min\{K+\phi(K),\bar K\}$, so it reaches the cap from $K$ iff $\phi>0$ on $(K,\bar K)$, and from every start iff $\phi>0$ on $(0,\bar K)$. *Near zero.* With $r>1$ the contest term is $O(K^r)=o(K)$, so $\phi(K)=\phi(0^+)-d_KK+o(K)$ with $\phi(0^+)=\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0+\delta_f(n-1)\bar K/n$. If all three inflows are zero, $\phi(0^+)=0$ and $\phi<0$ near zero: laggards below a threshold are excluded. If $\phi(0^+)>0$, exclusion needs $\phi$ to dip below zero somewhere in $(0,\bar K)$, which the sufficient condition rules out: $\phi$ is at least the linear function $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0+\delta_f\frac{n-1}n(\bar K-K)-d_KK$, positive on $(0,\bar K)$ whenever $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0\ge d_K\bar K$. *Leaders held.* A leader's inflow at the cap is at least $\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0-d_K\bar K-\delta_f\bar K/n$, since its win share is at least $1/n$ and its diffusion loss is at most $\delta_f\bar K/n$; this is non-negative when $\bar K\le(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)/(nd_K+\delta_f)$, which is the Scope bound. $\square$

*Growth variant.* Without depreciation, total capacity $S$ grows by $\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0$ a period, and the share deviation has multiplier $[S(1-\delta_f)+\kappa_c\Omega(1-\beta)r]/(S+\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$. Equal standing is therefore unstable at a date iff $\kappa_c\Omega((1-\beta)r-1)>\delta_fS+nk_0$ with $S$ the current total, a condition that changes with time unless the stakes grow with capacity. With $\Omega_t=gS_t$ and $k_0=0$ it is $\kappa_cg((1-\beta)r-1)>\delta_f$ at every date, and without diffusion it is $(1-\beta)r>1$: the condition under which Proposition {{#standprice}}(2) narrows the working objective, and the two coincide in 24 of 24 cases computed [num: SP; m02 C]. With diffusion the two thresholds separate, the narrowing threshold rising to $(1-\beta)r>1+\delta\delta_f/(1-\delta(1-d))$ and the instability threshold to $(1-\beta)r>1+\delta_f/(\kappa_cg)$ (S3): at $r=1.17$, $\delta_f=0.03$ the objective narrows while equal shares are stable [num: SP; m02 C].

*Numerics.* With $n=5$, $r=1.5$, a cap $\bar K=1.5$ below $K^\ast=2$ and a laggard starting at $0.1$: with neither sharing nor provision the laggard is excluded; with $\beta=0.02$ or $k_0=0.002$ it stalls near $0.06$; with $\beta=0.2$ or $k_0=0.03$ all reach the cap; the exact condition predicts each case [num: SM(9), SM(10)]. *The cap edge and diffusion* [num: CE; m04, f3 A]: the cap-equality case $n=2$, $r=3$, $\bar K=K^\ast=2.5$ from $(2.499,2.5)$ sends the laggard to $2.4\times10^{-32}$ in 400 periods, as the criterion $(1-\beta)r\omega\frac{n-1}n=1.5>1$ predicts; a cap of $0.98K^\ast$ restores it, and a strict cap ($0.97K^\ast$) restores a laggard just below in 400 of 400 random settings; at $\bar K=K^\ast$ the local criterion predicts the simulated outcome in 400 of 400, including $n=4$, $r=2$ with $\delta_f=0.3$ (returns), $0.1$ (stalls at $0.48K^\ast$) and $0$ (excluded). The global criterion $\phi>0$ on $(0,\bar K)$ agrees with simulation from 1% of the cap in 444 of 446 settings with leaders provably held; in the two disagreements the exclusion threshold ($0.7\%$ and $0.2\%$ of the cap) lies below the start, so the criterion, which speaks of every start, is right and the start was above the threshold. Diffusion alone restores: $n=2$, $r=2$, $d_K=0.2$, $\delta_f=0.6$, $\bar K=K^\ast$, no sharing or provision, $\phi>0$ on $(0,\bar K)$ and $(0.01,2.5)\to(2.5,2.5)$, the leader's small diffusion loss notwithstanding. Each of the three inflows suffices on its own at $n=5$, $r=1.5$, $\bar K=0.8$: with none, laggards at $0.05$ and $0.2$ are excluded ($0.5$ recovers); with $\beta=0.1$, $k_0=0.05$ or $\delta_f=0.15$ alone, all return. Diffusion is a source, not a guarantee: at $n=2$, $r=2$, $\bar K=0.9K^\ast$ with $\delta_f=0.05$ alone, a laggard from $0.3\bar K$ stalls at $0.36\bar K$, since $\phi(0^+)=0.056>0$ but $\min\phi=-0.013$; with no inflow at all it is excluded.

**{{!Remark:bench}} (A local feedback benchmark) [thm].** With $u$ a small deviation in an actor's success and $w$ a deviation in its favourable control over the rules, let $u'=(1-d_u)u+aw$ and $w'=bu+(1-d_w)w$ with cross-gains $a,b\ge0$ and decays $0<d_u,d_w\le1$. The system is stable exactly when $ab<d_ud_w$, that is, when the DC loop gain $(a/d_u)(b/d_w)$ is below one. *Proof.* The characteristic polynomial is $(\Lambda-1+d_u)(\Lambda-1+d_w)-ab=0$, with roots $1-\frac{d_u+d_w}2\pm\sqrt{\frac{(d_u-d_w)^2}4+ab}$. The larger root is below one iff $ab<d_ud_w$; the smaller then exceeds $1-(d_u+d_w)\ge-1$. $\square$ This is an algebraic identity; FB checks it against the spectral radius on 200,000 random pairs [num: FB]. It is of the same form as {{loopgain}} and not the same theorem: {{loopgain}}'s $\mathcal J$ is a rank-one perturbation of a diagonal through the win share, while this pair has no self-gain and couples only across, and no rank-one choice reproduces it; both say that a DC loop gain below one is stable. It is the stability condition of Richardson's arms-race model with control over rules in place of armaments (Richardson 1960). Its use is that each coefficient names a channel that can be measured. Instability does not prove capture or harm, and stability does not prove that an excursion stays within a safety constraint.

**{{!Remark:gammarange}} (Illustrative ranges; $\Gamma$ is not estimated) [num; conj].** No channel's $\Gamma$ has been estimated, and the core states none. What {{eq:gamma}} does permit is a reading of orders of magnitude. Since $(1-\beta)\omega\le1$, a single channel needs $r\ge(d+\delta_f)/d=1+\delta_f/d$ to reach $\Gamma=1$. With a lead diffusing at $0.1$ a month, the figure Remark {{#lead}} uses, and capacity turning over at $0.02$ to $0.05$ a month, that is $r\ge6$ to $3$; at $0.05$ a month diffusion, $r\ge3.5$ to $2$ [num: algebra of {{eq:gamma}}; Adv-O adv_checks §1]. Three readings follow, each a conjecture about which channel is which. A channel whose stock diffuses fast, such as algorithmic knowledge (EV9), carries a small gain at any decisiveness within these ranges. A channel whose diffusion is stopped, such as hardware whose export is controlled, has $\delta_f\to0$ and $\Gamma\to(1-\beta)r\omega$, so controls that preserve a lead raise the gain ({{chansign}}(1); Section 8.2). The reflexive channel, rule support, is a level channel whose decisiveness $r_j=aZ^\ast$ rises as correction falls ({{chansign}}), so no bound of this kind applies to it, and its gain is higher at the present concentration of standing than at parity (S13). What can be observed is not $\Gamma$ but the ratio of conduct's response to shared harm and to private cost, $1+\theta$, and its interaction with provision; Test Γ (Section 7.4) registers that observable.
