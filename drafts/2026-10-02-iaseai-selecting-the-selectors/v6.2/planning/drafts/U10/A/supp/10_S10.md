## S10. The generative alternative

This chapter states in full the two results on a common frontier and the two on verification and participation that Section 10 cites in one sentence each, proves {{share}} under both sharing conventions, and gives the margin of {{safeguards}} on both hosts, with the numbers of block HO.

### A common frontier

**{{!Proposition:commonfront}} (A common frontier with a common value) [thm].** *Let the value of capability be common: each of $n$ actors receives $\pi_i=\Omega(\mathcal E)/n-ce_i-\ell\mathcal E$, with $\Omega$ increasing and concave in total intensity $\mathcal E=\sum_je_j$. Then private first-order conditions give $\Omega'(\mathcal E)=n(c+\ell)$ against the joint optimum $\Omega'(\mathcal E)=c+n\ell$, so total intensity is at or below the joint optimum: the residual failure is too little speed, not too much. The joint optimum must also respect the host's floor ({{viab}}).*

*Proof.* Compare the two first-order conditions; concavity of $\Omega$ orders the two solutions, with $n(c+\ell)\ge c+n\ell$ iff $c\ge\ell$, and in either case the private solution is the one at which the marginal common value is spread over $n$ claimants. $\square$

**{{!Proposition:commonfrontn}} (A common frontier against racers) [thm; num: legacy v4, N22].** *Of $n$ actors, cooperators hold effort $e_c$ and racers choose theirs; whoever wins the decisive contest gains $\Omega$, and every loser bears $L_b$. Under separate frontiers every effort enters the contest separately; under a common frontier cooperators pool effort into one entity of strength $(me_c)^r$, which yields a common value $\Omega_c$ split equally among them and confers no dominance among them. With $n=4$, $\Omega=\Omega_c=L_b=c=1$, $\ell=0.3$, $e_c=0.05$ and $r=1.3$: (1) under separate frontiers the unique pure equilibrium is all racing; (2) under a common frontier all cooperating ($R=0.140>T=0.002$) and all racing are both strict equilibria, cooperating being a best response iff trust in each rival exceeds about 0.61, or 0.82 if a deviator best-responds to fixed intensities; (3) treating the others as a coalition maximizing their summed payoff, the coalition's cooperation is dominant; (4) a small pooled effort ($e_c=0.03$) leaves only all racing, and with $n=2$ all cooperating is an equilibrium for $r$ up to about 1.2; (5) holding $\Omega_c$ and $L_b$ fixed, the pooled effort needed to keep $T\le R$ is 0.0054, 0.035, 0.141 and 0.49 for $\Omega=0.5,1,2,4$.*

*Scope.* Racers' intensities are the symmetric fixed point of best responses, each verified as a global best response; cases without a symmetric pure equilibrium are excluded (`../v4/checks_v45.py`, N22, run under `--legacy`). A common frontier turns a dilemma into a coordination problem while the value of winning is moderate; when it is large, keeping ahead of defectors is the race moved up a level. Parts (4) and (5) are also a free-rider result: where frontier contributions are voluntary, pooled effort below the threshold leaves only the racing equilibrium, and the threshold rises steeply with the value of winning, which is why X10 collects frontier contributions by formula from the same base as the levy rather than by subscription.

### Proof of Proposition {{#share}}

(1) Pooled payoffs $\pi'_j=(1-\beta)\pi_j+\beta\bar\pi$ give $J_i=\big[(1-\beta)+\beta(1+\theta_0)/n\big]\pi_i+\big[\theta_0(1-\beta)+\beta(1+\theta_0)(n-1)/n\big]\bar\pi_{-i}$; dividing by the first coefficient gives $\theta_{\rm eff}(\beta)$, which is strictly increasing in $\beta$ for $\theta_0<n-1$ and equals $n-1$ at $\beta=1$. At $\theta_0=-1$, $J_i=(1-\beta)(\pi_i-\bar\pi_{-i})$, and under relative selection $\pi'_k-\pi'_l=(1-\beta)(\pi_k-\pi_l)$, so no ordering of carriers changes.

(2) With the promise kept with probability $\varrho$ and the shared part divided among all $n$, each payoff is $w_i\big[(1-\beta\varrho)\Omega_e+\Omega_s\big]+\varrho\beta\Omega_e/n-ce_i-\ell\mathcal E$; the shared term is common and cancels from relative selection, leaving the contest with prize $\Omega_{\rm eff}=(1-\beta\varrho)\Omega_e+\Omega_s$. If the shared part goes to the $n-1$ others only, the winner keeps $(1-\beta\varrho)\Omega_e$ and each loser receives $\beta\varrho\Omega_e/(n-1)$, so the winner's advantage over a loser is $\Omega_e\big(1-\beta\varrho\,n/(n-1)\big)+\Omega_s$: the contest with prize $\Omega_{\rm eff}=\big(1-\beta\varrho\,n/(n-1)\big)\Omega_e+\Omega_s$, the convention under which {{twochan}}(4) and {{wingives}} count what a winner gives. In either case a mutant at $y$ among residents at $x$ has relative payoff $\Omega_{\rm eff}\big(y^r-x^r\big)/\big(y^r+(n-1)x^r\big)-c(y-x)$, with zero slope at $x^\ast=r\Omega_{\rm eff}/(nc)$, concave for $r=1$, and with no profitable mutant on a grid for $r<1$. Under the first convention $\Omega_{\rm eff}>0$ for every $\beta\varrho<1$; under the second, for every $\beta\varrho<(n-1)/n$, beyond which the divisible part of winning pays no more than losing. With $\Omega_s>0$, $\Omega_{\rm eff}\ge\Omega_s>0$ at every $\beta$ under the first convention; under the second $\Omega_{\rm eff}>0$ for every $\beta$ iff $\Omega_s>\Omega_e/(n-1)$, the value at which the positional prize outweighs the divisible loss of a winner who has given everything to the $n-1$ others.

(3) is {{compound}}(1) with the reflexive channel of {{loopgain}} removed when $\chi=0$; the return to influence is then zero ({{influence}}) and the required rate is that of {{corrrate}}(1) without second-order selection. $\square$

*Numerics* [num: SH]. With $n=4$, $r=1$, $c=1$ and $\Omega_e=1$, the uninvadable intensity is 0.25 without a positional part and 0.125 with half the divisible prize shared among all; with $\Omega_s=1$, sharing all of $\Omega_e$ only halves it, from 0.5 to 0.25. With $\Omega_s=0.5$ the uninvadable intensities are 0.300 and 0.275 at $\beta=0.3$, and 0.225 and 0.175 at $\beta=0.6$, under the all-$n$ and the $n-1$ conventions respectively, each equal to $r\Omega_{\rm eff}/(nc)$ and with no mutant gaining on a grid. Strategic actors follow the same two prizes with the Nash intensity $r\Omega_{\rm eff}(n-1)/(n^2c)$: with $\Omega_s=0$, 0.13125 against 0.11250 at $\beta=0.3$, and 0.07500 against 0.03750 at $\beta=0.6$. In a common field of intensities $(0.2,0.05,0.1,0.1)$ the higher-intensity carrier out-earns the lower by 0.017 with half shared, and the difference turns negative only beyond 55% shared. An earlier version said that only changing what winning converts into reverses the direction; in this model none of the levers makes the selected intensity zero while a positional prize remains.

**Remark {{#twoorders}} in full.** At the first order, more contenders make the race fiercer ({{join}}(4)), and in a model of developers racing under a shared existential hazard, fragmentation raises speed and conditional risk, while a developer able to commit first to a slightly slower speed gains because its rivals slow too (Bueno de Mesquita, Dziuda and Polborn 2026; EV11). At the second order, cultivated influence rises with $\sum_kp_k^{1+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$ ({{corrrate}}(4)), so merging the frontier into one carrier maximizes the return to capturing its correctors. A merged frontier also raises connectivity and with it common-mode failure, so it keeps modules of independent lineage inside it ({{topology}}), and its correction outside it.

### Proof of Theorem {{#safeguards}}, and the margin on both hosts

(1) (I2) with margin in rule-making gives a full row sum below one, so $x_{\rm rule}\to0$ ({{certificate}}(2)); once (I1) is enacted, $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$ exceeds $\nu$ after a finite time, load has negative private payoff, and under replicator dynamics $E\to e_{\min}$. On the host {{eq:host}} the long-run level is then $1-h_Ve_{\min}/\rho$, which exceeds $V_{\min}$ iff $h_Ve_{\min}<\rho(1-V_{\min})$; V6.1 left this condition implicit in "a carrier of low enough load", and its earlier viability clause, "viable if, in addition, the host stays above its floor while the load falls", assumed what was to be shown and is withdrawn. Viability over the transient is a condition on the minimum of $V$ along the path. Along the path the race share declines at a rate increasing in $\Delta=\tau_{\rm eff}-\nu$, so by comparison the whole load path, and with it the host's path, is monotone in $\Delta$: a larger margin lowers the race share faster and keeps the host higher at every date. The set of margins at which the minimum stays above the floor is therefore an interval $[\Delta_{\min},\infty)$, and $\Delta_{\min}$ is unique and depends on the host ($\rho,h_V$), the floor, the initial composition and the speed of selection. On a host whose per-capita regeneration is lower at every level and negative below a threshold $V_u$, a transient that crosses $V_u$ is not recovered, so the margin must keep the minimum above $V_u$, and a normative floor below $V_u$ would make crossing the floor a loss that is not the first. For unanticipated harms, (I2) in evaluation gives $x_{\rm ev}\to0$, so masking falls to $m_{\rm out}<m_{\rm fl}$, or the conservative rule of {{alarm}} applies, and the alarm fires above the floor.

(2) Without (I1), {{load}} applies; without (I2) in rule-making, $x_{\rm rule}\to1$ and $\tau_{\rm eff}\to0$; without (I3), the silent crossing of {{slowroute}} follows; with rule-making captured first, the floor can be crossed when rule-making adapts slowly ({{slowroute}}).

(3) If $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\to+\infty$, the high-load carrier's cumulative advantage over every lower-load carrier is unbounded, and the argument of {{load}} applies under (reach): the host crosses its floor. Hence viability at every horizon requires that $B$ not tend to $+\infty$. That implies $\limsup_t\frac1t\int_0^t\tau_{\rm eff}\ge\nu$, since an average that stayed at most $\nu-\epsilon$ beyond some $T_0$ would send $B$ to infinity; the converse fails, as the numerics show. $\square$

*The transient and the margin* [num: HO]. On the logistic host {{eq:host}}, with liability in force from the start at $\Delta=0.08$ and the race strategy holding 99% of activity, the host dips to 0.1397 before recovering to 1.000 with $e_{\min}=0$, and to 0.1136 before recovering to 0.840 with $e_{\min}=0.1$. The minimum margin $\Delta_{\min}$ that keeps the transient above the floor is 0.108, 0.165 and 0.355 for floors of 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5 with $e_{\min}=0$, and 0.125, 0.192 and 0.414 with $e_{\min}=0.1$. Three independent integrators, a closed-form share with its own stopping rule, a replicator integrated jointly with the host, and the memo's own code, agree on these margins to 0.002. On the pointwise harsher host $\rho_A(V/A-1)(1-V)$, which degrades by itself below its threshold $V_u(e_{\min})$, with $e_{\min}=0.1$ and the same initial composition, $\Delta_{\min}$ is 0.260, 0.306, 0.359, 0.423 and 0.505 for $A=0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20,0.25$, against $V_u$ of 0.254, 0.308, 0.364, 0.421 and 0.481; a shortfall there is permanent, where on the logistic host any $\Delta>0$ recovers eventually (with $\Delta=0.02$ the logistic host falls to 0.0025 and still returns to 0.840). For $A\ge0.10$, $V_u$ lies above a normative floor of 0.3, so the floor must be set no lower than the host's threshold for crossing it to be the first loss. For (3): a liability $\tau=\nu-1/(2\sqrt{t+1})$ has a time average tending to $\nu$, yet from a race share of 0.1% the host crosses its floor at $t\approx89$; with $\tau=\nu$ it never does. The averaged condition of R1 is necessary and not sufficient; the cumulative one is the condition the proof uses.

These margins are an instance of the boundary of viable parameters under one fixed strategy of correction. The correctable kernel of {{kernel}} takes a union over admissible strategies and an intersection over disturbances in the justified range, and is not computed here; V6.1 called this "the first computable instance of the correctable kernel", which overstated it.

### Rival verification and participation, in full

**{{!Proposition:rivalverify}} (Rival verification) [thm].** *A rival's verifier whose full inbound row sum is below one, counting conversion from the verified party, from its own side and from shared sources such as models, supply chains and professional paradigms, is captured by none of them ({{certificate}}(2)). Rivalry keeps the verified party's contribution near zero; it does not by itself bound the rest of the row.*

*Proof.* Apply {{certificate}}(2) to the verifier's full row. A verifier taking 0.10 from the verified party and 0.95 from shared sources is captured once those sources are, whatever the rivalry. $\square$

*Scope.* Rival verifiers raise false alarms, can collude against third parties, and check compliance rather than judgment. A verifier biased toward finding violations is an uninformative sender of unverifiable claims ({{talk}}), so it must rest on evidence the other side can check. An accusation under rivalry can trigger prevention ({{preventown}}), so joint resolution precedes any response.

**{{!Proposition:participate}} (Participation) [thm; num: legacy v5, K7].** *Two actors have private types, strong or weak, with costs $c_s<c_w$, each strong with probability $p_s$ independently. Outside the arrangement, types are revealed in a complete-information all-pay contest for $\Omega+L_b$: a strong actor against a weak one gets $U=(\Omega+L_b)(1-c_s/c_w)-L_b$, otherwise $-L_b$; the race's survival is $\Phi^{\rm race}$. The arrangement yields a common value net of costs $S_a$, divided in shares $\mathsf s_i$, with survival $\Phi^R$; utility is $\Phi_i(V_i+\bar V)$. (1) Under complete information and transferable utility, a pair gains iff $\Phi^R(S_a+2\bar V)\ge\sum_i\Phi^{\rm race}_i(U_i+\bar V)$. (2) With private independent types and participation decided before types are revealed, Bayesian incentive compatibility makes each type's interim expected share independent of its report, budget balance makes the interim shares constants summing to one, and the arrangement is feasible without verification iff $\Phi^R(S_a+2\bar V)\ge\sum_i\sup_cE\big[\Phi^{\rm race}(c,t_j)(U_i+\bar V)\mid c\big]$; ex-post shares may still vary with reports. (3) Verification revealing types removes the information rents, which is distinct from timely detection of defection. (4) The information condition relaxes as $\Phi^{\rm race}/\Phi^R$ falls. (5) Under complete information an overwhelmingly strong actor takes part only for a share near its race payoff; raising the joint value, lowering the value of winning, compressing survival or changing cost asymmetry can still make equal shares feasible.*

*Numerics* [num: legacy v5, K7]. With $c_s/c_w=0.1$, $\Omega=1$, $L_b=0$, $S_a=1$ and every $\Phi=1$, the sum of suprema is 0.36, 0.90, 1.44 and 1.71 at $p_s=0.8,0.5,0.2,0.05$: feasible without verification in the first two cases and not in the last two, although cooperation is efficient for every pair; with $\Phi^R=0.95$, $\bar V=1$ and $p_s=0.2$, equal shares become feasible once $\Phi^{\rm race}/\Phi^R\le0.872$ (`../v5/checks_v5.py`, K7, run under `--legacy`).

*Scope.* Rare strong types expect much from the race: mutual optimism, a failure of bargaining under private information (Fearon 1995). Which mechanism is second-best depends on the mechanisms allowed and on commitment, not solved here. Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against domination are three different quantities in this model: (1) and (2) price the first, say nothing about the second, and the third is a constraint fixed before the shares are set (Section 10.3).
