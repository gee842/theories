# Part IV. Transition and judgment

Part IV asks how a change in what is selected can be reached from where the trajectory stands, and what is warranted now. Its results on trust, signals and pauses hold in the models they state; its tracks, readiness dimensions and requirements of a reachable path are definitions, each row with the support it cites; its precedents are observations whose limiting conditions are part of what they show; and its judgment is a recommendation under the standard of Section 8.1.

## 11. A reachable transition

> **Claim.** An end state that every participant would keep is not thereby one they can reach [def]. A path is reachable when it is survivable at every stage, preferred by every participant at every step given its beliefs, and held at every step until the slow variable that step moves has crossed its threshold [def]. Where a step moves a parameter without carrying the state across its threshold, or is released on levels before its dwell time, it is re-absorbed, so graduated steps reach only as a ratchet [thm∣prem]. Where the first steps are equilibria without trust, as narrow E1 is among actors who price in a certain lock, or costly signals that separate a survival-first actor from a relative one, trust is an outcome of the transition, not its precondition [thm]. A pause that changes nothing buys time, not survival [thm]. In the field model the hold a reform needs grows with the age of capture, without bound unless a lineage of the alternative persists that can be recruited when the rule changes [num].
>
> **Why.** Trust is needed to cooperate against a possible defector, to be believed and to stay, and a lower value of winning relaxes all three at once [thm]. In the field model rule support falls only at the rate at which it is corrected, while shares follow it with a lag, so levels improve before safety does [thm]. Each commitment moves a slow variable with its own time constant and meets its own compensating loop, so the commitments run as parallel tracks, started slowest first and binding in the order of the trust they need [def].

### 11.1 Trust, and what relaxes it

Trust enters a transition in three places. To cooperate against a possible defector, an actor needs its belief $\varpi$ that the other will cooperate to reach the threshold $g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ of {{assure}}. To be believed, a sender needs the receiver not to discount what it says for what it gains by saying it, which relative accounting erodes ({{talk}}). To stay, a participant needs confidence that the arrangement will persist, which the net gain from exit erodes where it grows with the capability held in check ({{overhang}}). Reliable instruments let parties cooperate without good will, but installing them is itself a first step taken under $\varpi$.

**{{!Corollary:relaxtrust}} (What relaxes the trust constraints) [thm].** Whatever lowers the value of winning relative to the joint value relaxes all three constraints at once; verification relaxes the first only on what it observes; a sanction relaxes the first and the third only while it can still be imposed. *In the notation of {{assure}}, {{talk}} and {{overhang}}:*
1. *Raising the value of mutual restraint relative to the value of winning, so that $T-R$ falls while $R>P-F$, raises $g_C$ and lowers both the belief threshold and the timely detection $(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ that cooperation needs ({{verify}}(1)); a wider working objective, a larger $\theta$, lowers the effective bias $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$ of talk; and a smaller relative term in every payoff lowers the net gain from exit, $V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay}$.*
2. *A larger probability $\upsilon$ of timely detection raises $g_C$ and, where $P>S$, lowers $g_D$, so it lowers the belief threshold; it carries no information about what it does not observe ({{talk}}(3)).*
3. *A larger sanction $F$ raises $g_C$, and it deters exit while $\upsilon F\ge V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay}$. It cannot be imposed on a defector whose position has become decisive, so against such a deviation it acts only through the bound on the breakout probability ({{enforce}}).*
4. *Lowering the vulnerability of restraint, $P-S$, lowers $g_D$ and with it the belief threshold.*

*Proof:* S11.

Sharing the divisible stakes and provision beneath the game lower $T-R$ and the relative term of exit; where they also lower the price of standing, they widen working objectives ({{standprice}}) and with them the range of informative talk [thm∣prem]. Sanctions relax assurance and exit at the price of escalation, which the comparison counts in $\Theta$ (Section 8.1). Trust is mainly an outcome of the transition, not its precondition. It follows from conduct that the lower value of winning makes rational and verification makes visible; the one step it must precede is the installing of instruments, and the first steps of Section 11.2 need none. In the author's words, "Trust is not a prerequisite—it is an outcome. It arises only when we have a shared understanding of reality and can reasonably expect others to act accordingly" [corpus: selfishness-spiral, 2025-03-14].

Not every obstacle is a lack of trust. Misjudged shared losses call for evidence; a real conflict of incentives, for changed payoffs and enforcement; a coordination failure, for verification and graduated steps (Osgood 1962); commitment and capture over time, for independent correction and a staged transition. A graduated step taken without trust is safe against a decisive defector where it leaves one's own detection and response intact, since then it does not raise a rival's chance of reaching a decisive position before it is answered, $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ ({{detectlink}}) [thm]. Narrow E1 is the first such step. It removes AI from decisions that are irreversible within the time for deliberation, not from the detection that answers a breakout, and among actors who price in a certain lock it is a gain, not a cost ({{delegatelock}}(1)).

### 11.2 Prediction, evidence and signals

A prediction about conduct, once those it describes act on it, enters the conduct it predicts. Models of behaviour can be performative, and also counter-performative, making the world less like themselves (MacKenzie 2006) [obs]. The prediction of this paper is meant to be of the second kind. It is conditional: where actors act on the working objectives the game selects, the game carries them toward an outcome none of them would choose. An actor that holds it, and finds that restraint gives it higher survival whether its rival restrains or races, restrains whatever it believes about the others ({{survassure}}(2)) [thm]. Shared, such a prediction can prevent its own fulfilment, and that is what a prediction written for coordination is for.

Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because the hazard is shared ({{talk}}(3)): a prediction carried only by assurances meets the third erosion. It has to be carried by what an actor can check without trusting the sender: a derivation from public premises; verifiable evidence, such as third-party disclosure of incidents and joint evaluation suites; protocols agreed in advance (Kamenica and Gentzkow 2011); demonstrations that make a dynamic visible; and costly signals [def]. Shared understanding removes one road to conflict, since under common priors mutual optimism alone cannot produce war (Fey and Ramsay 2007) [thm]. It does not remove the others: in an idealized experiment, falling behind drove unsafe choices while the stated level of shared risk changed them little, a finding of exploratory analysis (EV11) [obs]. A change of prediction is the first step, not a sufficient one. The next is conduct that a rival can read, which is also what tips a professed doctrine that few hold in private (Section 5.4).

**{{!Proposition:signal}} (When keeping AI out of one's own decision loops is a signal) [thm].** A step that the lock makes cheap for a survival-first actor and the contest makes costly for a relative one separates them; a step that is cheap to reverse buys restraint only in proportion to what it costs a relative actor. *A sender is survival-first and prices in the lock (type $\mathsf S$, with probability $1-\mu$) or holds a relative objective (type $\mathsf R$, with probability $\mu$). It can take a step $\mathsf E$: a verifiable, durable removal of AI from its own strategic decision loops for a period, open to inspection, which costs type $\mathsf t$ the amount $c_{\mathsf t}$. A receiver restrains iff its belief that the sender is $\mathsf S$ is at least $\varpi^\ast\in(0,1)$; its restraint is worth $\Pi_{\mathsf t}>0$ to type $\mathsf t$, including, for $\mathsf R$, what $\mathsf R$ gains by exploiting it.*
1. *An equilibrium in which $\mathsf S$ takes the step, $\mathsf R$ does not, and the receiver restrains after the step and not otherwise exists iff $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}$ and $c_{\mathsf R}\ge\Pi_{\mathsf R}$.*
2. *If $0\le c_{\mathsf R}<\Pi_{\mathsf R}$, there is no such equilibrium. Where prior trust falls short, $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, the receiver restrains after the step with probability at most $c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ in every equilibrium, a bound reached where $\mathsf R$ imitates just often enough to hold the receiver's belief at $\varpi^\ast$.*
3. *A step that is reversible or unverifiable, $c_{\mathsf R}\to0$, is therefore cheap talk: it buys no restraint that prior trust did not already give.*

*Scope.* Whether the step separates is a question of measured costs. Where the lock is certain, keeping AI out raises the survival-first type's own survival ({{delegatelock}}(1)), so $c_{\mathsf S}$ can be zero or negative and that type takes the step whatever the receiver does; a relative type delegates at every speed ({{delegate}}(3)), so a durable removal costs it the speed it forgoes for the period, and the longer the period, the larger $c_{\mathsf R}$. Where prior trust falls short and the inequalities of (1) are strict, the separating equilibrium is the only one that survives the intuitive criterion. *Proof:* S11 [num: TR].

Unilateral, verifiable narrow E1 is therefore a candidate first move. The step is cheap for an actor that prices in the lock and costly for one that values the speed delegation buys, which is the difference a signal needs [thm∣prem]. It answers a misjudgment of a rival's present objective; a correct forecast of what the game will select is answered only by changing the game (Section 3.8).

### 11.3 Four arrangements, and readiness

Keeping AI out of the contest, stopping, slowing and pausing are different choices, with different costs, different verification and different failures. Section 5.3 defined stopping (E2) and slowing (E3) by what they do to the growth channel of risk; here each is stated as an arrangement.

**E1: AI out of the contest.** *The narrow form* keeps human authority over the decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty: strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the time available for deliberation, such as the use of force and nuclear command. It comes with jointly verified limits, drilled fallback capacity, and an evidence channel independent of AI, so that a human judging an alarm has reasons of their own. Among actors who price in the lock it is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust, and "near certainty" is exact: the lock must fail with a probability below a bound that falls as the speed advantage grows ({{delegatelock}}(1); {{nearlock}}) [thm]. In tactical domains, where the speed advantage is large and the lock uncertain, delegation can be an equilibrium ({{delegatelock}}(2), (3)); bounded, reversible defensive automation, such as response in cyber defence within stated limits, falls under the test of O2 (Section 6.6), not under E1. *The broad form* is the scope: AI not an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, in labour, finance and persuasion as much as over shared conditions, judged by the game and not by the act and enforced at the three layers of Section 6.6. **Broad E1 is the scope; narrow E1 is the first step.** E1 is held in common. An actor that keeps AI out of the whole conduct of the contest alone, while others race, can survive less than by joining, as where staying out raises a rival's chance of a decisive position (X11) [num: TR]; that is why E1 begins with the decision loops in which the lock makes it an equilibrium.

**The deterrence objection.** Removing human discretion makes a threat credible, since a commitment that cannot be revoked is worth more than one that can (Schelling 1960), and in its strongest form semi-automated retaliation would relieve the pressure to launch on warning. Survivable, human-controlled forces give the same relief without removing judgment. The commitment value of automation is the lock itself, O2 lost in that domain ({{lockin}}). An automated commitment cannot tell a false alarm from an attack, and false alarms of early-warning systems have been stopped by human judgment, as in 1983, when an officer judged an alarm false and did not report an attack (Podvig 2002; Hoffman 2009) [obs: EV18]. And escalation at machine speed on a false alarm is a common risk that relative selection does not see ({{blind}}), so the contest under-prices it [thm∣prem]. X11 gives the answers in full.

**E2: stop.** Capability of the decisive class is not built while the conditions for governing it are absent: an accurate model of the coupling, the shared prediction, and common custody. Where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage, it is rolled back, which ends exposure fastest ({{freeze}}). For divergences that hide under evaluation, E2 and E3 are what lies beyond admission ({{masked}}). Its cost, if AI turns out not to be decisive, is the delay of benefits, including lives that earlier benefits would have saved; because E2 applies only to the decisive class, what is delayed is that class's margin over capable systems that are not decisive. **The cost can be large (crux C10)**, and it is irreversible for those who bear it. It does not foreclose later choice, while if AI is decisive the loss it avoids forecloses choice for everyone: the asymmetry of irreversibility concerns the option set, not the size of the losses (Arrow and Fisher 1974). Its coverage comes from consent or from coercion; coercion's risk of escalation is counted in $\Theta$, and the conditions under which a coercive restriction of frontier inputs enters even the comparison of designs are stated once, in Section 12.1.

**E3: develop at the pace of understanding.** In each hazard class, capability grows no faster than contestable coverage can follow, so the uncovered hazardous share stays summable ({{coverage}}) and each hazard mode keeps its robustness margin ({{tempo}}). It binds class by class, where coverage falls behind, and it binds a world that has stopped racing (Section 5.3). Its carriers are thresholds in capability and in effective compute tied to measured coverage per class (Section 10.4). Stopping is not slowing: E2 forgoes the decisive class; E3 paces each hazard class to its coverage.

**What can be verified.** E2 and E3 are the most verifiable among rivals, since chips, fabs, datacentres and compute can be observed. Thresholds stated in compute erode: with pre-training efficiency doubling about every eight months (90% interval about two to twenty-two months, on data to 2023), a nominal compute cap must tighten roughly two and a half to three times a year at the central estimate, a rate condition that verification must meet (EV9) [obs]; hardware mechanisms for verifying compute use are proposed, not deployed (EV17) [obs]. E1 is the hardest to verify, since it concerns software and internal procedure, and the most self-enforcing among actors who price in the lock, which is why it can begin as declared doctrine with inspected exercises.

**A temporary pause** is the fourth arrangement, and by itself it changes nothing.

**{{!Proposition:pausevalue}} (A pause is valued by what changes during it) [thm].** A pause that changes nothing buys time, not survival. *Let $h_t$ be the residual hazard of the irreversible event in the $t$-th active round, and let a pause add no hazard of its own. (1) If the same process resumes after the pause, so that the $t$-th active round has the same $h_t$, survival over any number of active rounds is unchanged, and so is its limit, which is zero where the $h_t$ are not summable ({{nonsum}}). (2) A pause after which the residual sequence is summable, where before it was not, keeps survival bounded away from zero. (3) In the field model, a pause that freezes shares while ordinary rule support keeps accumulating completes capture from every frozen share above $1/G$ ({{pausefield}}).*

*Proof:* S11 [num: TR].

A pause buys time to change the causal conditions: verification, residual hazards, human capacity, enforceable limits, shared provision, objectives and institutions. An indefinite stop, a lasting cap, or a pause that produces a different decision process is a different policy. **A pause with no relevant change is not a certificate of readiness to resume.**

**{{!Definition:readiness}} (Readiness to resume) [def].** *Readiness is assessed along seven dimensions, each with observable indicators; a threshold set by the risk decisions of the domain of use, with the error of measurement inside it; and an assessor whose own row sums are certified and who is insulated from rule-making during the pause. A dimension is met when its indicator has stayed above its threshold for its residence time, at least the time constant of the slow variable on which the dimension rests. Each dimension is decided by a sequential test registered in advance with two error rates: the rate of declaring it met when it is not, set by the protection standard, since resuming too early is the error that cannot be undone; and the rate of declaring it unmet when it is met, bounded so that the seven together hold back a ready state with no more than a registered probability. All seven must be met; none compensates for another.*

| Dimension | Question | Slow variable; the residence time is at least |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Technical reliability | Has the residual hazard of the relevant classes fallen, by evidence independent of the developer and valid against evaluation awareness? | Residual risk per class; the class's manifestation window ({{tempo}}) |
| Q2 Shared objectives | Have governing purposes moved from relative advantage toward shared provision? | The self-models that governing positions select; a turnover of those positions |
| Q3 Strategic incentives | Has the value of winning fallen relative to the joint value? | The won share of capacity, $\omega$ ({{scissors}}); a period of growing stakes, so that provision is seen to keep pace with them |
| Q4 Effective correction | Can evaluators obtain evidence, keep independent funding and execute findings? | Rule support in each corrector domain; its dwell ({{dwell}}), not the return of shares to their former level |
| Q5 Deployment constraints | Is the tempo of capability and delegation bound to correction capacity? | Coverage against hazardous variety; a generation of capability, with catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours level across it |
| Q6 Retained human agency | Can people still take over delegated functions? | Human capacity $\mathcal M$; its recovery time ({{lockin}}) |
| Q7 Custody and verifiability | Is decisive-class capability in common custody, and conduct verifiable faster than breakout? | The capability held in check; a period in which it grows, since the net gain from exit grows with it ({{overhang}}) |

These are dimensions to operationalize, not a checklist proved sufficient. They do not require that every person's private motives change first: binding institutions can change the effective game while private competitive preferences persist, which is why effective correction is among them. They presuppose the rows of {{require}} that no pause supplies and readiness does not ask, R1, R2, R7 and R8b ({{crosswalk}}). The residence condition is there because levels move before slow variables cross: shares fall back while rule support still sits in the basin of capture (Section 11.4). The decision rule is there because noise and conjunction together bias a fixed reading toward "not ready": seven dimensions, each read once with a 5% chance of missing a met dimension, pass a ready state with probability $0.95^7\approx0.70$; a pause held past its evidence raises the cost of staying, and coverage tips once that cost exceeds the cost of standing outside ({{unravel}}) [thm∣prem]. A sequential test turns noise into delay instead of bias, at error rates fixed in advance ({{readrule}}) [num: TR]. Readiness is the release condition of the second tier of Section 12.1, read on its own indicators rather than on those of activation read backwards. Resuming unchanged after a pause would repeat the process that made the pause necessary.

**The order.** E1 first, since it keeps humans as the players and, in its narrow form, is a strict equilibrium among actors who price in the lock; then E2 or E3, class by class: stopping before the decisive class is built, or bringing development below the speed at which understanding and response can follow.

### 11.4 Tracks, dwell and windows

Each commitment of the transition moves a slow variable, named in the third column of the table below. A commitment holds only if its slow variable crosses its threshold before the commitment is withdrawn ({{reabsorb}}), and the slow variables have different time constants. Started one after another in the order of the trust they need, the slowest would start last and finish after the window it serves had closed. The commitments are therefore tracks that run in parallel, started in the order of their time constants, slowest first, and binding in the order of the trust they need, from the first track to the fifth [def]. The two orders differ, and the start follows time: rule support in the corrector domains moves slowest, custody has to be prepared further ahead of need than any warning gives, and the openness of the fifth track has to be built in from the first, so tracks that bind late begin early. The tracks are a candidate to be tested, not a claim that every setting follows them: civilian services, frontier development and strategic decisions can need different paths (X11).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. | 2. | 3. Fund correction and shared services | A formula levy; evaluators with mandated access; services with continuity guarantees; reward separated from authority over correction | Rule support in each corrector domain; several institutional turnovers, more the deeper the lock-in ({{dwell}}) | Selecting the selectors; accounting | The formula becomes a prize; capture moves to the funders; reformers are selected out on the way to office | Row-sum certificates; lineage shares |

| 5. **Dwell.** A reform that insulates rule-making from shares lets rule support decay at its rate of correction, $\varepsilon d$, but shares answer to rule support and lag it: they keep rising while support stays above break-even, for $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$ [thm], and they are back at their starting level before the state has left the basin of capture [num: DW]. A rollback judged on share levels is therefore premature (Test D); what decides is where rule support stands against the separatrix. From a captured share of 0.99 the hold that is needed runs, in units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, from 1.89 to 10.45 as $G$ rises from 2 to 10 when rule-making is ten times slower than adoption, and from 5.86 to 16.18 when the two are equally fast [num: DW]. It grows with the depth of lock-in: at equal speeds and $G=2$ it is 5.86 from a share of 0.99 and 28.9 from $1-10^{-12}$, and at the captured state depth grows with the age of capture, so without a floor under the alternative the hold has no bound [num: DW]. A lineage of the alternative that persists inside selection, and is recruited when the rule changes, bounds it: with $G=10$ and rule-making ten times slower, a floor of 0.1% cuts the hold from 10.45 to 3.81, while a share protected outside selection, which does not recruit, leaves it at 10.44 ({{dwell}}) [num: DW]. These holds take the conversion gain as fixed during the reform; where capture has built capacity that goes on converting, the gain stays higher and so does the hold [num: DW]. For real institutions the magnitudes are a conjecture [conj].

That is the mechanism of decay and renewal. In the field model, an order in which forms can fail and be replaced can be reformed in bounded time, and one that has frozen cannot [num: DW]. In the author's words, "look at the ecosystem, death is not a bug but a feature, changes and mutations are what keeps things alive — create a rich space of systems, not reducing to one rule, one attractor" [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-06-05]. This is the second job of plurality that Section 10.4 names: lineages kept inside selection and able to grow make reform possible in bounded time, as well as decorrelating errors.

**The ratchet.** Graduated steps reach the threshold only as a ratchet [thm∣prem]. Each step has to carry its own slow variable across its threshold and be held there for its dwell before it is released, since a step that moves a parameter without crossing is re-absorbed ({{reabsorb}}) and one released on levels is premature. A transition is not shown to arrive by small steps that each feel safe; it arrives, if it does, by steps each of which is kept.

**Windows.** Whether frontier development is still the keystone, the domain whose reform would take down the captured domains outside the remaining core, is a conjecture [conj]. The window closes as conversion among the corrector domains grows (Section 4.5), which is why the correction track cannot wait for the trust that custody needs. Arrangements of this kind are often adopted when a crisis opens a window (Gunderson and Holling 2002) [obs]. A design prepared in advance, with its activation committed, is what a crisis can adopt; one adopted in a crisis must state what would end each emergency closure before adoption, or it will not afterwards. Smaller jurisdictions, users and those who depend on services controlled by others need standing on every track, because they cannot leave the shared exposure.

**Where things stand.** The first track has seeds in an international scientific assessment and a UN scientific panel, which has issued a first report and whose appointment one leading state opposed, calling the panel and the global dialogue beside it an overreach. The second has seeds in an affirmation by two leading states of human control over nuclear use and a bilateral dialogue with an incident channel, while the most recent multilateral declaration on military AI was endorsed by neither leading state and the most recent General Assembly resolution on it was opposed by one; narrow E1 has no multilateral document that both leading states now endorse. Meanwhile one leading state's official rejected a drift toward global governance, and two developers' written commitments were made contingent on rivals (EV2, EV7, EV8) [obs]. The seeds must become binding, verifiable and broader before the relevant losses or lock-in.

### 11.5 What a reachable path requires, and precedents

Read as a problem of control, the table asks whether the present state lies in the set from which the target can be reached and then held under admissible steps, the capture basin of viability theory (Aubin 1991). In the one-domain field model the hold of Section 11.4 is the minimum capture time ({{dwell}}); the basin for several domains, with disturbances, is not computed.

**Precedents.** The record of restraint among rivals qualifies all of this, and each precedent's limiting condition is where its analogy to AI is weakest (X11) [obs]. The 1946 proposals for international ownership of atomic energy failed on sequence and on enforcement against violators: **custody must precede decisiveness**. The Biological Weapons Convention shows that a weapon endangering its holder can be renounced unilaterally, where its military value was low and the renouncer held another deterrent, and that without verification a covert programme can persist. Nuclear power after a serious accident shows durable safety under unchanged motives where the shared loss returns to its source and no prize is decisive, with later lapses: a change of selection, not of motive. The Montreal Protocol shows coverage made attractive by provision and enforced by conditions of access, once substitutes were available and the value of winning was low. Rivals survived where nature or balancing supplied saturation; if advantage in AI is unsaturated, as the racers' own premise holds, safety must come from changing what is selected before advantage becomes decisive [thm∣prem].

Section 12 takes from here the release rule of its second tier, readiness held for its residence time and judged against the dwell, and the reason its slowest preparations are warranted now: what binds late has to begin early.
