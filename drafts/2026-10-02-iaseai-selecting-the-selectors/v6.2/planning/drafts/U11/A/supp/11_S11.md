## S11. The transition

### Proof of {{relaxtrust}}

Write, as in {{assure}}, $g_C=R-\upsilon(P-F)-(1-\upsilon)T$ and $g_D=(1-\upsilon)(P-S)$. Where both are positive, the belief threshold $\varpi^\ast=g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ falls as $g_C$ rises and rises with $g_D$.

*(1)* $\partial g_C/\partial R=1$ and $\partial g_C/\partial T=-(1-\upsilon)\le0$, so raising $R$ or lowering $T$ lowers $\varpi^\ast$. The timely detection that cooperation needs, $u^\ast=(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ from {{assure}}(2), has $\partial u^\ast/\partial R=-1/(T-P+F)<0$ and $\partial u^\ast/\partial T=(R-P+F)/(T-P+F)^2$, positive iff $R>P-F$. The effective bias of {{talk}}(1), $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$, has derivative $-2(n-1)b_0/(n-1+\theta)^2<0$ in $\theta$. The net gain from exit is $V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay}$ ({{overhang}}(1)), and an agreement that preserves relative positions preserves its relative term ({{overhang}}(3)), so lowering that term lowers the gain.

*(2)* $\partial g_C/\partial\upsilon=T-P+F>0$ under the premise of {{assure}}(2), and $\partial g_D/\partial\upsilon=-(P-S)<0$ where $P>S$. Verification reaches only what it observes: messages about the rest remain cheap talk, credible only where the effective bias is below $\frac14$ ({{talk}}).

*(3)* $\partial g_C/\partial F=\upsilon>0$; exit is deterred while $\upsilon F\ge V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay}$ for an irreversible, public exit, and while $\upsilon F\ge(1-\upsilon)(V^{\rm exit}-V^{\rm stay})$ for a reversible covert preparation ({{overhang}}(1)). A defector whose position has become decisive is beyond punishment, so against a deviation with $D_i>v_i$ restraint holds only through $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$ ({{enforce}}).

*(4)* $\partial g_D/\partial(P-S)=1-\upsilon\ge0$. $\square$

*The link to the levers* [thm∣prem]. Sharing a fraction $\beta$ of the contested stakes scales them by $1-\beta$ ({{racehazard}}), which lowers what racing against a restrainer gains over mutual restraint; provision beneath the game lowers the won share of capacity and with it $\Gamma_\delta$ ({{scissors}}; {{compound}}); and a lower $\Gamma_\delta$ lowers the price of standing and widens the working objective ({{standprice}}). The stage payoffs $T,R,P,S$ of {{assure}} are reduced forms of those contests, so the link holds where they are read from them.

### Proof of {{signal}}

The receiver restrains with probability $\rho_m$ after message $m\in\{\mathsf E,\mathsf N\}$, where $\mathsf N$ is not taking the step: $\rho_m=1$ if its posterior that the sender is $\mathsf S$ exceeds $\varpi^\ast$, $\rho_m=0$ if it falls short, and any $\rho_m$ at equality; after a message no type sends, any belief, and so any $\rho_m$, is admissible. Type $\mathsf t$ receives $\rho_{\mathsf E}\Pi_{\mathsf t}-c_{\mathsf t}$ if it takes the step and $\rho_{\mathsf N}\Pi_{\mathsf t}$ if not.

*(1)* In the candidate the posteriors are 1 after the step and 0 without it, so $\rho_{\mathsf E}=1$ and $\rho_{\mathsf N}=0$, since $\varpi^\ast\in(0,1)$. $\mathsf S$ takes the step iff $\Pi_{\mathsf S}-c_{\mathsf S}\ge0$, and $\mathsf R$ refrains iff $\Pi_{\mathsf R}-c_{\mathsf R}\le0$.

*(2)* If $c_{\mathsf R}<\Pi_{\mathsf R}$, $\mathsf R$ gains $\Pi_{\mathsf R}-c_{\mathsf R}>0$ by imitating the candidate of (1), which is therefore not an equilibrium. Let $0\le c_{\mathsf R}<\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ and $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, and take any equilibrium. The posteriors on the path average to the prior $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, so at most one message on the path has a posterior of $\varpi^\ast$ or more. If $\rho_{\mathsf N}>0$, then either nobody omits the step, so that the posterior after it is $1-\mu$ and $\rho_{\mathsf E}=0$, or the posterior after $\mathsf N$ is at least $\varpi^\ast$ and the one after $\mathsf E$, if $\mathsf E$ is sent, is below it, so again $\rho_{\mathsf E}=0$. If $\rho_{\mathsf N}=0$ and $\rho_{\mathsf E}>c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$, $\mathsf R$ takes the step with certainty, and whatever $\mathsf S$ does the posterior after the step is $(1-\mu)\sigma_{\mathsf S}/\big((1-\mu)\sigma_{\mathsf S}+\mu\big)\le1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, so $\rho_{\mathsf E}=0$, a contradiction. Hence $\rho_{\mathsf E}\le c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ in every equilibrium. The bound is reached in the equilibrium in which $\mathsf S$ takes the step, $\mathsf R$ takes it with probability $\sigma_{\mathsf R}=(1-\mu)(1-\varpi^\ast)/(\mu\varpi^\ast)\in(0,1)$, which holds the posterior after the step at $\varpi^\ast$, and the receiver restrains with probability $c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ after the step and not otherwise; $\mathsf R$ is then indifferent, and $\mathsf S$ takes the step iff $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$.

*(3)* follows from (2) as $c_{\mathsf R}\to0$. $\square$

*Refinement.* Let $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, $c_{\mathsf S}<\Pi_{\mathsf S}$ and $c_{\mathsf R}>\Pi_{\mathsf R}$. The step is strictly dominated for $\mathsf R$, whose payoff from it is at most $\Pi_{\mathsf R}-c_{\mathsf R}<0$. In any equilibrium in which $\mathsf S$ does not take the step, the posterior without it is $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, so $\rho_{\mathsf N}=0$ and $\mathsf S$ receives 0, while the step could give it up to $\Pi_{\mathsf S}-c_{\mathsf S}>0$. The intuitive criterion (Cho and Kreps 1987) then puts all weight after the step on $\mathsf S$, the receiver restrains after it, and $\mathsf S$ deviates; and no hybrid exists, since $\mathsf R$ never takes a dominated step. The separating equilibrium is the only one that survives. Where prior trust is already sufficient, $1-\mu\ge\varpi^\ast$, an equilibrium without the step and with restraint exists beside it, and the step is not needed.

*Numerics* [num: TR]. A search over strategy profiles, which does not use the conditions it tests, agrees with (1) in 300 random games and finds no equilibrium above the bound of (2) in 200, and the bounding equilibrium in every one of the 135 in which $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$. With $c_{\mathsf S}=-0.05$, $c_{\mathsf R}=0.30$, $\Pi_{\mathsf S}=0.40$ and $\Pi_{\mathsf R}=0.20$ the step separates. If it can be reversed cheaply, $c_{\mathsf R}=0.05$, it does not: with $\mu=0.5$ and $\varpi^\ast=0.6$, $\mathsf R$ imitates with probability 0.667, and the step buys restraint with probability 0.25 at most.

### Proof of {{pausevalue}}

*(1)* Let $h_t$ be the conditional probability of the irreversible event in the $t$-th active round, given survival to it, on each surviving history. A pause that adds no hazard of its own changes the calendar date of each active round and nothing else, so survival over the first $N$ active rounds, $\prod_{t\le N}(1-h_t)$ on each history, is the same with or without it, and so is the limit. By {{nonsum}}, it is zero where the $h_t$ are at least a non-summable sequence. *(2)* Where after the pause $h_t\le\bar h_t<1$ with $\sum_t\bar h_t<\infty$, survival is at least the product of the rounds before the change and $\prod_t(1-\bar h_t)>0$. *(3)* is {{pausefield}} with ordinary rule-making, $b_p=b$. $\square$

*Example* [num: TR]. With residual hazard 0.01 a round for 20 rounds and $0.01/t^{1.5}$ in the $t$-th round after a change, survival to round 10,000 is 0.80, and its limit is 0.80; with the hazard left at 0.01 a round it is $2\times10^{-44}$.

### The decision rule of readiness

**{{!Remark:readrule}} (Readiness under noise and conjunction) [thm].** A reading fixed in advance biases a conjunction of seven dimensions toward "not ready", more so as noise grows; a sequential test turns noise into delay. *(1) If each of seven dimensions, when met, is declared met with probability $1-\mathsf b_q$, independently, a ready state is declared ready with probability $\prod_q(1-\mathsf b_q)$, which is $0.95^7\approx0.70$ at $\mathsf b_q=0.05$. (2) Let each dimension be decided by a sequential probability-ratio test between a registered level below its threshold and one above it, with boundaries set for error rates $\mathsf a_q$ (declaring it met when it is not) and $\mathsf b_q$ (declaring it unmet when it is met), on independent readings with a non-degenerate distribution. The test ends with probability one, and its error rates are at most $\mathsf a_q/(1-\mathsf b_q)$ and $\mathsf b_q/(1-\mathsf a_q)$ whatever the noise (Wald 1945). Over seven dimensions a ready state is held back with probability at most $\sum_q\mathsf b_q/(1-\mathsf a_q)$.*

*Proof.* (1) is a product of independent events. (2) is Wald's termination theorem and his bounds for boundaries $\ln\big((1-\mathsf b_q)/\mathsf a_q\big)$ and $\ln\big(\mathsf b_q/(1-\mathsf a_q)\big)$ on the log-likelihood ratio, with a union bound over the seven. $\square$

*Numerics* [num: TR]. A fixed reading of 16 observations, designed for a 1% rate of false "met" and a 5% rate of missed "met" at unit noise, passes a met dimension with probability 0.95 at that noise and 0.37 at twice it; seven met dimensions then pass together with probability 0.71 and 0.001. Sequential tests with $\mathsf a_q=0.01$ and $\mathsf b_q=0.05/7$, on Gaussian readings at levels 0 and 1, keep both observed error rates below their bounds at noise 1, 2 and 4, while the mean number of readings needed to pass a met dimension rises from 10.5 to 38.7 and 148.6, about as the variance. Over seven such dimensions a ready state is held back with probability at most $0.05/0.99\approx0.0505$.

The residence condition of {{readiness}} sets the window over which the readings are taken: no test may end before the residence time has passed, so a dimension cannot be declared met before its slow variable could have crossed its threshold. The rates are registered asymmetrically because the errors are: declaring a dimension met too early is the error the protection standard weighs, since resuming cannot be undone; holding it back too long is bounded, since a pause held past its evidence spends coverage ({{unravel}}).

### Dwell, depth and a recruitable lineage

Take one domain of the field model {{eq:field}} with its own conversion $b$ and conversion gain $G=ab/(cd)>1$, and write it in the logit of the share, $u=\ln\big(x/(1-x)\big)$, and rule support in units of break-even, $\tilde z=az/c$: then $\dot u=\zeta c(\tilde z-1)$ and $\dot{\tilde z}=\varepsilon d(Gx-\tilde z)$. Capture is the state $x\to1$, $\tilde z=G$; absence of capture is locally stable, and the two basins are divided by the separatrix, the stable manifold of the interior saddle. A reform that insulates rule-making sets the conversion to zero for a time $T$ and then releases it.

**{{!Proposition:dwell}} (Dwell, depth and a recruitable lineage) [thm; num: DW].** *Start an insulating reform from the captured state with share $x_0$. (1) Rule support decays as $\tilde z=Ge^{-\varepsilon dt}$; the share keeps rising for $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$, and its logit is back at $u_0$ when $G(1-e^{-s})=s$, $s=\varepsilon dt$ [thm]. (2) The reform holds after release iff the insulated state has crossed below the separatrix, and the least such $T$, the dwell, exceeds the time at which the share is back at its starting level: from $x_0=0.99$, the dwell is 1.89, 5.32 and 10.45 times $1/(\varepsilon d)$ for $G=2$, 5 and 10 when rule-making is ten times slower than adoption, and 5.86, 10.34 and 16.18 when the two are equally fast, against a return to the starting level at $s=1.59$, 4.97 and 10.0 [num: DW]. (3) At the captured state the logit grows at the rate $\zeta c(G-1)$, so the depth of lock-in grows linearly with the age of capture [thm]; the dwell rises with depth, each further unit of logit depth adding about $\varepsilon d/(\zeta c)$ to $\varepsilon dT$, and so has no bound over the age of capture [num: DW]. (4) A lineage of the alternative that persists inside selection at a share of at least $\phi_{\rm alt}$, and grows when the rule changes, keeps the depth below $\ln\big((1-\phi_{\rm alt})/\phi_{\rm alt}\big)$ and bounds the dwell whatever the age of capture; a share of the alternative protected outside selection, which does not recruit, does not [num: DW].*

*Numerics* (DW: forward integration in logit coordinates, and independently a backward-integrated separatrix with the analytic insulated path, agreeing to three decimals). Depth without a floor, with $G=2$ and equal speeds: from $x_0=1-10^{-1}$, $1-10^{-2}$, $1-10^{-3}$, $1-10^{-6}$ and $1-10^{-12}$ the dwell is 3.45, 5.86, 8.17, 15.08 and 28.90; with $G=10$ it is 13.78 to 39.22; with rule-making ten times slower the increments are a tenth as large (1.59 to 4.34 at $G=2$; 10.21 to 12.76 at $G=10$). A floor on the alternative, with $G=10$ and rule-making ten times slower: 3.52 at $\phi_{\rm alt}=0.01$, 3.81 at 0.001, 4.60 at $10^{-6}$ and 6.04 at $10^{-12}$, against 10.45 without; with equal speeds, 9.48 and 11.79 at the first two floors, against 16.18. Re-seeding the alternative at the rate $\phi_{\rm alt}\zeta(G-1)$ also bounds the dwell (3.28 at $G=10$, rule-making ten times slower, $\phi_{\rm alt}=0.001$; 4.12 at $10^{-6}$), but re-seeding is symmetric and also re-seeds the practice wherever it has been removed. A share protected outside selection, $b\to b(1-\phi_{\rm alt})$ with $\phi_{\rm alt}=0.001$, leaves the dwell at 10.44.

*Proof of (1) and (3).* Under insulation $\dot{\tilde z}=-\varepsilon d\tilde z$, so $\tilde z=Ge^{-\varepsilon dt}$ and $u(t)=u_0+\zeta c\big[G(1-e^{-\varepsilon dt})/(\varepsilon d)-t\big]$, which rises while $\tilde z>1$ and is back at $u_0$ when $G(1-e^{-s})=s$. At the captured state $\tilde z=G$ and $\dot u=\zeta c(G-1)$. Once rule support has decayed, the logit falls at a rate approaching $\zeta c$, so each further unit of depth costs about $1/(\zeta c)$ of time. $\square$ (2) and (4) are numerical; the monotonicity of the dwell in depth, which (4) uses to turn a cap on depth into a cap on the dwell, is observed in every case computed and not proved.

*Scope.* One domain, quasi-static conversion and a fixed $G$. The dwell rises with $G$, so capacity that capture has built and that goes on converting keeps the effective gain, and the dwell, up. With several domains the reform must also take down the core that sustains the domain ({{reabsorb}}). For real institutions the magnitudes are a conjecture; the comparison they support, that a floor under a recruitable alternative bounds the hold and a frozen order's hold grows with its age, is the claim of Section 11.4. The share-freezing pause has its own length, given with {{pausefield}} in S4: a frozen share accumulates no lock-in.

*Dwell as capture.* In the language of viability theory (Aubin 1991; Cardaliaguet, Quincampoix and Saint-Pierre 1999), take insulation as the admissible control and the basin of absence as the target. The dwell is the minimum capture time from the captured state, and the states from which a hold of at most $T$ reaches the target form a capture basin. With several domains, controls that act on conversion as well as on rule support, and disturbances, the basin is not computed; that is why {{reachable}} is a table of requirements and not a capture-basin proposition.

### The tracks and their time constants

Each track of Section 11.4 rests on a slow variable, and the order in which the tracks start follows their time constants.
- *Track 1, the shared account of hazards*, rests on shared understanding, which cannot run ahead of the evidence: for a class whose evidence arrives only after a manifestation window, no measurement shortens it ({{tempo}}).
- *Track 2, narrow commitments*, rests on human capacity $\mathcal M$, which recovers from delegation in $\varrho_h^{-1}\ln\big[(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M)/(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M_{\min})\big]$ ({{lockin}}) and, where correction rests on it, also sets how fast correction must grow ({{humancorr}}).
- *Track 3, correction and shared services*, rests on rule support in each corrector domain, with the dwell of {{dwell}}. If $1/(\varepsilon d)$ is an institutional turnover of five to ten years, an assumption and not an estimate, the dwells computed there run from about a decade to more than a century [num: DW, with the assumed turnover]. That is why this track starts first and cannot wait for the trust of Track 4.
- *Track 4, delegation, access, custody and decision rights*, rests on the capability held in check, whose growth raises the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}); custody's installation and verification must take less than the time to a decisive position, so its preparation runs furthest ahead of need.
- *Track 5, broadening*, rests on the record of the arrangements under stress, which accrues only through cycles of stress and recovery, and on the self-models formed inside them, which change over generations. After a recovery, mobilization falls back and certificates can fail again ({{countervail}}), the regulatory cycle; openness of membership has to be built in from Track 1, since a bloc that closes early is hard to open later [conj].

### {{reachable}}, row by row

*Existence* rests on {{commonfrontn}}, {{participate}} and {{safeguards}}; *reachability* on {{assure}} with {{relaxtrust}}, {{survassure}}, {{delegatelock}} and {{signal}}; *survival of the transient* on {{safeguards}}(1), {{lockin}}, {{coverage}} and {{restraint}}; and *persistence* on {{overhang}}, {{certificate}}, {{rivalverify}}, {{reabsorb}}, {{dwell}} and {{unravel}}. The table claims no more than its rows. No result shows that the four can be met together, and graduated steps are shown to reach only as a ratchet, each step carrying its own slow variable across its threshold and held for its dwell, since a step that does not cross is re-absorbed ({{reabsorb}}) and one released on levels is premature ({{dwell}}).
