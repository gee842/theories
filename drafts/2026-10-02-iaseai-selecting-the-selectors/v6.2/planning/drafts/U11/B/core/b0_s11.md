# Part IV. Transition and judgment

Part IV asks how a change in what is selected is reached, and what is warranted now. Its claims are practical judgments, which need a stated standard, a comparison of credible paths and sensitivity to disputed premises (Section 1.4).

## 11. A reachable transition

> **Claim.** An attractive end state is not a reachable one [def]. Where the conditions of Section 2 hold during the transition, a path reaches a changed selection only if it is survivable at every stage, preferred by every participant at each step given its beliefs, and held at each step for its dwell time, so that graduated steps reach the threshold only as a ratchet [thm]. Trust is mainly an outcome of the transition, not its precondition [thm∣prem].
>
> **Why.** The conditions act on the transition itself: a change withdrawn before rule support has fallen below break-even is re-absorbed ({{reabsorb}}) [thm], and a prediction carried only by assurances meets the third erosion ({{talk}}) [thm].

### 11.1 Trust, and what relaxes it

Trust enters the transition in three forms: the belief $\varpi$ that the other will cooperate ({{assure}}), confidence in the instruments of verification, and confidence that the arrangement will persist. Reliable instruments let parties cooperate without good will; installing them is a first step, and a first step is taken under $\varpi$. Three constraints carry the trust a transition needs, and each depends on the same quantity. *Assurance:* cooperation is a best response only where $\varpi$ reaches $g_D/(g_C+g_D)$, and where the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating ($T>R$) verification has to supply what belief does not. *Talk:* the weight on winning biases every message about the shared hazard ({{talk}}). *Exit:* a commitment holds only while the sanction keeps pace with the net gain from leaving, which grows with the capability held in check ({{overhang}}).

**{{!Corollary:relaxtrust}} (What relaxes the trust constraints) [thm].** *Where the private value of winning falls relative to the joint value, $T-R$ falls, and with it the weight on winning, the bias of messages and the net gain from exit: all three constraints relax at once. Verification lowers the assurance threshold on the dimensions it covers and carries no unverifiable information. Sanctions relax assurance and exit at the price of escalation, and no sanction reaches a defector whose position has become decisive. Lowering the vulnerability of restraint, $P-S$, lowers the assurance threshold, as a common frontier does.*

*Proof:* S11.

Not every obstacle is a lack of trust, and the remedy follows the obstacle. A misjudged shared loss calls for evidence; a real conflict of incentives for changed payoffs and enforcement; a coordination failure for verification and graduated reciprocation (Osgood 1962); commitment and capture over time for independent correction and a transition held long enough to hold. Graduated steps answer the third obstacle only: they do not move a conflict of incentives, and against capture they reach only as a ratchet (Section 11.4). Where the levers of the corollary are pulled, trust is produced by the transition; what makes the shared prediction credible before they are pulled is the next question.

### 11.2 Prediction, evidence and signals

A prediction about a game whose outcome depends on what the players believe is one of the game's inputs. Where racing persists because each expects restraint to be exploited while a cooperative equilibrium exists (Section 5.4), a derivation from public premises that each actor checks for itself moves $\varpi$, and the equilibrium with it; where the players' model of one another is the error, the model that corrects it changes what they do. That is counter-performativity: a model can make the world less like itself (MacKenzie 2006), and it is the sense in which this theory hopes to make its own prediction false (Section 0). Its condition is the one the previous subsection drew: it acts where the outcome turns on a belief the prediction can change, a misjudged shared loss, a mistaken forecast of a rival's objective, a coordination failure. It does not change what the game selects regardless of belief, and where racing is dominant in a one-shot game the belief it would correct is simply correct.

Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because the hazard is shared, since a sender gains from the receiver's restraint whatever it intends ({{talk}}(3)). The prediction is therefore carried by what a receiver checks rather than believes: a derivation from public premises; verifiable evidence, such as third-party incident disclosure and joint evaluation suites; protocols agreed in advance, which commit the sender's disclosures before it knows what they will show (Kamenica and Gentzkow 2011); demonstrations that make a dynamic visible; and costly signals (Kydd 2000). Under common priors mutual optimism alone cannot produce war (Fey and Ramsay 2007); in an idealized laboratory race, in exploratory analysis, falling behind drove unsafe choices while the stated shared risk changed them little (EV11). A changed prediction is the first step and not a sufficient one: the payoffs that make falling behind unsurvivable have to change as well, which is what the arrangements below and Section 12 are for.

**{{!Proposition:signal}} (When keeping AI out of one's decision loops is a signal) [thm].** *Let a sender be survival-first and price in the lock, or hold a relative objective, and let it be able to remove AI verifiably and durably from its own strategic decision loops. The step separates the types, so that a receiver restrains after it and not otherwise, iff it costs the survival-first type no more than the receiver's restraint is worth to it and costs the relative type at least what it gains by inducing restraint. A reversible or unverifiable step is cheap talk.*

*Scope.* Whether it separates in practice depends on measured costs. By {{delegatelock}} the survival-first type's cost is zero or negative where it prices in a certain lock, so the condition that binds is the relative type's, and durability and inspection are what raise that type's cost. *Proof:* S11.

Unilateral, verifiable narrow E1 is therefore a candidate first move. It corrects a misjudgment of a rival's objective; it does not correct a correct forecast of what the game will select, which is why the arrangements that follow act on the game.

### 11.3 Four temporal arrangements, and readiness

Keeping AI out of the contest, stopping, slowing and pausing are different choices, and this subsection is their home: Section 5.3 defined stopping and slowing by what they act on, and Section 6.6 states how the first is enforced.

**E1: AI out of the contest.** The *narrow form* covers the decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty: strategic and escalatory decisions irreversible within the time available for deliberation, such as the use of force and nuclear command. Human authority over them is kept with jointly verified limits, drilled fallback capacity and an evidence channel independent of AI, so that a human judging an alarm has reasons of their own. Bounded, reversible defensive automation within stated limits, such as response in cyber defence, falls under the test of O2 in Section 6.6, not under E1. Among actors who price in a lock they take to be certain ($q_L=1$), keeping AI out of these loops is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust ({{delegatelock}}); where the lock is uncertain, as in tactical domains with a large speed advantage, delegation can be an equilibrium as well, and the step is then the signal of {{signal}} rather than an equilibrium. The *broad form* is the scope: AI not used as an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, in labour, finance and persuasion as much as over shared conditions, the unit being the game, not the act; Section 6.6 states the criterion and the three layers of its enforcement. The narrow form is the order in which to begin, not the scope [corpus: alignment-selection, 2026-09-26]. E1 is held in common: staying out of the contest alone while others race can be worse than joining ({{survassure}}), which is why it begins with decision loops, where it is self-enforcing, and is extended by agreement.

Deterrence theory objects to the narrow form: removing human discretion makes a threat credible (Schelling 1960), and automated retaliation would relieve the pressure to launch on warning. The commitment value is bought by removing the possibility of human overrule, which is the lock of {{lockin}} and, in its domain, O2 given up by choice; survivable, human-controlled forces give the same relief without removing judgment; an automated commitment cannot tell a false alarm from an attack, and in 1983 an officer judged an alarm false and did not report an attack (Podvig 2002; Hoffman 2009); and escalation at machine speed is a common risk that relative selection does not see ({{blind}}), so the contest under-prices it. X11 gives the answers in full.

**E2: Stop.** Capability of the decisive class is not built while the conditions for governing it are absent: an accurate model of the coupling, the shared prediction, and common custody. Where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage, it is rolled back, which is the fastest way to end exposure ({{freeze}}). Because E2 applies to the decisive class only, its cost, if AI turns out not to be decisive, is the delay of that class's margin over capable systems that are not decisive, including lives that earlier benefits would have saved. The cost can be large (crux C10) and, for those who bear it, irreversible; it does not foreclose later choice, whereas if AI is decisive the loss avoided forecloses choice for everyone. The asymmetry of irreversibility concerns the option set, not the size of the losses (Arrow and Fisher 1974). Coverage of E2 comes from consent or from coercion; coercion's escalation risk is counted in $\Theta$, and it enters the comparison of Section 12.1 only under conditions of its own. For divergences that hide under evaluation, admission reaches only what evaluation can see, so systems are admitted up to the capability at which control protocols that do not assume their cooperation stay safe, and beyond it E2 or E3 holds for that class ({{masked}}): the strongest alignment reason for either.

**E3: Develop at the pace of understanding.** The uncovered hazardous share stays summable ({{coverage}}) and each hazard mode keeps its robustness margin ({{tempo}}). Development directed at need, with deliberately bounded hazardous scope and correction that keeps growing, is a sufficient route ({{enough}}), and so is correction whose productivity AI raises as fast as it lowers the cost of control ({{corrrate}}); what defeats it is correction resting on a human capacity that delegation erodes ({{humancorr}}). Stopping and slowing are different arrangements: E2 acts on what is built, E3 on how fast, and a world that has left the race still needs E3.

**A temporary pause** is valued by what changes during it.

**{{!Proposition:pausevalue}} (A pause is valued by what changes during it) [thm].** *If the same process resumes after a pause, with the same residual sequence shifted, survival over any fixed number of rounds afterwards is unchanged and so is the long-run limit. A pause that makes the residual sequence summable where it was not keeps survival bounded away from zero. In the field model, a pause that freezes shares while ordinary rule support accumulates completes capture from any frozen share above $1/G$; with rule-making insulated it undoes capture from any frozen share below $x_\sigma$ iff it lasts at least $\ln\big(G/\hat S(x_p)\big)/(\varepsilon d)$, where $\hat S(x_p)$ is the height of the separatrix at the frozen share ({{pausefield}}; {{dwell}}).*

*Proof:* S11.

A pause buys time to change the causal conditions: verification, residual hazards, human capacity, enforceable limits, shared provision, objectives and institutions. An indefinite stop, a lasting cap or a pause that produces a different decision process is a different policy. **A pause with no relevant change is not a certificate of readiness to resume**, and a pause judged by share levels is misjudged, because levels improve before safety does (Section 11.4). What readiness is, then, has to be stated.

**{{!Definition:readiness}} (Readiness to resume) [def].** *Readiness is assessed along seven dimensions. Each has observable indicators, a threshold set by the risk decisions of the domain of use, a residence condition, and an assessor whose own row sums are certified and who is insulated from rule-making during the pause. A dimension is met when its indicator has stayed past its threshold, by a margin covering the indicator's measurement error, for the time constant of the slow variable the dimension rests on; a dimension once met is re-opened by a reading below threshold sustained for a stated fraction of that time, not by a single reading. All seven must be met and none compensates for another; they are judged one by one as each meets its residence, not all at one date.*

| Dimension | Question | Slow variable that sets its residence |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Technical reliability | Has the residual hazard of the relevant classes fallen, by evidence independent of the developer and valid against evaluation awareness? | The evidence lag of the class, $\tau_e$ ({{tempo}}) |
| Q2 Shared objectives | Have governing purposes moved from relative advantage toward shared provision? | The formation of minds and doctrines, generational [conj] |
| Q3 Strategic incentives | Has the value of winning fallen relative to the joint value? | The reinvestment that prices standing ({{standprice}}), moved by sharing and provision at the pace of institutional change |
| Q4 Effective correction | Can evaluators obtain evidence, keep independent funding and execute findings? | The turnover of rule support, $1/(\varepsilon d)$, and the hold it sets ({{dwell}}) |
| Q5 Deployment constraints | Is the tempo of capability and delegation bound to correction capacity? | The correction lag $\tau_c$ against the hazard's growth rate ({{tempo}}) |
| Q6 Retained human agency | Can people still take over delegated functions? | The recovery of human capacity after delegation, at rate $\varrho_h$ ({{lockin}}) |
| Q7 Custody and verifiability | Is decisive-class capability in common custody, and conduct verifiable faster than breakout? | Detection latency against the time to a decisive position ({{detectlink}}) |

The residence condition answers a bias the definition would otherwise carry: seven noisy indicators read at one date and all required at once say "not ready" more often than the world warrants, and that is pressure to exit; read one by one over their own windows, each dimension's error is its own, and a met dimension is not lost to noise. These are dimensions to operationalize, not a checklist proved sufficient, and they do not require that every person's private motives change first: binding institutions change the effective game while private competitive preferences persist, which is why effective correction is among them. Where each stands today is recorded in X11 and TESTS. Resuming unchanged after a pause repeats the process that made the pause necessary.

**The order, by trust and by verifiability.** By the trust each needs, E1 comes first, since it keeps humans as the players and is a strict equilibrium among actors who price in the lock; then E2 or E3, stopping before the decisive class is built, or bringing development below the speed at which understanding and response can follow. By verifiability, E2 and E3 come first, since chips, fabs and compute can be observed, though with pre-training efficiency doubling about every eight months (90% interval about two to twenty-two months, on data to 2023) a nominal compute cap must tighten roughly two and a half to three times a year at the central estimate (EV9), and hardware mechanisms for verifying compute use are proposed, not deployed (EV17); E1 is the hardest to verify, since it concerns software and procedure, and the most self-enforcing. A third order, by time constant, decides what is started first, and the next subsection gives it.

### 11.4 Parallel tracks, dwell times and windows

The transition is not a sequence of stages ordered by the trust each needs. It is a set of tracks that run in parallel, each with a slow variable and a time constant, and the slowest is started first, because a slow variable that is not moving when it is needed cannot be moved then. The tracks are a candidate to be tested, not a claim that every setting follows them; civilian services, frontier development and strategic decisions may need different leads (X11).

| Track | What is committed | Mechanisms acted on | Slow variable and time constant | Compensating loop to anticipate | Indicator of progress |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Shared account of hazards | A common taxonomy; shared measurement of coverage and residual risk per class, kept apart from measurement of who leads | Language; masking and attribution | Evaluation suites and incident channels: fast, months | Measurement of hazards turned into measurement of who leads; capture of the measurers | Agreement among independent measurements |
| B. Narrow, testable commitments | Narrow E1 with inspection of doctrine and exercises; an incident channel; pre-deployment access above thresholds | Decisiveness; attribution | Human capacity to decide without AI: it decays at $\delta_h$ under delegation and recovers at $\varrho_h$ without it ({{lockin}}) | Delegation moved outside the commitment; cheap imitation of the signal | Inspected exercises; catch rates of planted errors in drills |
| C. Fund correction and shared services | A formula levy; evaluators with mandated access; services with continuity guarantees; reward separated from authority over correction | Selecting the selectors; accounting | Rule support: turnover $1/(\varepsilon d)$, and a hold of two to sixteen such units in the field model ({{dwell}}) | The formula becomes a prize; capture moves to funders; reformers selected out on the way to office | Row-sum certificates; lineage shares |
| D. Delegation, access, custody, decision rights | Broad E1; tempo thresholds; common custody; common frontier projects | Compounding; tempo; decisiveness | The capability held in check, which raises the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}); the lead time of custody against $T_{\rm dec}$ | Leakage to unconstrained actors; overhang under the cap; competition moving to unshared dimensions | Conduct under sharing; overhang against exit hazard |
| E. Broaden as institutions prove themselves | Expansion by evidence: requirements and durability tests met under stress | The boundary of the system | Membership and the formation of minds: the slowest [conj] | Closure into a bloc; after a recovery, mobilization falls back and certificates fail ({{countervail}}) | Openness of membership; standing of non-members |

Slowest first means that Tracks C and D are begun before the trust they will need exists, because their slow variables, rule support and the lead time of custody, run on their own clocks and not on the clock of trust. That is the time-constant reason for the first two tiers of Section 12.1.

**Dwell depth.** In the field model a reform that insulates rule-making from shares has to be held until rule support falls below break-even, $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$, and then long enough to repay the lock-in accumulated meanwhile: in units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, the minimum hold is 1.89, 5.32 and 10.45 for $G=2$, 5 and 10 when rule-making is ten times slower than adoption, and 5.86, 10.34 and 16.18 when the two are equally fast [num: DW]. The share keeps rising during the first phase and is back at its starting level at $s=1.59$, 4.97 and 10.0, well before withdrawal is safe [num: DW], so a rollback judged on share levels is premature: what decides is where rule support stands relative to the separatrix, which is not directly observed, and near full share the depth of lock-in cannot be read from the level at all. The hold depends on that depth. Each unit of logit depth adds about $\varepsilon/\zeta$ units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, and at the captured state the logit grows at $\zeta c(G-1)$ per unit time, so without a floor on the alternative the hold grows with the age of capture, from 5.86 to 28.9 at $G=2$ as the alternative's share falls from one per cent to $10^{-12}$ [num: DW(e)]. A lineage of the alternative that can recruit when the rule changes bounds it: a recruitable lineage held at 0.1 per cent cuts the hold at $G=10$ from 10.45 to 3.81, and one kept outside selection, protected but not recruiting, leaves it at 10.44 [num: DW(b),(d)] ({{dwell}}). That is the mechanism behind a sentence of the corpus: an order in which forms can fail and be replaced can be reformed in bounded time, and one that has frozen cannot. In Ren's words, "I would look at decay as a form of growth" [corpus: possibility-space, 2026-10-02]. The numbers are the model's: for real institutions the dependence of the hold on depth and on a recruitable alternative is a conjecture to test [conj], and the model holds $G$ fixed during the reform, so where captured capacity is a sunk stock that decays slowly, the hold is longer.

**The ratchet.** Graduated steps reach the threshold only as a ratchet: each step crosses its own threshold and is held for its dwell, or it is re-absorbed ({{reabsorb}}); a sequence of steps none of which crosses is a sequence of re-absorptions, whatever trust it builds [thm, in the field model]. Each track has its own slow variable, and the step that crosses in one does not cross in another.

**Windows.** The keystone is still development [conj]; the window closes as conversion among the corrector domains rises, which is why Track C cannot wait for Track D's trust. Arrangements of this kind are often adopted when a crisis opens a window (Gunderson and Holling 2002); a design prepared in advance is what a crisis can adopt, and one adopted in a crisis has to state what would end each emergency closure before adoption, or it will not afterwards. Smaller jurisdictions, users and those who depend on services controlled by others need standing at every stage, because they cannot leave the shared exposure.

**Where things stand.** Track A has seeds in an international scientific assessment and a UN scientific panel, which has issued a first report and whose appointment one leading state opposed as an overreach; Track B in an affirmation of human control over nuclear use and a bilateral dialogue with an incident channel, while the most recent multilateral declaration on military AI was endorsed by neither leading state and the most recent General Assembly resolution on it was opposed by one. Meanwhile one leading state's official rejected a drift toward global governance, and two developers' written commitments were made contingent on rivals (EV2, EV7, EV8). Narrow E1 has no multilateral document that both leading states endorse; its shared basis is the bilateral affirmation (EV8). The seeds have to become binding and broader before the relevant losses or lock-in, and Tracks C and D, the slow ones, have no seed of comparable standing.

### 11.5 Requirements of a reachable path, and precedents

**{{!Table:reachable}} (Requirements of a reachable path) [def, with support].** *A path that changes what is selected has four separate requirements, and what is shown for each differs.*

| Requirement | What is shown, and where | What is not shown |
|---|---|---|
| An incentive-compatible state exists | All cooperating is a strict equilibrium under a common frontier with a moderate value of winning ({{commonfrontn}}) [thm]; the safeguards of {{safeguards}} give viability once in force, with a margin on a harsher host [thm; the margin num]; the forms of Section 10.5 carry their own ends | That the state is reached from here |
| It is reachable | In objectives, reaching it needs trust above the assurance threshold ({{assure}}) [thm]; in survival terms it can need little at the horizons that matter ({{survassure}}) [thm]; narrow E1 needs none among actors who price in a certain lock ({{delegatelock}}, $q_L=1$) [thm]; graduated steps reach the threshold only as a ratchet ({{reabsorb}}) [thm] | That graduated steps alone reach the threshold |
| The host survives the transient | Liability in force before capture keeps the host above its floor, through a transient dip ({{safeguards}}(1)) [thm]; human capacity recovers only where reversion comes before the lock ({{lockin}}) [thm]; the host's clock and the certificate's clock are compared, not assumed ({{slowroute}}) [thm∣prem] | That the two clocks fall in the right order on the present path: a condition to measure |
| It persists | Commitment keeping pace with the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}) [thm]; correctors with full row sums below one, certified ({{certificate}}, {{rivalverify}}) [thm]; rules fixed before capture ({{reabsorb}}) [thm]; each step held for its dwell, bounded by a recruitable lineage ({{dwell}}) [num] | That real institutions have the model's dwell: a conjecture |

Reachability in the sense a controller means it, that the present state lies in the set from which the target is reached while the host stays viable under admissible correction, is defined in S11 and proved only in the one-domain field model, where the minimum hold of {{dwell}} is the minimum capture time; the table is the definition, with the support each requirement has.

The record of restraint among rivals qualifies the table, and the limiting condition of each precedent is where its analogy to AI fails (X11, corrected against the sources). The 1946 proposals for international ownership of atomic energy failed on sequence and on enforcement against violators, which is the content of **custody must precede decisiveness**. The Biological Weapons Convention shows that a weapon endangering its holder can be renounced unilaterally, where its military value was low and the renouncer held another deterrent, and that without verification a covert programme can persist. Nuclear power after a serious accident shows durable safety under unchanged motives where the shared loss returns to its source and no prize is decisive, with later lapses: a change of selection, not of motive. The Montreal Protocol shows coverage made attractive by provision and enforced by conditions of access, once substitutes were available and the value of winning was low. Rivals survived when nature or balancing supplied saturation, and balancing by others is a saturation mechanism independent of technology (crux C5); if advantage in AI is unsaturated, as the racers' own premise holds, safety has to come from changing what is selected before advantage becomes decisive. Section 12 takes from this section the dwell and residence conditions for its second tier, and the time constants that put preparation before trust.
