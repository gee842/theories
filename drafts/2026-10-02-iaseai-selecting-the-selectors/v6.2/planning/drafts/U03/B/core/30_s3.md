## 3. One system: what relative selection cannot see

> **Claim.** All carriers share one host, and relative selection cannot see what all share: neither the host's decline nor a constraint that binds all alike [thm]. Where the coupling between actors is real, a held self-model that draws its boundary inside the system is an error, and selection favours the narrowest [thm]. Near equal standing, wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, a forward-looking actor weighs standing above what all share: with a fixed prize always, and with a prize that grows with capacity exactly where leads compound [thm∣prem, first order]. Each actor computes correctly; the game is wrong [thm]. Against failures that are global and irreversible, integration must be anticipated, and understanding is how actors come to count what selection cannot see [thm: {{>onegroup}}].
>
> **Why.** A term common to every payoff changes no comparison between carriers. A unit of relative advantage reinvested buys a larger share of a future that the common good only enlarges for everyone. At the scale of one host there is no rival host and no second attempt after a failure that cannot be undone.

### 3.1 Levels, measures and the host

Persons, firms, governments and evaluators have different interests: a citizen's continued agency is not a firm's market share or a government's tenure. Two levels are kept apart: *selection*, in which carriers change share and nobody chooses the outcome, and *strategy*, in which actors act on objectives. So are four measures: the *objective* $J_i$, the *material payoff* $\pi_i$, *true welfare* $W_i$, which counts the coupling between actors, and *survival* $\Phi_i$, a stated combination of O1 and O2. A result about selection transfers to strategic actors only if their objective is shown to be the selected one. A condition $V\in[0,1]$ is shared by every actor (secure infrastructure, biosecurity, cyber stability, human control over AI systems, a reliable information environment); it regenerates and is loaded by the field's activity $E$:
$${{!eq:host}}\qquad \dot V=V\big[\rho(1-V)-h_VE\big],\qquad \rho,h_V>0.$$
The host is the part of the shared conditions whose costs do not return to their source through customers, reputation, courts or the source's own exposure.

**{{!Definition:viab}} (Viability) [def].** A trajectory is *viable* if $V(t)\ge V_{\min}$ for all $t\ge0$, where the floor $V_{\min}\in(0,1)$ is a normative line, not an absorbing state.

Every actor persists only while the host does, so viability constrains every trajectory rather than entering as one payoff among others (Aubin 1991). This host is forgiving, regenerating fastest when most depleted and with no threshold of its own: results that a trajectory *crosses* the floor hold a fortiori for hosts that regenerate less at every level; results that it *stays* viable do not, and need a margin (S3).

### 3.2 Relative selection is blind to what all share

Carriers $k=1,\dots,n$ hold shares $p_k$ of the field's activity and run a fraction $e_k\in[0,\bar e]$ of it in ways that load the host, such as deploying ahead of evaluation, each unit bringing a private advantage $\nu>0$; the field's load is $E=\sum_kp_ke_k$, and carrier $k$'s material payoff is
$${{!eq:payoff}}\qquad \pi_k=\phi_k+(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})e_k+G(V),$$
with $\phi_k$ everything else, $G$ nondecreasing, and $\tau_{\rm eff}\ge0$ the effective liability correctors impose. With $\tau_{\rm eff}=0$, $e_k=\bar e$ is the *race strategy*.

**{{!Definition:relsel}} (Relative selection) [def].** A selection rule $\dot p=F(p,\pi)$ is *relative* if $F(p,\pi+\xi\mathbf 1)=F(p,\pi)$ for every constant $\xi$, and *payoff-monotone* if $\dot p_k/p_k-\dot p_l/p_l$ has the sign of $\pi_k-\pi_l$. Replicator dynamics, imitation and market-share competition driven by relative profitability are both (Alchian 1950).

**{{!Lemma:blind}} (Blindness, and what a common rule needs) [thm].** A term common to all is invisible to relative selection; a constraint of equal incidence is neutral among those who comply; and compliance survives where the expected sanction covers the gain from evasion.
1. *Under a relative rule the trajectory of shares is unchanged by any term common to all payoffs, in particular by $G(V(t))$ whatever the path of $V$; under a payoff-monotone rule a common term never changes which of two carriers grows relative to the other.*
2. *Incidence. A constraint costing every complier the same additive amount is a common term among compliers and leaves their relative growth unchanged: equal incidence is what makes a constraint neutral among those who comply, and nothing more. A constraint whose cost differs across carriers is selected on like any payoff difference, so it re-sorts the compliers and picks winners among them; one borne by a single carrier is selected against.*
3. *Evasion. If carriers of type $k$ can evade a constraint costing them $c_k$ against an expected sanction $\bar F_k$, the type's evading variant grows relative to its complying variant iff $c_k>\bar F_k$. Compliance therefore survives cumulative payoff-monotone selection if $\bar F_k\ge c_k$ for every type that can evade, whatever the incidence, and the compliant share tends to one when the inequalities are strict; across types the condition is sufficient, not necessary, since a type whose evasion pays can still be displaced by another type's compliers. If the sanction falls as evaders spread, because detection is funded by compliers or rule-making is captured by evaders, the outcome is set by its endpoints: where it exceeds the cost at full compliance and falls below it at full evasion, $\bar F(0)>c>\bar F(1)$, compliance and evasion are both stable, separated by the tipping share $x^\dagger$ at which $\bar F(x^\dagger)=c$; where $\bar F(1)\ge c$ compliance is globally stable, and where $\bar F(0)\le c$ evasion is.*

*Scope.* The host enters payoffs additively and identically (A1, S0). Part (1) is the known translation invariance of relative dynamics (Weibull 1995); what is added is the separation of neutrality from compliance and the connection to captured enforcement. *Proof:* S3 [num: CI].

The blindness that hides the host is also what protects a common rule: a constraint that covers every capable carrier and whose evasion is sanctioned above its gain turns the pathology into a protection. The two conditions in the lemma do different work. The sanction is what keeps a rule; equal incidence is what keeps a rule from becoming a weapon. A constraint whose cost falls more heavily on challengers than on incumbents is selected on like any payoff difference, so it picks winners among the compliant, and a rule written by the successful can bar entry while binding everyone in name: newcomers adopt it or are out. That is the second direction in which success reaches the selectors, barriers against challengers beside accommodation of the incumbent's own practice ({{>capture}}). A common *liability* on the harmful conduct works differently again: it makes the conduct pay less, and reverses selection once it exceeds the private advantage. Every route needs coverage and a sanction at least the gain from evasion, supplied by correctors that Section 4 shows success can capture.

**{{!Proposition:load}} (Selection raises the load) [thm].** Whatever carriers value, selection favours the conduct that loads the host until liability exceeds its private advantage. *Let selection be payoff-monotone and $\tau_{\rm eff}(t)<\nu$. If $\phi_k=\phi_l$ and $e_k>e_l$, then $p_k/p_l$ increases along every path of $V$, including paths below $V_{\min}$. With a race carrier present and cumulative advantage $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\,ds\to\infty$, the load tends to $\bar e$ and $\limsup_tV\le(1-h_V\bar e/\rho)^+$; hence if*
$${{!eq:reach}}\qquad h_V\bar e>\rho\,(1-V_{\min})\qquad\text{(reach)}$$
*the host eventually falls below its floor, however much any carrier values it. If $B(\infty)<\infty$, it need not.*

*Scope.* Other advantages correlated with restraint can offset the selection (S3). *Proof:* S3.

What carriers value decides which strategies they try, not which ones grow: evolutionary suicide, under stated conditions (Rankin, Bargum and Kokko 2007).

### 3.3 Accounting: what an objective can see

Selection acts as if only differences count; an objective can count more. Let actor $i$ act on
$${{!eq:objective}}\qquad J_i=\pi_i+\theta_i\,\bar\pi_{-i},\qquad\theta_i\in[-1,n-1],$$
where $\bar\pi_{-i}$ is the others' average payoff and $\theta_i$ marks where $i$'s self-model draws its boundary: $\theta=-1$ is the purely relative objective, $\theta=0$ the isolated self, and $\theta=n-1$ counts every contestant equally, and only the contestants.

**{{!Proposition:twochan}} (Two-channel accounting) [thm].** Every change in payoffs splits into a part shared by all and a transfer; the self-model only sets how much of each it sees. *For any change $\Delta\pi$ with mean $\bar\Delta$ and a common $\theta$,*
$${{!eq:twochaneq}}\qquad \Delta J_i=(1+\theta)\,\bar\Delta+\frac{n-1-\theta}{n-1}\,\big(\Delta\pi_i-\bar\Delta\big).$$
*(1) Of a gain or loss shared equally, each objective registers $(1+\theta)/n$ of the total. (2) At $\theta=-1$ the game in objectives is zero-sum and every shared gain or loss is invisible; at $\theta=n-1$ what remains is coordination, not a dilemma. (3) Relative selection is $\theta=-1$, and a weight $\lambda$ on winning, $\pi_i+\lambda(\pi_i-\bar\pi)$, is $\theta=-\lambda(n-1)/(n+\lambda(n-1))$ up to a positive factor. (4) A levy rebated equally enters as $\tau(n-1-\theta)/n$, and breaking a promise to give a fraction $\beta$ of a prize $\Omega$ to the others gains $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$. (5) An action with private gain $g$ that costs the host $H$, borne equally, is taken iff $g>(1+\theta)H/n$: a point of enough in accounting, absent at $\theta=-1$.*

*Proof:* S3, an identity [num: TC], with how two perturbations identify $\theta$ [num: TH], and why identification fails exactly where shared harm goes unseen.

### 3.4 Interdependence and the boundary error

Actors are coupled to one another as well as to the host, and the coupling has two signs. Divide payoffs into a *divisible* part $\pi^{\rm e}$, rents, services and the material gains of capability, and a *positional* part $\pi^{\rm s}$, standing, the power to set rules and the loss of being subordinated; the prize of a contest splits the same way, $\Omega=\Omega_e+\Omega_s$. In reduced form $i$'s true welfare is
$${{!eq:welfare}}\qquad W_i=\pi_i+\kappa\,\bar\pi^{\rm e}_{-i}+\kappa_s\,\bar\pi^{\rm s}_{-i},\qquad\kappa\in[0,n-1),\ \ \kappa_s\in[-1,0],$$
where $\kappa$ counts coupling through the divisible part that prices do not settle (shared hazards, failures propagating through concentrated nodes of common infrastructure, systemic risk) and $\kappa_s$ records that a rival's standing is one's own subordination. The divisible coupling is a material fact, not altruism: no company or nation is vertically integrated across the semiconductor chain, and the loss of one input gas was estimated to cost downstream production over a hundred times its direct value [obs: EV13]. The winner still depends on those it would dominate. *The contest:* each of $n$ actors chooses an intensity $e_i\ge0$, wins a prize $\Omega$ with probability $e_i^r/\sum_je_j^r$, with decisiveness $r\in(0,n/(n-1)]$, pays $c$ per unit, and bears a shared loss $\ell$ per unit of total intensity $\mathcal E$.

**{{!Proposition:boundary}} (The boundary error of the self-model) [thm].** A held self-model narrower than the real coupling raises intensity and lowers its holder's own welfare, and selection favours the narrowest. *(1) For every $\theta\in[-1,n-1)$ the game in objectives has the symmetric equilibrium $e(\theta)=r\Omega(n-1-\theta)/\big(n^2(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, decreasing in $\theta$, and true welfare there rises with $\theta$. (2) Against fixed intensities of the others, $i$'s true welfare is maximized by its best response when it weighs the others' divisible payoffs by $\kappa$ and their positional payoffs by $\kappa_s$; acting on $\theta_i<\kappa$ toward the divisible part lowers it whenever the chosen intensity changes and the maximizer is unique, while toward the positional part relative concern is accurate. (3) Selection on intensities favours what an actor with $\theta=-1$ would choose: the shared loss drops out, and at $r=1$ the prize is fully dissipated and with any shared risk every actor ends below zero (Schaffer 1988 for the finite-population evolutionarily stable intensity; Hehenkamp, Leininger and Possajennikov 2004 for full dissipation in this contest).*

*Scope.* The positional part is the reason the coupling carries two signs: another's gain in standing is one's own loss, so no widening of the self corrects relative concern about it (compare Powell 1991), and the positional part of a prize cannot be divided, only kept from forming or held in common custody (Section 10.2). For $r>1$ asymmetric equilibria with lower total intensity also exist; the symmetric one is used throughout (S3). *Proof:* S3 [num: RH, CB].

Part (2) is the sense in which an isolated held self-model is an *error*, not a preference: it draws a boundary inside one system, and the coupling punishes its holder. Part (3) is the sense in which the error is *selected*; where self-models are observed, a narrower commitment even extracts concessions, so accuracy, like inclusion, has to be built (S3). Because $e(\theta)$ falls with the shared loss at $1+\theta$ times the rate at which it falls with private cost, the ratio of intensity's two responses identifies $1+\theta$: this is the observable Test Γ registers, and it rises as provision widens the working objective [num: SP]. **Selected minds [conj]:** the same selection acts on those who decide; the corpus calls it *molochlization* [corpus: alignment-selection]. What the environment rewards, the mind promotes, and selected minds build the organizations that form the next minds (EV14). **Convergence [conj]:** where, for a planetary technology, unpriced coupling rises with power, because the host is shared and the harms prices do not settle grow with what is contested, the accurate self-model and the inclusive one converge, and understanding that we are one becomes first a matter of seeing correctly (crux C11).

### 3.5 The weight on winning, and how compounding prices it

A weight $\lambda$ on winning turns a commons that every developer would protect on its absolute payoff into one that each depletes: with $\Upsilon>1$ the value to the field of a unit of restraint over its private cost, full depletion is the equilibrium once $\lambda>(\Upsilon-n)/(n-1)$, every developer is worse off than under joint restraint, and under (reach) the host ends below its floor (**{{!Proposition:wow}}**, S3; Armstrong, Bostrom and Shulman 2016). A classical commons needs each actor's share of the loss to be small; selection on relative standing depletes it even among a privileged few, each of which would restrain on absolute payoffs. If exposure to the host falls with share, the host no longer cancels and selection favours insulation as well, so a carrier can stay viable while the host does not, a concentration of power V3 rejects (**{{!Remark:unequal}}**). Rank-based prizes supply some of the weight on winning; the loop of Section 2 supplies the rest wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, whatever anyone values.

**{{!Proposition:standprice}} (Compounding prices standing) [thm∣prem].** Near equal standing, compounding raises the weight a forward-looking actor puts on standing wherever reinvestment returns more through relative standing than through what all share. *In the contest of {{loopgain}}, let the prize depend on total stocks, with $g_j=\partial\Omega/\partial S^j\ge0$ its growth with total stock $j$, and let an actor holding $\theta_0<n-1$ discount at $\delta$ and reinvest its net payoff at the rates $\kappa_j$ at which winnings convert, with the others' conduct fixed. The reference state is a symmetric steady state, or a balanced-growth path when the prize is proportional to capacity. Write $\lambda_{\rm rel}$ for the discounted own future winnings bought by a unit of relative payoff reinvested, and $\lambda_{\rm com}$ for those bought by a unit received by all and reinvested by all. Then $1+\lambda_{\rm rel}=1/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$, with $\Gamma_\delta$ the discounted loop gain of {{loopgain}}(3) evaluated at the reference state, and $1+\lambda_{\rm com}=1/(1-\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta)$, with*
$$\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta=\sum_j\frac{\delta\kappa_jg_j}{1-\delta(1-d_j)}$$
*the common loop gain, both gains lying in $[0,1)$. To first order the actor acts on the objective of {{twochan}} with the working self-model $\theta$ given by*
$${{!eq:thetagamma}}\qquad \frac{1+\theta}{n-1-\theta}=\frac{1+\theta_0}{n-1-\theta_0}\cdot\frac{1+\lambda_{\rm com}}{1+\lambda_{\rm rel}}=\frac{1+\theta_0}{n-1-\theta_0}\cdot\frac{1-\Gamma_\delta}{1-\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta},$$
*so the working self-model is narrower than the held one, $\theta<\theta_0$, if and only if $\lambda_{\rm rel}>\lambda_{\rm com}$.*
1. *Fixed prize. Then $\lambda_{\rm com}=0$, and for $0\le\Gamma_\delta<1$ the working self-model always narrows: $\theta=\theta_\Gamma=\big[n\theta_0-(n-1)(1+\theta_0)\Gamma_\delta\big]/\big[n-(1+\theta_0)\Gamma_\delta\big]$, which falls to $-1$ as $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$ for every $\theta_0<n-1$. With it falls the weight of the common channel: the visibility of the host, the point of enough, the weight of a rebated levy and the gain from breaking a promise all move with $\Gamma_\delta$.*
2. *Prize proportional to total capacity, without provision or diffusion. Then $\Gamma_\delta/\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta=(1-\beta)r$, and the working self-model narrows if and only if $(1-\beta)r>1$, at every horizon: the condition under which equal standing is unstable ({{compound}}(1)). Looking ahead narrows the self exactly where leads compound, and widens it where they do not.*
3. *Beyond the domain. At $\Gamma_\delta\ge1$ no finite first-order weight represents the actor: the discounted value of a small relative advantage $\varepsilon$ is of order $\varepsilon^{a}$ with $a=\ln(1/\delta)/\ln\Lambda^\ast<1$, for $\Lambda^\ast$ the growth factor of a relative deviation ({{tdec}}), so the price of a unit of standing grows without bound as the advantage shrinks. The reading of a high gain is the limit from below.*

*Scope.* First order, at equal standing, with the others' conduct fixed. That net payoff is reinvested is load-bearing: if only gross winnings convert, the relative part of private cost is not amplified and the working self-model is narrower still [num: SP]. Where rivals respond to standing, the first-order form survives with the partial-equilibrium elasticity in place of $r$ (S2), and the number changes: in contests in which a lead discourages the follower, the price at parity is amplified, by $2(n-1)/n$ in a two-period contest with effort [thm] and by about 1.1 to 4.8 times in the two-actor Markov-perfect contests computed at this paper's parameters, up to about 7.9 under high patience, while a laggard's price falls, to below zero with a shared loss, so it stops contesting standing [num: MP]; where a lead instead encourages the follower, the response lowers the price, in one computed case slightly [num: MP]. These strategic numbers are held for outside review. Diffusion separates the two thresholds of (2): the working self-model narrows once $(1-\beta)r>1+\delta\delta_f/(1-\delta(1-d))$, while equal standing becomes unstable only at $(1-\beta)r>1+\delta_f/(\kappa g)$, so an objective narrows before equal standing is lost; and with a fixed prize at a stationary total the objective narrows while equal standing is stable, whenever $0<\Gamma<1$ [num: SP]. *Proof:* S3. The two-period equilibrium equals $e(\theta)$ by construction, an identity; the independent checks are the nonlinear stock model against both closed forms, and the sign rule in fourteen two-period cases including the knife-edge $(1-\beta)r=1$ [num: SP], with the cusp beyond the domain measured by its exponent [num: PD].

**{{!Remark:twoerrors}} (Two errors, and which one understanding corrects).** Two errors are distinct. The *held* self-model can draw its boundary inside the system, $\theta_0<\kappa$ on the divisible part: that is the boundary error of {{boundary}}(2), and accurate understanding corrects it. The *working* objective that compounding prices is not an error for the actor. An actor whose held self-model is the accurate one, $\theta_0=\kappa$, and who counts correctly what a unit of relative payoff buys, acts on the $\theta$ of {{eq:thetagamma}}, which with a fixed prize lies below the isolated self once $\Gamma_\delta>n\kappa/\big((n-1)(1+\kappa)\big)$ [thm: algebra of {{eq:thetagamma}}, S3; num: SP]. That is the correct first-order computation of its interest, and every actor acting on it is worse off than if all acted on $\kappa$ ({{boundary}}(1)): individually accurate, collectively self-defeating. Only a change of the game corrects it. That is the exact sense in which this paper judges the game, not the act. Understanding acts on the first error directly; on the second only through agreement to change what is selected (Section 12.2).

This is the exact form of an older observation, that concern for relative gains depends on how far gains convert into future advantage (Powell 1991), generalized by the common channel: what decides is how far gains convert into advantage relative to how far they grow what all share. Selection on conduct favours the relative objective ({{boundary}}(3)); compounding prices it into the working objective of whoever looks ahead; and the same loop selects at both ends, narrowing the objective of those who can keep pace and excluding those who cannot ({{compound}}(2)) [num: MP]. Growth whose return is common widens the self; decisiveness, which makes the return relative, narrows it. Patience cuts both ways: it strengthens repetition and raises the price of standing.

### 3.6 No outside

The system has one host, and at the planetary scale no rival group of the same kind. Evolution's largest innovations were integrations of units that could replicate alone into wholes (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry 1995), held mainly by interests aligned by construction and secondly by policing; cancer is integration failing. That cooperation within groups was favoured by competition between them is answered by the next result (X1).

**{{!Lemma:onegroup}} (What selection at any level cannot supply) [thm].** A trait whose benefit goes equally to everyone is selected against at every level of grouping. *Let a trait cost its bearer and benefit every member of the population equally. Under every partition into groups and every payoff-monotone rule, the total selection term of the Price equation and every within-group term are non-positive. The between-group term is non-positive when fitness is affine in payoff; otherwise it can take either sign, by Jensen's inequality across groups of unequal spread, without making the total positive. The trait can rise only through the transmission term, by biased transmission such as learning, persuasion or imitation guided by a model of the system, or through a rule that changes the bearer's relative payoff: policing, liability, reward for contribution that does not convert into advantage, an enforced common constraint (Price 1972; Frank 1995).*

*Scope.* A benefit confined to the bearer's own group can be favoured between groups. *Proof:* S3 [num: PR].

At the scale of one host, the benefits of the rules that keep it viable are common to all, so selection among firms, states or blocs cannot favour them. Rules whose benefits are partly local can still be found by polycentric experiment: a plurality of decorrelated units trying different rules above a common floor lets some fail while others carry on, as cells do, and with one global civilization that is both necessary and hard [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. For the common part two routes remain: adoption after failures that can be undone, by learning and by coalitions of the harmed (Becker 1983), and adoption before failure, guided by a model of the system. **Where the failure to be avoided is global and irreversible, only the second acts in time.** That is the formal content of the claim that **understanding that we are one, one interconnected system with one host and no outside, is a long-term requirement if growth and change are favoured**: not because nothing else can integrate at this scale, but because against failures that cannot be undone nothing else acts in time. Understanding acts in the lemma's own terms, as the transmission through which actors come to count what relative selection makes invisible. That such anticipation can succeed has no biological precedent; it is a conjecture that the transition of Section 11 has to make true [conj].

The corpus names five endgames of the dynamic [corpus: selfishness-spiral]: a freeze of mutual destruction; deadlock, a structure frozen by the consensus of the remaining players; a change of narrative by which the players come to see themselves as one whole and gain the ability to cooperate; collapse; and winner takes all. Four are results here: deterrence at saturation (Section 7.2), a captured configuration that cannot state what would end it (Section 4.5), the crossing results (Sections 3.2, 4.6, 5.3), and the decisive winner (Section 5.2). The third is the lemma's transmission term, and it is the purpose of this paper. Integration by shared fate needs exposure made common, not declared; and integration within a part is not integration of the whole. Inclusion that stops at the edge of a club is a club: where the contestants' activity yields them a concave common value and some of its harm falls on those outside the contest, contestants who weigh one another fully still over-produce that harm, and equal blocs that are inclusive within and act as one play the original game among themselves, with the shared loss scaled by bloc size, so every result about the race holds among blocs ({{club}}).

### 3.7 What "we are one" means

The phrase states a property of the system: all carriers belong to one host and are coupled through it and through one another, with no outside. It is not a preference everyone must adopt, and not a weight $\theta$. It does three jobs, each with its own support, and it does them through understanding.
1. **Empirical: shared dependencies.** One host; coupling through channels prices do not settle; no outside to absorb a global failure. The support is specific pathways of shared exposure, not the assertion that everything is connected, and an actor that draws its held boundary inside the system errs about its own interests ({{boundary}}).
2. **Normative: standing.** Everyone who bears the consequences has standing to contest the direction of what affects them, whether or not they compete (V1, V3). The corpus gives the norm three parts: the future kept open for all, the conditions of continuation shared, and no seizure of the shared continuation (共开、共续、不夺共续) [corpus: shengsheng]. It is declared, not derived; inclusion of the contestants alone is a club.
3. **Design: a selection that counts the system.** A system "knows it is one" when its selection counts what its parts depend on: consequences return to their source, success earns reward but cannot buy exemption from constraints that bind all or control over its own assessment, constraints cover every capable actor and are enforced, and correction stays plural. These are the institutional counterparts of the policing and shared fate by which earlier integrations held; Section 8.2 states them once as the marks of a generative order, and Section 10 gives them forms. They are the only remedy for the second error of {{twoerrors}}: a working objective that each actor computes correctly is changed by changing the game, not by argument.

**How the phrase acts: understanding.** None of the three jobs is done by declaring it. A prediction of an end no participant would choose makes coordination possible only if it is shared, so "we are one" is first the content of a shared understanding; that is why this paper is written. What is needed is accuracy, not sentiment: an actor that cares only for its own survival, and sees correctly that its survival runs through the host and the others, understands what is required.

At the level of the self, the accurate boundary is the one the author's account of the relational self describes [corpus: relational-self]. A self is not a point outside its relations that enters them afterwards; it is the action-centre of the relations that form it: body, care received, language, institutions and its own choices. "We are the ecology of our cells," and at the next scale we are the collective we belong to (我们即是集): one can be an individual and cannot disregard the whole, because one is part of what the whole is doing. The isolated self of Ψ1 below is the boundary error held as an identity, and a self that is a knot of relations is neither an atom nor a cell of a collective, which is why the theory needs neither pole of the individual–collective axis. 大同, in the same corpus, is a direction, not an end state: a maintained condition in which the many are nourished together and none swallows the rest (万物并育而不相害). **Oneness is not sameness.** Harmony makes things grow, sameness does not continue (和实生物，同则不继); a whole that suppresses difference becomes another totalizing attractor, and plurality of purposes and of correctors is a requirement of the design (Section 10).

### 3.8 The logic as held: Ψ

Held as a doctrine, the selection and the computation of Sections 3.2 to 3.5 become a logic: an objective that ranks relative standing on the decisive dimension without any point of enough, call it *unbounded relative*, and a selection that favours it without seeing what it costs the whole. **If a winner's logic is of this kind, falling behind cannot be survived, and every actor has reason first to oppose the logic itself** (如果胜利者的逻辑是cancer式的，落后并不可以承受，因此所有人都应该去首先反对这个逻辑) [val; corpus: possibility-space]. What is opposed is a logic, not an actor. "Cancer" in this paper means exactly one thing: the selection of such a subsystem, for its own growth relative to its neighbours, by a selection that cannot see that it is killing its host. A cancer cell does not fail to replicate; it replicates very well. Its failure is that it no longer knows it belongs to a body. The image is never applied to persons, peoples or institutions: doing so would itself be the attribution of the logic that this section warns against. The logic has three parts.

**{{!Definition:psi}} (Ψ, the winning model) [def].** Ψ is the configuration of self-model and doctrine that the selection pressure of winning favours. *Ψ1, the isolated self:* welfare is one's own payoff, others are rivals or instruments ($\theta\le0$). *Ψ2, naive Darwinism:* the world is a contest of all against all, and prevailing certifies fitness and right; "the winner takes all, and the winner justifies all" ($\theta=-1$, with the winners occupying the normative selectors). *Ψ3, winner-take-all compounding:* advantage compounds and converts across domains, and falling behind cannot be recovered (a high loop gain, held as a belief and built into institutions). Ψ includes the doctrine that treats these settings as laws of nature rather than parameters of a game that can be changed.

The corpus calls it the logic of winning (讲赢), as against the logic of reasons (讲理): once there is contention it becomes "I am right", and the self outranks the reasoning; cooperation reasons, contention wins (一旦争，则「我对」，我大于理；合作才讲理，斗则讲赢) [corpus: alignment-selection; jian-and-wenyan]. **Ψ is individually rational under compounding, collectively self-defeating, and false as a doctrine about nature.** *Individually rational:* its conduct wins, by {{boundary}}(3), {{standprice}} and {{blind}}; that competition selects organizations that behave *as if* they maximize (Alchian 1950) is part of this theory, not an objection to it. *Collectively self-defeating:* every actor acting on the narrowed objective is worse off than if all acted on the real coupling ({{boundary}}(1), {{twoerrors}}), and the host is depleted under (reach) however much any carrier values it. *False as a doctrine about nature:* Ψ misreads evolution, whose largest innovations were integrations; it misdescribes human beings, among whom the self-interest model failed in every one of fifteen small-scale societies studied (Henrich et al. 2001; EV14), though the carriers here are firms and states, and for those the point is not their members' nature but Alchian's selection on conduct, which the theory already contains; it treats as laws of nature what {{compound}} shows to be parameters of a game, sharing, convertibility, decisiveness, provision and diffusion; and its clause "the winner justifies all" is rejected by the values of Section 1.3, not by evidence. Ψ is also *partly made true*: Ψ3 holds where institutions built under Ψ make it hold, and in the computed contests the same structural gain supports both a smooth equilibrium and a winner-take-all one, so that the belief that a lead is decisive selects the equilibrium in which it is [num: MP; held for outside review]. In the author's words, "the locally winning strategy has taken the name of rationality" (局部胜出的策略拿走了理性的名字) [corpus: meta-attractor]. Ψ shapes conduct where it is held and where it is attributed: an actor with a wider objective still races if it believes its rivals hold Ψ. A misjudgment of a rival's current model can be corrected by verifiable conduct (Section 11.2); a correct forecast of what the game selects, only by changing what it selects.

What this section hands on is the working objective and the invisibility of what all share: selection cannot see the host, looking ahead narrows the objective wherever the relative return exceeds the common one, and what remains is correction, which Section 4 shows success can buy.
