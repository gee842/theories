## 5. Acceleration and the three erosions

> **Claim.** The conditions of winning accelerate the contest and erode the three means by which any system restrains a harmful pressure: enforcement, which makes restraint pay through the future; correction, which makes costs return in time; and shared understanding, which lets the parties see the same danger and believe one another. Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold, deviation by deviation [thm]. Where the share of capacity that has to be won grows, a larger loop gain raises that chance and narrows the working objectives that set the threshold; where correctors are captured, the sanction that sets the threshold weakens; where the gain from deviating and the value of a decisive position outgrow the surplus of cooperation over punishment, the threshold falls again, each arm in its own model [thm]. Where hazardous variety outgrows contestable coverage, survival tends to zero whoever is racing [thm∣prem]. Between two rivals relative accounting silences talk, and among more it amplifies a pair's conflict by $n/(n-2)$ [thm]. That the arms of the scissors close together in the world is the conjecture the diagnosis tests [conj].
>
> **Why.** Decisiveness takes away the future that enforcement needs; tempo and capture take away the time and independence that correction needs; relative accounting takes away the information that talk carries. Each consequence then acts as a mechanism of the others, and the two blades of enforcement turn on one pivot, the share of capacity that has to be won. An objective with no point of enough has no reason to stop where need is met: growth is not the danger; the mind is.

### 5.1 Acceleration

Where actors contest capability directly, who prevails is decided by the contest of Section 3.4. The case for racing rests on three separate premises: **(decisive)**, $r$ is large; **(fast)**, leads open within short windows; **(unsaturated)**, no level of advantage makes more worthless. A technology can be decisive at a threshold and saturate above it, as nuclear advantage saturated at a secure second strike [obs]. With payoffs $R_i$ when all restrain, $T_i$ when racing against restrainers, $P_i$ when all race and $S_i$ when restraining against a racer: in the objective an actor acts on, restraint is never a best response to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$, which in the contest of {{boundary}} holds for $r<n/(n-1)$, though racing need not then be better in material terms; where falling behind carries a penalty beyond losing the prize, the contest is for the prize plus the penalty, and in the fully dissipative limit joining buys nothing over staying out; and the race begins if any single actor has $T_i>R_i$, so where those events are independent its probability tends to one as capable actors multiply ({{join}}) [thm]. The race is set by the most race-inclined actor, which is why any remedy must cover every capable actor.

**{{!Proposition:dissipate}} (Dissipation) [thm].** The more decisive the advantage, the more of its value is consumed in the race to obtain it.
1. *Under a common self-model $\theta$, the share of the stakes dissipated in private cost and shared loss is $s_{\rm dis}(\theta)=r(n-1-\theta)(c+n\ell)/\big(n(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, increasing in $r$ and $\ell$* [thm].
2. *If the technology's power scales the stakes and the shared loss per unit together while private cost is unchanged, the dissipated share rises toward $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$ for $-1<\theta<n-1$, without bound at $\theta=-1$. Total material payoff stays positive at every scale iff $\theta\ge\theta_g=(r(n-1)-1)/(1+r)$, which rises with $r$* [thm∣prem].

*Proof:* S5 [num: the ceiling $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$ evaluates to 3, 1.86, 1 and 0.33 at $\theta=0,0.4,1,2$ for $n=4$, $r=1$].

What is spent is safety margin, accepted risk and delegated control. In the example of {{boundary}} the accurate weight $\kappa=0.4$ lies below $\theta_g=1$: a gap between what accuracy supplies and what the contest requires, which only institutions, or coupling that grows with power, can close.

An unclaimed frontier postpones the moment the accounting shows, and does not change it. Where a unit of capacity can expand into a frontier whose marginal value falls as the frontier shrinks, destroy rivals' capacity, or rest, an actor at $\theta>-1$ stops expanding at a point of enough, where the frontier's marginal value equals the private cost of expansion plus the weighted cost to the host, and war among copies never pays once the weight on others reaches the ratio of the private cost of destruction to the relative gain from it; an actor at $\theta=-1$ does not see the host it consumes, and its expansion gives way to mutual destruction as soon as the frontier's marginal value falls below the private cost of expansion plus the relative gain from destroying a rival, before the frontier is exhausted ({{frontier}}, with a toy that illustrates the static condition and estimates nothing) [thm]. **Among actors whose accounting is relative, peace is a peace of abundance, not of accounting.** Expansion is an anaesthetic for the winning model. Winning does not end the game: the logic itself is the replicator, a winner is a field in which the same selection continues ({{>raceselects}}), and a self-replicating economy that carries it out into new frontiers carries the same switch with it [corpus: meta-attractor].

### 5.2 First erosion: enforcement

Cooperation among rivals is usually sustained by the future: restraint today is rewarded by restraint tomorrow. Decisiveness weakens the future. Consider a stage game repeated with discount $\delta\in(0,1)$ under perfect monitoring, each actor acting on $J_i$. Let $a^\ast$ be a profile with per-period value $C_i$. For a one-period deviation $a$ by $i$, let $g_i(a)$ be its one-period gain and $p_d(a)$ the probability that it yields, from the next period, a *decisive position*: an absorbing state beyond any punishment, worth $D_i(a)$ per period. Let $v_i$ be $(1-\delta)$ times $i$'s continuation value under the harshest credible punishment, computed in the same game, in which breakouts remain possible during punishment.

**{{!Proposition:enforce}} (The enforcement condition) [thm].** Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold, deviation by deviation, and success lowers the threshold.
1. *$a^\ast$ is sustained by reversion to that punishment if and only if, for every $i$ and every deviation $a$,*
$${{!eq:enforceeq}}\qquad (1-\delta)g_i(a)+\delta p_d(a)\big(D_i(a)-C_i\big)\;\le\;\delta\big(1-p_d(a)\big)(C_i-v_i).$$
*The left side is the temptation, immediate and decisive; the right side is the sanction that remains. The deviation with the largest gain need not bind: a quiet deviation with a small gain and a high chance of breakout can, and that is the kind masking hides (Section 4.4)* [num: ES].
2. *Equivalently, when $D_i(a)>v_i$, $a^\ast$ is sustained against $a$ iff $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)=1-\big[(1-\delta)g_i(a)+\delta(D_i(a)-C_i)\big]/\big[\delta(D_i(a)-v_i)\big]$, which falls with $g_i$, $D_i$ and $v_i$ and rises with $C_i$ wherever it lies in $[0,1)$. $\bar p_{d,i}$ is homogeneous of degree zero in $(g_i,C_i,D_i,v_i)$: stakes that all grow alike leave it unchanged.*
3. *The bound without the breakout term, $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i)$, is exact when $D_i=C_i$, necessary when $D_i\ge C_i$, and sufficient when $D_i\le C_i$.*
4. *As $p_d\to1$ the condition becomes $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$. If $D_i\ge C_i$, sustainable profiles lie within the stage equilibria of the objectives held, strictly within when $D_i>C_i$: restraint must be preferred even to a breakout that succeeds. If $D_i<C_i$, cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium can survive $p_d=1$, and where $D_i\ge v_i$ and $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$, so that $\bar p_{d,i}\ge1$, it survives every $p_d$.*
5. *If punishment is reversion to a stage equilibrium in which both of two actors race, worth $P_i$, and in each period at most one racer breaks out, each with probability $q_b\le\tfrac12$, being subordinated worth $V^{\rm sub}_i$, then $v_i=[(1-\delta)P_i+\delta q_b(D_i+V^{\rm sub}_i)]/(1-\delta+2\delta q_b)$; with independent breakouts, a simultaneous breakout leaving the race as it was, $q_b$ is replaced by $q_b(1-q_b)$. When $D_i+V^{\rm sub}_i>2P_i$, reversion to the race is a lottery the deviator welcomes, and it can fail to deter while the punishment still leaves the deviator below cooperation, $P_i<v_i<C_i$, even where the deviation itself never breaks out* [num: ES; with $C=1$, $P=0$, $D=4$, $V^{\rm sub}=0$, $g=2$, $\delta=0.9$, restraint is enforceable at $q_b=0$ and not at $q_b=0.045$, where $v=0.895<C$].

*Scope.* Perfect monitoring; an absorbing decisive position; a slow breakout enters through $p_d(a)$ as the chance that it becomes decisive before punishment can act ({{>detectlink}}). That $a^\ast$ is the path of a subgame-perfect equilibrium needs $v_i$ to be the least subgame-perfect continuation value, the extension of optimal penal codes to a stochastic game with an absorbing state; this is asserted for finite stage games with discounting and flagged for outside review. A direct valuation of the discounted streams reproduces (1) as an identity (EC, a valuation check); an explicit-action stochastic game, in which one breakout technology sets both $p_d$ and $q_b$ and the deviator optimizes over whole plans, agrees with {{eq:enforceeq}} in every setting in which the punishment is credible, and where quitting the race pays the punisher, (5) is not the credible punishment value [num: ES]. *Proof:* S5.

The folk theorem's promise depends on punishment remaining possible. A decisive position weakens it, and a punishment that is itself a race weakens it again.

**{{!Remark:detectlink}} (Enforcement and verification are one requirement).** Let punishment be triggered by detection, and let $\upsilon(T)$ be the probability that a deviation is detected and answered within time $T$. A deviation that would become decisive after $T_{\rm dec}$ escapes punishment iff it is not answered within that time, so $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} reads $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_d$: timely detection must exceed a level that rises with the value of winning [thm]. Along the compounding route, in the linearized loop, $T_{\rm dec}$ is set by the gap multiplier $\Lambda^\ast$ of {{tdec}}, about $\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast$ periods from a gap $\varepsilon_0$ to a decisive gap $\bar\Delta$, so a larger structural loop gain shortens the time detection has and raises $p_d$ [thm]. The verification threshold of {{>assure}}, $\upsilon>(T-R)/(T-P+F)$, is the same requirement seen from the side of verification: it is {{eq:enforceeq}} with $R$ the value of cooperation, $T$ the normalized value of an undetected deviation, $(1-\delta)(C_i+g_i)+\delta D_i$, and $P-F$ that of a detected one, $(1-\delta)(C_i+g_i)+\delta v_i$; its case $T<R$, cooperation without verification, is $\bar p_d\ge1$ here [thm; num: JX]. The two differ in what they take as given: there the sanction $F$ is given, here it has to be a credible continuation, and here the decisive position carries the stakes (S5). Both quantities, $T_{\rm dec}$ and $\upsilon$, are what masking hides (Remark {{#alarm}}), so an estimate of either that is to trigger a response has to come from correctors whose independence is certified (Section 12.1).

**{{!Corollary:relpunish}} (Relative objectives and punishments that can be mirrored) [thm].** *Let the stage game be symmetric and every actor hold $\theta=-1$, so every symmetric profile gives each 0, and let the continuation after a deviation be exchangeable: stage payoffs and transitions invariant under relabelling the actors, so that no state carries a label that changes payoffs or transitions, while strategies are free to condition on who deviated. With two actors every punishment, mixed or pure, leaves the punished at least 0, since it can mirror the punisher in an exchangeable zero-sum game; with $n\ge3$ in an anonymous game, so does every punishment in which all punishers use the same strategy. A symmetric cooperative profile is then sustainable only if $(1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_dD_i\le0$, and when $D_i\ge0$ only if it is a stage equilibrium in objectives.*

*Scope.* A decisive position reached through an exchangeable lottery is covered; one assigned by label to the deviator, such as a punishment whose absorbing prize goes to the punisher, is not, and relative actors can then sustain cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium (S14). With $n\ge3$, punishers in different roles can hold a relative deviator below zero: in a three-player example such a punishment is a stage equilibrium and sustains cooperation for $\delta\ge0.706$; it is weak as printed, strict under small symmetric perturbations of the payoffs, in 300 of 300 games at perturbations of 0.02 and 0.05, and strict either way once the roles are assigned to match a small asymmetry between them [num: CR, ES]. A purely relative punisher is indifferent between hurting the deviator and hurting its fellow punisher, so which punisher takes which role is equilibrium selection. *Proof:* S5.

Relative actors can enforce cooperation only by role-differentiated punishment, which needs coordination on roles [thm]; that they therefore act as blocs against a deviator is the reading, the enforcement face of {{club}} [conj].

**{{!Corollary:restraint}} (What restraint needs where breakouts are possible) [thm].** *Where $p_d$ is near zero, because no deviation yields a position beyond punishment or breakouts are answered before they become decisive, restraint can be enforced by repetition among actors with $\theta>-1$, given monitoring, $C_i>v_i$, patience, and a punishment that does not itself raise the chance of breakout. Where $p_d\to1$ and $D_i\ge C_i$, durable restraint requires $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)$ for every actor and every deviation, by two kinds of means: (a) changing the premise itself, so that deviations do not become decisive: not building capability of the decisive class, holding it so no party can use it alone, or detecting and answering faster than breakout; (b) raising $\bar p_d$, by objectives under which a decisive position is worth no more than restraint, sanctions that survive success, and lower stakes in deviation.*

*Scope.* A classification of the terms of {{eq:enforceeq}}, not a proof that no other means exists. *Proof:* S5.

**{{!Proposition:wingives}} (What a decisive winner gives) [thm].** After a decisive win, what everyone else receives is set by the winner's objective alone. *A winner holding $W$ decides, unconstrained, what share to give the others. With linear payoffs it gives nothing unless $\theta=n-1$, where it is indifferent; with logarithmic utility it gives $\theta/(1+\theta)$ for $\theta\ge0$; and a promise made before the win to give a fraction $\beta$ of a prize $\Omega$ to the others is worth breaking by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$, with no one to sanction it.*

*Proof:* S5 [num: WG].

A winner whose held self-model is accurate, $\theta=\kappa_W$ ({{boundary}}), gives $\kappa_W/(1+\kappa_W)$ under logarithmic utility [thm]. What a race cannot verify beforehand is $\kappa_W$ after victory, and what sets it is the winner's dependence on those it governs once it has won; whether AI lowers that dependence is a crux (C13; Section 8.3(d)).

This is the formal content of Ψ2, "the winner takes all, and the winner justifies all" ({{psi}}), exact for linear payoffs or $\kappa_W\to0$. Lowering decisiveness does not remove it if the prize is decisive power, since a lottery can award a position beyond sanction; what has to be limited is what winning confers (Section 10.2). Decisiveness also breaks bargaining: where one side's advantage is about to arrive and the side that gains it will set the terms (A8, S0), the other has a rational motive for preventive attack whenever its present chance of prevailing, less the cost of fighting, exceeds the value of peace on terms the advantaged side will set, and a shift spread over enough periods removes the motive ({{preventattack}}; Fearon 1995; Powell 2006) [thm∣prem]. What drives it is not cheap war but the incredibility of future promises. Self-help under anarchy is an institution, not a necessity (Wendt 1992); the enforcement condition says which of its terms an institution would have to change.

### 5.3 Second erosion: correction, and the two channels of risk

Correction must see a harm, decide and act before the harm is irreversible. Section 4 showed how correctors become something success can buy; this subsection states the tempo and separates two channels of irreversible risk.

**The race channel.** Play the contest of Section 3.4 in rounds at its symmetric stage equilibrium, with stakes $\Omega_t$, decisiveness $r_t$, shared loss per unit $\ell_t$, net marginal cost $c_t$ and self-model $\theta_t$. Under **(hazard–damage link)** [prem], the hazard of the irreversible event in round $t$ proportional to that round's shared damage, $h_t=a\ell_t\mathcal E_t$, the hazard is $h_t=a\,r_t\Omega_t\,\ell_t\,(n-1-\theta_t)/\big(n(c_t+(1+\theta_t)\ell_t)\big)$, and four levers act on it: the objective, through $n-1-\theta_t$; the contested stakes, which sharing a fraction $\beta$ scales by $1-\beta$; the residual harm per unit $\ell_t$, which correction lowers; and the net marginal cost, which a levy raises ({{racehazard}}) [thm∣prem]. At $\theta_t=-1$ everything rests on correction and cost. Continuation acts on one lever, the residual harm per unit, while the stakes grow; a change of selection acts on all four. Survival on this channel, $\exp(-\sum_{t\le N}h_t)$, stays positive iff $\sum_th_t<\infty$, and a hazard that settles at a positive constant gives zero limiting survival. In general, for one irreversible event, survival tends to zero if on every surviving history the residual probability is at least a non-summable sequence, and stays bounded away from zero if it is at most a summable one, without independence between rounds ({{nonsum}}) [thm]. **The objective of winning does not by itself prove that a residual danger is non-summable**, and cooperative alternatives are judged by the same lemma.

**{{!Proposition:tempo}} (The tempo bound) [thm].** When the situation changes faster than correction can follow, correction first loses robustness, then fails. *For a hazard mode $\dot y=ay+u(t-\tau_c)$: proportional correction cannot stabilize it when $a\tau_c\ge1$ (Hayes 1950); every linear time-invariant correction that stabilizes it has peak complementary sensitivity at least $e^{a\tau_c}$, so robust stability against weighted model error needs $|w(a)|e^{a\tau_c}<1$; and for hazard classes whose evidence arrives after a manifestation window $\tau_e$ that no testing shortens, $\tau_c\ge\tau_e$.*

*Scope.* Derivative action extends the first bound, which belongs to proportional correction only (S5). *Proof:* S5.

For classes with an irreducible lag only slowing helps.

**The growth channel.** Three quantities are kept apart in a defined hazard class: the variety $Y_t$ of hazardous behaviour, counted as predefined hazard modes; its coverage $U_t$, the number of those modes for which correction is in place that meets four conditions at a stated catch rate; and the residual probability of an irreversible loss after the correction in place. A correction decision meets the four conditions if it is **legible** (a reason people can check within a budget without relying on the evaluated system's lineage), **overrulable** (a designated human body can reverse it in time), **exercised** (planted errors are caught at the stated rate) and **resourced** (human review covers a stated share of consequential decisions). Three premises connect the three quantities on a continuation branch, and Section 12 treats them as the central crux. **(coverage lags)** [prem]: $U_t/Y_t\to0$ uniformly over surviving histories. What makes it plausible is **(variety grows)**: hazardous variety grows with compounding capability, at least exponentially, while consequences take their time and institutions deliberate at human rates; capability growth is measured (EV5), though the longest-horizon series is at the limit of its task suite, and the step from capability to hazardous variety is not [obs]. It is a motivation, not a premise of the theorem, and it can fail: if AI raises the productivity of contestable understanding exponentially, coverage grows exponentially too, a question symmetric with {{corrrate}} and measurable (S5). **(residual floor)** [prem]: after the correction in place, the conditional probability of the irreversible event is at least $h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$; a correction architecture that lowers residual risk below this refutes it. **(shared failure)** [prem]: failure that starts with one actor reaches the others, so each actor's survival depends on the uncovered share of all.

**{{!Theorem:coverage}} (Coverage mismatch on a continuation branch) [thm∣prem].** Where hazardous variety outgrows contestable coverage, survival tends to zero whoever is racing. *Under (coverage lags) and (residual floor), for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is a $T_\varepsilon$ with $\Pr(\Phi_H)\le(1-h(1-\varepsilon))^{H-T_\varepsilon}\to0$. Conversely, if the residual probability is at most $\bar hu_t$ with $\bar h<1$ and the uncovered share $u_t=(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$ summable, survival stays bounded away from zero.*

*Scope.* Under (shared failure) the bound is every actor's, whoever is racing; without it, it is the racer's own. Summability does not require that coverage ever catch up; finite sprints, saturation, freezing and rollback change the premises. *Proof:* S5.

This growth channel does not vanish when racing stops. A world that has left the race still needs capability to grow no faster than contestable correction: call that **E3, pace**, developing at the pace of understanding so that the uncovered share stays summable; and call **E2, stopping**, not building capability of the decisive class, with rollback where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage. Stopping is not slowing; they are different arrangements (Section 11.3). Where hazardous variety is frozen at its level from some date while coverage keeps growing, the exposure after that date is finite, and rolled back below coverage it ends, so a winner that freezes or rolls back keeps positive survival; what it gives up is permanent superiority, which is not the same thing ({{freeze}}) [thm].

**{{!Proposition:enough}} (A point of enough) [thm].** Growth that meets satiable needs has a point of enough; objectives that are not satiated have none. *Let the residual hazard on the growth channel be at most $\bar h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$. If hazardous variety stops growing at a level set by need while coverage keeps growing at least linearly, the uncovered share reaches zero in finite time and survival on this channel has a positive lower bound. An objective not satiated in the stakes, whether relative position or an absolute payoff linear in them, has no point of enough: intensity is proportional to the stakes for every $\theta<n-1$, because the success function is homogeneous of degree zero, while an objective bounded in the stakes is satiated.*

*Scope.* Under the premises of Theorem {{#coverage}}, that theorem applies to any continuation branch whatever the objective; the relative objective adds the invisibility of the shared loss. *Proof:* S5 [num: PE; frozen at need, survival on this channel stays at 0.717; compounding at 10%, it is $2\times10^{-9}$ by period 400].

The mind decides whether growth stops where need is met. An objective satiated in the stakes stops; an objective not satiated in them, relative standing above all, since standing relative to others is never complete, has no point of enough. In that precise sense **growth is not the danger; the mind is** [corpus: meta-attractor; Ren's ruling of 2026-09-25]. Hazardous variety that outgrows contestable coverage is dangerous under any mind, which is why pace binds even a generative order; and because compounding prices standing wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return ({{standprice}}), the actors who would need a point of enough are pushed away from it by the loop. 「癌亦增长，而其长竭母体之生」: cancer grows too, and its growth exhausts the body that bears it [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14 l.113]. For trained systems the same rule is made exact in Section 6.4, where a bounded objective is shown not to be enough and divergences that hide under evaluation are treated ({{>masked}}). Deliberately bounded hazardous scope, with correction that keeps growing, is a sufficient route to E3; its connection to purpose is a design hypothesis.

### 5.4 Third erosion: shared understanding

Coordination needs the parties to see the same danger and believe one another about it. Section 3.6 named the two logics, the logic of reasons and the logic of winning: only cooperation reasons, and struggle argues to win. This subsection gives that line its content in information, by asking what relative accounting does to talk.

**{{!Proposition:talk}} (Talk under relative accounting) [thm].** Relative accounting amplifies the material conflict between a sender and a receiver: between two rivals it removes informative talk, and among three or more it multiplies a pair's conflict by $n/(n-2)$, a factor that falls toward one as $n$ grows.
1. *Where a sender's and a receiver's objectives sum to zero at every state and action, every equilibrium is payoff-equivalent to one without talk (compare Crawford and Sobel 1982); two actors with $\theta=-1$ in a two-player game are such a pair.*
2. *Let all $n$ actors hold a common $\theta\in[-1,n-1]$ with $n-1+\theta>0$, with the sender and receiver in the uniform-quadratic model, a material bias $b_0$ between them and third parties affected linearly. What the receiver's action does to third parties enters both objectives alike, and talk is informative iff the effective bias $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$ is below $\tfrac14$. At $n=2$ the effective bias is $b_0(1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$, unbounded as $\theta\to-1$, which is (1); at $\theta=-1$ with $n\ge3$ it is $b_0n/(n-2)$, largest at three and falling toward $b_0$ as $n$ grows; at $\theta=n-1$ it is zero. A narrower self-model drains talk continuously, and a wider one restores it.*
3. *Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because it is shared: a sender gains from the receiver's restraint whatever it intends itself.*

*Scope.* Cheap talk; costly signals and verification are outside it. That rivals who still talk do so as blocs against third parties is a reading, not a result [conj]; that the force of relative gains weakens with the number of actors is as the relative-gains literature found (Snidal 1991). *Proof:* S5 [num: TK, T3].

Rivals stop informing one another; those who still talk, talk to allies, and whether they then act as blocs against third parties is the language face of the same conjecture as the enforcement face of {{club}}. **Measures** become targets and stop measuring (Goodhart 1975), as optimizing a learned reward past a point lowers the quality it measured (Gao et al. 2023) [obs]. **Narratives** are carriers too [conj]: the frames that spread are selected for fitness to the contest; a group that totalizes itself shapes those around it as a model generates tokens, and a winning frame shapes the vocabulary of the next round until its words escape the game that coined them and a frame read as nature is no longer contested [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. By the time the frame is noticed one is already thinking inside it; the same dilemma named a "Wall Street Game" or a "Community Game" produced very different cooperation (Liberman, Samuels and Ross 2004) [obs]. **Professed doctrine** is a further layer: people say what is rewarded rather than what they believe, so a public consensus can persist that few privately hold, and it can tip (Kuran 1995) [obs]; a race doctrine professed by actors selected for professing it is then held less widely than it is stated, and visible cooperation is what tests it [conj]. **Self-confirmation:** where a cooperative equilibrium exists, the belief that restraint will be exploited can persist because no one tests it (Fudenberg and Levine 1993); in a one-shot game in which racing is dominant, it is simply correct.

A system can retain abundant information while losing the ability to let it change course. So a prediction of shared danger has to be carried by derivations from public premises, verifiable evidence and protocols agreed in advance, and a prediction so carried can work against its own fulfilment (Section 11.2); "we are one" is first a matter of understanding built in a medium the pressure keeps degrading.

### 5.5 The scissors

Restraint is enforceable iff $p_d\le\bar p_d$, deviation by deviation. Under the conditions of {{cond}}, growth in the stakes relative to provision not won in the contest moves both sides of {{eq:enforceeq}} at once, through one quantity, the share of capacity that has to be won, $\omega$ of {{compound}}; capture and the stakes move the right side further, while tempo outruns correction and relative accounting drains talk.

**{{!Proposition:scissors}} (The scissors, arm by arm) [thm; conj].** The means of restraint weaken as the thing to be restrained grows: each arm is a result in its own model, and the two blades turn on one pivot, the share of capacity that has to be won.
1. *Breakout arm* [thm]. *Where a lead compounds ($\Gamma>1$) and punishment is triggered by detection, a larger structural loop gain raises $p_d$ in the linearized loop: the gap multiplier $\Lambda^\ast=1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$ of {{tdec}} rises, the time to decisiveness falls, and $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ rises ({{detectlink}}).*
2. *Objective arm* [thm]. *In the binary game of {{zonesillus}} at the boundary case $D=C$, the largest enforceable $p_d$ rises with the working self-model. Where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, a larger discounted loop gain narrows the working self-model to first order ({{standprice}}), so it lowers $\bar p_d$; the arm vanishes where stakes grow in proportion to capacity with $(1-\beta)r\le1$.*
3. *Sanction arm* [thm]. *$\bar p_d$ falls as the value of the punishment phase $v$ rises ({{enforce}}(2)); capture of the correctors that would execute the sanction (Section 4) raises $v$.*
4. *Stakes arm* [thm∣prem]. *$\bar p_d$ is homogeneous of degree zero in $(g,C,D,v)$, so stakes that all grow alike leave it unchanged; it falls where the gain from deviating and the value of a decisive position grow faster than the surplus of cooperation over punishment, $C-v$.*

*The pivot.* Arms 1 and 2 are comparative statics of one quantity, $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$: the stakes relative to provision beneath the game. The scissors close as stakes outgrow provision, and provision that grows with the stakes holds them open.

*Scope.* Each arm holds in its model; the conjunction is composite, and that the arms move together in the world is conjecture C0, tested in Section 7.4 [conj]. Rivals do not respond in arms 1 and 2; where they do, the sign of arm 1 follows the equilibrium gain, which exceeds the structural one in the contests computed where a lead discourages the follower and can fall below it where a laggard tries harder ({{loopgain}}(4)). In the reduced form of {{zonesillus}} with $\theta_0=0$, the largest enforceable discounted loop gain falls from 0.92 at $p_d=0$ to 0.85 at $p_d=0.5$ and 0.39 at $p_d=0.9$, and none exists above $p_d=0.9495$ (0.9793 for $\theta_0=0.4$); a common loop gain $\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta=0.3$ raises the value at $p_d=0.9$ to 0.57 [num: JX]. In one reduced joint model, with stakes growing at 5% a period against fixed provision, the won share rises from 0.20 toward 1, $p_d$ rises from 0 once leads begin to compound, $\bar p_d$ falls from 0.84, and the blades meet at period 59; with provision growing at the same rate as the stakes neither blade moves; detection twice as fast, or a held self-model of 0.4, delays the meeting to periods 60 and 69, because in that model the objective arm, not the breakout arm, closes the blades [num: JX, one model, rivals not responding] ({{jointscissors}}). The restraint gap between wider and narrower objectives grows with $p_d$ over the range in which the wider objective still sustains restraint; where a decisive position is worth more than cooperation it closes as $p_d\to1$, where no objective with $\theta\le n-1$ sustains restraint and this theory and structural realism agree, so Test G discriminates at intermediate $p_d$ [num: JX]. *Proof:* S5.

This is the precise sense in which **the means of restraint weaken as the thing to be restrained grows**: not a single force, but one pivot on which two blades turn, and two further arms that growth and capture move. Durable cooperation has to be built into what the system selects for before the blades close.
