## S5. Acceleration and the three erosions

This chapter holds the full statements of the results that Section 5 carries in one sentence, the proofs of those it states in full, and the numbers behind every figure it quotes. Local symbols: $w_p=\delta(1-p_d)/(1-\delta)$ is the weight of the future that a deviation leaves open (the Perron root of S2 keeps $\Lambda^\ast$); $A$, $B$ and $K$ are the stage constants of the binary game of {{faithful}}, defined at {{zonesillus}}.

### S5.1 The race: who starts it, what it consumes, where it goes when the frontier closes

**{{!Proposition:join}} (Who starts the race) [thm].** *With payoffs $R_i$ when all restrain, $T_i$ when racing against restrainers, $P_i$ when all race and $S_i$ when restraining against a racer: (1) In the objective an actor acts on, if $S_i<P_i$, restraint is never a best response to a racing rival; in the contest of {{boundary}} this holds for $r<n/(n-1)$, and in the $n$-player game it also needs restraint against one racer to pay less than racing alongside it. (2) A best response in the objective does not imply $S<P$ in material payoff or true welfare: with $n=2$, $r=1$, $\Omega=c=1$, $\ell=0.1$ and $\theta=-1$, racing is the strict best response in the objective, while staying out gives $S^\pi=-0.05$ against $P^\pi=-0.10$, and with $\kappa=0.2$, $S^W=+0.04$ against $P^W=-0.12$. (3) If falling behind costs $L_b\ge0$ beyond losing the prize, joining instead of staying out gains, at $\theta=0$, $\frac{\Omega+L_b}{n}\big(1-\frac{r(n-1)}{n}\big)$ for $r\le n/(n-1)$, zero in the fully dissipative limit; with asymmetric costs $c_1<c_2$ the strongest keeps a rent $(\Omega+L_b)(1-c_1/c_2)$ (Hillman and Riley 1989; Baye, Kovenock and de Vries 1996). (4) If the events $T_i>R_i$ are independent with probabilities $\xi_i$, the race begins with probability $1-\prod_i(1-\xi_i)$, which tends to one iff $\sum_i\xi_i$ diverges.*

*Proof.* (2): at $\theta=-1$ intensity is $1/2$ and $P^\pi=-0.1$; against $e=1/2$ zero intensity gives $-0.05$ and the other $0.45$. (3): with penalty $L_b$ the contest has prize $\Omega+L_b$; joining minus staying out is $(\Omega+L_b)/n-(c+\ell)e$ with $e=r(n-1)(\Omega+L_b)/(n^2(c+\ell))$; in the symmetric all-pay contest each player's expected payoff is that of the lowest bid in its support, $-L_b$ (Baye, Kovenock and de Vries 1996), as for two actors with $r\ge2$ (Ewerhart 2015). (4): $\prod(1-\xi_i)\to0$ iff $\sum\xi_i=\infty$. $\square$

**Proof of {{dissipate}}.** At the symmetric equilibrium the win probabilities sum to one, so total material payoff is $\Omega-ne(c+n\ell)$; substituting $e(\theta)$ from {{boundary}}(1) gives $s_{\rm dis}$. With stakes $s\Omega$ and loss $s\ell$ the share is $r(n-1-\theta)(c+ns\ell)/(n(c+(1+\theta)s\ell))$, whose derivative in $s$ has the sign of $c\ell(n-1-\theta)>0$, with limit $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$. Total payoff stays positive at every scale iff the limit is at most one, $r(n-1-\theta)\le1+\theta$, that is, $\theta\ge\theta_g$. At $\theta=-1$ the share is $r(1+ns\ell/c)$. $\square$ At $\theta=0$ each actor's loss relative to joint restraint is $\frac{r(n-1)}n\frac\Omega n\frac{c+n\ell}{c+\ell}$, which exceeds $\Omega/n$ iff $r(n-1)(c+n\ell)>n(c+\ell)$ ($1.27\,\Omega/n$ for $n=4$, $r=1$, $\ell=0.3c$). If the shared loss per unit did not grow with power, the share would stay constant (1.007 at $\theta=0.4$).

**{{!Remark:frontier}} (The frontier switch) [thm; num].** *Let a unit of capacity expand into an unclaimed frontier at marginal value $\varphi(F)-c_x-(1+\theta)H/n$, with $\varphi$ falling as the remaining frontier $F$ shrinks and $H$ the cost to the host per unit of expansion; destroy rivals' capacity at value $-c_y-\theta\Delta_y/(n-1)$, with $\Delta_y$ the relative gain from a rival's loss; or rest. (1) If $\theta\ge-(n-1)c_y/\Delta_y$, war among copies never pays. (2) At $\theta=-1$ expansion stops only where $\varphi=c_x$; at $\theta>-1$ it stops at $\varphi=c_x+(1+\theta)H/n$, the point of enough. (3) At $\theta=-1$ expansion gives way to destruction once $\varphi(F)<c_x+\Delta_y/(n-1)-c_y$, before the frontier is exhausted.*

*Proof.* Each clause compares the three marginal values. At $\theta=-1$ the host term vanishes from the expansion value, which gives (2); destruction is worth $-c_y+\Delta_y/(n-1)$, positive iff $\Delta_y>(n-1)c_y$, and it is preferred to expansion once $\varphi-c_x$ falls below it, which gives (3); for $\theta\ge-(n-1)c_y/\Delta_y$ the destruction value is non-positive, which gives (1). $\square$

*Toy (FS).* Four copies of one design with compounding expansion, a finite frontier and a logistic host. At $\theta=-1$ expansion peaks at a stock of 494 near $t=25$; the switch comes with 32% of the frontier left (predicted at $F^\ast=322$ from (3), simulated at 320), and total stock then falls to about $10^{-9}$. At $\theta=0$ the copies stop at the point of enough, keep a stock of 518 and never fight; at $\theta=3$ they stop earlier, at 343. With asymmetric starts one survivor keeps 17% of the peak. With destruction too costly to pay, no accounting fights, but $\theta=-1$ drives the host to 0.109. One result runs against the narrative: in the run with war, the host's minimum under $\theta=-1$ (0.245) is higher than under $\theta=0$ (0.194), because capacity spent on war is not spent on the host (S14). The parameters were chosen by the author; the toy illustrates the static condition and estimates nothing (compare Hirshleifer 1991; Skaperdas 1992) [num: FS].

### S5.2 Enforcement

**Proof of {{enforce}}.** Cooperation is worth $C_i/(1-\delta)$. A one-period deviation $a$ is worth $C_i+g_i(a)+\delta\big[p_d(a)D_i(a)/(1-\delta)+(1-p_d(a))v_i/(1-\delta)\big]$, where $v_i/(1-\delta)$ is the continuation value of punishment computed in the same game, with breakouts possible during punishment. Multiplying the no-deviation condition by $1-\delta$ and rearranging gives {{eq:enforceeq}}. In this stochastic game with an absorbing state, discounting and bounded payoffs, the one-shot deviation principle holds, and it holds deviation by deviation: (1) is the no-gain condition for each $a$ with its own $(g_i(a),p_d(a),D_i(a))$, which is why the deviation with the largest gain need not be the one that binds. That $a^\ast$ is the path of some subgame-perfect equilibrium needs $v_i$ to be the least subgame-perfect continuation value, the extension of Abreu's optimal penal codes to stochastic games with an absorbing state; this is asserted for finite stage games with discounting and flagged for outside review. (2) Solve (1) for $p_d$; the monotonicities hold on the region where $\bar p_{d,i}\in[0,1)$ and are weak at its edges ($\partial\bar p_d/\partial D=0$ at $\bar p_d=0$); 47,832 random draws in the region, no violation [num: NB]. Homogeneity of degree zero is read off the expression. (3) The two bounds differ by $\delta p_d(D_i-C_i)$. (4) Set $p_d=1$; with $D_i\ge C_i$ the condition forces $g_i\le-\delta(D_i-C_i)/(1-\delta)\le0$; with $D_i<C_i$ and $D_i\ge v_i$, $\bar p_{d,i}\ge1$ iff $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$, in which case (1) holds for every $p_d\in[0,1]$. (5) With at most one breakout a period, the three events of a punishment period, $i$ breaks out, the other breaks out, neither, have probabilities $q_b$, $q_b$ and $1-2q_b$, which requires $q_b\le\frac12$; the punishment value satisfies $V_P=P_i+\delta\big[q_bD_i/(1-\delta)+q_bV^{\rm sub}_i/(1-\delta)+(1-2q_b)V_P\big]$, and $v_i=(1-\delta)V_P$ is the closed form. With independent breakouts the exclusive probabilities are $q_b(1-q_b)$ each and $q_b^2+(1-q_b)^2$ for the race continuing, since a simultaneous breakout leaves the race as it was, so the same closed form holds in $q_b(1-q_b)$. $v_i>P_i$ iff $D_i+V^{\rm sub}_i>2P_i$. $\square$

*Valuation check (EC).* Across 200,000 random settings, a third each with $D<C$, $D=C$ and $D>C$, value iteration on an explicit Markov chain agrees with (1) in every case; since the chain's values are the discounted streams that (1) rearranges, this checks the algebra, not the incentives. The bound without the breakout term says "sustainable" when deviation pays in 10,723 of 66,538 cases with $D>C$ and in none with $D\le C$, and says "not sustainable" when restraint is sustainable in 10,032 of 66,842 cases with $D<C$. Both of Codex's V6.0 counterexamples are reproduced (cooperation worth 30, deviation 22). Breakouts during punishment soften it where $D+V^{\rm sub}>2P$ (with $C=1$, $D=4$, $V^{\rm sub}=0$: $v=1.29$ at $q_b=0.1$ and $1.78$ at $0.45$) and harden it where $D+V^{\rm sub}<2P$ (with $D=3$, $V^{\rm sub}=-5$: $v=-0.64$ at $q_b=0.1$).

*Independent checks (ES).* (a) Per deviation: with $C=3$, $D=6$, $v=1$, $\delta=0.9$, a deviation with $g=1$ and $p_d=0.1$ is deterred (worth 17.5 against 30), and a quiet one with $g=0.2$ and $p_d=0.6$ is not (39.2 against 30). (b) The closed forms of (5) equal value iteration on the explicit chain, exclusive and independent: at $q_b=0.045$, $v=0.895$ and $0.872$; at $0.1$, $1.286$ and $1.237$; at $0.3$, $1.688$ and $1.582$. At $q_b=0.6$ the exclusive form is invalid ($1-2q_b<0$) and the independent form stays valid ($v=1.624$). (c) The window: with $C=1$, $P=0$, $D=4$, $V^{\rm sub}=0$, $g=2$, $\delta=0.9$, restraint at $p_d=0$ is enforceable iff $(1-\delta)g\le\delta(C-v)$, that is $v\le0.778$, which with $v(q_b)=3.6q_b/(0.1+1.8q_b)$ holds iff $q_b\le0.0354$, while $v<C$ iff $q_b<0.0556$: between the two, reversion to the race fails to deter although it still leaves the deviator below cooperation. The window is not a knife edge: for $g=0.5$ it is $(0.0498,0.0555)$ and for $g=5$ it is $(0.0159,0.0555)$. Racing is a best response inside the punishment at these values, so the punishment is credible. V6.1's example, $g=0.5$ and $q_b=0.1$, gave $v=1.29>C$, a different point: there the race with breakouts is preferred to cooperation itself, and "reversion" is no punishment at all. (d) In an explicit-action stochastic game, in which one breakout technology sets both the deviation's $p_d$ and the punishment's $q_b$ and player 1 optimizes over all deviation plans by value iteration, the best response agrees with {{eq:enforceeq}}, evaluated with $v$ from (5), in 19,965 of 19,965 random settings in which the punishment is credible; in 35 settings quitting the race pays the punisher, and there (5) is not the credible punishment value, which is the proposition's premise. (e) The exception in (4), carried by R4: with $C=1$, $D=0.9$, $v=0$, $g=2$, $\delta=0.9$, $\bar p_d=0.864$ and {{eq:enforceeq}} fails at $p_d=0.87$ and $0.9$, so $D<C$ alone exempts nothing; with $g=0.5$, $(1-\delta)g=0.05\le\delta(C-D)=0.09$ and restraint survives every $p_d$. (f) Homogeneity: scaling all of $(g,C,D,v)$ by 2 or by 10 leaves $\bar p_d=0.2963$ unchanged; scaling only $g$ and $D$ moves it to $0.1296$ and $-0.0037$ [num: ES].

**Derivation for {{detectlink}}.** Let detection and response arrive at a random time $\mathcal T$ after the deviation, with distribution $\upsilon(T)=\Pr(\mathcal T\le T)$, and let the deviation become decisive at $T_{\rm dec}$ unless it has been answered. It escapes punishment iff $\mathcal T>T_{\rm dec}$, so $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} in the form $p_d\le\bar p_d$ reads $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_d$. With a memoryless clock of mean $\tau$, $\upsilon(T)=1-e^{-T/\tau}$ and $p_d=e^{-T_{\rm dec}/\tau}$. Along the compounding route of {{tdec}}, $T_{\rm dec}=\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast$ with $\Lambda^\ast=1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$ in the linearized single-channel loop, so $\partial p_d/\partial\Gamma>0$ wherever $\Gamma>1$; from a 1% to a 50% gap, $T_{\rm dec}$ is 393, 100, 158, 41, 41 and 12 periods at $(\Gamma,d_K)=(1.2,0.05)$, $(1.2,0.2)$, $(1.5,0.05)$, $(1.5,0.2)$, $(3,0.05)$, $(3,0.2)$ with $\delta_f=0$ [num: NB; TD checks the general form]. *The correspondence with {{assure}}.* Write $R=C$, $T=(1-\delta)(C+g)+\delta D$ for the normalized value of an undetected deviation and $P-F=(1-\delta)(C+g)+\delta v$ for that of a detected one. Then $T-R=(1-\delta)g+\delta(D-C)$ and $T-(P-F)=\delta(D-v)$, so $(T-R)/(T-P+F)=1-\bar p_d$ exactly, and {{assure}}'s $\upsilon>(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ is $p_d<\bar p_d$; its case $T<R$ is $\bar p_d>1$, the exception of (4). The identity holds to $4\times10^{-11}$ over 100,000 random settings with $D>v$ [num: JX]. *Where the two differ.* The inspection game takes the sanction $F$ and the one-shot payoffs as given; {{eq:enforceeq}} requires the sanction to be a credible continuation, $v$, computed in a game in which breakouts remain possible, and lets the decisive position carry its own stakes through $\delta D$. So the two thresholds agree wherever the stage games coincide, and {{eq:enforceeq}} is the more demanding reading when the sanction has to be enforced by the same actors it is meant to protect. *Check of the clock.* Simulated escape probabilities under a memoryless clock equal $e^{-T_{\rm dec}/\tau}$ within sampling error (0.4485 against 0.4493 at $T_{\rm dec}=8$, $\tau=10$; 0.1349 against 0.1353 at $T_{\rm dec}=20$; 0.0818 against 0.0821 at $T_{\rm dec}=5$, $\tau=2$), and simulated deviation values agree with {{eq:enforceeq}} in the cases run [num: JX].

**Proof of {{relpunish}}.** At $\theta=-1$ every symmetric profile gives each objective 0. *Two actors.* Let the punisher use any strategy $\sigma_2$, pure or mixed, conditioning on the history as it likes. The punished plays its mirror, $\sigma_1(h)=\sigma_2(\mathrm{swap}(h))$: at every history, what the punisher would play with the roles swapped. Under an exchangeable continuation, stage payoffs and transitions invariant under relabelling the two, the joint distribution of the two payoff streams is then exchangeable, so the expected relative payoff is zero in every period and $v_1\ge0$. The one-period case is the antisymmetric matrix identity: the relative objective of a symmetric two-player game has $A=-A^\top$, and mirroring a mixed strategy $y$ gives $y^\top Ay=0$. *$n\ge3$.* In an anonymous game in which all punishers use the same, possibly mixed and independent, strategy, mirroring yields an exchangeable profile with expected relative payoff 0. In either case $v_i\ge0=C_i$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} requires $(1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_dD_i\le\delta(1-p_d)(0-v_i)\le0$. *Where the premise fails.* A continuation that carries a label, such as an absorbing prize that goes to the punisher whoever acts, is not exchangeable: the mirror does not copy the punisher's continuation state, and the bound fails. $\square$

*Numerics (CR, ES).* Over 20,000 random two-player games, mixed mirroring gives at most $2\times10^{-16}$ in absolute value; in 3,000 anonymous games with $n\ge3$, no violation. *The continuation premise.* An exchangeable continuation in which a Tullock lottery awards an absorbing $\pm10$ with probability 0.3 a period gives the mirrored punished a normalized relative value of $0.000000$; Codex's label-dependent continuation, in which the target takes $-10$ with probability one half whatever it does, holds it at $-8.18$, so a deviation is worth $-69.6<0$ and relative actors sustain cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium (S14); the symmetric variant of the same lottery gives a punishment value of 0 and a deviation worth $+4$, so it does not. *The role-differentiated counterexample.* Three players, actions (restrain or race) × (X or Y), payoff one to a player whose second component is unique, racing gaining 0.1 and costing each other 0.1. All restraining with X gives each 0, and the best deviation gains 1.2. The punishment in which the deviator and one punisher take X and the other punisher Y holds the deviator at $-0.5$ and is a stage equilibrium in objectives, so grim reversion to it is credible, and cooperation is sustained for $\delta\ge1.2/1.7=0.706$. A symmetric punishment could be mirrored for 0. *Strictness.* The printed punishment is weak: the two punishers' margins over their best deviations are 0 and 0 (the third's is 0.2). A perturbation of $+0.02$ on the X prize makes it strict (margins 0.01, 0.01, 0.2); $-0.02$ destroys it, and the roles must swap: with the roles swapped, a perturbation of $-0.05$ gives a strict punishment with $\delta^\ast=0.716$, while the unswapped assignment is not an equilibrium. Under small anonymous perturbations of all payoffs a strict role-differentiated punishment holding the deviator below cooperation exists in 300 of 300 games at perturbations of 0.02 and 0.05 and in 282 of 300 at 0.1 (median $\delta^\ast$ 0.708, 0.711, 0.713); a second perturbation scheme gives 400 of 400, 400 of 400 and 372 of 400 (median about 0.71). *Indifference.* A purely relative punisher gains $+0.950$ from attacking the deviator and $+0.950$ from attacking its fellow punisher, against $+0.550$ from abstaining, and both punishers prefer the Y role ($+1.0$ against $-0.5$): the punishment needs coordination on roles, and which punisher takes which is equilibrium selection [num: CR, ES]. Duersch, Oechssler and Schipper (2012) concern a dynamic imitation rule; the corollary uses only that a symmetric two-player zero-sum game has value 0, so rock–paper–scissors games are covered once mixed mirroring is allowed.

**Proof of {{restraint}}.** With $p_d=0$, {{eq:enforceeq}} reads $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-v_i)$, satisfiable for $C_i>v_i$ and $\delta$ large, given a punishment that does not raise the chance of breakout (else $v_i$ rises, {{enforce}}(5)); at $\theta=-1$ apply {{relpunish}}. With $p_d\to1$ and $D_i\ge C_i$, the condition is $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)$ for each deviation, and (a) and (b) classify the terms of its expression: (a) lowers $p_d$ directly, by removing the decisive class, by custody that no party can use alone, or by detection and response faster than breakout ({{detectlink}}: $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$); (b) raises $\bar p_{d,i}$ through $D_i$ relative to $C_i$, through $v_i$, or through $g_i$. $\square$

**{{!Remark:zonesillus}} (An illustration: the faithful-AI game at the boundary case) [thm; num].** In the binary game of {{faithful}} with a common $\theta$, write $A=\epsilon(b-d)-c$ for the stage gain from racing alone, $B=\epsilon(b/(n-1)+d)$ for the weighted cost of that deviation to the others, $K=c+n\epsilon d$ for the loss each bears in the all-race equilibrium relative to joint restraint, and $w_p=\delta(1-p_d)/(1-\delta)$. Restraint is a stage equilibrium when $\theta\ge A/B$, and, at the boundary case $D=C$, can be sustained by reversion when $\theta\ge\theta_{\rm rep}(p_d)=(A-w_pK)/(B+w_pK)$; equivalently, for a given working objective, restraint is enforceable iff $p_d\le\bar p_d(\theta)=1-(1-\delta)(A-\theta B)/\big(\delta K(1+\theta)\big)$, which rises with $\theta$. With $n=4$, $b=1$, $c=0.3$, $d=0.2$, $\epsilon=1$, $\delta=0.9$: $A/B=0.94$, and $\theta_{\rm rep}$ is $-0.901$, $-0.812$, $-0.322$, $+0.005$, $+0.634$ and $+0.937$ at $p_d=0,0.5,0.9,0.95,0.99,1$, by direct discounted streams, so an isolated self loses enforced restraint once $p_d>0.9495$ and a self at $\theta_0=0.4$ once $p_d>0.9793$ [num: BI, JX]. These are the thresholds of the boundary case, not bounds: a decisive position worth more than cooperation raises them (with $D-C=0.5$ in objective units, $\theta_{\rm rep}$ at $p_d=0.9$ rises from $-0.32$ to $+2.34$, and at $p_d=1$ no $\theta\le n-1$ sustains restraint). In the continuous contest intensity is positive for every $\theta<n-1$, so self-enforcing restraint there needs full inclusion of the contestants (S14).

*Test G.* Let $\theta$ be drawn uniformly on $[-1,3]$ and count the share of draws above 1 (wider) and below 1 (narrower) that sustain restraint; the restraint gap is the difference. At $D=C$ it rises with $p_d$: $+0.05$, $+0.09$, $+0.21$, $+0.34$, $+0.50$, $+0.82$ and $+0.97$ at $p_d=0,0.5,0.8,0.9,0.95,0.99,1$. With $D-C=0.5$ it is hump-shaped: $+0.05$, $+0.30$, $+0.92$, $+0.33$, $0$, $0$, $0$ at the same values, because at $p_d=1$ the condition is $(1-\delta)g\le\delta(C-D)<0$, impossible for $g\ge0$, so the enforceable set is empty for every $\theta\le n-1$ and this theory and structural realism agree there. The registered wording: the gap grows with $p_d$ over the range in which the wider objective still sustains restraint; where a decisive position is worth more than cooperation it closes as $p_d\to1$; the test discriminates at intermediate $p_d$ [num: JX].

**Proof of {{wingives}}.** With linear payoffs the objective $(1-s)W+\theta sW/(n-1)$ has slope $W(\theta/(n-1)-1)\le0$ in the share $s$ given away, zero only at $\theta=n-1$; with logarithmic utility the first-order condition of $\ln((1-s)W)+\theta\ln(sW/(n-1))$ gives $s=\theta/(1+\theta)$ (0.286, 0.5, 0.667, 0.75 at $\theta=0.4,1,2,3$); the promise is {{twochan}}(4), with the renege gain $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$ when the promised share goes to the $n-1$ others [num: WG; SM]. An accurate winner, $\theta=\kappa_W$, gives $\kappa_W/(1+\kappa_W)$. $\square$

**{{!Proposition:preventattack}} (Preventive attack under a shifting advantage) [thm∣prem].** *Two actors bargain over a flow of value 1 per period with discount $\delta$; fighting ends bargaining, A then obtaining the flow with its probability of prevailing less a cost $c_A$ per period; A prevails with probability $w_1$ today and $w_2<w_1$ after B's advantage arrives one period later. (1) Let $x$ be A's per-period share after the shift; fighting now, worth $(w_1-c_A)/(1-\delta)$, is rational if $w_1-c_A>(1-\delta)+\delta x$, and only if, when peace gives A the whole flow today. (2) Under (A8), the advantaged side setting the terms, B offers A its reservation value $x=\max(w_2-c_A,0)$. (3) Under other protocols $x$ can reach $\min(w_2+c_B,1)$ and attack can cease to be rational. (4) If $c_A\ge w_1$, war is worth at most zero. A shift spread over enough periods removes the motive.*

*Proof and numbers.* (1) and (2) compare the two discounted streams. (3): with $w_1=0.5$, $w_2=0$, $c_A=c_B=0.2$, $\delta=0.8$, A attacks under (A8), while if A sets the future terms peace gives A 1.8 and B 3.2, against 1.5 each from war. For the gradual shift, with $w$ falling from 0.5 to 0 over $N$ periods, $c_A=0.05$, $\delta=0.8$, attack is rational at the start for $N\le3$ and never for $N\ge4$, by backward induction on $V_t=\max(W_t,\delta V_{t+1})$ (`../v4/checks_v44.py`; compare Fearon 1995; Powell 2006). $\square$ The premise is the protocol (A8); the mechanism is the incredibility of a promise the advantaged side has no reason to keep, and it is the same incredibility that {{wingives}} states for a winner.

### S5.3 The two channels of risk

**{{!Proposition:racehazard}} (Race-generated hazard and its four levers) [thm∣prem].** *Play the contest of Section 3.4 in rounds at its symmetric stage equilibrium with stakes $\Omega_t$, decisiveness $r_t$, shared loss per unit $\ell_t$, net marginal cost $c_t$ and self-model $\theta_t$, under (hazard–damage link), $h_t=a\ell_t\mathcal E_t$. Then $h_t=a\,r_t\Omega_t\,\ell_t\,(n-1-\theta_t)/\big(n(c_t+(1+\theta_t)\ell_t)\big)$, and survival on this channel, $\exp(-\sum_{t\le N}h_t)$, stays positive iff $\sum_th_t<\infty$. Four levers act on $h_t$: the objective, through $n-1-\theta_t$; the contested stakes, which sharing a fraction $\beta$ scales by $1-\beta$; the residual harm per unit $\ell_t$, which correction lowers; and the net marginal cost, which a levy raises. At $\theta_t=-1$ everything rests on correction and cost. A hazard that settles at a positive constant gives zero limiting survival.*

*Proof.* By {{boundary}}(1), total intensity is $ne(\theta_t)$, which gives $h_t$; survival is $\prod_te^{-h_t}$ by the chain rule. Sharing a fraction $\beta$ of the divisible prize gives the contest with prize $(1-\beta)\Omega_t$ ({{share}}(2)), linear in $h_t$; a rebated levy enters at $\tau(n-1-\theta_t)/n$. Since $\ell/(c+(1+\theta)\ell)<1/(1+\theta)$, $\sum_tr_t\Omega_t(n-1-\theta_t)/(1+\theta_t)<\infty$ suffices for $\theta_t>-1$ whatever $\ell_t$. A background hazard from capability growth adds to $h_t$, and no lever of the contest removes it: that is the growth channel. $\square$ The decomposition is checked against a grid best response at one parameter set [num: RH]. It is a decomposition under a premise, not a necessity theorem.

**{{!Lemma:nonsum}} (Survival against a residual danger) [thm].** *Fix one irreversible event. If on every surviving history the residual probability in round $k$ is at least $h_k$ with $\sum_kh_k=\infty$, then $\Pr(\Phi_N)\le\prod_{k\le N}(1-h_k)\le\exp(-\sum_{k\le N}h_k)\to0$, by the chain rule, without independence between rounds; if it is at most $\bar h_k<1$ with $\sum\bar h_k<\infty$, survival is at least $\prod(1-\bar h_k)>0$.* With $h_k=0.2/k$ survival is 0.34, 0.22 and 0.14 by rounds 100, 1,000 and 10,000; with $0.2/k^2$ it settles at 0.70.

**Proof of {{tempo}}.** Proportional correction: Hayes (1950). Derivative action extends the range: for $a=1$, $\tau_c=1.5$, gains $k_p=1.02$, $k_d=0.644$ stabilize the mode (`../v4/checks_v42.py`). For the sensitivity bound, with plant $e^{-s\tau_c}/(s-a)$ and any stabilizing linear time-invariant controller, the complementary sensitivity is stable with value 1 at $s=a$; writing it as $e^{-s\tau_c}$ times a stable function and applying the maximum modulus principle gives $\|\mathcal T\|_\infty\ge e^{a\tau_c}$, and robust stability $\|w\mathcal T\|_\infty<1$ gives $|w(a)|e^{a\tau_c}<1$, necessary only: $w(s)=2(s-a)/(s+a)$ has $w(a)=0$ but $\|w\mathcal T\|_\infty\ge2e^{a\tau_c}$. Evidence cannot be acted on before it exists. $\square$

*What would make coverage lag.* Consequences take their time, and tools raise the throughput of observation without making consequences arrive sooner; institutions deliberate at rates set by attention and procedure; learning theoretical models and forming new mechanisms are human processes; and understanding produced with human time, $\dot U=A_UL_U^{\lambda_U}U^{\phi_U}$ with $\phi_U<1$ and bounded labour, grows polynomially (Jones 1995; Bloom et al. 2020). None settles the premise: if AI raises $A_U$ exponentially at rate $g_A$, $U$ grows exponentially at $g_A/(1-\phi_U)$. Measures: the safety of control protocols at a fixed audit budget across generations, and catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours, controlled for evaluation awareness. Under strict legibility, (coverage lags) is close to definitional for systems beyond human capability, and the empirical content lies in (residual floor), which a safety case with a numerical bound on the uncovered part would state (Clymer et al. 2024).

**Proof of {{coverage}}.** For $t\ge T_\varepsilon$ the conditional probability of the event is at least $h(1-\varepsilon)$ on every surviving history; the chain rule gives the bound. The converse is the upper part of {{nonsum}} with $\bar h_k=\bar hu_k$. Only (coverage lags) and (residual floor) are used: the growth of $Y_t$ enters nowhere, which is why (variety grows) is a motivation and not a premise. (residual floor) gives a liminf of the hazard of at least $h$, not that the hazard tends to $h$. With $u_t=1/(t+2)^2$ coverage stays behind forever and survival with $h=0.05$ stays above 0.98. With $h=0.05$, $\varepsilon=0.1$, survival eighty periods after $T_\varepsilon$ is at most 0.025. Under (shared failure) the event is one for all, so the bound is every actor's; without it the chain runs over the racer's own exposure. $\square$

**{{!Proposition:freeze}} (Freezing and rolling back) [thm].** *Let hazardous variety be frozen at $Y_\tau$ from $\tau$ while coverage grows. (1) The exposure after $\tau$ is $\int_\tau^\infty(1-U(t)/Y_\tau)^+dt$: $Y_\tau(1-U(\tau)/Y_\tau)^2/(2\dot U)$ for linear coverage, and $(\ln\varrho-1+1/\varrho)/g_U$ for coverage growing at rate $g_U$, with $\varrho=Y_\tau/U(\tau)>1$. (2) Rolled back to $Y\le U$, exposure stops. (3) If a sprint builds capability $\varrho_X$ times the rivals' level and hazardous variety is a fraction $\varphi$ of capability's, frozen exposure is zero when $\varphi\varrho_X\le1$. (4) For linear coverage the same frozen ratio costs more the later the sprint; this fails for some sub-exponential coverage.*

*Proof.* Integrate. For (4), let $\log U$ rise by 0.5 on each interval $[m^2,m^2+1]$ and stay constant otherwise: $\log U\approx0.5\sqrt t=o(t)$, while $\dot U/U=0.5$ inside each interval, so a sprint timed to end inside one costs less than an earlier one (S14). $\square$ With hazardous variety equal to capability's, growth 0.3, linear coverage $1+0.5t$ and $h=0.05$, a sprint to $\tau=10$ leaves survival 0.86; freezing then gives 0.53, and rolling back keeps 0.86. What the freezing winner gives up is permanent superiority: a frozen stock is overtaken by anyone who keeps growing, which is why Section 8.3(e) treats the sprint-and-freeze path by its incidence and not by this proposition alone.

**Proof of {{enough}}.** After $t_N$, $U_t\ge U_{t_N}+u(t-t_N)$, so the uncovered share is zero from $t_N+Y_N/u$ on, and {{nonsum}}'s upper part bounds survival below. The success function is homogeneous of degree zero, so symmetric intensity is proportional to the stakes for every $\theta<n-1$; an objective bounded in the stakes, such as $1-e^{-\Omega}$, has intensity bounded as they grow: for $n=4$, $r=1$, $c=1$, intensities 0.19, 1.88 and 18.75 for linear payoffs at stakes 1, 10 and 100, against 0.12, 0.19 and 0.19 for the saturating objective [num: SM]. In the growth-channel example, capability frozen at need leaves survival on the channel at 0.717, with coverage catching up at period 14; capability compounding at 10% a period leaves $1.8\times10^{-9}$ by period 400 [num: PE]. $\square$ An absolute objective linear in the stakes is as unsatiated as a relative one, which is why the core states the rule as satiation in the stakes and not as relative against absolute.

### S5.4 Talk

**Proof of {{talk}}.** (1) If the sender's and the receiver's payoffs sum to zero, each sender type induces, among the equilibrium actions, one that minimizes the receiver's payoff in its state, so the receiver's payoff is at most that of ignoring messages, which it can always do; equilibrium payoffs equal the no-talk values (TK: 300 random games, 1,096 pure equilibria, none whose receiver payoff differs from the no-talk value). (2) With a common $\theta$ among $n$ actors, $J_S=\pi_S+\frac\theta{n-1}\big(\pi_R+\sum_k\pi_k\big)$ and $J_R=\pi_R+\frac\theta{n-1}\big(\pi_S+\sum_k\pi_k\big)$, the sums over the third parties $k$. In the uniform-quadratic model with $\pi_S=-(y-u-b_0)^2$, $\pi_R=-(y-u)^2$ and third-party payoffs linear in $y$ with summed slope $L$, both objectives have curvature $-2(n-1+\theta)/(n-1)$, and the first-order conditions give
$$y_S-u=\frac{2b_0(n-1)+\theta L}{2(n-1+\theta)},\qquad y_R-u=\frac{2\theta b_0+\theta L}{2(n-1+\theta)},$$
whose difference, $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$, does not contain $L$: what the receiver's action does to third parties enters both objectives alike. Since the curvatures are equal, Crawford and Sobel's condition for an informative equilibrium in the uniform-quadratic model applies to this bias, and talk is informative iff it is below $\frac14$. At $\theta=-1$ the identity $J_S-J_R=\frac n{n-1}(\pi_S-\pi_R)$ holds directly, and the bias is $b_0n/(n-2)$; at $n=2$ it is $b_0(1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$, unbounded as $\theta\to-1$, which is (1). (3) A sender that intends to race prefers the receiver to cooperate: with $R=1$, $T=2\varepsilon$, $P=\varepsilon$, $S=0$, it gains $\varepsilon$ from the receiver's cooperation however small $\varepsilon$ is (compare Aumann 1990). $\square$

*Numerics (TK, T3).* The identity at $\theta=-1$ holds to $3.6\times10^{-15}$; numerically maximized ideal points match $b_0n/(n-2)$ for $n=3,4,6,10$ (0.150, 0.100, 0.075, 0.0625 at $b_0=0.05$) and the unified formula $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$ in 19 combinations of $n\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ and $\theta$, to $1.5\times10^{-8}$; with a nonlinear third-party term $-ky^2$ the gap measured in state units is still $b_0n/(n-2)$ to the grid's resolution. A two-message partition equilibrium, built directly with indifferent boundary types and numerical best responses, exists iff the effective bias is below $\frac14$ (for $n=3$: at $b_0=0.08$, bias 0.24, boundary type 0.02; at $b_0=0.1$, bias 0.30, none); with $b_0=0$ and a common cost to third parties, full revelation is an equilibrium [num: TK, T3]. That pairs who still talk align against third parties is a reading of (2), not a result; the amplification falls with $n$, as Snidal (1991) found for relative gains generally.

### S5.5 The scissors

**Proof of {{scissors}}.** *Arm 1.* {{tdec}} gives $T_{\rm dec}=\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast$ with $\Lambda^\ast=1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$ increasing in $\Gamma$ for $\Gamma>1$; the derivation for {{detectlink}} gives $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ with $\upsilon$ non-decreasing, so $p_d$ is non-decreasing in $\Gamma$. *Arm 2.* In the binary game of {{zonesillus}} at $D=C$, $\bar p_d(\theta)=1-(1-\delta)(A-\theta B)/(\delta K(1+\theta))$ rises with $\theta$ because $A-\theta B$ falls and $1+\theta$ rises; by {{standprice}}, to first order the working self-model is $\theta_\Gamma(\theta_0,\Gamma_\delta,\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta)$, which falls in $\Gamma_\delta$ wherever $\lambda_{\rm rel}>\lambda_{\rm com}$, and does not fall where the prize grows in proportion to capacity with $(1-\beta)r\le1$. *Arm 3.* $\partial\bar p_d/\partial v=-[(1-\delta)g+\delta(D-C)]/[\delta(D-v)^2]<0$ wherever $\bar p_d<1$; Section 4 gives the mechanism by which captured correctors raise the value of the punishment phase. *Arm 4.* $\bar p_d$ is unchanged when $g,C,D,v$ scale alike (homogeneity of degree zero); along a path on which $g$ and $D-C$ grow while $C-v$ is fixed it falls, by the monotonicities of {{enforce}}(2). *The pivot.* In the one-channel model of {{compound}}, $\Gamma=(1-\beta)r\omega d_K/(d_K+\delta_f)$ and $\Gamma_\delta=(1-\beta)r\omega\,\delta d_K/(1-\delta(1-d_K-\delta_f))$ are both proportional to $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$, so arms 1 and 2 are comparative statics of $\omega$, which rises with the stakes and falls with provision. $\square$

*The reduced form (JX).* In the binary game of {{zonesillus}} at $D=C$, let actors act on $\theta_\Gamma(\theta_0,\Gamma_\delta)$ with zero common return, and let one $p_d$ apply to the deviation while the punishment is the stage equilibrium of the binary game, so no breakout occurs during punishment ($q_b=0$); the largest discounted loop gain at which restraint remains enforceable is found by bisection on direct discounted streams, with no threshold formula. For $\theta_0=0$ it is 0.924, 0.852, 0.387 and 0.108 at $p_d=0,0.5,0.9,0.94$, and none exists above $p_d=0.9495$; for $\theta_0=0.4$ it is 0.953, 0.908, 0.621, 0.448, 0.377 and 0.161 at $p_d=0,0.5,0.9,0.94,0.95,0.97$, and none above $0.9793$. V6.1 printed 0.99 for both; the corrected values are these. With a common loop gain $\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta=0.3$ the value at $p_d=0.9$ rises from 0.387 to 0.571, and with $0.6$ to 0.755: a prize that grows with what all reinvest widens the enforceable range [num: JX]. Rivals do not respond in this form; a breakout technology that also acted during punishment would raise $v$ ({{enforce}}(5)) and lower every figure.

**{{!Remark:jointscissors}} (One reduced joint model) [num].** The arms are proved separately; this model runs them together along one path of the pivot, so that the conjunction can at least be seen in one place. *Setup.* One ratio channel, capacity, in the model of {{compound}}, quasi-static, rivals not responding; stakes $\Omega_t=\Omega_0e^{g_\Omega t}$ and provision $k_0(t)=k_0e^{g_kt}$ given exogenously, so that the won share is $\omega(t)=\kappa_c\Omega_t/(\kappa_c\Omega_t+nk_0(t))$; the structural gain $\Gamma(t)=(1-\beta)r\omega(t)d_K/(d_K+\delta_f)$ and the discounted gain $\Gamma_\delta(t)$ from {{loopgain}}(3); the gap multiplier $\Lambda^\ast(t)$ and $T_{\rm dec}(t)=\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast(t)$ from {{tdec}}, infinite while $\Lambda^\ast\le1$; detection and response as a memoryless clock of mean $\tau$, so $p_d(t)=e^{-T_{\rm dec}(t)/\tau}$ ({{detectlink}}); the working objective $\theta(t)=\theta_\Gamma(\theta_0,\Gamma_\delta(t))$ of {{standprice}} with zero common return, since the prize grows exogenously and not with what all reinvest; and the bound $\bar p_d(\theta(t))$ of the binary game at $D=C$ ({{zonesillus}}), the stakes arm switched off to isolate the pivot. Breakouts occur only through the deviation; the punishment is the stage equilibrium without breakouts. Parameters: $n=4$, $r=1.5$, $\beta=0$, $\kappa_c=1$, $d_K=0.5$, $\delta_f=0$, $\delta=0.8$, $\Omega_0=k_0=1$, $\varepsilon_0=0.01$, $\bar\Delta=0.5$, $\tau=12$, $\theta_0=0$, and the stage game $b=1$, $c=0.3$, $d=0.2$, $\epsilon=1$. *Report.* (i) With stakes growing at $g_\Omega=0.05$ against fixed provision, the won share rises from 0.20 toward 1; $\Gamma$ passes 1 near period 42, after which $T_{\rm dec}$ becomes finite and $p_d$ rises from 0; $\theta$ narrows from $-0.16$ and $\bar p_d$ falls from 0.842; the blades meet at period 59, where $\omega=0.83$, $\Gamma=1.24$, $T_{\rm dec}=34$ periods, $p_d=0.057$, $\theta=-0.78$ and $\bar p_d=0.045$. If the stage payoffs $b$ and $d$ scale with the stakes, they meet at period 47. (ii) With provision growing at the same rate as the stakes, $g_k=g_\Omega$, the won share is constant and neither blade moves over the whole horizon. (iii) Detection twice as fast, $\tau=6$, moves the meeting only from period 59 to 60, and a held self-model $\theta_0=0.4$ moves it to period 69: in this model the objective arm, not the breakout arm, does the closing, since $p_d$ is still small when $\bar p_d$ reaches it. The same block runs a second specification, with a nonlinear $T_{\rm dec}$, $r=3$ and the stage game scaled with the stakes, in which the blades close at a won share of about 0.55 (0.54 when one breakout technology also acts during punishment), at or below the objective blade's own threshold in 96 of 96 parameter settings, with the breakout blade alone closing them in 12 [num: JX]. *Label.* One reduced model, with rivals not responding and the stakes arm switched off; in the world the co-movement of the arms is conjecture C0, tested by Test E (Section 7.4). What the model shows is narrower and exact: along a path on which stakes outgrow provision beneath the game, both blades of {{eq:enforceeq}} move toward each other through the one quantity $\omega$, and along a path on which provision keeps pace, neither moves.
