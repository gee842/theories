## 5. Acceleration and the three erosions

> **Claim.** The conditions of winning accelerate the contest and erode the three means by which any system restrains a harmful pressure: enforcement, which makes restraint pay through the future; correction, which makes costs return in time; and shared understanding, which lets the parties see the same danger and believe one another [thm, each in its model]. Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold that success lowers [thm]. Where leads compound, a larger share of capacity that has to be won raises that chance; where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, it narrows the objectives that set the threshold; both blades of these scissors turn on that one share [thm, arm by arm; num, jointly in one model]. Where hazardous variety outgrows contestable coverage, survival goes to zero whoever is racing [thm∣prem]. Relative accounting inflates the conflict between any sender and receiver, and between two rivals it silences talk [thm]. That the blades move together in the world is a conjecture (C0).
>
> **Why.** Decisiveness takes away the future that enforcement needs; tempo and capture take away the time and independence that correction needs; relative accounting takes away the information that talk carries. And an objective not satiated in the stakes has no point of enough, so nothing in it stops growth where need is met.

### 5.1 Acceleration

Where actors contest capability directly, who prevails is decided by the contest of Section 3.4. The case for racing rests on three separate premises: **(decisive)**, $r$ is large; **(fast)**, leads open within short windows; **(unsaturated)**, no level of advantage makes more worthless. A technology can be decisive at a threshold and saturate above it, as nuclear advantage saturated at a secure second strike. Write $R_i$ for an actor's payoff when all restrain, $T_i$ when it races against restrainers, $P_i$ when all race and $S_i$ when it restrains against a racer. Restraint is never a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$, which in the contest of Section 3.4 holds for $r<n/(n-1)$, given, among more than two, that restraining against one racer pays less than racing beside it; racing need not then be better in material terms; joining buys nothing over staying out in the fully dissipative limit; and if the events $T_i>R_i$ are independent, the race begins with a probability that tends to one as capable actors multiply exactly when the sum of their probabilities diverges (**{{!Proposition:join}}**, S5). The race is set by the most race-inclined actor, which is why any remedy must cover every capable actor.

**{{!Proposition:dissipate}} (Dissipation) [thm].** The more decisive the advantage, the more of its value is consumed in the race to obtain it.
1. *Under a common self-model $\theta$, the share of the stakes dissipated in private cost and shared loss is $s_{\rm dis}(\theta)=r(n-1-\theta)(c+n\ell)/\big(n(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, increasing in $r$ and $\ell$.*
2. *If the technology's power scales the stakes and the shared loss per unit together while private cost is unchanged [prem], the dissipated share rises toward $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$ for $-1<\theta<n-1$, without bound at $\theta=-1$. Total material payoff stays positive at every scale iff $\theta\ge\theta_g=(r(n-1)-1)/(1+r)$, which rises with $r$.*

*Proof:* S5, with the ceilings at four self-models [num: legacy v5].

What is spent is safety margin, accepted risk and delegated control. With $n=4$ and $r=1$, an accurate weight toward what can be divided of $\kappa=0.4$, the value of {{twoerrors}}, lies below $\theta_g=1$: a gap between what accuracy supplies and what the contest requires, which only institutions, or coupling that grows with power, can close (crux C11).

**{{!Remark:frontier}} (The frontier switch: what cancer does when the body runs out) [thm].** *Let capacity expand into an unclaimed frontier, destroy rivals' capacity, or rest. At $\theta=-1$ the host is invisible, and expansion gives way to mutual destruction before the frontier is exhausted, once the frontier's marginal value falls below the private cost of expansion plus the standing that destroying a rival gains; for $\theta$ high enough, war among copies never pays and expansion stops at a point of enough.* S5 gives the conditions, and a toy of four copies with the one finding in it that runs against this narrative [num: FS].

Among actors whose accounting is relative, peace is therefore a peace of abundance, not of accounting. Expansion is an anaesthetic for the winning model. Winning does not end the game: the logic itself is the replicator, a winner is a field in which the same selection continues (Section 7.2), and a self-replicating economy that carries it out into new frontiers carries the same switch with it [corpus: meta-attractor].

### 5.2 First erosion: enforcement

Cooperation among rivals is usually sustained by the future: restraint today is rewarded by restraint tomorrow. Decisiveness weakens the future. Consider a stage game repeated with discount $\delta\in(0,1)$ under perfect monitoring, each actor acting on $J_i$. Let $a^\ast$ be a profile with per-period value $C_i$. A one-period deviation $a$ by $i$ gains $g_i(a)$ and yields, from the next period and with probability $p_d(a)$, a *decisive position*: an absorbing state beyond any punishment, worth $D_i$ per period. Let $v_i$ be $(1-\delta)$ times $i$'s continuation value under the harshest credible punishment, computed in the same game, in which breakouts remain possible during punishment.

**{{!Proposition:enforce}} (The enforcement condition) [thm].** Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold, and success lowers the threshold.
1. *$a^\ast$ is sustained by reversion to that punishment if and only if, for every $i$ and every one-period deviation $a$,*
$${{!eq:enforceeq}}\qquad (1-\delta)g_i(a)+\delta p_d(a)(D_i-C_i)\;\le\;\delta\big(1-p_d(a)\big)(C_i-v_i).$$
*The left side is the temptation, immediate and decisive; the right side is the sanction that remains. The deviation with the largest gain need not bind: a quiet one, with a small gain and a high chance of breakout, can.*
2. *Equivalently, for a deviation with $D_i>v_i$, it is deterred iff $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)=1-\big[(1-\delta)g_i(a)+\delta(D_i-C_i)\big]/\big[\delta(D_i-v_i)\big]$, which falls with $g_i$, $D_i$ and $v_i$ and rises with $C_i$ wherever it lies in $[0,1)$, weakly at the edges.*
3. *The bound without the breakout term, $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i)$, is exact when $D_i=C_i$, necessary when $D_i\ge C_i$, and sufficient when $D_i\le C_i$.*
4. *As $p_d\to1$ the condition becomes $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$. If $D_i\ge C_i$, sustainable profiles lie within the stage equilibria of the objectives held, strictly within when $D_i>C_i$: restraint must be preferred even to a breakout that succeeds. If $D_i<C_i$, cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium survives $p_d=1$ exactly when that inequality holds.*
5. *Let punishment be reversion to a stage equilibrium in which both of two actors race, worth $P_i$, and let at most one racer break out in a period, each with probability $q_b\le\frac12$, the other then subordinated, worth $V^{\rm sub}_i$ per period. Then $v_i=\big[(1-\delta)P_i+\delta q_b(D_i+V^{\rm sub}_i)\big]/(1-\delta+2\delta q_b)$; if instead breakouts are independent and a simultaneous breakout leaves the race as it was, $q_b$ is replaced by $q_b(1-q_b)$. When $D_i+V^{\rm sub}_i>2P_i$, reversion to the race is a lottery the deviator welcomes. It can then fail to deter while the punishment is still worse than cooperation, $P_i<v_i<C_i$, even where the deviation itself never breaks out: with $C_i=1$, $P_i=0$, $D_i=4$, $V^{\rm sub}_i=0$, $g_i=2$ and $\delta=0.9$, restraint is enforceable at $q_b=0$ and fails for every $q_b$ in $(0.0354,0.0556)$, where still $v_i<C_i$.* [num: ES]

*Scope.* Perfect monitoring and an absorbing decisive position. For (1) to characterize every subgame-perfect path, $v_i$ must be the least credible continuation value; that it is, computed as here, extends Abreu's optimal penal codes to stochastic games with an absorbing state, and is asserted for finite stage games with discounting and flagged for outside review (S5). A slow breakout enters through $p_d$, the chance that it becomes decisive before punishment can act ({{>detectlink}}). A valuation check confirms the algebra of (1) [identity: EC]; in an explicit-action stochastic game in which one breakout technology sets both $p_d$ and $q_b$, the deviator's best response over all deviation plans agrees with {{eq:enforceeq}} in all 19,965 random settings in which the punishment is credible [num: ES]. *Proof:* S5.

The folk theorem's promise depends on punishment remaining possible. A decisive position weakens it, and a punishment that is itself a race weakens it again. Which deviation binds is a matter of both terms: with $C_i=3$, $D_i=6$, $v_i=1$ and $\delta=0.9$, a deviation gaining 1 with $p_d=0.1$ is deterred and one gaining 0.2 with $p_d=0.6$ is not [num: ES]. Here the deviation that binds is the quiet one, the kind that masking hides (Section 4.4).

**{{!Remark:detectlink}} (Detection and the breakout term) [thm].** *Let punishment be triggered by detection and act before decisiveness, let $\upsilon(T)$ be the probability that a deviation is detected and answered within $T$ periods, and let a breakout need $T_{\rm dec}$ periods to become decisive. Then $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} reads $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d,i}$: timely detection must exceed a level that rises with the value of winning. If detection and response run on a Poisson clock of mean $\tau_{\rm det}$, $p_d=e^{-T_{\rm dec}/\tau_{\rm det}}$. Read the payoffs of {{>assure}} as continuation values: cooperation $R=C_i$; a deviation that becomes decisive $T=(1-\delta)(C_i+g_i)+\delta D_i$; a deviation detected and answered $P-F=(1-\delta)(C_i+g_i)+\delta v_i$. Its verification threshold $(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ is then exactly $1-\bar p_{d,i}$, and its case $T<R$ is $\bar p_{d,i}>1$, where restraint needs no detection.*

*Scope.* The two conditions differ in what they take as given. The verification threshold is the condition under which cooperating beats defecting against a cooperator, with the sanction $F$ given and trust deciding the rest; {{eq:enforceeq}} is the condition on the equilibrium path, with $v_i$ computed in the same game and required to be credible. *Proof:* S5 [identity: JX; num: JX].

Both sides of {{eq:enforceeq}} are therefore observable: $p_d$ is detection set against the time to decisiveness, which a structural loop gain above one shortens ({{tdec}}), and $\bar p_d$ is the value of winning set against the sanction that remains.

**{{!Corollary:relpunish}} (Relative objectives and punishments that can be imitated) [thm].** *Let the stage game be symmetric, every actor hold $\theta=-1$, and the punishment phase be exchangeable: its payoffs and transitions are invariant under relabelling the actors, and it starts from a state that treats the punished and the punishers alike, though strategies may condition on who deviated. Every symmetric profile then gives each 0. With two actors every punishment, mixed or pure, leaves the punished at least 0, since it can mirror the punisher; with $n\ge3$ in an anonymous game, so does every punishment in which all punishers use the same strategy. A symmetric cooperative profile is then sustainable only if $(1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_dD_i\le0$, and when $D_i\ge0$ only if it is a stage equilibrium in objectives.*

*Scope.* A decisive position reached through an exchangeable lottery is covered. A continuation that treats the deviator asymmetrically escapes the bound: where the punishment's absorbing prize goes by label to the punisher, relative actors sustain cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium [num: ES]. With $n\ge3$, punishers in different roles can hold a relative deviator below zero. In a three-player example such a punishment sustains cooperation for $\delta\ge0.706$; it is a weak stage equilibrium as printed, strict under any small asymmetry between the roles' prizes, and strict in nearly all nearby games under small anonymous perturbations. Both punishers prefer the same role, and a purely relative punisher is indifferent between hurting the deviator and hurting its fellow, so which punisher takes which role is a problem of equilibrium selection [num: CR]. *Proof:* S5.

Relative actors can therefore enforce cooperation only by role-differentiated punishment, which needs coordination on roles [thm]. That they then enforce it as blocs is the reading, not the result [conj]: the enforcement face of {{club}}.

**{{!Corollary:restraint}} (What restraint needs where breakouts are possible) [thm].** *Where $p_d$ is near zero, because no deviation yields a position beyond punishment or breakouts are answered before they become decisive, restraint can be enforced by repetition among actors with $\theta>-1$, given monitoring, $C_i>v_i$, patience, and a punishment that does not itself raise the chance of breakout. Restraint survives every value of $p_d$ iff {{eq:enforceeq}} holds at $p_d=0$ and at $p_d=1$; where $D_i\ge v_i$ this is $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$ for every $i$, which for a deviation with a positive gain needs $D_i<C_i$. Otherwise durable restraint requires $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$ for every $i$ and every deviation, by two kinds of means: (a) changing the premise itself, so that deviations do not become decisive: not building capability of the decisive class, holding it so that no party can use it alone, or detecting and answering faster than breakout; (b) raising $\bar p_d$: objectives under which a decisive position is worth no more than restraint, sanctions that survive success, and lower stakes in deviation.*

*Scope.* A classification of the terms of {{eq:enforceeq}}, not a proof that no other means exists. A decisive position worth less than cooperation does not by itself exempt: with $C_i=1$, $D_i=0.9$, $v_i=0$, $g_i=2$ and $\delta=0.9$, $\bar p_{d,i}=0.864$ and restraint fails above it [num: ES]. *Proof:* S5.

**{{!Proposition:wingives}} (What a decisive winner gives) [thm].** After a decisive win, what everyone else receives is set by the winner's objective alone. *A winner holding $W$ decides, unconstrained, what share to give the $n-1$ others. With linear payoffs it gives nothing unless $\theta=n-1$, where it is indifferent; with logarithmic utility it gives $\theta/(1+\theta)$ for $\theta\ge0$; and a promise made before the win to give a fraction $\beta$ of a prize $\Omega$ to the others is worth breaking by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$, with no one to sanction it.*

*Proof:* S5 [num: WG, SM].

This is the formal content of "the winner takes all, and the winner justifies all". Its strongest answer is a winner that still needs the governed. A winner whose self-model is accurate toward what can be divided, $\theta=\kappa_W$ ({{boundary}}(2)), gives $\kappa_W/(1+\kappa_W)$ under logarithmic utility [thm]. What the race cannot verify beforehand is $\kappa_W$ after victory: the winner's dependence on the governed once it has won. AI is the technology that can substitute for the work and the assent of the governed, and so lower that dependence; whether it does is crux C13 (Section 12.3), and Section 8.3 meets the benign winner at its strongest. Lowering decisiveness does not remove the problem if the prize is decisive power, since a lottery can award a position beyond sanction; what has to be limited is what winning confers (Section 10.2).

Decisiveness also breaks bargaining. When one side's advantage is about to arrive and the side that gains it will set the terms, the other has a rational motive for preventive attack whenever its present chance of prevailing, less the cost of fighting, exceeds the value of peace on terms the advantaged side will set; a shift spread over enough periods removes the motive (**{{!Proposition:preventattack}}**, S5; Fearon 1995; Powell 2006). What drives it is not cheap war but the incredibility of future promises, and compounding is what makes the shift arrive. Self-help under anarchy is an institution, not a necessity (Wendt 1992); the enforcement condition says which of its terms an institution would have to change.

### 5.3 Second erosion: correction, and the two channels of risk

Correction must see a harm, decide and act before the harm is irreversible. Section 4 showed how correctors become something success can buy; this subsection states the tempo and separates two channels of irreversible risk.

**The race channel.** Play the contest of Section 3.4 in rounds at its symmetric stage equilibrium, with stakes $\Omega_t$, decisiveness $r_t$, shared loss per unit $\ell_t$, net marginal cost $c_t$ and self-model $\theta_t$, under **(hazard–damage link)**: the hazard of the irreversible event in round $t$ is proportional to the shared damage, $h_t=a\ell_t\mathcal E_t$.

**{{!Proposition:racehazard}} (Race-generated hazard and its four levers) [thm∣prem].** *Under the link, $h_t=a\,r_t\Omega_t\,\ell_t\,(n-1-\theta_t)/\big(n(c_t+(1+\theta_t)\ell_t)\big)$, and survival on this channel, $\exp(-\sum_{t\le N}h_t)$, stays positive iff $\sum_th_t<\infty$. Four levers act on $h_t$: the objective, through $n-1-\theta_t$; the contested stakes, which sharing a fraction $\beta$ scales by $1-\beta$; the residual harm per unit $\ell_t$, which correction lowers; and the net marginal cost, which a levy raises. At $\theta_t=-1$ everything rests on correction and cost. A hazard that settles at a positive constant gives zero limiting survival.*

*Scope.* A decomposition under a premise, not a necessity theorem. *Proof:* S5 [identity: RH].

Continuation acts on one lever, the residual harm per unit, while the stakes grow; a change of selection acts on all four. In general, survival goes to zero if the true residual probability on every surviving history is at least a non-summable sequence, and stays bounded away from zero if it is at most a summable one, without independence between rounds (**{{!Lemma:nonsum}}**, S5). **The objective of winning does not by itself prove that a residual danger is non-summable**, and cooperative alternatives are judged by the same lemma.

**{{!Proposition:tempo}} (The tempo bound) [thm].** When the situation changes faster than correction can follow, correction first loses robustness, then fails. *For a hazard mode $\dot y=ay+u(t-\tau_c)$: proportional correction cannot stabilize it when $a\tau_c\ge1$ (Hayes 1950); every linear time-invariant correction that stabilizes it has peak complementary sensitivity at least $e^{a\tau_c}$, so robust stability against weighted model error needs $|w(a)|e^{a\tau_c}<1$; and for hazard classes whose evidence arrives after a manifestation window $\tau_e$ that no testing shortens, $\tau_c\ge\tau_e$.*

*Scope.* Derivative action extends the first bound, which belongs to proportional correction only (S5). *Proof:* S5.

For classes with an irreducible lag only slowing helps.

**The growth channel.** Three quantities are kept apart: the variety $Y_t$ of hazardous behaviour in a defined class; its coverage $U_t$ by correction that remains contestable; and the residual probability of an irreversible loss after the correction in place. A correction decision counts toward $U_t$ if it is **legible** (a reason people can check within a budget without relying on the evaluated system's lineage), **overrulable** (a designated human body can reverse it in time), **exercised** (planted errors are caught at a stated rate) and **resourced** (human review covers a stated share of consequential decisions). What makes coverage fall behind on a continuation branch is **(variety grows) [prem]**: hazardous variety grows with compounding capability, at least exponentially. Capability growth is measured, though the longest-horizon series is at the limit of its task suite, and the step from capability to hazardous variety is not [obs: EV5]. The theorem below does not use this premise; it uses two others, and Section 12 treats them as the central crux. **(coverage lags) [prem]:** $U_t/Y_t\to0$ uniformly over surviving histories. Consequences take their time and institutions deliberate at human rates; but if AI raises the productivity of contestable understanding exponentially, coverage grows exponentially too and AI-assisted correction can keep pace, a question symmetric with {{corrrate}} and measurable (S5). **(residual floor) [prem]:** after the correction in place, the conditional probability of the irreversible event is at least $h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$; a correction architecture that actually lowers residual risk refutes it. **(shared failure) [prem]:** failure that starts with one actor reaches the others.

**{{!Theorem:coverage}} (Coverage mismatch on a continuation branch) [thm∣prem].** Where contestable coverage falls ever further behind hazardous variety, survival goes to zero whoever is racing. *Under (coverage lags) and (residual floor), for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is a $T_\varepsilon$ with $\Pr(\Phi_H)\le(1-h(1-\varepsilon))^{H-T_\varepsilon}\to0$. Conversely, if the residual probability is at most $\bar hu_t$ with $\bar h<1$ and the uncovered share $u_t=(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$ is summable, survival stays bounded away from zero.*

*Scope.* Summability does not require that coverage ever catch up; finite sprints, saturation, freezing and rollback change the premises. *Proof:* S5.

This growth channel does not vanish when racing stops. A world that has left the race still needs capability to grow no faster than contestable correction can follow: call that **E3**, developing at the pace of understanding. Call **E2** stopping: not building capability of the decisive class, with rollback where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage. They are different arrangements (Section 11.3): stopping is not slowing. Divergences that hide under evaluation give both their strongest reason from inside alignment ({{>masked}}). Freezing hazardous variety after a sprint makes the exposure finite, and rolling it back below coverage ends it, so a winner that freezes or rolls back keeps positive survival; what it gives up is permanent superiority, which is not the same thing (**{{!Proposition:freeze}}**, S5).

**{{!Proposition:enough}} (A point of enough) [thm].** Growth that meets satiable needs has a point of enough; objectives that are not satiated have none. *Let the residual hazard on the growth channel be at most $\bar h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$. If hazardous variety stops growing at a level set by need while coverage keeps growing at least linearly, the uncovered share reaches zero in finite time and survival on this channel has a positive lower bound. An objective not satiated in the stakes, whether relative position or an absolute payoff linear in them, has no point of enough: intensity is proportional to the stakes for every $\theta<n-1$, because the success function is homogeneous of degree zero, while an objective bounded in the stakes is satiated.*

*Scope.* Under its premises {{coverage}} applies to any continuation branch whatever the objective; the relative objective adds the invisibility of the shared loss. *Proof:* S5 [num: PE: frozen at need, survival on this channel stays at 0.717; compounding at 10%, it is $1.8\times10^{-9}$ by period 400].

The proposition separates what grows from what decides whether growth stops. The mind decides whether growth stops where need is met: an objective not satiated in the stakes, relative standing above all, has no point of enough, since standing relative to others is never complete [thm]. In that precise sense, **growth is not the danger; the mind is** [corpus: ruling of 2026-09-25]. The author's book puts it in one line, 癌亦增长，而其长竭母体之生: cancer grows too, but its growth exhausts the life of its host [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14]. The rule has one boundary: hazardous variety that outgrows contestable coverage is dangerous under any mind, which is why pace binds even a generative order [thm∣prem]. Because compounding also prices standing wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return ({{standprice}}), the actors who would need a point of enough are pushed away from it by the loop. Deliberately bounded hazardous scope, with correction that keeps growing, is a sufficient route to E3; its connection to purpose is a design hypothesis. Section 6.4 states the same rule for the minds that training produces.

### 5.4 Third erosion: shared understanding

Coordination needs the parties to see the same danger and believe one another about it. Contest changes what talk is for. Where the parties cooperate, an argument is judged by whether it is right; where they contend, by whether it wins, and "I am right" outranks what is right. The author's line is 合作才讲理，斗则讲赢: in cooperation one reasons, in struggle one argues to win [corpus: alignment-selection; jian-and-wenyan, 2026-05-05].

**{{!Proposition:talk}} (Talk under relative accounting) [thm].** Relative accounting inflates the conflict between any sender and receiver; between two rivals it silences talk.
1. *Let all $n$ actors hold a common $\theta\in[-1,n-1]$ with $n-1+\theta>0$, let a sender and a receiver play the uniform-quadratic game with a material bias $b_0$ between them, and let the receiver's action affect third parties linearly. Their objectives then differ by the effective bias $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$, whatever the third-party terms, and informative talk exists iff it is below $\frac14$.*
2. *With $n\ge3$ at $\theta=-1$ the effective bias is $b_0n/(n-2)$: three times the material bias among three, falling toward it as $n$ grows. With $n=2$ it is $b_0(1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$, without bound as $\theta\to-1$. At $\theta=-1$ two actors' objectives sum to zero at every state and action, and in any such game every equilibrium is payoff-equivalent to one without talk (compare Crawford and Sobel 1982), so no talk is informative whatever the material bias.*
3. *Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because it is shared: a sender gains from the receiver's restraint whatever it intends itself.*

*Scope.* Cheap talk; costly signals and verification are outside it. With third-party payoffs quadratic in the receiver's action, the effective bias in units of the state is unchanged [num: TK]. *Proof:* S5 [num: TK, T3].

A narrower self-model drains talk continuously, so the loop that narrows working objectives ({{standprice}}) drains talk with them. The amplification at $\theta=-1$ is greatest among three and fades as actors multiply, as the relative-gains literature found for cooperation (Snidal 1991). Rivals stop informing one another; that pairs who still talk align as blocs against third parties is a reading, not a result [conj]: the language face of {{club}}.

**Measures** become targets and stop measuring (Goodhart 1975), as optimizing a learned reward past a point lowers the quality it measured (Gao et al. 2023). **Narratives** are carriers too [conj]: the frames that spread are selected for fitness to the contest; a group that totalizes itself shapes those around it as a model generates tokens, and a winning frame shapes the vocabulary of the next round until its words escape the game that coined them and a frame read as nature is no longer contested [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. By the time the frame is noticed one is already thinking inside it; the same dilemma named a "Wall Street Game" or a "Community Game" produced very different cooperation (Liberman, Samuels and Ross 2004). **Professed frames:** where professing a frame is rewarded, a doctrine can be held more widely in public than in private (Kuran 1995). A race doctrine professed by actors selected for professing it can then be held less widely than it is stated, and visible cooperation can tip it [conj]. **Self-confirmation:** where a cooperative equilibrium exists, the belief that restraint will be exploited can persist because no one tests it (Fudenberg and Levine 1993); in a one-shot game in which racing is dominant, it is simply correct.

A system can retain abundant information while losing the ability to let it change course. So a prediction of shared danger has to be carried by derivations from public premises, verifiable evidence and protocols agreed in advance (Section 11.2), and "we are one" is first a matter of understanding built in a medium the pressure keeps degrading.

### 5.5 The scissors

Restraint is enforceable iff $p_d\le\bar p_d$ ({{enforce}}), and growth moves both sides at once. The two main movements share a cause. Under the conditions of {{cond}}, the larger the share of capacity that has to be won, the faster a lead compounds toward decisiveness, and the more the discounted gain of the same loop narrows the objectives that set the bound. Captured sanctioners and stakes that outgrow cooperation lower the bound further, while tempo outruns correction (Section 5.3) and narrowing objectives drain talk (Section 5.4).

**{{!Proposition:scissors}} (The scissors, arm by arm) [thm∣prem].** Growth in the share of capacity that has to be won moves both sides of {{eq:enforceeq}} toward failure: the breakout probability where leads compound, its bound wherever the working objective narrows. *Let breakouts be detected and answered on a clock $\upsilon$ ({{detectlink}}), and let $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$ be the won share of capacity, the stakes set against provision beneath the game ({{compound}}).*
1. *Breakout arm [thm, linearized]. Where $\Gamma>1$ and a decisive gap $\bar\Delta$ is reachable, a lead grows from $\varepsilon_0$ to $\bar\Delta$ in about $T_{\rm dec}=\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast$ periods, with $\Lambda^\ast-1=(1-\beta)r\omega d_K-d_K-\delta_f$ in one channel ({{tdec}}). A larger $\omega$ raises $\Lambda^\ast$, shortens $T_{\rm dec}$ and raises $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$.*
2. *Objective arm [thm, first order]. Let the stage game and $a^\ast$ be symmetric, let a deviation raise the deviator above the mean payoff while lowering the mean, let punishment revert to a symmetric profile whose mean payoff is below cooperation's, and let $D_i-C_i$ not fall as the objective narrows. Then, where $D_i>v_i$ and $\bar p_d\in[0,1]$, a narrower working objective lowers $\bar p_d$, since by {{eq:twochaneq}} it raises the deviation gain and shrinks the sanction $C_i-v_i=(1+\theta)(\bar\pi_C-\bar\pi_P)$. With a fixed prize, the working objective narrows as $\Gamma_\delta$ rises ({{standprice}}), and $\Gamma_\delta$ is proportional to $\omega$; with a prize that grows with capacity, it narrows exactly where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return.*
3. *Sanction arm [thm]. $\bar p_d$ falls as $v_i$ rises, so captured sanctioners lower it; a falling cost of control speeds that capture ({{corrrate}}).*
4. *Stakes arm [thm]. $\bar p_d$ is homogeneous of degree zero in $(g_i,C_i,D_i,v_i)$: stakes that scale every term alike leave it unchanged. Where $(1-\delta)g_i$ and $D_i-C_i$ grow by a common factor faster than $C_i-v_i$, it falls, wherever $C_i>v_i$ and $\bar p_d<1$.*

*The breakout and objective arms turn on one pivot: $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma_\delta$ are both proportional to $\omega$. Stakes that outgrow provision beneath the game close both blades; provision that grows with the stakes holds both open.*

*Scope.* Each arm holds in its own model, as marked; with diffusion a gap can settle short of decisive, and the first arm is then silent [num: TD]. That the arms move together in the world is conjecture C0, which Test E examines (Section 7.4). In the binary game of {{zonesillus}} at $D=C$, with rivals not responding and punishment free of breakouts, the largest discounted loop gain at which restraint stays enforceable falls from 0.924 at $p_d=0$ to 0.387 at $p_d=0.9$, and none is enforceable above $p_d=0.9495$ for $\theta_0=0$ [num: JX]. In one reduced joint model, with the stakes arm switched off ({{jointscissors}}), restraint is enforceable iff the won share stays below 0.551, or 0.542 with one breakout technology throughout, the punishment race included; with stakes growing 5% a period against fixed provision from a won share of 0.20, the blades cross at period 31.8, and with provision growing as fast as the stakes neither moves. In that model the objective blade does most of the closing, and the breakout blade alone closes in only 12 of the 96 settings tried [num: JX, one reduced model, rivals not responding]. *Proof:* S5.

The arms also fix where Test G discriminates. The gap in restraint between wider and narrower objectives grows with $p_d$ while the wider still sustains restraint; where a decisive position is worth more than cooperation it closes as $p_d\to1$, where no objective sustains restraint and this theory and structural realism agree, so the test discriminates at intermediate $p_d$ [num: JX].

This is the precise sense in which **the means of restraint weaken as the thing to be restrained grows**. Growth as such does not close the blades: where the stakes outgrow provision beneath the game, growth moves temptation up and restraint down at once, and where provision grows with them, the pivot does not turn. That is the image of trees inside the enforcement condition. Durable cooperation has to be built into what the system selects for before the blades close.

Each erosion runs faster with a more powerful instrument; Section 6 asks what AI does to the game, and what the game does to AI.
