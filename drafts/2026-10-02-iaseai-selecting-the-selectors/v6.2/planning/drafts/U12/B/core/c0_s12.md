## 12. The judgment

> **Claim.** Under the protection standard and the present evidence, a change in what the system selects for is warranted now, in three tiers sorted by the kind of justification each step has [val]. Steps whose justification waits on no crux are taken now: measurement that decides the cruxes, preparation whose lead time exceeds any warning, steps that are equilibria without trust, and research that is a public good; beside them stand steps warranted now until a named crux is measured favourable [rec]. The substance of the change, stopping the decisive class (E2), common custody and development at the pace of understanding (E3), is prepared now and activated on registered indicators by a mechanism committed in advance [rec]. Whole designs, the coercive restriction of frontier inputs among them, are compared under one range of uncertainty; where every worst case crosses the tolerance, as each does under the rule for that range, the ranking is a judgment at the standard's third step, and the preference for changing selection rests on the asymmetry of reversibility across every failure mode [val].
>
> **Why.** Measurement has value on every reading, and preparation costs little beside a lead time that no warning covers [rec]. Custody has to exist before anyone could hold decisive capability alone, and the trigger fires where restraint is least enforceable, so an activation decided then would face the condition it is meant to prevent [thm∣prem]. One failure is irreversible on every path, one party coming to hold decisive capability: the race selects for it, a design built to Section 10 contests it, and that design's other failures have named reversals [thm∣prem; val].

### 12.1 The judgment, in tiers

The judgment applies the protection standard of Section 8.1 to the present evidence, and sorts what it recommends by the kind of justification each step has, not by who would accept it. The tiers are kinds of justification, not stages: the first two begin together, and what has the longest time constant starts first (Section 11.4).

**Tier 1(a). Warranted now, on every reading of the cruxes [rec].** A step belongs here only if its justification does not depend on how any crux comes out: measurement that decides the cruxes, preparation whose lead time exceeds any warning, a step that is an equilibrium without trust, or research that is a public good.
1. **Shared measurement** of hazards, coverage and residual risk per class, kept apart from measurement of who leads, with masked classes measured adversarially; audited disclosure of data on conversion into correctors, from every party, with a sanction above the gain from omission, since omission is the cheapest capture ({{blind}}(3)); certified row sums; the correlation of evaluators' misses on common test sets, and control evaluations in which the red team knows the protocol ({{topology}}); and the share of evaluation done by the evaluated lineage, wherever models evaluate models ({{alarm}}).
2. **Verification research and pilots**: of compute, training and custody, and of detection faster than breakout.
3. **Alignment and control research, commonly funded and openly published.** What lowers the alignment tax is a public good that competitors undersupply, and once published it is common to all; where it brings a class's tax to zero or below, the safeguard spreads without coordination (Section 6.4).
4. **Preparation of common custody**: its design, institutions, triggers and activation rule registered now, and the commitment to activate taken now.
5. **Narrow E1, beginning with declared doctrine and inspected exercises**: AI kept out of the decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty, the strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the time available for deliberation, with drilled human fallback and an evidence channel independent of AI, and verified wherever verification exists. Among actors who price in a lock they take to be certain, it is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust ({{delegatelock}}(1)), and "near certainty" has an exact bound ({{nearlock}}); taken verifiably by one actor alone, it is the candidate first move of {{signal}}.

TESTS sets each item against every crux, and each survives for a reason of its own kind. Measurement is worth most where the readings disagree. Where decisive advantage never arrives, preparing custody costs only the preparation; where it arrives, custody begun at a warning is in place too late wherever building it takes longer than the warning gives (Section 7.2). Research that lowers the tax pays whatever the tax turns out to be. Narrow E1 needs no reading of anyone's intentions.

**Tier 1(b). Warranted now; withdrawn only where a named crux is measured favourable [rec].** Each item names the measurement that would withdraw it, or the part of it a measurement can withdraw. The burden of proof follows the protection standard. Capture arrives before the evidence that would show it: an alarm averaged over captured evaluators fires late or not at all ({{masking}}), and where capture precedes evidence the host crosses its floor before any alarm ({{slowroute}}) [thm∣prem]. A protection of correction that waits for proof of capture waits for proof that comes too late, so each item stands until its crux is measured favourable, not only after it is measured unfavourable [val].

6. **Independent correction**: formula funding at arm's length, mandated access, rotation and rule-making insulated from success, growing at the rate of {{corrrate}}, faster by $w\delta_h$ where it rests on human capacity that delegation erodes ({{humancorr}}). Withdrawn beyond present institutions where row sums are measured below one under present funding and access, with findings executed against leaders (C2; Test M); the measurement of item 1 continues, since a certificate valid today can fail later.
7. **Common admission constraints**, before consequential deployment, internal deployment included, for every class of divergence whose costs fall on common terms, sanctioned above the gain from evasion for every type and of equal incidence among those who comply (R8a, R8b of {{require}}), with audits for hidden objectives and loyalties (Section 6.3). Withdrawn for a class whose tax is measured zero or negative, since its safeguard then pays privately (C9). For masked classes, admission is by capability threshold, with control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation ({{masked}}); that part gives way to admission on evaluated behaviour up to the capability at which independent auditors are shown to bound masking (C12).
8. **Shared provision of useful services**, good enough, distributed and replaceable, **and the divisible gains of capability shared as they arise**, where they can begin. They realize joint value on every reading of the cruxes (V1, V5) [val], and provision not won in the contest lowers the won share on which the scissors turn ({{scissors}}) [thm]. What a measurement can withdraw is the reliance on them to change conduct: if conduct is unchanged under substantial sharing (C6), or provision leaves the weight on winning where it was (C8), that reliance moves to custody and pace [rec]. They are not accepted across camps, and the judgment does not claim that they are.

**Tier 2. Prepared now; activated by mechanism on registered indicators [rec].** This tier carries the substance of the change. Three arrangements are prepared now and activated when registered indicators show the conditions they answer approaching: stopping the decisive class (E2), with rollback where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage; common custody of capability of the decisive class, in force; and development at the pace of understanding (E3), for each hazard class whose coverage falls behind. Stopping is not slowing: E2 concerns the decisive class, E3 the pace of each hazard class. The premise of the race is the reason to prepare them now. That a rival might reach a decisive position is the case in which repetition stops enforcing restraint, and the means of restraint it leaves have to exist before the case arrives (Section 7.2).
- *Trigger.* **A trigger fires when the estimated time to a decisive position, less the time custody needs to be installed and verified, falls below a margin registered in advance**, and custody and E2 activate. Its indicators are the persistence of leads, read on every measure since leads differ by measure, the gap between open and closed models (C5), and detection latency against the time from breakout to decisiveness (C7). E3 activates for a hazard class when its coverage falls behind: residual risk per class not falling, catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours falling as capability rises (C1, C3a), and, for masked classes, independent auditors' detection at fixed access falling with capability (C12). Waiting until decisiveness is shown would be too late.
- *Estimator.* The time to a decisive position is the quantity that masking hides and strategic disclosure shades. It is estimated by correctors whose row sums are certified (C2) and whose misses are measured to be uncorrelated (C3b), from channels that observe actions directly, such as compute, deployments, custody and the performance of contracts, under the conservative rule of {{alarm}}: any bound on masking comes from evidence the observed does not control [rec].
- *Commitment.* **Activation is committed in advance and executed by mechanism, not decided afresh when the trigger fires.** The trigger fires where restraint is least enforceable ({{enforce}}(4)) and the blades of {{scissors}} are closing, so a collective decision taken there would face the very condition it is meant to prevent [thm∣prem]. Preparation shortens the delay of the response from building, deciding and detecting to deciding and detecting, and commitment leaves only detecting: that is the delay {{tempo}} bounds when the mode to be held is a lead that compounds [thm∣prem]. The fast loop stays fast, with review after the fact (Section 10.5).
- *Release.* Release reads indicators different from those of activation, and requires them to hold for the dwell time of the slow variable each step rests on ({{readiness}}; Section 11.4): levels recover before safety does, and capture is harder to undo than to prevent ({{humancorr}}(2)) [num: QS]. A release read from the activation indicators run backwards would come too early.

**Tier 3. Compared, not settled [val].** The whole designs are: the separately held configuration of Section 10.5, developed in X10; a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions; a verified agreement with shared provision; regulated competition that meets {{require}}; an agreement of the type of Section 8.3(g); the benign winner; and the coercive restriction of frontier inputs. They are compared with continuation and with one another on the durability tests of {{durability}}, in one range $\Theta$ that includes each one's own failure modes.

The coercive restriction of frontier inputs enters the comparison only under three conditions of its own, each set by a mechanism of Part I. Its burden is equal, the restrictor's own frontier work included, since a stop borne unequally re-sorts those it binds, is evaded wherever evasion pays their type, and is read as a bid for a lead ({{blind}}(2), (3)) [thm]. It is executed by correctors independent of every party it restricts, the restrictor included, with full inbound row sums below one, since otherwise the restrictor is the largest conversion target there is ({{influence}}) [thm]. And the chamber of those affected consents (V3; R7) [val]. Its escalation hazard is counted in $\Theta$ ({{preventown}}). Even under the three conditions the theory does not derive that its net effect is positive: denial raises the weight on winning and the trust threshold (Section 8.3(f)).

Under the rule for $\Theta$ the worst case of every path includes an irreversible loss, so none meets the tolerance, and the ranking falls to the standard's third step: prefer the lowest bound that keeps options of correction open (Section 8.1). That step needs estimates on both sides, which the cruxes would supply and do not yet; the ranking is therefore a judgment, and it is stated as one [val]. The preference for changing selection rests on two reasons, read from the failure modes that X8 lists path by path, each with its support.
- **(i) Reversibility, over every failure mode.** Each of the design's own failure modes has a named reversal short of O1 and O2, executed by someone other than the failing party. Dissolution falls back into separately held functions, with protections against unilateral domination entrenched; a function whose certificate fails loses executive force until it is recertified, its assets passing to other providers; an interrupted service is carried by the share cap, escrow and funded standby; collusion among the checks, once measured correlation of misses exposes it, is undone by rotation and plural lineage; overreach of a common constraint ends at its sunset, its stated end condition or an appeal with the burden on the compact. These reversals are designed, and none has yet been shown to work; and the design's coercive fallback can escalate irreversibly, as every coercive path can. Continuation's failure modes are reversible only while no position is decisive and no floor has been crossed. Its worst, coverage outgrown, capture before evidence and a decisive position reached, have no reversal ({{coverage}}, {{slowroute}}, {{wingives}}), and for hazards that manifest late its premises cannot be measured before they bind ({{tempo}}). This is what the third step means by keeping options of correction open: a state from which a named reversal still avoids the protected losses lies in the correctable kernel ({{kernel}}), if the reversal works, and a failure without one has left it.
- **(ii) The irreversible failure every path shares.** One failure has no reversal on any path: one party comes to hold decisive capability, by a decisive win on continuation, by restraint failing after victory for the benign winner, by a freeze broken after a sprint, by a covert project escaping a verified agreement, by the conversion of a restrictor's correctors, or, for the design, by capture of the custody of decisive capability (X8). The race produces that failure by construction and selects for it ({{raceselects}}) [thm∣prem]; the design contests it by separation, plural lineages and the tests D4 and D5, and the guardian test of {{guardian}} applies to custody first. The same loss stands on both sides of the comparison; what differs is whether a path selects for it or against it.

A third reason waits on measurement. Where measurement moves paths inside the tolerance, the standard's second step applies, and there the design's case is the joint value it realizes and the race dissipates (Section 10.1); in its worst case no path is inside the tolerance now. A reader who weighs these reasons differently can reach another ranking; Section 12.3 says which measurements would move it.

**The scope.** The tiers are an order, not a scope. The scope is broad E1: AI not used as an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, in labour, finance and persuasion as much as over shared conditions (Section 6.6). Narrow E1 is the first step, and the tiers are the order in which the game itself is changed, by lowering its convertibility, its decisiveness and the conversion of success into control.

### 12.2 Why, and where the leverage is

The judgment follows the chain of Section 1.2, each link with its own support, and it acts where the chain says the system can be moved. "Root" has two senses. *Causally*, the root is a loop, not a belief: selection shapes minds and institutions, and the minds and institutions it selects reshape selection; neither comes first. *As a point of leverage*, the root is understanding, and it acts in two places. It is the transmission through which actors come to count what relative selection makes invisible ({{onegroup}}); and it is how actors agree on which parameters to move, and hold them there for their dwell time.

**The theory of change.** The two places answer the two errors of {{twoerrors}}. Understanding corrects the held boundary directly: an actor that sees the coupling counts it. The working objective that compounding sets is each actor's correct computation of its interest, and no argument changes it; there understanding acts only by making agreement possible, and only agreement with executive force changes the game. Growing understanding without executive authority may change nothing (Section 4.3). Where convertibility and decisiveness are high, transmission is itself selected against, and understanding acts through agreement on structural change or not at all, not on anyone's weights directly (compare Powell 1991) [thm∣prem]. The sequence is therefore understanding shared, agreement on what winning confers, that agreement given force, and the force held long enough not to be re-absorbed.

**The hierarchy and the thresholds.** Meadows ranks twelve places to intervene in a system, from parameters, the weakest (#12), to the paradigm and the power to transcend it (#1) (Meadows 1999). The account here sits inside that ranking. The structural loop gain is the gain of the reinforcing loop (#7); correction is the balancing loop (#8); consequences returned and measurement shared are information flows (#6); one rule for all and the separation rule are rules (#5). Selecting the selectors is the reinforcing loop seizing the power over rules and structure (#4, #5), which is why this paper's levers sit on the coupling between them, the conversion of success into control ($\chi$, $b_{ij}$). Changing what winning confers is a change of goal (#3), made by moving parameters, sharing, provision and convertibility, across their thresholds. That is Meadows's own exception: a parameter is weak until it moves into a range that changes a place higher on the list. Below its threshold a reform is re-absorbed, and across it, it is not ({{reabsorb}}) [thm]. In the author's words, "If we don't solve dominant attractors, focusing on minor adjustments won't change the outcome" [corpus: meta-attractor]. The paradigm, the logic of winning against an accurate model of one system, decides which parameters are moved and whether they are held long enough, and it can change only if the system is understood. Both accounts stand: the paradigm is the highest place, and it acts through thresholds.

A lever can also be pushed the wrong way, which Meadows found to be the common case. Speeding the decay of capacity is such a push: a contest of ratios does not see a decay common to all, so it leaves the structural loop gain unchanged without diffusion and raises it with diffusion (Section 2.2) [thm]. Correction lowers the gain only where it acts on what success converts into.

**Delays and buffers.** The high places move slowest and meet the most resistance, and the path from understanding (#2) to agreement on rules (#5) runs through transmission, which relative selection works against ({{onegroup}}). Two low places buy the time that path needs, and meet little resistance. Delay (#9): where the delay of correction cannot be shortened, the lever is to slow the change it must follow until the delay is short against it, as Meadows advises, and that is E3 ({{tempo}}). Buffers (#11): a lineage of the alternative that can recruit shortens the time a change must be held before it is safe (Section 11.4), and standby capacity for essential services and the host's margin above its floor lengthen the time the system can absorb a shock before a floor is crossed ({{safeguards}}). What they buy is dwell time.

The first step of the sequence is the one a paper can take. That is why this one is written as a prediction to be shared: shared, a prediction of this kind can work against its own fulfilment (Section 11.2).

### 12.3 Cruxes

Each crux is evaluated on its own; TESTS gives the present evidence, the next measurements, the registered tests, and the three outcomes that would redirect the argument, classified before they are seen. C1 is the central crux: on it turn E3 and the case for E2 (Section 5.3).

| Crux | What it is | What a measurement would change |
|---|---|---|
| **C0 Interlock** | Whether moving a shared parameter moves several thresholds of {{thresholds}}, and the signatures of several groups, together | If interventions move only their own signature over the dwell time, the interlock fails for that parameter, and the thresholds it enters are separate problems |
| **C1 Residual risk after correction** | Whether hazardous variety outgrows contestable coverage on the present path; masked classes measured adversarially, as for C12 | Residual risk per class falling under present incentives: E3 and the case for E2 lapse for that class, and their triggers are withdrawn |
| **C2 Conversion into control** | The size of the channels by which success buys influence over correctors | Row sums below one under present funding and access, with findings executed against leaders (Test M): item 6 is withdrawn beyond present institutions, while measurement continues |
| **C3a Scalable oversight** | Whether AI-assisted oversight raises contestable coverage as fast as hazardous variety: catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours as capability rises | If it does, the case for slowing weakens; the separation of evaluators from success stands |
| **C3b Independence of AI oversight** | Whether AI overseers fail independently of what they oversee: correlation of misses and rates of collusion, same lineage against different | Correlated misses or collusion within a lineage: AI-assisted oversight counts toward coverage only from independent lineages, and redundant checks stop at the ceiling of {{topology}} |
| **C4 Durability** | The breakdown and capture of an arrangement that changes selection, enforcement against its strongest participant included; the winner's own correctors | A design failing its stress tests worse than continuation is rejected, without vindicating the race, and another design meeting {{require}} is needed; a leading coalition with certified correctors passes D4 (Test I) |
| **C5 Decisiveness and saturation** | Whether advantage is decisive and unsaturated; leads differ by measure | Leads that do not persist on any measure, and others balancing a rising actor: activation of custody and of E2 lapses; preparation stays |
| **C6, C8 Rewards and the drive** | Whether shared rewards change conduct, and whether provision beneath the game lowers the belief in a struggle for survival and with it the weight on winning | Conduct unchanged under substantial sharing: reliance moves from sharing to custody and pace; provision still lowers the won share through capacity |
| **C7 Detection against breakout** | Detection and response time against the time from breakout to decisiveness | Detection reliably faster: timely detection keeps $p_d$ small ({{detectlink}}), and custody is not the only route to it |
| **C9a Capability gap** | Whether countering a divergence costs capability at fixed compute, class by class | Zero or negative for a class: its safeguard pays privately, and item 7 is withdrawn for it |
| **C9b Monitoring and control overhead** | The compute, latency and labour that monitoring and control protocols add | Its size sets what admission by control protocol costs each developer against what it protects |
| **C9c The speed tax** | The time lost waiting for evidence that arrives only after a manifestation window | Positive wherever evidence lags ({{tempo}}), so no measurement removes it: its size sets what a common pace costs, not whether one is needed |
| **C9d Cost of tax-lowering research** | What it costs to bring a class's tax to zero or below | Low: commonly funded, openly published research (item 3) is the cheapest lever, since what it publishes spreads without coordination |
| **C10 Forgone benefits of E2** | The decisive class's margin over capable systems that are not decisive | A large margin reachable only through the decisive class raises E2's cost and moves the comparison toward E3; its weight is normative (N1) |
| **C11 Coupling and power** | Whether unpriced coupling grows with power | If it does not, the gap between accuracy and what the contest requires falls on institutions |
| **C12 Verifiability** | Whether admission can bound masking at the admitted capability: independent auditors' detection of planted and emergent hidden objectives at fixed access as capability rises | Favourable: admission on evaluated behaviour keeps masked classes out up to that capability. Unfavourable: they pass to capability thresholds with control that does not assume cooperation, and beyond them to E2 or E3 ({{masked}}) |
| **C13 Dependence on the governed** | Whether AI lowers the dependence of institutions, and of a winner, on people's labour, consent and judgment | If it does, alignment to a principal stops meaning alignment to the people it serves, and what an accurate winner gives the governed falls with that dependence ({{wingives}}): the benign winner's strongest form weakens, and its residual risk rises |
| **N1–N3** | Discounting and risk attitude; the weight of O2 against O1; correction no one can understand | Normative choices, to be stated by each reader |
| **N4 Standing of AI systems** | Whether AI systems have standing in V3's sense | Granting it leaves the priority of O2 during the transition in place (Section 6.6) and adds the systems to those whose capacity to contest an optimization V3 protects |

None of the cruxes is a conjunction of the others; a conjunction would say that regulated competition wins only by becoming this paper's own design. If C1 and C2 were both measured favourable, the diagnosis of the present path would weaken. If C5 and C7 were both favourable, the custody requirement would lapse; the requirements on correction, coverage, measurement and admission would stand, and so would the facts that make them requirements: one host, coupled actors, no outside. If regulated competition in a set of jurisdictions meets a requirement, this paper will say so without renaming it.

### 12.4 Conclusion

Where whatever can gain or lose share is selected by relative success, what is won converts into the capacity to win again, and a lead can become decisive, success raises the stakes and the gain from defection, and it weakens, each in its own model, the sanctions, the correctors and the shared language that could restrain it; that these move together as the stakes outgrow what does not have to be won is the conjecture the diagnosis tests (C0). That is the selection pressure of winning. Where success can also buy the selectors, the selection itself reproduces the weakening, and that closure is the meta-attractor of winning. It accelerates the contest, makes it harder and harder to regulate, and alienates language. Inside it each actor computes correctly and the game is wrong: wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, looking ahead weighs standing above what all share, so the logic of winning is individually rational, collectively self-defeating, and false as a doctrine about nature. AI is its most powerful instrument and can become its next carrier, and aligning each system to its principal does not align the game among principals. There is one host and no outside: no rival whole whose competition could drive the next integration, and no second attempt after a failure that cannot be undone. Against such failures integration must be anticipated, which is why understanding that we are one, one interconnected system, is a long-term requirement if growth and change are to continue. Oneness is not sameness; it is the accurate recognition of the system each part belongs to, and the design of a selection that counts it.

The way out is not to abolish competition, growth or selection. It is to change what winning confers, so that the same reflexive structure becomes generative and persists through what all share, not through its winners. A change in that direction has seven marks: consequences return to their source (G1); winning confers reward, not more advantage in the same game (G2); success cannot buy control over its own assessment, and correction stays plural, decorrelated and in common hands (G3); decisive capability is held in common or not built (G4); provision of the conditions of a decent life sits beneath the game (G5); measurement and language are shared (G6); pace is bound to understanding (G7). Any arrangement that does these things, whatever it is called, changes the basin. Change the basin first; then add speed.

The meaning is affirmative, and it comes before the commitment. We are not only our separate lives: we are the larger process of becoming, going on through us, and that is good; in the author's words, "we are not just our life. We are the whole process of the universe doing this and itself and this is great" [corpus: shengsheng]. Decay and renewal belong to its growth, which is also why an order whose forms can fail and be replaced is one that can still be reformed (Section 11.4). Humanity has a great future in it, and so can the minds we are now making. What has to be opposed is not growth, competition, technology or any people, but one logic: a part that forgets it belongs to a body. AI is a planetary technology, for all, not a means for one side to grow bigger at the others' expense (V1). What it is for is medicine, learning, science, competent and answerable authority, provision that, like trees, sustains the system it belongs to without becoming it, and human agency kept in the decisions that matter. What has to be agreed is not who comes first, but what every participant needs in order to go on, and how the means of correction stay in common hands.
