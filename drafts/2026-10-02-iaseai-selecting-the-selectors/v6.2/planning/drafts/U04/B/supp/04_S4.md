## S4. Selecting the selectors

### Proof of {{influence}}, and the effect of scope

Support keyed to the carrier obeys $\dot z^\chi_i=\varepsilon_i(\chi q\sum_j\hat b_{ij}y_j-d_iz^\chi_i)$ from zero; its horizon average is $z_i^{\chi\ast}L_i$ with $z_i^{\chi\ast}=\chi q\sum_j\hat b_{ij}y_j/d_i$ and $L_i=1-(1-e^{-\varepsilon_id_iT_h})/(\varepsilon_id_iT_h)$. Weighting the per-decision advantage $a_iz^\chi_i$ by $y_i$ and summing gives $\chi q\,y^\top My$, with $y=p\bar y$: share enters once through the success converted and once through the decisions the support favours. Shared support cancels. Differentiating gives the gradient, and under faithful transmission $\dot{\bar q}\propto\operatorname{Cov}(q,\Delta\pi(q))$ (Price 1970). With a floor $f>0$, $\Delta\pi'(0)>0$ iff $\chi p^2\bar y^\top M\bar y>fp^{\xi_f}$, that is, $p>p^\ast$, and for $p<p^\ast$ capacity loses for every $q>0$, since $\mathrm{Cost}$ is convex in $q$. The comparative statics follow from $c_iL_i\hat G_{ij}=a_i\hat b_{ij}\varepsilon_iT_h\,\ell_L(\varepsilon_id_iT_h)$ with $\ell_L(v)=[1-(1-e^{-v})/v]/v$ decreasing. $\square$

**{{!Proposition:scope}} (Scope) [thm].** *If the carrier has share $p$ in each domain of a set $\mathcal S$, with $\hat G$ equal to $\hat g$ on the diagonal and $\hat\varphi$ off it and $c_iL_i=cL$, the return is $\chi cL\,p^2|\mathcal S|(\hat g+(|\mathcal S|-1)\hat\varphi)$: superlinear in $|\mathcal S|$ at fixed $p$ when $\hat\varphi>0$, and rising with scope at fixed total size only if $\hat\varphi>\hat g$.* (Sum over $\mathcal S$ and substitute $p=Y/|\mathcal S|$.)

*Relation to earlier work.* Influence over policy bought with contributions, in equilibrium with the policymaker's other aims, is the subject of protection for sale (Grossman and Helpman 1994); what is added is that the return to influence over correctors scales with the square of share and with its appropriability, so the capacity is selected in the largest carriers, and that spending can be classified by what the rule would do rather than by its stated aim (EV4). Construction of a shared resource is undermined by competition, and monopolizing the constructed niche restores the incentive to build (Krakauer, Page and Erwin 2009); {{influence}} has the same appropriability logic, with the selection rule as the object modified, a field of domains, and the opposite valence. Dynamic models of constitutions show present decisions changing future decision rights (Acemoglu, Egorov and Sonin 2012); whether a model of that family reproduces Section 4 is open. Fixed-cost participation in lobbying (Bombardini 2008) is the empirical counterpart of the threshold. Appropriability plays the role relatedness plays in Hamilton's rule.

### Full statement and proof of {{noinside}}

*Let carriers differ in an investment $u\ge0$, at private cost $C_u(u)$, that raises the correction rate $d$ for everyone. (1) Among otherwise identical carriers of the same practice, those investing more are selected against under any payoff-monotone rule. (2) Holding the host fixed, correction lowers the payoff of a carrier that holds rule support. (3) A challenger invests only if its own share of the benefit exceeds $C_u'(0)$, so dispersed challengers free-ride. (4) Correction keyed to the corrector yields a return to influence of the form of {{influence}}; whether it produces capture depends on whether row sums cross one. (5) If $\Upsilon>1$ and correction makes liability effective, an incumbent can gain from correction applied to all carriers, while correction applied to it alone costs it rent and share.*

Reputation or competition among institutions sustains independent correction only when contributions and outcomes are observed (Panchanathan and Boyd 2004; Sigmund et al. 2010). *Proof.* (1) Two carriers differing only in $u$ are affected identically by the change in $d$; their payoff difference is $C_u(u_l)-C_u(u_k)<0$ for $u_k>u_l$. (2) At fixed $V$ a carrier's support declines in $d$. (3) is the public-goods condition. (4) is immediate. (5) compares $\nu\bar e(\Upsilon-1)$ with the lost rent. $\square$

### Proof of {{corrrate}}

With $L_i=1$ and uniform $d$, $M=\hat A/d$ and $\Delta\pi(q)=\chi q\hat Y/d-\mathrm{Cost}(q;p)$, so $q^\ast=\big[(\chi\hat Y/d-fp^{\xi_f})_+/(\gamma p^{\xi_v})\big]^{1/\eta}$.

*(1).* With $f=0$,
$$G_{\rm ev}=\frac{A}{c_{\rm ev}d}+Z_\eta\,(\gamma p^{\xi_v})^{-1/\eta}d^{-(1+\eta)/\eta},\qquad Z_\eta=a_{\rm ev}\hat B(\chi\hat Y)^{1/\eta}/c_{\rm ev},$$
strictly decreasing in $d$; this gives (1), and for $\eta=1$, $d^\ast=\big(A+\sqrt{A^2+4c_{\rm ev}Z}\big)/(2c_{\rm ev})$ with $Z=a_{\rm ev}\hat B\chi\hat Y/(\gamma p^{\xi_v})$. A floor only lowers $q^\ast$, so $d^\ast\ge A/c_{\rm ev}$ with equality iff $q^\ast=0$; the cultivated term rises with $\chi$, with $\hat Y$ and $\hat B$ (size and scope, {{scope}}) and as $\gamma$ falls.

*(2).* Along $\gamma_0e^{-\alpha_{\rm ctl}t}$ and $d_0e^{\alpha_{\rm cor}t}$, the first term of the quasi-static row sum $G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}$ is nonincreasing and the second is proportional to $e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$, whatever $\xi_v$, since the size factor $p^{-\xi_v/\eta}$ is constant. *Sufficient half.* Under any adjustment that starts at or below $q^\ast$ and does not raise capacity while it is above $q^\ast$, $q(t)\le\max\{q(0),\sup_{s\le t}q^\ast(s)\}$. Since $d$ is nondecreasing, $(A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq^\ast(s))/(c_{\rm ev}d(t))\le G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}(s)$ for $s\le t$, so
$$G_{\rm ev}(t)\le\max\Big\{G_{\rm ev}(0),\ \sup_{s\le t}G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}(s)\Big\}\le G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}(0)<1$$
when $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and $d_0>d^\ast(\gamma_0)$. *Necessary half.* If $q\ge\rho q^\ast$ eventually, with $\rho>0$, the cultivated term is at least $\rho$ times the quasi-static one, which grows without bound below the rate law. Once the captured set holds at least half its decisions, rule support in evaluation is driven by $\hat Bq\ge\rho\hat Bq^\ast$, which grows as $e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$; integrating gives $a_{\rm ev}z_{\rm ev}(t)\ge c\,e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$, so $\operatorname{logit}x_{\rm ev}\to\infty$. The rate condition does not repair a certificate invalid at the start.

*(3).* $q^\ast>0$ iff $\chi\hat Y/d>fp^{\xi_f}$ iff $d<d_f$. With $f$ fixed, $d\ge d_f$ ends cultivation, so the requirement levels off at $\max\{A/c_{\rm ev},d_f\}$. Under $[H_f]$, $d_f\propto1/f$ rises at $\alpha_f$ (V6.1's S4 said "falls"; the sign is corrected), and $d/d_f=(d_0/d_f(0))e^{(\alpha_{\rm cor}-\alpha_f)t}$. If $\alpha_{\rm cor}>\alpha_f$, cultivation ends at $T_f=\ln(d_f(0)/d_0)/(\alpha_{\rm cor}-\alpha_f)$, or never starts if $d_0\ge d_f(0)$, and afterwards $G_{\rm ev}=A/(c_{\rm ev}d)\to0$. If $\alpha_{\rm cor}=\alpha_f$, $d/d_f$ is constant: nothing is cultivated if $d_0\ge d_f(0)$, and otherwise $q^\ast=(1-d_0/d_f(0))^{1/\eta}$ times its value without the floor, so (2) applies with that fraction. If $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_f$, $d/d_f\to0$, the factor $(1-d/d_f)^{1/\eta}\to1$, and $q^\ast$ approaches its value without the floor. *Transient.* Before $T_f$ the cultivated term grows at $(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})/\eta$, damped only by $(1-d/d_f)^{1/\eta}$; with $\alpha_{\rm cor}$ slightly above $\alpha_f$, $T_f$ is long and the row sum passes one long before cultivation ends (FF). *Lump sum.* With a cost $Fp^{\xi_f}$ paid once if $q>0$, cultivation pays iff the maximized surplus $\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}(\chi\hat Y/d)^{(1+\eta)/\eta}(\gamma p^{\xi_v})^{-1/\eta}$ exceeds $Fp^{\xi_f}$. With $\hat Y\propto p^2$ the size threshold is proportional to $F^{\eta/(2+2\eta-\xi_v-\eta\xi_f)}$, against $f^{1/(2-\xi_f)}$ for the per-unit floor. Once cultivation pays, $q^\ast$ is its value without any floor, and as $\gamma\to0$ the surplus grows without bound, so the lump sum stops binding and the requirement grows as it does without a floor.

*(4).* Parts of share $p/k$ have $\hat Y$ divided by $k^2$ and $p^{\xi_v}$ by $k^{\xi_v}$, so $q^\ast$ is divided by $k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$; the $k$ parts together convert $p\,q^\ast_{\rm part}$, so total cultivated conversion is divided by the same factor, and the cultivated term reaches a given level later by $\Delta t=(2-\xi_v)\ln k/(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})$. With $f>0$ fixed, parts below the threshold cultivate nothing. With $q_k^\ast\propto p_k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$ and conversion proportional to $p_kq_k^\ast$, the total is proportional to $\sum_kp_k^{1+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$. $\square$

*Further parts.* If AI lowers the unit cost of independent correction at rate $\alpha_{uc}$ and its budget grows at $\alpha_B$, then $\alpha_{\rm cor}=\alpha_B+\alpha_{uc}$, and eventual failure needs $\alpha_{\rm ctl}>(1+\eta)(\alpha_B+\alpha_{uc})$; correction keeps pace without budget growth once $\alpha_{uc}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$. With a finite horizon, while $\varepsilon_idT_h\ll1$ the required growth is about $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/\eta$, a pre-asymptotic interval. With concave conversion $\hat bq^{\xi_c}$, the required rate becomes $\xi_c\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$. The model implies that influence spending rises with share as $p^{\xi_v+(1+\eta)(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$, at least quadratically, which is testable.

*Numerics (CS).* The size threshold at $f=0.05$ is 0.2236 per carrier and 0.0500 per decision, and 0.1717 and 0.0998 at $\xi_f=0.3$ and $0.7$. ### Capacity off its selected level

The rate law of {{corrrate}}(2) takes capacity at its selected level. Selection on capacity is itself a dynamic, and the joint model shows where the quasi-static reading is conservative and where it is optimistic.

**{{!Proposition:capdyn}} (The rate law off the selected level) [thm; num].** *Take the evaluation domain of {{eq:field}} with row sum {{eq:rowsum}}, $[H_\gamma]$, $f=0$ and $d=d_0e^{\alpha_{\rm cor}t}$, and let capacity $q$ adjust toward its selected level $q^\ast(t)$, which grows at $g=(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-\alpha_{\rm cor})/\eta$.*
1. *(Sufficiency under any adjustment.) If capacity starts at or below $q^\ast$ and does not rise while above it, the sufficient half of {{corrrate}}(2) holds whatever the speed of adjustment. While $q^\ast$ rises, such capacity stays below it, so the quasi-static failure date is the earliest [thm].*
2. *(Necessity needs tracking.) Below the rate law the certificate fails beyond a finite time whenever $\liminf_tq/q^\ast>0$ [thm]. (a) Under adjustment at a constant rate $\kappa_q$, $\dot q=\kappa_q(q^\ast-q)$, $q/q^\ast\to\kappa_q/(\kappa_q+g)$ and failure is delayed, asymptotically, by $\eta\ln(1+g/\kappa_q)/(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})$ [thm]. (b) Under gradient selection with constant additive variance $V$, $\dot q=V\partial_q\Delta\pi$, the speed of adjustment falls as control cheapens and $q(t)\le q(0)+V\chi\hat Y/(d_0\alpha_{\rm cor})$, so for every $\alpha_{\rm cor}>0$ the certificate holds from some date on, after a possible transient failure [thm]. (c) Under gradient selection with variance proportional to $q^2$, capacity tracks its selected level below the rate law [num].*
3. *(Reversal.) In a self-sustaining captured domain, a reform that raises $d$ for a hold and is then withdrawn must be held longer when capacity lags its selected level than the quasi-static model says [num].*

*Proof.* (1) is the sufficiency argument of {{corrrate}}(2); while $q^\ast$ rises and $q(0)\le q^\ast(0)$, any such capacity satisfies $q\le q^\ast$, so $G_{\rm ev}\le G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}$ at every date. (2) The first claim is the necessity argument. (a) The linear equation has the particular solution $q=\kappa_qq^\ast/(\kappa_q+g)$, which shifts the exponential of the cultivated term by $\ln(1+g/\kappa_q)$ at rate $(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})/\eta$. (b) $\dot q=V(\chi\hat Y/d-\gamma p^{\xi_v}q^\eta)\le V\chi\hat Ye^{-\alpha_{\rm cor}t}/d_0$; integrating bounds $q$, and then $G_{\rm ev}\le(A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq_{\max})/(c_{\rm ev}d)\to0$. (c) At $\eta=1$ the relaxation rate is proportional to $Vq^{\ast2}\gamma$, which grows as $e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-2\alpha_{\rm cor})t}$ below the rate law; the tracking is checked numerically. (3) is numerical. $\square$

The necessary half is therefore a result about carriers that optimize, or adjust at a rate that does not fall as control cheapens; capacity that must leap toward an optimum running away from it is closer to strategic choice than to gradual selection, and that is the case a requirement should be designed against.

*Numerics (QS).* Four dynamics in the CS setting ($\chi=\hat Y=\eta=1$, $\xi_v=0$, $p=0.6$, $A=0.3$, $a_{\rm ev}\hat B=c_{\rm ev}=1$, $d_0=2$, $\gamma=e^{-0.2t}$, rate law 0.1; $\zeta=1$, $\varepsilon=0.5$, $x_{\rm ev}(0)=0.01$), in two implementations that agree to within 0.1. At $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0.09$ the dates are 145.9, 161.5, 183.0, 222.9, 146.2 and never; at 0.11 the certificate holds under every dynamic. Simulated delays against the formula of (2)(a): 4.11/4.05, 9.29/9.16 and 18.11/17.92 at $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0.05$; 8.40/8.41, 19.70/19.71 and 40.23/40.24 at 0.08. Under additive selection, $q(600)=7.29$ against the bound 9.18 at $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0.02$, and 2.18 against 3.78 at 0.05, with the row sum never above 0.51. ### Proof of {{humancorr}}, and the hysteresis of release

*(1).* With $\mathcal M(t)=\mathcal M_0e^{-\delta_ht}$, $d_{\rm eff}(t)=d_0e^{(\alpha_{\rm cor}-w\delta_h)t}$. Carriers select capacity against the correction that acts, and the certificate is computed with it, so {{corrrate}}(2) applies to $d_{\rm eff}$ with growth rate $\alpha_{\rm cor}-w\delta_h$, and both halves hold with the threshold raised by $w\delta_h$. If erosion stops at $\mathcal M_{\min}>0$, $d_{\rm eff}$ tends to $d(\mathcal M_{\min}/\mathcal M_0)^w$: the rate condition is unchanged, and the level $d$ must exceed the requirement by the factor $(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})^w$. $\square$

*(2), the mechanism.* Captured evaluation reports that delegation is safe; delegation rises; human capacity erodes; correction weakens; capture deepens. This is a second positive loop, through human capacity, beside the conversion loop of {{eq:field}}, and it makes capture bistable over a band of correction rates: a domain that is not captured stays uncaptured at rates at which a captured one stays captured.

*Numerics (QS; systems review, joint simulation).* With $w=1$ and pure erosion, the threshold is exactly 0.15 and 0.20 at $\delta_h=0.05$ and 0.1, against a rate law of 0.1. With human capacity following $\dot{\mathcal M}=-\delta_hD\mathcal M+\varrho_h(1-D)(1-\mathcal M)$, delegation $D(t)=1-0.8e^{-0.1t}$ and $w=1$, the largest $\alpha_{\rm cor}$ at which evaluation is still captured by $t=600$ rises from 0.096 without the coupling to 0.145, 0.190 and 0.231 at $\delta_h=0.05$, 0.1 and 0.2 (recovery $\varrho_h=0.05$, 0.05 and 0.1), near $0.1+\delta_h$ and below it at a finite horizon. For (2), in one domain at a constant cost of control, with $\delta_h=0.2$, $\varrho_h=0.05$, $w=\tfrac12$ and delegation $D=0.1+k_Dx_{\rm ev}$, the smallest permanent correction rate that undoes capture from the captured state is 0.634 at $k_D=0$, matching the 0.638 above which the certificate holds without the loop; 1.006 at $k_D=0.3$; and 1.675 at $k_D=0.6$, where captured human capacity is 0.40 and 0.14 of its uncaptured level. These are 1.6 and 2.6 times the certificate rate. The form $d_{\rm eff}=d(\mathcal M/\mathcal M_0)^w$ is a modelling choice inside the open interface "Human capacity and correction" (S13).

### Mobilization ({{countervail}})

Let those harmed mobilize in proportion to perceived harm, $d=d_0+k_B(1-\tilde V)$. Since $1-\tilde V=(1-m)(1-V)$, masking attenuates the feedback by $1-m$ exactly when it is needed, and even with accurate reports $d\le d_0+k_B$, which cannot keep a certificate against a requirement that rises without bound. After a recovery, mobilization falls back to $d_0$ and the certificate can fail again: a regulatory cycle. With a per-unit floor, enough gain and speed can keep the certificate over a stated interval.

### Proof of {{masking}}, and the late alarm

$\tilde V$ increases in $V$ and equals $V_{\rm alarm}$ at $V=(V_{\rm alarm}-m)/(1-m)$. For $V_{\min}\le V\le V_{\rm alarm}$ and $m>m_{\rm fl}$, $\tilde V\ge V_{\min}+m(1-V_{\min})>V_{\rm alarm}$. At $V^\ast$, $\tilde V\ge V^\ast+\bar m(1-V^\ast)>V_{\rm alarm}$. $\square$ If $V\to0$ and eventually $m\le m_1<V_{\rm alarm}$, the alarm fires. If $V$ declines from $V_{\rm alarm}$ as $\dot V=-k_VV$ with constant $m<V_{\rm alarm}$, the alarm is delayed by $k_V^{-1}\ln[(1-m)V_{\rm alarm}/(V_{\rm alarm}-m)]$, which diverges as $m\uparrow V_{\rm alarm}$.

### {{alarm}} in full

(1) With an upper bound $m_{\rm in}$ known, an alarm on reports at $V_{\rm target}+m_{\rm in}(1-V_{\rm target})$ fires no later than the true condition reaches $V_{\rm target}$, for every $m\le m_{\rm in}$. (2) A bound estimated inside the evaluated lineage is itself masked. (3) For masking by the evaluated model, a bound estimated by evaluators independent in funding and appointment is still a reading of the model's behaviour, which the model can shape (evaluation awareness, sandbagging); the bound must come from evidence its behaviour does not control: probes of weights and activations, audits of training data, and elicitation by fine-tuning, which recovers capability that a model withholds under prompting. (4) Where models evaluate models, through AI feedback, model-written evaluations and models as judges, $x_{\rm ev}$ is the share of that evaluation done by the evaluated lineage and can be read from who built the evaluator; checks of the same lineage add a common cause of their own (Section 6.5). (5) A disjunctive alarm fails silently only if $m_{\rm out}>m_{\rm fl}$. (6) For static, memoryless affine masking $\tilde V=m+(1-m)V$, the correlation coefficient of reports is that of $V$, exactly. *Numerics (AC):* on a stochastic logistic host with load 0.4 and true condition 0.36, masking at $m=0,0.25,0.5$ gives mean reports 0.358, 0.519 and 0.679 with the same lag-one autocorrelation, 0.9814, from which the condition recovered is 0.376; a report filter with memory (exponential smoothing at 0.8) drives the recovered value to 0.042, and the indicator fails. It also needs long series, and once used as an alarm it becomes a target; faking dynamics is nevertheless costlier than faking levels, and the indicator can be cross-checked against channels that observe actions directly.

### The field model

**{{!Lemma:fieldlemma}} (The field is cooperative) [thm].** *With $N_D$ domains, $[0,1]^{N_D}\times[0,\infty)^{N_D}$ is forward invariant under {{eq:field}}, $\limsup_tz_i\le\sum_jb_{ij}/d_i$, and the system is cooperative, so its flow preserves the componentwise order (Hirsch 1985; Smith 1995).*

*Proof of {{certificate}}.* (1) At $Z_{\mathcal C}$ the Jacobian is block lower-triangular, with diagonal entries $\mp\zeta_ic_i(G_i(\mathcal C)-1)$ and $-\varepsilon_id_i$. (2) By the lemma, eventually $a_iz_i-c_i\le c_i(\sum_jG_{ij}-1)/2<0$. (3) Take $G_{i1}>1$ for $i\ne1$, $G_{12}>1$, and other off-diagonal gains zero. (4) Pruning, by comparison. $\square$

*Proof of {{reabsorb}}.* Comparison and pruning; the re-seeding case is the cascade from the domains that remain captured. **Cascades.** If $x_j\to1$ on a seed, $x_i\to1$ on its cascade hull, the closure of $\mathcal S_{k+1}=\mathcal S_k\cup\{i:x_i(0)>0,\ G_i(\mathcal S_k)>1\}$ (Kempe, Kleinberg and Tardos 2003; Morris 2000), by induction using a lower bound on rule support.

*The keystone example.* In the first version of this model, a liability reform at development unwound capture in every domain while development was a keystone; once the other domains' mutual conversion rose to 1.1, capture survived the complete decoupling of development.

*Proof of {{pausefield}}.* The frozen share drives $z\to b_px_p/d$. The equilibria are $(0,0)$, $(1,b/d)$ and the saddle $(1/G,c/a)$. The function $axz-cx-\frac{ad}{2b}z^2$ is nondecreasing along solutions, so every solution converges to an equilibrium and the stable manifold of the saddle separates the basins; the point $(x_p,bx_p/d)$ lies on the nullcline through the saddle, and $x_\sigma>1/G$ by strong cooperativity. $\square$ *Length of an insulated pause.* By cooperativity the basin of absence is a down-set, bounded by the separatrix. With rule-making insulated, rule support decays from its captured level $b/d$ as $(b/d)e^{-\varepsilon dt}$ while the share stays at $x_p$; at release the state is in the basin of absence iff it lies below the separatrix, whose height at $x_p$ is $S(x_p)$ in units of $c/a$. In those units the captured level is $G$, so the pause must last $\ln\big(G/S(x_p)\big)/(\varepsilon d)$: zero where $S(x_p)\ge G$, and no finite length where $x_p\ge x_\sigma$. *Numerics (DW; systems review, separatrix by backward integration):* $x_\sigma$ is 0.676 when rule-making is as fast as adoption with $G=2$ and 0.984 when it is ten times slower; 0.321 and 0.775 at $G=5$. In units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, with $G=2$ and equal speeds the pause is zero from $x_p=0.3$ ($S=2.15$), 0.69 from $x_p=0.5$, 1.50 from 0.6, and infinite from 0.676; with rule-making ten times slower, 0.24 from $x_p=0.1$ and 2.08 from 0.95; with $G=5$ and equal speeds, 1.61 from $x_p=0.2$, 3.45 from 0.3, and infinite from 0.321. Dwell times of a reform that insulates rule-making without freezing shares follow in S11. This result also appears as Proposition 10 of the author's paper of 28 September 2026 (LEDGER records the overlap; attribution is the author's to settle).

*What the field model cannot show.* It is cooperative, so it has no attracting cycles and describes lock-in, not reform cycles; a delayed, masked negative loop from the host to correction could produce cycles and is not modelled (a delayed balancing channel is treated in S2). *Recovering the parameters.* Integrating {{eq:field}} gives $\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{logit}x_i=\zeta_ic_i[\sum_jG_{ij}(k_i*x_j)(t)-1]$ plus a decaying transient, with kernel $k_i(s)=\varepsilon_id_ie^{-\varepsilon_id_is}$; series for the shares identify the conversions. This is recoverability, not causal identification.

### Proof of {{slowroute}}, and the two clocks

By (a) and {{load}}, on every alarm-free path the load tends to $\bar e$ and $\limsup_tV\le(1-h_V\bar e/\rho)^+<V_{\min}$ under (reach). By (e), $m(t)>m_{\rm fl}$ throughout the band $[V_{\min},V_{\rm alarm}]$, so by {{masking}} no alarm fires there, and the host passes below $V_{\min}$ on an alarm-free path. If $m_{\rm in}\le m_{\rm fl}$, then $m\le m_{\rm fl}$ and the alarm fires at some $V\ge V_{\min}$, later than without masking. *Clock form.* By (b), (c) and {{corrrate}}(2), with its part (3) where the floor falls, the certificate fails and $x_{\rm ev}\to1$, so after a finite date $T_{\rm cap}$ the share $x_{\rm ev}$ stays above the threshold of (e). If $T_{\rm cap}<T_A$, every date at which $V$ lies in the band is later than $T_{\rm cap}$, so (e) holds and the first part applies. $\square$ The load is at most $\bar e$, so by comparison the host declines no faster than under full load; when $h_V\bar e<\rho$ it cannot reach the alarm before $(\rho-h_V\bar e)^{-1}\ln[(1-V^\ast/V_0)/(1-V^\ast/V_{\rm alarm})]$, with $V^\ast=1-h_V\bar e/\rho<V_{\rm alarm}$, a lower bound on $T_A$; in every case $T_A$ is finite under (reach).

Conditions (b) and (c) are not used in the first part: they make (e) likely and do not imply it. Two computations show the clocks deciding. *Numerics (SR):* on the logistic host with $\rho=0.05$, $h_V=0.08$, full load from $V=1$, floor 0.3, alarm 0.6 and $m_{\rm in}=0.5$ ($m_{\rm fl}=0.43$, so (e) needs $x_{\rm ev}>0.857$), capture of evaluation centred at $t=0$, past the threshold from $t=3.6$, leaves the alarm silent and the floor is crossed at $t=21$; with capture centred at $t=20$ or $t=45$, $x_{\rm ev}\to1$ still holds, but the alarm fires at $t=7.4$, before the floor: (b) and (c) without (e). *Numerics (QS; systems review, capture endogenous):* with correction not growing and capacity at its selected level, evaluation is captured near $t=23.5$; on the same host with a race share rising from 0.05 at rate 0.12, independent evaluation raises the alarm at $t=32.1$ with $V=0.58$, while capacity at its selected level or adjusting at rates 1 and 0.1 delays the alarm to $t=58.8$ with $V=0.20$, after the floor; capacity adjusting at 0.03, or under additive-variance selection, leaves evaluation uncaptured when the alarm is due, and it fires at $t=32.1$. With correction growing at 0.05, capture comes too late under every dynamic and the alarm fires at $t=32.1$. Whether capture precedes evidence thus depends on the time constant of capacity as well as on the rate law, which is why (e) is a premise to check and not a consequence.
