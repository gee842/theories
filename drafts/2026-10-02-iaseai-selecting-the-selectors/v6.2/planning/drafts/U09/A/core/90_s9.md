## 9. What any adequate arrangement must do

> **Claim.** What every adequate arrangement must do can be stated before designs are compared, each requirement with its kind of support, necessary in a named model over a named horizon, sufficient, or normative, and with the conditions under which it does not bind [def]. Where carriers are selected by relative success, a race carrier is present and effective liability is below the private advantage, no carrier supplies a common constraint alone; where rule-makers are themselves in a contest of standing, none imposes one alone; and where rule-making is captured, enacting a liability above the advantage is not enough [thm]. Several requirements therefore cannot be met by any actor alone, and competition among actors does not supply them [thm∣prem].
>
> **Why.** A requirement stated with its model and its exceptions can be checked against any design, regulated competition included, which can then be found to meet it [def]. A common constraint adopted by one carrier is a cost that relative selection removes, and those who would impose it on all are carriers in a contest of their own [thm]. Every sanction, observation and count of coverage the requirements call for is a corrector's act [def]; where success can buy the correctors, an enacted liability falls to nothing, and the other requirements weaken through the interfaces {{thresholds}} marks [thm∣prem: the interfaces marked †].

Part I proved its thresholds one model at a time, each with a side on which a protected outcome is lost ({{thresholds}}). Read in reverse, such a result is a requirement: an arrangement that does not keep the field on the safe side loses the outcome, in that model and over that horizon. Each row of the table carries its kind of support, with the tag of the result it rests on; the model and horizon in which it is needed; and the conditions under which it does not bind, so that no row is stronger than its model [def]. Where a condition meets a row rather than excusing it, the table says so. The means by which a design meets a row are not rows; they are the subject of Section 10. The table organizes results proved elsewhere and ranks no design. Read together, its rows state with their models the third job of "we are one" (Section 3.7): a selection that counts the system.

**Reading the rows.** Two senses of coverage are kept apart: R2's is of actors, the reach of the common constraints; R5's is of hazard modes, the share of hazardous variety under correction that meets the four conditions of Section 5.3. R8 is split because its clauses do different work, as Section 3.2 showed: the sanction keeps a constraint, and equal incidence keeps it from becoming a weapon. R9 is the row for O2. The capacity to take decisions back survives delegation only if it is kept in practice, which costs strength where the contest is decided by strength ({{lockin}}); only a lock that every actor knows to be certain makes keeping AI out an equilibrium without any arrangement.

**The row the others rest on.** Every row but R7 is carried out by correctors. A liability binds at $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, what captured rule-making leaves of it (Section 4.1); the sanction that bounds a breakout and the one that keeps an admission constraint are applied by correctors, and the second is itself subject to capture ({{nested}}(3)); detection, and the verification of capacity kept in practice, are theirs; and assessment that success has captured does not count toward coverage [def]. A design that meets the other rows with correctors success can buy therefore meets R1 on paper only, since effective liability falls to zero as rule-making is captured, and loses the others through the interfaces {{thresholds}} marks † [thm∣prem: those interfaces]. R3, correction that success cannot buy, is the row on which the table stands: the reflexive closure of {{meta}}, seen from the side of what must be done.

**What the table does not list.** Sharing, provision beneath the game and reward that does not convert into control are not rows, because the models give them as alternatives, not necessities: equal standing is stable under sharing, lower decisiveness, provision not won or diffusion, any one of which can carry the margin ({{compound}}) [thm]. What sets them apart is that each meets several rows at once. Provision that grows with the stakes holds the won share still, and with it both sides of R4 ({{scissors}}); reward that does not convert into control, $\chi=0$, brings R3a's requirement down to its level without second-order selection ({{corrrate}}(1)) [thm]. They are the levers of Section 10.2, and two of the generative marks of Section 8.2 name them. S9 gives the crosswalk ({{crosswalk}}): for each row, the threshold it guards, the mark that meets it, the durability test that checks it and the readiness dimension that asks it.

**Where admission does not reach.** Three limits of R8a are not exceptions to it, and a design meets each by other means. Admission placed at release does not reach internal deployment, where the systems most likely to become carriers, those that automate AI research, are used first (Section 6.4); the row therefore places admission before consequential deployment. Admission acts for the last time at release: openly released weights can be fine-tuned around any constraint (Section 6.4), so for an openly released class the decision to release is the admission decision for every later use, and what remains afterwards is a liability on use where harm can be attributed, the route of R1. And a masked divergence, one that changes under evaluation or appears only in some contexts, escapes admission conditioned on evaluated behaviour once the expected sanction admission can impose falls below the tax ({{masked}}). For masked classes R8a is necessary and not sufficient: admission is stated in capability, up to the level at which control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation remain safe, and beyond it the class falls under R5, stopped (E2) or held to the pace at which control covers it (E3).

**Sufficient safeguards, and form.** Four safeguards are sufficient and not necessary. Full inbound row sums below one certify each corrector domain against every state of the others ({{certificate}}(2)). The safeguards (I1)–(I4) of Section 10.3 give viability once in force, with a liability margin set by the host, the floor, the initial composition and the speed of selection, which a host that degrades by itself below a threshold needs from the start ({{>safeguards}}). Not building the decisive class, holding it in common custody, and detecting and answering faster than breakout meet R4 by changing its premise ({{restraint}}). And verification whose expected sanction is at least the net gain from exit deters irreversible exit from an agreement whose overhang grows ({{overhang}}). The requirements constrain functions, not institutional form: one institution can meet them, and so can mutually recognized institutions of several jurisdictions, a federation or a polycentric complex [def]. Regulated competition meets the rows its rules carry where those rules reach every capable actor and its correctors are separated from success [def]; what a single rule-maker cannot do is the next result.

**{{!Proposition:nounilateral}} (No unilateral solution) [thm].** Neither a carrier nor a rule-maker in a contest of standing supplies the common constraints alone, and enacting them is not enough while rule-making is captured. *Let selection among carriers be payoff-monotone, with other advantages equal and a race carrier present.*
1. *While effective liability is below the private advantage, $\tau_{\rm eff}<\nu$, a carrier's unilateral contribution to the safeguards, lower load or correction keyed to no one, lowers its payoff relative to otherwise identical carriers and is selected against.*
2. *Let no single rule-maker reach every carrier whose combined load satisfies (reach), and let the $n_s$ rule-makers be in a contest of standing in which imposing the liability on the carriers one reaches is restraint in the commons of {{wow}}, worth $\Upsilon_s$ to the field per unit of its private cost. If each weighs winning by $\lambda_s>(\Upsilon_s-n_s)/(n_s-1)$, no rule-maker imposes the liability alone in equilibrium.*
3. *Where captured rule-making leaves effective liability $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, load has a negative private payoff, and selection lowers it, only once $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})>\nu$: enacting $\tau>\nu$ is not enough.*

*Proof:* S9.

**Which rows need more than one party.** R2 does by definition, and so does the shared measurement of R6; R1 does by parts (1) and (2), with part (3) tying it to R3. R3 is a second-order public good, which competitors do not supply ({{noinside}}). Of the means of R4, custody needs more than one party, not building binds only the one that refrains, and detection faster than breakout needs access to the conduct of others. Under (shared failure) each actor's survival turns on the uncovered share of all, so a pace kept by one leaves R5 unmet for every actor ({{coverage}}). Under $(A_\tau)$ an admission constraint adopted by one developer is selected against ({{nested}}(3)). And where the contest is decided by strength, capacity kept in practice costs strength, so R9 kept by one is kept at a loss ({{lockin}}). Where the mechanisms are active, a minimum of cooperation is therefore necessary: commitments binding every capable actor, with a layer of correction that no competitor controls [thm∣prem]. Whether an agreement among rule-makers is itself an equilibrium is governed by {{assure}}; whether the present branch is one on which the mechanisms are active is Part II's diagnosis; and how much cooperation is warranted now is the judgment of Section 12 [val].

**Coverage can unravel.** A common constraint protects only while it covers every capable actor, and coverage is itself an equilibrium that can tip. A participant that leaves becomes a carrier the constraint does not reach, whose share can grow (R2), and against it the constraint borne by those who remain is a cost borne by some, which relative selection removes ({{blind}}(2)). Where what deters leaving falls as more stand outside, because sanctions are funded by participants, access to shared provision is worth less, or rule-making is captured by those outside, staying and leaving are both stable, separated by a tipping share, as compliance and evasion are in {{blind}}(3) ({{unravel}}) [thm∣prem]. One large exit can carry the outside share past it, and the exits that follow need no further shock: beyond that share, standing outside is what selection favours (compare minimum participation in Barrett 2003). An arrangement therefore states its participation threshold, type by type, and the conditions of access that keep the cost of standing outside above the cost of staying however many stand outside. A design that cannot state its threshold has not shown that it can last.

Section 10 takes the table function by function: what form each row needs, and how an order that meets the rows persists through what all share rather than through its winners.
