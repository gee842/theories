# Part I. Mechanism: what the conditions of winning do

Part I states the mechanism and proves its results in specified models, each with its premises inside its statement; whether the present trajectory meets their conditions and shows their signatures is the question of Part II.

## 2. The conditions of winning and their reflexive closure

> **Claim.** Three conditions together produce a selection pressure of winning: selection by relative success; winnings that convert into the capacity to win again; and capacity that converts decisively into winning [def]. Near equal standing, with the prize and the actors' conduct fixed, they combine into one structural loop gain, and where it exceeds one, leads compound [thm]. Where success can also buy control over the selection itself, the pressure closes on itself: the meta-attractor of winning [def].
>
> **Why.** The conditions multiply: remove any one and the gain is zero [thm]. Sharing and diffusion lower the gain in every channel; provision not won in the contest lowers it where decisiveness rises less than in proportion to the stock, as with capacity; correction lowers it wherever decisiveness rises fast enough with the stock, in particular wherever it rises at least in proportion, as with rule support, and not where it only speeds a decay common to all, which a contest of ratios does not see [thm]. Where rivals respond, the same form holds with an equilibrium decisiveness [thm], and in the contests computed where a lead discourages the follower, leads compound below a structural gain of one [num].

### 2.1 Success is not contribution

Every selection process favours something. It need not favour the continued functioning of the system in which selection occurs.

Consider two organizations that depend on the same shared infrastructure. One spends resources maintaining it; the other redirects them into expansion while using what others maintain. Neglect becomes a competitive advantage when maintenance costs are immediate, deterioration is delayed, and those harmed cannot make the expanding organization bear the consequences. The neglectful organization need not be more capable in general, only at securing the resources that decide survival and expansion; if those resources let its practices spread, the system selects against some of the behaviour that keeps it functioning. **Success within a selection process is distinct from contribution to the conditions that support it.**

Selection on one dimension does not select the best. It removes the balanced. The step is analytic: an agent that spends nothing on what the measure does not count, including the costs it imposes on others and on the future, has more to spend on what the measure counts, and so outscores on it an agent of equal means with broader concerns. The author put the premise in one line: "The agent that only optimiz[es] capital value, and discard[s] all other values, including externalities, will have the highest capital efficiency" [corpus: meta-attractor]. What remains after many rounds is what the filter lets through, not a sample of human nature; how far real populations have been filtered in this way is an empirical question for Part II.

Nothing here requires that people be or become selfish. A population of practices changes because some practices expand, are imitated or replace others, while many individuals keep their values; the question is not only what people believe is good but which practices receive the means to continue. The primitive is selection, not greed.

### 2.2 Three interconnected conditions

**{{!Definition:cond}} (The selection pressure of winning) [def].** *Carriers* are whatever selection acts on: reproducible units that can gain or lose share, and that carry a way of succeeding from one round to the next. Cell lineages, practices, firms, research programmes, members of a federation, states, doctrines and trained AI systems are carriers. A system of carriers is under a *selection pressure of winning* when three conditions hold together, each to a degree.
1. **Relative selection.** What is won is allocated by success relative to the others; a share $\beta$ of it, possibly zero, is divided equally whatever the outcome.
2. **Convertibility.** Part of what is won converts into stocks that raise future winning: capacity, talent, data, standing, rule support. Stocks also come from sources not won in the contest. This *provision not won in the contest* is what the prose calls *provision beneath the game*.
3. **Decisiveness.** Stocks convert into winning by an increasing success function; $r$ measures how decisively.

When among the stocks that success can buy is control over the selection itself (the rules, the evaluators and correctors, the formation of the minds that will hold positions, the language in which success is described), the pressure *closes on itself* (Section 2.3). The pressure needs no intention and no conspiracy; it acts on whatever is reproducible and can be won.

The conditions state formally a loop the author put in one sentence: "if you use what you have to compete for more and what you win is yours, and you use that to keep compet[ing], we will end up racing faster and faster and trade everything for winning" [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. *What you win is yours* is relative selection with nothing shared; *use what you have to compete for more* is convertibility; *keep compet[ing]* is reinvestment that never reaches a point of enough; and *trade everything for winning* is the filter of Section 2.1.

**{{!Proposition:loopgain}} (One loop gain) [thm].** The conditions multiply into a single structural loop gain: equal standing breaks when it exceeds one; discounted, it prices standing while the discounted loop is stable; and when rivals respond, the same form holds with an equilibrium decisiveness.

*Model.* $n$ carriers contest a prize $\Omega$ of fixed size each period. A share $\beta$ is divided equally; the rest goes to carrier $i$ with probability or share $p_i=e^{\sigma_i}/\sum_ke^{\sigma_k}$, where strength $\sigma_i$ increases in $i$'s stocks $X_i^j$ and, except in (4), depends on nothing else. Stock $j$ receives a share $\kappa_j$ of what is won, an inflow $k_{0j}\ge0$ not won in the contest, decays or is corrected at rate $d_j>0$, and diffuses toward the mean at rate $\delta_{f,j}$, with $d_j+\delta_{f,j}\le1$:
$${{!eq:stocks}}\qquad X_i^{j\prime}=(1-d_j)X_i^j+\kappa_j\Omega\big[(1-\beta)p_i+\beta/n\big]+k_{0j}-\delta_{f,j}\big(X_i^j-\bar X^j\big).$$
At equal standing write $r_j=\partial\sigma/\partial\ln X^j\ge0$ for the structural decisiveness of stock $j$, and $\omega_j=\kappa_j\Omega/(\kappa_j\Omega+nk_{0j})$ for the share of stock $j$ that has to be won, set by the stakes against provision not won in the contest. Where $p_i$ is a share, {{eq:stocks}} is deterministic; where it is a probability (a lottery), {{eq:stocks}} gives the expected next stocks, equal standing is not a fixed point of the random process, and the stability of (1) is that of the first-order dynamics of the expected deviation. The *structural loop gain* is
$${{!eq:gamma}}\qquad \Gamma=(1-\beta)\sum_j r_j\,\omega_j\,\frac{d_j}{d_j+\delta_{f,j}}.$$
1. *Equal standing is locally asymptotically stable if $\Gamma<1$ and unstable if $\Gamma>1$. The channels add because every stock is fed by the same winnings through one win share: compounding through capacity and the reflexive closure through rule support are terms of one sum, and channels each too weak to open the loop can open it together.*
2. *$\Gamma=0$ if any condition is removed: all of the prize divided equally ($\beta=1$), nothing convertible ($\kappa_j=0$ for all $j$), or no decisiveness ($r_j=0$ for all $j$).*
3. *(The price of standing.) Hold the prize and the others' conduct fixed, and let an actor discount at $\delta$ and reinvest its net payoff at the rates $\kappa_j$ at which winnings convert. Write*
$$\Gamma_\delta=(1-\beta)\sum_jr_j\,\omega_j\,\frac{\delta d_j}{1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})},$$
*which is below $\Gamma$ and tends to it as $\delta\to1$. If $0\le\Gamma_\delta<1$, a unit of relative net payoff today is worth, to first order, $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ units of the actor's own future winnings, the closed-loop gain of the discounted loop, and this price grows without bound as $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$. If $\Gamma_\delta\ge1$, no first-order price exists: the value of standing is continuous at equal standing with infinite slope.*
4. *(Rivals who respond.) Let strength depend also on an intensity each carrier chooses, and let carriers follow a symmetric Markov equilibrium that is differentiable at equal standing and in which each carrier's equilibrium win share $p^{\rm eq}_i$ rises with each of its own stocks. At equal standing take a relative shift $y^j$ of stock $j$: the actor's up by $y^j$, each other carrier's down by $y^j/(n-1)$. Write $r^{\rm eq}_j=nX^{j\ast}\,dp^{\rm eq}_i/dy^j$, the decisiveness read off the equilibrium win share with every intensity responding, which equals $\frac{n^2}{n-1}\,\partial p^{\rm eq}_i/\partial\ln X^j_i$; and $r^{\rm pr}_j$ for the same derivative along the same shift with the actor's own intensity held and its rivals' at their Markov policies. Then (1) and (2) hold with $r^{\rm eq}_j$ in place of $r_j$, which defines the equilibrium gain $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$; and, where $p_i$ is a share, (3) holds with $r^{\rm pr}_j$ in place of $r_j$, which defines $\Gamma^{\rm pr}_\delta$, plus, where intensity imposes a shared loss, the value of the change in that loss that the rivals' responses bring. Here $r^{\rm pr}_j$ is taken along the relative shift itself, not along a move of the actor's stock alone, since with the actor's intensity held the two differ wherever the rivals' policies depend on the level of the stocks.*

*Scope.* Local, at equal standing; {{>standprice}} lets the prize grow. The channels must reinforce and be fed through one win share: a countervailing channel is not a negative term of $\Gamma$, and one that answers with delay can oscillate with growing amplitude while the net gain is below one ({{delayosc}}); a stock fed by a separate decision, such as lobbying bought from profits, is not a term of the sum. Part (4) keeps the form and changes the number ({{eqgain}}). In the two-actor contests computed, where a lead discourages the follower, $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$ exceeds one already at a structural gain of 0.45, so equal standing breaks although $\Gamma<1$ [num: MP]; where a laggard tries harder, the strategic correction shrinks and in one case reverses, and in many small rounds a winner-take-all equilibrium coexists with the smooth one that (4) describes [num: MP; both held for outside review]. The structural $\Gamma$ is therefore a benchmark, not a bound in either direction. Where $p_i$ is a probability, the price in (3) and (4) is that of the first-order dynamics of the expected deviation; the value of a win in the lottery itself, from its Bellman equation over finite jumps, is a different object, which coincides with it in the linear case and with small jumps and otherwise only approximately, and once rivals respond strongly it is computed, not given by (4) ({{eqgain}}). *Proof:* S2 [num: LG, PD, MP].

The single-channel case gives the compounding condition in closed form, and with it the formal content of provision beneath the game.

**{{!Proposition:compound}} (When winnings compound, and what enough requires) [thm].** *Let capacity $K_i$ receive a share $\kappa_c$ of what $i$ wins, an inflow $k_0$ not won in the contest, depreciation at rate $d_K$ and diffusion toward the mean at rate $\delta_f$, with $p_i=K_i^r/\sum_jK_j^r$ and a prize of fixed size. Let $K^\ast$ and $S^\ast=nK^\ast$ be individual and total capacity at equal standing, $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$ the share of capacity that has to be won, and $\Gamma_0=(1-\beta)r\omega$ the structural gain without diffusion.*
1. *Equal standing is locally stable if $\kappa_c\Omega\big((1-\beta)r-1\big)<nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$ and unstable if the inequality is reversed. Without provision and diffusion the condition for stability is $(1-\beta)r<1$, and $\kappa_c$ sets only the level of capacity. With provision, a smaller convertible share stabilizes equal standing.*
2. *A point of enough. Let every carrier but one stop reinvesting at a cap $\bar K\le K^\ast$ and stay there, and let the remaining one, a laggard, start below the cap. Its net inflow is*
$$\phi(K)=\kappa_c\Omega\Big[(1-\beta)\frac{K^r}{K^r+(n-1)\bar K^r}+\frac\beta n\Big]+k_0-d_KK+\delta_f\,\frac{n-1}n\,(\bar K-K).$$
   *(i) If $\bar K<K^\ast$, a laggard just below the cap returns to it. If $\bar K=K^\ast$, it returns if $\Gamma_0(n-1)/n<1+\delta_f(n-1)/(nd_K)$ and falls away if the inequality is reversed; without diffusion the condition for return is $\Gamma<n/(n-1)$.*
   *(ii) The laggard returns from every start if and only if $\phi>0$ on $(0,\bar K)$. When $r>1$, $\phi(0^+)=\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0+\delta_f(n-1)\bar K/n$, so return from every start needs an inflow not tied to rank: sharing, provision or diffusion. Without any of the three, laggards below a threshold are excluded. Such an inflow is necessary, not sufficient; $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0\ge d_K\bar K$ suffices.*

*Scope.* The prize is fixed; S2 gives the condition of (1) date by date when capacity grows under it without depreciation. If the prize instead grows in proportion to total capacity, $\Omega=gS$, with no provision, equal shares are unstable iff $\kappa_cg\big((1-\beta)r-1\big)>\delta_f$, and without diffusion iff $(1-\beta)r>1$, which is also where looking ahead narrows the working objective ({{>standprice}}). At equality in (1) the proposition is silent. That the others stay at the cap, a premise of (2), holds whatever the laggard does when $\bar K\le(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)/(nd_K+\delta_f)$; above that bound, as at $\bar K=K^\ast$ with diffusion, (2)(i) still holds to first order with every carrier moving, except on the knife edge $\delta_f=d_K\Gamma_0$, where the first order is silent [thm, local]; simulations in which every carrier moves agree in ten named cases and in 400 random settings [num: CE]. *Proof:* S2 [num: CE, SP].

Each clause of the author's sentence can therefore be removed, each with its condition: share what is won until $(1-\beta)r<1$; make winnings non-convertible, which works only together with provision not won in the contest; or stop at a point of enough, which holds from every start only with an inflow not tied to rank. The second is the author's rule of game design: "a winner of the game should be rewarded with anything other than more advantages of the game, otherwise the game will eventually converge and winner will take all. Then the game collapses" [corpus: possibility-space]. **Provision beneath the game is not only support for those who fall behind. It is what makes non-convertible rewards work and, with sharing and diffusion, what makes a point of enough hold.** The phrase carries three senses, and the formal results concern the first. (a) *Capability provided to contestants*, the inflow $k_0$ into their decisive stocks, such as a common layer of compute, open evaluation infrastructure and public technical knowledge: it lowers the won share $\omega$, and with it the gain [thm]. (b) *Provision to persons*, the conditions of a decent life: it acts through $\omega$ only where it enters a contestant's decisive stock, as equal provision raises the capacity of a state when the contestants are states, and otherwise through the drive to win (crux C8) [conj] or through the stakes. (c) *Essential services from many providers*: they act through caps on dependence and funded standby (X8, under D5). The author's image for it is trees: "Trees are providers" [corpus: shengsheng]. Diffusion, the third inflow, is a source, not a guarantee: alone it can leave a laggard stalled short of the cap [num: CE]. Who stops at a cap is a question of objectives, not of the loop ({{>enough}}).

**Which levers lower the gain.** The levers act channel by channel, according to how decisiveness responds to the level of a stock ({{chansign}}) [thm]. In a ratio channel, such as capacity in a contest of shares, only relative size counts: provision lowers the gain, and a faster decay common to all never does, since a contest of ratios does not see it, the first instance of what {{>blind}} says of every common term; it raises the discounted gain instead, since a stock that turns over faster is made more of recent winnings. In a level channel, such as rule support that offsets a cost, decisiveness rises in proportion to the stock: correction lowers the gain, and provision, which adds the same to everyone, changes nothing. Between the two, correction lowers the gain wherever decisiveness rises fast enough with the stock, a threshold below proportion that {{chansign}}(2) states exactly, and provision wherever it rises less than in proportion. Sharing and diffusion lower the gain in every channel, so measures that stop diffusion in order to keep a lead raise the gain they mean to exploit [thm]; what should diffuse, and what should not, is a question for Section 10. Trees for capacity, correction for rule support, sharing and openness for all: this is "change what winning confers", made exact. In these terms *what winning confers* is the share divided equally ($\beta$), how far what is won converts into stocks against what is provided without being won ($\kappa$ against $k_0$), diffusion, how far success converts into control over the selection ($\chi$, Section 4), and whether the prize is positional (Section 10.2). Lowering decisiveness $r$, how capacity converts into winning, is a parallel lever on the same game, not part of what winning confers; a *change of the game* means either.

**What the loop gain does not decide.** $\Gamma$ decides whether a relative gap grows, not how fast: the speed is the Perron root $\Lambda^\ast$ of the linearized loop, $1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$ for one channel, so the same gain on a slowly turning stock opens leads slowly ({{tdec}}). Two further conditions decide what the loop does to those who live with it, and $\Gamma$ decides neither: whether a lead can become decisive faster than detection and response, a position beyond any sanction, which the loop reaches only through the time a gap needs to become decisive (Section 5.2); and whether capability grows faster than the correction that can contest it (Section 5.3). $\Gamma$ is not estimated for any channel; {{gammarange}} shows at illustrative values what $\Gamma>1$ asks of each parameter, and Section 7.4 registers what can be observed in its place. The pressure is therefore not one quantity but a set of interconnected conditions with one structural loop gain at its core ({{>thresholds}}).

### 2.3 The reflexive closure: the meta-attractor

An ordinary adaptive system improves its state $x$ against a fixed rule $\mathcal S$ that scores states. Where the rule is endogenous, because what can be won includes control over the selection itself, the outcome of the last round of scoring decides who writes the next round's scorecard.

Consider a research community that allocates funding and positions by publication counts, because reliable knowledge is hard to evaluate directly. Practices that publish more receive more funding and students and are more widely copied. Add one step: their practitioners come to hold the positions that decide recruitment, training and evaluation. The practices the metric favours then shape the institutions that choose and interpret it, and newcomers must adopt them to do research at all. The decisive transition is from *being selected by a rule* to *helping reproduce the rule that selects you*, and it needs no manipulation: ordinary decisions about whom to hire, what to teach and which evidence to collect suffice.

The loop need not preserve its winners. An organization can fail while its operating logic passes to its successors; a leader can be replaced by an opponent who finds that holding the position requires the same practices; participants can reject the system's consequences while reproducing the conduct their participation depends on. **The persistent entity can be a way of succeeding**, which is why {{cond}} calls what is selected a carrier, and why intentions alone cannot account for recurrence.

**{{!Definition:meta}} (Meta-attractor) [def].** Let the state update be $x'=\mathcal F(x,\mathcal S)$ and the rule update $\mathcal S'=\mathcal G(x,\mathcal S)$. Three objects are distinguished, and each claim in this paper says which it concerns.
1. **The mechanism: reflexive selection.** A positive loop runs through the rule: the rule moves resources toward some states, and the states so resourced move the rule further in their favour. In {{loopgain}} it is every channel whose stock is control over selection.
2. **An attracting configuration in a specified model:** a compact invariant set of the joint flow, asymptotically stable, with a basin that is a proper subset of the space. A positive loop is necessary for multistability (Thomas's rule; Soulé 2003), not sufficient. The field model of Section 4 exhibits such configurations.
3. **A long transient:** trajectories stay near a region for a long time relative to one round of selection, without the region being attracting.

A *meta-attractor* is the mechanism of (1). Wherever this paper calls it attracting, the claim is (2), in the model where it has been shown; a claim of (2) about the world is a conjecture.

**The meta-attractor of winning** is the case in which the pressure of {{cond}} is the rule: success under relative selection converts into control over the selection that judges the next round. In conditional form, *recursive selection creates second-order selection for control over future selection when the resulting benefits are sufficiently appropriable, persistent and large relative to the costs of changing and defending the rules.* Where (2) holds, long-run behaviour maximizes the rule whatever purpose it was introduced to serve (Goodhart 1975; Campbell 1979), and by the time a measure has stopped measuring, the people and institutions it elevated decide whether it is replaced.

The smallest model of the mechanism is a pair: a carrier's success and its favourable control over the rules, each decaying at a rate in $(0,1]$ and feeding the other with a non-negative coefficient, are stable exactly when the product of the two coefficients is below the product of the two rates ({{bench}}) [thm]. This is Richardson's arms-race condition with control over rules in place of armaments (Richardson 1960), of the same form as {{loopgain}} but not a case of it, and each coefficient names a channel that can be measured.

The pattern is not fated. Positive feedback can saturate, oscillate, split or break; energetic ceilings, heterogeneity, delays, constraints at other scales and reflexive awareness cap it. The corpus calls it "more like a hurricane than a black hole" [corpus: meta-attractor]: a structure sustained by a flow, which decays when the flow is cut.

*Why "meta-attractor".* Beside the "generator functions" of catastrophic risk, which name the same underlying forces as preconditions, the name adds the reflexive step in which selection rewrites its own criterion, the persistence that step confers, the layer of minds and language through which it runs, and a generative counterpart.

**{{!Remark:pathgen}} (Pathological and generative meta-attractors).** Reflexive selection is not itself the problem: science, the rule of law and open-source ecosystems also persist through the conditions they maintain. A meta-attractor is *pathological* to the degree that its reproduction depletes shared conditions that selection does not price, weakens the channels through which such costs register and trigger correction, or makes safe alternatives unreachable within the relevant time, budget and authority. It is *generative* to the degree that its reproduction strengthens independent correction and keeps alternatives reachable: success funds correctors it cannot control, and what is rewarded is contribution to the shared conditions. That order is not selected among carriers, since correction keyed to no one is selected against among otherwise identical carriers ({{>noinside}}); it has to be built and held by the whole, through coverage and formula funding (Section 10.4). The way out is therefore not the abolition of selection but a change in what is selected for. The corpus calls the passage "change the basin, then add speed" [corpus: meta-attractor].

### 2.4 Not bound to any substrate

The pressure needs only relative selection, convertible winnings and decisiveness; its closure needs only that the successful can reshape their selection. A cancer lineage evades the signals that limit proliferation and the immune response that would remove it (Hanahan and Weinberg 2011; Aktipis et al. 2015); markets with increasing returns lock in early leads (Arthur 1989), and their winners can buy the rules (Stigler 1971); in security competition a gain for one is read as a threat to another; in science, metrics allocate the positions that then set the metrics; and a policy in training is selected by a reward its developer sets, the developer by a contest. Nothing in {{cond}} refers to a culture, a political system or a species: a civilization on another planet would face the same pressure wherever the three conditions hold. Discussion X2 gives the instances in full, and X1 the lineage of the ideas.

What is specific to AI is not the pressure but how far, in either direction, AI moves its parameters. Where it raises decisiveness, it raises the gain; where it speeds the turnover of stocks, a gain above one opens leads faster ({{tdec}}); where it lowers the cost of control faster than the cost of independent correction, it strengthens the reflexive channels (Section 4); where it raises the growth of hazardous behaviour faster than contestable coverage, it moves the second condition the loop gain does not decide (Section 5.3); and where it is shared, diffused or provided beneath the game, it lowers the gain in the channels named above. Trained AI systems are carriers, and under the conditions of Section 6.4 the pressure and its closure run inside them.

A structural loop gain says when leads compound, not what those inside the loop can see; Section 3 asks what relative selection makes invisible, and what the discounted gain does to the objective of whoever looks ahead.
