## 9. What any adequate arrangement must do

> **Claim.** What every adequate arrangement must do can be stated before designs are compared: each requirement with its kind of support, necessary in a named model over a named horizon, sufficient, or normative, and with the conditions under which it does not bind [def]. Where (reach) holds with a race carrier present, and no single rule-maker reaches every carrier whose load can carry the host below its floor, several requirements cannot be met by any actor alone, and competition among actors does not supply them [thm].
>
> **Why.** A requirement stated with its model and its exceptions can be checked against any design, regulated competition included, which can then be found to meet it [def]. A constraint that one carrier adopts alone is a cost borne by it alone, which relative selection removes while effective liability is below the private advantage; a rule-maker that does not reach every such carrier leaves the host to those it does not reach; and rule-makers in a contest of standing, weighing it above a stated level, do not impose a liability alone [thm]. Every sanction, observation and count of coverage that the requirements call for is a corrector's act [def]: where success buys the rule-makers, an enacted liability binds only in the part of rule-making not bought [thm], and the other requirements weaken through the interfaces that {{thresholds}} marks [thm∣prem].

Part I proved its results one model at a time, and {{thresholds}} reads them as thresholds, each with a side on which a protected outcome is lost. Read in reverse, such a result is a requirement: an arrangement that does not keep the field on the safe side loses the outcome, in that model and over that horizon. The kind of support decides how a row may be used. A necessary row is a test: a design that lacks it fails in the row's model within its horizon. A sufficient row is a route, not a test. A normative row rests on a value of Section 1.3 and is declared, not derived. The means by which a design meets a row are not rows; they are the subject of Section 10. Read together, the rows state with their models the third job of "we are one" (Section 3.7), a selection that counts the system. S9 derives each row, fixes its scope with counterexamples, and maps the rows onto the paper's other lists ({{crosswalk}}).

**Reading the rows.** Two senses of coverage are kept apart: R2's is of actors, the reach of the common constraints; R5's is of hazard modes, the share of hazardous variety under correction that meets the four conditions of Section 5.3. R8 is split because its clauses do different work (Section 3.2): the sanction keeps a constraint, and equal incidence keeps it from becoming a weapon against challengers, the second direction of capture ({{capture}}). R9 is the row for O2: the capacity to take decisions back survives delegation only where it is kept in practice, and keeping it costs strength where the contest is decided by strength ({{lockin}}).

**The row the others rest on.** Every row but R7 rests on the work of correctors [def]. A liability binds at $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, what captured rule-making leaves of it (Section 4.1); the sanctions that bound a breakout and keep an admission constraint are applied by correctors, and are themselves subject to capture ({{nested}}(3)); detection, and the verification that capacity is kept in practice, are theirs; and assessment that success has captured does not count toward coverage. A design that meets the other rows with correctors that success can buy therefore meets R1 on paper only, since effective liability falls to zero as rule-making is captured [thm], and loses the others through the interfaces that {{thresholds}} marks † [thm∣prem]. R3, correction that success cannot buy, is the row on which the table stands: the closure of {{meta}}, seen from the side of what must be done.

**What the table does not list.** Sharing, provision beneath the game and reward that does not convert into control are not rows, because the models give them as alternatives, not necessities: equal standing is stable under sharing, lower decisiveness, provision not won in the contest or diffusion, any one of which can carry the margin ({{compound}}) [thm]. What sets them apart is that each meets several rows at once. Provision that grows with the stakes holds the won share still, and with it both sides of R4 ({{scissors}}); reward that does not convert into control, $\chi=0$, brings R3a's requirement down to a level without second-order selection ({{corrrate}}(1)) [thm]. They are the levers of Section 10.2, and two of the generative marks of Section 8.2 name them.

**Where admission does not reach.** Admission reaches only what it admits, and three things lie beyond it. None makes R8a unnecessary, which is why they stand here and not in the last column; a design meets each by other means. Systems that automate a developer's own research are used first inside it, where a condition imposed at release cannot see them (Section 6.4); R8a is therefore stated for consequential deployment, internal deployment included. Openly released weights can be fine-tuned around any constraint (Section 6.4), so for an openly released class the decision to release is the admission decision for every later use, and what remains afterwards is a liability on use where harm can be attributed, the route of R1. And a masked divergence shows under evaluation only as far as the system's behaviour lets it, so admission conditioned on evaluated behaviour keeps it out only while the detection it allows, times the sanction, exceeds the tax ({{masked}}). For masked classes R8a is necessary and not sufficient: admission is stated in capability, up to the level at which control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation remain safe, and beyond it the class falls under R5, stopped (E2) or held to the pace at which control covers it (E3). Whether admission can bound masking at the admitted capability is crux C12.

**Sufficient safeguards, and form.** Four safeguards are sufficient and not necessary. Full inbound row sums below one certify each corrector domain against every state of the others ({{certificate}}(2)); they say nothing about whether the correctors' errors are independent ({{topology}}). The safeguards (I1)–(I4) of Section 10.3 give viability once in force, with a liability margin set by the host, the floor, the initial composition and the speed of selection; on a host that degrades by itself below a threshold, a shortfall in that margin is permanent ({{>safeguards}}). Not building the decisive class, holding it in common custody, and detecting and answering faster than breakout meet R4 by changing the premise of {{enforce}} rather than its terms ({{restraint}}(a)). And verification whose expected sanction is at least the net gain from exit deters irreversible exit from an agreement whose overhang grows ({{overhang}}). The requirements constrain functions, not institutional form: one institution can meet them, and so can mutually recognized institutions of several jurisdictions, a federation or a polycentric complex [def]. Regulated competition meets the rows its rules carry, where those rules reach every capable actor and its correctors are separated from success [def]; what a single rule-maker cannot do is the next result.

**{{!Proposition:nounilateral}} (No unilateral solution) [thm].** Correction and coverage are not supplied by any one carrier, or by any one rule-maker in a contest of standing, and enacting a liability is not enough while rule-making is captured. *Let selection among carriers be payoff-monotone, with other advantages equal and a race carrier present.*
1. *While effective liability is below the private advantage, $\tau_{\rm eff}<\nu$, a carrier's unilateral contribution to the safeguards, whether lower load, correction keyed to no one or the cost of verification, lowers its payoff relative to otherwise identical carriers and is selected against.*
2. *Let no single rule-maker reach every carrier whose combined load satisfies (reach). Then no rule-maker alone meets R1 or R2, whatever it imposes. Let the $n_s$ rule-makers be in a contest of standing in which imposing the liability on the carriers one reaches is restraint in the commons of {{wow}}, worth $\Upsilon_s>1$ to the field per unit of its private cost. If each weighs winning by $\lambda_s>(\Upsilon_s-n_s)/(n_s-1)$, no rule-maker imposes the liability alone in equilibrium; whether they impose it jointly is governed by {{assure}}.*
3. *Where captured rule-making leaves effective liability $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, load has a negative private payoff, and selection lowers it, only once $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})>\nu$: enacting $\tau>\nu$ is not enough while $x_{\rm rule}\ge1-\nu/\tau$.*

*Proof:* S9.

**Which rows need more than one party.** R2 does by definition, and so does the shared measurement of R6. R1 does by parts (1) and (2), and part (3) ties it to R3, a second-order public good that competitors do not supply ({{noinside}}). Of the means of R4, custody needs more than one party, not building binds only the one that refrains, and detection faster than breakout needs access to the conduct of others. Under (shared failure) each actor's survival turns on the uncovered share of all, so a pace kept by one leaves R5 unmet for every actor ({{coverage}}). Under $(A_\tau)$ an admission constraint adopted by one developer is selected against ({{nested}}(3)). And where the contest is decided by strength, capacity kept in practice costs strength, so R9 kept by one is kept at a loss ({{lockin}}). Where the mechanisms are active, a minimum of cooperation is therefore necessary: binding commitments covering every capable actor, with a layer of correction that no competitor controls [thm∣prem]. This is a result conditional on its models, combined with evidence and a stated standard, not a proof from first principles. Whether the present branch is one on which the mechanisms are active is Part II's diagnosis, and how much cooperation is warranted now is the judgment of Section 12 [val].

**Coverage can unravel.** A common constraint protects only while it covers every capable actor, and coverage is itself an equilibrium that can tip. A participant that leaves becomes a carrier the constraint does not reach (R2), and against it the constraint borne by those who remain is a cost borne by some, which relative selection removes ({{blind}}(2)). Where the cost of standing outside falls as more stand outside, because sanctions are funded by participants, shared provision is worth less with fewer inside, or rule-making is captured by those outside, staying and leaving are both stable, separated by a tipping share, as compliance and evasion are in {{blind}}(3) ({{unravel}}) [thm∣prem]. One large exit can carry the outside share past it, and the exits that follow need no further shock (compare minimum participation in Barrett 2003). A readiness rule that demands every dimension at once under noisy measurement errs toward holding a pause longer than its evidence warrants, which raises the cost of staying (Section 11.3) [conj]. Coverage can also fall without an exit, when the largest participant is exempted (EV18, read in X10) [obs], so the threshold is stated in coverage weighted by share, not in numbers of parties. An arrangement therefore states its participation threshold, type by type, and the conditions of access that keep the cost of standing outside above the cost of staying however many stand outside. A design that cannot state its threshold has not shown that it can last.

Section 10 takes the table function by function: what form each row needs, and how an order that meets the rows persists through what all share rather than through its winners.
