## 3. One system: what relative selection cannot see

> **Claim.** All carriers share one host, and relative selection cannot see what all share: neither the host's decline nor a constraint that costs all alike [thm]. Where coupling between actors is real, a held self-model that draws its boundary inside the system is an error toward what can be divided, and selection favours the narrowest [thm]. Near equal standing and to first order, wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, a forward-looking actor weighs standing above what all share: with a fixed prize always, and with a prize proportional to capacity, without provision or diffusion, exactly where leads compound [thm∣prem]. Each actor computes correctly, and all are worse off for it: the game is wrong, and only a change in what winning confers corrects it [thm∣prem]. However carriers are grouped, selection does not favour what benefits all, so against failures that are global and irreversible integration must be anticipated, and understanding is how actors come to count what selection cannot see [thm].
>
> **Why.** A term common to every payoff changes no comparison between carriers. A unit of relative advantage reinvested buys a larger share of a future that a common gain only enlarges for everyone. At the scale of one host there is no rival host to select among, and no second attempt after a failure that cannot be undone.

### 3.1 Levels, measures and the host

Persons, firms, governments and evaluators have different interests: a citizen's continued agency is not a firm's market share or a government's tenure. Two levels are kept apart: *selection*, in which carriers change share and nobody chooses the outcome, and *strategy*, in which actors act on objectives. So are four measures: the *objective* $J_i$ an actor acts on, its *material payoff* $\pi_i$, its *true welfare* $W_i$, which counts the coupling between actors, and *survival* $\Phi_i$, a stated combination of O1 and O2. A result about selection transfers to strategic actors only if their objective is shown to be the selected one. A condition $V\in[0,1]$ is shared by every actor (secure infrastructure, biosecurity, cyber stability, human control over AI systems, a reliable information environment); it regenerates, and is loaded by the field's activity $E$:
$${{!eq:host}}\qquad \dot V=V\big[\rho(1-V)-h_VE\big],\qquad \rho,h_V>0.$$
The *host* is the body every carrier lives in (母体), in no parasitic sense: the part of the shared conditions whose costs do not return to their source through customers, reputation, courts or the source's own exposure.

**{{!Definition:viab}} (Viability) [def].** A trajectory is *viable* if $V(t)\ge V_{\min}$ for all $t\ge0$, where the floor $V_{\min}\in(0,1)$ is a normative line, not an absorbing state.

Every actor persists only while the host does, so viability constrains every trajectory rather than entering as one payoff among others (Aubin 1991). This host is forgiving: per unit it regenerates fastest when most depleted, and it has no threshold of its own. Results that a trajectory *crosses* the floor therefore hold a fortiori for hosts that regenerate less at every level; results that it *stays* viable do not, and need a margin (S3).

### 3.2 Relative selection is blind to what all share

Carriers $k=1,\dots,n$ hold shares $p_k$ of the field's activity and run a fraction $e_k\in[0,\bar e]$ of it in ways that load the host, such as deploying ahead of evaluation, each unit bringing a private advantage $\nu>0$. The field's load is $E=\sum_kp_ke_k$, and carrier $k$'s material payoff is
$${{!eq:payoff}}\qquad \pi_k=\phi_k+(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})e_k+G(V),$$
with $\phi_k$ everything else, $G$ nondecreasing, and $\tau_{\rm eff}\ge0$ the effective liability correctors impose. With $\tau_{\rm eff}=0$, $e_k=\bar e$ is the *race strategy*.

**{{!Definition:relsel}} (Relative selection) [def].** A selection rule $\dot p=\mathcal F(p,\pi)$ is *relative* if $\mathcal F(p,\pi+\xi\mathbf 1)=\mathcal F(p,\pi)$ for every constant $\xi$, and *payoff-monotone* if $\dot p_k/p_k-\dot p_l/p_l$ has the sign of $\pi_k-\pi_l$. Replicator dynamics, imitation and market-share competition driven by relative profitability are both (Alchian 1950).

**{{!Lemma:blind}} (Blindness, and what a common rule needs) [thm].** A term common to all is invisible to relative selection. A constraint is neutral among those who comply if it costs them alike, and compliance survives wherever evasion pays no type that can evade.
1. *Under a relative rule the trajectory of shares is unchanged by any term common to all payoffs, in particular by $G(V(t))$ whatever the path of $V$. Under a payoff-monotone rule a common term never changes which of two carriers grows relative to the other.*
2. *Incidence. A constraint that costs every complier the same additive amount is a common term among them and leaves their relative growth unchanged: equal incidence makes a constraint neutral among those who comply, and does nothing more. One whose cost $c_k$ differs across carriers is selected on like any other payoff difference, and re-sorts the compliers toward those it costs least; one borne by a single carrier is selected against.*
3. *Evasion. If carriers of type $k$ can evade a constraint costing them $c_k$ against an expected sanction $\bar F_k$, the evading variant of type $k$ grows relative to its complying variant iff $c_k>\bar F_k$. Under cumulative payoff-monotone selection, compliance therefore survives if $\bar F_k\ge c_k$ for every type that can evade, whatever the incidence, and its share tends to one if every such inequality is strict. If, for one type, the sanction falls as evaders spread, because detection is funded by compliers or rule-making is captured by evaders, with $\bar F(x)$ continuous and strictly decreasing in the evading share $x$, then compliance and evasion are both stable, separated by the tipping share $x^\dagger$ at which $\bar F(x^\dagger)=c$, iff $\bar F(0)>c>\bar F(1)$; otherwise compliance is globally stable if $\bar F(1)\ge c$, and evasion if $\bar F(0)\le c$.*

*Scope.* The host enters payoffs additively and identically (A1, S0). Across types the condition of (3) is sufficient, not necessary: a type whose evasion pays still loses its evaders when another type's compliers earn more (S3). Part (1) is the known translation invariance of relative dynamics (Weibull 1995); what is added is the separation of neutrality from compliance, and the connection to captured enforcement. *Proof:* S3 [num: CI].

The blindness that hides the host is also what protects a common rule: a constraint that covers every capable carrier, costs them alike and is sanctioned above the gain from evasion turns the pathology into a protection. The two conditions do different work. The sanction keeps a rule; equal incidence keeps a rule from becoming a weapon. A constraint that costs challengers more than incumbents picks winners among the compliant (part 2), and a rule written by the successful can bar entry while binding everyone in name: newcomers adopt it or are out. That is the second direction in which success reaches the selectors, barriers against challengers beside accommodation of the incumbent's own practice ({{>capture}}). A common *liability* on the harmful conduct works differently again: it makes the conduct pay less, and reverses selection once it exceeds the private advantage. Every route needs coverage and a sanction at least the gain from evasion, supplied by correctors that Section 4 shows success can capture.

**{{!Proposition:load}} (Selection raises the load) [thm].** Whatever carriers value, and other advantages equal, selection favours the conduct that loads the host until liability exceeds its private advantage. *Let selection be payoff-monotone and $\tau_{\rm eff}(t)<\nu$. If $\phi_k=\phi_l$ and $e_k>e_l$, then $p_k/p_l$ increases along every path of $V$, including paths below $V_{\min}$. If all $\phi_k$ are equal, a race carrier is present and the cumulative advantage $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\,ds$ grows without bound, the load tends to $\bar e$ and $\limsup_tV\le(1-h_V\bar e/\rho)^+$; hence if*
$${{!eq:reach}}\qquad h_V\bar e>\rho\,(1-V_{\min})\qquad\text{(reach)}$$
*the host eventually falls below its floor, however much any carrier values it. If $B(\infty)<\infty$, it need not.*

*Scope.* Other advantages correlated with restraint can offset the selection (S3). *Proof:* S3.

What carriers value decides which strategies they try, not which ones grow: evolutionary suicide, under stated conditions (Rankin, Bargum and Kokko 2007).

### 3.3 Accounting: what an objective can see

Selection acts as if only differences count; an objective can count more. Let actor $i$ act on
$${{!eq:objective}}\qquad J_i=\pi_i+\theta_i\,\bar\pi_{-i},\qquad\theta_i\in[-1,n-1],$$
where $\bar\pi_{-i}$ is the others' average payoff and $\theta_i$ marks where $i$'s self-model draws its boundary: $\theta=-1$ is the purely relative objective, $\theta=0$ the isolated self, and $\theta=n-1$ counts every contestant equally, and only the contestants.

**{{!Proposition:twochan}} (Two-channel accounting) [thm].** Every change in payoffs splits into a part shared by all and a transfer; the self-model only sets how much of each it sees. *For any change $\Delta\pi$ with mean $\bar\Delta$ and a common $\theta$,*
$${{!eq:twochaneq}}\qquad \Delta J_i=(1+\theta)\,\bar\Delta+\frac{n-1-\theta}{n-1}\,\big(\Delta\pi_i-\bar\Delta\big).$$
*(1) Of a gain or loss shared equally, each objective registers $(1+\theta)/n$ of the total. (2) At $\theta=-1$ the game in objectives is zero-sum and every shared gain or loss is invisible; at $\theta=n-1$ what remains is coordination, not a dilemma. (3) Relative selection is $\theta=-1$, and a weight $\lambda$ on winning, $\pi_i+\lambda(\pi_i-\bar\pi)$, is $\theta=-\lambda(n-1)/(n+\lambda(n-1))$ up to a positive factor. (4) A levy rebated equally enters as $\tau(n-1-\theta)/n$, and breaking a promise to give a fraction $\beta$ of a prize $\Omega$ to the others gains $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$. (5) An action with private gain $g$ that costs the host $H$, borne equally, is taken iff $g>(1+\theta)H/n$: a point of enough in accounting, absent at $\theta=-1$.*

*Proof:* S3, an identity [num: TC], with how two perturbations identify $\theta$ [num: TH], and why identification fails exactly where shared harm goes unseen.

### 3.4 Interdependence and the boundary error

Actors are coupled to one another as well as to the host, and the coupling has two signs. Split each payoff into a *divisible* part $\pi^{\rm e}$, such as rents, services and the material gains of capability, and a *positional* part $\pi^{\rm s}$: standing, the power to set rules, the loss of being subordinated. In reduced form $i$'s true welfare is
$${{!eq:welfare}}\qquad W_i=\pi_i+\kappa\,\bar\pi^{\rm e}_{-i}+\kappa_s\,\bar\pi^{\rm s}_{-i},\qquad\kappa\in[0,n-1),\quad\kappa_s\in[-1,0].$$
The coupling $\kappa$ counts what prices do not settle in the divisible part: shared hazards, failures propagating through concentrated nodes of common infrastructure, systemic risk. It is a material fact, not altruism: no company or nation is vertically integrated across the semiconductor chain, and the loss of one input gas was estimated to cost downstream production over a hundred times its direct value [obs: EV13]. The winner still depends on those it would dominate. The positional weight $\kappa_s\le0$ records that a rival's standing is one's own subordination [def]. *The contest:* each of $n$ actors chooses an intensity $e_i\ge0$, wins a prize $\Omega=\Omega_e+\Omega_s$, divisible and positional, with probability $w_i=e_i^r/\sum_je_j^r$, with decisiveness $r\in(0,n/(n-1)]$, pays $c$ per unit, and bears a shared loss $\ell$ per unit of total intensity $\mathcal E$.

**{{!Proposition:boundary}} (The boundary error of the self-model) [thm].** Toward what can be divided, a held self-model narrower than the real coupling raises intensity and lowers its holder's own welfare, and selection favours the narrowest. *(1) For every $\theta\in[-1,n-1)$ the game in objectives has the symmetric equilibrium $e(\theta)=r\Omega(n-1-\theta)/\big(n^2(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, decreasing in $\theta$, and true welfare there rises with $\theta$. (2) Against fixed intensities of the others, $i$'s true welfare is maximized by its best response under the objective that weighs the others' divisible payoffs by $\kappa$ and their positional payoffs by $\kappa_s$. Acting on a weight $\theta_i<\kappa$ toward the divisible part lowers that welfare whenever it changes the chosen intensity and the maximizer is unique; toward the positional part, relative concern is accurate. (3) Selection on intensities favours what an actor with $\theta=-1$ would choose: the shared loss drops out, and at $r=1$ the prize is fully dissipated and with any shared loss every actor ends below zero (Schaffer 1988 for the finite-population evolutionarily stable intensity; Hehenkamp, Leininger and Possajennikov 2004 for full dissipation in this contest).*

*Scope.* For $r>1$, asymmetric equilibria with lower total intensity also exist in a band below $n/(n-1)$; the symmetric one is used throughout. *Proof:* S3 [num: RH].

Part (2) is the sense in which a narrow held self-model is an *error* toward what can be divided, not a preference: it draws a boundary inside one system, and the coupling punishes its holder. Part (3) is the sense in which the error is *selected*; where self-models are observed, a narrower commitment even extracts concessions, so accuracy, like inclusion, has to be built (S3). The weight acted on is observable: $e(\theta)$ depends on private cost and shared loss only through $c+(1+\theta)\ell$, so intensity responds to the shared loss $1+\theta$ times as strongly as to private cost, the ratio that Test Γ reads (Section 7.4) [thm; num: SP].

Toward the positional part, realism is right: where the prize is standing itself, concern for relative gains is accurate (Grieco 1988; Powell 1991). That locates the crux of design. A positional prize cannot be divided (Section 10.2), so the answer is to keep such prizes from forming, by holding decisive capability in common or not building it, not to ask actors to widen their selves over them.

**Selected minds [conj].** The same selection acts on those who decide; the corpus calls it *molochlization* [corpus: alignment-selection]. What the environment rewards, the mind promotes, and selected minds build the organizations that form the next minds (EV14). **Convergence [conj].** For a planetary technology, unpriced coupling rises with power, since the host is shared and the harms that prices do not settle grow with what is contested. If it does, then toward everything that can be divided the accurate self-model and the inclusive one converge, and understanding that we are one becomes first a matter of seeing correctly (crux C11).

### 3.5 The weight on winning, and how compounding sets it

A weight $\lambda$ on winning turns a commons that every developer would protect on its absolute payoff into one that each depletes: in the steady-state commons of S3, with $\Upsilon>1$ the value to the field of a unit of restraint over its private cost, full depletion is the equilibrium once $\lambda>(\Upsilon-n)/(n-1)$, every developer is worse off than under joint restraint, and under (reach) the host ends below its floor (**{{!Proposition:wow}}**, S3; Armstrong, Bostrom and Shulman 2016). A classical commons needs each actor's share of the loss to be small; selection on relative standing depletes it even among a privileged few. If exposure to the host falls with share, the host no longer cancels and selection favours insulation as well, so a carrier can stay viable while the host does not: a concentration of power that V3 rejects (**{{!Remark:unequal}}**). Rank-based prizes supply part of the weight on winning; the loop of Section 2 supplies the rest, whatever anyone values, wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return.

**{{!Proposition:standprice}} (Compounding prices standing) [thm∣prem].** Near equal standing, compounding narrows the self-model a forward-looking actor acts on exactly where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return. *In the model of {{loopgain}}, let the prize grow with the total stocks; take as reference a symmetric steady state or, where the prize is proportional to the stocks, the balanced path; and let an actor hold the self-model $\theta_0<n-1$, discount at $\delta$ and convert its net payoff into stocks at the rates $\kappa_j$, the others' conduct held fixed. Let $\lambda_{\rm rel}$ be what a unit of relative net payoff today is worth in the actor's discounted future winnings, and $\lambda_{\rm com}$ what a unit received and reinvested alike by every actor is worth. Where the discounted loop gain $\Gamma_\delta$ of {{loopgain}}(3), taken at the reference state, and the common loop gain $\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta$, the discounted gain of the loop through which reinvestment by all enlarges the prize (S3 gives both in closed form), lie in $[0,1)$, $1+\lambda_{\rm rel}=1/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ and $1+\lambda_{\rm com}=1/(1-\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta)$, and to first order the actor acts on the objective of {{twochan}} with the working self-model $\theta$ given by*
$${{!eq:thetagamma}}\qquad \frac{1+\theta}{n-1-\theta}=\frac{1+\theta_0}{n-1-\theta_0}\cdot\frac{1+\lambda_{\rm com}}{1+\lambda_{\rm rel}}=\frac{1+\theta_0}{n-1-\theta_0}\cdot\frac{1-\Gamma_\delta}{1-\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta},$$
*so that, whatever $\theta_0<n-1$, the working self-model is narrower than the held one iff $\lambda_{\rm rel}>\lambda_{\rm com}$.*
1. *Fixed prize. Then $\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta=0$, so the working self-model narrows whenever $\Gamma_\delta>0$, including where equal standing is stable, to*
$$\theta_\Gamma=\frac{n\theta_0-(n-1)(1+\theta_0)\Gamma_\delta}{n-(1+\theta_0)\Gamma_\delta},$$
*which falls to $-1$ as $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$ for every $\theta_0<n-1$. With it the common channel loses weight in {{twochan}}: the host and the point of enough recede from view, while a rebated levy and the gain from breaking a promise weigh more.*
2. *Prize proportional to capacity. In the one-channel model of {{compound}}, with the prize proportional to total capacity and neither provision nor diffusion, $\Gamma_\delta/\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta=(1-\beta)r$, and $\lambda_{\rm rel}>\lambda_{\rm com}$ iff $(1-\beta)r>1$, over every horizon: the condition under which equal standing is unstable ({{compound}}). Looking ahead narrows the self exactly where leads compound, and widens it where they do not.*

*Scope.* First order at equal standing, the others' conduct fixed. That net payoff is reinvested is load-bearing: where only gross winnings convert, the relative part of private cost is not amplified and the working weight is narrower still [num: SP]. At $\Gamma_\delta\ge1$ no finite weight represents the actor ({{loopgain}}(3)), so "where the loop gain is high" reads as the limit $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$. Diffusion separates the two conditions of (2): the self-model narrows before equal standing is lost, in a band that {{growprize}} gives in closed form [thm; num: SP]. Where rivals respond to standing, the relative return is read from the price elasticity of {{loopgain}}(4), own intensity held and rivals responding, in place of $r$, and with a fixed prize {{eq:thetagamma}} keeps its form. In contests in which a lead discourages the follower, the price at parity is then amplified, by exactly $2(n-1)/n$ in a two-period contest with effort [thm] and by about 1.05 to 7.9 times in the two-actor Markov-perfect contests computed, while a laggard's price falls, below zero with a shared loss, and it stops contesting standing; a strong motive to catch up turns the correction the other way (Harris and Vickers 1987; Konrad and Kovenock 2009) [num: MP; held for outside review]. *Proof:* S3.

This is the exact form of an older observation, that concern for relative gains depends on how far gains convert into future advantage (Powell 1991), with the common channel added: what decides is how far gains convert into advantage relative to how far they enlarge what all share. Selection on conduct favours the relative objective ({{boundary}}(3)); compounding prices standing in whoever looks ahead; and the loop selects at both ends, narrowing the objective of those who can keep pace and excluding those who cannot ({{compound}}(2)). Patience cuts both ways: it strengthens repetition and raises the price of standing. Inside the actor, growth whose return is common widens the self-model, while decisiveness, which makes the return relative, narrows it; a part of the stakes that does not grow with capacity, as standing does not, adds to the relative return only; and provision not won in the contest lowers that return by lowering the won share, the sign Test Γ reads (S3) [num: SP].

**{{!Remark:twoerrors}} (Two errors, and which one understanding corrects).** The *held* self-model can draw its boundary inside the system, $\theta_0<\kappa$ toward what can be divided: the boundary error of {{boundary}}(2), which accurate understanding corrects. The *working* objective that compounding sets is no error for the actor but the correct first-order computation of its interest, and accuracy does not protect against it: with a fixed prize, an actor whose held weight is the coupling, $\theta_0=\kappa$, acts below the isolated self once $\Gamma_\delta>n\kappa/\big((n-1)(1+\kappa)\big)$, 0.381 for $n=4$ and $\kappa=0.4$ [thm; num: SP]. Every actor acting on it is worse off than if all acted on $\kappa$ ({{boundary}}(1)): individually accurate, collectively self-defeating. Only a change of the game corrects it. That is the exact sense in which this paper judges the game, not the act. Understanding acts on the first error directly, and on the second only through agreement to change what winning confers (Section 12.2).

### 3.6 No outside

The system has one host, and at the planetary scale no rival group of the same kind. Evolution's largest innovations were integrations of units that could replicate alone into wholes, held mainly by interests aligned by construction and secondly by policing; cancer is integration failing (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry 1995). That cooperation within groups was favoured by competition between groups is answered by the next result (X1).

**{{!Lemma:onegroup}} (What selection at any level cannot supply) [thm].** A trait whose benefit goes equally to everyone is selected against overall and within every group, however the population is grouped. *Let a trait cost its bearer and benefit every member of the population equally. Under every partition into groups and every payoff-monotone rule, the total selection term of the Price equation and every within-group term are non-positive. The between-group term is non-positive where fitness is affine in payoff; otherwise it can be positive, by Jensen's inequality across groups of unequal spread, but it never makes the total positive. The trait can rise only through the transmission term, by biased transmission such as learning, persuasion or imitation guided by a model of the system, or through a rule that changes the bearer's relative payoff: policing, liability, reward for contribution that does not convert into advantage, an enforced common constraint (Price 1972; Frank 1995).*

*Scope.* A benefit confined to the bearer's own group can be favoured between groups. *Proof:* S3 [num: PR].

At the scale of one host the benefits of the rules that keep it viable are common to all, so however firms, states or blocs are grouped, selection does not favour those rules. Rules whose benefits are partly local can still be found by polycentric experiment: a plurality of decorrelated units trying different rules above a common floor lets some fail while others carry on, as cells do, and with one global civilization that is both necessary and hard [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. For the common part two routes remain: adoption after failures that can be undone, by learning and by coalitions of the harmed (Becker 1983), and adoption before failure, guided by a model of the system. **Where the failure to be avoided is global and irreversible, only the second acts in time.** That is the formal content of the claim that **understanding that we are one, one interconnected system with one host and no outside, is a long-term requirement if growth and change are favoured**: not because nothing else can integrate at this scale, but because against failures that cannot be undone nothing else acts in time. Understanding acts in the lemma's own terms, as the transmission through which actors come to count what relative selection makes invisible. That such anticipation can succeed has no biological precedent; it is a conjecture that the transition of Section 11 has to make true [conj].

The corpus names five endgames of the dynamic [corpus: selfishness-spiral]: a freeze of mutual destruction; deadlock, a structure frozen by the consensus of the remaining players; a change of narrative by which the players come to see themselves as one whole and so gain the ability to cooperate; collapse; and winner takes all. Four are results here: deterrence at saturation (Section 7.2), a captured configuration that cannot state what would end it (Section 4.5), the crossing results (Sections 3.2, 4.6 and 5.3), and the decisive winner (Section 5.2). The third is the lemma's transmission term, and it is the purpose of this paper. Integration by shared fate needs exposure made common, not declared; and integration within a part is not integration of the whole.

**{{!Proposition:club}} (Clubs and blocs) [thm].** Inclusion that stops at the edge of a club over-produces the harm it does not bear, and blocs replay the race one level up. *(1) Let the contestants' activity yield them a strictly concave common value $\Omega(\mathcal E)$ of total intensity, let each contestant bear a shared loss $\ell$ per unit, and let $m_o$ non-contestants each bear $\ell_o$ per unit. Contestants who weigh one another fully choose more intensity than the system optimum, and so over-produce the harm to those outside, whenever $m_o\ell_o>0$ and their own choice is positive. (2) If the contestants form equal blocs of $n_b$ members, each inclusive within and acting as one, the bloc game is the contest of Section 3.4 with $n/n_b$ players and shared loss $n_b\ell$ per unit of total intensity, so every result stated for that contest holds among blocs.*

*Scope.* Unequal blocs with $r\ne1$ make the bloc game asymmetric. *Proof:* S3 [num: CB, SM].

### 3.7 What "we are one" means

The phrase states a property of the system: all carriers belong to one host and are coupled through it and through one another, with no outside. It is not a preference everyone must adopt, and not a weight $\theta$. It does three jobs, each with its own support, and it does them through understanding.
1. **Empirical: shared dependencies.** One host; coupling through channels that prices do not settle; no outside to absorb a global failure. The support is specific pathways of shared exposure, not the assertion that everything is connected, and an actor that draws its held boundary inside the system errs about its own interests ({{boundary}}).
2. **Normative: standing.** Everyone who bears the consequences has standing to contest the direction of what affects them, whether or not they compete (V1, V3). The corpus gives the norm three parts: the future kept open for all, the conditions of continuation shared, and no seizure of the shared continuation (共开、共续、不夺共续) [corpus: shengsheng]. It is declared, not derived; inclusion of the contestants alone is a club ({{club}}).
3. **Design: a selection that counts the system.** A system "knows it is one" when its selection counts what its parts depend on: consequences return to their source; success earns reward but cannot buy exemption from the constraints all share, or control over its own assessment; those constraints cover every capable actor and are enforced; correction stays plural and decorrelated. These are the institutional counterparts of the policing and shared fate by which earlier integrations held; Section 8.2 states them as the marks of a generative order, and Section 10 gives them forms.

**How the phrase acts: understanding.** None of the three jobs is done by declaring it. A prediction of an end no participant would choose makes coordination possible only if it is shared, so "we are one" is first the content of a shared understanding; that is why this paper is written. What is needed is accuracy, not sentiment: an actor that cares only for its own survival, and sees correctly that its survival runs through the host and the others, understands what is required. The two errors of {{twoerrors}} divide the work: "we are one" understood corrects the held boundary, and only "we are one" built into what selection rewards corrects the working objective, which each actor computes correctly and no argument changes.

At the level of the self, the accurate boundary is the one the author's account of the relational self describes [corpus: relational-self]. A self is not a point outside its relations that enters them afterwards; it is the action-centre in which the relations that form it gather: body, care received, language, institutions and its own choices. "We are the ecology of our cells," and at the next scale the same holds: we may be individuals, but we cannot disregard the collective, because we are the collective (我们即是集). The isolated self of Ψ1 below is the boundary error held as an identity, and a self that is a knot of relations is neither an atom nor a cell of a collective, which is why the theory needs neither pole of the individual–collective axis. 大同, in the same corpus, is a direction, not an end state: a maintained condition in which the many are nourished together and do not harm one another (万物并育而不相害). **Oneness is not sameness.** Harmony makes things grow; sameness does not continue (和实生物，同则不继). A whole that suppresses difference becomes another totalizing attractor, and plurality of purposes and of correctors is a requirement of the design (Section 10).

### 3.8 The logic as held: Ψ

If a winner's logic is cancer-like, falling behind cannot be survived, and every actor has reason first to oppose the logic itself (如果胜利者的逻辑是cancer式的，落后并不可以承受，因此所有人都应该去首先反对这个逻辑) [corpus: possibility-space]. The ruling compresses the results above: under such a logic those who fall behind are excluded rather than left a smaller share ({{compound}}(2)), the winner has no point of enough at which to stop ({{twochan}}(5)), and the selection that favours it cannot see the host it consumes ({{blind}}). The premise is the race's own; the conclusion is the opposite, since to win under this logic is to carry it forward. What is opposed is a logic, not an actor.

Call an objective *unbounded relative* if it ranks relative standing on the decisive dimension without any point of enough. "Cancer" in this paper means exactly one thing: the selection of such a subsystem, for its own growth relative to its neighbours, by a selection that cannot see that it is killing its host. A cancer cell does not fail to replicate; it replicates very well. Its failure is that it no longer knows it belongs to a body. The image is never applied to persons, peoples or institutions: doing so would itself be the attribution of the logic that this section warns against. Held as self-model and doctrine, the logic has three parts.

**{{!Definition:psi}} (Ψ, the winning model) [def].** Ψ is the configuration of self-model and doctrine that the selection pressure of winning favours. *Ψ1, the isolated self:* welfare is one's own payoff, and others are rivals or instruments ($\theta\le0$). *Ψ2, naive Darwinism:* the world is a contest of all against all, and prevailing certifies fitness and right; "the winner takes all, and the winner justifies all" [corpus: selfishness-spiral] ($\theta=-1$, with the winners occupying the normative selectors). *Ψ3, winner-take-all compounding:* advantage compounds and converts across domains, and falling behind cannot be recovered (a high loop gain, held as a belief and built into institutions). Ψ includes the doctrine that treats these settings as laws of nature rather than parameters of a game that can be changed.

The unbounded relative objective is Ψ1 at $\theta=-1$, with Ψ2 certifying the winner and Ψ3 compounding the win. The corpus calls it the logic of winning (讲赢), as against the logic of reasons (讲理): once there is contention, it becomes "I am right", and the self outranks the reason; only cooperation reasons, and struggle argues to win (一旦争，则「我对」，我大于理；合作才讲理，斗则讲赢) [corpus: alignment-selection; jian-and-wenyan]. **Ψ is individually rational where compounding prices standing, collectively self-defeating, and false as a doctrine about nature.** *Individually rational:* there its conduct is each actor's correct computation ({{twoerrors}}), and selection favours that conduct whether or not anyone holds the doctrine ({{boundary}}(3), {{load}}); that competition selects organizations that behave *as if* they maximize (Alchian 1950) is part of this theory, not an objection to it. In the author's words, "the strategy that wins locally takes the name of rationality" (局部胜出的策略拿走了理性的名字) [corpus: meta-attractor]. *Collectively self-defeating:* every actor acting on it ends worse off than if all acted on the real coupling ({{boundary}}(1)), and under (reach) the host falls below its floor however much any carrier values it ({{load}}, {{wow}}). *False as a doctrine about nature:* held as a self-model, Ψ1 draws a boundary that the coupling punishes ({{boundary}}(2)); Ψ2 misreads evolution, whose largest innovations were integrations (Section 3.6), and misdescribes persons, among whom the self-interest model failed in every one of fifteen small-scale societies studied (Henrich et al. 2001) [obs: EV14], while firms and states are shaped by selection on conduct rather than by belief; Ψ3 treats as laws of nature what are parameters of a game that can be changed, sharing, convertibility, decisiveness, provision and diffusion ({{compound}}); and the clause "the winner justifies all" is rejected by the values of Section 1.3, not by evidence.

Ψ is also *partly made true*: Ψ3 holds where institutions built under Ψ make it hold. In the Markov-perfect contests computed, one structural loop gain supports both a smooth equilibrium and, when contests run in many small rounds, a winner-take-all one whose price of standing is about 9 to 12.5 times the structural value ({{eqgain}}) [num: MP; held for outside review]. If expectations select between them, a belief that leads are decisive selects the equilibrium in which they are [conj].

Ψ shapes conduct where it is held and where it is attributed: an actor with a wider objective still races if it believes its rivals hold Ψ. A misjudgment of a rival's current model can be corrected by verifiable conduct (Section 11.2); a correct forecast of what the game selects, only by changing what it selects.

Selection cannot see what all share, and looking ahead does not cure it: wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, it narrows the working objective, on which Section 5's threshold of enforcement turns. What remains is correction, and Section 4 shows that correction is itself something success can buy.
