## S7. The present trajectory

### Proofs of Proposition {{#branches}}, Proposition {{#raceselects}} and Corollary {{#racepremise}}

*Proposition {{#branches}}.* Each item is the cited result read under its branch's condition.

(1) *Branch I.* As $p_d\to1$ with $D_i\ge C_i$, {{eq:enforceeq}} becomes $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)\le0$, so only profiles with $g_i\le0$, stage equilibria of the objectives held, are sustained ({{enforce}}(4)). The best-response and joining statements are {{join}}; dissipation is {{dissipate}}; delegation is {{delegate}}(2) in the additive model of S6, where $L_e/n>L_c\Delta\psi$ suffices for dominance as speed grows, and {{delegatelock}} with $q_L=1$; the failure of correction beyond its robust reach is {{tempo}}; the motive for preventive attack is {{preventattack}}.

(2) *Branch I′.* Above the saturating level further advantage changes no outcome, so a deviation from above it yields no position beyond punishment and $p_d=0$. {{eq:enforceeq}} then reduces to $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-v_i)$, which repetition enforces on the terms of {{restraint}}. The race channel ({{racehazard}}) and the growth channel ({{coverage}}) do not pass through $p_d$, so their hazards remain.

(3) *Branch II.* $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ where punishment is triggered by detection ({{detectlink}}), and {{enforce}}(2) gives enforceability for every actor with $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$. In the bargaining model of S5, with A's chance of prevailing falling from 0.5 to 0 over $N$ periods, $c_A=0.05$ and $\delta=0.8$, attack is rational at the start for $N\le3$ and never for $N\ge4$, by backward induction on $V_t=\max(W_t,\delta V_{t+1})$ (S5). Modes with $a\tau_c<1$ admit a stabilizing proportional correction ({{tempo}}). For delegation: in the model of {{delegate}}, when $k$ others delegate the gain from delegating is $L_e[w_i(1,k)-w_i(0,k)]$, increasing in the speed $s$ and proportional to the loss from defeat $L_e$, while its cost $L_c\Delta\psi(1-\psi_0)^{n-1-k}(1-\psi_1)^k$ does not depend on $L_e$. The speed at which gain and cost are equal therefore rises as $L_e/L_c$ falls, and if the limiting gain falls below the cost for some $k$, delegation never becomes dominant. That a defeat answered before it becomes decisive carries a smaller $L_e$ is the premise of the item.

(4) *Branch III.* {{boundary}}(1) gives $e(\theta)=r\Omega(n-1-\theta)/\big(n^2(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, {{dissipate}}(1) a dissipated share proportional to $r$, and {{racehazard}} a hazard proportional to $r_t$. Low structural decisiveness does not give Branch III: a capacity channel with gain 0.8 and a rule-support channel with gain 0.3 give $\Gamma=1.1$, and a relative gap of $10^{-4}$ grows to about 4 in 4,000 periods of the nonlinear model [num: LG]; and in the Markov-perfect contests computed, the equilibrium gain is 1.11 at a structural gain of 0.45 and 2.83 at 0.6 [num: MP]. $\square$

*Proposition {{#raceselects}}.* (1) Write $\pi=\Pr(E)\in(0,1)$. Since $\Pr(W)=\Pr(W\mid E)\pi+\Pr(W\mid\neg E)(1-\pi)<\Pr(W\mid E)$ when $\Pr(W\mid E)>\Pr(W\mid\neg E)$, Bayes' rule gives $\Pr(E\mid W)=\Pr(W\mid E)\pi/\Pr(W)>\pi$. Under cumulative payoff-monotone selection, conduct that raises relative success grows in share round by round ({{load}}), so the shift compounds. (2) If winning depends on $X$ only through $E$, then $\Pr(X\mid W,E)=\Pr(X\mid E)$ and $\Pr(X\mid W,\neg E)=\Pr(X\mid\neg E)$, so
$$\Pr(X\mid W)-\Pr(X)=\big[\Pr(X\mid E)-\Pr(X\mid\neg E)\big]\big[\Pr(E\mid W)-\Pr(E)\big],$$
which is positive under the premise and part (1). Without the premise the inequality for conduct before victory does not carry to conduct after it. (3) After a decisive win, a promise to give a fraction $\beta$ of the prize to the others is worth breaking by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$, with no one to sanction it ({{wingives}}): the commitment problem (Fearon 1995; Powell 2006), sharpened by uncertainty about intentions (Mearsheimer 2001). An actor that decides by its worst case over a stated set of possibilities, and applies that rule to every action (Gilboa and Schmeidler 1989), counts the worst; a survival-first actor need not. $\square$

*Corollary {{#racepremise}}.* (1) {{join}} gives that restraint is not a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S<P$; in Branch I, and in Branch II without timely detection, $p_d$ is large, and by {{enforce}}(4) no punishment by repetition restores restraint that is not a stage equilibrium. (2) {{wingives}} and {{raceselects}}(2). (3) The protection standard of Section 8.1 judges a course by the bound it places on O1 and O2 for those it affects. A course that leaves their protection after a win to the winner's objective, and under the premise shifts winners toward objectives that give least, offers no bound that does not rest on that objective. This step is normative (V1–V3), not a derivation. $\square$

### Near parity: the support for Section 7.2

In the computed contests where rivals respond and a lead discourages the follower [num: MP]:
- *The price at parity is amplified.* The computed price exceeds the structural value $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ by 1.05 to 3.15 times at depreciation 0.1, by 1.12 to 4.75 times at 0.2, and by 7.87 times under high patience ({{eqgain}}; S3).
- *Summed over the two contestants, the price is higher at parity than at a three-to-one split in every case computed.* With large jumps ($r=1$) at $\Gamma=0.3$ the prices at shares 0.75 and 0.25 are 0.2703 and 0.1003, together 0.371, against $2\times0.1934=0.387$ at parity; at $\Gamma=0.6$, 0.6543 and 0.1065 against $2\times1.1762$; with small jumps ($r=4$) at $\Gamma=0.3$, 0.0856 and 0.0093 against $2\times0.1941$.
- *Not every leader's own price is highest at parity.* In the large-jump case at $\Gamma=0.3$ the leader's price at 0.75 exceeds the price at parity (0.2703 against 0.1934). "Highest at parity" holds for the contest as a whole and for the laggard, not for every leader.
- *Effort is highest at parity.* In a tug-of-war contest absorbing at a lead of $\pm K$ ($K=3$, $\delta=0.95$), total effort one step from parity is 0.405 of its value at parity and two steps away 0.013; the laggard's effort is 0.138 and then 0.000 of its parity value.
- *The laggard stops contesting.* The laggard's price at a share of 0.25 is 57% of the structural value at $\Gamma=0.3$ and 25% at 0.6, and below zero with a shared loss ($-0.039$).

Where a strong motive to catch up makes the laggard try harder, the strategic correction shrinks and in one computed case reverses ($r^{\rm eq}/r=0.993$) [num: MP]. The statement of Section 7.2 is therefore one about contests in which a lead discourages the follower; it is held for outside review with the other Markov-perfect results.
