# Part II. Diagnosis: the present trajectory

Part II asks on which side of each threshold of Part I the present trajectory stands. Its claims are observations, held to one standard for every actor and with their gaps named; the theorems it uses are Part I's.

## 7. The present trajectory

> **Claim.** The thresholds of Part I turn on a few shared parameters, above all the share of capacity that has to be won, the convertibility of success into control, and decisiveness, each threshold shown in its own model [thm]. The dynamics of the present trajectory are signatures of the conditions, with named gaps in the evidence [obs]. Where a deviation can reach a decisive position before it is answered, the case the premise of the race describes, repetition stops enforcing restraint, and racing can be each actor's best response to a racing rival [thm]. The race as a collective course leaves everyone's protection after a win to the winner's objective and, where restraint before victory and after it go together, selects winners whose objectives give least [thm∣prem]; the premise of the race therefore cannot justify that course, and is a reason to prepare custody now [val]. That the thresholds move together as the stakes grow is the conjecture the diagnosis tests [conj].
>
> **Why.** Part I's results hold in their models; whether their conditions hold now is a question of evidence, applied with one standard to every actor. A parameter that enters several thresholds moves each of them in that threshold's own model, and whether the parameters themselves move together in the world is a separate claim, registered to be tested rather than assumed.

Read down the fourth column and the same three parameters recur. Decisiveness $r$ and the share of capacity that has to be won, $\omega$, set compounding, standing and the breakout probability directly, and talk and delegation through the working objective that standing sets; the convertibility of success into control, $\chi$, enters eight of the nine rows, five of them through an interface marked †. That is the formal sense in which the conditions are interconnected: not a single pressure, but a few parameters on which several thresholds turn, so that a parameter moved one way moves every threshold it enters toward the same side wherever the sign is shown [thm, cell by cell; S7]. Patience is the exception that cuts both ways: it strengthens repetition and raises the price of standing (Section 3.5). Growth of the stakes against provision raises $\omega$, and in one reduced model the breakout probability rises and the enforceable bound falls together until they cross, while neither moves when provision grows with the stakes ({{scissors}}) [num: JX]. Whether the parameters move together in the world is C0 [conj].

Two further entries carry the argument. **Provision not won in the contest lowers, through capacity, the loop gain, the price of standing and the breakout probability at once** ({{chansign}}, {{standprice}}, {{scissors}}): the formal content of the image of trees, and the pivot on which the scissors turn. It does not act on the reflexive channel, where it adds the same to every level; that channel is answered by correction and non-appropriability (Section 2.2). **Promised sharing raises the danger in enforcement**, because a decisive winner can break the promise ({{wingives}}): what is shared as it arises lowers the gain from deviating, while what is promised for after victory raises the value of a decisive position by what reneging saves. S7 gives the full matrix of parameters against thresholds, with four entries for every sign: the intervention, what is held fixed, the model that supplies the sign, and the interface that is not proved.

**Four loops.** The author describes the pressure as four loops [corpus: selfishness-spiral], and the rows of {{thresholds}} are their formal parts. *Compounding*: "selfish gets rewarded and it compounds, it out grows other behavior patterns", where a frame is selfish when its ledger counts only its own measures, the narrow self-model of Section 3. *Capture of the rules, and adopt or be out*: "NVFs [narrative-value frames] define rules of the game, and the rules of the game determine the rewards of the players", and those who are rewarded are watched: "other agents observe them and understand in order to compete with them they also have to follow their rules". *The naturalized narrative*: "as they accumulate more, they create and slowly dominate the narrative", until the pattern is taken "as 'human nature', as granted, as everyone has to follow this because we can not change it". *Regulator lag*: the consensus code of norms and laws "has to be able to keep up with the pace of change and complexity of the society … in order to keep selfishness in check. (so basically our understanding of the world has to keep up with reality)"; where the world changes faster, the first three run unchecked.

Formally the four are not alike, and they do not run in series. Compounding and capture are reinforcing channels on one state, the concentration of standing; fed by one win share, their gains add in $\Gamma$, so channels each too weak to open the loop can open it together ({{loopgain}}(1)) [thm]. "Adopt or be out" is capture's second direction: a rule whose incidence falls on challengers picks winners among the compliant ({{blind}}(2), {{capture}}) [thm]. The naturalized narrative is a reinforcing loop through the formation of minds, stated as a conjecture (Section 5.4) [conj]. Regulator lag is not a fourth reinforcing loop but the balancing loop of correction and mobilization ({{countervail}}), weakened and delayed: capture and the erosion of human capacity lower its gain ({{corrrate}}, {{humancorr}}), and tempo, masking and alienated language lengthen its delay ({{tempo}}, {{masking}}, {{talk}}) [thm, each in its model]. The danger is a shift of dominance from the balancing loop to the reinforcing ones, across delays that run from the turnover of capacity, about $1/(d_K+\delta_f)$ periods, through the settling of rule support, about $1/(\varepsilon d)$, to the formation of minds over generations, which no model here contains. A balancing loop cannot be subtracted from the reinforcing gain: one that answers late oscillates with growing amplitude while the net gain is below one, at a net gain of 0.25 with modulus 1.0075 and a period of 51.6 rounds in the case computed ({{delayosc}}) [num: CH]. Correction that arrives late can destabilize what it is meant to steady.

Three intensifiers act on all four [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. Paradigms converge: "more and more agents will also becoming single value optimizers through iteration", which removes the plurality on which decorrelated correction depends ({{topology}}). Harm and capture reinforce each other, so a crisis becomes a new field of accumulation. And as fewer hold more, more compete over less, which raises the intensity of what remains.

**The drive, the driver and the substrate.** The loops have a *drive*: drives, above all the belief that one is in a struggle for survival, which narrows the held self-model, shortens the horizon and so weakens the repetition on which enforcement rests. The author named it the attractor of attractors: "如果相信自己在生存状态的文化，attractor大多都会totalize" (a culture that believes itself in a state of survival totalizes most of its attractors) [corpus: meta-attractor]. The drive is not a factor of $\Gamma$; it acts through the working objective. It is also partly made by the loop: a discounted gain near one narrows the working objective as the belief would ({{standprice}}(1)) [thm], and where contests run in many small rounds, an expectation that leads are decisive can select an equilibrium in which they are ({{eqgain}}) [num: MP; held for outside review]. A belief the game makes partly true is hard to correct by argument and easier to remove by changing the game ({{twoerrors}}): provision beneath the game takes away its ground (Section 10.4). Among persons, the desire for status, respect freely conferred, is a fundamental motive (Anderson, Hildreth and Howland 2015), with two routes to rank, dominance and prestige (Henrich and Gil-White 2001). The pressure channels it into dominance [conj]; a generative order can channel it into prestige for contribution, provided prestige does not convert into control over the shared conditions or their correctors, a condition on convertibility that can be measured (EV16). The *driver* is technology, AI above all, which moves the parameters of every row of {{thresholds}} in either direction (Section 2.4). The *substrate* is the one host that all carriers share (Section 3).

### 7.2 Contest branches: what the premise of the race can and cannot justify

The three parts of the premise of the race (Section 5.1), (decisive), (fast) and (unsaturated), cut the possible contests into four branches by what a deviation can buy. The first two cuts are the two questions of the enforcement condition: whether a position beyond sanction exists, and whether it arrives before it is answered. Saturation is the third.

**{{!Proposition:branches}} (Classification of contest branches) [thm, branch by branch].** Each branch is a set of results already proved, read under that branch's condition. *Let AI be an instrument of the contest.*
1. *Branch I: decisive, fast and unsaturated, so that $p_d\to1$ [thm]. Where a decisive position is worth at least cooperation, restraint that is not a stage equilibrium of the objectives held cannot be enforced by repetition ({{enforce}}(4)). Unilateral restraint is not a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S<P$, while joining buys nothing materially in the fully dissipative limit ({{join}}); racing dissipates the prize ({{dissipate}}); delegation becomes dominant as speed grows when its gain exceeds its share of the expected control loss, and is never chosen by actors who price in a certain lock ({{delegate}}, {{delegatelock}}); modes beyond the robust reach of correction fail unless slowed ({{tempo}}); and a coming shift gives a rational motive for preventive attack ({{preventattack}}).*
2. *Branch I′: decisive but saturating, so that advantage converts decisively up to a level above which further advantage changes no outcome [thm]. The contest has an object up to that level and none beyond: above it $p_d=0$, and restraint is enforceable by repetition on the terms of {{restraint}}, while the hazards of development, which do not pass through $p_d$, remain ({{coverage}}, {{racehazard}}).*
3. *Branch II: decisive but slow, so that a lead can become decisive but detection and response act within the time it needs with probability $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ near one, and $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ is small ({{detectlink}}) [thm∣prem]. Restraint is enforceable for every actor whose bound $\bar p_d$ exceeds that $p_d$ ({{enforce}}(2)); a shift spread over enough periods gives no motive for preventive attack ({{preventattack}}); modes with $a\tau_c<1$ stay within proportional correction ({{tempo}}); and delegation becomes dominant only at a higher speed, because the speed at which it dominates rises as the loss from defeat falls against the shared loss of control ({{delegate}}), and a defeat answered before it becomes decisive costs less than one that is not [prem]. Correction and bargaining have time to work.*
4. *Branch III: not decisive, so that no deviation yields a position beyond sanction ($p_d=0$) and leads do not compound in equilibrium ($\Gamma^{\rm eq}<1$) [thm]. Intensity, dissipation and the race-generated hazard are proportional to decisiveness ({{boundary}}(1), {{dissipate}}(1), {{racehazard}}): what vanishes as $r\to0$ is the object of the race, not every incentive to compete.*

*Scope.* Each item holds in the model of the result it cites. Low structural decisiveness does not place a contest in Branch III: a reflexive channel adds 0.3 to a capacity channel of 0.8 and opens the loop at 1.1 [num: LG], and rivals who respond lift the equilibrium gain above one from a structural gain of 0.45 in the contests computed [num: MP]. Within Branch I, where a decisive position is worth less than cooperation, cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium can survive $p_d=1$ ({{enforce}}(4)). Which branch AI is on is an empirical question, and for saturation there is no evidence either way (EV5; C5). *Proof:* S7.

Branch I′ is how nuclear rivalry stabilized once a survivable second strike was secure: the contest kept its object up to that level and lost it beyond (X11) [obs]. Balancing among rivals supplies a second kind of saturation that does not depend on the technology (X1).

The race also selects which winner arrives.

**{{!Proposition:raceselects}} (What the race selects) [thm∣prem].** The race selects winners by their conduct before victory, and, under one premise, by their conduct after it. *Let actors race for a decisive position; let $W$ be the event that an actor wins, $E$ that it does not restrain while ahead, and $X$ that, having won, it extends its dominance rather than limiting it.*
1. *If restraint while ahead lowers the probability of winning, $\Pr(W\mid E)>\Pr(W\mid\neg E)$, then $\Pr(E\mid W)>\Pr(E)$ whenever $0<\Pr(E)<1$: winners are selected for not restraining, and repeated rounds compound the shift ({{load}}) [thm].*
2. *If, in addition, conduct before victory and after it are positively correlated, $\Pr(X\mid E)>\Pr(X\mid\neg E)$ [prem], and winning depends on $X$ only through $E$, then $\Pr(X\mid W)>\Pr(X)$: the race shifts winners toward those that extend dominance [thm∣prem].*
3. *No actor can rely on a winner's stated limits on its use of a decisive position, since after a decisive win no one can sanction a broken promise ({{wingives}}): the commitment problem (Fearon 1995; Powell 2006) [thm].*

*Scope.* The correlation in (2) is a premise; its base rate is not established (C4; Test I). *Proof:* S7.

A winner is not a point but a field of agencies, firms, laboratories and systems, and what it does after victory is decided by selection inside it, to which Sections 2 to 4 apply; Test I measures that field's correctors. Where AI lowers the winner's dependence on the people it governs (C13), what restrains it after victory narrows to its own objective (Section 5.2), and an AI aligned to the winner is not thereby aligned to them (Section 6).

**{{!Corollary:racepremise}} (What the premise of the race cannot justify) [thm∣prem; val].**
1. *In Branch I, or in Branch II without timely detection, racing can be each actor's best response to a racing rival ({{join}}) [thm].*
2. *The race as a collective course leaves everyone's protection after a win to the winner's objective ({{wingives}}) and, under the premise of {{raceselects}}(2), shifts winners toward those whose objectives give least [thm∣prem].*
3. *The premise of the race, that a rival might win decisively, therefore cannot justify the race as a collective course, including in the form in which the race is safe provided the right actor wins [val].*

*Scope.* Part (3) applies the protection standard of Section 8.1, which rests on V1 to V3, to parts (1) and (2). It does not say that an actor is wrong to race given that others race; it says that what each can rationally do cannot be what all should choose. *Proof:* S7.

The premise that a rival might win decisively describes the case in which repetition stops enforcing restraint. There restraint rests on keeping breakouts improbable, by not building, by custody, or by detection faster than breakout, or on objectives under which restraint is already a stage equilibrium ({{restraint}}); the race supplies neither. The first is the institutional form of the third job of Section 3.7, a selection that counts the system; the second needs objectives wider than accuracy alone supplies (Section 5.1), unless coupling grows with power (C11). In neither case is "we are one" a parameter the result sets. **Oneness is what these requirements are requirements of**: one host, coupled actors, and no outside to absorb the failure.

The race's own premise is therefore the strongest reason to act before it comes true. Custody, the means it leaves, has to be prepared while leads are short; and because the approach of a decisive position is the moment at which restraint is least enforceable ({{enforce}}(4)), a decision to activate custody taken then would face the very condition it is meant to prevent. Activation has to be committed in advance and executed by mechanism (Section 12.1, tier 2).

Present evidence suggests that decisive advantage has not arrived: leads are short on every measure, open models trail closed ones by about four months, and algorithmic efficiency diffuses. Whether the lead has changed hands depends on the measure: by one composite index the frontier has stayed with developers in one state, while on a preference leaderboard the gap at the top is a few per cent and the lead is reported to have been traded (EV5, EV9; C5). Short leads cut both ways. They support enforcement: no breakout has yet put anyone beyond punishment, so enforced cooperation remains available, and common custody is a preparation, not an emergency, with the window to arrange it still open. They also place the contest near parity, which is where standing is priced highest: in the computed contests where rivals respond, the price of standing at parity is amplified, its sum over the two contestants is higher at parity than at a three-to-one split in every case computed, total effort is highest at parity and falls as a lead opens, and a laggard's price falls toward zero (Section 3.5; S7) [num: MP]. The window for custody and the hardest-fought stretch of the contest are the same period. Oneness is a property of the system on every branch; what changes between them is the institutional form its accurate recognition requires.

Readers in international relations will find the nearest neighbour of this account in defensive realism, which it extends by a shared hazard in development that endangers its holder, and from which it departs by treating self-help as a selected institution rather than a given (Wendt 1992). Where offense has the advantage and is hard to tell from defense, AI sits in the doubly dangerous quadrant (Jervis 1978; X1).

The signatures sustain one another at the level of what can be observed: W supplies the success that C converts into control; C removes the correction that would make the shared visible; N makes cooperation look naive, which keeps W in place. T6 is not merely a prejudice: where racing is dominant, or among purely relative actors, restraint really is unenforceable ({{relpunish}}), and the remedy is a change in what is selected, not a better argument. The trajectory is not uniform: seeds of cooperation exist, and the question is whether they become binding and broader in time. The reinvestment of success in influence over rules, the link to Section 4, is now visible as political spending in both directions but remains the least measured, since what Section 4 predicts is appropriable support that scales with share. Capability growth is measured, with the longest-horizon series at the limit of its instrument, while the growth of hazardous behaviour and of residual risk is not, which is why Section 12 treats it as the central crux.

### 7.4 Which layer, and how the diagnosis could fail

The diagnosis is at the layer of mechanism ({{meta}}(1)), partly evidenced; whether the present configuration is attracting or a long transient is open (Test D). That the six signatures load on one latent factor is a research question, not a registered prediction. What is registered is what the conditions predict and their absorbed components do not (TESTS lists who else predicts each).
- **Test Γ.** $\Gamma$ is not estimated (Section 2.2); what can be observed in its place is the weight conduct gives to shared harm, $1+\theta$, measured as the ratio of conduct's response to shared harm to its response to private cost (S3, which also gives its confound with how much of the harm is perceived). It should rise with provision not won in the contest relative to the stakes, and with diffusion, holding declared objectives fixed, because both lower the discounted gain through capacity and so widen the working objective ({{standprice}}, {{chansign}}). A fixed relative preference predicts no such interaction.
- **Test E.** Moving one shared parameter should move signatures in more than one group, where a rule aimed at one signature should not.
- **Test G.** The gap in restraint between wider and narrower objectives should grow with the breakout probability over the range in which the wider objective still sustains restraint. Where a decisive position is worth more than cooperation, the gap closes as $p_d\to1$, since no objective then sustains restraint ({{enforce}}(4)), and this theory and structural realism agree; the test discriminates at intermediate $p_d$ [num: JX].

**What would refute it.** Suppose a system moves every threshold of {{thresholds}} to its safe side for every capable actor. If the signatures then persist unchanged over the dwell time, the claim fails. If moving one shared parameter changes only its own signature over that time, the claim that the conditions interlock fails for that parameter. Conversely, durable safety obtained by constraining conduct while the thresholds stay where they were, maintained as stakes and speed grow and classified as such before the outcome is known, would refute the claim that the conditions must change.

If the diagnosis holds, the question is what would change it. Part III compares paths by one standard, and takes from this section {{thresholds}}, as the test of what a path changes, and the status column of Section 7.3, as the evidence that bounds the range of uncertainty over which every path is judged.
