## S7. The present trajectory

### Proofs of {{branches}}, {{raceselects}} and {{racepremise}}

*{{branches}}.* Each item of Branch I is the cited result under its own condition; the branch adds nothing to them. Branch I′: by definition of the saturating level, advantage above it changes no outcome, so a deviation that reaches it yields no absorbing position worth more than cooperation, $D_i\le C_i$ above the level and $p_d=0$ for the part of the contest beyond it; {{restraint}} then applies. Hazards in development are the growth channel of Section 5.3, which does not depend on whether advantage is decisive, so {{coverage}} applies to them under its premises. Branch II: where punishment is triggered by detection and acts before decisiveness, $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ ({{detectlink}}), so detection and response faster than a lead becomes decisive keeps $p_d$ small and {{restraint}}'s first case holds; the time at which delegation becomes dominant recedes because the gain from speed is bounded by the time a decision has before it is answered ({{delegate}}); modes with $a\tau_c<1$ are within proportional correction by {{tempo}}; and backward induction in the multi-period bargaining model of S5 shows that a shift in advantage spread over enough periods gives no motive for attack, since at every date the value of fighting now falls below the value of the next offer ({{preventattack}}, S5: with $w$ falling from 0.5 to 0 over $N$ periods, $c_A=0.05$ and $\delta=0.8$, attack is rational at the start for $N\le3$ and never for $N\ge4$). Branch III: intensity, dissipation and shared loss are proportional to $r$ by {{boundary}}(1) and {{dissipate}}(1); with $p_d=0$ no breakout term enters {{eq:enforceeq}}. That low decisiveness alone does not place a contest in Branch III follows from {{loopgain}}(1), since the channels add: with $r=0.8$ in the capacity channel and a reflexive channel of rule support, $\Gamma=0.8+0.3=1.1$ and the relative gap grows [num: LG]; where rivals respond, the equilibrium gain decides, and in the contests computed it exceeds the structural one wherever a lead discourages the follower ({{eqgain}}) [num: MP]. $\square$

*{{raceselects}}.* The first sentence is the commitment problem: a winner that could bind itself in advance to limits on the use of a decisive position would have no one to enforce the bond, and promises about conduct after a shift in power are not credible to the party that will lose (Fearon 1995; Powell 2006), with uncertainty about intentions compounding it (Mearsheimer 2001). An actor that decides by its worst case over a stated set of possibilities, and applies that rule to every action (Gilboa and Schmeidler 1989), counts the worst winner; a survival-first actor need not, and the proposition does not need it to. The second sentence is Bayes' rule. Write $E$ for the event that a contestant extends dominance once it holds it, and $W$ for the event that it wins. If restraint while ahead lowers the probability of winning, $\Pr(W\mid E)>\Pr(W\mid\neg E)$, and since $\Pr(W)$ is a mixture of the two, $\Pr(E\mid W)=\Pr(W\mid E)\Pr(E)/\Pr(W)>\Pr(E)$. Repeated selection compounds it: by {{load}}, under payoff-monotone selection the share of the conduct that does not restrain grows along every path, so the shift at each round starts from a distribution already shifted. The third sentence carries the inequality from conduct before victory to conduct after it, which needs the stated correlation: a contestant selected for not restraining itself while ahead is, by premise, less likely to restrain itself once no one can make it. The fourth sentence is Section 2.3 applied to the winner: a winner is a field of carriers under the same selection, so what it does after victory is decided by which of its parts the contest promoted, and by its remaining dependence on the governed, which sets what it gives in {{wingives}} and is the crux C13. $\square$

*{{racepremise}}.* {{join}}(1) gives the individual best response: restraint is never a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$, and in Branch II without timely detection the effective $p_d$ is not small, so the repetition case of {{restraint}} does not apply. {{wingives}} gives the first half of the collective course, since after a decisive win what everyone else receives is set by the winner's objective alone, and {{raceselects}} gives the second. The final sentence is a value judgment: that a course which leaves every participant's protection to a winner's objective, and selects winners whose objectives give least, cannot be justified as a collective course rests on V1 (no actor's victory is an adequate criterion of success), V2 (O1 and O2 protected first) and V3 (those affected keep a capacity to contest). An actor that rejects V1–V3 can accept the formal part and reject the conclusion. $\square$

*Reading the matrix.* Every sign in the "Model" column is proved in the model named [thm]. Where the interface column reads "none", the parameter and the threshold live in one model, and the arrow is proved inside it. Where it names an interface, the arrow is proved inside its model and the join is a hypothesis, which is why Table {{#thresholds}} marks those cells. Three of the joins carry most of the weight: *loop gain and breakout probability*, through which compounding reaches the enforcement condition, and whose linearized form the reduced joint model of S5 exercises [num: JX(f), JX(g)]; *loop gain and working objectives*, through which compounding reaches $\bar p_d$ and talk, first order with others' conduct fixed, and amplified where rivals respond ({{eqgain}}); and *stocks and stage payoffs*, which is stated here as an assumption because no model in this paper derives the stage game of Section 5.2 from the stock dynamics of Section 2.2. That the parameters move together in the world, so that growth moves several thresholds at once, is conjecture C0, which Tests Γ and E address.

### The mechanism index

The loops of Section 7.1 act through mechanisms that overlap; each is stated separately because each has its own formal anchor, measure, counter-mechanism and analogue in other substrates. They concern three objects that reinforce one another without being interchangeable: what actors value (accounting and boundary), what actors believe (observation, attribution and language), and what institutions reward, permit and protect (compounding, selecting the selectors, decisiveness and tempo).
