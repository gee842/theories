# Part II. Diagnosis: the present trajectory

Part II asks whether the present trajectory meets the conditions whose consequences Part I proved. Its claims are observations with dated rows in the evidence register, read by one standard for every actor; its one formal result classifies the contest by the terms of the enforcement condition.

## 7. The present trajectory

> **Claim.** Part I's thresholds turn on a few shared parameters, above all the share of capacity that has to be won, the convertibility of success into control, and decisiveness [thm, cell by cell]. The present trajectory's dynamics are signatures of the conditions, with named gaps in the evidence [obs]. That the thresholds move together under growth is the conjecture the diagnosis tests [conj]. The premise that motivates the race describes the case in which repetition stops enforcing restraint [thm∣prem]; it cannot justify the race as a collective course, and it is a reason to prepare custody now [val].
>
> **Why.** Each threshold of Part I is one inequality in a specified model, and the same parameters enter several of them, so a change in one moves several [thm]. Whether their conditions hold now, and whether the parameters move together in the world, is a question of evidence, applied with one standard to every actor [val].

Three parameters appear in most rows: the share of capacity that has to be won, the convertibility of success into control, and decisiveness. That is the formal sense in which the conditions are interconnected: not a single pressure from which every signature follows, but a few parameters on which several thresholds turn, so that a change in one moves several [thm]. Each cell is a result in its own model; the cells marked ‡ transfer a sign across models through an assumption that S13 lists, and S7 gives the matrix of parameters against thresholds with four entries for every arrow: what is moved, what is held fixed, which model supplies the sign, and which interface is unproved. That the parameters move together in the world, so that growth moves the thresholds together, is the conjecture C0, tested in Section 7.4 [conj].

Five entries carry the argument. **Provision not won in the contest lowers, through capacity, the structural gain, the drift of the working objective and the breakout probability at once** ({{chansign}}(3), {{standprice}}, {{tdec}}): the formal content of the image of trees, and the single lever that acts on both blades of the scissors, since the breakout arm and the objective arm turn on the won share $\omega$; in the reduced joint model of S5, provision that grows with the stakes holds both blades still, and fixed provision lets them cross [num: JX(f)]. In a level channel such as rule support, provision adds the same to everyone and changes nothing; there the levers are correction and non-appropriability. **Correction acts on conversion, not on decay**: it lowers the gain of rule support and the row sums of the correctors, while faster decay of capacity, a term common to all, is invisible to a contest of ratios and raises the discounted gain ({{chansign}}(2)); a shared symbol is not a shared mechanism, and S7 gives the two as separate rows. **Diffusion lowers the gain in every channel**, and a measure that blocks it in order to keep a lead raises the gain it means to exploit [thm]. **Patience enters two rows with opposite signs**: it raises the discounted gain and with it the price of standing, and it strengthens the repetition on which enforcement rests. **Promised sharing raises the danger in enforcement**, because a decisive winner can break the promise ({{wingives}}), while contemporaneous sharing lowers the gain from deviating.

**The four loops, in the author's words.** The corpus states the dynamic as four loops [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. *Compounding*: "If a system reward[s the] winner more edge to win, eventually the system go[es] into winner takes all": what is won is kept and used to win more. *Capture of the rules, and adopt or be out*: the successful reach the positions where rules are written and rewrite them, so that "other agents face a brutal choice: either adopt the same reductionist optimization or face extinction", and "many are also unconsciously captured by the dominant value paradigm". *Naturalized narrative*: "as they accumulate more, they create and slowly dominate the narrative, so its environment becomes more leaning to construct other subjects with this narrative", until the next iteration "take[s] it as 'human nature', as granted"; "winner takes all, but also winner justifies all". *Regulator lag*: every society keeps a regulator against compounding selfishness, its consensus code of norms, laws and shared understanding, and "the speed of update of the consensus code … has to be able to keep up with the pace of change and complexity of the society, in order to keep selfishness in check"; the lag is produced by the first three loops, and it is the window in which they run unchecked. Three intensifiers act on all four: paradigms converge, as "more and more agents will also becoming single value optimizers through iteration" [corpus: selfishness-spiral], which removes the plurality that independent correction needs; as fewer accumulate more, more compete over less; and harm and capture reinforce each other, so that a crisis becomes a new field of accumulation.

**The formal correspondence.** The four loops differ in kind. Compounding and capture are reinforcing channels that add in one structural loop gain and share one state, the concentration of standing ({{loopgain}}(1)): they run in parallel, not in series [thm]. The naturalized narrative is a reinforcing loop stated as a conjecture, since frames are carriers only by hypothesis (Section 5.4) [conj]. Regulator lag is the balancing loop of correction, and the first three loops act on its two properties: capture lowers its gain, by lowering the liability a captured rule domain imposes and the share of evaluation that reports the host's condition ({{certificate}}, {{reabsorb}}), and tempo, masking and the alienation of language lengthen its delay, by shortening the time correction has, hiding the harm that would start it, and delaying the recognition that would mobilize it ({{tempo}}, {{masking}}, {{talk}}) [thm, each in its model]. The danger is a shift of loop dominance from the balancing loop to the reinforcing ones, across delays that run from the turnover of capacity, about $1/(d_K+\delta_f)$, through rule support and the adoption of a practice, to the formation of minds, which is generational. A balancing channel that answers late is not a negative term of $\Gamma$: with a reinforcing channel of gain $0.5$ and a countervailing one of gain $-0.05$ that acts through a slow stock, the net gain is $0.25$, below one, and deviations still grow, as an oscillation of period about 52 rounds that reaches 292 times its start after 600 [num: CH(e)] ({{delayosc}}). What the regulator does to the loop is a question of its delay, not only of its gain, which is the author's fourth loop stated as a property of the second.

**The drive, the driver and the substrate.** The *drive* is what raises the weight on winning and shortens the horizon: above all, the belief that one is in a struggle for survival, and the fear of falling behind it produces, which makes every participant faster. The mechanism is totalization: an actor that believes it is in a survival state subordinates every other value it holds to the contest, and a culture that believes it mostly does the same; in the corpus this belief is "the attractor of attractors" (如果相信自己在生存状态的文化，attractor 大多都会 totalize，这是一个 meta attractor 的 attractor) [corpus: meta-attractor]. The drive is not a factor of $\Gamma$. Part of it is endogenous to the loop: compounding sets the working objective through $\theta_\Gamma$ ({{standprice}}), so a high discounted gain produces the conduct of a struggle for survival in actors who hold no such belief [thm]; part is held, and whether provision beneath the game lowers it is crux C8 [conj]. Among persons the desire for status, respect freely conferred, is a fundamental motive (Anderson, Hildreth and Howland 2015) with two routes, dominance and prestige (Henrich and Gil-White 2001); the pressure channels it into dominance [conj], and a generative order can channel it into prestige for contribution that does not convert into control (EV16; V5). The *driver* is technology, AI above all, which moves the parameters of every row of the table, in either direction (Section 2.4). The *substrate* is the shared host.

### 7.2 Contest branches: what the premise of the race can and cannot justify

The three parts of the race's premise (Section 5.1) define four branches, and the terms of the enforcement condition say which branch a contest is in. **Branch III**, not decisive: no lead pays more than it costs to hold, so the structural gain and, where rivals respond, the equilibrium gain are below one, and $p_d=0$. Low decisiveness alone is not enough for this branch, since reflexive channels add to the gain: with $r=0.8$ a channel of rule support lifts $\Gamma$ to $1.1$ [num: LG]. **Branch I′**, decisive but saturating: racing buys a saturating level of advantage, and above it $p_d=0$. **Branch II**, decisive but slow: detection and response arrive before a lead becomes decisive, $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ near one, so the effective $p_d$ stays small. **Branch I**, decisive, fast and unsaturated: $p_d\to1$.

**{{!Proposition:branches}} (Classification of contest branches) [thm, each branch citing its result].** *Let AI be an instrument of the contest, and let the branch be set by the terms of {{eq:enforceeq}} and the saturating level.*
- *In Branch I, unilateral restraint is not a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$, while joining buys nothing materially in the fully dissipative limit ({{join}}); racing dissipates the prize, the more the more decisive the advantage ({{dissipate}}); delegation becomes dominant as speed grows in additive stakes, and is never chosen by actors who price in a certain lock ({{delegate}}, {{delegatelock}}); hazard modes beyond the robust reach of correction fail unless slowed ({{tempo}}); and a coming shift in advantage gives a rational motive for preventive attack ({{preventattack}}) [thm].*
- *In Branch I′ the contest has an object up to the saturating level and none beyond: above that level further advantage changes no outcome, so $p_d=0$ there and restraint is enforceable by repetition ({{restraint}}), while hazards in development remain and {{coverage}} applies to them on any continuation branch [thm].*
- *In Branch II the time at which delegation becomes dominant recedes ({{delegate}}); hazard modes with $a\tau_c<1$ stay within proportional correction ({{tempo}}); a shift in advantage spread over enough periods gives no motive for preventive attack ({{preventattack}}); and with $p_d$ small, restraint is enforceable by repetition among actors with $\theta>-1$ ({{restraint}}): correction and bargaining can work [thm].*
- *In Branch III intensity, dissipation and shared loss are proportional to $r$ ({{boundary}}(1), {{dissipate}}): what vanishes is the object of the race, not every incentive to compete [thm].*

*Scope.* Membership is set by quantities that are measured, not assumed: the decisive level, the detection lag and the saturating level (cruxes C5, C7), and a technology can move a contest between branches. The branches collect results proved elsewhere; nothing here is proved beyond them. *Proof:* S7.

Branch I′ has a precedent: nuclear advantage saturated at a survivable second strike, and deterrence among rivals stabilized there, while hazards in development remained [obs: X11]. Whether advantage in AI saturates is not known; the register records no evidence either way (gap 15), and Section 11.5 states the consequence conditionally.

The race also selects which winner arrives.

**{{!Proposition:raceselects}} (What the race selects) [thm∣prem].** *No actor can rely on a winner's stated limits on its use of decisive advantage: the commitment problem (Fearon 1995; Powell 2006) [thm]. If restraint while ahead lowers the probability of winning, then $\Pr(\text{extends}\mid\text{wins})>\Pr(\text{extends})$ for conduct before victory, and repeated selection compounds the shift ({{load}}) [thm]. Under the premise that restraint before victory and after it are positively correlated, the race therefore shifts the distribution of winners toward those that extend dominance after victory [thm∣prem]. What a winner does after victory depends on selection inside it, because the winner is itself a field of agencies, firms, laboratories and systems, and on how far it still depends on those it has come to govern ({{wingives}}; crux C13).*

*Scope.* The premise is stated as one; its base rate is not established (crux C4; Test I). *Proof:* S7.

The last clause is where "our aligned AI is us" fails from the other side: aligning a system to an institution is not aligning it to the people the institution serves, and an institution whose dependence on them AI removes is a winner whose restraint nothing enforces (Davidson, Finnveden and Hadshar 2025; Kulveit et al. 2025).

**{{!Corollary:racepremise}} (What the premise of the race cannot justify).** *In Branch I, or in Branch II without timely detection, racing is each actor's best response in its objective to a racing rival ({{join}}) [thm]. The race as a collective course leaves everyone's protection after a win to the winner's objective ({{wingives}}) and, under the premise of {{raceselects}}, shifts winners toward those whose objectives give least [thm∣prem].* Therefore the premise of the race cannot justify the race as a collective course [val: V1–V3].

*Proof:* S7.

The premise that a rival might win decisively describes exactly the case in which repetition stops enforcing restraint. There restraint rests on one of two things ({{restraint}}): keeping breakouts improbable, by not building the decisive class, by custody, or by detection faster than breakout; or objectives under which restraint is already a stage equilibrium. The race supplies neither. The first is the institutional form of the selection criterion of Section 3.7, a selection that counts the system; the second needs objectives wider than accuracy alone supplies (Section 5.1), unless coupling grows with power (C11). In neither case is "we are one" a parameter the result sets. **Oneness is what these requirements are requirements of**: one host, coupled actors, and no outside to absorb the failure. And the premise, taken seriously, is the reason to prepare common custody now and to commit its activation in advance: the trigger would fire where restraint is least enforceable ({{enforce}}(4)), and a decision taken then faces the condition it is meant to prevent (Section 12.1).

Present evidence suggests that decisive advantage has not arrived: leads are short on every measure, open models trail closed ones by about four months, and algorithmic efficiency diffuses. Whether the lead has changed hands depends on the measure: by one composite index the frontier has stayed with developers in one state, while on a preference leaderboard the top position has been traded and the gap is a few per cent [obs: EV5, EV9; C5]. The evidence cuts both ways. Short leads support enforcement: no breakout has occurred, enforced cooperation remains available, and common custody is a preparation, not an emergency, with the window to arrange it still open. Short leads also place the contest near parity, and near parity is where the price of standing is highest: in the two-actor contests computed, where a lead discourages the follower, the price at parity exceeds its structural value by about 1.05 to 7.9 times, while a laggard at a quarter share prices standing at 25 to 57 per cent of the structural value, and below zero with a shared loss ({{eqgain}}) [num: MP]. The observation that argues against present decisiveness therefore also says that the working objectives of the actors nearest the top are now the narrowest. Oneness is a property of the system in every branch; what changes with the branch is the institutional form its accurate recognition requires. Readers in international relations will find the theory's nearest neighbour in defensive realism, which it extends by a hazard in development that endangers its holder and departs from by treating self-help as a selected institution (Jervis 1978; Wendt 1992); where offense has the advantage and is hard to tell from defense, AI sits in the doubly dangerous quadrant of that framework (X1).

The signatures sustain one another, the loops of Section 7.1 read at the level of what can be observed: W supplies the success that C converts into control; C removes the correction that would make the shared visible; N makes cooperation look naive, which keeps W in place. T6 is not merely a prejudice: where racing is dominant, or among purely relative actors, restraint is unenforceable ({{relpunish}}), and the remedy is a change in what is selected, not a better argument. Nor is it merely correct: the belief makes part of its own truth. In the author's words, "if we don't believe that others will also compete and play this winner takes all game, even if we don't ourselves, and not participating means losing, and we can not lose but keeps on going, this kind of thinking itself creates the trap and reality" [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. The trajectory is not uniform: seeds of cooperation exist on both sides, in different documents, and the question is whether they become binding and broader in time. The reinvestment of success in influence over rules, the link to Section 4, is now visible as political spending in both directions, but it remains the least measured, since what Section 4 predicts is appropriable support scaling with share; and capability growth is measured, with the longest-horizon series at the limit of its instrument, while the growth of hazardous behaviour and residual risk is not, which is why Section 12 treats it as the central crux.

### 7.4 Which layer, and how the diagnosis could fail

The diagnosis is at the layer of mechanism ({{meta}}(1)), partly evidenced; whether the present configuration is attracting or a long transient is open (Test D). That the signatures load on one latent factor is a research question, not a registered prediction. What is registered is what the conditions predict and their absorbed components do not (TESTS lists who else predicts each). **Test Γ.** The observable is the ratio of conduct's response to shared harm and to private cost, which in the contest of Section 3.4 equals $1+\theta$ for the working self-model acted on ({{boundary}}) [thm; num: SP(G)]. It should rise with provision not won in the contest relative to the stakes, and with diffusion, holding declared objectives fixed, because provision lowers the won share and with it the discounted gain that narrows the working objective; in one computed case it rises from $0.58$ to $0.81$ as the won share falls from one to a half [num: SP(H)]. A fixed relative preference predicts no such interaction. **Test E.** Moving one shared parameter should move signatures in more than one group, where a rule aimed at one signature should not; this is the test of C0. **Test G.** The gap in restraint between wider and narrower objectives should grow with the breakout probability over the range in which the wider objective still sustains restraint; where a decisive position is worth more than cooperation, the gap closes as $p_d\to1$, where no objective sustains restraint and this theory and structural realism agree, so the test discriminates at intermediate $p_d$ [num: JX(c)].

**What would refute it.** Suppose a system moves every threshold of Table {{#thresholds}} to its safe side for every capable actor, by the marks that Section 8.2 lists. If the signatures then persist unchanged over the dwell time of Section 11.4, the claim fails. If moving one shared parameter changes only its own signature over that time, the claim that the conditions interlock fails for that parameter. Conversely, durable safety obtained by constraining conduct while the thresholds stay where they were, maintained as stakes and speed grow and classified as such before the outcome is known, would refute the claim that the conditions must change.

If the diagnosis holds, the question is what would change it. The status column of Section 7.3 is what the range of uncertainty of Section 8.1 has to be consistent with on every path, and Part III compares the paths by one standard.
