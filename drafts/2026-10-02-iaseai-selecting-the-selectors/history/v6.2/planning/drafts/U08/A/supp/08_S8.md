## S8. Comparing paths

### Proof of Lemma {{#fallback}}

Couple the two paths by a common uniform draw $\mathsf u_t$ each period: the event occurs on a path at $t$ when $\mathsf u_t$ falls below that path's conditional hazard given the shared world history. On every history on which both have survived to $t$, the two conditions give $A$'s hazard at most $C$'s, so $\{A\text{ fails at }t\}\subseteq\{C\text{ fails at }t\}$. By induction the event "$C$ survives to $H$" is contained in "$A$ survives to $H$", path by path, whatever the rule by which $A$ dissolves, so $\Phi^A(H)\ge\Phi^C(H)$. $\square$

*A step that fails.* Comparing after conditioning on the dissolution date, $\Phi^A(H\mid\tau)\ge\Phi^C(H)$ for every $\tau$, is not valid when dissolution depends on the state. In a two-state Markov world in which bad states make dissolution more likely, the conditional comparison fails (0.291 against 0.324 at $H=30$, conditioning on dissolution at $t=1$) while the coupled comparison holds at horizons 10, 30 and 100; an exact dynamic programme over (world state, arrangement active, each path alive) with a common uniform gives $\Phi^A=0.884,0.551,0.077$ against $\Phi^C=0.813,0.461,0.062$ at those horizons [num: SM(4), SM(14)]. If $C$'s hazard is a deterministic schedule, the two conditions need only be stated on $A$'s histories. *Example [num: FD].* With dissolution at 2% a period and a fallback that restarts continuation ten periods later, the arrangement survives with probability 0.976, 0.864 and 0.211 at horizons 10, 30 and 100, against 0.875, 0.575 and 0.026 for continuation.

### Survival at a common horizon

**{{!Proposition:survassure}} (Assurance in survival terms at a common horizon) [thm].** *Survival is computed with per-period conditional probabilities over a common horizon $H$. Let $h_R$ be the hazard under restraint, $q_{\rm I}$ the loss of the ability to overrule by delegation, $q_{\rm II}$ the shared hazard, $q_W$ the hazard of preventive attack, $p_{\rm dec}$ and $p^S_{\rm dec}$ the probability that a racer's advantage becomes decisive when all race and when the actor stays out, and $p_{\rm ext}$ the actor's belief that a decisive winner would extend its dominance over it. With independence, $\Phi^R=1-h_R$, $\Phi^T=(1-q_{\rm I})(1-q^T_{\rm II})(1-q_W)$, $\Phi^P=(1-q_{\rm I})(1-q^P_{\rm II})(1-q_W)(1-p_{\rm dec}\tfrac{n-1}n)$ and $\Phi^S=(1-q^S_{\rm II})(1-p^S_{\rm dec}p_{\rm ext})$. (1) The advantage of cooperating is $\varpi(\Phi^R-\Phi^T)+(1-\varpi)(\Phi^S-\Phi^P)$: dominance, a stag hunt with threshold $(\Phi^P-\Phi^S)/((\Phi^R-\Phi^T)+(\Phi^P-\Phi^S))$, chicken, or a prisoner's dilemma according to the signs. (2) Where $\Phi^S\ge\Phi^P$ and $\Phi^R\ge\Phi^T$, an actor that holds the prediction restrains whatever it believes about the other. (3) Mutual racing is a strict equilibrium at every horizon with $\Phi^P_H>\Phi^S_H$. (4) For constant hazards, $\Phi^B(H)/\Phi^R(H)=((1-q_B)/(1-q_R))^H$. (5) If an actor gives the premises of Section 5.3 probability $p_{\rm prem}$, the classification at horizon $H$ uses mixtures, and when the arrangement's own survival also decays the signs can fail at long horizons.*

*Proof:* the advantage is linear in $\varpi$; substitute. $\square$ *Numbers* ($n=2$, $p_{\rm ext}=1$) [num: `../v4/checks_v44.py`, the survival-classification block; `../v5/checks_v5.py`, SR]: with $q_{\rm I}=0.6$, shared hazards 0.3 (0.4 when all race), $q_W=0.2$, $p_{\rm dec}=0.6$ and $h_R=0.1$, the survivals $\Phi^R,\Phi^T,\Phi^P,\Phi^S$ are 0.90, 0.22, 0.13 and 0.28, and cooperation is dominant; with low forced delegation the game is a stag hunt with threshold 0.16; for a leader that wins with probability 0.8 if it races, the threshold is 0.42 when the shared hazard is 0.3 and 0.11 when it is 0.8: for the leader, the shared hazard carries the argument. With $p_{\rm prem}=0.99$, the arrangement's survival $e^{-0.005H}$ and race branches decaying at 0.05 a period when the premises hold, the arrangement beats the mixed race at $H=10$, 100 and 600, by 0.34, 0.59 and 0.04, but not at 1,000, where both are near zero and racing is dominant.

*Discounting.* With utility linear in consumption, discount rate $\rho$, a constant race hazard $h$ and growth rates $g_r$ racing and $g_p$ paced (pacing taken as hazard-free), the race is worth $c_0/(\rho+h-g_r)$ and pacing $c_0/(\rho-g_p)$, so the race is preferred iff $h<g_r-g_p$, though it ends in catastrophe with certainty; a rising hazard reverses the preference only if it comes to exceed the growth differential [thm]. This is our derivation. It is the special case of Jones's (2024) trade-off, $v(c^\ast)=g/\delta$ with $v(c)=u(c)/(u'(c)c)$, in which the value of a year of life does not rise with consumption. With curvature $\gamma\ge1$ it does, and Jones's planner stops: at a 1% annual hazard and 10% growth, with log utility after about 40 years at about one-in-three cumulative risk, with $\gamma=2$ after about 4.5 years at about 4%; at a 2% hazard, with log utility, at once. Jones also finds that mortality improvements from AI raise the acceptable risk (to about 25% for $\gamma=2$ or 3), which belongs to crux C10, and that absent them a lower social discount rate lowers the acceptable risk toward zero [obs: EV11].

### The alternatives: results stated briefly in the core

**{{!Remark:lead}} (What a lead buys).** (1) With diffusion at rate $\delta_f$, a lead $\Delta_0$ held by a leader that pauses obeys $\dot\Delta=-\delta_f\Delta-1$ and is exhausted after $\delta_f^{-1}\ln(1+\delta_f\Delta_0)<\Delta_0$: a six-month lead diffusing at 0.1 a month lasts 4.7 months, a one-year lead 7.9 months, a two-year lead 12.2 months [num: LD, `../v4/checks_v45.py`]. (2) The margin is time, but what it must buy is coverage, which rises by about $\dot U\Delta$ while uncovered variety grows; for hazard classes with an evidence lag above $\Delta$ it buys nothing. (3) Whether it suffices is judged by the leader's own evaluators under time pressure. (4) In a static contest the follower raises effort when the leader slows (Dixit 1987); in dynamic races a large lead discourages it (Harris and Vickers 1987; Konrad and Kovenock 2009), and a leader that sees its window closing has a motive to strike first (Van Evera 1999). The commitment result of Bueno de Mesquita, Dziuda and Polborn (2026) is local and does not depend on holding a lead: whoever can commit first to a slightly slower speed gains, because the others slow. On their own median estimates, the planners of one detailed scenario judge that spending part of a lead buys about a third of the reduction in takeover risk that a coordinated slowdown buys (EV10) [obs].

**{{!Remark:sprint}} (The finite sprint as a trade-off).** A finite sprint trades four quantities: exposure during and after it, zero after a freeze if the hazardous ratio built is at most one ({{freeze}}); erosion of the frozen edge as rivals developing within understanding catch up; rivals' responses, which cannot verify which kind of winner they face; and maintenance of the advantage, which requires stopping others' growth. The last is where the mechanism bites. A stop imposed by the sprinter on everyone else is a common constraint of the most unequal incidence: it is selected against and read as a bid for a lead ({{blind}}(2)), and under relative accounting the others' best reply is prevention or imitation, not compliance. The sprinter that holds the frozen decisive class is the largest conversion target the theory describes, since the gain from converting its own evaluators scales with its share ({{influence}}; {{corrrate}}(4)), and no corrector outside it certifies them. Equal incidence and uncaptured correction are the two properties the sprint would need, and they are what Sections 3 and 4 show erode. Common custody holds the same class unraced; it differs on D4, correctors of different lineage and funding certified outside the holder, and on D5, the strongest participant's violation answered by dependence caps and standby rather than by the violator's own judgment; X8 compares the two row by row. The sprint can preserve the sprinter's survival while giving up permanent superiority. Excluding it as a deviation from a cooperative arrangement needs its net deviation payoff to be negative ({{participate}}).

**Proposition {{#preventown}} in full.** *Facing a rival it believes certain to race, and comparing by its own survival: (1) a single strike that succeeds with probability $s_P$, returning the field to something like the arrangement, and escalates to catastrophe with probability $\varepsilon_P$, gives $(1-\varepsilon_P)[s_P\Phi^R+(1-s_P)\Phi^P]$, and beats staying out and racing unless $\varepsilon_P\ge1-\max(\Phi^S,\Phi^P)/(s_P\Phi^R+(1-s_P)\Phi^P)$; (2) sustained prevention against a target that rebuilds with probability at least $b_P$ a period, each strike escalating with probability at least $\varepsilon_{\min}$, has per-period hazard at least $b_P\varepsilon_{\min}$; (3) at common horizons, sustained prevention with hazard 0.01 beats racing with 0.05: 0.90 against 0.60 at $H=10$, 0.60 against 0.08 at $H=50$, 0.13 against 0.00 at $H=200$.* *Proof:* compare and solve; {{nonsum}}; $0.99^H>0.95^H$. $\square$ With $\Phi^R=1$, $s_P=0.7$, $\varepsilon_P=0.2$ and race branches near zero, the strike gives 0.56 [num: FS, `../v5/checks_v5.py`]. Shifts that cannot be observed raise the chance of mistaken prevention (Debs and Monteiro 2014); verification that supports deterrence can also supply targets (Coe and Vaynman 2020). An actor that manipulates shared risk as an instrument (Schelling 1966) plays chicken in survival terms, with mixed equilibria and wars of attrition. When the prior that a capable rival holds an unbounded relative objective is low, a survival-first actor's best course is to propose verifiable limits and to take narrow E1 verifiably in its own loops, the costly signal of {{signal}}.

**{{!Remark:insure}} (Why insuring against falling behind is not an exit).** Bounding the penalty for falling behind with autonomous, good-enough provision and defence does not leave the contest, for three reasons. The penalty is set by the winner, not the laggard, so the bound is as reliable as the belief that a winner would not extend its dominance; a winner with a relative objective has a motive to destroy what the laggard secured; and the insurance accepts the frame of the contest, hedging against losing instead of declining the game. Defence that makes decisive advantage unattainable for everyone is a different thing: it lowers decisiveness for all and belongs to E2, if it stays under human oversight. Shared provision belongs to a world that has left the contest, and is G5, not insurance.

### Proofs for the verified agreement

**Proposition {{#assure}} in full.** *Payoffs $R,T,P,S$; a defection detected in time is reversed, the defector receiving $P-F$ and the cooperator $P$; an undetected defection yields $T$ and $S$. With $T_\upsilon=\upsilon(P-F)+(1-\upsilon)T$, $g_C=R-T_\upsilon$ and $g_D=(1-\upsilon)(P-S)$: (1) the advantage of cooperating is $\varpi g_C-(1-\varpi)g_D$, with the four cases of the core and, if both are negative, chicken; (2) the verification and trust conditions as stated; (3) cooperation is risk-dominant iff $g_C>g_D$, a property of payoffs selected under global games and adaptive play (Carlsson and van Damme 1993; Kandori, Mailath and Rob 1993; Young 1993); (4) with $n$ players and independent belief $\varpi_1$ about each rival, $\varpi_1^{n-1}\ge\varpi^\ast$ suffices only if a cooperator gets at least $S$ when some but not all others defect and the advantage of defecting in any such state is at most $g_D$.* *Proof:* (1) cooperating yields $\varpi R+(1-\varpi)(\upsilon P+(1-\upsilon)S)$ and defecting $\varpi T_\upsilon+(1-\varpi)P$; (2) rearrange; (4) bound the intermediate states. Without the second bound the condition fails: with advantages $(1,-10,-1)$ by number of defecting others in a three-player game, each rival cooperating with probability $\sqrt{0.5}$ gives $-3.73$ although all others cooperate with probability 0.5 [num: N13, `../v5/checks_v5.py`]. $\square$ The threshold in (2) is the deterrence condition of the inspection game (Avenhaus, von Stengel and Zamir 2002); what this paper adds is its identity with the enforcement condition below, and the comparative statics of both under growth.

**{{!Proposition:overhang}} (Commitment under a growing overhang) [thm].** *Let $\mathcal O(t)$ be the capability an agreement holds in check, $X$ and $X_{\rm stay}$ the values of exiting first and of staying, exit occur at hazard $\mathcal H$, a fraction $\mu_x$ of the overhang be destroyed on exit, and the expected loss from exit be $\mathcal L=\mathcal H(1-\mu_x)\Lambda_x(\mathcal O)$ with $\Lambda_x$ increasing. (1) Deterring an irreversible, public exit requires $\upsilon F\ge X-X_{\rm stay}$; deterring a reversible covert preparation requires $\upsilon F\ge(1-\upsilon)(X-X_{\rm stay})$; the net exit gain grows with the overhang when leaving with a decisive advantage gains faster than staying with one's share. (2) $\dot{\mathcal L}/\mathcal L=\dot{\mathcal H}/\mathcal H-\dot\mu_x/(1-\mu_x)+\varepsilon_\Lambda\dot{\mathcal O}/\mathcal O$, so growth of the overhang faster than the decline of the exit hazard is not sufficient for the loss to rise. (3) An agreement that preserves relative positions preserves the relative term of every payoff, and with it the motive in $X$.* *Proof:* expected values; differentiate $\ln\mathcal L$ (counter-case: $\mathcal O=e^{2t}$, $\mathcal H=e^{-1.5t}$, $\Lambda_x=\sqrt{\mathcal O}$ and $\mu_x=0$ give $\mathcal L=e^{-0.5t}$); proportional caps leave each relative weight unchanged. $\square$

*Theorem {{#verify}}, by clause.* (1) Rearrange $R>T_\upsilon$. (2) Timely detection is detection before decisiveness, and $T_{\rm dec}$ falls as the loop gain rises ({{tdec}}). (3) Unverifiable information: {{talk}}; future commitments: {{overhang}}(1); the verifier's own reliability: Section 4, since the verifier is a corrector and success can buy it. (4) Bounding the largest undetected defection bounds $T$. The rate clause compares the coalition's advantage in effective compute with its erosion by diffusion; effective compute is algorithmic efficiency times compute, so a coalition that caps compute keeps its bound only while its compute advantage grows as fast as diffusion erodes its algorithmic one. Nothing here derives that it does: the clause is the premise (advantage keeps pace), and the rate of erosion is an observation (EV9). Revealing types is the information role of {{participate}}. $\square$

*The detect link.* Where punishment is triggered by detection and acts before decisiveness, $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, with $\upsilon(T)$ the probability that detection and response occur within $T$ ({{detectlink}}; derivation in S5). Then {{eq:enforceeq}} reads $\upsilon\ge1-\bar p_d$. Under the continuation reading, in which the continuation after a detected defection is the stage game's punishment, $1-\bar p_d$ and $(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ are numerically identical, and the case $T<R$ of {{assure}}(2) is $\bar p_d\ge1$ [num: JX(d)]. The two statements differ in what they take as given: {{assure}} takes the sanction $F$ and the trust $\varpi$; {{eq:enforceeq}} takes a credible continuation $v_i$. With detection as a Poisson clock of mean $\tau$, the simulated probability that a breakout escapes within $T_{\rm dec}$ is $e^{-T_{\rm dec}/\tau}$ within sampling error [num: JX(e)].

*An instance.* One detailed plan among leading powers to slow development and delay superintelligence to about 2040 combines compute declarations, supply-chain accounting, inference-only retrofits and recomputation of workloads, research transparency, mutually assured destruction of compute if the deal declines, and hardened reserves of about two per cent of pre-deal compute; it bounds covert projects rather than requiring near-perfect detection, the fourth route of {{verify}}. One author's scenario judgment puts decline at about four per cent a year in years five to ten, half dissolution and half impairment, cumulatively near half over ten years (EV10) [obs]; X8 gives the durability and failure-mode rows.

### An illustration of the two cruxes

Let the per-period hazard on a path be 0.05 times its uncovered share plus a background 0.001, and on the cooperative path add a dissolution hazard times the loss its fallback carries. Survival at horizons 10, 30 and 100 [num: CMP, `../v5/checks_v5.py`]: continuation with the uncovered share rising from 0.2 by 0.01 a period, 0.871, 0.567, 0.028; continuation with a summable uncovered share, 0.966, 0.941, 0.873; cooperation with a summable share and dissolution 2% a period with fallback loss 0.5, 0.873, 0.696, 0.319; cooperation with dissolution 0.2%, 0.956, 0.913, 0.789. The numbers are illustrative. They show that a well-corrected continuation can beat a fragile arrangement, and that a rising uncovered share makes any modestly durable arrangement better past the near term.

### The failure-mode table: method

The table in X8 that Section 12.1 compares on is built in four steps, the same for every path, its own design included. First, the failure modes are enumerated from two sources: the path's own documents, read for what they say ends or suspends it, and the rule for $\Theta$, which admits every mode a named mechanism of Part I produces and the register does not contradict. Second, each mode is classified as reversible or not. A mode is reversible when a reversal exists that is named, stops short of O1 and O2, and is executed by someone other than the party whose failure it is; "the arrangement is renegotiated" is a reversal only if the documents say who can compel it. A mode is irreversible when it is a decisive position ({{wingives}}), a crossing of the host's floor ({{safeguards}}(3)), the loss of informed veto over a delegation ({{delegatelock}}), or an escalation under {{preventown}}; these are the exits from the correctable kernel of Definition {{#kernel}}. Third, each mode is given the measurement that would show it under way, drawn from the signatures and cruxes of Sections 7.3 and 12.3, so that the table and the trigger of Section 12.1 use one set of indicators. Fourth, every cell names the kind of evidence behind it, with one standard for every row: the path's own documents, a scenario judgment by its authors, an observation in the register, a result of this paper, or none; a cell with none says so, and the paper's own design is marked by the same rule, since its documents are X10 and its hazard of breakdown has no estimate and no base rate in the register. The reversibility asymmetry on which Section 12.1 rests is read off the second column, mode by mode, not from the labels.
