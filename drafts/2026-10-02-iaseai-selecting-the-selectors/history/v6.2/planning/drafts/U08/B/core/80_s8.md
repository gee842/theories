# Part III. Responses: what would change the trajectory

Part III asks what would change the trajectory. Its standard of comparison is a judgment of value, its descriptions of arrangements are definitions, and its formal results hold in their models; which whole design to prefer is left to Part IV.

## 8. Comparing paths, and the alternatives on their strongest terms

> **Claim.** Paths are compared by one standard that protects irreversible losses first and applies one range of uncertainty to every path, each path's own failure modes included [val]. An arrangement is described by what it changes, not by its name: which thresholds of {{thresholds}} it moves, to which side and for whom, and whether its enforcement survives the arrival of a decisive position [def]. An arrangement whose fallback is no more hazardous than continuation, and whose own hazards while it lasts, capture and collusion included, are no higher, survives at least as well as continuation at every horizon, however its dissolution is decided [thm]. Every alternative that does not change what winning confers, met at its strongest, keeps some threshold on its dangerous side for some capable actor, each in the model of the result that shows it [thm∣prem]. That arrangements which constrain conduct while leaving the thresholds where they were erode as stakes and speed grow, while those that move them for every capable actor persist, is a registered prediction [conj].
>
> **Why.** Labels such as "regulation", "cooperation" or "agreement" decide nothing: every arrangement that changes the map from conduct to share, resources and standing changes selection, and what decides is the direction, for whom, and whether the change survives success. A lead decays and is judged by its holder; a winner's restraint after victory rests on its need for the governed; a freeze after a sprint is a stop of the most unequal incidence, held by the largest conversion target; verification bounds defection and leaves the value of winning in place.

### 8.1 The standard of comparison

**The protection standard [val].** Each candidate path is evaluated at common horizons, near (about five years), medium (about twenty) and long (fifty or more), over one justified range $\Theta$ of uncertain parameters applied to every path. First, bound the probability of O1 and O2 within each horizon over $\Theta$. Second, among paths whose bound stays below a stated tolerance, prefer the one with greater useful benefit, more fairly distributed, that keeps more options of correction open. Third, if no path meets the tolerance, prefer the one with the lowest bound that keeps options open. This is robust satisficing on irreversible losses, a normative choice, and the criteria it declines rank paths otherwise. An expected-value criterion can favour a small probability of a very large loss when the expected benefit is large. Under discounted expected utility the acceptable existential risk turns on how fast the value of a year of life rises with consumption: at a 1% annual hazard and 10% growth, a planner with logarithmic utility accepts about a one-in-three cumulative risk for a 55-fold gain in consumption, and with curvature 2 about 4% (Jones 2024) [obs: EV11]; if utility is linear in consumption, so that the value of life does not rise with it, a course is preferred whenever the hazard it adds is below the growth it adds, though it ends in catastrophe with certainty (S8) [thm]. O1 protects catastrophic, common-cause losses of life, and the standard does not discount them; deaths from benefits forgone are counted as a cost of the path, not as O1, and their weight is the normative crux N1, with the margin that only the decisive class could supply measured as crux C10.

**The range $\Theta$ [val].** A parameter value enters $\Theta$ if it is consistent with the dated evidence of the register and is produced by a stated mechanism whose premises are named, with a precedent or base rate where one exists. A value that needs a premise the register contradicts is excluded. The same rule applies to every path: a rising uncovered share on the continuation path enters, because its premises are named mechanisms the register does not contradict (EV5–EV7); and equally the capture of a common custodian, dissolution, coercive enforcement, the interruption of services and collusion among checks enter on the cooperative path, with the failure modes of every alternative of Section 8.3 (DISCUSSION X8). Every entry of $\Theta$ cites its source.

**{{!Definition:kernel}} (The correctable kernel) [def].** *The correctable kernel is the set of states of the world from which some non-anticipative strategy of admissible correction avoids O1 and O2 against every sequence of errors in the justified range. In viability theory it is a discriminating, or guaranteed-viability, kernel of a game against disturbances (Cardaliaguet 1996; Cardaliaguet, Quincampoix and Saint-Pierre 1999), not the viability kernel of undisturbed dynamics.*

Leaving the kernel is the loss of guaranteed correctability, a point of no guaranteed return earlier than the losses themselves. The corpus describes openness by four capacities, each a condition of the kernel [corpus: possibility-space]: *not yet exhausted* (未), no decisive end state reached; *still possible* (可), plurality of lineages, purposes and correctors kept; *still responsive* (应), humans able to suspend, modify or replace; *still able to turn back* (转), reversal possible. A closure made to protect openness, such as a defence or an emergency power, can state what would make it withdraw; a closure that cannot state that has left the kernel. The standard's second step is a preference for staying inside it. This paper computes no kernel, which takes the union over strategies of correction and the intersection over sequences of error; a viable boundary computed for one strategy, as in Section 10.3, is not one.

**{{!Lemma:fallback}} (When an arrangement cannot do worse) [thm].** Two design conditions guarantee that an arrangement does no worse than continuation at any horizon. *Let both paths be driven by the same exogenous world process, the irreversible event on each occurring at date $t$ when a common uniform draw falls below that path's conditional hazard. Assume, on every history on which both have survived: (safe fallback) once arrangement $A$ has dissolved, its hazard is no higher than continuation $C$'s; and (bounded own risk) while $A$ lasts, its hazard, its own hazards of capture, restriction and collusion included, is no higher than $C$'s. Then $A$'s survival is at least $C$'s at every horizon, whatever the rule, possibly endogenous, by which $A$ dissolves.*

*Scope.* Where bounded own risk fails, there is no pointwise dominance and the standard compares the worst cases of both; that is where regulated competition can win. *Proof:* S8, which also shows that comparing after conditioning on the dissolution date fails when dissolution depends on the state [num: SM(4), SM(14), FD].

The two conditions are what the tests D1 and D2 of {{>durability}} read from an arrangement's own documents. An agreement whose fallback is a short sprint from preserved relative standing violates the first; a design whose own concentration, capture or collusion can exceed continuation's hazards violates the second (crux C4).

Comparing by survival does not by itself make restraint an equilibrium. Write $\Phi^R$, $\Phi^T$, $\Phi^P$ and $\Phi^S$ for an actor's probability of surviving to a common horizon when all restrain, when it races against restrainers, when all race, and when it restrains against a racer, each counting the hazards of that branch: delegation's loss of the capacity to overrule, the shared hazard, preventive attack, a rival's decisive win. With trust $\varpi$ that the other restrains, the advantage of restraint is $\varpi(\Phi^R-\Phi^T)+(1-\varpi)(\Phi^S-\Phi^P)$, so the game in survival terms is dominance, a stag hunt, chicken or a prisoner's dilemma by the signs of the two differences; mutual racing stays a strict equilibrium at every horizon at which racing beside a racer survives better than staying out, $\Phi^P>\Phi^S$; and where the arrangement's own survival also decays, signs that hold at short horizons can reverse at long ones (**{{!Proposition:survassure}}**, S8) [thm]. For a leader whose exit would hand a rival a decisive win, the shared hazard is what carries the case for restraint [num: S8, arithmetic of the closed forms].

So paths are compared at common finite horizons, not at an infinite one at which both branches of a hazardous world can tend to zero. The protection standard does not by itself rank continuation with strong correction against a design that changes selection: under the rule for $\Theta$ the worst case of each includes an irreversible loss, so both fail the tolerance there and the comparison falls to the standard's third step. Section 12.1 states the ranking at that step as a judgment, with its reasons, over the failure modes of every path that DISCUSSION X8 lists.

### 8.2 Profiles, not labels

**{{!Definition:profile}} (What an arrangement changes) [def].** *An arrangement is described by a profile: which mechanisms of Part I it neutralizes, weakens, leaves or strengthens, read as the thresholds of {{thresholds}} it moves and the side to which it moves them; its coverage (unilateral, plurilateral, every capable actor); its bindingness (Abbott et al. 2000); whether its enforcement survives a decisive position, and if it prevents one from arriving, by what verified mechanism; whether its beneficiaries can amend it; its fallback and own risk; and its durability. And selection: every arrangement that changes the map from conduct to share, resources and standing changes selection, so what is recorded is the direction, whether it moves the thresholds toward their safe side or away from it, and whether in every capable actor's game or only in rivals'.*

Export controls aimed at preserving a lead change selection, toward a lead: they aim at decisiveness, and blocking diffusion to rivals raises the structural gain {{eq:gamma}} in the channels it reaches. Deterrence by threatened sabotage acts on $p_d$, as custody does, by a different means and with a different fallback.

**The generative direction.** A change of selection in the generative direction has seven marks:
- **G1** consequences return to their source;
- **G2** winning confers reward, not more advantage in the same game;
- **G3** success cannot buy control over its own assessment, and correction stays plural, decorrelated and in common hands;
- **G4** decisive capability is held in common or not built;
- **G5** provision of the conditions of a decent life sits beneath the game;
- **G6** measurement and language are shared;
- **G7** pace is bound to understanding.

Each reverses thresholds of {{thresholds}} [thm, each in the model of its row]: G1 load, and the alignment tax where admission returns a divergence's common cost to its developer; G2 and G5 together compounding and standing, since non-convertible reward works only with provision not won in the contest, G5 through the won share, the pivot of the scissors ({{scissors}}); G3 capture; G4 enforcement, by keeping $p_d$ small for every deviation ({{restraint}}); G6 talk; G7 coverage and tempo and, applied to decisions, delegation, which narrow E1 keeps within the pace of human correction. G2 is the author's rule of game design (Section 2.2), and the seven are the marks that Section 3.7 asks of a selection that counts the system. What this paper argues for is a change with these marks, covering every capable actor, enforceable and durable, whatever it is called.

**{{!Definition:durability}} (Durability tests) [def].** *An arrangement is tested on its own documents and estimates, not on assumptions added by the tester: D1, its fallback if it dissolves; D2, its estimated hazard of breakdown and what drives it; D3, who keeps informed veto, modification, replacement and exit after any delegation; D4, whether its own correctors have full inbound row sums below one; D5, what happens when its strongest participant violates it; D6, succession and the continuity of essential services; D7, common causes and collusion among its checks and systems; D8, whether membership is open or stops at a club.*

The tests apply to every path, the cooperative design of Section 10 included, and by one evidence standard: each entry is read from the arrangement's own documents and estimates, and where those give none, the entry says so (DISCUSSION X8). An arrangement with the form of cooperation that fails D1–D3 on its own estimates may be called a *pseudo-solution*; the label summarizes test results and does not replace them. **Registered prediction [conj]:** arrangements that constrain conduct while leaving the thresholds of {{thresholds}} where they were erode as stakes and speed grow; those that move them to their safe side for every capable actor persist. The classification uses the profile, never the outcome.

### 8.3 The alternatives

Each alternative is stated in its strongest form, on its merits, and answered by mechanism: what remains, which threshold it leaves, and what would show that it suffices. DISCUSSION X8 gives each in full.

**(a) Technical alignment and AI-assisted correction.** *Strongest form:* control scoped to early transformative systems and used under control to accelerate alignment and oversight while the window lasts (Greenblatt, Shlegeris, Sachan and Roger 2024); weak supervisors eliciting part of a stronger model's capability (Burns et al. 2023); auditors with access to training data finding a planted objective that black-box access missed (Marks et al. 2025); oversight with the right topology ({{topology}}); AI-assisted correction keeping pace with cheapening control ({{corrrate}}); research that drives the alignment tax to zero or below, class by class, so that safeguards spread without coordination (Section 6.4); and, in its proponents' own plans, a strong international agreement ranked first. *What remains:* principal alignment does not change the game among principals ({{faithful}}); the window lasts only as long as the race allows; systems are retained for performance ({{retain}}), and progress that lowers the hazard per unit of racing or delegation is absorbed by more of both ({{homeostasis}}); redundant oversight is capped by the common causes selection produces ({{topology}}), and AI-assisted correction is only as independent as its funding, access, appointment and lineage; time is a part of the tax that no technique removes, and masked divergences need capability bounds that no developer can hold alone ({{masked}}); and which divergences are tolerated is set at the outermost loop ({{nested}}). *Profile:* it leaves compounding, standing and enforcement where they were, and changes selection only when held by correctors separated from success, under constraints common to all.

**(b) Regulated competition with strong correction.** *Strongest form:* liability and licensing make consequences return; evaluation access is mandated and thresholds fixed in advance; where the effective hazard is high and capability transparent, pacing can be the unique equilibrium without any agreement (Fudenberg and Koh 2026); civil nuclear power after a major accident, and aviation with independent investigation and insurance pricing, show durable safety with unchanged commercial motives [obs]. *What remains:* coverage across jurisdictions, since the race is set by the most race-inclined actor ({{join}}); the rule-makers are themselves in a contest of standing ({{>nounilateral}}); liability cannot exceed the assets of the liable (Shavell 1986), so a catastrophic or common harm cannot be turned wholly into a private cost; two developers' frameworks condition commitments on rivals, and one unreviewed study codes 77% of the changes it traced, in twelve pairs of consecutive framework versions, as weakening [obs: EV2]; and the precedents had no decisive prize. Pacing needs two things, a hazard that the racer itself bears and transparency; G1 and G6 secure them, and the conditions of winning erode them: relative accounting weighs a shared hazard by $1+\theta$, and not at all at $\theta=-1$ ({{twochan}}), and quiet deviations, captured evaluation and masked divergences defeat transparency ({{enforce}}, {{masking}}, {{masked}}; DISCUSSION X8). *Profile:* it depends on content. Regulation that returns consequences and separates evaluators from success, across every capable actor, is the change this paper argues for, whatever it is called; regulation that constrains conduct while success can still buy the rule-makers is predicted to erode as stakes and speed grow.

**(c) Leadership and a safety margin.** *Strongest form:* a leader spends part of its lead on safety; a large lead can discourage a follower (Harris and Vickers 1987; Konrad and Kovenock 2009); and a developer able to commit first to a slightly slower speed gains, because rivals slow too, a local result that does not need a lead (Bueno de Mesquita, Dziuda and Polborn 2026). *What remains:* a lead shrinks by diffusion even when the follower does not accelerate (**{{!Remark:lead}}**, S8) [thm]; spending it returns the contest toward parity, where intensity is greatest and standing is priced highest (Section 7.2); what the margin must buy is coverage, and for hazard classes whose evidence lag exceeds it, it buys nothing ({{tempo}}); and whether it suffices is judged inside the contest, by the leader's own evaluators. *Profile:* it acts on tempo for a time and leaves the objective of winning in place. Its best case, a first commitment to slow that rivals answer by slowing, is a first step of the transition (Section 11), not a substitute for it.

**(d) The benign winner: win, bind, share.** *Strongest form:* a coalition seeks a clear advantage, including military superiority and the denial of key inputs to adversaries, and then offers its benefits to others in exchange for their support (EV1). It persuades by promising both security and benefits; both rest on the winner's restraint after the fact. Victors have bound themselves through institutions (Ikenberry 2001), a dominant power can supply public goods (Kindleberger 1973), and an encompassing ruler internalizes the shared conditions (Olson 1993). By this paper's own results the form is strongest where the winner still needs the governed: a winner accurate toward what can be divided gives them $\kappa_W/(1+\kappa_W)$ (Section 5.2). *What remains:* after a decisive win what the others receive is set by the winner's objective alone, and a promise to share is worth breaking ({{wingives}}) [thm]; the winner's need for the governed after victory is what the race cannot verify beforehand and what AI can lower (C13); where conduct before victory and after it go together, the race shifts winners toward those that extend dominance ({{raceselects}}) [thm∣prem]; and the winner is itself a field whose restraint depends on whether its own correctors can be bought by its successes. *Profile:* it changes nothing in selection before victory. What would show it suffices are the commitment tests: binding limits on what victory confers, benefits shared as they arise rather than promised, and the coalition's own correctors certified independent, with succession rules that survive changes of leadership (Test I). A winner that adopts these has changed what it selects for.

**(e) A finite sprint, then restraint.** *Strongest form:* sprint to a decisive lead, then freeze or roll back and govern at the tempo of understanding. A winner that freezes keeps positive survival and gives up only permanent superiority ({{freeze}}), and after a decisive win no contest remains to price standing. *What remains*, by kind of objection. Exposure: the path passes through its greatest exposure, and through the rivals' strongest motive for preventive attack ({{preventattack}}), before any restraint begins; whether that exposure is survivable is C1 and C5. Maintenance: a decisive advantage not used to stop others' growth does not stay decisive, and using it so ends their O2, which V2 and V3 protect (**{{!Remark:sprint}}**, S8). Mechanism, the paper's own answer: the freeze is a stop that one actor imposes on all the others, the most unequal incidence a constraint can have, so it re-sorts those it binds and is evaded wherever evasion pays their type ({{blind}}) [thm], and they read it as a lead being kept; and it is held by the largest conversion target there is, since the return to influence over correctors grows with the square of share ({{influence}}) [thm]. The stop is not what separates the sprint from E2 and common custody, which also stop every actor from building the decisive class; incidence and holder are. The sprint's freeze answers to the sprinter's own correctors and, when the sprinter breaks it, to no one (D4, D5); custody is designed for separated functions under correctors certified independent of every party, certificates not yet measured (C4; DISCUSSION X8 sets the two side by side). *Profile:* nothing changes in selection during the sprint.

**(f) Deterrence and prevention.** Prevention can be survival-preferred by the preventer.

**{{!Proposition:preventown}} (Prevention under the preventer's own criterion) [thm].** *Facing a rival it believes certain to race, an actor comparing paths by its own survival at a common horizon strictly prefers a single strike, which escalates to catastrophe with probability $\varepsilon_P$ and otherwise succeeds with probability $s_P$ in returning the field to something like the arrangement, leaving the race as it was if it fails, to both staying out and joining iff $\varepsilon_P<1-\max(\Phi^S,\Phi^P)/\big(s_P\Phi^R+(1-s_P)\Phi^P\big)$; as the racing branches $\Phi^S$ and $\Phi^P$ deteriorate, this bound rises toward one wherever $s_P\Phi^R>0$. Sustained prevention against a target that rebuilds with probability at least $b_P$ a period, each strike escalating with probability at least $\varepsilon_{\min}$, has a per-period hazard of at least $b_P\varepsilon_{\min}$.*

*Scope.* Prevention that permanently ends the target's capacity, or converts into joint monitoring, is a different branch. *Proof:* S8.

The objections are three, and none changes the criterion. Normatively, prevention ends or endangers others' O1 and O2 (V2, V3). Epistemically, aimed at a rival believed certain to race, it can make a rival that was in fact survival-first act as the type it was feared to be (Jervis 1976; Kydd 1997). Technically, detecting unobservable progress and striking at machine speed brings the delegation of Section 6.3. Deterrence by threatened sabotage acts on $p_d$ by threat, observing datacentres and making compute thresholds salient (Hendrycks, Schmidt and Wang 2025); its fallback is war or race, its credibility can reverse under survival-first comparisons, its red lines are hard to state and to monitor (DISCUSSION X8), and algorithmic progress erodes thresholds stated in compute [obs: EV9].

Insuring against falling behind is not an exit either: the penalty is set by the winner, not the laggard, and insurance accepts the frame of the contest, hedging against losing instead of declining the game (**{{!Remark:insure}}**, S8). That is the case the ruling of Section 3.8 closes: against a winner of that logic falling behind cannot be made survivable, so a remedy has to oppose the logic, not hedge inside it [corpus: possibility-space]. Provision differs by what it is for. Held by no one, beneath the game, it lowers the won share for every actor (G5); held by one actor as insurance against losing, it is a position in the contest, which a winner with a relative objective has a motive to destroy.

**The hard case.** A survival-first actor that gives a low probability to a capable rival's holding an unbounded relative objective (Section 3.8) has a best course: to propose verifiable limits and to take narrow E1 verifiably in its own strategic loops, a costly signal ({{>signal}}). With a high probability it compares denial, a coalition that stops the decisive class, and prevention, and coverage of every capable actor then comes from consent or from coercion. Coercive denial raises the weight on winning and, if it escalates, falls under {{preventown}}. The theory does not derive that its net effect is positive and states the case as hard; Section 12.1 states, once, the conditions under which a coercive restriction of frontier inputs enters even the comparison of designs.

**(g) A verified agreement over a contest that remains.** Rivals agree to slow and to delay decisive capability, with compute declarations, supply-chain accounting, recomputation of workloads and mutually assured destruction of compute if the agreement dissolves. Let the payoffs of mutual restraint, of racing against a restrainer, of mutual racing and of restraining against a racer be $R$, $T$, $P$ and $S$; let verification detect a defection in time with probability $\upsilon$, reversing it with a sanction $F$; let trust $\varpi$ be the belief that the other cooperates; and write $g_C=R-\upsilon(P-F)-(1-\upsilon)T$ for what cooperating gains against a cooperator and $g_D=(1-\upsilon)(P-S)$ for what it loses against a defector.

**{{!Proposition:assure}} (Assurance) [thm].** *Cooperation is a best response iff $\varpi\ge g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ when both are positive; no trust suffices if $g_C\le0<g_D$; cooperation is dominant if $g_D\le0<g_C$. Where $T-P+F>0$ and the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating, $T>R$, cooperating against a cooperator pays iff $\upsilon>(T-R)/(T-P+F)$; where $T<R$, it pays without verification.*

*Scope.* The threshold is the deterrence condition of the inspection game (Avenhaus, von Stengel and Zamir 2002). Read with its payoffs as continuation values, it is the enforcement condition seen from verification, $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d}$ ({{detectlink}}): timely detection must exceed a level that rises with the value of winning. *Proof:* S8, with the $n$-player bounds.

**{{!Theorem:verify}} (Verification and the value of winning) [thm].** Verification is required exactly when, and to the degree that, the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating.
1. *For a fixed sanction, the timely detection that cooperation needs, $(T-R)/(T-P+F)$, tends to one as $T$ grows.*
2. *Detection counts only if it comes before a defection becomes decisive, so that $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$; acceleration, which shortens $T_{\rm dec}$, lowers the timely detection that any given instrument achieves.*
3. *Verification can also lower $T$ by bounding the largest defection that would go undetected, for instance by accounting for compute, which does not need near-certain detection. The bound holds while the verified coalition's advantage in effective compute stays above the ratio at which an undetected project would become decisive [def]; since effective compute is algorithmic efficiency times compute, the coalition's compute advantage must then grow at least as fast as diffusion erodes its algorithmic one, and whether it does is not derived here [conj; EV9].*

*Scope.* Verification does not carry unverifiable information ({{talk}}), its own reliability (Section 4), or future commitments, unless the sanction keeps pace with the net gain from exit, which can grow with the capability held in check: an agreement that preserves relative positions preserves the relative term of every payoff, and with it the motive to exit (**{{!Proposition:overhang}}**, S8) [thm]. *Proof:* S8.

One detailed plan of this kind is designed to be enforceable without trust, with proportional reserves and a coalition that must stay ahead of covert projects (EV10; DISCUSSION X8). It is an instance of a position, keeping the contest underneath and bounding it by verification, and the answer above is to the position, not to the plan's figures. *Profile:* it slows and caps, acting on tempo and decisiveness, and keeps relative standing in its enforcement and fallback, and with it the motive to exit ({{overhang}}). That makes it a bridge; whether it can be the end depends on whether it lowers the value of winning or only bounds defection.

The comparison turns on two quantities. If continuation keeps the uncovered hazardous share summable, it can beat a fragile cooperative arrangement; if the uncovered share rises on the continuation path, any cooperative arrangement with a modest hazard of dissolution does better at every horizon past the near term [num: S8; legacy v5, CMP]. The first is crux C1; the second is what Sections 10 and 11 design for and test. Under the protection standard both paths fail the tolerance in their worst case, so the comparison moves to the standard's third step, which needs estimates on both sides; those estimates are what the cruxes of Section 12 would supply. An expected-value criterion that puts low probability on a rising uncovered share and values speed highly can prefer continuation, and a discounted criterion with utility linear in consumption prefers racing whenever the hazard it adds is below the growth it adds. These disagreements are the normative cruxes. DISCUSSION X8 lists, path by path, each failure mode in $\Theta$, whether and into what it can be reversed, whether that is derived or only designed, and what would show it: the list that Section 12.1 compares at the third step. The analysis does not establish that every cooperative arrangement outperforms regulated competition, and Section 12 does not claim it.

Section 9 asks what any adequate arrangement must do, whatever it is called, and which of its requirements no actor can meet alone, taking from here the profile, the marks G1–G7 and the durability tests; Section 12 takes up the third step of the standard.
