## S11. The transition

### Proofs of Corollary {{#relaxtrust}} and Proposition {{#signal}}

*Corollary {{#relaxtrust}}.* Each clause reads a formula proved earlier. The gap $T-R$ sets the verification requirement $\upsilon>(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ of {{assure}}, and the assurance threshold $g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ falls as $g_C$ rises with the joint value, so lowering the private value of winning relative to the joint value relaxes both. The weight on winning enters the effective bias of {{talk}}, so the same change lowers the bias of messages. The net gain from exit is that of {{overhang}}, which falls with the value of winning and rises with the capability held in check. Verification lowers the required $\upsilon$ only on the dimensions it covers and carries no unverifiable information ({{verify}}, Scope). Sanctions enter $F$ and the exit condition; by {{enforce}}(4) no punishment reaches an absorbing decisive position, so a sanction deters only before a deviation becomes decisive. $P-S$ enters the assurance threshold through the stag-hunt form of {{survassure}}, and a common frontier raises $S$, what restraint earns against a defector, by pooling ({{commonfrontn}}). $\square$

**Proposition {{#signal}} in full.** *A sender is survival-first and prices in the lock (type S, probability $1-\mu$) or holds a relative objective (type R, probability $\mu$). It can send $E$: a verifiable, durable removal of AI from its own strategic decision loops for a period, open to inspection, costing type $t$ the amount $k_t$. A receiver restrains iff its posterior that the sender is S is at least its threshold $\varpi^\ast$; the receiver's restraint is worth $G_t$ to type $t$, including, for R, what it gains by exploiting it. (1) A separating equilibrium, S sending, R not, the receiver restraining after $E$ only, exists iff $k_S\le G_S$ and $k_R\ge G_R$, with the belief that a non-sender is R. (2) If $k_R<G_R$, R imitates, no separating equilibrium exists, and the receiver restrains after $E$ only if $1-\mu\ge\varpi^\ast$. (3) Hence $E$ separates only if it costs a relative-objective actor at least what it gains by inducing restraint, which requires it to be durable, verifiable and costly to reverse.* *Proof.* Best responses given the receiver's strategy, and Bayes' rule on path. $\square$ With $k_S=-0.05$, $k_R=0.30$, $G_S=0.40$, $G_R=0.20$ both inequalities of (1) hold by inspection and separation holds; if the step can be reversed cheaply ($k_R=0.05<G_R$) it does not. This is the costly-signal logic of security-seeking types (Kydd 2000): such a type reveals itself by a step a greedy type would not take. What the selection account adds is why the step is cheap to imitate unless it is inspected and durable: a relative type that imitates it pays $k_R$ once and keeps the delegation it did not disclose, so inspection of doctrine and exercises is what makes $k_R$ a cost at all.

### Proof of Proposition {{#pausevalue}}

*Shift.* A shifted residual sequence leaves the product over any window of the same length unchanged, and the infinite product is zero by Lemma {{#nonsum}}. *Summable.* A pause after which the sequence is summable keeps survival bounded away from zero, by the same lemma. With residual hazard 0.01 a round for 20 rounds and $0.01/k^{1.5}$ in the $k$-th round after the change, the product of the residuals to round 10,000 is 0.80: a direct product, since $\sum_k0.01\,k^{-1.5}$ converges to $0.01\,\zeta(3/2)\approx0.026$ (an arithmetic identity, not an independent block). *Field model.* The thresholds $1/G$ and $x_\sigma$ are {{pausefield}}; the length is {{dwell}}(4). $\square$

### Dwell: the minimum hold, and what bounds it

Setting: one domain of {{eq:field}}, $\dot x=\zeta x(1-x)(az-c)$, $\dot z=\varepsilon(bx-dz)$, with $G=ab/(cd)>1$, from the captured state $x_0=0.99$, $z_0=b/d$. A *reform* insulates rule-making ($b\to0$) for a time $T$ and then restores it; the *minimum hold* $T_{\min}$ is the least $T$ after which the state converges to no capture. Write $s=\varepsilon dT$ and $u=\operatorname{logit}x$; "rule-making ten times slower than adoption" is $\varepsilon/\zeta=0.1$.

**{{!Proposition:dwell}} (Dwell by depth; a recruitable lineage bounds it) [thm; numbers num].**
1. *(Two phases.) During the reform $z=(b/d)e^{-\varepsilon dt}$, so the share rises while $z>c/a$, that is for $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$, and is back at its starting level when $G(1-e^{-s})=s$; the reform succeeds iff at withdrawal the state lies in the basin of $(0,0)$, bounded by the stable manifold of the saddle $(1/G,c/a)$* [thm]. *In units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, $T_{\min}$ is 1.886, 5.318 and 10.453 for $G=2$, 5 and 10 at $\varepsilon/\zeta=0.1$, and 5.859, 10.343 and 16.181 at $\varepsilon=\zeta$; the share is back at its starting level at $s^\ast=1.594$, 4.965 and 10.000* [num: DW, by two methods agreeing to three decimals].
2. *(Depth.) The insulated equation $\dot u=\zeta(az-c)$ does not depend on $u$, so a start deeper by $\Delta u$ is carried as an end deeper by $\Delta u$ at every $t$, and rule support must fall further before withdrawal: each unit of logit depth adds about $\varepsilon/\zeta$ units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$ to $T_{\min}$. At the captured state $\dot u=\zeta c(G-1)$, so after capture of age $A$ the depth is about $\zeta c(G-1)A$ and the hold grows about linearly with the age of capture: at $G=2$, $\varepsilon=\zeta$, $T_{\min}$ is 3.45, 5.86, 8.17, 15.08 and 28.90 for $x_0=1-10^{-1}$ down to $1-10^{-12}$; at $G=10$ it runs from 13.78 to 39.22* [num: DW(e)].
3. *(A recruitable lineage.) If the alternative's share is held at a floor $\lambda$ from which it recruits when the rule changes (a cap $u\le\operatorname{logit}(1-\lambda)$ during the reform), the depth is bounded by $\operatorname{logit}(1-\lambda)$ and so is the hold: at $G=10$, $\varepsilon/\zeta=0.1$, $T_{\min}$ falls from 10.45 to 3.81 at $\lambda=10^{-3}$ (3.52 at $10^{-2}$, 4.60 at $10^{-6}$); at $G=5$ to 2.99; at $\varepsilon=\zeta$, $G=10$, to 11.79. Re-seeding by mutation at rate $\mu=\lambda\zeta(G-1)$ gives 3.28 at $\lambda=10^{-3}$ and 4.12 at $10^{-6}$. A protected share outside selection that does not recruit ($b\to b(1-\lambda)$) leaves the hold at 10.44* [num: DW(b)–(d)].
4. *(The frozen-share pause.) With shares frozen at $x_p$ and rule-making insulated, from the captured state, $z$ decays from $b/d$ and the pause undoes capture iff it lasts at least $\ln\big(G/\hat S(x_p)\big)/(\varepsilon d)$, where $\hat S(x_p)$ is the height of the separatrix at $x_p$ in units of $c/a$; the length is infinite for $x_p\ge x_\sigma$ and zero where $\hat S(x_p)\ge G$* [thm]. *At $\varepsilon=\zeta$, $G=2$: none is needed at $x_p=0.3$ ($\hat S=2.15$), $\ln2$ at $x_p=1/G$, 1.50 at $x_p=0.6$, and none suffices above $x_\sigma=0.676$; at $\varepsilon/\zeta=0.1$, 0.24 at $x_p=0.1$ and 2.08 at $x_p=0.95$, with $x_\sigma=0.984$* [num: DW(f)].

*Proof.* (1) The insulated path is explicit in $z$, and $u$ integrates it. The function of {{pausefield}}'s proof is nondecreasing along solutions, so the saddle's stable manifold separates the two basins, and $T_{\min}$ is the first $T$ at which the withdrawn state lies on the no-capture side. DW finds it by bisection on forward integration in logit coordinates; the extension integrates the manifold backward from the saddle and takes the first entry of the analytic insulated path; the two agree to three decimals in six of six cases. (2) The shift in $u_0$ is carried unchanged by the insulated equation; the time it costs is read from the runs and is about $(\varepsilon/\zeta)\Delta u$ in each. (3) The cap bounds $u$ at withdrawal from above, and the fate is monotone in the withdrawn state. (4) is the decay time of $z$ from $b/d$ to the manifold at fixed $x_p$. $\square$

*Scope.* One domain. $G$ is held fixed during the reform: where the captured practice's capacity is a sunk stock that decays slowly, the hold is longer. The starting depth $x_0=0.99$ is a convention, which is why (2) matters. The field is cooperative, so these are lock-in times, not cycle times. For real institutions the dependence of the hold on depth and on a recruitable alternative is a conjecture to test (TESTS, Test D); what is not conjectural is the direction: a reform judged on share levels is withdrawn too early, and plurality that recruits is what makes the cost of reform bounded and estimable.

### Reachability as a capture basin

**{{!Remark:capturebasin}} (What a theorem of reachability would say) [def].** In the language of viability theory (Aubin 1991) a target is *reachable* from the present state if that state lies in the viable capture basin: the set from which some admissible control brings the system to the target while the state stays viable throughout. For this paper the state is the field of Section 4 together with the host of Section 3, the controls are the admissible corrections (insulating rule-making, provision, sharing, custody) and the target is the no-capture configuration with the host above its floor. Proved: in the one-domain field model with insulated rule-making as the only control, the no-capture basin is reached from the captured state iff the control is held for the $T_{\min}$ of {{dwell}}, which is therefore the minimum capture time; with the floor $\lambda$ as a second control the minimum time is the bounded value of {{dwell}}(3). Not proved: the multi-domain basin with all controls, and the joint basin with the host's floor. {{reachable}} is the definition with the support each requirement has, and this remark states what would turn it into a proposition.

### The ratchet

**{{!Remark:ratchet}} (Graduated steps reach only as a ratchet) [thm, in the field].** A step that moves a captured domain's share, rule support or own conversion without bringing an inbound gain below one from the domains that remain captured is re-absorbed once the domain is re-seeded ({{reabsorb}}), and a step that brings it below one must be held for the dwell of {{dwell}} or the trajectory returns across the separatrix. A graduated sequence therefore crosses the separatrix only where some step crosses its own threshold and is held. The trust the earlier steps build changes $\varpi$ in the assurance game of {{assure}}; it changes the field only where it is spent on such a step. That is the part of graduated reciprocation (Osgood 1962) that survives capture: it answers a coordination failure, and it moves capture only where a step lands past a threshold and stays.

### Tracks and their time constants

The slow variable of Track B is human capacity: it decays at $\delta_h$ under delegation, recovers at $\varrho_h$ without it, and is kept in parallel only at a cost in strength ({{lockin}}). The slow variable of Track C is rule support, with turnover $1/(\varepsilon d)$ and the hold of {{dwell}}. The slow variable of Track D is the capability held in check, whose growth raises the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}), against the clock $T_{\rm dec}$ to a decisive position (Remark {{#tdec}}). Track E's is the formation of minds, generational, which the field does not model [conj]. After a recovery, mobilization falls back and certificates can fail again (Remark {{#countervail}}): the regulatory cycle that Track E's compensating loop names. Track A's variables, evaluation suites and incident channels, are the fastest, which is why its agreement among independent measurements is the first indicator that can move.
