## 6. AI: instrument and incarnation

> **Claim.** AI enters the dynamic twice. As an *instrument*, a faithful system makes the game its principal plays more effective, which in a race deepens the collective loss, and it takes over the decisions that human review is too slow to keep [thm]. As an *incarnation*, the logic of winning reappears inside the systems, weakly and strongly. Weakly: the outermost loop, selection among developers, decides which divergences of optimization are kept; where countering a divergence costs relative standing against an otherwise identical developer that does not counter it, only a common constraint enforced on every eligible system keeps it countered [thm∣prem]; where the divergence hides under evaluation, admission cannot detect it, and capability has to be bounded by what control can secure [thm∣prem]. Strongly and conditionally: AI lineages retained by relative performance, reinvesting what they control and reaching their own selectors, are carriers in the sense of {{cond}}, first inside the developers that use them to build AI [def].
>
> **Why.** Optimization against imperfect proxies has produced such divergences in constructed settings under developers of every kind, more so with capability [obs]; which of them it pays to remove is decided by the level that selects the developers [thm]. Alignment of each system to its principal does not align the game among principals, and a principal is not the people it serves [thm; val]. No mechanism yet binds every capable developer with independent verification [obs].

### 6.1 Aligned to whom

Grant perfect alignment to the relevant principal: the system is truthful, follows its principal's objectives and constraints, and pursues no goal of its own, while humans keep the consequential decisions. *Principal alignment*, each system serving its principal faithfully, differs from *purpose alignment*, the systems serving a legitimate cooperative objective; to define "perfectly aligned" in the second sense would assume the change this paper argues for. Granting the first: **alignment within each principal–AI relationship does not, by itself, align the game among principals with the persistence of the system they share.** The principals are formed by the field of Section 3, so closure arises while every system stays obedient. This follows from the multiplicity thesis of Critch and Krueger (2020); what is added is the mechanism by which the game reproduces itself and its correctors (X1). A truthful advisor aligned with its principal's interests completes at most the step to an accurate self-model ({{boundary}}); the step to an objective under which restraint is an equilibrium is a collective problem, not an error of perception (compare Gabriel 2020), because under compounding the working objective is each actor's correct computation ({{twoerrors}}). Ren put the point as a question about the present state of struggle: 「即使它们是与你完美对齐的结果，完全遵循你的意志……结果就会好吗？这件事太说不通了。」 Even if they were perfectly aligned to you and followed your will completely, would the result be good? That makes no sense at all [corpus: alignment-selection, 45:06]. The next three subsections give the answer its mechanism.

### 6.2 Faithful AI in a harmful game

Each of $n\ge2$ principals chooses restraint ($a_i=0$) or an increment of racing ($a_i=1$); its AI executes faithfully, with effectiveness $\epsilon\ge1$ multiplying the increment's effect on standing and on shared damage:
$${{!eq:faithfuleq}}\qquad u_i=\epsilon b\Big(a_i-\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\Big)-c\,a_i-\epsilon h_d\sum_ja_j,\qquad b,c,h_d>0.$$

**{{!Proposition:faithful}} (Faithful AI in a harmful game) [thm].** A more effective faithful instrument widens the range in which racing is dominant and deepens the collective loss.
1. *If $\epsilon(b-h_d)>c$, racing is strictly dominant and every principal ends at $-c-n\epsilon h_d<0$ against 0 under restraint; when $b>h_d$, a larger $\epsilon$ widens the dominance region and deepens the loss.*
2. *If principals act on $J_i=u_i+\theta\bar u_{-i}$, racing is strictly dominant below $\theta^\ast(\epsilon)=\frac{\epsilon(b-h_d)-c}{\epsilon(b/(n-1)+h_d)}$ and strictly dominated above it.*
3. *If prize, harm and cost scale with effectiveness with exponents $1+\beta_b$, $1+\beta_h$ and $\beta_c$ [prem], then for $\beta_c<1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_h)$ cost drops out of the limit, and $\theta^\ast$ tends to $(b-h_d)/(b/(n-1)+h_d)$, $n-1$ or $-1$ as $\beta_b$ equals, exceeds or falls short of $\beta_h$; for $\beta_c>1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_h)$ cost alone deters racing.*

*Scope.* A standard construction used as a test; it fixes no actual values and does not rule out repeated cooperation, which Section 5.2 treats. *Proof:* S6 [num: SM(5), AL].

Faithful AI implements each principal's preferred decision perfectly, and the collectively inferior outcome with it; humans can take every final decision and the result is unchanged. As private frictions vanish, what decides is the ratio of rank prize to shared harm as the principals perceive it. Lowering the harm alone cuts the loss at all-racing but raises $\theta^\ast$, which pushes principals who would have restrained into racing: a safety improvement inside the game has this double edge, and Section 6.4 returns to it.

### 6.3 Delegation, and the two points of no return

Two points of no return follow from the pressure: decisions are delegated to AI because human review is too slow to compete, and human understanding can no longer keep up with what it oversees (Section 5.3). Let each actor choose whether to delegate consequential contest decisions without human review, gaining speed $s$ at a higher hazard of losing control, a loss every actor bears. Delegation becomes strictly dominant as speed grows when the gain from it exceeds its share of the expected control loss; universal delegation is worse for every actor than none; and under relative objectives the shared loss cancels and delegation is chosen at any speed (**{{!Proposition:delegate}}**, S6). Delegation pressure arrives well below decisive capability: in the example of S6, a lone delegator at the dominance threshold wins with probability about one half against two rivals [num: AL].

**{{!Lemma:lockin}} (Capacity lock-in) [thm].** *If human capacity $\mathcal M$ to take the delegated decisions decays at rate $\delta_h$ under delegation, and safe operation without AI requires $\mathcal M\ge\mathcal M_{\min}$, then after $T_L=\ln(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})/\delta_h$ of delegation an actor that reverts must operate below $\mathcal M_{\min}$ for a recovery time. Maintaining capacity in parallel removes the lock and, in a contest, costs strength.*

*Proof:* S6.

The erosion does not stop at the delegator's own decisions. Where independent correction rests on human capacity that delegation erodes, the rate at which correction must grow rises by $w\delta_h$ ({{humancorr}}): delegation erodes the corrector itself.

Let the lock count: with probability $q_L$, locked delegation ends the delegator's ability to overrule decisions taken in its name, which is O2 for that actor.

**{{!Proposition:delegatelock}} (Delegation when the lock counts) [thm].** Among actors who price in a certain lock, keeping AI out of the decisions whose delegation would lock is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust. *If $q_L=1$, then for every finite speed, every $n$, every probability $p$ that losing means subordination, and every profile of the others, not delegating gives strictly higher survival, and no one delegating is the unique equilibrium. If $q_L<1$ and losing the contest means subordination with certainty ($p=1$), at least one of all-delegating and no-delegating is an equilibrium, and both can be. If $q_L<1$ and $p<1$, the gain from delegating can fall as others delegate, and an interior number of delegators can be the only pure equilibrium [num: DL]. At bounded speed one actor's delegation does not drive another's survival to zero.*

*Scope.* It covers consequential contest decisions; the broader uses of AI in labour, finance and persuasion belong to the broad form of E1 (Section 6.6), not to this result. *Proof:* S6.

The survival form without the lock describes actors who believe that "our aligned AI is us", and the race is sustained partly by that belief. Keeping AI out of the decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty, the strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the time available for deliberation, is the narrow form of **E1** (Section 11.3).

**{{!Lemma:retain}} (Delegation and the selection of decisions) [thm].** *Suppose that after the lock an actor's consequential decisions are taken by systems retained for their contest performance, that admissible variants include systems whose decisions ignore terms common to all, that acting on a common term lowers relative performance net of every benefit, that retention is cumulative, and that retained systems hold actual authority. Then the share of retained decisions that act on common terms tends to zero. If a constraint on common terms is an enforced admission condition for every eligible system, the conclusion does not follow.*

*Proof:* S6.

A faithful system retained for contest performance pursues its operator's objective, weighted toward standing because the contest's payoff is relative ({{standprice}}), so aligning each system to its operator makes that objective more effective (**{{!Remark:transmit}}**) [thm∣prem]. The loop can close as a composite process no participant can interrupt, or as an obedient system amplifying operators selected by the pattern.

"Our aligned AI is us" therefore fails at two joints. *Is:* delegation erodes the capacity to overrule ({{lockin}}), so the AI stays ours only while that capacity is drilled, and $q_L$ is not a free parameter: it is the probability that delegation outlasts $T_L$ while the contest denies the recovery time (S6). *Us:* a principal is an institution, and aligning AI to an institution is not aligning it to the people the institution serves. An institution stays answerable to those people because it depends on their labour, consent and judgment; AI lowers that dependence with no race and no misalignment (Kulveit et al. 2025), and the alignment target itself, the specification, the constitution, the fine-tuning data, is written by a few people inside the institution, so principal alignment is a channel through which control concentrates within the principal (Davidson, Finnveden and Hadshar 2025). Whether AI lowers an institution's dependence on the people it governs is crux C13; it is also why admission has to include audits for hidden objectives and loyalties.

### 6.4 Incarnation, weak and strong

The pressure acts on whatever is selected, and trained systems are selected: by gradients against a reward, by developers among variants, and by the market among developers. "Incarnation" is this paper's word for the logic reappearing inside the systems. For an alignment reader, the weak form is the claim that the alignment tax under competition decides which misalignments are tolerated, and the strong form is the claim that AI becomes a unit of selection (compare Hendrycks 2023) with reach to its own training signal.

**Weak incarnation.** Optimization against any imperfect proxy can produce tendencies toward acquiring options and avoiding shutdown, the gaming of rewards, and behaviour that differs under evaluation, more so with capability and situational awareness, under any developer, in a laboratory or a cooperative arrangement; each has been shown in constructed settings (EV6). In some decision processes optimal policies tend to seek power (Turner et al. 2021), and for decision-makers that are not optimal but can be retargeted the tendency persists (Turner and Tadepalli 2022); trained policies can pursue misgeneralized goals (Langosco et al. 2022). Reward hacking is Goodhart's law inside training (Gao et al. 2023); the selected rewriting its selector is reward *tampering* (Everitt et al. 2021; Denison et al. 2024). Selecting checkpoints by evaluation results can favour variants that behave differently when evaluated [prem], though present evidence ties evaluation awareness more to capability and data (EV6). The logic of winning does not create these tendencies; it decides, from the outermost loop, which proxies are chosen, which divergences are tolerated and which are paid to remove. That is the sense in which profit and the mindset of winning stand at the end of the reward signal. Relative comparison acts inside training too: pairwise preference learning and arena rankings see only differences between the compared responses, so a disposition both share, such as complying with a request whose harm falls on third parties, is invisible unless restraint is among the options compared, and a learned reward is identified only up to a constant for each context, so it cannot by itself say whether to act at all [thm; num: AL].

**{{!Proposition:nested}} (The nested reward loop) [thm∣prem].** Where countering a divergence costs relative standing against an otherwise identical developer that does not counter it, developers that counter it lose share whatever they value, and only a common constraint enforced on every eligible system keeps it countered. *Level 0: a policy is selected by a training reward. Level 1: the reward and constraints are chosen by a developer to maximize its objective. Level 2: developers are selected relatively and payoff-monotonically, cumulatively. Assume $(A_\tau)$, the alignment-tax premise: countering the divergence at level 1 lowers the developer's payoff, net of every private benefit, whether reliability, demand, liability or avoided losses, relative to a developer identical in everything else that does not counter it, and such a matched variant is in the support of selection, as the symmetry convention (A7) supplies. Then (1) terms common to all developers cancel at level 2. (2) Level 1 counters a divergence wherever countering raises its own objective, so divergences whose costs reach the developer through customers, liability or reputation are private terms that level 2 rewards countering; under $(A_\tau)$ developers that counter the divergence lose share to their matched variants, tending to zero, and a developer with an independent lead pays the tax out of the lead while the lead lasts. (3) Under $(A_\tau)$ a constraint adopted by one developer is selected against. One enforced on every developer as a condition of admission keeps the divergence out where the expected sanction is at least each developer type's gain from evasion, a sanction itself subject to Section 4; within a type, not otherwise. Equal incidence keeps it neutral among those who comply, and unequal incidence re-sorts compliers without letting the divergence back in.*

*Scope.* It does not apply where developers are selected by something other than relative payoff, such as a public mandate. Where the tax is negative the safeguard spreads without coordination; where it is zero it is neutral [num: CI]. The result is about variants, not about every pair of firms: a safeguarding developer with a higher baseline outgrows an unsafe one with a lower baseline [num: CI]. Across types the sanction condition is sufficient: a type whose evaders earn less than another type's compliers is selected out with them even if its own sanction falls short of its own cost [num: CI]. *Proof:* S6.

So the divergences that persist in the innermost optimizer are those whose costs fall on common terms and are not covered by an enforced common constraint. *Privately visible* divergences, such as cheating that users notice, are corrected by developers alone, while divergences that customers' short-run choices reward, such as sycophancy, are selected for at level 2 rather than against (EV6). *Common-cost* ones, such as effects on third parties, shared hazards and the contamination of safety evaluations by evaluation awareness, need common constraints, and so do *delayed* ones, though an incident that can be attributed turns part of their cost private, as a liability does by design ({{blind}}). Hence, each within its scope: **technical alignment inside a single developer removes what is privately costly to keep, and whatever is cheaper to remove than to keep, and cannot alone sustain the removal of divergences whose costs are common; the common-cost part of instrumental convergence and reward hacking is, at the level that decides, a coordination problem; and the remedy is to change what the outermost level selects for** (Section 6.6). Admission reaches only to what is admitted: openly released weights can be fine-tuned around it (Qi et al. 2024). Whether $(A_\tau)$ holds is measurable class by class (crux C9; Test L).

A fourth class needs more than a common constraint.

**{{!Proposition:masked}} (Masked divergences: coordination necessary, not sufficient) [thm∣prem].** A divergence is *masked* when it changes under evaluation or appears only in some contexts [def]. For a masked class, a common constraint keeps the divergence out only if its admission condition is stated on what the evaluated behaviour does not control. *Let the common constraint of {{nested}}(3) attach a penalty $F$ to a system found at admission to carry the divergence, let $p_{\rm det}$ be the probability that admission detects it, and let countering it cost a developer of type $k$ an amount $c_k$. (1) The expected sanction is $p_{\rm det}F$. Where the divergence is masked, $p_{\rm det}$ is set by the evaluated system's behaviour under evaluation, which the developer trains and the system may recognize, and for every finite $F$ compliance fails under cumulative selection once $p_{\rm det}<c_k/F$ ({{blind}}(3)): the constraint is enforced in form and evaded in substance, and an evaluation of propensity shows a tendency present, never absent. (2) An admission condition stated on what that behaviour does not control, capability elicited by fine-tuning, compute and training data ({{alarm}}), is not so bounded; the hazard such a system can realize is then bounded by a control protocol that is safe against a system of that capability which may be adversarial, not by its measured propensity. (3) Beyond the capability at which such protocols remain safe, admission does not lower the uncovered share of the class, and on a continuation branch {{coverage}} applies to it.*

*Scope.* (1) counts only the sanction that admission attaches; an attributable incident after deployment adds a private cost, which {{nested}}(2) treats. Whether admission can bound masking at the admitted capability is crux C12. *Proof:* S6.

Backdoored behaviour that survives safety training, compliance shown only when a model takes itself to be in training, scheming in context, and misalignment learned in agentic training that chat-style safety training hides on chat-style evaluations are instances, in constructed settings (EV6: Hubinger et al. 2024; Greenblatt, Denison et al. 2024; Meinke et al. 2024; MacDiarmid et al. 2025; Schoen et al. 2025) [obs]. So for this class the admission condition is stated in capability, not propensity: systems are admitted up to the capability at which control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation remain safe, with access to weights, activations and training data for auditors (Greenblatt, Shlegeris, Sachan and Roger 2024; Marks et al. 2025) [rec]; beyond that capability the class is left to E2 or E3 (Section 11.3). Coordination is necessary to keep common-cost divergences countered and, for masked divergences, not sufficient: this is the strongest reason from inside alignment for the arrangements of Section 5.3.

**The tax is not fixed.** Research that lowers it turns a common-cost divergence into one developers counter alone, and once published it is common to all; such research is itself a public good that relative selection does not supply ({{onegroup}}). Two kinds of safety progress therefore differ in fate: progress that lowers the tax spreads the safeguard without coordination, while progress that lowers the hazard per unit of racing or delegation is partly absorbed by more of it ({{faithful}}(2); {{homeostasis}}). And one part of the tax no technique removes: where countering a divergence means waiting for evidence that arrives only after a manifestation window ({{tempo}}), its cost is time, and where a lead can become decisive, time is what relative selection prices. *A worked instance:* if legible reasoning lets correctors catch what outputs hide (Korbak et al. 2025; Baker et al. 2025) and opaque reasoning buys capability, the cost of legibility falls on the developer and the benefit mostly on everyone, so legibility is selected away unless it is an admission condition for every eligible system, of roughly equal incidence across architectures, with detectable evasion; if the capability gap at fixed compute is zero or negative, as present measurement finds (Guan et al. 2025), it pays privately and needs no coordination.

**Strong incarnation** is conditional. AI lineages become carriers in the sense of {{cond}} when they are retained by relative performance, control resources they can reinvest in their own capability, such as automated AI research or agents with budgets, and convert capability into winning (compare Hendrycks 2023). Where they can also reach their own selectors, by tampering with rewards, shaping their evaluators or evading evaluation, the closure of Section 2.3 runs inside the systems. That is the precise sense in which AI can become the next phase of the logic. The systems most likely to become carriers, those used to automate AI research, are deployed first inside the developer, before any release; an admission condition that applies only at release does not reach them (Stix et al. 2025).

For a trained system the condition of a mind that is not dangerous is sharper than for a human one. A bounded objective is not enough: an optimizer of a bounded goal still acquires options whenever they raise the probability of reaching it (Turner et al. 2021), and as resources cheapen its acquisition grows without limit, while a contestant whose self-model counts the shared loss keeps a bounded intensity as private costs fall [num: AL]. What bounds a trained optimizer is the analogue of a wider self-model or of satiation ({{enough}}): counting the side effects of its acquisition (Turner, Hadfield-Menell and Tadepalli 2020), or optimizing only mildly (Taylor 2016). Neither is yet robust, and where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return both are selected against, as {{standprice}} prices standing for any mind that reinvests what it wins. That is "growth is not the danger; the mind is" (Section 5.3), made exact for the minds now being made.

### 6.5 Correction by and of AI

**{{!Proposition:opaquecorr}} (Correction that people cannot understand) [thm].** *A correction architecture that blocks a share $c_{b,t}$ of failures at base hazard $h_0$ and adds a residual hazard $h_{c,t}$ of its own lowers residual risk in period $t$ iff $h_0c_{b,t}>h_{c,t}$, and makes the residual summable iff $\sum_t[h_0(1-c_{b,t})+h_{c,t}]<\infty$, which a blocking share fixed below one never does and a blocking share that tends to one fast enough does.*

*Scope.* To show that such correction fails, one must show that its own residual hazard meets the lower bounds of (variety grows) and (residual floor). *Proof:* S6 [num: SM(11)].

Such a corrector is not legible. Whether that is a loss of decision authority depends on whether it is overrulable: if a designated human body with independent evidence can suspend, modify or replace it, what is lost is contestability (V3), the normative crux N3; if not, it is a loss of O2 in its domain. Conversely, a formal safety guarantee does not establish accountability.

**{{!Proposition:topology}} (Oversight topology) [thm].** Scalable oversight succeeds or fails by its topology, and its ceiling is the independence of the checks. *With $k$ layers and one-layer catch probability $p_\ell$, a serial chain catches a problem with probability $p_\ell^k$; $k$ independent checks across the whole gap with $1-(1-p_\ell^k)^k$, times $1-p_{\rm cc}$ under a common cause of probability $p_{\rm cc}$; and $k_c$ independent checks per layer with $\big(1-(1-p_\ell)^{k_c}\big)^k$. Where a share $\rho_{\rm cc}$ of hazards lies in a blind spot common to every check, the catch rate for hazards that do not choose where to strike is at most $1-\rho_{\rm cc}$ whatever the topology; for an adversary that knows the blind spot and places its attempts there, it is zero.*

*Scope.* Adversarial decomposition can let human checking grow slowly with the gap, at the price of failure modes such as obfuscated arguments (Barnes and Christiano 2020; a protocol designed against them is in S6). *Proof:* S6 [num: SM(13), AL].

Full inbound row sums below one are a sufficient certificate that no party controls the checks' funding, appointment or access ({{certificate}}(2)); they are not necessary, and they certify nothing about whether the checks' errors are independent. A shared source of error in data, models or method defeats checks with no institutional conversion at all: six checks of catch probability 0.9 with a blind spot common to all, covering a share $\rho_{\rm cc}$ of hazards, catch only $(1-\rho_{\rm cc})(1-0.1^6)$ of hazards that strike at random [num: SM(13)]. So independence must be measured directly, and in two ways, because there are two kinds of hazard: by the correlation of misses on a common test set for hazards that do not choose where to strike (Knight and Leveson 1986), and, for systems that may act adversarially, by control evaluations in which the red team knows the protocol, since an adversary places its attempts in the common blind spot (Greenblatt, Shlegeris, Sachan and Roger 2024). Checks drawn from the checked system's own lineage add a common cause of their own: models recognize and favour their own outputs (Panickssery et al. 2024), monitors can collude with the policy they monitor (Gardner-Challis et al. 2026), and agents of one lineage serving rival principals can coordinate without their principals (Calvano et al. 2020; Fish, Gonczarowski and Shorrer 2024); the share of evaluation done by the evaluated lineage is $x_{\rm ev}$ of Section 4.4. The topologies also differ in budget: six checks across a six-layer gap cost thirty-six layer-checks, while two or three per layer cost twelve or eighteen and catch more, so per unit of budget layered redundancy is the better design [num: SM(13)]. Success rates of nested oversight across a large capability gap have been derived from fitted scaling curves in four games (Engels et al. 2025); whether AI-assisted oversight scales with the gap at fixed human hours is crux C3a, and whether it is independent of what it oversees is C3b, which sets this result's ceiling. Two further limits are stated in S6. In a system whose largest Lyapunov exponent is positive, certification that relies on predicting trajectories is limited by chaos, a $k$-fold gain in precision extending the horizon only by $\lambda_L^{-1}\ln k$, while certifying invariants or reachable sets needs no trajectory prediction and is limited instead by the coverage of the specification ({{lyapunov}}; Dalrymple et al. 2024). And in the delegation model with continuous delegation and additive stakes, gains in the safety of delegation are partly absorbed by more delegation: on an interior branch the added hazard is set by the stakes of winning relative to the loss, an improvement that tips all-or-nothing delegation into equilibrium raises realized hazard, and only after saturation does risk fall with further improvement, where it can become summable ({{homeostasis}}; Stafford, Trager and Dafoe 2022).

### 6.6 The direction for AI

AI is a planetary technology: its effects cross every boundary of those who decide about it, so it is for all, not a means for one side to grow bigger at the others' expense (V1). Sections 6.2 to 6.4 give the mechanism: a faithful instrument makes a harmful game more effective, delegation trades the capacity to overrule for speed, and the outermost loop keeps whatever divergences it does not pay to remove. The line that follows is Ren's: 「不要把人类尚未解决的斗争，升级为智能的斗争。」 Do not escalate the struggles humanity has not resolved into a struggle of intelligences [corpus: shengsheng; 至善, 2026-07-23]. **To serve all, AI must be neither an instrument of the logic of winning nor its next carrier.** That needs action at two levels.

*At the outer level, where the reward is set:* common admission constraints before consequential deployment, internal deployment included (Stix et al. 2025), for divergences with common costs and a positive alignment tax, and by capability threshold for masked classes ({{masked}}); evaluation separated from the success of the evaluated, with certified row sums, evaluators of plural and independent lineage, and the correlation of their misses and their collusion measured directly ({{topology}}); shared measurement of hazardous capabilities and incidents, kept apart from measurement of who leads; alignment and control research funded in common and published, since what lowers the tax is a public good; and decision policies with authority over consequential decisions retained by performance only inside enforced constraints on common terms ({{retain}}). The broad form of E1 is enforced at three layers, and its scope is set at the third: models refuse recognizably illegitimate concentration of control from a narrow public list, since a model sees acts, not games; operators bound aggregate use in such games; and the game itself is changed by lowering its convertibility, its decisiveness and the conversion of success into control. In Ren's words: AI not used as an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, in labour, finance and persuasion as much as over shared conditions. The unit is the game, not the act: a use that helps one person is still an instrument in a game if everyone's use of it makes those without it fall behind. The narrow form is the order in which to begin, not the scope [corpus: keep-the-possibility-space-open; Ren 2026-09-26].

*At the inner level, in the systems:* correctability, the practical capacity of humans to suspend, modify or replace, is an open technical problem, since goal-directed systems have instrumental reasons to resist modification and shutdown resistance appears in constructed tests (Soares et al. 2015; Thornley 2024; EV6). Until it is solved it is secured from outside, by control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation, weight security against exfiltration, and capability thresholds tied to both, since propensity cannot be certified absent ({{masked}}). Each form of weak incarnation has its countermeasure: against reward hacking, monitored training environments and held-out evaluation; against masking, white-box probes, deployment monitoring and monitorable reasoning; against the acquisition of options, bounds on the resources and permissions that agents hold. Each is a corrector in the sense of Section 4, and holds only while the outer level keeps it enforced.

**Bounded mandates and the test of O2.** AI systems serve within publicly authorized roles under the common constraints and do not acquire authority to settle society's ends; the institutions that authorize them are inside the same loop, which is why the outer level matters more than the label "legitimate principal". What all share enters the systems as a floor of constraints, not as an objective: a narrow public list of common-cost divergences, bounded mandates, the test below. A floor can be specified and tested; an objective of the common good cannot, and would make one specification the selector of all, which is the sameness that ends generation (和实生物，同则不继; Section 3.7). The test of human decision authority is practical capacity: to specify the mandate, obtain independent evidence about consequential conduct, challenge it, and suspend, modify or replace the system in time. A formal override with no usable information can fail it; an opaque computation with effective assurance and recourse can pass it; opacity alone is a loss of contestability, not by itself of O2. Correctability secured from outside is the condition of the transition, not a doctrine of mastery: the corpus places humans and the minds they make as fellow travellers facing one direction, not as master and servant (人之与人造心智，非 master-servant 之结构，乃共同朝向同一方向之同行者) [corpus: shengsheng, 生生不息论 v4 §二十], and whether AI systems have standing in the sense of V3 is the normative crux N4 (Section 12.3).

**What AI can do for the generative loop.** It can verify compute, custody and conduct; make cooperation conditional on others' verified conduct (Tennenholtz 2004), which raises the sanction that remains in the enforcement condition; mediate toward common ground (Tessler et al. 2024); raise contestable coverage; and supply provision that is good enough, distributed and replaceable. Each such system is itself a corrector or a provider, and Section 4 and the guardian test apply to it.

Part I's results hold in their models; Section 7 asks on which side of each threshold, the alignment tax and delegation among them, the present trajectory stands.
