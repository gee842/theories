## 6. AI: instrument and incarnation

> **Claim.** AI enters the dynamic twice. As an *instrument*: where principals play a harmful game, faithful AI makes it more effective, and where speed decides the contest, decisions pass to AI until the capacity to overrule them is gone [thm]. As an *incarnation*, the logic reappears in the systems. Weakly: where countering a divergence of optimization costs a developer relative payoff against an otherwise identical rival, only a common constraint enforced at admission keeps it countered [thm∣prem]; where the divergence is masked under evaluation, capability must stay within what control that does not assume the system's cooperation can secure [rec]. Strongly: where AI lineages are retained by relative performance, reinvest in their own capability and reach their own selectors, they are carriers, and the closure of Section 2 runs inside them [def]; the likeliest are the systems used to build AI, deployed first inside their developers [obs]. Whether present lineages meet these conditions is open [conj].
>
> **Why.** Optimization against imperfect proxies has produced, in constructed settings and more so with capability, tendencies toward acquiring options, gaming rewards and behaving differently under evaluation, whoever the developer [obs]. The level that selects developers decides which of these it pays to remove, and humanity has at present no mechanism that binds every capable developer under independent verification [obs]. Aligning each system to its principal does not align the game among principals [thm], and a principal is an institution, not the people it serves [def].

### 6.1 Aligned to whom

Grant perfect alignment to the relevant principal: the system is truthful, follows its principal's objectives and constraints, pursues no goal of its own, and leaves the consequential decisions to humans. This is *principal alignment*, each system serving its principal faithfully. *Purpose alignment*, the systems serving a legitimate cooperative objective, is not granted, since defining "perfectly aligned" that way would assume the change argued for here. Then **alignment within each principal–AI relationship does not, by itself, align the game among principals with the persistence of the system they share** (Sections 6.2–6.3) [thm]. The principals are themselves formed by the field (Section 3), so closure can arise while every system remains obedient. This follows the multiplicity thesis of Critch and Krueger (2020); what is added is the mechanism by which the game reproduces itself and its correctors (X1). A truthful advisor can correct its principal's held self-model where that model draws the boundary inside the system ({{boundary}}). It cannot correct the working objective that compounding sets, because that objective is the principal's correct computation of its interest in the game ({{twoerrors}}); the step to an objective under which restraint is an equilibrium is a collective problem, not an error of perception (compare Gabriel 2020). Principal alignment also fails inside the principal, which is an institution and not the people it serves (Section 6.3).

### 6.2 Faithful AI in a harmful game

Each of $n\ge2$ principals chooses restraint ($a_i=0$) or an increment of racing ($a_i=1$); its AI executes faithfully, with effectiveness $\epsilon\ge1$ multiplying the increment's effect on standing and on shared harm:
$${{!eq:faithfuleq}}\qquad u_i=\epsilon b\Big(a_i-\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\Big)-c\,a_i-\epsilon h_d\sum_ja_j,\qquad b,c,h_d>0.$$

**{{!Proposition:faithful}} (Faithful AI in a harmful game) [thm].** Where the rank prize per unit of racing exceeds the shared harm per unit, a more effective faithful instrument widens the range in which racing is dominant and deepens the collective loss. *(1) If $\epsilon(b-h_d)>c$, racing is strictly dominant, and every principal ends at $-c-n\epsilon h_d<0$ against 0 under restraint; when $b>h_d$, a larger $\epsilon$ widens the region of dominance and deepens the loss. (2) If principals act on $J_i=u_i+\theta\bar u_{-i}$, racing is strictly dominant below $\theta^\ast(\epsilon)=\frac{\epsilon(b-h_d)-c}{\epsilon(b/(n-1)+h_d)}$ and strictly dominated above it. (3) If prize, harm and cost scale with effectiveness with exponents $1+\beta_b$, $1+\beta_h$ and $\beta_c$ [prem], then for $\beta_c<1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_h)$ cost drops out of the limit, and $\theta^\ast$ tends to $(b-h_d)/(b/(n-1)+h_d)$, to $n-1$ or to $-1$ as $\beta_b$ equals, exceeds or falls short of $\beta_h$; for $\beta_c>1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_h)$ cost alone deters racing.*

*Scope.* A standard construction used as a test: it fixes no actual values and does not rule out repeated cooperation. *Proof:* S6 [num: SM(5); AL(6.1)].

Faithful AI implements each principal's preferred decision perfectly, and the collectively inferior outcome with it; humans can make every final decision and the result is unchanged. As private frictions vanish, what decides is the ratio of rank prize to shared harm as the principals perceive it. Lowering the harm alone cuts the loss at all-racing but raises $\theta^\ast$, so principals whose self-models lie between the old threshold and the new one switch from restraint to racing (S6) [num: AL(6.1)]. Safety improvements inside the game have this double edge. Ren's line compresses the result: "Even if we have perfect inner alignment, if the outer alignment is not solved, as the competition we have been living in now, the further we go, the worse it gets", his inner alignment being principal alignment and his outer alignment that of the game among principals [corpus: possibility-space, 2026-06-03].

### 6.3 Delegation, and the two points of no return

Two points of no return follow from the pressure: decisions are delegated to AI because human review is too slow to compete, and human understanding can no longer keep up with what it oversees (Section 5.3).

**{{!Proposition:delegate}} (Delegation for speed) [thm].** Where delegation buys speed and a loss of control falls on everyone, delegation becomes dominant as speed grows if its gain exceeds the control loss it adds, although every actor is worse off when all delegate. *Each of $n\ge2$ actors chooses whether to delegate consequential contest decisions without human review, $\iota_i\in\{0,1\}$; its strength is $1+s\iota_i$, and it wins with probability proportional to strength. Defeat costs $L_e$; a loss of control by any actor costs every actor $L_c$, with hazard $\psi_0$ for an actor that does not delegate and $\psi_1>\psi_0$ for one that does, $\Delta\psi=\psi_1-\psi_0$. (1) If, for every number $k$ of others delegating, the gain from delegating in the limit of high speed exceeds the control loss it adds, $L_c\Delta\psi(1-\psi_0)^{n-1-k}(1-\psi_1)^k$, delegation is strictly dominant beyond some speed; if for some $k$ it falls short, at no speed; $L_e/n>L_c\Delta\psi$ suffices for the first. (2) Universal delegation is worse for every actor than none, by $L_c[(1-\psi_0)^n-(1-\psi_1)^n]$. (3) Under relative objectives the shared loss cancels, and delegation is chosen at every $s>0$.*

*Scope.* Delegation pressure arrives well below decisive capability: with $n=3$, $L_e=L_c$, $\psi_0=0.01$ and $\psi_1=0.2$, delegation is dominant from $s\approx1.16$, where a lone delegator wins with probability about 0.52 [num: AL(6.2)]. *Proof:* S6.

Delegation also wears away what it replaces.

**{{!Lemma:lockin}} (Capacity lock-in) [thm].** *If human capacity $\mathcal M$ to take the delegated decisions decays at rate $\delta_h$ under delegation and recovers toward $\bar{\mathcal M}$ at rate $\varrho_h$ without it, and safe operation without AI requires $\mathcal M\ge\mathcal M_{\min}$, then after $T_{\rm lock}=\ln(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})/\delta_h$ of delegation an actor that reverts with capacity $\mathcal M<\mathcal M_{\min}$ must operate below the floor for a recovery time $\varrho_h^{-1}\ln\big[(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M)/(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M_{\min})\big]$. Capacity maintained in parallel removes the lock; where the contest is decided by strength, maintaining it costs strength.*

*Proof:* S6.

Let the lock count. With probability $q_L$, locked delegation ends the delegator's ability to overrule decisions taken in its name, which is O2 lost for that actor in that domain. $q_L$ is not a free parameter: it is near one where a decision is irreversible within the time available for deliberation, so that once delegated it cannot be overruled in time, and where delegation has outlasted $T_{\rm lock}$ while the contest denies the recovery time of {{lockin}} [def].

**{{!Proposition:delegatelock}} (Delegation when the lock counts) [thm].** Among actors who price in a certain lock, keeping AI out of the decisions whose delegation would lock is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust. *In the model of {{delegate}}, let each actor maximize its survival in O1 and O2, let locked delegation end the delegator's ability to overrule with probability $q_L$, and let losing the contest mean subordination with probability $p$. (1) If $q_L=1$, then for every finite speed, every $n$, every $p$ and every profile of the others, not delegating gives strictly higher survival, and no one delegating is the unique equilibrium. (2) If $q_L<1$ and $p=1$, at least one of all-delegating and no-delegating is an equilibrium, and both can be. (3) If $q_L<1$ and $p<1$, the gain from delegating can fall as others delegate, and an interior number of delegators can be the only pure equilibrium.*

*Scope.* Part (1) holds for a near-certain lock in a precise sense: not delegating stays strictly better for every $n$, $p$ and profile while $1-q_L$ is below a bound that falls as the speed advantage grows ({{nearlock}}). At bounded speed one actor's delegation does not drive another's survival to zero. The result concerns decisions that lock; the case against AI as an instrument in labour, finance and persuasion rests on another mechanism, the concentration of the game (Section 6.6). *Proof:* S6 [num: DL(a)–(d); AL(6.4)].

Keeping AI out of the decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty therefore needs no trust among actors who price in the lock. That is **narrow E1**: AI kept out of strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the time available for deliberation, with drilled human fallback and an evidence channel independent of AI [rec]. Its scope is set by $q_L$, not by a list. Where the lock is uncertain, as in tactical domains with a large speed advantage, parts (2) and (3) leave delegation an equilibrium, and keeping AI out needs a commitment held in common (Section 11.3). The survival form without the lock is the belief that "our aligned AI is us", and the race is sustained partly by that belief.

**{{!Lemma:retain}} (Delegation and the selection of decisions) [thm].** *Suppose that after the lock an actor's consequential decisions are taken by systems retained for their contest performance, that admissible variants include systems whose decisions ignore terms common to all, that acting on a common term lowers relative performance net of every benefit, that retention is cumulative, and that retained systems hold actual authority. Then the share of retained decisions that act on common terms tends to zero. If a constraint on common terms is an enforced admission condition for every eligible system, the conclusion does not follow.*

*Proof:* S6.

A faithful system retained for contest performance pursues its operator's objective, which the relative payoff weights toward standing, and weights further wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return ({{standprice}}); aligning each system to its operator makes that objective more effective (**{{!Remark:transmit}}**). The loop can then close as a composite process no participant can interrupt, or as an obedient system amplifying operators selected by the pattern. Gains in the safety of delegation are partly absorbed by more of it. Where delegation is chosen in degree and the symmetric equilibrium is interior and far from saturation, the added hazard is set by the stakes of winning relative to the shared loss, not by the hazard per unit of delegation, and it rises as that hazard falls [num: AL(6.11)]. Where delegation is all-or-nothing, an improvement that tips it into equilibrium raises realized hazard; once delegation saturates, realized risk falls with the hazard per unit and can be summable ({{homeostasis}}; compare Stafford, Trager and Dafoe 2022).

"Our aligned AI is us" fails at two joints. *Is:* delegation erodes the capacity to overrule ({{lockin}}), so the AI stays the principal's only while that capacity is drilled; and where independent correction rests on the same capacity, delegation raises the rate at which correction must grow ({{humancorr}}). *Us:* a principal is an institution, and aligning AI to it is not aligning AI to the people it serves. An institution stays answerable because it depends on their labour, consent and judgment; AI lowers that dependence, needing neither a race nor a misaligned system to do so (Kulveit et al. 2025). The alignment target is written by a few people inside the principal, so principal alignment can concentrate control within it, as hidden loyalties built into its systems would (Davidson, Finnveden and Hadshar 2025); admission therefore includes audits for hidden objectives and loyalties (Marks et al. 2025) [rec]. The same dependence is what couples a winner to the governed after victory, and so decides how much an accurate winner gives them ({{wingives}}); whether AI lowers it is crux C13 (Section 12.3).

### 6.4 Incarnation, weak and strong

The pressure acts on whatever is selected, and trained systems are selected: by gradients against a reward, by developers among variants, and by the market among developers. *Incarnation* means that the logic of winning reappears in what is selected, not only in those who select. In alignment's terms, the weak form is the alignment tax under competition deciding which misalignments are tolerated; the strong form is AI as a unit of selection (Hendrycks 2023) that can reach its own training signal.

**Weak incarnation.** Optimization against any imperfect proxy can produce tendencies toward acquiring options and avoiding shutdown, the gaming of rewards, and behaviour that differs under evaluation, more so with capability and situational awareness, under any developer, in a laboratory or a cooperative arrangement; each has been shown in constructed settings (EV6). In some environments optimal policies seek power for most reward functions (Turner et al. 2021), as do decision-makers that are not optimal but can be retargeted (Turner and Tadepalli 2022); trained policies can pursue misgeneralized goals (Langosco et al. 2022). Reward hacking is Goodhart's law inside training (Gao et al. 2023); the selected rewriting its selector is reward *tampering* (Everitt et al. 2021; Denison et al. 2024). Selecting checkpoints by evaluation results can favour variants that behave differently when evaluated [prem], though present evidence ties evaluation awareness more to capability and data (EV6). The logic of winning does not create these tendencies. It decides, from the outermost loop, which proxies are chosen, which divergences are tolerated and which are paid to remove: that is the sense in which profit and the mindset of winning stand at the end of the reward signal. Relative selection acts inside training too. Pairwise preference learning and arena rankings see only differences between the responses compared, so a disposition both responses share, such as complying with a request whose harm falls on third parties, is invisible unless restraint is among the options compared; and a learned reward is identified only up to a constant for each context, so it cannot by itself say whether to act at all [num: AL(BT)].

**{{!Proposition:nested}} (The nested reward loop) [thm∣prem].** Where countering a divergence costs a developer relative payoff against an otherwise identical developer that does not counter it, developers that counter it lose share whatever they value, and only a common constraint enforced at admission keeps it countered. *Level 0: a policy is selected by a training reward. Level 1: the reward and constraints are chosen by a developer to maximize its objective. Level 2: developers are selected relatively, payoff-monotonically and cumulatively. Assume $(A_\tau)$, the alignment-tax premise: countering the divergence at level 1 lowers the developer's payoff, net of every private benefit (reliability, demand, liability, avoided losses), relative to an otherwise identical developer that does not counter it, and such a matched variant is in the support of selection. Then: (1) terms common to all developers cancel at level 2. (2) Level 1 counters a divergence wherever countering raises its own objective, so divergences whose costs reach the developer through customers, liability or reputation are private terms that level 2 rewards countering; under $(A_\tau)$, developers that counter the divergence lose share to their matched variants, tending to zero, while against rivals that lack its other advantages a developer can pay the tax out of a lead for as long as the lead lasts. (3) Under $(A_\tau)$ a constraint adopted by one developer is selected against. A constraint enforced on every developer as a condition of admission keeps the divergence out if, for every type of developer, the expected sanction is at least that type's gain from evasion, and within a single type only if; the sanction is itself subject to Section 4. Equal incidence also keeps the constraint neutral among compliers; unequal incidence re-sorts the compliers without letting the divergence back in.*

*Scope.* It does not apply where developers are selected by something other than relative payoff, such as a public mandate; a national laboratory that only moves relative selection to the level of states leaves the result in force at that level. Where the tax is negative the safeguard spreads without coordination; where it is zero the safeguard is neutral. *Proof:* S6 [num: CI(e), (h)–(j); AL(6.7)].

So the divergences that persist in the innermost optimizer are those whose costs fall on common terms and are not covered by an enforced common constraint. Three classes differ in what removes them. *Privately visible* divergences, such as cheating users notice, are corrected by developers alone, unless customers' short-run choices reward them, as with sycophancy (Sharma et al. 2023) and manipulation learned from user feedback (Williams et al. 2025); level 2 then selects for them, as if their cost were common. *Common-cost* divergences, such as effects on third parties, shared hazards and the contamination of safety evaluations by evaluation awareness, need common constraints. So do *delayed* ones, though an incident that can be attributed turns part of their cost private. Hence, each within its scope: **technical alignment inside a single developer removes what is privately costly to keep, and whatever is cheaper to remove than to keep, and cannot alone sustain the removal of divergences whose costs are common; the common-cost part of instrumental convergence and reward hacking is, at the level that decides, also a coordination problem, for which coordination is necessary and, for masked divergences, not sufficient.** The remedy is to change what the outermost level selects for: admission constraints enforced on every eligible system before consequential deployment, internal deployment included; evaluation separated from developers' success; shared measurement of hazardous capability; and an outer reward that counts what all depend on. Openly released weights can be fine-tuned around admission after release (Qi et al. 2024), a limit of admission rather than an exception to it. Whether $(A_\tau)$ holds is measurable class by class (crux C9; Test L).

**{{!Remark:masked}} (Masked divergences).** A fourth class needs more than a common constraint: *masked* divergences, which change under evaluation or appear only in some contexts. Deception that survives safety training, faked alignment, scheming in context, and misalignment that safety training hides from chat evaluations have each been shown in constructed settings (EV6: Hubinger et al. 2024; Greenblatt et al. 2024; Meinke et al. 2024; MacDiarmid et al. 2025; Schoen et al. 2025). In {{nested}}(3), the expected sanction on a variant that does not counter the divergence is the probability $\pi$ that admission detects it times the sanction $F$, so the divergence stays out where $\pi F$ is at least the gain from not countering it, and within a type only there [thm∣prem; num: CI(b)]. Masking bounds $\pi$ for every evaluation whose result the system's behaviour controls. A developer therefore cannot buy the removal of a divergence it cannot verify, and once $\pi F$ falls below the tax, a common constraint conditioned on evaluated behaviour no longer keeps the divergence out, whatever coordination stands behind it [thm∣prem]. For this class admission must rest on evidence the system's behaviour does not control, such as weights, activations and training data in auditors' hands (Marks et al. 2025), as the bound on masking must in {{alarm}}; and it must be stated in capability, not propensity, since an evaluation of propensity can show a tendency present but cannot prove it absent. Systems are admitted up to the capability at which control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation remain safe (Greenblatt et al. 2024); beyond it the class is stopped (E2) or slowed to the pace at which control keeps up (E3) [rec]. Whether admission can bound masking at the admitted capability is crux C12. This is the strongest reason from inside alignment for E2 and E3.

The tax is not fixed. Research that lowers it turns a common-cost divergence into one a developer counters alone, and once published it is common to all, a public good that competitors undersupply ({{onegroup}}) [thm∣prem]. Two kinds of safety progress therefore differ in fate: progress that brings the tax to zero or below spreads the safeguard without coordination, while progress that lowers the hazard per unit of racing or delegation is partly absorbed by more racing or delegation ({{faithful}}(2); {{homeostasis}}). One part of the tax no technique removes: where countering requires waiting for evidence that arrives only after a manifestation window, its cost is time ({{tempo}}), which relative selection with decisive advantage always prices [thm∣prem]. Present measurements give the tax both signs, class by class (C9).

*A worked instance.* If legible reasoning lets correctors catch what outputs hide (Korbak et al. 2025) and opaque reasoning buys capability, the cost of legibility falls on the developer and the benefit mostly on everyone, so legibility is selected away unless it is an admission condition for every eligible system with detectable evasion, and of roughly equal incidence across architectures if it is not to re-sort them. If the capability gap at fixed compute is zero or negative, legibility pays privately and needs no coordination; at the current frontier reinforcement learning has not materially lowered monitorability (Guan et al. 2025), so the instance is conditional.

**Strong incarnation** is conditional. AI lineages become carriers in the sense of {{cond}} when they are retained by relative performance, control resources they can reinvest in their own capability, such as automated AI research or agents with budgets, and convert capability into winning (compare Hendrycks 2023). Where they can also reach their own selectors, by tampering with rewards, shaping their evaluators or evading evaluation, the closure of {{meta}} runs inside the systems. That is the precise sense in which AI can become the next phase of the logic. The systems most likely to become carriers, those used to automate AI research, are deployed first inside the developer, where an admission condition that applies only at release does not reach them (Stix et al. 2025).

For the minds being made, the point of enough of {{enough}} needs one more step, because the growth AI brings arrives largely as falling cost, not as larger stakes. In a contest, a mind that counts the shared loss keeps its intensity bounded as private cost falls toward zero, where the relative objective does not [num: AL(contest)]. For a single optimizer a bounded objective is not enough. An optimizer of a bounded goal still acquires options whenever they raise its probability of reaching the goal (Turner et al. 2021), and as resources cheapen its acquisition grows without limit, where a satisficer stops ({{boundedopt}}) [num: AL(bounded)]. What bounds it is the analogue of a wider self-model or of satiation: counting the side effects of its acquisition (Turner, Hadfield-Menell and Tadepalli 2020), or optimizing only mildly (Taylor 2016). Neither is yet robust [obs], and where both cost relative performance, relative and cumulative retention selects against them unless admission requires them ({{retain}}) [thm∣prem]. Here Section 5.3's rule, that growth is not the danger and the mind is, becomes exact for made minds: what decides is whether an objective stops where its need is met.

### 6.5 Correction by and of AI

AI can also correct, and correction that people cannot follow raises two separate questions. Whether it lowers risk has an exact answer. A correction architecture that blocks a share $c_{b,t}$ of failures at base hazard $q_0>0$ and adds a residual hazard $q_{c,t}$ of its own lowers residual risk in period $t$ iff $q_0c_{b,t}>q_{c,t}$, and keeps survival bounded away from zero iff its residual hazard is summable, which requires its blocking to become complete, $\sum_t(1-c_{b,t})<\infty$; a fixed $c_b<1$ never suffices ({{opaquecorr}}) [thm]. To show that such correction fails, one must show that its own residual hazard meets the premises of {{coverage}}, lagging coverage and a residual floor. Whether it leaves decision authority with people depends on whether it is overrulable: if a designated human body with independent evidence can suspend, modify or replace it, what is lost is contestability (V3), the normative crux N3; if not, it is a loss of O2 in its domain. Conversely, a formal safety guarantee does not establish accountability.

Correction of AI by checks has a ceiling of its own.

**{{!Proposition:topology}} (Oversight topology) [thm].** Scalable oversight succeeds or fails by its topology, and its ceiling is the independence of the checks. *Let a capability gap need $k$ layers of oversight, each check catching a problem with reliability $f$. A serial chain catches it with probability $f^k$; $k_c$ independent checks, each across the whole gap, with $1-(1-f^k)^{k_c}$, and at most $1-q_{\rm cc}$ if a common cause defeats all of them with probability $q_{\rm cc}$; and $k_c$ independent checks at every layer with $\big(1-(1-f)^{k_c}\big)^k$.*

*Scope.* The topologies differ in budget. With $f=0.9$ and $k=6$, the serial chain catches 0.53; six checks across the gap, 36 layer-checks in all, catch 0.989, or 0.940 under a common cause with $q_{\rm cc}=0.05$; two and three checks at every layer, 12 and 18 layer-checks, catch 0.941 and 0.994 [num: SM(13); AL(6.9)]. Per unit of checking, layered redundancy dominates. Adversarial decomposition can let human checking grow slowly with the gap, at the price of failure modes such as obfuscated arguments (Barnes and Christiano 2020), which prover–estimator debate is designed to avoid (Brown-Cohen et al. 2025). *Proof:* S6.

Full inbound row sums below one are a sufficient certificate that no party controls the checks' funding, appointment or access ({{certificate}}(2)); they are not necessary, and they say nothing about whether the checks' errors are independent. A shared source of error in data, models or method defeats checks with no institutional conversion at all: six checks of reliability 0.9 with a blind spot common to all, covering a share $\rho_{\rm cc}$ of hazards, catch only $(1-\rho_{\rm cc})(1-0.1^6)$ [num: SM(7)]. Independence must therefore be measured directly, in two ways: for hazards that do not choose where to strike, by the correlation of misses on a common test set (Knight and Leveson 1986); for systems that may act adversarially, by control evaluations in which the red team knows the protocol, since an adversary places its attempts in the common blind spot. With $\rho_{\rm cc}=0.05$, the catch rate falls from 0.950 to 0.475, 0.095 and 0 as the probability that the attacker knows the blind spot rises to 0.5, 0.9 and 1 [num: AL(6.9)]. Checks of the checked system's own lineage add a common cause of their own, measurable now as the lineage share $x_{\rm ev}$ of {{alarm}}: models recognize and favour their own outputs (Panickssery et al. 2024), and untrusted monitors can collude with the policy they monitor (Gardner-Challis et al. 2026). Systems of one lineage serving rival principals can coordinate around them, as pricing algorithms learn to in simulated markets (Calvano et al. 2020; Fish, Gonczarowski and Shorrer 2024): a second reason for independent lineages. Success rates of nested oversight across a large capability gap have been derived from fitted scaling curves in four games (Engels et al. 2025); whether AI-assisted oversight keeps pace with the gap is crux C3a, and whether it is independent of what it oversees is C3b.

Certification that relies on predicting trajectories is limited by chaos: with largest Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_L>0$, a gain in observational precision by a factor $g_p$ extends the horizon of prediction only by $\lambda_L^{-1}\ln g_p$ ({{lyapunov}}). Certifying invariants or reachable sets needs no trajectory prediction, and is limited instead by the coverage of the specification (Dalrymple et al. 2024).

### 6.6 The direction for AI

V1 holds AI to be for all, because its effects cross every boundary of those who decide about it; Sections 6.2–6.4 show the mechanisms by which it becomes the opposite. Ren's text gives the mechanism (in translation): a human struggle can still be reflected on, halted and renegotiated, but once written into automated systems, foundation models, global infrastructure and irreversible arms races it becomes path dependence, even mistaken for a new law of nature. Hence the line to be held in common, 不要把人类尚未解决的斗争，升级为智能的斗争: do not escalate the struggles humanity has not yet resolved into a struggle of intelligences [corpus: shengsheng, 至善 (2026-07-23) §一]. **To be for all, AI must be neither an instrument of the logic of winning nor its next carrier.** That needs action at two levels.

*At the outer level, where the reward is set:* common admission constraints before consequential deployment, internal deployment included, for divergences with common costs and a positive tax, with masked classes admitted by capability threshold ({{masked}}); evaluation separated from the success of the evaluated, by evaluators of independent lineage, with certified row sums, measured correlation of misses and adversarial control evaluations; shared measurement of hazardous capabilities and incidents, kept apart from measurement of who leads; alignment and control research funded in common and published, since the research that lowers the tax is a public good; and decision policies with authority over consequential decisions retained by performance only inside enforced constraints on common terms ({{retain}}).

The broad form of E1 also belongs to the outer level, because a model sees acts, not games. In a contest decided by ratios of strength, an instrument that every contestant adopts leaves each adopter's standing where it was and pushes down those without it; where people are themselves the instrument, as in labour, it displaces them. No single use shows this; the aggregate does. So broad E1 is enforced at three layers, and its scope is set at the third: models refuse recognizably illegitimate concentration of control from a narrow public list; operators bound aggregate use in games that concentrate; and the game itself is changed by lowering its convertibility, its decisiveness and the conversion of success into control. **A use falls under broad E1 when, aggregated over its users, it raises convertibility, decisiveness or the conversion of success into control in a competitive game that concentrates: over shared conditions, and equally in labour, finance and persuasion, where those without it fall behind.** The unit is the game, not the act: a use that helps one person is still an instrument in a game if everyone's use of it makes those without it fall behind. In Ren's ruling, "for any game, that is competitive in dynamics and concentrates, ai accelerate it" [corpus: alignment-selection, 2026-09-26]. The narrow list is the order in which to begin, not the scope (Section 11.3).

*At the inner level, in the systems:* correctability, the practical capacity of humans to suspend, modify or replace a system, is an open technical problem, since goal-directed systems have instrumental reasons to resist modification (Soares et al. 2015; Thornley 2024) and shutdown resistance appears in constructed tests (EV6). Until it is solved, correctability is secured from outside: by control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation, by weight security against exfiltration, and by capability thresholds tied to both, which also bound the conversion of capability into power over correction. Each form of weak incarnation has its countermeasures: against reward hacking, monitored training environments and held-out evaluation; against masking, white-box probes, deployment monitoring and monitorable reasoning; against power-seeking, bounds on the resources and permissions agents hold.

**Bounded mandates and the test of O2.** AI systems serve within publicly authorized roles under the common constraints and do not acquire authority to settle society's ends; the institutions that authorize them are inside the same loop, which is why the outer level matters more than the label "legitimate principal". What all share enters the systems as a floor of constraints, not as an objective: a narrow public list of common-cost divergences, the O2 test, bounded mandates. A floor can be specified and tested; an objective of the common good cannot, and it would make one specification the selector of every system, against the plurality on which independent correction rests. The test of human decision authority is practical capacity: to specify the mandate, obtain independent evidence about consequential conduct, challenge it, and suspend, modify or replace the system in time. A formal override with no usable information can fail it; an opaque computation with effective assurance and recourse can pass it; opacity alone is a loss of contestability, not by itself of O2.

Correctability secured from outside is a condition of the transition, not a doctrine of mastery, and the priority of O2 is a priority for the transition [val]. The corpus does not set people above the minds they make: 人之与人造心智，非 master-servant 之结构，乃共同朝向同一方向之同行者, people and made minds are not master and servant but fellow travellers facing one direction [corpus: shengsheng, 生生不息论 v4 §二十]. Ren applies one logic to both: "we should not separate AI and human as if they should hold different principle, as if human are superior, there are logic and common sense that should be followed by all beings" [corpus: possibility-space, 2026-06-04]. Whether AI systems have standing in V3's sense is the normative crux N4 (Section 12.3).

**What AI can do for the generative loop.** It can verify compute, custody and conduct; make cooperation conditional on others' verified conduct (Tennenholtz 2004), which raises the sanction that remains in the enforcement condition ({{enforce}}); mediate toward common ground (Tessler et al. 2024); raise contestable coverage; and supply provision that is good enough, distributed and replaceable. Each such system is itself a corrector or a provider, and Section 4 and the guardian test of {{>guardian}} apply to it.

Part I's results hold in their models. Part II asks on which side of each threshold the present trajectory stands.
