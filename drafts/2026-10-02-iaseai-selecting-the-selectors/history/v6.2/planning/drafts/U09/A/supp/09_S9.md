## S9. Requirements

### Table {{#require}}, row by row

Each row is its cited result read as a condition on arrangements, in that result's model and over its horizon; the last column of the table states when the result does not apply. The derivations, and the examples that fix each row's scope, follow. Two kinds of entry in that column are kept apart: conditions under which a row is not needed, and conditions under which it is met without design, which the table marks as met, not excused.

- **R1, consequences return.** *The sharper form.* In the model of {{load}}, with other advantages equal and a race carrier present, $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\,ds\to\infty$ sends the race carrier's share to one and the load to $\bar e$, so $\limsup_tV\le(1-h_V\bar e/\rho)^+$, which (reach) puts below the floor. Viability at every horizon therefore needs $B(t)\not\to\infty$. That implies the form of {{safeguards}}(3): if $B(t_k)\le M$ along some $t_k\to\infty$, then $\frac1{t_k}\int_0^{t_k}\tau_{\rm eff}\,ds\ge\nu-M/t_k$, so $\limsup_t\frac1t\int_0^t\tau_{\rm eff}\,ds\ge\nu$. The converse fails, so the upper average is the weaker condition: with $\tau_{\rm eff}=\nu-1/(2\sqrt{t+1})$ the time average of liability tends to $\nu$ while $B(t)=\sqrt{t+1}-1\to\infty$, and on the logistic host with $\rho=0.05$, $h_V=0.08$ and floor 0.3, starting from a race share of 0.001, the host crosses its floor at $t=89$; with $\tau_{\rm eff}=\nu$ it never does [num: HO]. *Not at a finite horizon.* A trajectory with zero liability need not cross the floor before $H$: with the host starting at full capacity, floor 0.2, $\rho=0.01$, $h_V=0.02$ and zero liability, even full load from the start keeps $V(50)\ge0.435$, and the host crosses the floor only at $t=109.9$, although (reach) holds [num: HO]. At a finite horizon the necessary condition is weaker: liability must keep the cumulative advantage below that of the path that just reaches the floor at $H$, by monotone comparison.
- **R2, coverage of every actor whose share can grow.** Let a carrier of the race type stand outside the common constraints while the covered carriers bear effective liability $\tau_{\rm eff}$. Its payoff exceeds that of a covered carrier with load $e_l$ by $\nu\bar e-(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})e_l$, positive whenever the constraint costs the covered anything, so its cumulative advantage over every covered carrier tends to infinity; where nothing bounds its share, the share tends to one and {{load}} applies, the constraints on the others notwithstanding [thm]. Coverage is needed on a second ground: the race begins if any one actor prefers racing to restraint, and where those events are independent its probability tends to one as capable actors multiply ({{join}}). *Not needed* where harms stay local; where the uncovered carriers' combined share is bounded at $\bar p_{\rm unc}$ with $h_V\bar p_{\rm unc}\bar e<\rho(1-V_{\min})$, so that their load cannot carry the host below its floor; or within a horizon shorter than the crossing time.
- **R3a, the rate law.** {{corrrate}}(2) gives both halves in the field model with a seeded evaluation domain, $[H_\gamma]$, a positive cultivation channel and no per-unit floor. *Sufficient:* with $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and $d_0>d^\ast(\gamma_0)$ the certificate holds at every date, for any adjustment of capacity that starts at or below its selected level and does not raise capacity while it is above that level. *Necessary* where capacity tracks its selected level, $\liminf_tq/q^\ast>0$, as for carriers that optimize and for adjustment at a constant rate: below the rate law the certificate fails beyond a finite time. Where correction rests on human capacity eroded at $\delta_h$, with elasticity $w$, both halves hold with the threshold raised by $w\delta_h$ ({{humancorr}}(1)); where the erosion stops at a floor $\mathcal M_{\min}$, the rate returns to $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and the required level rises by the factor $(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})^w$. *Not needed:* with $\chi=0$, or the conversion channels removed, a unit of capacity returns nothing, and the requirement is $A/c_{\rm ev}$, its level without second-order selection ({{corrrate}}(1)); with a per-unit cost floor that AI does not lower, cultivation stops once $d\ge d_f$ and the requirement levels off at $\max\{A/c_{\rm ev},d_f\}$, a level, not a rate; with a floor that falls at $\alpha_f<\alpha_{\rm cor}$, cultivation ends at a finite date and the certificate holds from some date on, though not necessarily before it: with $f_0=0.1$, $\alpha_f=0.05$ and $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0.051$ it fails at $t=38.4$ while cultivation ends only at $t=588$ [num: FF]; with capacity selected gradually and its variance not rising faster than capacity itself, capacity stays bounded and correction growing at any positive rate certifies in the end, after a possible transient failure ({{capdyn}}); with nothing captured; and within a horizon shorter than the failure time. A lump-sum cost of capacity does not belong on this list: it delays cultivation and leaves the requirement unbounded as control cheapens ({{corrrate}}(3)) [num: FF].
- **R3b, no core.** {{certificate}}(4): no capture from every initial state needs an empty core of mutually sustaining capture, since a nonempty core's configuration is stable and attracts the states near it. *Not needed* if only the states actually reached must be excluded: with a row sum of two, the low initial state $(0.1,0.1)$ stays in the invariant rectangle $[0,0.1]\times[0,0.2]$ and converges to no capture [num: FA]. A full row sum below one is the stronger certificate, sufficient from every state ({{certificate}}(2)).
- **R4, breakouts within the bound.** {{enforce}}(2): a deviation with $D_i(a)>v_i$ is deterred iff $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)$, so enforced restraint needs the inequality for every actor and every such deviation; {{restraint}} classifies the means. *Not needed* where no deviation reaches a position beyond punishment ($p_d=0$: Branch III, or Branch I′ above its saturating level, {{branches}}), where the breakout term vanishes and ordinary repetition decides on the terms of {{restraint}}; or where $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$ for every actor and deviation, that is $\bar p_{d,i}\ge1$. Then {{eq:enforceeq}} holds at every $p_d\in[0,1]$, because for $D_i\ge v_i$ its left side is $(1-\delta)g_i-\delta p_d(C_i-D_i)\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-D_i)\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i)$. A decisive position worth less than cooperation does not give this by itself: with $C_i=1$, $D_i=0.9$, $v_i=0$, $g_i=2$ and $\delta=0.9$, $\bar p_{d,i}=0.864$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} fails at $p_d=0.87$ and $0.9$; with $g_i=0.5$, so that $(1-\delta)g_i=0.05\le0.09=\delta(C_i-D_i)$, it holds at $p_d=0$, $0.5$, $0.9$ and $1$ [num: ES]. *Met, not excused,* in Branch II: detection and response within the time a breakout needs give $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, and R4 reads $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d,i}$ ({{detectlink}}).
- **R5, a summable uncovered share.** *Necessary under (residual floor) alone.* If on every surviving history the uncovered share is at least $\underline u_t$, (residual floor) makes the conditional probability of the irreversible event at $t$ at least $h\underline u_t$, and the chain rule gives
$$\Pr(\Phi_H)\le\prod_{t<H}(1-h\underline u_t)\le\exp\Big(-h\sum_{t<H}\underline u_t\Big),$$
which tends to zero when $\sum_t\underline u_t=\infty$. Survival bounded away from zero therefore needs a summable uncovered share [thm∣prem]. *Sufficient* under a residual bound below one: if the residual probability is at most $\bar hu_t$ with $\bar h<1$ and $\sum_tu_t<\infty$, survival stays bounded away from zero ({{coverage}}). Under (shared failure) both statements are every actor's. Neither (coverage lags) nor (variety grows) enters the necessity; they say when R5 fails on a continuation branch, which is what {{coverage}} adds. *Not needed* where (residual floor) fails, as where a correction architecture lowers residual risk below $hu_t$ with blocking that becomes complete ({{opaquecorr}}); at a finite horizon what counts is the exposure up to it, which a freeze after a sprint keeps finite and a rollback below coverage ends ({{freeze}}). *Met, not excused,* where hazardous variety stops growing at a level set by need while coverage keeps growing, so that the uncovered share reaches zero in finite time ({{enough}}).
- **R6, independent observation.** {{alarm}}: where an upper bound on masking comes from a channel the field does not control, an alarm on reports set correspondingly higher fires no later than the true condition reaches its target; for masking by the evaluated model, the bound must come from evidence its behaviour does not control. {{talk}}: between two rivals with relative objectives cheap talk carries nothing, so trust without good will needs signals that can be verified. Both are sufficient. *Necessary on one route:* where restraint rests on detection and response, {{detectlink}} turns R4 into the condition $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d,i}$ on timely detection. *Not needed* where channels that observe actions directly, such as verifiable deployments, compute and the performance of contracts, suffice for the purpose at hand.
- **R7, standing.** V3. {{club}}(1) shows that inclusion stopping at the edge of a club over-produces harm to those outside relative to the system optimum; it does not show that O1 or O2 is reached, so the requirement is normative.
- **R8a, admission constraints that hold.** {{nested}}(3), with {{blind}}(3) type by type: under relative, cumulative selection among developers and $(A_\tau)$, with a matched variant admissible, a divergence whose costs fall on common terms persists unless a constraint enforced on every developer as a condition of admission covers it, and that constraint keeps the divergence out if, for every type that can evade it, the expected sanction is at least that type's gain from evasion, and within a type only if. Across types the condition is sufficient, not necessary. In one field with equal baselines, compliance takes the field iff the best-paid variant complies, so a type whose evasion pays still loses its evaders to another type's compliers when those earn more: with compliance costs 0.1 and 0.2 and expected sanctions 1.0 and 0.15, the second type's evasion pays and still vanishes, while with 0.05 in place of 0.15 evasion takes the field [num: CI]. The matched variant is load-bearing: a safeguarded developer with baseline 2 and tax 0.1 outgrows an unsafe one with baseline 1, and falls to a share of $2\times10^{-9}$ by $t=200$ against its matched twin; a tax of 0.01 is paid out of a lead of 0.02 for as long as the lead lasts, and not out of a lead of 0.005 [num: CI]. *Not needed* where developers are selected by something other than relative payoff, such as a public mandate, though a national laboratory that only moves relative selection to the level of states leaves it in force there; where the tax is zero or negative; and where decisions are not retained by contest performance ({{retain}}). *The liability route.* A liability on the developer for the harm of a divergence enters its payoff as a private cost of not countering it. Where the harm can be attributed and the liability collected, and it exceeds the tax, $(A_\tau)$ fails and the safeguard spreads without coordination ({{nested}}, Scope). It reaches what can be attributed: delayed and masked divergences are where attribution fails, harms beyond the developer's assets call for regulation in advance (Section 10.3), and the liability is itself imposed by correctors (R3). *Limits, not exceptions.* Admission placed at release does not reach internal deployment; openly released weights can be fine-tuned around any constraint, so the decision to release is the last at which admission acts; and for masked divergences, admission conditioned on evaluated behaviour fails once the expected sanction it can impose falls below the tax ({{masked}}), so admission is stated in capability, up to the level at which control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation remain safe, with the class under R5 beyond it.
- **R8b, equal incidence.** {{blind}}(2): a constraint that costs every complier the same additive amount is a common term among them and leaves their relative growth unchanged, and one whose cost differs re-sorts them toward those it costs least. Equal incidence is therefore necessary and sufficient for neutrality among compliers, and not needed to keep a divergence out: with compliance costs 0.1 and 0.2 and a sanction of 1, the compliers re-sort to shares 0.88 and 0.12 by $t=20$ while the compliant share is 0.999999975, and with equal costs their relative shares stay at 1.000000 [num: CI]. What it protects against is the second direction of capture, a rule whose cost falls on challengers ({{capture}}); that this protection is required rests on V5, since a rule that bars challengers gives the successful more advantage in the same game [val].
- **R9, human decision authority kept.** {{lockin}}: with the capacity to take delegated decisions decaying at $\delta_h$ under delegation and recovering at $\varrho_h$ without it, an actor that has delegated for longer than $T_{\rm lock}=\ln(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})/\delta_h$ and reverts must operate below the floor of safe operation for a recovery time. Where operating below that floor is not an option, as in a contest that does not pause, the delegator can no longer overrule: the loss O2 names, in that domain. Capacity kept in practice removes the lock, and where the contest is decided by strength it costs strength, so under relative selection it is not kept alone ({{blind}}(2)). Where delegation is dominant at the speed reached ({{delegate}}(1)), or the lock is uncertain, so that delegating by all can be an equilibrium ({{delegatelock}}(2)), the capacity survives only under a commitment binding every actor [thm∣prem]. *Not needed* where delegation leaves the capacity intact, or where automation is bounded and reversible within the time available, which the test of O2 in Section 6.6 decides. *Met without an arrangement* where every actor prices in a lock it knows to be certain: not delegating is then a strict equilibrium that needs no trust ({{delegatelock}}(1)), the narrow E1 of Section 11.3.

**Sufficient safeguards.** Full row sums below one certify each corrector domain against every state of the others ({{certificate}}(2)). The safeguards (I1)–(I4) give viability once in force, and over the transient when the liability margin is at least $\Delta_{\min}$: on the logistic host with the race strategy at 99% of activity, $\Delta_{\min}$ is 0.108, 0.165 and 0.355 for floors 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5, and on a host that degrades by itself below a threshold the margin must keep the transient above that threshold, 0.26 to 0.51 in the cases computed, with a shortfall that is permanent ({{safeguards}}) [num: HO]. Not building the decisive class, common custody, and detection and response faster than breakout lower $p_d$ by changing the premise of R4 ({{restraint}}). Verification with an expected sanction $\upsilon F$ at least the net gain from exit deters irreversible exit from an agreement whose overhang grows ({{overhang}}).

### Proof of {{nounilateral}}

(1) With other advantages equal, a carrier that loads less than another earns $\nu-\tau_{\rm eff}$ less per unit of the difference, so while $\tau_{\rm eff}<\nu$ its share falls relative to the other's ({{load}}). Correction keyed to no one costs the carrier that funds it and benefits every carrier, so it is selected against among otherwise identical carriers ({{noinside}}). Either contribution is a cost borne by one carrier, which relative selection removes ({{blind}}(2)).

(2) Let a rule-maker reach only some carriers. Imposing the liability on them lowers their payoffs against the carriers it does not reach, by (1), and so lowers the rule-maker's standing among the $n_s$ rule-makers, while the benefit, a host kept above its floor, is common to all. Unilateral imposition is therefore restraint in the commons of {{wow}}, with $\Upsilon_s$ the value to the field of a unit of it over its private cost, and full depletion, no rule-maker imposing, is the equilibrium once $\lambda_s>(\Upsilon_s-n_s)/(n_s-1)$. Whether an agreement among the rule-makers is an equilibrium is governed by {{assure}}.

(3) Where captured rule-making leaves $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$ (Section 4.1), the private payoff of a unit of load is $\nu-\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, negative iff $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})>\nu$, and only then does the selection of {{load}} reverse. That captured rule-making acts on liability in this form is the join of the field model to the load model, an interface of S13. $\square$

### Coverage can unravel

**{{!Remark:unravel}} (The participation threshold) [thm∣prem].** *Let $x$ be the share of capable activity outside an arrangement, let a participant of type $k$ bear a cost $c_k$ of its common constraints, and let the expected cost of standing outside, the sanction, the access forgone and the provision lost, be $s_k(x)$, continuous and strictly decreasing in $x$, because sanctions are funded by participants, shared provision is worth less with fewer inside, or rule-making is captured by those outside. Under cumulative, payoff-monotone selection between staying and leaving within a type, this is {{blind}}(3) with evasion read as exit. If $s_k(0)>c_k>s_k(1)$, staying and leaving are both stable, separated by the tipping share $x_k^\dagger$ at which $s_k(x_k^\dagger)=c_k$; if $s_k(1)\ge c_k$ staying is globally stable, and if $s_k(0)\le c_k$ leaving is. Participants who choose by best response, each staying iff $s_k(x)\ge c_k$, have the same two stable states and the same tipping share. The arrangement's participation threshold is the coverage $1-\min_kx_k^\dagger$, the minimum taken over the types that tip, set by the type for which leaving first pays.*

An exit moves the outside share at once by the leaver's own share, so one large participant can carry it past a type's tipping share, and the exits of that type then follow without a further shock, raising the outside share toward the next threshold. With a cost of staying of 0.3 and a cost of standing outside of $0.48(1-x)$ the tipping share is 0.375; with $0.6-0.4x$ it is 0.75; with $0.5+0.5(1-x)$, which never falls below 0.5, staying is globally stable [num: CI, evasion read as exit]. Conditions of access act on $s_k$: trade and access tied to participation, provision available only inside, and sanctions financed by formula rather than by the participants keep the cost of standing outside from falling as others leave, and once $s_k(1)\ge c_k$ for every type nothing can unravel. What raises the cost of staying moves the threshold the other way. A pause whose end is judged on several conjunctive indicators measured with noise errs toward "not ready", so an arrangement that holds participants longer than its evidence warrants spends its coverage (Section 11.3) [conj].

### The crosswalk

The paper states what an adequate arrangement must do in several places, each for its own purpose: the thresholds of Part I ({{thresholds}}), the generative marks G1–G7 (Section 8.2), the safeguards (I1)–(I4) (Section 10.3), the durability tests D1–D8 ({{durability}}) and the readiness dimensions Q1–Q7 ({{readiness}}). The crosswalk maps each row of {{require}} onto the others, by label; each list is stated only in its home. A dash means that no entry of that list asks for the row.

*Read by threshold.* Every threshold is guarded by some row. Compounding and Standing are guarded only through R4, by the time a lead needs to become decisive and by the bound the working objective sets; what reverses them directly is not a requirement but a mark, because the models give sharing, provision and non-convertible reward as alternatives, any one of which can carry the margin ({{compound}}).

*Read by mark.* G1, G3, G4, G6 and G7 each answer rows. G2 and G5 answer none alone and meet several at once, through the won share and through convertibility, which is why the paper argues for them (Section 10). No mark answers R7 or R9: the marks describe a change in what is selected, and standing (V3) and decision authority (O2) are what any selection must leave in place.

*Read by test.* D4, D5, D7 and D8 test rows directly, and D3 tests R7, R8a and R9 after delegation. D1, D2 and D6 test no requirement; they test how an arrangement fails and what it keeps running, and their counterparts are the fallback ({{fallback}}), the participation threshold ({{unravel}}) and the continuity of essential services, O3, which no row carries because the rows concern what the contest selects.

*Read by readiness.* Q1 and Q3–Q7 each ask a row. Q2 asks about governing purposes, which no row requires: requirements constrain functions, and selection acts whatever carriers value ({{load}}). R1, R2, R7 and R8b have no readiness dimension. Readiness is asked of the frontier during a pause and presupposes them; a pause that ends with R1 or R2 unmet resumes a field in which load is selected ({{load}}).

*Other lists.* The four properties of provision in Section 10.1 are rows applied to provision itself: distributed is R3, many providers below the size at which influence over correctors pays ({{influence}}); good enough is R4 and R5, a point of enough that confers no decisive advantage ({{enough}}); replaceable is R9, human capacity kept above its lock-in floor ({{lockin}}); and for everyone is R2 and R7. The outer level of Section 6.6 is R8a, R3a and R6 for AI, and its inner level, correctability secured from outside, is R8a's capability threshold for masked classes together with R9. The requirements of {{reachable}} are of the path to an arrangement, not of the arrangement; persistence, the last of them, asks for the verification safeguard, row sums below one and (I4), each held for its dwell time.
