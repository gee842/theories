## S9. Requirements

### Table {{#require}}, row by row

Each necessary row is the cited result read as a necessary condition in its model and over its horizon; the last column of the core's table states when the cited result does not apply. The derivations and the counterexamples that fix the scope follow. Numbers are from the blocks of S15 named in brackets.

- **R1, consequences return.** {{safeguards}}(3) and {{load}}: under (reach), with a race carrier present, viability at every horizon needs long-run effective liability at least the private advantage. *The two forms.* If the cumulative advantage $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\,ds$ grows without bound, {{load}} applies and the host falls below its floor, so viability at every horizon needs $B$ not to tend to $+\infty$. That implies $\limsup_t\frac1t\int_0^t\tau_{\rm eff}\,ds\ge\nu$: were the upper limit below $\nu$, there would be $\epsilon>0$ and $T_0$ with the running average at most $\nu-\epsilon$ beyond $T_0$, and then $B(t)\ge\epsilon(t-T_0)+B(T_0)\to\infty$. The average form is what {{safeguards}}(3) states, and its proof in S10 uses exactly this step; the cumulative form is sharper, and the table's first column names both. *The average form is necessary and not sufficient:* with $\tau_{\rm eff}=\nu-1/(2\sqrt{t+1})$ the running average tends to $\nu$, so the upper limit is met with equality, yet $B(t)=\sqrt{t+1}-1\to\infty$, and from a race share of 0.1% the host crosses its floor at $t=89$; with $\tau_{\rm eff}=\nu$ it stays above it [num: HO]. *Not at a finite horizon:* a zero-liability trajectory need not cross the floor before $H$. With the host at full capacity, floor 0.2, $\rho=0.01$, $h_V=0.02$ and zero liability, even full load from the start keeps $V(50)\ge0.435$, and crosses the floor only at $t=109.9$, which is $\ln3/\rho$ here, although (reach) holds [num: HO]. At a finite horizon the necessary condition is weaker: liability must keep the cumulative advantage below the path that just reaches the floor at $H$, by monotone comparison.
- **R2, coverage.** {{load}} and {{nounilateral}}(1): an uncovered race carrier with unbounded cumulative advantage takes the field; {{join}}: the race begins if any single capable actor has $T_i>R_i$, so coverage must reach the most race-inclined, and the probability that some actor is of that kind tends to one as capable actors multiply. *Not needed* when harms are local, when the uncovered carriers' combined maximal load cannot reach the floor, $h_Vp_{\rm unc}\bar e<\rho(1-V_{\min})$, or within a horizon shorter than the crossing time.
- **R3a, the rate law.** {{corrrate}}(2) in both halves, and {{humancorr}}(1). *Sufficient at any speed:* if $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and $d_0>d^\ast(\gamma_0)$, the certificate holds at every date for every adjustment of capacity that starts at or below its selected level and does not raise capacity while it is above that level, because evaluation's row sum is then bounded by its quasi-static value, which is nonincreasing (S4). *Necessary where capacity tracks:* if $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and $\liminf_tq/q^\ast>0$, the cultivated term is at least a fixed fraction of its quasi-static value, which grows without bound, so the certificate fails beyond a finite time. Optimizing carriers track; adjustment at a constant rate $k$ tracks, with failure delayed by about $\eta\ln(1+g/k)/(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})$, $g=(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-\alpha_{\rm cor})/\eta$, within 1.4% of the simulated delay in six cases [num: QS]; gradient selection whose variance grows with capacity tracks; gradient selection with constant variance does not, because its adjustment rate falls with the cost of control and its capacity stays bounded by $q_0+V\chi\hat Yp^{2-\xi_v}/(d_0\alpha_{\rm cor})$, so that the certificate holds eventually for every $\alpha_{\rm cor}>0$ [num: QS]. A requirement wants the sufficient half, which is the safe design rule; the necessary half says when a slower rate is a real failure and not a delayed one. *The human-capacity term:* where correction acts at $d_{\rm eff}=d\,(\mathcal M/\mathcal M_0)^w$ and delegation erodes $\mathcal M$ at $\delta_h$, $d_{\rm eff}$ grows at $\alpha_{\rm cor}-w\delta_h$, so both halves hold with the threshold raised to $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)+w\delta_h$ [num: QS]. *Not needed* with $\chi=0$ or the channels removed; with nothing captured; within a horizon shorter than the failure time; with a per-unit cost of control that AI does not lower, or lowers more slowly than correction grows, since cultivation then ends at a finite date and the certificate thereafter needs only $d>A/c_{\rm ev}$, provided it also holds through the transient ({{corrrate}}(3)); and where capacity adapts more slowly than its selected level drifts, the constant-variance case above. The quasi-static model is conservative while the cost of control falls monotonically, and optimistic after a reform is withdrawn and wherever correction rests on a slow variable that the contest erodes; both cases are S4's.
- **R3b, no core.** {{certificate}}(4): no capture from every initial state needs an empty core of mutually sustaining capture. *Not needed* if only the states actually reached must be excluded: with a row sum of two, the low initial state $(0.1,0.1)$ stays in the invariant rectangle $[0,0.1]\times[0,0.2]$ and converges to no capture, with $x=3.2\times10^{-10}$ at $t=20$ [num: FA]. A full row sum below one is the stronger, sufficient certificate, against every state ({{certificate}}(2)); it is not necessary, and it does not certify that the correctors' errors are independent ({{topology}}).
- **R4, enforceable breakouts.** {{enforce}}(2): where $D_i>v_i$, restraint is sustained iff $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}=1-[(1-\delta)g_i+\delta(D_i-C_i)]/[\delta(D_i-v_i)]$, deviation by deviation; {{restraint}} classifies the means; {{detectlink}} writes the same condition as $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d,i}$. *The exception.* Restraint survives every $p_d\in[0,1]$ iff {{eq:enforceeq}} holds at $p_d=0$ and at $p_d=1$, the two ends of a condition affine in $p_d$; at $p_d=1$ it reads $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$. Directly: for $D_i>v_i$ and this inequality, $(1-\delta)g_i-\delta p_d(C_i-D_i)\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-D_i)\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i)$ for every $p_d$, so $\bar p_{d,i}\ge1$. *$D_i<C_i$ alone is not an exception:* with $C=1$, $D=0.9$, $v=0$, $g=2$ and $\delta=0.9$, $\bar p_d=0.864$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} fails at $p_d=0.87$ and $0.9$ although $D<C$; with $g=0.5$, $(1-\delta)g=0.05\le\delta(C-D)=0.09$ and restraint holds at $p_d=0$, $0.5$, $0.9$ and $1$ [num: ES]. V6.1's column entry "$D<C$" is withdrawn. *Met without design* in Branch III, above the saturating level of Branch I′, and in Branch II with timely detection, each a route by which $p_d$ is small ({{branches}}); and where $D_i\le v_i$ the bound of {{enforce}}(2) does not bind, since the condition is then weakest at $p_d=0$ and the ordinary condition of repetition, $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-v_i)$, decides.
- **R5, summable uncovered share.** {{coverage}} and its converse, with {{nonsum}}: under (residual floor) a non-summable uncovered share gives a non-summable residual hazard, and survival tends to zero; a summable one keeps it bounded away from zero. *Not needed* if (coverage lags) or (residual floor) fails: a correction architecture whose blocked share tends to one makes residual risk summable and refutes (residual floor) for its class ({{opaquecorr}}; S6); at finite horizons the exposure of {{freeze}} replaces it. (variety grows) is not a premise of the theorem: it is what makes (coverage lags) plausible on a continuation branch, and its failure removes the motive, not the result.
- **R6, independent observation.** {{alarm}} and {{masking}}: an alarm set above a known bound on masking fires no later than the true condition reaches its target, and the bound must come from a channel the evaluated does not control; {{talk}}: trust without good will needs objectives not opposed on the topic of talk, or signals that are costly or verifiable; {{detectlink}}: where punishment is triggered by detection, the clock $\upsilon$ is what enforcement rests on. Sufficient; not necessary where channels that observe actions directly, compute, deployments and the performance of contracts, replace host reports, since they do not pass through the masking $m$ of {{eq:mask}}.
- **R7, standing.** V3. {{club}}(1) shows that harm to outsiders is over-produced relative to the system optimum whenever $m_o\ell_o>0$; it does not show that O1 or O2 is reached, so the requirement is normative, declared and not derived.
- **R8, admission constraints.** {{nested}}(3), {{retain}} and {{blind}}(3). Under relative, cumulative selection among developers, $(A_\tau)$ and a matched unsafe variant, a divergence with common costs persists unless a constraint enforced on every developer as a condition of admission covers it, and the constraint keeps the divergence out iff, for every type that can evade, the expected sanction is at least that type's gain from evasion. *The split.* Neutrality among compliers and the survival of compliance are two conditions with two sources. Equal incidence ({{blind}}(2)) preserves the compliers' relative shares; it is not what keeps the divergence out. With two developer types whose compliance costs are 0.1 and 0.2 and a sanction of 1 for evasion, the compliant share is $1-2.5\times10^{-8}$ at $t=20$ while the compliers are re-sorted to 0.88 and 0.12; with equal costs their ratio is preserved; with sanctions 1 and 0.15 the second type's evasion pays and still loses to the first type's compliers, and with 1 and 0.05 evasion takes over, so "sanction at least the gain from evasion for every type" is sufficient for compliance across types and necessary within a type [num: CI]. *The matched variant.* A safeguarded developer with baseline 2 and a tax of 0.1 against an unsafe one with baseline 1 takes the whole field; its matched twin, baseline 2 without the tax, reduces it to $2\times10^{-9}$ at $t=200$; a tax of 0.01 paid from a lead of 0.02 survives, from 0.005 it does not [num: CI]. The tax is paid out of a lead while the lead lasts, and {{nested}}(2) is about variants, not about every pair of developers. *Not needed* where developers are selected by public mandate; where the tax is zero or negative for the class, so that the safeguard spreads without coordination (C9); where decisions are not retained by contest performance; and where a liability turns the common cost into a private one, the route of R1 and of Section 3.2. *Limits*, stated under the core's table and not in its last column, because none removes the requirement: admission reaches only what it admits. Internal deployment is why the requirement is stated for consequential deployment, internal deployment included. Openly released weights can be fine-tuned around the constraint after release. For a masked divergence, {{masked}}: the detection probability $p_{\rm det}$ is bounded by what the system shows under evaluation, so once $p_{\rm det}F$ falls below the tax, admission conditioned on evaluated behaviour no longer keeps the divergence out, and admission is by capability threshold, with E2 or E3 beyond it (C12).

**Sufficient safeguards.** Full row sums below one certify each corrector domain against every state ({{certificate}}(2)), and say nothing about the independence of errors ({{topology}}). The safeguards of Section 10.3 give viability with a margin $\Delta_{\min}$ set by the host, the floor, the initial composition and the speed of selection, 0.108, 0.165 and 0.355 for floors 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5 on the forgiving host with the race strategy at 99%, and on a host that degrades by itself below a threshold the margin must also keep the transient above that threshold, where a shortfall is permanent ({{safeguards}}(1)) [num: HO]. Not building, custody and detection faster than breakout change the premise of {{enforce}} ({{restraint}}(a)). Verification with $\upsilon F$ at least the net exit gain deters irreversible exit ({{overhang}}).

### Proof of {{#nounilateral}}

(1) With $\tau_{\rm eff}<\nu$, a contribution to a safeguard, whether a lower load, the funding of correction keyed to no one, or the cost of verification, lowers the contributor's payoff and no other carrier's. Under payoff-monotone selection a cost borne by a single carrier is selected against ({{blind}}(2), the single-carrier case), and correction keyed to no one is selected against among otherwise identical carriers whatever it does for the field ({{noinside}}). (2) A liability imposed by one rule-maker leaves the carriers it does not reach uncovered; by hypothesis their combined maximal load satisfies (reach), so their cumulative advantage is unbounded and {{load}} applies: the host crosses its floor whatever that rule-maker imposes on its own carriers, and neither R1 nor R2 is met alone. With the rule-makers as the players of the steady-state commons of S3, each choosing how much liability to impose on the carriers it reaches, the host's gain from a unit of liability is common to all rule-makers and the loss of standing by the carriers it reaches falls on the imposer alone, so unilateral imposition is restraint in {{wow}} and is not chosen once $\lambda_s>(\Upsilon_s-n_s)/(n_s-1)$. Whether an agreement among them to impose it jointly is an equilibrium is governed by {{assure}}. (3) By {{eq:payoff}} with $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, the private payoff of a unit of load is $\nu-\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, negative iff $x_{\rm rule}<1-\nu/\tau$. $\square$

### The participation threshold is a tipping share

Let a fraction $x$ of the covered carriers evade or leave a common constraint whose compliance costs $c$, and let the expected sanction on evasion be funded by the compliers, or detection be run by them, so that it falls as they thin out: $s(x)$ decreasing in $x$ with $s(0)>c>s(1)$. Under payoff-monotone selection the evading share follows $\dot x\propto x(1-x)\,(c-s(x))$: full compliance and full evasion are both stable, and the tipping share $x^\ast$ is the root of $s(x)=c$ ({{blind}}(3); S3). With $s(x)=0.48(1-x)$ and $c=0.3$, $x^\ast=0.375$; with $s(x)=0.6-0.4x$, $x^\ast=0.75$; where $s(1)>c$ compliance is the only attractor, and where $s(0)<c$ evasion is [num: CI]. The participation threshold of the core's paragraph is the coverage at the tipping point, $1-x^\ast$: below it the sanction no longer exceeds the gain from evasion for some type, and the constraint borne by the remaining compliers becomes a cost borne by them alone ({{nounilateral}}(1)). The exit of one large participant moves $x$ by that participant's share at once, which is why the threshold is stated in coverage weighted by share and not in numbers of parties (compare Barrett 2003 on minimum participation). The instance at EV18, one rule for all in which the largest participant's groups were exempted from two of its rules, is read in X10; here it fixes the kind of event the paragraph describes: an exemption at the top is a fall in coverage weighted by share, without any fall in the number of parties.

**What the crosswalk shows.** Every necessary requirement holds one threshold of {{thresholds}} on its safe side, and three thresholds have no requirement: Compounding, Standing and Delegation are moved by provision beneath the game, by sharing and by E1, which Sections 10 and 11.3 treat as design and arrangement, since the paper does not show any of them necessary.
