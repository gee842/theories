## 9. What any adequate arrangement must do

> **Claim.** Before designs are compared, what every adequate design must do can be stated, each requirement with the kind of support it has: necessary in a named model over a named horizon, sufficient, or normative [def]. Where (reach) holds with a race carrier present, and where no single rule-maker reaches every carrier whose load can reach the floor, several of the requirements cannot be met by any actor alone, and competition among actors does not supply them [thm].
>
> **Why.** A requirement stated with its model and horizon can be checked against any design, regulated competition included, which can then be found to meet it. A unilateral contribution to a safeguard is a cost borne by one carrier, which relative selection removes; and the rule-makers who could impose a liability on every carrier are themselves in a contest of standing.

Part I proved, one model at a time, where restraint fails. Read as requirements, the same results say what any arrangement must do, and in which model and over which horizon each is necessary. The kind of support decides how a row may be used. A necessary row is a test: a design that lacks it fails in the row's model within its horizon, and the last column names the routes by which a design can do without it. A sufficient row is a route, not a test. A normative row rests on a value of Section 1.3 and is declared, not derived. S9 derives each row, fixes its scope with the counterexamples, and gives the crosswalk from these rows to the thresholds of {{thresholds}}, the generative marks of Section 8.2, the durability tests of {{durability}} and the readiness dimensions of Section 11.3, so that the paper's lists are read as views of one set of functions.

**Where admission does not reach.** Admission reaches only what it admits, and three things lie beyond it. None of them makes R8 unnecessary, which is why they stand here and not in the last column. Systems used inside a developer before any release, above all those that automate the developer's own research, are deployed first where a condition imposed at release cannot see them; R8 is therefore stated for consequential deployment with internal deployment included. Openly released weights can be fine-tuned around a constraint after release (Section 6.4). And a masked divergence shows under evaluation only as far as the system's own behaviour lets it, so admission conditioned on evaluated behaviour keeps it out only while the detection it allows, times the sanction, still exceeds the tax; for masked classes admission is by capability threshold, up to the capability at which control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation remain safe, and beyond it E2 or E3 for the class ({{masked}}; C12). Equal incidence is not among R8's conditions. It keeps a constraint neutral among those who comply ({{blind}}(2)); a constraint of unequal incidence re-sorts the compliers without letting the divergence back in [num: CI]. What unequal incidence does is turn a rule into a barrier against challengers, the second direction of capture ({{capture}}), so a design wants it for the reason Section 4 gives, not for compliance.

The requirements constrain functions, not institutional form: one institution can meet them, and so can mutually recognized institutions of several jurisdictions, a federation, or a polycentric complex. Four further safeguards are sufficient and not necessary: full inbound row sums below one for every corrector, which certify each against every state of the others ({{certificate}}(2)) and say nothing about whether the correctors' errors are independent ({{topology}}); the safeguards of Section 10.3, with the margin that every host sets ({{>safeguards}}); not building, custody, or detection faster than breakout, which change the premise of {{enforce}} rather than its terms ({{restraint}}(a)); and verification with a sanction at least the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}).

**{{!Proposition:nounilateral}} (No unilateral solution) [thm].** Correction and coverage are not supplied by any one carrier or rule-maker. *Assume (reach) and payoff-monotone selection among carriers differing only in load, with a race carrier present. (1) While effective liability is below the private advantage, $\tau_{\rm eff}<\nu$, a carrier's unilateral contribution to any safeguard of this section is a cost borne by it alone, and is selected against. (2) Let the liability be imposed by $n_s$ rule-makers, none reaching every carrier, the carriers any one of them does not reach having a combined maximal load that satisfies (reach); and let the rule-makers themselves be players of the steady-state commons of {{wow}}, with a weight $\lambda_s$ on their standing and $\Upsilon_s>1$ the value to all of them of a unit of liability over its private cost. Then no rule-maker alone meets R1 or R2, whatever it imposes; and if $\lambda_s>(\Upsilon_s-n_s)/(n_s-1)$, none imposes the liability alone in equilibrium, while whether they impose it jointly is governed by assurance ({{assure}}). (3) Restraint is a carrier's best response only once the effective liability $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$ exceeds the advantage: enacting $\tau>\nu$ is not enough while rule-making is captured, $x_{\rm rule}\ge1-\nu/\tau$.*

*Proof:* S9.

R2 needs more than one party by definition. R3 is a second-order public good that competitors do not supply ({{noinside}}). Of the means of R4, custody needs more than one party, and not building binds only the one that refrains. Where the mechanisms are active, a minimum of cooperation is therefore necessary: binding commitments covering every capable actor, with a layer of correction that no competitor controls. This is a result conditional on its models, combined with evidence and a stated standard, not a proof from first principles.

**Coverage can unravel.** The protection of {{blind}} holds only while coverage holds. When a large participant leaves, the constraint borne by those who remain becomes unilateral and is selected against ({{nounilateral}}(1)); and where detection is funded by the compliers or rule-making is captured by evaders, the sanction falls as the uncovered share grows, so that compliance and evasion are both stable, separated by a tipping share at which the expected sanction equals the cost of compliance ({{blind}}(3)) [num: CI, tipping shares 0.375 and 0.75 in two examples]. One exit can therefore trigger further exits (compare minimum participation in Barrett 2003), and a readiness rule that demands every dimension at once under noisy measurement produces exit pressure of its own (Section 11.3). An arrangement therefore states its participation threshold, the coverage at which its constraints remain neutral among compliers and its sanction stays above the gain from evasion for every type, and the conditions of access that keep coverage above it; a recent instance of one rule for all in which the largest participant's groups were exempted from two of its rules is recorded at EV18 and read in X10. A design that cannot state its threshold has not shown that it can last.
