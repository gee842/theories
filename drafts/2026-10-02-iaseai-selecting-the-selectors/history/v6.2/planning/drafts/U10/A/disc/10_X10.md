## X10. The design in full

One configuration is developed far enough to expose its assumptions. It is a candidate, compared in Section 12.1 with other designs under one range of uncertainty: a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions, a verification agreement together with shared provision, and regulated competition meeting the requirements of Section 9. The text does not claim it is superior; which design is best is part of crux C4. It is called here a *compact*: an arrangement for shared AI provision and correction, made of separately held functions rather than one institution. Each clause below is written so that a party under assessment cannot turn it against the assessment, and the hardest functions, execution and the fallback, carry the financing, appointment and certificates that the others carry.

### Functions, held separately

Each function is held by a different body, separately financed and separately corrected, so that correction lies outside what it corrects. On a planet, "outside" means outside the function: a different source of funds, a different lineage, and certificates anyone can recompute from public data. Every body whose findings or decisions have force carries a full inbound row-sum certificate ({{certificate}}(2)), recomputed on a schedule from the disclosed conversion data of the disclosure function below.

**Separation rule.** No body both revises the levy formula and appoints evaluators, and neither of those executes findings. Each has its own row-sum certificate. Supermajorities and the chamber's consent apply to the slow loops, amendment and the formula; the fast loop, restricting a specific capability, stays fast, with review after the fact, because every veto point adds delay, and the margin of {{tempo}} depends on it.

**Monocentric by function, nested by option.** Separately held functions are each held by one body: this is a separation of powers at one scale, monocentric function by function, and not a polycentric complex of overlapping and mutually adjusting jurisdictions (Ostrom 1990; Dietz, Ostrom and Stern 2003). The compact can instead be nested: each function held at national, regional and planetary levels, above a common floor of admission, custody and disclosure that no level may lower, with mutual recognition of certificates between levels, and with the fast loop placed at the level where the capability is held. Nesting adds redundancy of correction and local knowledge; it also multiplies the bodies whose certificates must hold and the seams a party under assessment can play one level against another through, and the common floor must then be entrenched at every level. The requirements of Section 9 constrain functions, not the number of centres; the choice is open.

**Voice without vote.** Providers and developers, the parties the rules constrain, have standing to be heard, through notice and comment on every rule and standard and through technical input to evaluation, and they disclose their own conversion like every other party; they have no vote on the rules under which they are assessed and no part in appointing those who assess them. The experience of commons is that regulated users excluded from revision withhold local knowledge and compliance, and that the strongest of them hold an exit option (Ostrom 1990); the result of Section 4 is that a party able to revise its own assessment captures it ({{influence}}), and in this field the regulated are the largest conversion targets the theory describes. The compact takes the second risk as the larger. The tension is real, and the chamber, the appeal and the open channels of comment are where it is managed, not dissolved.

**The chamber.** It is a corrector, and Section 4 applies to it. Its members are drawn by a rule no participant controls, they rotate, and its inbound row sum is certified like any evaluator's; its removal conditions are in the table. The regress of guardians ends not at a trusted body but at node-by-node certificates that many parties can recompute from public data; what remains of the regress falls on standard-setting and on the disclosure registry, which have their own rows.

**Recusal, and the clock.** A participant under assessment does not vote on revisions of the purpose under which it is assessed. Assessments are opened by the evaluation network on stated evidence and lapse if not concluded within their deadline; the deadline stops running while the assessed party withholds access, data or personnel that the assessment requires, and the time withheld is itself recorded as evidence.

**Injection is the injector's violation.** Row sums count conversion from the party under assessment ({{certificate}}(2); {{rivalverify}}), so a party could push an evaluator's row sum over one by sending it funds, staff or data, and so void the findings against itself; the same move repeated could manufacture the certificate failures that trigger dissolution. The compact closes both: conversion into an evaluator from a party it assesses, outside the evaluator's disclosed channels, is a violation by that party; it does not suspend findings against that party; the assessment is reassigned to an evaluator of different lineage; and certificate failures attributable to a party under assessment do not count toward any end condition.

**The levy, its base and its index.** A levy is paid into two funds by formula. The *base* is wider than the activity the compact constrains: total compute, or treaty shares by economic size, with a surcharge on frontier compute above a threshold. A base confined to frontier compute would shrink under exactly the stop (E2) and slowing (E3) the compact exists to be able to impose, and would make its correctors stakeholders in the activity they constrain. The *correction fund* is indexed to the coverage each hazard class requires, the $d^\ast$ of {{corrrate}}, not to the field's capability and not to perceived harm, so that correctors do not become stakeholders in capability growth and do not lapse after a recovery. Because $d^\ast$ is computed from measured conversion, a payer could lower it by moving influence into unmeasured channels, and a recipient could raise it by exaggeration; so $d^\ast$ is estimated as a conservative upper bound by a body that does not receive the fund, checked against direct measurements that do not depend on the conversion data, miss correlation on common test sets and the catch rate of planted defects at fixed human hours, and its rate of decline is capped, so that an apparent recovery lowers the fund only slowly. Frontier contributions to the common layer are paid by formula from the same base, not subscribed voluntarily, because pooled effort below a threshold leaves only the racing equilibrium and the threshold rises steeply with the value of winning ({{commonfrontn}}(4)(5)). The formula is fixed for a registered period; revision needs the supermajority and the chamber, and revisers are separated from beneficiaries and carry row-sum certificates, because by {{influence}} the formula is a conversion target: **the levy formula is itself a prize.**

**End conditions.** If coverage falls below the participation threshold, or a stated number of its functions' certificates fail for a stated period for reasons not attributable to any party under assessment, the compact dissolves into its separately held functions, each continuing under its own rule. Protections against unilateral domination are entrenched. Every power that confers authority states what would end it ({{guardian}}).

**Other provisions.** A charter with three purposes: useful provision for all participants' populations; protection against O1 and O2; effective correction. Membership open to every state and provider above a capability threshold on non-discriminatory terms, participation made individually rational by access to shared services, a share of the dividend and capacity building ({{participate}}). Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against unilateral domination kept apart. Baseline access to useful services with continuity guarantees; portable interfaces, escrow of systems providing essential services, and support for each participant to audit, operate and replace them. Narrow E1 with human authorization and a minimum time for deliberation for decisions on the use of force and on nuclear command, drilled parallel capacity, an evidence channel independent of AI, and doctrine open to mutual inspection. Admission constraints common to every system, before consequential deployment and internal deployment included, for divergences with common costs, with decision policies retained by performance only inside them; masked classes admitted by capability threshold with control protocols that do not assume cooperation ({{masked}}). Tempo: no building of the decisive class while readiness is unmet; capability thresholds by evaluation, and input thresholds in effective compute that tighten with algorithmic progress. Graduated remedies: notice; restriction of a specific capability; suspension of specific deployments; never suspension of essential services; emergency powers narrow, time-limited, reviewable and stating what would end them. Providers earn revenue and contracts by usefulness and have no rights to appoint evaluators, set thresholds, raise the cost of entry against challengers or amend the rules. Bounded competition continues inside: providers compete on usefulness, cost and reliability, and jurisdictions keep plural local purposes.

### Hard cases

**An adverse finding against a weaker participant.** Pending appeal, only the capability at issue may be restricted; essential services continue through alternative providers; the appellant sees the evidence under confidentiality, the panel decides within the deadline, and compensation follows if the finding is overturned. The power to suspend could become containment; the safeguards are the narrow scope of interim measures, the burden of evidence on the compact, and the appellant's standing.

**An adverse finding against the leading provider.** Suspending it could interrupt services for everyone, so formally equal procedures would be unequal in effect. The share cap, escrowed systems and funded standby capacity mean that restricting the leader's hazardous capability need not stop services. The author's rule for this case is that "the greater the power, the stricter the regulation it must receive, because it shapes everyone's conditions" (力愈大者，受调当愈严) [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14 第十四章]; the compact's version is that the leader's row in every certificate is weighted by its share, since the return to influence rises with the square of share ({{influence}}). Execution falls first to the leader's host jurisdiction. If that fails, the fallback is a restriction of inputs to the decisive class. It is coercive, the same form whose endorsement Section 8.3 withholds, and it has one home: the compared-not-settled tier of Section 12.1, which admits it only under three conditions of its own and counts its escalation risk in $\Theta$ ({{preventown}}). This chapter states no version of those conditions. The theory does not derive that its net effect is positive. Where neither consent nor such a fallback is available, the case is the hard case of Section 8.3. This is the hardest case, and the design does not dissolve it; it reduces the asymmetry of dependence that would otherwise make enforcement against the strongest impossible, and it gives the body that would execute the restriction a financing, an appointment and a certificate that do not run through the party restricted.

**A coalition of insiders.** Rival elites agree on mutual secrecy and immunity while weakening public correction. Four channels answer to people outside the coalition: the chamber, protected reporting, evaluators funded by formula, and publication of findings by default after a fixed delay; the disclosure registry makes the coalition's own conversion visible, and omission is the violation.

**An unjustified restriction.** The compact is used to contain a developing participant. It has standing to challenge, the burden is on the compact to show evidence of hazard, restrictions carry sunsets, and the compact has obligations to build capacity.

**Succession and withdrawal.** A change of leadership triggers review, not suspension. A withdrawal by a large participant makes the constraints borne by the rest unilateral and can trigger further exits; the participation threshold and the conditions of market access are what keep coverage above it (Section 9, "coverage can unravel"). Because the decisive class was not built and benefits and knowledge were diffused, a withdrawing party starts any race far from decisive capability and with a lower value of winning.

**Loss of an essential service.** Redundancy, escrow and standby capacity carry the service.

**A stalled assessment.** The assessed party withholds access until the deadline. The clock stops while access is withheld; the withholding is recorded as evidence; and interim measures confined to the capability at issue remain available on the evidence already in hand.

Three things could make its hazard of breakdown lower than that of an agreement that keeps the contest underneath: shared benefits lower the value of winning ({{relaxtrust}}; {{share}}); keeping AI out of strategic loops is a strict equilibrium among informed actors who price in a near-certain lock ({{delegatelock}}); and its fallback is not a decisive race from parity ({{fallback}}). These are hypotheses about the design, not established results.

### Federations, at both orders

Competition among member units for capital and residents can become a race to the bottom, and the strongest members can capture the centre. At the first order, common minimum standards bind every member, and fiscal equalization gives members capacity they do not have to win, both widening the effective self of members that value their absolute payoff ({{share}}(1)) and supplying the $k_0$ of the loop gain; for states that act strategically, pooling raises the working self-model rather than only slowing the race. At the second order, equal representation of small members and supermajorities for constitutional change keep the centre from being bought by the largest, a precedent for voting that does not follow compute. A global minimum tax on corporate profits is a recent instance of one rule for all, and of how coverage unravels: the largest participant's groups were exempted from two of its rules in recognition of that participant's own tax arrangements (EVIDENCE EV18), the case of Section 9's participation threshold seen from the inside, in which the party whose compliance matters most obtains the exemption that makes the rule unilateral for the rest. The same federal form can select for winning or for contribution; what decides is what it rewards.
