## X10. The design in full

One configuration is developed far enough to expose its assumptions. It is one candidate among those Section 12.1 compares under one range of uncertainty: a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions, a verification agreement with shared provision, regulated competition meeting the requirements of Section 9, an agreement of the type of Section 8.3(g) and the benign winner are others, and the coercive restriction of frontier inputs enters that comparison only under conditions of its own (Section 12.1). The text does not claim the configuration is superior; which design is best is part of crux C4. It is called here a *compact*: an arrangement for shared AI provision and correction, made of separately held functions rather than one institution.

### Functions, held separately

Each function is held by a different body, separately financed and separately corrected, so that correction lies outside what it corrects. On a planet, "outside" means outside the function: a different source of funds, a different lineage, and certificates anyone can recompute from public data. Every body has a rule of financing, a rule of appointment, a corrector, and a condition that ends or reverts it, so that each can answer the withdrawal question of the guardian test ({{guardian}}). Three funds are fed by formula from the levy below: a *service fund* for provision, a *correction fund* for standard-setting, evaluation, disclosure, the chamber and the appeal, and an *execution fund* for execution and custody. The rules body is financed by treaty contributions outside the levy, so that the body that sets the formula receives nothing from it.

**One body per function, and nesting.** Each function is still held by one body. That is a separation of powers at one scale, not polycentricity in the sense of overlapping jurisdictions at several scales that adjust to one another, which is a different candidate in Section 12.1; "many hands" applies inside evaluation only. The functions can be nested at national, regional and global levels above a common floor, with mutual recognition, as the learning row of Section 10.5 already does for variants of the design. Nesting is an option, not a requirement: nested governance of a planetary commons is itself an open problem (Stern 2011), and every level is a further conversion target.

**Separation rule.** No body both revises the levy formula and appoints evaluators, and neither of those executes findings. Each has its own row-sum certificate. Supermajorities and the chamber's consent apply to the slow loops, amendment and the formula; the fast loop, restricting a specific capability, stays fast, with review after the fact, because every veto point adds delay, and the robustness margin of {{tempo}} depends on it.

**The chamber.** It is a corrector, and Section 4 applies to it. Its members are drawn by a rule no participant controls, they rotate, its inbound row sum is certified like any evaluator's, and it can be renewed by a new draw. The regress of guardians ends not at a trusted body but at node-by-node certificates that many parties can recompute from public data; what remains of the regress falls on standard-setting, which has its own row, and on disclosure, without which no certificate can be recomputed.

**Conversion by the assessed is the assessed party's violation.** Row sums count conversion into an evaluator from the party it assesses ({{rivalverify}}). An assessed party could therefore push an evaluator's row sum above one by sending it funds, staff or data, and so strip its findings of executive force, or manufacture the certificate failures that trigger dissolution. Conversion into an evaluator from a party it assesses, outside the evaluator's disclosed channels, is a violation by that party: it does not suspend findings against that party, and the assessment is reassigned to an evaluator of different lineage.

**Recusal and deadlines.** A participant under assessment does not vote on revisions of the purpose under which it is assessed. Assessments are opened by the evaluation network on stated evidence and lapse if not concluded within their deadline; the deadline stops while the assessed party withholds access, so delay by the assessed cannot make an assessment lapse.

**Held-out metrics.** A held-out metric published at registration is no longer held out: it becomes a target (Section 5.4). Metrics are committed in advance by a cryptographic hash and revealed only at evaluation, so that anyone can check that the metric was fixed beforehand and no one can train on it.

**Disclosure.** Certificates are only as good as the data on conversion into correctors, and omission is the cheapest capture: a channel left unreported counts for nothing in a row sum. The disclosure registry keeps mandatory, audited records of funding, staff, data and access flowing into every corrector, with a sanction above the gain from omission ({{blind}}(3)). Without it the certificates of the design could not be recomputed by anyone, so it is the layer on which every other certificate rests, and it gets what every corrector gets: separate funding, an audit of a different lineage, and its own certificate.

**The levy formula is itself a prize.** Its base is broader than the activity it constrains: treaty contributions set by economic size, or a levy on total compute, plus a surcharge on frontier compute above a threshold. The base then does not shrink when E2 stops the decisive class or E3 slows a hazard class, while the surcharge still prices the frontier. The correction fund is indexed to the coverage each hazard class requires, the $d^\ast$ of {{corrrate}}, not to the field's capability and not to perceived harm, so that correctors do not become stakeholders in capability growth and do not lapse after a recovery. $d^\ast$ is computed from measured conversion, so payers gain by moving influence into channels that are not measured, and correctors who received the fund would gain by overstating it. It is therefore estimated, as a conservative upper bound, by a body that receives none of the correction fund, such as the rules body, checked against direct measurement of the correctors' independence (the correlation of misses on common test sets, the catch rates of planted defects), and allowed to fall by at most a registered rate per period. The formula is fixed for a registered period; revision needs the supermajority and the chamber, and revisers are separated from beneficiaries and carry row-sum certificates, because by {{influence}} the formula is a conversion target.

**Frontier contributions by formula.** In a common frontier, cooperation is an equilibrium only while the pooled effort stays above a threshold that rises steeply with the value of winning ({{commonfrontn}}), and effort in the pool is a good shared by every member, which members choosing one by one under-supply (S10). Contributions to the common frontier are therefore set by formula from the same base as the levy, not left to each participant.

**Providers: voice without vote.** Providers earn revenue and contracts by usefulness and have no rights to appoint evaluators, set thresholds, exclude entrants or amend the rules. They have voice: notice and comment on standards and rules, technical input to evaluation, and a channel to contest a finding through the appeal, with their own conversion into every body disclosed. Commons whose users take no part in revising their rules lose local knowledge and compliance (Ostrom 1990), and here the strongest users also have an exit option; a vote for the regulated is what the theory of capture warns against. The design takes voice without vote as the line between the two, and the tension is real: a voice that is heard more when its holder is larger is a channel of conversion, which disclosure and the row sums have to count.

**End conditions.** If coverage falls below the participation threshold, or a stated number of the functions' certificates fail for a stated period for reasons not attributable to an assessed party, the compact dissolves into its separately held functions, each continuing under its own rule. Protections against unilateral domination are entrenched. Every power that confers authority states what would end it.

**Against Ostrom's principles.** Boundaries (capability thresholds and hazard classes), monitoring and graduated sanctions are present. Proportionality of benefits and costs is partial: the largest payers have no vote, and strong types take part only for shares near their race payoffs ({{participate}}). Collective choice is answered by voice without vote. Conflict resolution is central, through one appeal, and neither cheap nor local, which nesting would answer. Recognition by an outside authority has no analogue at the scale of the planet; the host jurisdiction's first execution is the design's only link to the sovereignty of its participants. Nesting is the option above. The principles were drawn from commons that lasted at smaller scales (Ostrom 1990), and their transfer to a global commons is itself in question (Stern 2011).

**Other provisions.** A charter with three purposes: useful provision for all participants' populations; protection against O1 and O2; effective correction. Membership open to every state and provider above a capability threshold on non-discriminatory terms, participation made individually rational by access to shared services, a share of the dividend and capacity building. Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against unilateral domination kept apart (Section 10.3). Baseline access to useful services with continuity guarantees; portable interfaces, escrow of systems providing essential services, and support for each participant to audit, operate and replace them. Narrow E1 first, for decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty: human authorization and a minimum time for deliberation for decisions on the use of force and on nuclear command, drilled parallel capacity, and doctrine open to mutual inspection; broad E1 as the scope (Section 11.3). Admission constraints common to every system, before consequential deployment, internal deployment included, for divergences with common costs and a positive tax, with decision policies retained by performance only inside them; for masked classes, admission by capability threshold with control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation ({{masked}}). Commonly funded, openly published alignment and control research. Pace: no building of the decisive class while readiness is unmet; capability thresholds by evaluation, and input thresholds in effective compute that tighten with algorithmic progress. Graduated remedies: notice; restriction of a specific capability; suspension of specific deployments; never suspension of essential services; emergency powers narrow, time-limited, reviewable and stating what would end them. Bounded competition continues inside: providers compete on usefulness, cost and reliability, and jurisdictions keep plural local purposes.

### Hard cases

**An adverse finding against a weaker participant.** Pending appeal, only the capability at issue may be restricted; essential services continue through alternative providers; the appellant sees the evidence under confidentiality, the panel decides within the deadline, and compensation follows if the finding is overturned. The power to suspend could become containment; the safeguards are the narrow scope of interim measures, the burden of evidence on the compact, and the appellant's standing.

**An adverse finding against the leading provider.** Suspending it could interrupt services for everyone, so formally equal procedures would be unequal in effect. The share cap, escrowed systems and funded standby capacity mean that restricting the leader's hazardous capability need not stop services. Execution falls first to the leader's host jurisdiction. If that fails, what remains is the coercive restriction of inputs to the decisive class, the same form whose endorsement Section 8.3 withholds. The design does not settle it: it enters only as the candidate of Section 12.1, under the conditions stated there, with its escalation risk counted in $\Theta$, and the theory does not derive that its net effect is positive. Where neither consent nor such a restriction is available, the case is the hard case of Section 8.3. This is the hardest case, and the design does not dissolve it; it reduces the asymmetry of dependence that would otherwise make enforcement against the strongest impossible.

**A coalition of insiders.** Rival elites agree on mutual secrecy and immunity while weakening public correction. Four channels answer to people outside the coalition: the chamber, protected reporting, evaluators funded by formula, and publication of findings by default after a fixed delay.

**An unjustified restriction.** The compact is used to contain a developing participant. It has standing to challenge, the burden is on the compact to show evidence of hazard, restrictions carry sunsets, and the compact has obligations to build capacity.

**Succession and withdrawal.** A change of leadership triggers review, not suspension. A withdrawal by a large participant makes the constraints borne by the rest unilateral and can trigger further exits; the participation threshold and the conditions of market access are what keep coverage above it (Section 9). Because the decisive class was not built and benefits and knowledge were diffused, a withdrawing party starts any race far from decisive capability and with a lower value of winning.

**Loss of an essential service.** Redundancy, escrow and standby capacity carry the service.

| D5 Strongest violates | Dependence caps and standby reduce the asymmetry; beyond them, only the candidate of Section 12.1 | The hard case remains |
| D6 Succession | Review rather than suspension; continuity of essential services | By design |
| D7 Common causes | Evaluators and reference systems of different lineage and funding; collusion among AI monitors a design variable; miss correlation measured, and control evaluations in which the red team knows the protocol | To be tested |
| D8 Exclusion | Open membership; the chamber for non-members' populations | Fails if membership closes into a club |

Three things could make its hazard of breakdown lower than that of an agreement that keeps the contest underneath: shared benefits lower the value of winning ({{relaxtrust}}; {{share}}); keeping AI out of the strategic loops whose delegation would lock with near certainty is a strict equilibrium among actors who price in the lock ({{delegatelock}}); and its fallback is not a decisive race from parity ({{fallback}}). These are hypotheses about the design, not established results.

### Federations, at both orders

Competition among member units for capital and residents can become a race to the bottom, and the strongest members can capture the centre. At the first order, common minimum standards bind every member, and fiscal equalization gives members capacity they do not have to win, both widening the effective self of members that value their absolute payoff and supplying the $k_0$ of the loop gain; for members that act strategically, pooling raises the working self-model rather than only slowing the race ({{share}}(1)). At the second order, equal representation of small members and supermajorities for constitutional change keep the centre from being bought by the largest, a precedent for voting that does not follow compute. A global minimum tax on corporate profits is a recent instance of one rule for all, and of how coverage unravels: the largest participant's groups were exempted from two of its rules in recognition of that participant's own minimum-tax rules (EVIDENCE EV18). The same federal form can select for winning or for contribution; what decides is what it rewards.
