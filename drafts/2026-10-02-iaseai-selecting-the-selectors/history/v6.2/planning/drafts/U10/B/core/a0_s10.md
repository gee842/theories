## 10. The generative alternative

> **Claim.** The reflexive structure that lets success buy control over future selection can instead make contribution fund the conditions of further contribution [def]. The way out of an optimization trap is then neither a better optimization of the same objective nor the abolition of selection, competition or growth, but a change in what winning confers, so that the loop strengthens the shared host and independent correction instead of consuming them [val]. Sharing and diffusion lower the structural gain in every channel, provision not won in the contest lowers it through capacity, and correction with non-appropriability removes the reflexive channels; none of them makes the selected intensity zero while a positional prize remains, so that part has to be kept from forming or held in common [thm]. Where carriers differ only in load and one of low load is present, four safeguards return consequences to their source, and the host stays above its floor throughout iff liability exceeds the private advantage by a margin the host sets [thm]. The generative order persists through what all share, not through its winners, and its form follows function, not the axes of centralization or of individual and collective [def]; whether it attracts is a conjecture [conj].
>
> **Why.** A term common to all is invisible to relative selection, so an order whose support no carrier can claim is not selected among carriers; it is held by coverage, formula funding and a loop that runs through the whole [thm]. Each counter-mechanism of Part I requires its own form: one rule for all where coverage is the condition, many hands where independence is, common custody where a decisive position would otherwise form [def]. Every remedy for the selection of selectors is itself a selector, so no better guardian is an answer; a structure in which errors cannot grow without limit is [val].

### 10.1 The purpose

Taken out of the contest, capability stops being spent on standing. What the race dissipates, a share of the stakes that rises with decisiveness and with the shared loss ({{dissipate}}), becomes available for need: medicine and public health, education, science, competent administration, infrastructure, and the long-horizon work that fragmented incentives under-supply. The joint value is large; the race consumes it, and a change in what is selected realizes it (V1).

**Authority as a productive capacity (V4).** Authority can coordinate investment, guarantee services, support learning and act on shared purposes. The ideal the theory draws on justifies authority by its responsibility for improving people's lives and the historical trajectory. It enters the design as a purpose to be sustained, not as a premise that it is realized: how institutions select and form officeholders, reward service, hear unwelcome evidence and revise harmful practices are causal variables, and a stated mission is not proof of its implementation. The author's book states the principle as "where power reaches, responsibility follows" (力之所及，责之所在) [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14, ch. 18]: a larger causal radius brings larger responsibility, not larger entitlement. With the norm of a shared future (Section 3.7), it is the institutional consequence of the relational self.

**Provision that does not become the system.** Taken out of the contest, AI can be what infrastructure is: provision of the conditions of a decent life, built to meet need rather than to gain standing. Trees are the image, in the author's words "the lungs of the ecosystem": they provide shelter and fruit, ask no return for it, and do not "become the whole ecosystem" [corpus: shengsheng]. The image carries the two conditions of the design: provision that asks no return is no gate, and provision that does not become the whole does not close. Part I supplies four properties. Such provision is *distributed*, many providers below the size at which influence over correctors pays ({{influence}}); *good enough*, stopping growth once a need is met, which keeps its hazard modes within correction and confers no decisive advantage ({{enough}}); *replaceable*, so that human capacity stays above its lock-in floor ({{lockin}}); and *for everyone*, understanding and provision alike, not for a class of guardians (Section 10.4). Contribution is rewarded with revenue, contracts and recognition; what success does not buy is the right to appoint evaluators, set thresholds, exclude entrants or amend the governing rules (V5), which cuts the conversion of success into rule support (Section 4) and keeps the incentive to contribute. What is open is who builds and funds such provision, not who holds it: by its conditions, no one does.

### 10.2 What winning confers: sharing, convertibility and provision beneath the game

The prize of the contest has two parts (Section 3.4): a *divisible* part $\Omega_e$, rents, revenues, services and the material gains of capability, and a *positional* part $\Omega_s$, decisive standing, the power to set the rules and the loss of being subordinated. The levers act on the two parts differently, and on the channels of the loop differently ({{chansign}}).

The most direct change takes the frontier itself out of the contest by pooling effort. Where the value of capability is common to all, private incentives give too little intensity rather than too much, and the residual failure is speed ({{commonfront}}). Where winning still confers dominance on whoever is outside the pool, what pooling changes is the set of equilibria.

**{{!Proposition:commonfrontn}} (A common frontier) [num].** In the contest below, a frontier that pools effort and confers no dominance among those who pool it makes cooperation an equilibrium beside the race while the value of winning is moderate, and the pooled effort it needs rises steeply with that value. *Of $n$ actors, cooperators hold effort $e_c$ and racers choose theirs; whoever wins the decisive contest gains $\Omega$, and every loser bears $L_b$. Under separate frontiers each effort enters the contest separately; under a common frontier the $n_c$ cooperators pool their effort into one entity of strength $(n_ce_c)^r$, whose common value $\Omega_c$ is split equally and confers no dominance among them. With $n=4$, $\Omega=\Omega_c=L_b=c=1$, a shared loss $\ell=0.3$, $e_c=0.05$ and $r=1.3$: under separate frontiers the only pure equilibrium is all racing; under a common frontier all cooperating and all racing are both strict equilibria, and cooperating is a best response iff trust in each rival exceeds about 0.61; with a smaller pooled effort, $e_c=0.03$, only all racing remains; and, with $\Omega_c$ and $L_b$ fixed, the pooled effort at which racing against cooperators stops paying more than cooperating rises from 0.0054 to 0.035, 0.14 and 0.49 as $\Omega$ rises from 0.5 to 1, 2 and 4.*

*Scope.* One parameterization, with racers' intensities at the symmetric fixed point of best responses, each verified as a global best response; cases without a symmetric pure equilibrium are excluded. Where a deviator best-responds to fixed intensities, the trust needed is about 0.82; with $n=2$, all cooperating is an equilibrium for $r$ up to about 1.2. *Proof:* S10 [num: legacy v4.5, N22].

A common frontier does not end the contest. Allocation within a joint project is itself a contest, and the project, as the largest carrier, has the largest return to influence over its correctors ({{influence}}). Effort in the pool benefits every member alike, so selection within the pool favours the member that supplies least (compare {{onegroup}}), while cooperation needs the pooled effort above a threshold that rises steeply with the value of winning; Discussion X10 therefore sets contributions by formula. When the value of winning is large, keeping ahead of those outside the pool is the race moved up a level.

**{{!Proposition:share}} (What sharing, pooling and convertibility change) [thm].** Sharing and provision not won in the contest can stabilize equal standing, and cutting the conversion of success into control removes the reflexive channels; none of them makes the selected intensity zero while a positional prize remains. *In the contest of Section 3.4, with prize $\Omega=\Omega_e+\Omega_s$:*
1. *Pooling payoffs, so that each actor receives $1-\beta$ of its own payoff and $\beta$ times the average of all, makes an actor holding $\theta_0\in(-1,n-1)$ act as if it held a self-model that rises strictly with $\beta$ and reaches $n-1$ at $\beta=1$. At $\theta_0=-1$, and under relative selection, pooling only scales payoff differences by $1-\beta$.*
2. *Sharing a fraction $\beta$ of the divisible prize, with the promise kept with probability $\varrho$, is the contest with the effective prize $\Omega_{\rm eff}=(1-\beta\varrho)\Omega_e+\Omega_s$ when the shared part is divided among all $n$, the winner included, and $\Omega_{\rm eff}=\big(1-\beta\varrho\,n/(n-1)\big)\Omega_e+\Omega_s$ when it goes to the $n-1$ others, the convention of {{wingives}}. Equilibrium intensity under every common self-model, dissipation and the race hazard are proportional to $\Omega_{\rm eff}$. Under relative selection in one field with $r\le1$, the uninvadable intensity is $r\Omega_{\rm eff}/(nc)$, positive wherever $\Omega_{\rm eff}>0$: with the share divided among all, for every $\beta<1$, and for every $\beta$ when $\Omega_s>0$, since then $\Omega_{\rm eff}\ge\Omega_s$.*
3. *Where capacity receives provision $k_0$ not won in the contest, a smaller convertible share stabilizes equal standing ({{compound}}(1)); without provision and diffusion the convertible share sets only the level of capacity, so winnings made non-convertible stabilize standing only together with provision. Where success cannot be converted into control over correction ($\chi=0$), the reflexive channels of {{loopgain}} carry no gain, the return to influence over correctors is zero ({{influence}}), and the correction that evaluation requires falls to its value without second-order selection, $A/c_{\rm ev}$ ({{corrrate}}(1)).*

*Scope.* Parts (1) and (2) hold in the one-shot contest and its relative-selection version; part (3) collects the stated results. With a positional part as large as the divisible one, sharing all of the divisible prize only halves the uninvadable intensity [num: SH]. *Proof:* S10 [num: SH].

**Three levers, and where each acts.** Pooling widens the effective self of any actor that values its absolute payoff, and does nothing under relative selection. Sharing scales every channel of the loop by $1-\beta$, but only if it is credible: contemporaneous, escrowed or under shared control, before decisiveness. Sharing promised for after a decisive win is worth breaking ({{wingives}}), so it raises the value of a decisive position. Changing what winning converts into acts channel by channel (Section 2.2): through capacity it needs provision not won in the contest, which lowers the won share $\omega$, since a smaller convertible share alone sets only the level; through rule support it is correction with the cut $\chi\to0$, which removes the reflexive closure.

**The positional part cannot be divided.** Toward the positional part a rival's standing is one's own subordination, $\kappa_s\le0$, and relative concern is accurate ({{boundary}}(2)). Sharing does not reach it: however much of the divisible prize is shared among all, the uninvadable intensity stays at least $r\Omega_s/(nc)$ (part (2)) [thm]. Lowering decisiveness does not remove it, since a lottery can still award a position beyond sanction. It can only be kept from forming, by not building capability of the decisive class and by verified limits on what any winner may do, or held in common custody, so that no party can use it alone ({{restraint}}). Realism is right about positional goods, and that is where the crux of design lies: not in asking actors to widen their selves over what cannot be shared, but in keeping such prizes from forming. What a design must limit is what winning confers.

**What should diffuse.** Diffusion lowers the gain in every channel and is the third inflow not tied to rank, so blocking it to keep a lead raises the gain (Section 2.2). Diffusion of capability of the decisive class, or of weights that admission cannot reach once released (Section 6.4), defeats custody and admission. The line is drawn by function, not by openness as such: knowledge, services and provision diffuse; capability of the decisive class does not, and is held in common or not built [def].

**{{!Remark:twoorders}} (Concentration at two orders).** At the first order more contenders make the race fiercer ({{join}}(4)); at the second, cultivated influence over correctors rises with a high moment of shares ({{corrrate}}(4)), which calls for removing common control. The design that serves both merges the frontier and disperses and insulates correction, keeping several lineages inside the merged frontier against common-mode failure ({{topology}}). Neither "centralize" nor "decentralize" is the answer, because the two orders call for opposite forms.

### 10.3 Safeguards, verification and participation

Four safeguards make consequences return on the host of Section 3. **(I1)** A liability above the private advantage of load, enacted in advance for anticipated harms and on the alarm for others, so that effective liability exceeds the advantage once rule-making is uncaptured; for harms beyond the assets of the liable this is regulation in advance rather than liability (Shavell 1984), and near a threshold a quantity does better than a price (Weitzman 1974). **(I2)** Independent correctors in evaluation and rule-making, with $d(t)$ above $d^\ast(t)$ by a margin. **(I3)** Independent evidence: masking kept below its threshold, or a conservative alarm on a bound on masking established independently of the field ({{alarm}}). **(I4)** All three in force before the corrector domains are captured.

**{{!Theorem:safeguards}} (Sufficient safeguards, their margin, and a necessary condition) [thm].** The four safeguards return consequences to their source, and keep the host above its floor throughout iff liability exceeds the private advantage by a margin that the host sets. *Let selection be replicator dynamics among carriers that differ only in load, with a carrier of load $e_{\min}$ present, $h_Ve_{\min}<\rho(1-V_{\min})$.*
1. *Sufficiency, with a margin. Under (I1)–(I4), effective liability exceeds the private advantage after a finite time, for unanticipated harms after an alarm that fires above the floor, and the load falls to $e_{\min}$; on the host {{eq:host}} the long-run level $1-h_Ve_{\min}/\rho$ exceeds the floor. From the state at which the margin $\Delta=\tau_{\rm eff}-\nu$ takes force, the trajectory stays above the floor throughout iff $\Delta\ge\Delta_{\min}$, a threshold set by the host, the floor, that state's composition and the speed of selection. Below it, the host of {{eq:host}} dips under its floor and recovers. A host that regenerates less at every level and degrades by itself below a threshold $V_u$ does not recover, so there the margin must also keep the transient above $V_u$, and the floor must be set no lower than $V_u$ for crossing it to be the first loss.*
2. *Non-redundancy. For each of (I1)–(I3) there are parameters satisfying the other two under which the host crosses its floor, and parameters under which all three come into force only after capture and the host crosses it.*
3. *A necessary condition. Under (reach), with a race carrier present, a trajectory viable at every horizon keeps the cumulative advantage $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\,ds$ from tending to $+\infty$, and so has long-run average effective liability, the lim sup of its time average, at least the private advantage.*

*Scope.* The margin belongs to the transient, not to the long run. With the race strategy at 99% of activity, the forgiving host needs $\Delta_{\min}$ of 0.108, 0.165 and 0.355 for floors 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5, and a margin of 0.08 dips to 0.14 before recovering; on a threshold host with $e_{\min}=0.1$, $\Delta_{\min}$ rises from 0.26 to 0.51 as its threshold parameter rises from 0.05 to 0.25, and a shortfall there is permanent [num: HO]. Part (3) is necessary, not sufficient: liability whose average tends to the advantage while the cumulative advantage grows like $\sqrt t$ lets the host cross its floor [num: HO]. The theorem computes, for one fixed strategy, an instance of the boundary of viable parameters; the correctable kernel of {{kernel}} takes a union over strategies and an intersection over disturbances, and is not computed here. *Proof:* S10.

Verification by rivals is correction too, and Section 4 applies to it. A rival's verifier is captured by none of the sources it counts if its full inbound row sum is below one, counting conversion from the verified party, from its own side and from shared sources such as models, supply chains and professional paradigms; rivalry keeps the verified party's contribution near zero and bounds nothing else in the row ({{rivalverify}}). Rival verifiers also raise false alarms, can collude against third parties and check compliance rather than judgment, and an accusation between rivals can trigger prevention, so joint resolution precedes any response.

Participation has its own conditions ({{participate}}). Under complete information an overwhelmingly strong actor takes part only for a share near its race payoff. Under private types and without verification, each type's interim share must be independent of its report, and the arrangement is feasible iff the joint value covers the sum of the types' best race payoffs; it can therefore fail where strong types are rare although cooperation is efficient for every pair [num: legacy v4.5, K7], the mutual optimism that makes bargaining fail under private information (Fearon 1995). Verification that reveals types removes the information rents, and the constraint relaxes as survival in the race falls below survival under the arrangement.

Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against unilateral domination are three different things, and a design keeps them apart. Participation can be made individually rational through shares of value and access to services; voting rights need not follow capability; and protection against domination is fixed first and not traded for participation. If, for some set of actors, participation and the independence of correction cannot both be had, the design is infeasible for them, and says so rather than buy participation with control over correction.

### 10.4 The generative loop and its closure

The reflexive structure of Section 2 has no sign of its own: what success converts into decides whether the loop consumes the shared conditions or renews them. The author's book states the two signs as two loops of the same structure [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14, ch. 14]. In the *generative loop*, security makes trust possible, trust cooperation, cooperation abundance, abundance sufficiency and slack, and these allow wider care and longer foresight, which make better order and more security. In the *degenerative loop*, fear produces defence, defence struggle, struggle seizure and concentration, and these scarcity and resentment; trust collapses, and fear deepens. The first turns more agents into generators; the second turns more of them into defenders, instruments or exiles. In institutions the generative loop runs from shared capability to distributed agency, to plural observation, criticism, maintenance and repair, to the renewal of the commons, and so to a greater capacity for cooperation and further shared capability. It is carried by funding, education, stewardship and maintenance, shared infrastructure, independent evaluation, conflict resolution, succession, protection against enclosure, real exit and appeal, and the material security that makes restraint survivable [corpus: meta-attractor]. Goodwill is not a carrier.

The criterion is generative or degenerative, not cooperative or competitive. Cooperation on the wrong thing can be worse than competition on it: a cartel is cooperation, and inclusion that stops at a club over-produces the harm it does not bear ({{club}}). Bounded competition in products, science and service continues inside a generative order; what is removed is competition for control over the shared conditions and over the correctors.

Each loop of Section 7.1, and the drive behind them, has a dual: a strand of the generative loop with a carrier, a condition and a test. The five strands are the marks G6, G2, G5, G3 and G7 of Section 8.2 stated as loops; G1 and G4 take the forms of the safeguards of Section 10.3 and of custody in Section 10.5.

| Strand | Dual of | Carrier | Condition | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reasoning instead of winning** (讲理 rather than 讲赢) | The naturalized narrative, and the talk that relative accounting silences | Derivations from public premises; verifiable evidence; protocols agreed in advance; costly signals | Objectives not opposed on the topic of talk: an effective bias below $\frac14$ ({{talk}}) | Informativeness between rivals rises as contested stakes fall |
| **Reward separated from advantage** | Compounding | Separation of reward from authority over correction; sharing as gains arise | A structural gain below one with margin, evaluated at the present distribution of standing, where the reflexive channel's gain scales with a high moment of shares ({{corrrate}}(4)); $\chi\to0$; a cap on any provider's share of essential services | Conduct changes in proportion to what is shared; where it does not, advantage runs through channels outside the shared prize (C6) |
| **Provision beneath the game** | The drive | Shared provision, good enough, distributed and replaceable: the $k_0$ of the loop gain, acting through capacity | Not tied to standing; no inferiority in receiving it | $1+\theta$, the ratio of conduct's response to shared harm and to private cost, rises with provision relative to the stakes (Test Γ) |
| **Correction in common hands, plural and decorrelated** | Capture of the rules, and adopt or be out | Certified correctors; lineages that do not fail from one cause; audited disclosure of conversion; exit from providers | Funded by formula, appointed at arm's length, growing at the rate of {{corrrate}}, raised by $w\delta_h$ where it rests on human capacity ({{humancorr}}) | Correctors' row sums stay below one as the field grows, and their misses stay uncorrelated |
| **Pace bound to understanding** | Regulator lag | Thresholds in capability and effective compute tied to measured coverage per hazard class; drilled human capacity | $a\tau_c<1$ with margin for each hazard mode ({{tempo}}); the uncovered share summable ({{coverage}}) | Catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours stay level as capability grows |

For persons, provision beneath the game removes the ground of the belief that one must defeat others first. In the corpus's words, the common foundation does not prescribe how anyone lives; it lets everyone be entitled to live without first defeating others (共同基础不是规定所有人如何生活，而是让所有人不必通过战胜别人，才有资格活着) [corpus: shengsheng, 至善 §三]. For states, service obligations and common custody act on survival itself; for providers, $k_0$ is literally capability not won: a common layer of compute, evaluation infrastructure and services. Each strand faces the questions the pathological loops answer: how it keeps obtaining resources, how it meets free riding and the pressure to expand, how it allows itself to be corrected, and how it avoids turning "protecting the whole" into a purpose no one can challenge. Exit is free from providers and services, where it is selection with the opposite sign; it is not free from the common constraints, whose protection depends on there being no outside to them.

**How the generative order persists.** In the pathological meta-attractor the loop runs through the winners: success buys support for the rule that selected it. In the generative order it cannot, because the second strand cuts that conversion, and support that no carrier can claim is a term common to all, which relative selection cancels ({{blind}}). The generative order is therefore not selected *among* carriers ({{noinside}}). Inside it carriers are still selected, for usefulness; what is not selected among them is the rule itself. The rule persists as a common constraint persists: by coverage, so that no one can step outside the common floor; by financing fixed in formula, so that its correctors depend on no one's success; and by a loop that runs through the whole, in which provision, security and slack make the rule worth keeping to everyone at once. Its positive loop runs through the system, not through its winners; its negative loop gives it correctability, through correctors funded and appointed so that no one keys them. A design in which those rewarded for contribution come to hold the positions that maintain the rule takes the path of the imperial examination, created to dilute hereditary capture and turned over centuries into a selection for the examination's form: an anti-attractor can become an attractor [corpus: meta-attractor].

**Is the generative order an attractor?** Because it is not selected among carriers, whether it attracts is a question about the whole-level loop and about coverage, and it is a conjecture [conj]; its persistence has to be designed and measured. The same mathematics gives one caution. A cooperative order whose loop gain greatly exceeds its damping also resists the correction of its own errors, and a balancing loop that answers late overshoots ({{delayosc}}). Plurality of purposes, correctors and lineages is therefore a requirement, not a decoration (Section 3.7), and it does a second job: an order whose forms can fail and be replaced can be reformed in bounded time, where one that has frozen cannot (Section 11.4).

**The reform dilemma.** Reform needs influence, and the game is what produces influence. The path by which a reformer gains the capacity to change the rules runs through the selection the reform is meant to change, so wherever that selection has closed, it removes the reform before the capacity to make it arrives: those who reach the positions from which rules are changed are those the rules favoured, and they arrive formed by them. This is the guardian problem in motion. The theory has two answers, and neither is a better guardian. Reform can act where the selection has not yet closed: at a keystone while its window is open (Section 4.5), or in a crisis with a design prepared, and its activation committed, in advance (Sections 11.4 and 12.1). And the capacity to reform can be built outside the contest for influence, by correction funded by formula and keyed to no one, so that it does not have to be won.

**{{!Remark:guardian}} (The guardian test).** Every remedy for the selection of selectors is itself a selector. Philosopher-kings, priestly classes, enlightened stewards and new universities have each been proposed as the guardians who would hold the line, and each is a corrector to which Section 4 applies; what the author asks of every one of them is "the corruption of the constraining mechanism itself" [corpus: alignment-selection; meta-attractor]. No better guardian is an answer. What can be an answer is a structure in which every guardian is guarded and errors cannot grow without limit. The author's book puts it in two lines: the regulators must themselves be regulated (调节者亦须被调节), and real order is not finding people who are always right but building a structure in which error cannot grow without limit (真正之秩序，非得永远正确之人，乃立错误不能无限扩大之构) [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14, ch. 14]. Every arrangement, and every institution within it, is asked four questions before adoption, the provision, the commons of understanding and the correctors of this section included, since each can become a position that winning reaches. *Capture:* are its correctors' full inbound row sums below one, counting every party and shared source ({{certificate}})? *Withdrawal:* does every closure it makes state what would end it? *Totalization:* can it make itself the whole, so that "protecting the whole" becomes a purpose no one can challenge? *Distribution of understanding:* does understanding go to everyone, or to a class whose position depends on others' not understanding? The author's answer to the last is that "everyone has access to the understanding itself directly" [corpus: alignment-selection].

### 10.5 Design by function

The design question is usually posed along ideological axes: centralization or decentralization, the individual or the collective, markets or states. The mechanisms support neither pole. Each counter-mechanism of Part I requires a particular form, and the forms differ by function: coverage requires one rule for all, independence requires many hands, and a position beyond sanction must be held in common or not formed. In the author's words, "I don't think decentralization is the fundamental issue. It is the molochlization selection process that makes [centralization or decentralization] dangerous" [corpus: meta-attractor].

| Function | What the mechanism requires | Form that follows |
|---|---|---|
| Common constraints: admission, the return of consequences, levies | Coverage of every capable actor; for every type that can evade, an expected sanction at least its gain from evasion; equal incidence, so that the rule does not re-sort the compliant ({{blind}}) | **One rule for all** |
| Custody of capability of the decisive class | No unilateral holding before a decisive position can form ({{restraint}}) | **Common custody, or not built** |
| Correction and evaluation | Independence and decorrelation: full inbound row sums below one, measured correlation of misses, control evaluations in which the red team knows the protocol ({{certificate}}, {{topology}}) | **Many hands**, certified, of different lineage |
| Who sets standards | One standard with many measurers makes standard-setting a keystone, and Goodhart acts on it | **Standard-setters certified and rotating, separated from measurers and measured, with held-out metrics committed in advance** |
| Measurement and language | Shared, checkable, dynamic indicators ({{alarm}}) | **One standard, many measurers** |
| Disclosure of conversion data | Certificates are only as good as the data on conversion into correctors, and omission is the cheapest capture ({{blind}}(3)) | **Mandatory, audited disclosure, with a sanction above the gain from omission** |
| Pace | The uncovered hazardous share summable; for each hazard mode, $a\tau_c<1$ with margin ({{coverage}}, {{tempo}}) | **Pace bound to understanding:** capability thresholds tied to measured coverage per class, input thresholds that tighten with algorithmic progress |
| Human decision authority (O2) | Delegation that would lock with near certainty, or erode the capacity to overrule below its floor, is not undone ({{lockin}}, {{delegatelock}}) | **Narrow E1 first, broad E1 as the scope**, with human capacity kept in practice by drills |
| Alignment and control of the systems | Weak incarnation arises under any developer; what lowers the tax is a public good that competitors undersupply; masked divergences escape evaluation ({{nested}}, {{masked}}) | **Commonly funded, openly published alignment and control research; at admission, control protocols that do not assume cooperation, tied to capability thresholds** |
| Learning about the design itself | Global, irreversible failures allow no second attempt; reversible ones can be learned from ({{onegroup}}) | **Bounded parallel variants above a common floor, with shared evaluation and adoption by evidence** |
| Provision of essential services | Good enough, distributed, replaceable | **Distributed provision under common guarantees** of continuity and portability |
| The frontier of hazardous capability | Merging lowers the race at the first order and raises the return to influence at the second; it also loses yardstick competition and lineage diversity ({{twoorders}}) | **Merged accounting and custody of the frontier, plural lineages inside it, correction outside it** |
| Standing of those affected (V3) | Inclusion that stops at a club over-produces the harm it does not bear ({{club}}) | **A chamber of those affected**, drawn by a rule no participant controls |
| Due process against correctors | Every corrector is a selector that can err or be captured (Section 4; {{guardian}}) | **An appeal independent of the correctors it reviews**, with standing, access to evidence and deadlines |
| Authority (V4) | Answerable to those affected; not purchasable by success | **Separation rule plus chamber:** set by row sums and contestability, not by the degree of concentration |

The rows give each requirement of Section 9 a form, and S9 crosswalks them. They are derived from function, as design principles for commons were derived from the institutions that lasted (Ostrom 1990), and with the same caution about scale: nested governance of a planetary commons is itself an open problem (Stern 2011).

**Federations as a precedent at both orders.** Competition among members for capital and residents can become a race to the bottom, and the strongest members can capture the centre. At the first order, common minimum standards bind every member, and fiscal equalization gives members capacity they do not have to win, the $k_0$ of the loop gain. At the second, equal representation of small members and supermajorities for constitutional change keep the centre from being bought by the largest, a precedent for voting that does not follow compute. The same federal form can select for winning or for contribution; what decides is what it rewards (X10).

**A candidate configuration.** One configuration is developed in Discussion X10 far enough to expose its assumptions, as one candidate among those that Section 12.1 compares under one range of uncertainty. It is a set of separately held functions, not one institution. Common provision, standard-setting, evaluation, disclosure, rules and the levy formula, execution, custody, a chamber of those affected, and appeal are each held by a different body, separately financed and separately corrected, so that correction lies outside what it corrects. On a planet "outside" means outside the function: a different source of funds, a different lineage, and certificates anyone can recompute from public data. Each function is still held by one body: that is a separation of powers at one scale, not a polycentric complex of overlapping jurisdictions that adjust to one another, which is a different candidate, and nesting the functions at national, regional and global levels above a common floor is an option, not a requirement. No body both revises the levy formula and appoints evaluators, and neither executes findings; supermajorities and the chamber's consent govern the slow loops, amendment and the formula, while the fast loop, restricting a specific capability, stays fast with review after the fact, since every veto point adds delay ({{tempo}}). The levy that funds correction is indexed to the coverage each hazard class requires, not to capability or perceived harm, so that correctors do not become stakeholders in capability growth, and the formula is itself treated as a prize. Providers have voice without vote, with their own conversion disclosed: a voice in rule-making keeps the local knowledge and compliance it brings (Ostrom 1990), a vote would let success weigh in on its own assessment, and the tension between the two is real. The configuration states its end: if coverage falls below its participation threshold, or a stated number of certificates fail for a stated period for reasons not attributable to an assessed party, it dissolves into its separately held functions, while protections against unilateral domination stay entrenched. By {{influence}} such an arrangement is the largest conversion target the theory describes, so capture of its own correctors (D4) is its hardest case, not a design row. Where execution through a host jurisdiction fails against the strongest participant (D5), what remains is the coercive restriction of frontier inputs, which the design does not settle; Section 12.1 compares it under conditions of its own.

The generative alternative is thus a set of strands, forms and stated ends, and an attractive end state is not a reachable one; Section 11 asks how such an order is reached and held, step by step, when trust is an outcome of the transition rather than its precondition.
