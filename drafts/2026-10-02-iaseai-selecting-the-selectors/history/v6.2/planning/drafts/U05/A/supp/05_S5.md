## S5. Acceleration and the three erosions

### Proposition {{#join}} in full, and proof

*(1) In the objective an actor acts on, if $S_i<P_i$, restraint is never a best response to a racing rival; in the contest of {{boundary}} this holds for $r<n/(n-1)$, and in the $n$-player game it also needs restraint against one racer to pay less than racing alongside it. (2) A best response in the objective does not imply $S<P$ in material payoff or true welfare: with $n=2$, $r=1$, $\Omega=c=1$, $\ell=0.1$ and $\theta=-1$, racing is the strict best response in the objective, while staying out gives $S^\pi=-0.05$ against $P^\pi=-0.10$, and with $\kappa=0.2$, $S^W=+0.04$ against $P^W=-0.12$. (3) If falling behind costs $L_b\ge0$ beyond losing the prize, joining instead of staying out gains, at $\theta=0$, $\frac{\Omega+L_b}{n}\big(1-\frac{r(n-1)}{n}\big)$ for $r\le n/(n-1)$, zero in the fully dissipative limit; with asymmetric costs $c_1<c_2$ the strongest keeps a rent $(\Omega+L_b)(1-c_1/c_2)$ (Hillman and Riley 1989; Baye, Kovenock and de Vries 1996). (4) If the events $T_i>R_i$ are independent with probabilities $\xi_i$, the race begins with probability $1-\prod_i(1-\xi_i)$, which tends to one iff $\sum_i\xi_i$ diverges.*

*Proof.* (2): at $\theta=-1$ intensity is $1/2$ and $P^\pi=-0.1$; against $e=1/2$ zero intensity gives $-0.05$ and the other $0.45$. (3): with penalty $L_b$ the contest has prize $\Omega+L_b$; joining minus staying out is $(\Omega+L_b)/n-(c+\ell)e$ with $e=r(n-1)(\Omega+L_b)/(n^2(c+\ell))$; in the symmetric all-pay contest each player's expected payoff is that of the lowest bid in its support, $-L_b$ (Baye, Kovenock and de Vries 1996), as for two actors with $r\ge2$ (Ewerhart 2015). (4): $\prod(1-\xi_i)\to0$ iff $\sum\xi_i=\infty$. $\square$

### Proof of Proposition {{#dissipate}}

At the symmetric equilibrium the win probabilities sum to one, so total material payoff is $\Omega-ne(c+n\ell)$; substituting $e(\theta)$ gives $s_{\rm dis}$. With stakes $s\Omega$ and loss $s\ell$ the share is $r(n-1-\theta)(c+ns\ell)/(n(c+(1+\theta)s\ell))$, whose derivative in $s$ has the sign of $c\ell(n-1-\theta)>0$, with limit $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$. Total payoff stays positive at every scale iff the limit is at most one, $r(n-1-\theta)\le1+\theta$, that is, $\theta\ge\theta_g$. At $\theta=-1$ the share is $r(1+ns\ell/c)$. $\square$

For $n=4$ and $r=1$ the ceiling $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$ is 3, 1.86, 1 and 0.33 at $\theta=0$, 0.4, 1 and 2, and $\theta_g=1$; with $\ell=0.3c$ the share at unit scale is 1.27 at $\theta=0$ and 1.007 at $\theta=0.4$, and it rises toward each ceiling as the scale grows [num: legacy v5, Prop 1.3]. At $\theta=0$ each actor's loss relative to joint restraint is $\frac{r(n-1)}n\frac\Omega n\frac{c+n\ell}{c+\ell}$, which exceeds $\Omega/n$ iff $r(n-1)(c+n\ell)>n(c+\ell)$: $1.27\,\Omega/n$ in that example. If the shared loss per unit did not grow with power, the share would stay at its unit-scale value.

### Remark {{#frontier}} in full

Let a unit of capacity do one of three things. It can expand into an unclaimed frontier at marginal value $\varphi(F)-c_x-(1+\theta)H/n$, where $\varphi$ falls as the remaining frontier $F$ shrinks, $c_x$ is the private cost and $H$ the cost to the host, borne equally ({{twochan}}(5)). It can destroy rivals' capacity at value $-c_y-\theta\Delta_y/(n-1)$, where $c_y$ is the private cost and $\Delta_y$ the capacity destroyed. Or it can rest. The symbols are local to this remark.
1. *If $\theta\ge-(n-1)c_y/\Delta_y$, war among copies never pays.*
2. *At $\theta=-1$ expansion stops only where $\varphi=c_x$; at $\theta>-1$ it stops at $\varphi=c_x+(1+\theta)H/n$, the point of enough.*
3. *At $\theta=-1$ expansion gives way to destruction once $\varphi(F)<c_x+\Delta_y/(n-1)-c_y$, before the frontier is exhausted.*

*Proof.* Compare the three marginal values: destruction pays iff its value is positive, which is (1); at $\theta=-1$ the host term vanishes, which gives (2) and, compared with destruction, (3). $\square$

*Toy.* Four copies of one design with compounding expansion, a frontier of 1,000 and a logistic host. At $\theta=-1$ expansion peaks at a stock of 494 near $t=25$; the switch comes with 32% of the frontier left (predicted by (3) at 322, simulated at 320), and total stock then falls to about $4\times10^{-9}$. At $\theta=0$ the copies stop at the point of enough, keep a stock of 518 and never fight; at $\theta=3$ they stop earlier, at 343. With asymmetric starts one survivor keeps 17% of the peak. With destruction too costly to pay, no accounting fights, but $\theta=-1$ drives the host to 0.109. One result runs against the narrative: in the run with war, the host's minimum under $\theta=-1$ (0.245) is higher than under $\theta=0$ (0.194), because capacity spent on war is not spent on the host [num: FS]. The parameters were chosen by the author; the toy illustrates the static condition and estimates nothing (compare Hirshleifer 1991; Skaperdas 1992).

The corpus arrives at the switch from the other side, as the phase after the frontier in which a logic organised as outward struggle turns on itself, and as one of the two endgames in which fighting continues until everything within reach is consumed [corpus: meta-attractor, 2026-10-01 and 2026-10-04].

### Proof of Proposition {{#enforce}}

Cooperation is worth $C_i/(1-\delta)$. A one-period deviation $a$ is worth $C_i+g_i(a)+\delta\big[p_d(a)D_i/(1-\delta)+(1-p_d(a))v_i/(1-\delta)\big]$, where $v_i/(1-\delta)$ is the continuation value of punishment computed in the same game, with breakouts possible during punishment. Multiplying the no-deviation condition by $1-\delta$ and rearranging gives {{eq:enforceeq}} for that deviation. In this stochastic game with an absorbing state, discounting and bounded payoffs, the one-shot deviation principle holds deviation by deviation, so the condition for every deviation is necessary and sufficient for the given punishment value; when $p_d$ is common to all deviations, the deviation with the largest gain binds. That $a^\ast$ is the path of some subgame-perfect equilibrium needs $v_i$ to be the least subgame-perfect continuation value, the extension of Abreu's optimal penal codes to stochastic games with an absorbing state; this is asserted for finite stage games with discounting and flagged for outside review. (2) Solve (1) for $p_d$. With $\alpha=(1-\delta)g_i/\delta$, $b=D_i-C_i$ and $c=C_i-v_i$, $\bar p_{d,i}=(c-\alpha)/(b+c)$; it falls in $\alpha$ where $b+c>0$, falls in $b$ where $\bar p_{d,i}\ge0$, and rises in $c$ where $\bar p_{d,i}\le1$, so the monotonicities hold on $[0,1)$ and are weak at its edges: at $\bar p_{d,i}=0$ the derivative in $D_i$ is zero [num: NB; ES]. (3) The two bounds differ by $\delta p_d(D_i-C_i)$. (4) Set $p_d=1$; with $D_i\ge C_i$ the condition forces $g_i\le-\delta(D_i-C_i)/(1-\delta)\le0$. (5) With exclusive breakouts the punishment phase satisfies $V_P=P_i+\delta\big[q_bD_i/(1-\delta)+q_bV^{\rm sub}_i/(1-\delta)+(1-2q_b)V_P\big]$, which needs the three events to partition the period, $q_b\le\frac12$; solve. With independent breakouts each racer breaks out alone with probability $q_b(1-q_b)$, and the race continues with probability $q_b^2+(1-q_b)^2$, which gives the same closed form in $q_b(1-q_b)$ and holds for every $q_b\in[0,1]$. In the example, restraint is enforceable at $p_d=0$ iff $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-v_i)$, that is $v_i\le0.7778$; with $v_i(q_b)=3.6q_b/(0.1+1.8q_b)$ this holds iff $q_b\le0.0354$, and $v_i<C_i$ iff $q_b<0.0556$. $\square$

*Numerics (ES; EC).*
- *Per deviation.* With $C=3$, $D=6$, $v=1$, $\delta=0.9$, deviation X ($g=1$, $p_d=0.1$) is deterred, its value 17.5 against 30 for cooperation (temptation 0.37 against a remaining sanction of 1.62), while deviation Y ($g=0.2$, $p_d=0.6$) is not, 39.2 against 30 (temptation 1.64 against 0.72) [num: ES].
- *Breakouts during punishment.* The closed forms equal value iteration on the explicit chain, exclusive ($v=0.8950$, 1.2857, 1.6875 at $q_b=0.045$, 0.1, 0.3) and independent (0.8723, 1.2366, 1.5816). At $q_b=0.6$ the exclusive form is invalid ($1-2q_b<0$) and the independent one gives 1.6241. The window of the example is not knife-edge: deterrence fails while $v<C$ on $[0.0498,0.0555]$, $[0.0354,0.0555]$ and $[0.0159,0.0555]$ for $g=0.5$, 2 and 5, and racing in the punishment phase is a best response in the cases checked [num: ES].
- *Best response over plans.* In 20,000 random settings of an explicit-action stochastic game in which one breakout technology sets both $p_d$ and $q_b$, the deviator's optimum over all deviation plans is computed by value iteration on the decision process. Cooperation is sustained in 6,821. In 35 settings the punisher prefers to quit the race, so (5) does not give the credible punishment value and the theorem's premise fails; in the other 19,965, {{eq:enforceeq}} agrees with the best response in every case [num: ES].
- *Valuation check.* Comparing the discounted value of the fixed deviation path with cooperation in 200,000 random settings, a third each with $D<C$, $D=C$ and $D>C$, reproduces (1) in every case; since that comparison is {{eq:enforceeq}} rearranged, as exact rational arithmetic confirms (no disagreement in 20,000 draws), it checks the algebra and not the incentives [identity: EC; ES]. The bound without the breakout term says "sustainable" when deviation pays in 10,723 of 66,538 cases with $D>C$ and in none with $D\le C$, and says "not sustainable" when restraint is sustainable in 10,032 of 66,842 cases with $D<C$. Codex's two counterexamples are reproduced (cooperation worth 30, deviation 22) [identity: EC].
- *A different point.* With $D=4$, $V^{\rm sub}=0$, $g=0.5$ and $q_b=0.1$, the punishment value rises to 1.29, above $C=1$, and restraint is not sustainable even at $p_d=0$. There both actors prefer the race with breakouts to cooperation itself: what fails is the preference for cooperation, not deterrence. With $D=3$, $V^{\rm sub}=-5$ breakouts make the punishment harsher [num: EC; ES].
- *Homogeneity.* $\bar p_d=0.2963$ is unchanged when all of $(g,C,D,v)$ are scaled by 2 or 10; scaling only $g$ and $D$ moves it to 0.1296 and $-0.0037$ [num: ES].

### Derivation of Remark {{#detectlink}}

If punishment is triggered by detection and acts before decisiveness, a deviation is answered with probability $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ and otherwise becomes decisive, so $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$. Substituting in {{eq:enforceeq}} and solving as in {{enforce}}(2) gives $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d,i}$. A Poisson clock of rate $1/\tau_{\rm det}$ gives $\upsilon(T)=1-e^{-T/\tau_{\rm det}}$.

For {{assure}}, take its one-shot payoffs as continuation values normalized by $1-\delta$: $R=C_i$; $T=(1-\delta)(C_i+g_i)+\delta D_i$, the deviation that becomes decisive; $P-F=(1-\delta)(C_i+g_i)+\delta v_i$, the deviation detected and answered. Then $T-R=(1-\delta)g_i+\delta(D_i-C_i)$ and $T-(P-F)=\delta(D_i-v_i)$, so $(T-R)/(T-P+F)=1-\bar p_{d,i}$, and $T<R$ iff $\bar p_{d,i}>1$. Over 100,000 random settings with $D>v$ the two thresholds differ by at most $4.4\times10^{-11}$ [identity: JX]. A Poisson clock simulated directly gives escape probabilities within four standard errors of $e^{-T_{\rm dec}/\tau_{\rm det}}$ (0.4485 against 0.4493 at $T_{\rm dec}=8$, $\tau_{\rm det}=10$), and simulated deviation values agree with {{eq:enforceeq}}: with $C=1$, $D=1.5$, $v=0$, $g=0.3$ and $\tau_{\rm det}=10$, deviating is worth 0.738 against 1 at $T_{\rm dec}=8$ and is deterred, and 1.235 at $T_{\rm dec}=2$ and is not [num: JX].

*Where they differ.* {{assure}} states when cooperating beats defecting against a cooperator, given the sanction $F$; trust in the other's cooperation then decides whether cooperating is a best response, and the credibility of $F$ is not examined. {{eq:enforceeq}} is the condition on the equilibrium path: the other restrains, and $v_i$ is computed in the same game and required to be credible. Where the stage games coincide and trust is one, they are the same requirement. Acceleration acts on both through $T_{\rm dec}$, which a structural loop gain above one shortens ({{tdec}}), so detection that is timely today stops being timely as leads open faster.

### Proof of Corollary {{#relpunish}}

At $\theta=-1$ every symmetric profile gives each objective 0. *Two actors.* Let the punisher use any strategy, and let the punished play its mirror, at every history what the punisher would play with the roles swapped. Because the punishment phase is exchangeable and starts from a state that treats both alike, the joint distribution of the two payoff streams is invariant under swapping the players, so their expected objectives are equal; since they sum to zero at $\theta=-1$, both are 0, and $v_i\ge0$. In a stage game this is the identity $y^\top Ay=0$ for the antisymmetric matrix $A=-A^\top$ of a symmetric zero-sum game, mixed strategies included. *$n\ge3$.* In an anonymous game with all punishers using the same, possibly mixed and independent, strategy, imitation yields an exchangeable profile with expected relative payoff 0. In either case $v_i\ge0=C_i$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} requires $(1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_dD_i\le\delta(1-p_d)(0-v_i)\le0$. $\square$

*Numerics (CR; ES).* Over 20,000 random two-player games, mixed imitation gives at most $2\times10^{-16}$ in absolute value; in 3,000 anonymous games with $n\ge3$, no violation [num: CR]. An exchangeable continuation, a lottery with an absorbing prize to either player, leaves the punished at 0. A label-dependent one, in which the target bears the loss whatever it does, holds it at $-8.18$, so a deviation is worth $-69.6$ and cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium is sustained among relative actors; the symmetric variant gives 0 and a deviation worth $+4$, so it is not [num: ES].

*The role-differentiated counterexample.* Three players choose (restrain or race) × (X or Y); a player whose second component is unique gains one, and racing gains 0.1 and costs each other 0.1. All restraining with X gives each 0, and the best deviation gains 1.2. The punishment in which the deviator and one punisher take X and the other punisher Y holds the deviator at $-0.5$ and is a stage equilibrium in objectives, so grim reversion to it is credible, and cooperation is sustained for $\delta\ge1.2/1.7=0.706$; a symmetric punishment could be imitated for 0 [num: CR]. As printed the punishment is weak: the best unilateral deviations in it gain 0, 0 and $-0.2$. Adding 0.02 to the prize for a unique X makes it strict; subtracting 0.02 destroys it, and the roles must swap. Under any small asymmetry between the roles' prizes the matching assignment is strict ($\delta^\ast=0.706$), and under random anonymous perturbations a strict punishment holding the deviator below cooperation exists in 300 of 300, 300 of 300 and 282 of 300 games at perturbation sizes 0.02, 0.05 and 0.1, with median $\delta^\ast$ 0.708, 0.711 and 0.713 [num: CR]. The deviator is held at $-0.5$ in a second parameterization as well ($\delta^\ast=0.730$). Both punishers prefer the Y role ($+1.0$ against $-0.5$), and in a targeted-attack game a punisher at $\theta=-1$ is exactly indifferent between hurting the deviator and hurting its fellow punisher (0.95 against 0.95) [num: CR]. Blocs therefore need coordination on roles. Duersch, Oechssler and Schipper (2012) concern a dynamic imitation rule; the corollary uses only that a symmetric two-player zero-sum game has value 0, so rock–paper–scissors games are covered once mixed imitation is allowed.

### Proof of Corollary {{#restraint}}

With $p_d=0$, {{eq:enforceeq}} reads $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-v_i)$, satisfiable for $C_i>v_i$ and $\delta$ large, given a punishment that does not raise the chance of breakout (else $v_i$ rises, {{enforce}}(5)); at $\theta=-1$ apply {{relpunish}}. The condition is linear in $p_d$, so it holds for every $p_d\in[0,1]$ iff it holds at both ends; at $p_d=1$ it is $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$, which implies the condition at $p_d=0$ when $D_i\ge v_i$. Otherwise the condition is $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$, and (a) and (b) classify the terms of its expression: (a) lowers $p_d$ directly; (b) raises $\bar p_{d,i}$ through $D_i$ relative to $C_i$, through $v_i$, or through $g_i$. A slow breakout enters with $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ ({{detectlink}}). $\square$ With $C=1$, $D=0.9$, $v=0$, $g=2$ and $\delta=0.9$, $\bar p_d=0.8642$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} holds at $p_d=0.86$ and fails at 0.87; with $g=0.5$, $(1-\delta)g\le\delta(C-D)$ and restraint holds at every $p_d$ [num: ES].

**{{!Remark:zonesillus}} (An illustration: the faithful-AI game at the boundary case).** In the binary game of {{faithful}} with a common $\theta$, write $A=\epsilon(b-d)-c$, $B=\epsilon(b/(n-1)+d)$, $K=c+n\epsilon d$ and $\Lambda_p=\delta(1-p_d)/(1-\delta)$. Restraint is a stage equilibrium when $\theta\ge A/B$, and, at the boundary case $D=C$ with punishment by reversion to the race without breakouts, can be sustained when $\theta\ge\theta_{\rm rep}=(A-\Lambda_pK)/(B+\Lambda_pK)$. With $n=4$, $b=1$, $c=0.3$, $d=0.2$, $\epsilon=1$ and $\delta=0.9$: $A/B=0.94$, and $\theta_{\rm rep}$ is $-0.90$, $-0.81$, $-0.32$, $+0.005$, $+0.63$ and $+0.94$ at $p_d=0$, 0.5, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99 and 1, both from the closed form and from direct discounted streams, with racing credible at each [identity: BI; num: JX]. An isolated self ($\theta=0$) loses enforced restraint once $p_d>0.9495$, and $\theta=0.4$ once $p_d>0.9793$ [num: JX]. These are the thresholds of the boundary case, not bounds. A decisive position worth more than cooperation raises them: with $D-C=0.5$ in objective units, $\theta_{\rm rep}$ at $p_d=0.9$ rises from $-0.32$ to $+2.34$, and at $p_d=1$ no $\theta\le n-1$ sustains restraint ({{enforce}}(4)) [num: NB; JX]. So the restraint gap between wider and narrower objectives grows with $p_d$ over the range in which the wider objective still sustains restraint, and when $D>C$ it closes as $p_d\to1$, where this theory and structural realism agree. For $\theta$ uniform on $[-1,3]$, the share sustaining restraint among $\theta>1$ less that among $\theta<1$ is $+0.05$, $+0.09$, $+0.21$, $+0.34$, $+0.50$, $+0.82$ and $+0.97$ at $p_d=0$, 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99 and 1 when $D=C$, and $+0.05$, $+0.30$, $+0.92$, $+0.33$, 0, 0 and 0 when $D-C=0.5$: Test G discriminates at intermediate $p_d$ [num: JX]. In the continuous contest intensity is positive for every $\theta<n-1$, so self-enforcing restraint there needs full inclusion of the contestants.

### Proofs of Propositions {{#wingives}} and {{#preventattack}}

*Winner.* With linear payoffs the objective $(1-s)W+\theta sW/(n-1)$ has slope $W(\theta/(n-1)-1)$; with logarithmic utility the first-order condition of $\ln((1-s)W)+\theta\ln(sW/(n-1))$ gives $s=\theta/(1+\theta)$, which a grid search confirms at $\theta=0.4$, 1, 2 and 3 (0.286, 0.500, 0.667, 0.750) [num: WG]. A winner acting on its true welfare toward what can be divided has $\theta=\kappa_W$ by {{boundary}}(2), so it gives $\kappa_W/(1+\kappa_W)$; toward a positional prize its accurate weight is $\kappa_s\le0$, and it gives nothing. The promise is {{twochan}}(4): a share promised to the $n-1$ others is worth breaking by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$, and one split among all $n$ by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/n$ [num: SM]. $\square$

*Preventive attack, in full.* Two actors bargain over a flow of value 1 per period with discount $\delta$; fighting ends bargaining, A then obtaining the flow with its probability of prevailing less a cost $c_A$ per period; A prevails with probability $w_1$ today and $w_2<w_1$ after B's advantage arrives one period later. (1) Let $x$ be A's per-period share after the shift; fighting now, worth $(w_1-c_A)/(1-\delta)$, is rational if $w_1-c_A>(1-\delta)+\delta x$, and only if, when peace gives A the whole flow today. (2) Under (A8), B offers A its reservation value $x=\max(w_2-c_A,0)$. (3) Under other protocols $x$ can reach $\min(w_2+c_B,1)$ and attack can cease to be rational: with $w_1=0.5$, $w_2=0$, $c_A=c_B=0.2$ and $\delta=0.8$, A attacks under (A8), while if A sets the future terms peace gives A 1.8 and B 3.2, against 1.5 each from war [num: legacy v4.2]. (4) If $c_A\ge w_1$, war is worth at most zero. A shift spread over enough periods removes the motive: with $w$ falling from 0.5 to 0 over $N$ periods, $c_A=0.05$ and $\delta=0.8$, attack is rational at the start for $N\le3$ and never for $N\ge4$, by backward induction on $V_t=\max(W_t,\delta V_{t+1})$ [num: legacy v4.4]. In the loop of Section 2 the shift is the opening of a lead, at the speed of {{tdec}}.

### Proofs for the race and growth channels

*Proposition {{#racehazard}}.* By {{boundary}}(1), total intensity is $ne(\theta_t)$, which gives $h_t$; survival is $\prod_te^{-h_t}$ by the chain rule. Sharing a fraction $\beta$ of the divisible prize gives the contest with prize $(1-\beta)\Omega_t$ ({{share}}), linear in $h_t$; a rebated levy enters at $\tau(n-1-\theta_t)/n$. Since $\ell/(c+(1+\theta)\ell)<1/(1+\theta)$, $\sum_tr_t\Omega_t(n-1-\theta_t)/(1+\theta_t)<\infty$ suffices for $\theta_t>-1$ whatever $\ell_t$. A background hazard from capability growth adds to $h_t$, and no lever removes it. The closed form agrees with direct evaluation by construction, and the symmetric intensity with a best response on a grid (0.11444) [identity: RH, with an independent grid best response]. $\square$

*Lemma {{#nonsum}} in full.* Fix one irreversible event. If on every surviving history the residual probability in round $k$ is at least $h_k$ with $\sum_kh_k=\infty$, then $\Pr(\Phi_N)\le\prod_{k\le N}(1-h_k)\le\exp(-\sum_{k\le N}h_k)\to0$, by the chain rule, without independence; if it is at most $\bar h_k<1$ with $\sum\bar h_k<\infty$, survival is at least $\prod(1-\bar h_k)>0$. With $h_k=0.2/k$ survival is 0.34, 0.22 and 0.14 by rounds 100, 1,000 and 10,000; with $0.2/k^2$ it settles at 0.70 [num: legacy v5, CR].

*Proposition {{#tempo}}.* Proportional correction: Hayes (1950). Derivative action extends the range: for $a=1$ and $\tau_c=1.5$, gains $k_p=1.02$, $k_d=0.644$ stabilize the mode, where proportional correction alone diverges [num: legacy v4.2, C-2]. For the sensitivity bound, with plant $e^{-s\tau_c}/(s-a)$ and any stabilizing linear time-invariant controller, the complementary sensitivity is stable with value 1 at $s=a$; writing it as $e^{-s\tau_c}$ times a stable function and applying the maximum modulus principle gives $\|\mathcal T\|_\infty\ge e^{a\tau_c}$, and robust stability $\|w\mathcal T\|_\infty<1$ gives $|w(a)|e^{a\tau_c}<1$, necessary only: $w(s)=2(s-a)/(s+a)$ has $w(a)=0$ but $\|w\mathcal T\|_\infty\ge2e^{a\tau_c}$. Evidence cannot be acted on before it exists. $\square$

*Why coverage may lag.* Consequences take their time, and tools raise the throughput of observation without making consequences arrive sooner; institutions deliberate at rates set by attention and procedure; learning theoretical models and forming new mechanisms are human processes; and understanding produced with human time, $\dot U=A_UL_U^{\lambda_U}U^{\phi_U}$ with $\phi_U<1$ and bounded labour, grows polynomially (Jones 1995; Bloom et al. 2020). None settles the premise: if AI raises $A_U$ exponentially at rate $g_A$, $U$ grows exponentially at $g_A/(1-\phi_U)$. Where correction leans on AI, the human capacity it rests on can erode, which raises the rate correction needs ({{humancorr}}). Measures: the safety of control protocols at a fixed audit budget across generations, and catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours, controlled for evaluation awareness. Under strict legibility, (coverage lags) is close to definitional for systems beyond human capability, and the empirical content lies in (residual floor), which a safety case with a numerical bound on the uncovered part would state (Clymer et al. 2024). (variety grows) is what makes (coverage lags) expected on a continuation branch; the theorem does not use it, and R5's list of conditions under which the requirement is not needed keeps it.

*Theorem {{#coverage}}.* For $t\ge T_\varepsilon$, (coverage lags) gives $U_t/Y_t\le\varepsilon$ on every surviving history, so by (residual floor) the conditional probability of the event is at least $h(1-\varepsilon)$; the chain rule gives the bound. The growth of $Y_t$ is never used. The converse is the upper part of {{nonsum}} with $\bar h_k=\bar hu_k$. (residual floor) gives a liminf of the hazard of at least $h$, not that the hazard tends to $h$. With $u_t=1/(t+2)^2$ coverage stays behind forever and survival with $h=0.05$ stays above 0.98; with $h=0.05$ and $\varepsilon=0.1$, survival eighty periods after $T_\varepsilon$ is at most 0.025 [num: legacy v5, MM]. $\square$

**Proposition {{#freeze}} in full.** *Let hazardous variety be frozen at $Y_\tau$ from $\tau$ while coverage grows. (1) The exposure after $\tau$ is $\int_\tau^\infty(1-U(t)/Y_\tau)^+dt$: $Y_\tau(1-U(\tau)/Y_\tau)^2/(2\dot U)$ for linear coverage, and $(\ln\varrho-1+1/\varrho)/g_U$ for coverage growing at rate $g_U$, with $\varrho=Y_\tau/U(\tau)>1$. (2) Rolled back to $Y\le U$, exposure stops. (3) If a sprint builds capability $\varrho_X$ times the rivals' level and hazardous variety is a fraction $\varphi$ of capability's, frozen exposure is zero when $\varphi\varrho_X\le1$. (4) For linear coverage the same frozen ratio costs more the later the sprint; this fails for some sub-exponential coverage.* *Proof.* Integrate. For (4), let $\log U$ rise by 0.5 on each interval $[n^2,n^2+1]$ and stay constant otherwise: $\log U\approx0.5\sqrt t=o(t)$, while $\dot U/U=0.5$ inside each interval [num: legacy v5, MM]. $\square$ With hazardous variety equal to capability's, growth 0.3, linear coverage $1+0.5t$ and $h=0.05$, a sprint to $\tau=10$ leaves survival 0.86; freezing then gives 0.53, and rolling back keeps 0.86 [num: legacy v4.5].

*Proposition {{#enough}}.* After $t_N$, $U_t\ge U_{t_N}+u(t-t_N)$, so the uncovered share is zero from $t_N+Y_N/u$ on, and {{nonsum}}'s upper part bounds survival below. Frozen at need, survival on this channel stays at 0.717, coverage catching up at $t=14$; compounding at 10%, it is $1.8\times10^{-9}$ by period 400 [num: PE]. The success function is homogeneous of degree zero, so symmetric intensity is proportional to the stakes for every $\theta<n-1$; an objective bounded in the stakes, such as $1-e^{-\Omega}$, has intensity bounded as they grow. With $n=4$, $r=1$ and $c=1$, intensities are 0.19, 1.88 and 18.75 for linear payoffs at stakes 1, 10 and 100, and 0.12, 0.19 and 0.19 for the saturating one [num: SM]. $\square$

### Proof of Proposition {{#talk}}

(1) Let all $n$ actors hold $\theta$, so $J_S=\pi_S+\frac\theta{n-1}\big(\pi_R+\sum_k\pi_k\big)$ and $J_R=\pi_R+\frac\theta{n-1}\big(\pi_S+\sum_k\pi_k\big)$, the sums over third parties. With $\pi_S=-(y-u-b_0)^2$, $\pi_R=-(y-u)^2$ and $\sum_k\pi_k$ linear in $y$ with total slope $L$, both objectives are quadratic in $y$ with curvature $1+\theta/(n-1)$, positive iff $n-1+\theta>0$. Their ideal points are
$$y_S-u=\frac{b_0+\theta L/(2(n-1))}{1+\theta/(n-1)},\qquad y_R-u=\frac{\theta b_0/(n-1)+\theta L/(2(n-1))}{1+\theta/(n-1)},$$
which differ by $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$, independent of $L$. Each objective is then, up to a positive factor and terms that do not depend on $y$, the uniform-quadratic payoff of Crawford and Sobel with that difference as the bias, and their condition for an informative equilibrium applies to it. Numerically maximized ideal points match the formula in 19 combinations of $n\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ and $\theta$, to $1.5\times10^{-8}$ [num: TK].

(2) At $\theta=-1$ with $n$ actors, $J_k=\pi_k-\frac1{n-1}\sum_{j\ne k}\pi_j$, and direct expansion gives $J_S-J_R=\frac n{n-1}(\pi_S-\pi_R)$ and $J_S+J_R=\frac{n-2}{n-1}(\pi_S+\pi_R)-\frac2{n-1}\sum_{k\ne S,R}\pi_k$: effects on third parties enter both alike. The identity holds to $3.6\times10^{-15}$ [num: T3]. With $n\ge3$ the effective bias of (1) is $b_0n/(n-2)$: at $b_0=0.05$ it is 0.150, 0.100, 0.075 and 0.0625 for $n=3$, 4, 6 and 10, so informative talk needs a material bias below 0.083, 0.125, 0.167 and 0.200 [num: TK]. A two-message partition equilibrium, built directly with indifferent boundary types and numerical best responses, exists iff the effective bias is below 1/4, and at exactly 1/4 there is none [num: T3]. With $b_0=0$ and a common cost to third parties, full revelation is an equilibrium. With $n=2$ at $\theta=-1$ the curvature vanishes and the objectives sum to zero. If a sender's and a receiver's payoffs sum to zero, each sender type induces, among the equilibrium actions, one that minimizes the receiver's payoff in its state, so the receiver's payoff is at most that of ignoring messages, which it can always do; equilibrium payoffs equal the no-talk values (300 games, no exception) [num: TK]. With third-party payoff $-ky^2$ instead of linear, the gap in units of the state is still $b_0n/(n-2)$ to the grid's resolution [num: TK].

(3) A sender that intends to race prefers the receiver to cooperate: with $R=1$, $T=2\varepsilon$, $P=\varepsilon$ and $S=0$, it gains $\varepsilon$ from the receiver's cooperation however small $\varepsilon$ is (compare Aumann 1990). $\square$

### The scissors: proof, the reduced form, and a joint model

*Proof of {{scissors}}.* *(1)* By {{tdec}}, one channel's gap factor is $\Lambda^\ast=1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$, and $(d_K+\delta_f)\Gamma=(1-\beta)r\omega d_K$ by {{eq:gamma}}, so $\Lambda^\ast-1=(1-\beta)r\omega d_K-d_K-\delta_f$, increasing in $\omega$. Where $\Lambda^\ast>1$, $T_{\rm dec}=\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast$ falls with $\omega$, and since $\upsilon$ is non-decreasing, $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ rises. With several channels the Perron root of {{eq:secular}} rises with each channel's gain term, since the right side of the secular equation increases in each term and decreases in $\Lambda$ above its poles. *(2)* By {{eq:twochaneq}} the deviation gain in objectives is $g_i(\theta)=(1+\theta)\bar\Delta+\frac{n-1-\theta}{n-1}(\Delta\pi_i-\bar\Delta)$, which falls in $\theta$ when $\bar\Delta\le0<\Delta\pi_i-\bar\Delta$. At a symmetric profile $J_i=(1+\theta)\bar\pi$, so $C_i-v_i=(1+\theta)(\bar\pi_C-\bar\pi_P)$, which rises in $\theta$. Write $\bar p_{d,i}=(c-\alpha)/(b+c)$ as in the proof of {{enforce}}: a narrower $\theta$ raises $\alpha$, lowers $c$ and does not lower $b$, and each lowers $\bar p_{d,i}$ where $b+c>0$ and $0\le\bar p_{d,i}\le1$. With a fixed prize, $\partial\theta_\Gamma/\partial\Gamma_\delta$ has the sign of $\theta_0-(n-1)<0$, and $\Gamma_\delta=(1-\beta)r\omega\,\delta d_K/\big(1-\delta(1-d_K-\delta_f)\big)$ is proportional to $\omega$; with a growing prize, {{standprice}} gives the sign. *(3)* $\bar p_{d,i}$ falls in $v_i$ ({{enforce}}(2)). *(4)* $\bar p_{d,i}$ is unchanged when $(g_i,C_i,D_i,v_i)$ are scaled alike. If $\alpha$ and $b$ are multiplied by $k$ relative to $c$, $\bar p_{d,i}=(c-k\alpha)/(kb+c)$, whose derivative in $k$ is $-c(\alpha+b)/(kb+c)^2<0$ where $c>0$ and $\alpha+b>0$, that is $\bar p_{d,i}<1$ [num: ES]. $\square$

*The reduced form.* In the binary game of {{zonesillus}} at $D=C$, let actors act on $\theta_\Gamma(\theta_0,\Gamma_\delta)$ with the compounding contest running alongside, so that its state effect enters to first order through the price of standing, rivals not responding and punishment by reversion to the race without breakouts. By direct valuation of the cooperation and deviation paths, the largest discounted loop gain at which restraint remains enforceable is, for $\theta_0=0$ and $\theta_0=0.4$: 0.924 and 0.953 at $p_d=0$; 0.852 and 0.908 at $p_d=0.5$; 0.387 and 0.621 at $p_d=0.9$; none above $p_d=0.9495$ and $0.9793$. With a common return, the prize growing with capacity, the objective blade opens again: at $p_d=0.9$ the value rises from 0.387 to 0.571 at $\Gamma^{\rm c}_\delta=0.3$ and to 0.755 at 0.6 [num: JX].

**{{!Remark:jointscissors}} (Both blades in one reduced model) [num].** The arms of {{scissors}} are proved one at a time; this remark computes them together in one model, which estimates nothing. *Model.* Four actors; capacity follows {{eq:stocks}} with one ratio channel, $p_i=K_i^r/\sum_jK_j^r$, $r=3$, $d_K=0.2$, $\delta_f=0.05$ and nothing shared; the won share $\omega$ fixes provision against the stakes. $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma_\delta$ follow from $\omega$. $T_{\rm dec}$ is the number of periods in which the nonlinear stock dynamics take a lead from 5% to 50%, with stakes and provision held at their current values while it opens (the linearized time is printed beside it). Detection and response run on a Poisson clock of mean 10 periods, so $p_d=e^{-T_{\rm dec}/10}$. Actors act on $\theta_\Gamma(0,\Gamma_\delta)$, and on $-1$ at $\Gamma_\delta\ge1$, the limit from below. The stage game is that of {{zonesillus}} with every payoff, the decisive value included, proportional to the stakes, so that $\bar p_d$ does not depend on their scale and the stakes arm is off. The decisive position is worth either $D=C$ or $D-C=0.5$ in objective units per unit of stakes. Punishment is reversion to the race, (i) without breakouts, or (ii) with one breakout technology throughout: during the punishment race each racer breaks out with the same $p_d$, independently, a simultaneous breakout leaving the race as it was, and the subordinated being worth the race.

*Results.* At $D=C$ restraint is enforceable iff $\omega<0.551$ under (i) and $\omega<0.542$ under (ii). With $p_d$ held at zero, the objective blade alone closes at 0.556; with the objective held at $\theta_0$, the breakout blade alone never closes, since $p_d$ reaches only 0.45 at $\omega=0.9$. At the crossing $\Gamma=1.32$, $\Gamma_\delta=0.92$ and $p_d=0.10$. At $D-C=0.5$ the thresholds are 0.536 and 0.504. Along paths on which $\omega/(1-\omega)$ grows at the rate of the stakes less that of provision: with stakes growing 5% a period and provision fixed, from $\omega=0.20$, $p_d$ rises from 0 to 0.07 and $\bar p_d$ falls from 0.911 to 0.386 by period 30, and the blades cross at period 31.8; with provision growing as fast as the stakes, neither moves ($\omega=0.20$, $\bar p_d=0.911$ throughout); with provision outgrowing the stakes from $\omega=0.60$, enforceability returns within ten periods. Over 96 settings of the detection clock (5 to 40 periods), diffusion (0 to 0.1), held self-model (0 and 0.4), decisiveness (2 and 3) and $D-C$ (0 and 0.5), the joint threshold lies at or below each blade's own in all 96, strictly below in 84, and the breakout blade alone closes in 12 [num: JX, one reduced model; rivals do not respond]. In this model the objective blade does most of the closing; the breakout blade moves the crossing earlier. A second reduced model, with the time to decisiveness linearized and different parameters, also has the blades meet when the stakes outgrow provision and never when provision grows with them, with the breakout probability small at the meeting [num: JX]. That the blades move together in the world is conjecture C0.
