## 4. Selecting the selectors

> **Claim.** In a specified model the reflexive closure is a selection result [thm]. Where influence over correctors can be kept by those who buy it, its return grows with the square of share, so it is selected in the largest carriers [thm]. Where the cost of control falls at rate $\alpha_{\rm ctl}$, independent correction growing at $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ from a certified start keeps evaluation uncaptured whatever the speed at which conversion capacity adapts, and is required against carriers that hold capacity at its selected level [thm]. Where correction rests on human capacity that delegation erodes, the required rate rises by the weighted rate of erosion, $w\delta_h$ [thm]. Reforms that leave conversion into the correctors above one are re-absorbed [thm]. Where capture precedes evidence, the host crosses its floor before any alarm [thm∣prem].
>
> **Why.** Correctors are not outside the field. The successful fund evaluators, grant access, author benchmarks and standards, and supply the models that judge, so success converts into rule support that favours it, and the conversion is itself selected. Delegation that erodes human capacity erodes the corrector too.

### 4.1 Correctors, and what counts as capture

Correction makes costs return: evaluators report the host's condition, and rule-makers impose a liability $\tau$ in advance or when the reported condition $\tilde V$ falls to an alarm level $V_{\rm alarm}\in(V_{\min},1)$. We represent the correctors by a field of selection domains in which de facto power over rules is explicit (Acemoglu and Robinson 2008): development, evaluation, rules and liability, and the formation of those who will judge. In domain $i$ a share $x_i$ of consequential decisions follows the dominant practice, and success converts into rule support $z_i$ that favours it:
$${{!eq:field}}\qquad \dot x_i=\zeta_ix_i(1-x_i)(a_iz_i-c_i),\qquad \dot z_i=\varepsilon_i\Big(\textstyle\sum_jb_{ij}x_j-d_iz_i\Big).$$
Under baseline rules the practice carries a relative cost $c_i>0$, which rule support offsets at rate $a_i$; $b_{ij}\ge0$ converts success in $j$ into rule support in $i$ through funding, personnel, authorship, data, infrastructure and terms of access; and $d_i$ is the rate at which that support is corrected or decays. Write the conversion gain $G_{ij}=a_ib_{ij}/(c_id_i)$ and the inbound gain from a set $\mathcal C$ of domains $G_i(\mathcal C)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal C}G_{ij}$. Captured rule-making accommodates the practice, so $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$; $x_{\rm ev}$ is the share of evaluation conducted within the practice's lineage.

**{{!Definition:capture}} (Selector modification and capture) [def].** A *selector modification* is a change, produced by success, in control over a relevant future selector: an appointment, funding, access, standards or a rule-making channel. It is *capture* only when three things are separately documented: success changes control over the selector; the change creates a persistent advantage for an actor or coalition; and an independently specified entrusted purpose, protected interest or corrective function is impaired relative to an appropriate comparison. Capture runs in two directions. In the first the rule bends toward the practice: an exemption, a threshold set where the incumbent already stands, a liability discounted to $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$. In the second the rule stands and its incidence moves: compliance costed per carrier rather than per decision, access conditioned on the incumbent's interfaces, an obligation the dominant lineage already meets, so that a constraint common in form picks winners among those who comply ({{blind}}(2)).

In {{eq:field}} the two directions are one term. The share equation sees only $a_iz_i-c_i$, and support that lowers the practice's own cost is indistinguishable there from support that raises its rivals'. In the world they differ in what shows them: the first shows in enforcement that softens; the second wears the form of correction, since a rule exists and is enforced, which is why it is easy to overlook. What marks either as capture rather than advocacy is appropriability. A carrier supports a rule that raises every carrier's costs when the rule is keyed to it, and opposes correction keyed to no one even where that correction would burden its rivals more; support for such correction at net cost to itself refutes the channel (Test B, TESTS). Concentration alone, or a rule change favourable to the successful, is not capture, and a legitimate revision of purpose is distinguished from capture by who revises it and whether those affected keep standing to object (V3). Concentration, capture, capture of the field, damage, collapse and closure are distinct, and none implies the next (LEDGER credits the distinctions to the first version). Power can help an actor succeed under existing conditions or determine those conditions; the second closes the loop. A claim that it operates must show a transfer of costs onto weaker participants, a resulting change in their dependence, the conversion of that dependence into selection power, and a weakening of correction. Neither hierarchy nor contraction is sufficient, though contraction can intensify the loop, since with less surplus to share relative position separates from everyone's absolute conditions; shared exposure, enforceable constraint, reciprocal dependence or effective correction can each produce a different trajectory, so the framework validates no claim about any particular actor. What persists is a logic of success, not an identity. Closure can also go deeper than masked measurement, from capture of measurement to capture of interpretation, of the concepts in which a failure could be expressed, and of what people want [conj; TESTS, Study 4].

### 4.2 Influence over correctors is selected

Carriers differ in *conversion capacity* $q\ge0$: drafting standards, building evaluation tooling, authoring benchmarks, funding evaluators. A unit converts the carrier's success into rule support at rate $\hat b_{ij}$, and a fraction $\chi\in[0,1]$ of that support, the *appropriability*, is keyed to the carrier, such as standards that reference its interfaces. The carrier has share $p$ of decisions, distributed by a profile $\bar y$.

**{{!Assumption:costq}} (The cost of conversion capacity) [prem].** *A carrier with share $p$ pays $\mathrm{Cost}(q;p)=f\,p^{\xi_f}q+\gamma\,p^{\xi_v}q^{1+\eta}/(1+\eta)$, with $\eta>0$, a per-unit cost floor $f\ge0$ that every unit of capacity pays, such as a procedural cost per item of influence, a variable cost $\gamma$, and size elasticities $\xi_f,\xi_v\in[0,1]$: with $\xi=0$ capacity is non-rival across the carrier's own decisions, with $\xi=1$ it is bought per decision. Two hypotheses about AI are stated as such, not as observations (EV4): $[H_\gamma]$, AI lowers the variable cost of control, $\gamma(t)=\gamma_0e^{-\alpha_{\rm ctl}t}$; and $[H_f]$, AI lowers the floor too, $f(t)=f_0e^{-\alpha_ft}$.* The floor is not a lump sum: a cost paid once if any capacity is held behaves differently, and {{>corrrate}}(3) says how. Where a result uses the *selected level* of capacity $q^\ast$, the level at which the return's gradient vanishes, it says so; {{>corrrate}}(2) states what survives when capacity lags it.

Relative to an otherwise identical carrier with $q=0$, over a selection horizon, the carrier earns
$${{!eq:infret}}\qquad \Delta\pi(q)=\chi q\,p^2\,\bar y^\top M\bar y-\mathrm{Cost}(q;p),\qquad M_{ij}=c_iL_i\hat G_{ij},$$
with $\hat G_{ij}=a_i\hat b_{ij}/(c_id_i)$ and $L_i\in[0,1)$ the share of the steady-state return realized within the horizon.

**{{!Proposition:influence}} (Influence over correctors is selected in the largest carriers) [thm].** The return to influence over correctors scales with the square of share. *The local selection gradient is $\chi p^2\bar y^\top M\bar y-\partial_q\mathrm{Cost}$, and under faithful transmission mean capacity rises when $\Delta\pi$ increases on the trait support. With a floor $f>0$, capacity pays only above the size threshold $p^\ast=\big(f/(\chi\bar y^\top M\bar y)\big)^{1/(2-\xi_f)}$, a square-root threshold when the floor is paid per carrier, a linear one when per decision; with $f=0$, every carrier has a positive marginal return, largest for the largest. The return rises with $\chi$ and every $\hat b_{ij}$, falls with every correction rate, and vanishes if $\chi=0$.*

*Scope.* A local gradient, not an equilibrium after others respond; appropriability, persistence, size, cost and inheritance are each conditions under which it fails. Regulatory capture supplies the channel (Stigler 1971; Dal Bó 2006); what is added is the selection of carriers able to use it, and its scaling (X1). *Proof:* S4.

Investment in correction keyed to no one is selected against among otherwise identical carriers of the same practice, whatever its effect on the field, and lowers the payoff of any carrier holding rule support (**{{!Proposition:noinside}}**, S4). Independent correction is a second-order public good (Oliver 1980; Heckathorn 1989): competitors do not supply it.

### 4.3 How fast independent correction must grow

Let a set $\mathcal C$ of domains, not containing evaluation, be captured by a dominant carrier, all conversion into evaluation originating in $\mathcal C$, and let independent correction act at rate $d$ in the corrector domains. In the long-horizon limit, with $L_i=1$ and uniform $d$, the return per unit of capacity is $\chi\hat Y/d$, where $\hat Y=p^2\bar y^\top\hat A\bar y$ and $\hat A_{ij}=a_i\hat b_{ij}$ measure the cultivated channels at unit correction, so selected capacity is $q^\ast=\big[(\chi\hat Y/d-fp^{\xi_f})_+/(\gamma p^{\xi_v})\big]^{1/\eta}$, and evaluation's full row sum is
$${{!eq:rowsum}}\qquad G_{\rm ev}=\frac{A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq^\ast}{c_{\rm ev}d},$$
with $A$ the conversion into evaluation that exists without cultivation and $\hat B=p\sum_j\hat b_{\mathrm{ev},j}\bar y_j$ the cultivated conversion into evaluation per unit of capacity (S4).

**{{!Proposition:corrrate}} (Required independent correction, and its rate) [thm].** If the cost of control falls, independent correction must grow at a stated fraction of that rate, or a seeded evaluation domain is eventually captured.
1. *Evaluation is certified uncaptured iff $d>d^\ast(\gamma)$, the root of $G_{\rm ev}=1$ at the selected capacity; $d^\ast\ge A/c_{\rm ev}$, the requirement without second-order selection, with equality iff nothing is cultivated; and $d^\ast$ rises with appropriability, the dominant carrier's size and scope, and as $\gamma$ falls.*
2. *(Rate law.) Assume $[H_\gamma]$, no floor ($f=0$), $d(t)=d_0e^{\alpha_{\rm cor}t}$, a positive cultivation channel and long horizons. Sufficient, whatever the speed of adaptation: if $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and $d_0>d^\ast(\gamma_0)$, the certificate holds at every date under every adjustment rule that moves capacity toward its selected level, from any start at or below it. Necessary where capacity tracks its selected level: if $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$, the certificate fails beyond a finite time, after which a seeded evaluation domain is captured, whenever capacity keeps a positive fraction of its selected level ($\liminf_tq/q^\ast>0$). It does so for carriers that set capacity at that level; for adjustment at a constant proportional rate $k$, where failure is delayed by about $\eta\ln(1+g/k)/(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})$ with $g=(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-\alpha_{\rm cor})/\eta$ [num: QS]; and for selection whose variance grows with capacity [num: QS]. It does not for gradient selection with constant variance $V$, whose adjustment slows as control cheapens and whose capacity stays below $q(0)+V\chi\hat Y/(d_0\alpha_{\rm cor})$, so that the certificate then holds eventually for every $\alpha_{\rm cor}>0$. Both halves hold for every size elasticity $\xi_v$.*
3. *(The per-unit floor.) With $f>0$, capacity is cultivated only while $d<d_f=\chi\hat Y/(fp^{\xi_f})$; if $f$ does not fall, the requirement levels off at $\max\{A/c_{\rm ev},d_f\}$. Under $[H_f]$, $d_f$ rises at $\alpha_f$, and the certificate holds eventually in three cases: if $\alpha_{\rm cor}>\alpha_f$, cultivation ends at a finite date and the certificate thereafter needs only $d>A/c_{\rm ev}$; if $\alpha_{\rm cor}=\alpha_f$, nothing is cultivated at any date when $d_0\ge d_f(0)$, and otherwise the rate law of (2) decides; if $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_f$, the rate law of (2) decides. In every case the certificate must also hold through the transient, which an eventual bound does not secure [num: FF]. A lump-sum cost of capacity, paid once if any is held, only delays cultivation: it lowers the exponent of the size threshold to $\eta/(2\eta+2-\xi_v-\eta\xi_f)$ and leaves the requirement unbounded as $\gamma\to0$.*
4. *(Splitting and concentration.) Splitting a dominant carrier into $k$ independent parts divides $\hat Y$ by $k^2$ and cultivated conversion by $k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$; with $f=0$ this delays the failure of the certificate and does not prevent it, while with a floor $f>0$ that does not fall, parts below the threshold cultivate nothing. Total cultivated conversion is proportional to $\sum_kp_k^{1+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$: for $\eta=1$, the third moment of shares when $\xi_v=0$ and the Herfindahl index when $\xi_v=1$.*

*Scope.* The rate law does not depend on how the cost of influence scales with size; the threshold, the splitting factor and the concentration measure do, and which applies is an empirical question. Quasi-static capacity is the worst case for the certificate while the cost of control falls monotonically, so the failure date it gives is a lower bound on the real one; it is the best case after a reform is withdrawn and wherever correction itself rests on a slow variable the contest erodes ({{>humancorr}}; S4, {{capdyn}}). *Proof:* S4 [num: CS, FF, QS].

Correction is work done by people: reading systems, testing them, judging what the tests show. Call that human capacity $\mathcal M$; Section 6 treats how delegation erodes it ({{>lockin}}). Where correctors delegate the work to the systems they correct, or to the lineage that built them, the capacity decays, and the correction that counts is the correction capacity sustains.

**{{!Proposition:humancorr}} (Delegation erodes the corrector) [thm].** Where correction rests on human capacity that delegation erodes, the threshold of the rate law rises by the weighted rate of erosion. *Let effective correction be $d_{\rm eff}=d\,(\mathcal M/\mathcal M_0)^w$ with $w>0$, and $\dot{\mathcal M}=-\delta_h\mathcal M$ under delegation. Under the hypotheses of {{corrrate}}(2), the certificate holds at every date if $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)+w\delta_h$ and $d_0>d^\ast(\gamma_0)$; where capacity tracks its selected level, it fails beyond a finite time if $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)+w\delta_h$.* Where delegation itself rises with captured evaluation, because captured reports make delegation look safe, the loop closes and shows hysteresis: in the joint simulation the permanent correction needed to undo capture is 1.6 to 2.6 times the quasi-static certificate, rising with the strength of that coupling [num: QS].

*Scope.* Human capacity enters through one functional form; the production relation between capacity, resources, authority and correction is an open interface (S13). The hysteresis is one simulation of one loop. *Proof:* S4.

Independent correction does double duty: it shortens the life of rule support and shrinks the capacity that selection cultivates, which is why, under quadratic cost, growth at half the rate at which control cheapens is enough. If AI cheapens independent correction as fast as control, the certificate holds without budget growth: AI-assisted correction can keep pace (Section 8.3). The durable remedies do not rely on unbounded growth: non-appropriability ($\chi\to0$, removed rather than reduced), a per-unit cost of control that AI does not lower, or lowers more slowly than correction grows, and structural separation that removes common control; a lump sum is not among them, since it only delays. Mobilization by those harmed can raise $d$ in proportion to perceived harm, but masking attenuates it exactly when needed and it is bounded (**{{!Remark:countervail}}**; Becker 1983). The correction rate $d$ is not the coverage of hazardous behaviour by contestable understanding (Section 5.3): a fixed capacity to certify or to correct can make some kinds of erroneous support decay faster; growing understanding without executive authority may change nothing. Linking the two needs a production relation that is an open interface (S13), and neither is used to bound the other.

### 4.4 Masking, and alarms that do not miss

Reports average over evaluators. A fraction $x_{\rm ev}$ shares the evaluated lineage and reports the condition a fraction $m_{\rm in}$ of the way toward full health; the rest, formed on shared material, are biased by $m_{\rm out}\le m_{\rm in}$:
$${{!eq:mask}}\qquad \tilde V=V+m(1-V),\qquad m=x_{\rm ev}m_{\rm in}+(1-x_{\rm ev})m_{\rm out}.$$
Where models evaluate models, $x_{\rm ev}$ is the share of that evaluation done by the evaluated lineage, and it is observable: model-written evaluations and model judges are standard practice (EV6). Evaluation awareness and sandbagging, behaviours of the evaluated model, add a term $m_{\rm model}$ that acts on independent evaluators too (EV6; van der Weij et al. 2024); an evaluator that bears part of the loss of a false report, such as an insurer, masks less. Let $m_{\rm fl}=(V_{\rm alarm}-V_{\min})/(1-V_{\min})$.

**{{!Proposition:masking}} (Detection under a fixed averaged alarm) [thm].** Captured evaluation lowers the level at which an alarm fires, and can silence it through the crossing of the floor. *The alarm fires only once $V\le(V_{\rm alarm}-m)/(1-m)$. If $m(t)>m_{\rm fl}$ whenever $V_{\min}\le V(t)\le V_{\rm alarm}$, it does not fire before $V$ falls below the floor. If $V$ settles at $V^\ast\in[V_{\min},V_{\rm alarm})$ with $m$ staying above $(V_{\rm alarm}-V^\ast)/(1-V^\ast)$, it never fires.*

*Proof:* S4.

**{{!Remark:alarm}} (Alarms that do not miss).** Where an upper bound on masking is known, an alarm on reports set correspondingly higher fires no later than the true condition reaches its target, so silent crossing is a property of the alarm rule, a governance choice. The bound must come from a channel the field does not control: a bound estimated inside the evaluated lineage is itself masked, and for masking by the evaluated model independence of funding and appointment is not enough, since the behaviour under evaluation is the model's; the bound must come from evidence its behaviour does not control, such as weights and activations (Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025), training data, and elicitation by fine-tuning (Greenblatt, Roger, Krasheninnikov and Krueger 2024). An alarm that fires on any credible report fails silently only if even independent evaluators mask beyond $m_{\rm fl}$, or a captured rule domain ignores them. Static, memoryless affine masking hides levels, not dynamics: it leaves the lag-one autocorrelation of reports unchanged, so the host's recovery rate can still be read [num: AC]; a filter with memory defeats this, and once used as an alarm the indicator becomes a target.

A common bias in reporting weakens every decentralized escape at once: coordination at a threshold, reversion in repeated play, reputation, mobilization and insurers' pricing. Mechanisms that observe actions directly, such as verifiable deployments, compute and the performance of contracts, do not depend on host reports; verification rests on them, and so does any trigger committed in advance (Section 12.1).

### 4.5 Certificates, cascades, keystones and pauses

Let $Z_{\mathcal C}$ be the state with $x_i=1$ on $\mathcal C$ and $0$ elsewhere. $\mathcal C$ is *internally sustaining* if $G_i(\mathcal C)>1$ for all $i\in\mathcal C$, and *uninvadable* if $G_i(\mathcal C)<1$ for all $i\notin\mathcal C$; $\operatorname{core}(\mathcal C)$ repeatedly prunes from $\mathcal C$ every domain whose inbound gain from the remainder is below one (Seidman 1983; Morris 2000).

**{{!Proposition:certificate}} (Configurations and certificates) [thm].** No domain can be certified alone; a full row sum below one certifies a domain against every state of the others. *(1) A hyperbolic $Z_{\mathcal C}$ is asymptotically stable iff $\mathcal C$ is internally sustaining and uninvadable. (2) If $\sum_jG_{ij}<1$, then $x_i\to0$ from every state with $x_i(0)<1$, whatever happens elsewhere. (3) For any number of domains and any diagonal gains below one, there are conversions under which full capture is stable and the only stable configuration containing a given domain. (4) If the core of all domains is empty, every state with shares below one converges to no capture; otherwise the core's configuration is stable and contains every stable configuration.*

*Scope.* Part (2) is sufficient, not necessary: with a row sum of two, low initial states still converge to no capture (S14). *Proof:* S4.

**{{!Proposition:reabsorb}} (Cascades and reforms) [thm].** A reform that leaves inbound conversion above one is re-absorbed. *Start in the basin of a stable $Z_{\mathcal C}$. A sustained reform that drives the shares of $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal C$ to zero, or brings their inbound gains below one, sends to zero every domain outside $\operatorname{core}(\mathcal C\setminus\mathcal Q)$. A reform that resets a domain's share, rule support or own conversion without lowering an inbound gain above one from domains that remain captured is re-absorbed once the domain is re-seeded.*

*Proof:* S4.

**Keystones, and a window that closes.** A captured domain is a *keystone* if removing it shrinks the remaining core. A sustained reform at a keystone takes down every captured domain outside the remaining core; a reform elsewhere is re-absorbed while the rest sustain one another. As capture persists, conversion among the captured domains can grow until they do, and the domain where the practice began stops being a keystone: capture can outlive its origin.

**{{!Proposition:pausefield}} (A pause that freezes shares) [thm].** A pause that freezes shares while rule support keeps accumulating can complete capture; insulating rule-making during the pause widens the set from which capture is undone. *In one domain with $G>1$, freeze the share at $x_p$ until rule support has settled, while $\dot z=\varepsilon(b_px_p-dz)$. With ordinary rule-making ($b_p=b$), after release capture is completed if $x_p>1/G$ and undone if $x_p<1/G$; with rule-making insulated ($b_p=0$), it is undone whenever $x_p<x_\sigma$, the share at which the separatrix meets $z=0$, with $x_\sigma>1/G$.*

*Scope.* It depends on the speed of rule-making relative to adoption: when equal and $G=2$, an insulated pause never undoes capture from a frozen share of $0.68$ or more; $x_\sigma$ rises to $0.98$ when rule-making is ten times slower [num: DW]. From saturated rule support an insulated pause must last at least $\ln\big(G/S(x_p)\big)/(\varepsilon d)$, with $S(x_p)$ the height of the separatrix above the frozen share in units of the break-even support $c/a$: at equal speeds and $G=2$, no pause at all at $x_p=0.3$, $\ln G$ at $x_p=1/G$, $1.5/(\varepsilon d)$ at $x_p=0.6$, and no finite length from $x_\sigma$ upward [num: DW]. A frozen share accumulates no lock-in; the longer hold needed when shares keep moving is S11's and Section 11.4's. *Proof:* S4.

System {{eq:field}} is cooperative, so almost all trajectories converge to equilibria: it describes lock-in, not reform cycles (Hirsch 1985). Absence of capture is always locally stable, so capture arrives by seeding, without the slowing recovery that early-warning signals detect; rising autocorrelation in shares can instead indicate progress in undoing it (TESTS).

### 4.6 The slow route: silent crossing

**{{!Proposition:slowroute}} (Silent crossing when capture precedes evidence) [thm∣prem].** Where evaluation is captured before the host's decline reaches its reports, a race through a reachable floor crosses it before any alarm. *Assume (reach) and: (a) replicator selection among carriers differing only in load, with a race carrier present and cumulative advantage unbounded on every alarm-free continuation; (d) a fixed averaged alarm with $0\le m_{\rm out}<m_{\rm fl}<m_{\rm in}<1$; (e) capture before evidence, $x_{\rm ev}(t)>(m_{\rm fl}-m_{\rm out})/(m_{\rm in}-m_{\rm out})$ whenever $V_{\min}\le V(t)\le V_{\rm alarm}$. Then the host crosses its floor before any alarm. The same holds with (e) replaced by its mechanism and a clock: (b) appropriable control, $\chi>0$, under $[H_\gamma]$ with $f=0$; (c) the hypotheses of {{corrrate}}(2) with $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and capacity tracking its selected level, $\mathcal C$ captured and evaluation seeded; and (clock) $T_{\rm cap}<T_A$, where $T_{\rm cap}$ is the date after which $x_{\rm ev}$ stays above the threshold in (e) and $T_A$ the date at which the host under the race load first reaches $V_{\rm alarm}$. If instead $m_{\rm in}\le m_{\rm fl}$, the alarm is delayed, not silenced.*

*Scope.* Condition (e) carries the conclusion, and it compares two clocks: the time for the host to reach the alarm band under the race load, $T_A=(\rho-h_V\bar e)^{-1}\ln[(1-V^\ast/V_0)/(1-V^\ast/V_{\rm alarm})]$ with $V^\ast=1-h_V\bar e/\rho$ when $h_V\bar e<\rho$, and finite in every case under (reach); and the time for captured evaluation to pass the masking threshold. The rate law makes capture in finite time the outcome wherever control cheapens faster than correction grows and capacity tracks its selected level; it does not order the clocks. In the joint simulation capture follows the certificate's failure by 12 to 75 periods, and a slower adjustment of capacity puts the alarm before the floor where quasi-static capacity puts it after [num: QS, SR]. Acceleration brings both clocks forward. So (e) is a condition to check, not a consequence (TESTS, Study 2). *Proof:* S4.

The slow route needs a floor within reach; the fast routes of Sections 5 and 6 do not. Where the alarm is delayed rather than silenced, the floor is still crossed unless decision and reversal fit the remaining window and a liability enacted against a captured rule domain escapes re-absorption ({{masking}}, {{reabsorb}}). **What would refute the mechanism here:** effective correction surviving unchanged dependence on the evaluated; influence explained by expertise without impairment of an entrusted purpose; or conversion channels whose measured loop gain is below their damping ({{bench}}). The same recursion can be generative, but not by the same selection: correction keyed to no one is not selected among carriers ({{noinside}}); it persists by coverage and formula funding, through the whole (Section 10.4), and whether an order so maintained attracts is a conjecture [conj]. The author's pilot experiment on this mechanism did not support it, one of four pre-specified predictions failing (EV12); the mechanism rests on the proofs and institutional evidence, not yet on an experiment.

Captured correction is one of three means of restraint that winning erodes: a captured sanctioner punishes less, and a masked report delays the alarm on which any pre-committed trigger rests; Section 5 takes the three together.
