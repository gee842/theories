## S4. Selecting the selectors

### Proof of {{influence}}, and the effect of scope

Support keyed to the carrier obeys $\dot z^\chi_i=\varepsilon_i(\chi q\sum_j\hat b_{ij}y_j-d_iz^\chi_i)$ from zero; its horizon average is $z_i^{\chi\ast}L_i$ with $z_i^{\chi\ast}=\chi q\sum_j\hat b_{ij}y_j/d_i$ and $L_i=1-(1-e^{-\varepsilon_id_iT_h})/(\varepsilon_id_iT_h)$. Weighting the per-decision advantage $a_iz^\chi_i$ by $y_i$ and summing gives $\chi q\,y^\top My$, with $y=p\bar y$; shared support cancels. Differentiating gives the gradient, and under faithful transmission $\dot{\bar q}\propto\operatorname{Cov}(q,\Delta\pi(q))$ (Price 1970). With $f>0$ and the floor $fp^{\xi_f}q$, $\Delta\pi'(0)>0$ iff $\chi p^2\bar y^\top M\bar y>fp^{\xi_f}$, that is, $p>p^\ast$, and for $p<p^\ast$ capacity loses for every $q>0$. The comparative statics follow from $c_iL_i\hat G_{ij}=a_i\hat b_{ij}\varepsilon_iT_h\,\ell_L(\varepsilon_id_iT_h)$ with $\ell_L(v)=[1-(1-e^{-v})/v]/v$ decreasing. $\square$

**{{!Proposition:scope}} (Scope) [thm].** *If the carrier has share $p$ in each domain of a set $\mathcal S$, with $\hat G$ equal to $\hat g$ on the diagonal and $\hat\varphi$ off it and $c_iL_i=cL$, the return is $\chi cL\,p^2|\mathcal S|(\hat g+(|\mathcal S|-1)\hat\varphi)$: superlinear in $|\mathcal S|$ at fixed $p$ when $\hat\varphi>0$, and rising with scope at fixed total size only if $\hat\varphi>\hat g$.* (Sum over $\mathcal S$ and substitute $p=Y/|\mathcal S|$.)

*Relation to earlier work.* Construction of a shared resource is undermined by competition, and monopolizing the constructed niche restores the incentive to build (Krakauer, Page and Erwin 2009); {{influence}} has the same appropriability logic, with the selection rule as the object modified, a field of domains, and the opposite valence. Dynamic models of constitutions show present decisions changing future decision rights (Acemoglu, Egorov and Sonin 2012); whether a model of that family reproduces Section 4 is open. Fixed-cost participation in lobbying (Bombardini 2008) is the empirical counterpart of the threshold. Appropriability plays the role relatedness plays in Hamilton's rule.

### Full statement and proof of {{noinside}}

*Let carriers differ in an investment $u\ge0$, at private cost $C_u(u)$, that raises the correction rate $d$ for everyone. (1) Among otherwise identical carriers of the same practice, those investing more are selected against under any payoff-monotone rule. (2) Holding the host fixed, correction lowers the payoff of a carrier that holds rule support. (3) A challenger invests only if its own share of the benefit exceeds $C_u'(0)$, so dispersed challengers free-ride. (4) Correction keyed to the corrector yields a return to influence of the form of {{influence}}; whether it produces capture depends on whether row sums cross one. (5) If $\Upsilon>1$ and correction makes liability effective, an incumbent can gain from correction applied to all carriers, while correction applied to it alone costs it rent and share.*

Reputation or competition among institutions sustains independent correction only when contributions and outcomes are observed (Panchanathan and Boyd 2004; Sigmund et al. 2010). *Proof.* (1) Two carriers differing only in $u$ are affected identically by the change in $d$; their payoff difference is $C_u(u_l)-C_u(u_k)<0$ for $u_k>u_l$. (2) At fixed $V$ a carrier's support declines in $d$. (3) is the public-goods condition. (4) is immediate. (5) compares $\nu\bar e(\Upsilon-1)$ with the lost rent. $\square$

### The selected level, the row sum and the proof of {{corrrate}}

With $L_i=1$ and uniform $d$, $M=\hat A/d$ and {{eq:infret}} reads $\Delta\pi(q)=\chi q\hat Y/d-\mathrm{Cost}(q;p)$. Its gradient vanishes at $q^\ast=\big[(\chi\hat Y/d-fp^{\xi_f})_+/(\gamma p^{\xi_v})\big]^{1/\eta}$: capacity is cultivated iff $\chi\hat Y/d>fp^{\xi_f}$, that is, iff $d<d_f=\chi\hat Y/(fp^{\xi_f})$. Conversion into evaluation from the captured set is $A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq$ with $\hat B=p\sum_j\hat b_{\mathrm{ev},j}\bar y_j$, which gives {{eq:rowsum}}. Splitting the carrier into $k$ equal parts divides $\hat Y$ by $k^2$, since $\hat Y\propto p^2$ at a fixed profile.

*(1).* At the selected level, with $f=0$,
$$G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}(d)=\frac{A}{c_{\rm ev}d}+Z_\eta\,(\gamma p^{\xi_v})^{-1/\eta}d^{-(1+\eta)/\eta},\qquad Z_\eta=\frac{a_{\rm ev}\hat B(\chi\hat Y)^{1/\eta}}{c_{\rm ev}},$$
strictly decreasing in $d$; with $f>0$ the cultivated term carries the extra factor $(1-d/d_f)_+^{1/\eta}$ and is still decreasing. So $d>d^\ast$ iff $G_{\rm ev}<1$, where $d^\ast$ is the unique root of $G_{\rm ev}=1$; at $d=A/c_{\rm ev}$ the first term alone equals one, so $d^\ast\ge A/c_{\rm ev}$ with equality iff $q^\ast(d^\ast)=0$. For $\eta=1$ and $f=0$, $d^\ast=\big(A+\sqrt{A^2+4c_{\rm ev}Z}\big)/(2c_{\rm ev})$ with $Z=a_{\rm ev}\hat B\chi\hat Y/(\gamma p^{\xi_v})$. The cultivated term rises with $\chi$, with $\hat Y$ (the square of share, and scope through $\bar y^\top\hat A\bar y$) and as $\gamma$ falls, so $d^\ast$ does too.

*(2), sufficient.* Let $q(t)$ follow any rule under which capacity moves toward its selected level, from $q(0)\le q^\ast(0)$: then $q(t)\le\sup_{s\le t}q^\ast(s)$, because $q$ can exceed $q^\ast$ only by having been above it earlier. Write $G_{\rm ev}(t)=(A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq(t))/(c_{\rm ev}d(t))$. Since $d$ is nondecreasing, for every $s\le t$, $(A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq^\ast(s))/(c_{\rm ev}d(t))\le G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}(s)$; hence $G_{\rm ev}(t)\le\sup_{s\le t}G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}(s)$. Along $\gamma_0e^{-\alpha_{\rm ctl}t}$ and $d_0e^{\alpha_{\rm cor}t}$ the first term of $G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}$ is nonincreasing and the cultivated term is proportional to $e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$, whatever $\xi_v$, since the size factor $p^{-\xi_v/\eta}$ is constant; so when $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$, $G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}$ is nonincreasing and $G_{\rm ev}(t)\le G^{\rm QS}_{\rm ev}(0)<1$ for all $t$. The rate condition does not repair a certificate invalid at the start.

*(2), necessary.* If $\liminf_tq/q^\ast=\rho>0$, then eventually $q\ge\frac\rho2q^\ast$ and the cultivated term of $G_{\rm ev}$ is at least $\frac\rho2$ times the quasi-static one, which grows without bound when $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$; so $G_{\rm ev}>1$ beyond a finite date. Once the captured set holds at least half its decisions, $q^\ast$ grows as $e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$, and integrating rule support in {{eq:field}} gives $a_{\rm ev}z_{\rm ev}(t)\ge c\,e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$ for a constant $c>0$, so $\operatorname{logit}x_{\rm ev}\to\infty$: a seeded evaluation domain is captured. *The named dynamics.* Carriers that set capacity at its selected level have $q/q^\ast=1$. Under adjustment at a constant proportional rate, $\dot q=k(q^\ast-q)$ with $q^\ast\propto e^{gt}$, $g=(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-\alpha_{\rm cor})/\eta$, the ratio $q/q^\ast$ tends to $k/(k+g)$, and a cultivated term scaled by $k/(k+g)$ reaches a given level later by $\ln(1+g/k)\big/[(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})/\eta]$, the stated delay. Under gradient selection with variance proportional to $q^2$, $\dot q=Vq^2(\chi\hat Y/d-\gamma p^{\xi_v}q^\eta)$, the relaxation rate at the selected level is $V\eta\gamma p^{\xi_v}q^{\ast\,1+\eta}\propto\gamma^{-1/\eta}d^{-(1+\eta)/\eta}\propto e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$, which grows exactly where the cultivated term does, so below the threshold capacity tracks its selected level with a vanishing lag [num: QS]. Under gradient selection with constant variance, $\dot q=V(\chi\hat Y/d-\gamma p^{\xi_v}q^\eta)\le V\chi\hat Y/d(t)$, so $q(t)\le q(0)+V\chi\hat Y/(d_0\alpha_{\rm cor})$ for every $\alpha_{\rm cor}>0$; capacity is bounded, $G_{\rm ev}\le(A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq_{\max})/(c_{\rm ev}d(t))\to0$, and the certificate holds eventually, after at most a transient failure. The adjustment rate of this dynamics, $V\eta\gamma p^{\xi_v}q^{\eta-1}$, falls with $\gamma$: selection that must keep jumping toward a receding optimum is strategic choice, not gradual selection, which is why the necessary half is stated for carriers that track the level.

*(3).* $q^\ast>0$ iff $d<d_f$. With $f$ fixed, $q^\ast=0$ once $d\ge d_f$, so the requirement levels off at $\max\{A/c_{\rm ev},d_f\}$. Under $[H_f]$, $d_f(t)=d_f(0)e^{\alpha_ft}$ rises, and $d/d_f=(d_0/d_f(0))\,e^{(\alpha_{\rm cor}-\alpha_f)t}$. If $\alpha_{\rm cor}>\alpha_f$, $d/d_f\to\infty$: cultivation ends at $T_f=\ln\big(d_f(0)/d_0\big)/(\alpha_{\rm cor}-\alpha_f)$ (or never began), after which $G_{\rm ev}=A/(c_{\rm ev}d)\to0$. If $\alpha_{\rm cor}=\alpha_f$, $d/d_f$ is constant: with $d_0\ge d_f(0)$ nothing is cultivated at any date; with $d_0<d_f(0)$, $q^\ast=\big[(\chi\hat Y/d)(1-d_0/d_f(0))/(\gamma p^{\xi_v})\big]^{1/\eta}$ grows as in the $f=0$ case up to a constant, and (2) decides. If $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_f$, $d/d_f\to0$, the bracket $(1-d/d_f)^{1/\eta}\to1$, and $q^\ast$ is asymptotically the $f=0$ level, so (2) decides. *The transient.* An eventual certificate is not one at every date: when $\alpha_{\rm cor}$ exceeds $\alpha_f$ by little, $T_f$ is long and the cultivated term grows meanwhile at rate $(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})/\eta$ [num: FF]. *Lump sum.* With a cost $Fp^{\xi_f}$ paid once if any capacity is held, the carrier cultivates the $f=0$ level $q^\ast_0$ iff the maximized surplus exceeds it: $\Delta\pi(q^\ast_0)=\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\,q^\ast_0\,\chi\hat Y/d\propto p^{2+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$, so the size threshold is $p^\ast\propto F^{\eta/(2\eta+2-\xi_v-\eta\xi_f)}$, below the exponent $1/(2-\xi_f)$ of the per-unit floor; and once cultivation pays, $q^\ast=q^\ast_0$ grows without bound as $\gamma\to0$, so the requirement is not capped.

*(4).* Parts of share $1/k$ have $\hat Y$ divided by $k^2$ and the size factor $p^{-\xi_v/\eta}$ multiplied by $k^{\xi_v/\eta}$, so $q^\ast$ is divided by $k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$ and the cultivated term reaches a given level later by $\Delta t=(2-\xi_v)\ln k/(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})$; with $f>0$ fixed, parts below the threshold cultivate nothing; and with $q_k^\ast\propto p_k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$ and conversion proportional to $p_kq_k^\ast$, the total is proportional to $\sum_kp_k^{1+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$. $\square$

*Further parts.* If AI lowers the unit cost of independent correction at rate $\alpha_{uc}$ and its budget grows at $\alpha_B$, then $\alpha_{\rm cor}=\alpha_B+\alpha_{uc}$, and eventual failure needs $\alpha_{\rm ctl}>(1+\eta)(\alpha_B+\alpha_{uc})$. With a finite horizon, while $\varepsilon_idT_h\ll1$ the required growth is about $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/\eta$, a pre-asymptotic interval. With concave conversion $\hat bq^{\xi_c}$, the required rate becomes $\xi_c\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$. The model implies that influence spending rises with share as $p^{\xi_v+(1+\eta)(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$, at least quadratically, which is testable (Test B).

### The rate law under finite adjustment

The quasi-static assumption of V6.1 is replaced by a joint model. One evaluation domain follows {{eq:field}}, $\dot x=\zeta x(1-x)(az-c)$ and $\dot z=\varepsilon(A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq-dz)$, while capacity $q$ follows one of four dynamics: quasi-static, QS, $q=q^\ast$; constant-rate adjustment, R($k$), $\dot q=k(q^\ast-q)$; gradient selection with constant variance, GA($V$), $\dot q=V\,\partial_q\Delta\pi$; and gradient selection with variance proportional to $q^2$, GM($V$), $\dot q=Vq^2\,\partial_q\Delta\pi$. Stiff parts are integrated by exact exponential steps.

**{{!Proposition:capdyn}} (The rate law under finite adjustment) [thm; num].**
1. *[thm] The sufficient half of {{corrrate}}(2) holds for every adjustment rule under which capacity never rises while above its selected level, from any start at or below that level.*
2. *[thm] The necessary half holds iff $\liminf_tq/q^\ast>0$. Under R($k$) the ratio tends to $k/(k+g)$ and failure is delayed by $\eta\ln(1+g/k)/(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})$; under GA($V$), $q\le q(0)+V\chi\hat Y/(d_0\alpha_{\rm cor})$ and the certificate holds eventually for every $\alpha_{\rm cor}>0$; under GM($V$) the ratio tends to one below the threshold [num].*
3. *[num] After a reform that raises $d$ in a captured domain and is then withdrawn, the hold needed to undo capture is 6 to 14 per cent longer than the quasi-static estimate at the parameters computed, and more when adjustment is slow relative to $\varepsilon d$.*
4. *[num] The order of the two clocks of {{slowroute}} turns on the adjustment speed of capacity: with the same host and the same correction, quasi-static and fast-adjusting capacity put the alarm after the floor where slow adjustment puts it before.*
5. *[num] Where correction rests on human capacity that delegation erodes, the certificate behaves as {{humancorr}} states, and a loop from capture through masking to delegation shows hysteresis.*

*Reading.* Quasi-static capacity is conservative while the cost of control falls monotonically, since lag only delays failure, and optimistic after reversals, where capacity is a stock, and wherever correction itself rests on a slow variable the contest erodes. TESTS Study 2's expected failure signature changes accordingly: a certificate failing with correction growing slightly above the rate law is not produced by capacity lag, which only delays failure; it points to an eroded slow variable in correction.

### Proof of {{humancorr}}, and the hysteresis loop

Under delegation $\mathcal M(t)=\mathcal M_0e^{-\delta_ht}$, so $d_{\rm eff}(t)=d_0e^{(\alpha_{\rm cor}-w\delta_h)t}$. The certificate at the selected level depends on correction only through $d_{\rm eff}$, so {{corrrate}}(2) applies with $\alpha_{\rm cor}$ replaced by $\alpha_{\rm cor}-w\delta_h$; the start condition is unchanged because $\mathcal M(0)=\mathcal M_0$. Where delegation is partial, $D(t)\in[0,1]$, and capacity recovers at $\varrho_h$ while not delegated, $\dot{\mathcal M}=-\delta_hD\mathcal M+\varrho_h(1-D)(1-\mathcal M)$, the shift is at most $w\delta_h$ and is approached as $D\to1$. $\square$

### Mobilization ({{countervail}})

Let those harmed mobilize in proportion to perceived harm, $d=d_0+k_B(1-\tilde V)$. Since $1-\tilde V=(1-m)(1-V)$, masking attenuates the feedback by $1-m$ exactly when it is needed, and even with accurate reports $d\le d_0+k_B$, which cannot keep a certificate against a requirement that rises without bound. After a recovery, mobilization falls back to $d_0$ and the certificate can fail again: a regulatory cycle. With a per-unit floor that AI does not lower, enough gain and speed can keep the certificate over a stated interval, since the requirement then levels off ({{corrrate}}(3)).

### Proof of {{masking}}, and the late alarm

$\tilde V$ increases in $V$ and equals $V_{\rm alarm}$ at $V=(V_{\rm alarm}-m)/(1-m)$. For $V_{\min}\le V\le V_{\rm alarm}$ and $m>m_{\rm fl}$, $\tilde V\ge V_{\min}+m(1-V_{\min})>V_{\rm alarm}$. At $V^\ast$, $\tilde V\ge V^\ast+\bar m(1-V^\ast)>V_{\rm alarm}$. $\square$ If $V\to0$ and eventually $m\le m_1<V_{\rm alarm}$, the alarm fires. If $V$ declines from $V_{\rm alarm}$ as $\dot V=-v_dV$ with constant $m<V_{\rm alarm}$, the alarm is delayed by $v_d^{-1}\ln[(1-m)V_{\rm alarm}/(V_{\rm alarm}-m)]$, which diverges as $m\uparrow V_{\rm alarm}$.

### {{alarm}} in full

(1) With an upper bound $m_{\rm in}$ known, an alarm on reports at $V_{\rm target}+m_{\rm in}(1-V_{\rm target})$ fires no later than the true condition reaches $V_{\rm target}$, for every $m\le m_{\rm in}$. (2) A bound estimated inside the evaluated lineage is itself masked. (3) A disjunctive alarm, firing on any credible report, fails silently only if $m_{\rm out}>m_{\rm fl}$. (4) For static, memoryless affine masking $\tilde V=m+(1-m)V$, the correlation coefficient of reports is that of $V$, exactly. (5) For masking by the evaluated model, $m_{\rm model}$, the bound in (1) cannot come from the behaviour under evaluation, whoever funds or appoints the evaluator, because that behaviour is the model's; it must come from evidence the behaviour does not control: weights and activations, which linear probes read for strategic deception (Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025), training data, and elicitation by fine-tuning, which recovers capability that a password-locked model withholds (Greenblatt, Roger, Krasheninnikov and Krueger 2024). Where models evaluate models, $x_{\rm ev}$ is observable as the share of evaluation written or judged by the evaluated lineage (EV6). *Numerics (AC):* on a stochastic logistic host with load 0.4 and true condition 0.36, masking at $m=0,0.25,0.5$ gives mean reports 0.358, 0.519 and 0.679 with the same lag-one autocorrelation, 0.9814, from which the condition recovered is 0.376; a report filter with memory (exponential smoothing at 0.8) drives the recovered value to 0.042, and the indicator fails. It also needs long series, and once used as an alarm it becomes a target; faking dynamics is nevertheless costlier than faking levels, and the indicator can be cross-checked against channels that observe actions directly.

### The field model

**{{!Lemma:fieldlemma}} (The field is cooperative) [thm].** *$[0,1]^D\times[0,\infty)^D$ is forward invariant under {{eq:field}}, $\limsup_tz_i\le\sum_jb_{ij}/d_i$, and the system is cooperative in the sense of Smith (1995), so its flow preserves the componentwise order (Hirsch 1985).*

*Proof of {{certificate}}.* (1) At $Z_{\mathcal C}$ the Jacobian is block lower-triangular, with diagonal entries $\mp\zeta_ic_i(G_i(\mathcal C)-1)$ and $-\varepsilon_id_i$. (2) By the lemma, eventually $a_iz_i-c_i\le c_i(\sum_jG_{ij}-1)/2<0$. (3) Take $G_{i1}>1$ for $i\ne1$, $G_{12}>1$, and other off-diagonal gains zero. (4) Pruning, by comparison. $\square$ Part (2) is sufficient and not necessary: with a row sum of two and a start at $(0.1,0.1)$ the state stays in $[0,0.1]\times[0,0.2]$ and converges to no capture (FA; S14).

*Proof of {{reabsorb}}.* Comparison and pruning; the re-seeding case is the cascade from the domains that remain captured. **Cascades.** If $x_j\to1$ on a seed, $x_i\to1$ on its cascade hull, the closure of $\mathcal S_{k+1}=\mathcal S_k\cup\{i:x_i(0)>0,\ G_i(\mathcal S_k)>1\}$ (Kempe, Kleinberg and Tardos 2003; Morris 2000), by induction using a lower bound on rule support.

*The keystone example.* In the first version of this model, a liability reform at development unwound capture in every domain while development was a keystone; once the other domains' mutual conversion rose to 1.1, capture survived the complete decoupling of development.

*Proof of {{pausefield}}.* The frozen share drives $z\to b_px_p/d$. The equilibria of the released system are $(0,0)$, $(1,b/d)$ and the saddle $(1/G,c/a)$. The function $axz-cx-\frac{ad}{2b}z^2$ is nondecreasing along solutions, so every solution converges to an equilibrium and the stable manifold of the saddle separates the basins; with ordinary rule-making the point $(x_p,bx_p/d)$ lies on the nullcline through the saddle, which decides by $x_p$ against $1/G$; with rule-making insulated the release point is $(x_p,0)$, and $x_\sigma>1/G$ by strong cooperativity. $\square$ *Numerics (DW):* by forward integration in logit coordinates, $x_\sigma$ is 0.676 when rule-making is as fast as adoption with $G=2$ and 0.984 when it is ten times slower; 0.321 and 0.775 for $G=5$. *The length of a frozen-share pause.* With rule-making insulated and support starting saturated at $b/d=G\,c/a$, the pause must last until $z$ falls below the separatrix at $x_p$: $z(T)=(b/d)e^{-\varepsilon dT}=S(x_p)\,c/a$, so $T=\ln\big(G/S(x_p)\big)/(\varepsilon d)$ with $S(x_p)$ the separatrix height at the frozen share in units of $c/a$; it is negative, no pause needed, where the separatrix passes above saturated support, $\ln G$ at $x_p=1/G$ where $S=1$, and infinite from $x_\sigma$ up, where the separatrix meets $z=0$. At equal speeds and $G=2$: $S=3.92$, $2.15$, $1.00$, $0.45$ at $x_p=0.1,0.3,0.5,0.6$, so $\varepsilon dT=-0.67$, $-0.07$, $0.69$, $1.50$, and infinite at 0.8 and 0.95; with rule-making ten times slower, $\varepsilon dT=0.24$, $0.48$, $0.69$, $0.81$, $1.17$, $2.08$ at $x_p=0.1,0.3,0.5,0.6,0.8,0.95$; at $G=5$ and equal speeds, $0.82$ at $x_p=0.1$, $1.61=\ln 5$ at $0.2$, $3.45$ at $0.3$, infinite from $0.6$ [num: DW; Sys s2c]. A frozen share accumulates no lock-in, so this formula replaces V6.1's "$\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$ plus the time to repay" for this pause; that sentence belongs to the hold with shares moving, in S11 and Section 11.4. This result also appears as Proposition 10 of the author's paper of 28 September 2026 (LEDGER records the overlap; attribution is the author's to settle).

*What the field model cannot show.* It is cooperative, so it has no attracting cycles and describes lock-in, not reform cycles; a delayed, masked negative loop from the host to correction could produce cycles and is not modelled, and the mobilization of {{countervail}} is one such loop. *Recovering the parameters.* Integrating {{eq:field}} gives $\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{logit}x_i=\zeta_ic_i[\sum_jG_{ij}(k_i*x_j)(t)-1]$ plus a decaying transient, with kernel $k_i(s)=\varepsilon_id_ie^{-\varepsilon_id_is}$; series for the shares identify the conversions. This is recoverability, not causal identification.

### Proof of {{slowroute}}, and the two clocks

By (a) and {{load}}, on every alarm-free path the load tends to $\bar e$ and $V$ declines toward a limit below $V_{\min}$ under (reach). By (e), $m(t)>m_{\rm fl}$ throughout the band $[V_{\min},V_{\rm alarm}]$, so by {{masking}} no alarm fires there, and the host reaches $V<V_{\min}$ on an alarm-free path. $\square$ *The clock version.* By (b) and (c), {{corrrate}}(2) gives $x_{\rm ev}\to1$, so the threshold $(m_{\rm fl}-m_{\rm out})/(m_{\rm in}-m_{\rm out})<1$ is passed at a finite $T_{\rm cap}$ after which $x_{\rm ev}$ stays above it; if $T_{\rm cap}<T_A$, then (e) holds on the band, since the host enters it at $T_A$ at the earliest, and the first argument applies. $\square$ When $h_V\bar e<\rho$ the host cannot reach the alarm before $T_A=(\rho-h_V\bar e)^{-1}\ln[(1-V^\ast/V_0)/(1-V^\ast/V_{\rm alarm})]$ with $V^\ast=1-h_V\bar e/\rho$; (b) and (c) say nothing about $T_A$, which is why they do not imply (e). V6.1 listed (b) and (c) among the premises although its proof did not use them; the clock version is the statement they do support.

*Numerics (SR).* Two computations show (b) and (c) without (e). With exogenous capture: a host $\dot V=V(\rho(1-V)-h_V)$ from $V_0=1$ under the race load, $\rho=0.05$, $h_V=0.08$, floor 0.3, alarm 0.6, $m_{\rm in}=0.5$, $m_{\rm out}=0$, so $m_{\rm fl}=0.43$ and the threshold is $x_{\rm ev}>0.857$, crosses the floor at $t=21.0$; if evaluation is captured along a logistic of width 2 centred at $t=0$, the threshold is passed at $t=3.6$, no alarm ever fires and the crossing is silent; centred at $t=20$ or $t=45$, the threshold is passed at $t=23.6$ or $48.6$, and the alarm fires at $t=7.4$, before the floor, although $x_{\rm ev}\to1$ in every case [num: SR; f7(F)]. With endogenous capture in the joint model (the CS parameters, $m_{\rm in}=0.5$, floor 0.3, alarm 0.6, a race share rising from 0.05 at rate 0.12, $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0$): independent evaluation alarms at $t=32.1$ with $V=0.579$, before the floor; under QS, R(1) and R(0.1), where evaluation is captured by $t\approx23.5$, the alarm comes at $t=58.8$ with $V=0.200$, after the floor; under R(0.03) and GA(1) it comes at $t=32.1$, before it. With $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0.05$, where capture under QS arrives at $t=44.9$, every dynamics alarms at $t=32.1$, before the floor [num: SR; Sys s5 E3, s5b E3′]. So the order of the clocks turns on the rate of correction and on the adjustment speed of capacity, and acceleration of the contest moves both: (e) is checked, not derived. The V6.1 illustration with two exogenous clocks is replaced by these runs, which make its point with the same host parameters.
