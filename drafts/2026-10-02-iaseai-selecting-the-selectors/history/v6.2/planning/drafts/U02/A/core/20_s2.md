# Part I. Mechanism: what the conditions of winning do

Part I states the mechanism and proves its results in specified models; whether their conditions hold in the world is the question of Part II.

## 2. The conditions of winning and their reflexive closure

> **Claim.** Three conditions, holding together, produce a selection pressure of winning: selection by relative success; winnings that convert into the capacity to win again; and capacity that converts decisively into winning [def]. Near equal standing they combine into one structural loop gain, and where it exceeds one, leads compound [thm]. Where success can also buy control over the selection itself, the pressure closes on itself: the meta-attractor of winning [def].
>
> **Why.** The conditions multiply: remove any one and the gain is zero [thm]. Sharing and diffusion lower the gain in every channel; provision not won in the contest lowers it in channels whose decisiveness rises less than in proportion to the stock, as with capacity; correction lowers it in channels whose decisiveness rises with the stock's level, as with rule support [thm]. Correction that only speeds the decay of capacity does not lower it, because a ratio contest does not see a decay common to all [thm]. Where the discounted gain is below one, it prices a unit of relative standing in the actor's own future winnings [thm]; what standing is worth against what all share is the question of {{>standprice}}.

### 2.1 Success is not contribution

Every selection process favours something. It need not favour the continued functioning of the system in which selection occurs.

Consider two organizations that depend on the same shared infrastructure. One spends resources maintaining it. The other redirects those resources into expansion while continuing to use what others maintain. Neglect becomes a competitive advantage when maintenance costs are immediate, deterioration is delayed, and those harmed cannot make the expanding organization bear the consequences. The neglectful organization need not be more capable in general. It need only be more capable at securing the resources that decide survival and expansion. If those resources let its practices spread, the system selects against some of the behaviour that keeps it functioning. **Success within a selection process is distinct from contribution to the conditions that support it.**

Selection on one dimension does not select the best. It removes the balanced. The reason is arithmetic, not psychology: an agent that optimizes only the value that is measured and rewarded, and discards every other value, externalities included, is by construction the most efficient at that measure, because every further concern it carried could only bind. The author states the premise in those terms: "the agent that only optimiz[es] capital value, and discard[s] all other values, including externalities, will have the highest capital efficiency" [corpus: meta-attractor]. What remains after many rounds is what the filter lets through.

Nothing here requires that people be or become selfish. A population of practices changes because some practices expand, are imitated or replace others, while many individuals keep their values. The question is not only what people believe is good but which practices receive the means to continue. The primitive is selection, not greed.

### 2.2 Three interconnected conditions

**{{!Definition:cond}} (The selection pressure of winning) [def].** *Carriers* are any reproducible units that can gain or lose share: cell lineages, practices, firms, research programmes, members of a federation, states, doctrines, trained AI systems. A system of carriers is under a *selection pressure of winning* when three conditions hold together, each to a degree.
1. **Relative selection.** What is won is allocated by success relative to the others; a share $\beta$ of it is divided equally whatever the outcome, and the rest by relative success.
2. **Convertibility.** Part of what is won converts into stocks that raise future winning: capacity, talent, data, standing, rule support. Stocks also come from sources not won in the contest. This paper calls that inflow *provision not won in the contest* in its results and *provision beneath the game* in prose; the two are one quantity, and trees are its image (Section 10.1).
3. **Decisiveness.** Stocks convert into winning by an increasing success function; $r$ measures how decisively.

When among the stocks that success can buy is control over the selection itself (the rules, the evaluators and correctors, the formation of minds, the language in which success is described), the pressure closes on itself (Section 2.3). The pressure needs no intention, no conspiracy and no particular culture; it acts on whatever is reproducible and can be won.

The conditions state formally a loop the author put in one sentence: "if you use what you have to compete for more and what you win is yours, and you use that to keep compet[ing], we will end up racing faster and faster and trade everything for winning" [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. The three clauses are the three conditions; the last clause is what Section 3 derives. They combine in one quantity.

**{{!Proposition:loopgain}} (One loop gain) [thm].** The conditions multiply into a single structural loop gain. Equal standing breaks when the gain exceeds one, and its discounted form prices standing while it is below one.

*Model.* $n$ carriers contest a prize $\Omega$ each period. A share $\beta$ is divided equally; the rest goes to carrier $i$ with probability or share $p_i=e^{\sigma_i}/\sum_ke^{\sigma_k}$, where strength $\sigma_i$ increases in $i$'s stocks $X_i^j$. Stock $j$ receives a share $\kappa_j$ of what is won, an inflow $k_{0j}\ge0$ not won in the contest, decays or is corrected at rate $d_j>0$, and diffuses toward the mean at rate $\delta_{f,j}$, with $d_j+\delta_{f,j}\le1$:
$${{!eq:stocks}}\qquad X_i^{j\prime}=(1-d_j)X_i^j+\kappa_j\Omega\big[(1-\beta)p_i+\beta/n\big]+k_{0j}-\delta_{f,j}\big(X_i^j-\bar X^j\big).$$
The prize is fixed, and in (1)–(3) so is every carrier's conduct: strength depends on stocks alone. At equal standing write $r_j=\partial\sigma/\partial\ln X^j\ge0$ for the structural decisiveness of stock $j$ and $\omega_j=\kappa_j\Omega/(\kappa_j\Omega+nk_{0j})$ for the share of stock $j$ that is won. Let
$${{!eq:gamma}}\qquad \Gamma=(1-\beta)\sum_j r_j\,\omega_j\,\frac{d_j}{d_j+\delta_{f,j}}.$$
1. *Equal standing is locally asymptotically stable if $\Gamma<1$ and unstable if $\Gamma>1$. The channels add because every stock is fed by the same winnings through one win share: compounding through capacity and the reflexive closure through rule support are terms of one sum, and channels each too weak to open the loop can open it together.*
2. *$\Gamma=0$ if any condition is removed: all of the prize divided equally ($\beta=1$), nothing convertible ($\kappa_j=0$ for all $j$), or no decisiveness ($r_j=0$ for all $j$).*
3. *(The price of standing.) Hold the prize and the others' conduct fixed, and let an actor discount at $\delta$ and convert its relative net payoff into stocks at the rates $\kappa_j$. Write*
$$\Gamma_\delta=(1-\beta)\sum_jr_j\,\omega_j\,\frac{\delta d_j}{1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})},\qquad \Gamma_\delta<\Gamma,\quad \Gamma_\delta\to\Gamma\ \text{as}\ \delta\to1.$$
*If $0\le\Gamma_\delta<1$, a unit of relative net payoff today is worth, to first order, $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ units of the actor's own future winnings: the closed-loop gain of the discounted loop. The price grows without bound as $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$. At $\Gamma_\delta\ge1$ no first-order price exists: the value of standing is continuous at equal standing with infinite slope, so no finite weight on standing represents the actor there.*
4. *(Rivals who respond.) Let strength also depend on an intensity each carrier chooses, and let carriers follow a symmetric Markov equilibrium that is differentiable at equal standing and in which a carrier's equilibrium win share rises with each of its own stocks. Then (1) and (2) hold along the equilibrium path with $r_j$ replaced by the equilibrium decisiveness $r^{\rm eq}_j$, the response of the equilibrium win share to a relative shift of standing in stock $j$ with every intensity responding; and (3) holds with $r^{\rm pr}_j$, the same response with the actor's own intensity held at its current level, plus the change in shared loss that the rivals' responses bring.*

*Scope.* Local, at equal standing, for reinforcing channels: a countervailing channel is not a negative term of $\Gamma$, and with delay a balancing channel produces growing oscillation at a net gain below one ({{delayosc}}). A stock fed by an expenditure chosen under another rule, lobbying bought from profits by a separate decision, is not a term of the sum in (1) (S2). The prize is fixed; {{>standprice}} lets it grow with capacity and prices standing against what all share. The structural $\Gamma$ is a benchmark. In the contests computed a lead discourages the follower and the leader presses, so $r^{\rm eq}>r$: the equilibrium decisiveness runs from near the structural one to about five times it, equal standing is already unstable at a structural gain of 0.45, and the price at parity is up to about three times the structural price [num: MP]; where a contest runs in many small rounds, a winner-take-all equilibrium coexists with the smooth one at a structural gain of 0.2 [num: MP; held for outside review]. So a structural $\Gamma<1$ does not by itself ensure stability. Where catch-up is strong the correction runs the other way, with $r^{\rm eq}/r=0.993$ in one computed contest [num: MP; held for outside review]; where it is so strong that a carrier's win share falls with one of its own stocks, the sign pattern of (4) breaks and the equilibrium gain alone does not decide [thm]. The stability calculation of (1)–(2) is standard (X1 gives the lineage); what is new is the organization, with provision and diffusion inside the gain, and the consequence for objectives (Section 3.5). $\Gamma$ is not estimated for any channel; S2 gives illustrative ranges. *Proof:* S2.

The single-channel case gives the compounding condition in closed form, and with it the formal content of provision beneath the game.

**{{!Proposition:compound}} (When winnings compound, and what enough requires) [thm].** *Let capacity $K_i$ receive a share $\kappa_c$ of what $i$ wins, an inflow $k_0$ not won in the contest, depreciation at rate $d_K$ and diffusion at rate $\delta_f$, with $p_i=K_i^r/\sum_jK_j^r$; let $K^\ast$ and $S^\ast=nK^\ast$ be individual and total capacity at equal standing, and $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$ the share of capacity that is won.*
1. *Equal standing is locally stable if $\kappa_c\Omega\big((1-\beta)r-1\big)<nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$ and unstable if the inequality is reversed. Without provision and diffusion the condition is $(1-\beta)r\lessgtr1$, and $\kappa_c$ sets only the level of capacity. With provision, a smaller convertible share stabilizes equal standing.*
2. *A point of enough. Hold every other carrier at a cap $\bar K\le K^\ast$ at which it stops reinvesting. (i) If $\bar K<K^\ast$, a laggard just below the cap returns to it. At $\bar K=K^\ast$ it returns if $(1-\beta)r\omega\,\frac{n-1}{n}<1+\frac{\delta_f(n-1)}{nd_K}$ and is excluded if the inequality is reversed; without diffusion the condition is $\Gamma<n/(n-1)$. (ii) With the others at the cap, a laggard at $K<\bar K$ has net inflow*
$$\phi(K)=\kappa_c\Omega\Big[(1-\beta)\frac{K^r}{K^r+(n-1)\bar K^r}+\frac\beta n\Big]+k_0-d_KK+\delta_f\,\frac{n-1}n\,(\bar K-K),$$
*and it returns to the cap from every start iff $\phi>0$ on $(0,\bar K)$. When $r>1$ the contest term vanishes faster than $K$ near zero, so $\phi(0^+)=\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0+\delta_f(n-1)\bar K/n$: recovery from every start needs an inflow not tied to rank, from sharing, provision or diffusion, and $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0\ge d_K\bar K$ suffices. Without all three, laggards below a threshold are excluded.*

*Scope.* Fixed prize; with total capacity growing and no depreciation, the condition of (1) holds date by date with the current total, and is constant exactly when the stakes are proportional to capacity, where it is also the condition under which {{>standprice}} narrows the working objective (S2). The others hold the cap by their own inflow when $\bar K\le(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)/(nd_K+\delta_f)$; above that bound their diffusion loss to the laggard enters, and the joint dynamics restore the cap in every case checked [num: CE]. Equality in (1) is a neutral linearization and is not decided. *Proof:* S2.

Each clause of the author's sentence names a lever, and {{compound}} says what each needs. Share what is won until $(1-\beta)r\le1$. Make winnings non-convertible, which works only together with provision not won in the contest, since without either there is no capacity at all. Stop at a point of enough, which holds from every start only with an inflow not tied to rank: sharing, provision or diffusion, and diffusion is a source, not a guarantee [num: CE]. **Provision beneath the game is not only support for those who fall behind. It is what makes non-convertible rewards work and what makes a point of enough hold**, and knowledge that flows is the third such inflow, which is why openness of what is not decisive restores standing as well as eroding leads (Section 10.2). These levers act through capacity. Faster decay of capacity does not lower the gain, because a contest of ratios does not see a decay common to all, the first instance of what {{>blind}} says of every common term; the reflexive channel is answered by correction and by closing the conversion of success into control over selection ({{chansign}}; Section 4). The rule the author drew from game design acts on convertibility: "a winner of the game should be rewarded with anything other than more advantages of the game, otherwise the game will eventually converge and winner will take all. Then the game collapses" [corpus: keep-the-possibility-space-open].

**What the loop gain does not decide.** $\Gamma$ sets whether a relative gap grows, not how fast: the speed is the Perron root $\Lambda^\ast$ of the linearized loop, $1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$ for one channel, so the same gain on a slowly turning stock opens leads slowly ({{tdec}}). Two further conditions decide what the loop does to those who live with it, and neither is a function of $\Gamma$. The first is whether a position beyond any sanction can be reached: whether a lead can become decisive, and how fast against detection (Section 5.2). The second is whether capability grows faster than the correction that can contest it (Section 5.3). Among three or more actors, what relative accounting does to talk is not a function of $\Gamma$ either (Section 5.4). The core of the pressure is one structural loop gain; the pressure as a whole is a set of interconnected conditions, linked by a few shared parameters and by one shared state, the concentration of standing. {{>thresholds}} sets out the links.

### 2.3 The reflexive closure: the meta-attractor

An ordinary adaptive system improves its state $x$ against a fixed rule $\mathcal S$ that scores states. The pressure closes on itself when the rule is endogenous: when what can be won includes influence over the rules, the evaluators and correctors, the formation of the minds that will hold positions, and the language in which success is described. The outcome of the last round of scoring then decides who writes the next round's scorecard.

Consider a research community that allocates funding and positions by publication counts, because reliable knowledge is hard to evaluate directly. Practices that produce more publications receive more funding, attract more students and are more widely copied. Now add one step: their practitioners come to occupy the positions that decide recruitment, training and evaluation. The practices the metric favours begin to shape the institutions that choose and interpret the metric, and newcomers must adopt them to obtain the resources needed to do research at all. The decisive transition is from *being selected by a rule* to *helping reproduce the rule that selects you*. It needs no manipulation; ordinary decisions about whom to hire, what to teach and which evidence to collect suffice.

The loop need not preserve its winners. An organization can fail while its operating logic passes to its successors; a leader can be replaced by an opponent who finds that holding the position requires the same practices; participants can reject the system's consequences while reproducing the conduct their participation depends on. **The persistent entity can be a way of succeeding.** Structural explanation does not erase responsibility, which differs with knowledge, discretion and power. It explains why intentions alone cannot account for recurrence.

**{{!Definition:meta}} (Meta-attractor) [def].** Let the state update be $x'=\mathcal F(x,\mathcal S)$ and the rule update $\mathcal S'=\mathcal G(x,\mathcal S)$. Three objects are distinguished, and each claim in this paper says which it concerns.
1. **The mechanism: reflexive selection.** A positive loop runs through the rule: the rule moves resources toward some states, and the states so resourced move the rule further in their favour. In {{loopgain}} it is every channel whose stock is control over selection.
2. **An attracting configuration in a specified model:** a compact invariant set of the joint flow, asymptotically stable, with a basin that is a proper subset of the space. A positive loop is necessary for multistability (Thomas's rule; Soulé 2003), not sufficient. The field model of Section 4 exhibits such configurations.
3. **A long transient:** trajectories stay near a region for a long time relative to one round of selection, without the region being attracting.

A *meta-attractor* is the mechanism of (1). Wherever this paper calls it attracting, the claim is (2), in the model where it has been shown; a claim of (2) about the world is a conjecture.

**The meta-attractor of winning** is the case in which the pressure of {{cond}} is the rule: success under relative selection converts into control over the selection that judges the next round. The conditional form keeps its conditions in the sentence: *recursive selection creates second-order selection for control over future selection when the resulting benefits are sufficiently appropriable, persistent and large relative to the costs of changing and defending the rules.* Where (2) holds, long-run behaviour maximizes the rule whatever purpose it was introduced to serve (Goodhart 1975; Campbell 1979), and by the time a measure has stopped measuring, the people and institutions it elevated decide whether it is replaced.

The smallest model of (1) is a pair in deviations, an actor's success and its favourable control over the rules, each feeding the other and each decaying; it is stable exactly when the product of the two cross-gains is below the product of the two decay rates ({{bench}}), Richardson's arms-race condition with control over rules in place of armaments. It is of the same form as {{loopgain}} and not the same theorem; its use is that each coefficient names a channel that can be measured, and neither its instability nor its stability by itself proves capture or safety.

The pattern is not fated. Positive feedback can saturate, oscillate, split or break; energetic ceilings, heterogeneity, delays, constraints at other scales and reflexive awareness cap it. It is "more like a hurricane than a black hole" [corpus: meta-attractor]: a structure sustained by a flow, which decays when the flow is cut.

*Why "meta-attractor".* The name is kept over "generator functions" of catastrophic risk, which name causes without the reflexive step in which selection rewrites its own criterion, without persistence, without the layer of minds and language, and without a generative counterpart. One concept covers the mechanism and its generative dual, and every claim says which of the three senses it uses.

**{{!Remark:pathgen}} (Pathological and generative meta-attractors).** Reflexive selection is not itself the problem: science, the rule of law and open-source ecosystems also persist through the conditions they maintain. A meta-attractor is *pathological* to the degree that its reproduction depletes shared conditions that selection does not price, weakens the channels through which such costs register and trigger correction, or makes safe alternatives unreachable within the relevant time, budget and authority. It is *generative* to the degree that its reproduction strengthens independent correction and keeps alternatives reachable: what is rewarded is contribution to the shared conditions, and success funds correctors it cannot control. That last flow is not selected by any carrier for itself, since correction keyed to no one confers no relative advantage ({{>noinside}}); it is held by the whole, through coverage and formula funding (Section 10.4). The way out is therefore not the abolition of selection but a change in what is selected for (Section 10): "change the basin, then add speed" [corpus: meta-attractor].

### 2.4 Not bound to any substrate

The pressure needs only relative selection, convertible winnings and decisiveness; its closure needs only that the successful can reshape their selection. A cancer lineage evades the signals that limit proliferation, recruits surrounding tissue and evades the immune response that would remove it (Hanahan and Weinberg 2011; Aktipis et al. 2015). Markets with increasing returns lock in early leads (Arthur 1989), and the successful can buy the rules of the game (Stigler 1971). In security competition a gain for one is read as a threat to another. Members of a federation compete for capital and residents, and the strongest can capture the centre. In science, metrics allocate positions and the positions then set the metrics. In training, a policy is selected by a reward that a developer sets, and the developer is selected by a contest. Nothing in {{cond}} refers to a culture, a political system or a species: a civilization on another planet would face the same pressure wherever selection is relative, winnings convert and the successful can edit the selection. Discussion X2 gives the instances in full, and X1 the lineage of the ideas.

What is specific to AI is not the pressure but how far AI moves its parameters. It raises decisiveness and the speed at which leads open. Where it lowers the cost of control faster than the cost of independent correction, it strengthens the reflexive channels. It raises the growth of hazardous behaviour relative to contestable coverage. And it can become a carrier itself (Section 6).

A structural loop gain says when leads compound and, discounted, what a unit of standing is worth in future winnings; it does not say what those inside the loop can see, and Section 3 asks what relative selection makes invisible and what looking ahead does to the objective.
