## 5. Acceleration and the three erosions

> **Claim.** The conditions of winning accelerate the contest and erode the three means of restraining a harmful pressure: enforcement, correction and shared understanding [thm]. Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold, deviation by deviation, and success lowers the threshold [thm]. Where leads compound, a larger share of capacity that has to be won raises that chance; where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, the same share narrows the working objectives that set the threshold; captured correctors and outgrown stakes lower it further, each arm in its own model [thm], and in one reduced model the two arms on that pivot close together [num]. Where contestable coverage falls ever further behind hazardous variety and failure is shared, survival tends to zero whoever is racing [thm∣prem]. Between two rivals relative accounting silences talk; among three or more it amplifies their conflict by $n/(n-2)$ [thm]. That the arms close together in the world is conjecture C0 [conj].
>
> **Why.** Decisiveness takes away the future that enforcement needs, tempo and capture the time and independence that correction needs, relative accounting the information that talk carries, and each erosion feeds the others [thm]. An objective not satiated in the stakes has no point of enough [thm]: growth is not the danger; the mind is.

### 5.1 Acceleration

Where actors contest capability directly, who prevails is decided by the contest of Section 3.4. The case for racing rests on three separate premises: **(decisive)**, $r$ is large; **(fast)**, leads open within short windows; **(unsaturated)**, no level of advantage makes more worthless; a technology can be decisive at a threshold and saturate above it (Section 7.2). Write $R_i$ for an actor's payoff when all restrain, $T_i$ when it races against restrainers, $P_i$ when all race and $S_i$ when it restrains against a racer. In the objective an actor acts on, restraint is never a best response to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$, which in the contest holds for $r<n/(n-1)$, given, among more than two, that restraining against one racer pays less than racing beside it; racing need not then be better in material terms, and where falling behind costs something beyond losing the prize, the contest is for the prize plus that penalty, which in the fully dissipative limit buys the joiner nothing over staying out. The race begins if any single actor has $T_i>R_i$: where those events are independent, its probability tends to one as capable actors multiply, exactly when the sum of their probabilities diverges (**{{!Proposition:join}}**, S5) [thm]. The race is set by the most race-inclined actor, which is why any remedy must cover every capable actor.

**{{!Proposition:dissipate}} (Dissipation) [thm].** The more decisive the advantage, the more of its value is consumed in the race to obtain it.
1. *Under a common self-model $\theta$, the share of the stakes dissipated in private cost and shared loss is $s_{\rm dis}(\theta)=r(n-1-\theta)(c+n\ell)/\big(n(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, increasing in $r$ and $\ell$.*
2. *If the technology's power scales the stakes and the shared loss per unit together while private cost is unchanged [prem], the dissipated share rises toward $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$ for $-1<\theta<n-1$, without bound at $\theta=-1$. Total material payoff stays positive at every scale iff $\theta\ge\theta_g=(r(n-1)-1)/(1+r)$, which rises with $r$.*

*Proof:* S5, with the ceilings at four self-models [num: legacy v5].

What is spent is safety margin, accepted risk and delegated control. With $n=4$ and $r=1$, an accurate weight $\kappa=0.4$ toward what can be divided, the value used in {{twoerrors}}, lies below $\theta_g=1$: a gap between what accuracy supplies and what the contest requires, which only institutions, or coupling that grows with power, can close (crux C11).

**{{!Remark:frontier}} (The frontier switch: what cancer does when the body runs out) [thm].** *Let capacity expand into an unclaimed frontier, destroy rivals' capacity, or rest. At $\theta=-1$ the host is invisible, and expansion gives way to mutual destruction before the frontier is exhausted, once the frontier's marginal value, net of the cost of expanding, falls below the net gain in standing from destroying a rival; at any $\theta>-1$ expansion stops at a point of enough, and war among copies never pays wherever destruction's private cost exceeds what the objective held gains from a rival's loss, which holds for every $\theta$ above a bound below zero.* S5 states the conditions exactly, with a toy of four copies and the one finding in it that runs against this narrative [num: FS].

Among actors whose accounting is relative, peace is therefore a peace of abundance, not of accounting. Expansion is an anaesthetic for the winning model. Winning does not end the game: the logic itself is the replicator, a winner is a field in which the same selection continues ({{>raceselects}}), and a self-replicating economy that carries it out into new frontiers carries the same switch with it [corpus: meta-attractor].

### 5.2 First erosion: enforcement

Cooperation among rivals is usually sustained by the future: restraint today is rewarded by restraint tomorrow. Decisiveness weakens the future. Consider a stage game repeated with discount $\delta\in(0,1)$ under perfect monitoring, each actor acting on $J_i$. Let $a^\ast$ be a profile with per-period value $C_i$. For a one-period deviation $a$ by $i$, let $g_i(a)$ be its one-period gain and $p_d(a)$ the probability that it yields, from the next period, a *decisive position*: an absorbing state beyond any punishment, worth $D_i(a)$ per period. Let $v_i$ be $(1-\delta)$ times $i$'s continuation value under the harshest credible punishment, computed in the same game, in which breakouts remain possible during punishment.

**{{!Proposition:enforce}} (The enforcement condition) [thm].** Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold, deviation by deviation, and success lowers the threshold.
1. *$a^\ast$ is sustained by reversion to that punishment if and only if, for every $i$ and every one-period deviation $a$,*
$${{!eq:enforceeq}}\qquad (1-\delta)g_i(a)+\delta p_d(a)\big(D_i(a)-C_i\big)\;\le\;\delta\big(1-p_d(a)\big)(C_i-v_i).$$
*The left side is the temptation, immediate and decisive; the right side is the sanction that remains. The deviation with the largest gain need not bind: a quiet one, with a small gain and a high chance of breakout, can.*
2. *Equivalently, a deviation with $D_i(a)>v_i$ is deterred iff $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)=1-\big[(1-\delta)g_i(a)+\delta(D_i(a)-C_i)\big]/\big[\delta(D_i(a)-v_i)\big]$. Wherever it lies in $[0,1)$, $\bar p_{d,i}$ falls with $g_i$, $D_i$ and $v_i$ and rises with $C_i$, weakly in $D_i$ where $\bar p_{d,i}=0$; it is homogeneous of degree zero in $(g_i,C_i,D_i,v_i)$, so stakes that all grow alike leave it unchanged.*
3. *The bound without the breakout term, $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i)$, is exact when $D_i=C_i$, necessary when $D_i\ge C_i$, and sufficient when $D_i\le C_i$.*
4. *As $p_d\to1$ the condition becomes $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$. If $D_i\ge C_i$, sustainable profiles lie within the stage equilibria of the objectives held, strictly within when $D_i>C_i$: restraint must be preferred even to a breakout that succeeds. If $D_i<C_i$, cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium can survive $p_d=1$.*
5. *Let punishment be reversion to a stage equilibrium in which both of two actors race, worth $P_i$, and let at most one racer break out in a period, each with probability $q_b\le\frac12$; when the other breaks out, $i$ is subordinated, worth $V^{\rm sub}_i$ per period. Then $v_i=\big[(1-\delta)P_i+\delta q_b(D_i+V^{\rm sub}_i)\big]/(1-\delta+2\delta q_b)$; if instead breakouts are independent and a simultaneous breakout leaves the race as it was, $q_b$ is replaced by $q_b(1-q_b)$. When $D_i+V^{\rm sub}_i>2P_i$, reversion to the race is a lottery the deviator welcomes, and it can fail to deter while still leaving the deviator below cooperation, $P_i<v_i<C_i$, even where the deviation itself never breaks out; S5 gives an instance that fails over a window of $q_b$ [num: ES].*

*Scope.* Perfect monitoring and an absorbing decisive position; a slow breakout enters through $p_d$, the chance that it becomes decisive before punishment can act ({{>detectlink}}). Part (1) decides whether $a^\ast$ is the path of some subgame-perfect equilibrium only with $v_i$ the least subgame-perfect continuation value, an extension of optimal penal codes (Abreu 1988) to this stochastic game that is asserted for finite stage games with discounting and flagged for outside review. Part (5) gives the credible punishment value only where racing on, not quitting the race, is the punisher's best response. *Proof:* S5, with a valuation check of part (1) and the deviator's best response over all deviation plans, which agrees with {{eq:enforceeq}} wherever the punishment is credible [identity: EC; num: ES].

The folk theorem's promise depends on punishment remaining possible; a decisive position weakens it, and a punishment that is itself a race weakens it again. Which deviation binds depends on both terms of the temptation, and what binds is the quiet deviation, the kind that masking hides (Section 4.4): in S5's instance the deviation with the larger gain is deterred and the quieter one with the higher chance of breakout is not [num: ES].

**{{!Remark:detectlink}} (Timely detection: enforcement seen from verification) [thm].** *Let punishment be triggered by detection and act before decisiveness, let $\upsilon(T)$ be the probability that a deviation is detected and answered within $T$ periods, and let a breakout need $T_{\rm dec}$ periods to become decisive. Then $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, and for a deviation with $D_i>v_i$, {{eq:enforceeq}} reads $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d,i}$: timely detection must exceed a level that rises with the value of winning. With detection and response on a memoryless clock of mean $\tau_{\rm det}$, $p_d=e^{-T_{\rm dec}/\tau_{\rm det}}$. With the payoffs of {{>assure}} read as continuation values, cooperation $R=C_i$, a deviation that becomes decisive $T=(1-\delta)(C_i+g_i)+\delta D_i$ and one detected and answered $P-F=(1-\delta)(C_i+g_i)+\delta v_i$, its verification threshold $(T-R)/(T-P+F)$ is exactly $1-\bar p_{d,i}$, and its case $T<R$, cooperation without verification, is $\bar p_{d,i}>1$.*

*Scope.* The two conditions differ in what they take as given: the verification threshold takes the sanction and trust as given, {{eq:enforceeq}} a credible continuation computed in a game in which breakouts remain possible (S5). *Proof:* S5 [identity: JX; num: JX].

Both sides of {{eq:enforceeq}} are thereby tied to what can be measured. Both $T_{\rm dec}$, which a structural loop gain above one shortens ({{tdec}}), and $\upsilon$ are what masking hides ({{alarm}}), so an estimate of either that is to trigger a response has to come from correctors whose independence is certified (Section 12.1).

**{{!Corollary:relpunish}} (Relative objectives and punishments that can be mirrored) [thm].** *Let the stage game be symmetric, every actor hold $\theta=-1$, and the punishment phase be exchangeable: its payoffs and transitions are invariant under relabelling the actors, and it starts from a state that treats the punished and the punishers alike, though strategies may condition on who deviated. Every symmetric profile then gives each 0. With two actors every punishment, mixed or pure, leaves the punished at least 0, since it can mirror the punisher; with $n\ge3$ in an anonymous game, so does every punishment in which all punishers use the same strategy. A symmetric cooperative profile is then sustainable only if $(1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_dD_i\le0$, and when $D_i\ge0$ only if it is a stage equilibrium in objectives.*

*Scope.* A decisive position reached through an exchangeable lottery is covered. Two things escape the bound: a continuation that carries a label, an absorbing loss that can strike only the target; and, with $n\ge3$, punishers in different roles, a punishment that is weak as printed, strict under small asymmetries between the roles, and needs the roles coordinated, a problem of equilibrium selection (S5) [num: ES; CR]. *Proof:* S5.

Among relative actors in a symmetric game, then, cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium rests on punishments that break the symmetry [thm]. That such actors enforce it as blocs is a reading, not a result [conj]: the enforcement face of {{club}}.

**{{!Corollary:restraint}} (What restraint needs where breakouts are possible) [thm].** *Where $p_d$ is near zero, because no deviation yields a position beyond punishment or breakouts are answered before they become decisive, restraint can be enforced by repetition among actors with $\theta>-1$, given monitoring, $C_i>v_i$, patience, and a punishment that does not itself raise the chance of breakout. Restraint survives every value of $p_d$ iff {{eq:enforceeq}} holds at $p_d=0$ and at $p_d=1$; where $D_i\ge v_i$ this is $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$ for every $i$ and every deviation, which for a deviation with a positive gain needs $D_i<C_i$. Otherwise, where $D_i>v_i$, durable restraint requires $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)$ for every $i$ and every deviation, by two kinds of means: (a) changing the premise itself, so that deviations do not become decisive: not building capability of the decisive class, holding it so that no party can use it alone, or detecting and answering faster than breakout; (b) raising $\bar p_d$: objectives under which a decisive position is worth no more than restraint, sanctions that survive success, and lower stakes in deviation.*

*Scope.* A classification of the terms of {{eq:enforceeq}}, not a proof that no other means exists. A decisive position worth less than cooperation does not by itself exempt: with $D_i<C_i$ but $(1-\delta)g_i>\delta(C_i-D_i)$, restraint still fails above $\bar p_{d,i}$ (S5 gives an instance) [num: ES]. *Proof:* S5.

**{{!Proposition:wingives}} (What a decisive winner gives) [thm].** After a decisive win, what everyone else receives is set by the winner's objective alone. *A winner holding $W$ decides, unconstrained, what share to give the $n-1$ others. With linear payoffs it gives nothing unless $\theta=n-1$, where it is indifferent; with logarithmic utility it gives $\theta/(1+\theta)$ for $\theta\ge0$; and a promise made before the win to give a fraction $\beta$ of a prize $\Omega$ to the others is worth breaking by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$, with no one to sanction it.*

*Proof:* S5 [num: WG, SM].

This is the formal content of Ψ2, "the winner takes all, and the winner justifies all" ({{psi}}), exact for linear payoffs. Its strongest answer is a winner that still needs the governed: a winner whose self-model is accurate toward what can be divided, $\theta=\kappa_W$ ({{boundary}}(2)), gives $\kappa_W/(1+\kappa_W)$ under logarithmic utility [thm]. What the race cannot verify beforehand is $\kappa_W$ after victory, the winner's dependence on the governed once it has won, which AI can lower by substituting for their work and their assent: whether it does is crux C13 (Section 12.3), and Section 8.3 meets the benign winner at its strongest. Lowering decisiveness does not remove the problem if the prize is decisive power, since a lottery can award a position beyond sanction; what has to be limited is what winning confers (Section 10.2).

Decisiveness also breaks bargaining. Where one side's advantage is about to arrive and the side that gains it will set the terms (A8, S0), the other has a rational motive for preventive attack whenever its present chance of prevailing, less the cost of fighting, exceeds the value of peace on those terms; a shift spread over enough periods removes the motive (**{{!Proposition:preventattack}}**, S5; Fearon 1995; Powell 2006) [thm∣prem]. What drives it is not cheap war but the incredibility of future promises, and where leads compound, the loop is what brings the shift. Self-help under anarchy is an institution, not a necessity (Wendt 1992); the enforcement condition says which of its terms an institution would have to change.

### 5.3 Second erosion: correction, and the two channels of risk

Correction must see a harm, decide and act before the harm is irreversible. Section 4 showed how correctors become something success can buy; this subsection states the tempo and separates two channels of irreversible risk.

**The race channel.** Play the contest of Section 3.4 in rounds at its symmetric stage equilibrium, with stakes $\Omega_t$, decisiveness $r_t$, shared loss per unit $\ell_t$, net marginal cost $c_t$ and self-model $\theta_t$, under **(hazard–damage link)** [prem]: the hazard of the irreversible event in round $t$ is proportional to the shared damage, $h_t=a\ell_t\mathcal E_t$.

**{{!Proposition:racehazard}} (Race-generated hazard and its four levers) [thm∣prem].** *Under the link, $h_t=a\,r_t\Omega_t\,\ell_t\,(n-1-\theta_t)/\big(n(c_t+(1+\theta_t)\ell_t)\big)$, and survival on this channel, $\exp(-\sum_{t\le N}h_t)$, stays positive iff $\sum_th_t<\infty$. Four levers act on $h_t$: the objective, through $n-1-\theta_t$; the contested stakes, which sharing a fraction $\beta$ scales by $1-\beta$; the residual harm per unit $\ell_t$, which correction lowers; and the net marginal cost, which a levy raises. At $\theta_t=-1$ everything rests on correction and cost. A hazard that settles at a positive constant gives zero limiting survival.*

*Scope.* A decomposition under a premise, not a necessity theorem. *Proof:* S5 [identity: RH].

Continuation acts on one lever, the residual harm per unit, while the stakes grow; a change of selection acts on all four. In general, for one irreversible event, survival tends to zero if on every surviving history the residual probability is at least a non-summable sequence, and stays bounded away from zero if it is at most a summable one, without independence between rounds (**{{!Lemma:nonsum}}**, S5) [thm]. **The objective of winning does not by itself prove that a residual danger is non-summable**, and cooperative alternatives are judged by the same lemma.

**{{!Proposition:tempo}} (The tempo bound) [thm].** When the situation changes faster than correction can follow, correction first loses robustness, then fails. *For a hazard mode $\dot y=ay+u(t-\tau_c)$: proportional correction cannot stabilize it when $a\tau_c\ge1$ (Hayes 1950); every linear time-invariant correction that stabilizes it has peak complementary sensitivity at least $e^{a\tau_c}$, so robust stability against weighted model error needs $|w(a)|e^{a\tau_c}<1$; and for hazard classes whose evidence arrives after a manifestation window $\tau_e$ that no testing shortens, $\tau_c\ge\tau_e$.*

*Scope.* Derivative action extends the first bound, which belongs to proportional correction only (S5). *Proof:* S5.

For classes with an irreducible lag only slowing helps.

**The growth channel.** In a defined hazard class, three quantities are kept apart: the variety $Y_t$ of hazardous behaviour, counted as predefined hazard modes; its coverage $U_t$, the number of those modes under correction that meets four conditions at a stated catch rate; and the residual probability of an irreversible loss after the correction in place. A correction decision meets the four conditions if it is **legible** (a reason people can check within a budget without relying on the evaluated system's lineage), **overrulable** (a designated human body can reverse it in time), **exercised** (planted errors are caught at the stated rate) and **resourced** (human review covers a stated share of consequential decisions). Three premises connect the three quantities on a continuation branch, and Section 12 treats them as the central crux. **(coverage lags)** [prem]: $U_t/Y_t\to0$ uniformly over surviving histories. What makes it expected is **(variety grows)**: hazardous variety grows with compounding capability, at least exponentially, while consequences take their time and institutions deliberate at human rates; capability growth is measured, the step from capability to hazardous variety is not (Section 7.3) [obs: EV5]. It is a motivation, not a premise of the theorem, and it can fail: if AI raises the productivity of contestable understanding exponentially, coverage grows exponentially too and AI-assisted correction can keep pace, a question symmetric with {{corrrate}} and measurable (S5). **(residual floor)** [prem]: after the correction in place, the conditional probability of the irreversible event is at least $h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$; a correction architecture that lowers residual risk below this refutes it. **(shared failure)** [prem]: failure that starts with one actor reaches the others, so each actor's survival depends on the uncovered share of all.

**{{!Theorem:coverage}} (Coverage mismatch on a continuation branch) [thm∣prem].** Where contestable coverage falls ever further behind hazardous variety and failure is shared, survival tends to zero whoever is racing. *Under (coverage lags) and (residual floor), for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is a $T_\varepsilon$ with $\Pr(\Phi_H)\le(1-h(1-\varepsilon))^{H-T_\varepsilon}\to0$. Conversely, if the residual probability is at most $\bar hu_t$ with $\bar h<1$ and the uncovered share $u_t=(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$ is summable, survival stays bounded away from zero.*

*Scope.* Under (shared failure) the bound is every actor's, whoever is racing; without it, it is the racer's own. Summability does not require that coverage ever catch up; finite sprints, saturation, freezing and rollback change the premises. *Proof:* S5.

This growth channel does not vanish when racing stops. A world that has left the race still needs capability to grow no faster than contestable correction can follow, so that the uncovered share stays summable: call that **E3**, developing at the pace of understanding. Call **E2** stopping: not building capability of the decisive class, with rollback where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage. They are different arrangements (Section 11.3): stopping is not slowing. Divergences that hide under evaluation give both their strongest reason from inside alignment ({{>masked}}). Where hazardous variety is frozen after a sprint while coverage keeps growing, the exposure is finite, and rolled back below coverage it ends; so a winner that freezes or rolls back keeps positive survival, and what it gives up is permanent superiority, which is not the same thing (**{{!Proposition:freeze}}**, S5) [thm].

**{{!Proposition:enough}} (A point of enough) [thm].** Growth that meets satiable needs has a point of enough; objectives that are not satiated have none. *Let the residual hazard on the growth channel be at most $\bar h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$. If hazardous variety stops growing at a level set by need while coverage keeps growing at least linearly, the uncovered share reaches zero in finite time and survival on this channel has a positive lower bound. An objective not satiated in the stakes, whether relative position or an absolute payoff linear in them, has no point of enough: intensity is proportional to the stakes for every $\theta<n-1$, because the success function is homogeneous of degree zero, while an objective bounded in the stakes is satiated.*

*Scope.* Under its premises {{coverage}} applies to any continuation branch whatever the objective; the relative objective adds the invisibility of the shared loss. *Proof:* S5, with the frozen and the compounding case in numbers [num: PE].

The proposition separates what grows from what decides whether growth stops where need is met: an objective not satiated in the stakes, relative standing above all, has no point of enough, since standing relative to others is never complete [thm]. In that precise sense, **growth is not the danger; the mind is** [corpus: meta-attractor; Ren, 2026-09-25]. The author's book puts it in one line, 癌亦增长，而其长竭母体之生: cancer grows too, but its growth exhausts the life of its host [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14]. The rule has one boundary: hazardous variety that outgrows contestable coverage is dangerous under any mind, which is why pace binds even a generative order [thm∣prem]. Because compounding also prices standing wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return ({{standprice}}), the actors who would need a point of enough are pushed away from it by the loop. Deliberately bounded hazardous scope, with correction that keeps growing, is a sufficient route to E3; its connection to purpose is a design hypothesis. Section 6.4 states the same rule for the minds that training produces.

### 5.4 Third erosion: shared understanding

Coordination needs the parties to see the same danger and believe one another about it, and contest changes what talk is for. Ψ named the two logics ({{psi}}): only cooperation reasons, and struggle argues to win. This subsection gives that line its content in information, by asking what relative accounting does to talk.

**{{!Proposition:talk}} (Talk under relative accounting) [thm].** Relative accounting amplifies the conflict between a sender and a receiver: between two rivals it silences talk, and among three or more it multiplies their conflict by $n/(n-2)$, a factor that falls toward one as actors multiply.
1. *Let all $n$ actors hold a common $\theta\in[-1,n-1]$ with $n-1+\theta>0$, let a sender and a receiver play the uniform-quadratic game with a material bias $b_0$ between them, and let the receiver's action affect third parties linearly. What it does to third parties then enters both objectives alike: their ideal actions differ by the effective bias $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$, and informative talk exists iff it is below $\frac14$.*
2. *At $\theta=-1$ with $n\ge3$ the effective bias is $b_0n/(n-2)$. With $n=2$ it is $b_0(1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$, without bound as $\theta\to-1$; at $\theta=-1$ two actors' objectives sum to zero at every state and action, and in any such game every equilibrium is payoff-equivalent to one without talk (compare Crawford and Sobel 1982), whatever the material bias.*
3. *Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because it is shared: a sender gains from the receiver's restraint whatever it intends itself.*

*Scope.* Cheap talk; costly signals and verification are outside it. Linear third-party payoffs are not load-bearing: quadratic ones leave the effective bias unchanged (S5) [num: TK]. *Proof:* S5 [num: TK, T3].

A narrower self-model drains talk continuously, so the loop that narrows working objectives ({{standprice}}) drains talk with them [thm]. The amplification fades as actors multiply, as the relative-gains literature found for cooperation (Snidal 1991). Rivals stop informing one another; that pairs who still talk align as blocs against third parties is a reading, not a result [conj]: the language face of {{club}}.

**Measures** become targets and stop measuring (Goodhart 1975; inside training, Gao et al. 2023) [obs]. **Narratives** are carriers too [conj]: the frames that spread are selected for fitness to the contest; a group that totalizes itself shapes those around it as a model generates tokens, and a winning frame shapes the vocabulary of the next round until its words escape the game that coined them and a frame read as nature is no longer contested [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. By the time the frame is noticed one is already thinking inside it; the same dilemma named a "Wall Street Game" or a "Community Game" produced very different cooperation (Liberman, Samuels and Ross 2004) [obs]. **Professed doctrine** is a further layer: people say what is rewarded rather than what they believe, so a public consensus can persist that few privately hold, and then tip (Kuran 1995) [obs]. A race doctrine professed by actors selected for professing it can then be held less widely than it is stated, and visible cooperation can tip it [conj]. **Self-confirmation:** where a cooperative equilibrium exists, the belief that restraint will be exploited can persist because no one tests it (Fudenberg and Levine 1993); in a one-shot game in which racing is dominant, it is simply correct.

A system can retain abundant information while losing the ability to let it change course. So a prediction of shared danger has to be carried by derivations from public premises, verifiable evidence and protocols agreed in advance, and a prediction so carried can work against its own fulfilment (Section 11.2); "we are one" is first a matter of understanding built in a medium the pressure keeps degrading.

### 5.5 The scissors

Restraint is enforceable iff $p_d\le\bar p_d$, deviation by deviation. Under the conditions of {{cond}}, growth in the stakes relative to provision not won in the contest moves both sides of {{eq:enforceeq}} at once, while tempo outruns correction (Section 5.3) and narrowing objectives drain talk (Section 5.4).

**{{!Proposition:scissors}} (The scissors, arm by arm) [thm].** Growth in the share of capacity that has to be won moves both sides of {{eq:enforceeq}} toward failure: the breakout probability where leads compound, its bound wherever the working objective narrows; captured correctors and outgrown stakes lower the bound further. *Let breakouts be detected and answered on a clock $\upsilon$ ({{detectlink}}), let $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$ be the won share of capacity, the stakes set against provision beneath the game ({{compound}}), and hold the rivals' conduct fixed.*
1. *Breakout arm. Where $\Gamma>1$ and a decisive gap $\bar\Delta$ is reachable, a lead grows from $\varepsilon_0$ to $\bar\Delta$ in about $T_{\rm dec}=\ln(\bar\Delta/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\Lambda^\ast$ periods in the linearized loop, with $\Lambda^\ast-1=(1-\beta)r\omega d_K-d_K-\delta_f$ in one channel ({{tdec}}). A larger $\omega$ raises $\Lambda^\ast$, shortens $T_{\rm dec}$ and so raises $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, strictly wherever $\upsilon$ is increasing; with several channels the Perron root rises with each channel's won share.*
2. *Objective arm. Let the stage game and $a^\ast$ be symmetric, let a deviation raise the deviator above the mean payoff while lowering the mean, let punishment revert, without breakouts, to a symmetric profile whose mean payoff is below cooperation's, and let $D_i-C_i$ not fall as the objective narrows. Then, where $D_i>v_i$ and $\bar p_{d,i}\in[0,1]$, a narrower working objective lowers $\bar p_{d,i}$: by {{eq:twochaneq}} it raises the deviation's gain in objectives and shrinks the sanction $C_i-v_i=(1+\theta)(\bar\pi_C-\bar\pi_P)$. To first order, with a fixed prize, the working objective narrows as $\Gamma_\delta$ rises, and in one channel $\Gamma_\delta$ is proportional to $\omega$; with a prize that grows with capacity, it narrows exactly where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return ({{standprice}}).*
3. *Sanction arm. $\bar p_{d,i}$ falls as $v_i$ rises, wherever it is below one; correctors whose capture weakens the sanction raise $v_i$, and a falling cost of control speeds that capture ({{corrrate}}).*
4. *Stakes arm. Stakes that scale $g_i$, $C_i$, $D_i$ and $v_i$ alike leave $\bar p_{d,i}$ unchanged ({{enforce}}(2)). Where $(1-\delta)g_i$ and $D_i-C_i$ grow by a common factor relative to $C_i-v_i$, it falls, wherever $C_i>v_i$ and $\bar p_{d,i}<1$.*

*The pivot: in one channel $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma_\delta$ are both proportional to $\omega$, and with several both rise with each channel's won share, so the breakout and objective arms are comparative statics of one quantity. Stakes that outgrow provision beneath the game move both blades toward closing; provision that grows with the stakes holds both still.*

*Scope.* Each arm holds in its own model, as stated; with diffusion a gap can settle short of decisive, and the first arm is then silent [num: TD]. Where rivals respond, the first arm runs on the equilibrium gain of {{loopgain}}(4), which exceeds the structural gain where a lead discourages the follower and can fall below it where a laggard tries harder ({{eqgain}}). That the arms move together in the world is conjecture C0, which Test E examines (Section 7.4) [conj]. In the binary game of {{zonesillus}} at $D=C$, with rivals not responding and punishment free of breakouts, the largest discounted loop gain at which restraint stays enforceable falls as $p_d$ rises, to none above $p_d=0.9495$ for $\theta_0=0$ [num: JX]. In one reduced joint model with the stakes arm switched off ({{jointscissors}}), restraint is enforceable iff the won share stays below a threshold; stakes that outgrow fixed provision carry it across, provision growing as fast as the stakes leaves both blades still, and the objective blade does most of the closing [num: JX, one reduced model, rivals not responding]. *Proof:* S5.

The arms also fix where Test G discriminates: at intermediate $p_d$, since the restraint gap between wider and narrower objectives grows with $p_d$ while the wider still sustains restraint and, where a decisive position is worth more than cooperation, closes as $p_d\to1$, where no objective sustains restraint (Section 7.4; S5) [num: JX].

This is the precise sense in which **the means of restraint weaken as the thing to be restrained grows**, and in which growth as such does not close the blades: where the stakes outgrow provision beneath the game, growth moves temptation up and restraint down at once, and where provision grows with them it does not. That is the image of trees inside the enforcement condition. Durable cooperation has to be built into what the system selects for before the blades close.

Each erosion runs faster with a more powerful instrument; Section 6 asks what AI does to the game, and what the game does to AI, and takes from here the enforcement condition and the two channels of risk.
