# Part I. Mechanism: what the conditions of winning do

Part I states the mechanism and proves its results in specified models, each with its premises inside its statement; whether the present trajectory meets their conditions and shows their signatures is the question of Part II.

## 2. The conditions of winning and their reflexive closure

> **Claim.** Three conditions together produce a selection pressure of winning: selection by relative success; winnings that convert into the capacity to win again; and capacity that converts decisively into winning [def]. Near equal standing, with the prize and the actors' conduct fixed, they combine into one structural loop gain, and where it exceeds one, leads compound [thm]. Where success can also buy control over the selection itself, the pressure closes on itself: the meta-attractor of winning [def].
>
> **Why.** The conditions multiply: remove any one and the gain is zero [thm]. Sharing and diffusion lower the gain in every channel; provision not won in the contest lowers it where decisiveness rises less than in proportion to the stock, as with capacity; correction lowers it where decisiveness rises at least in proportion, as with rule support, and not where it only speeds a decay common to all, which a contest of ratios does not see [thm]. Where rivals respond, the same form holds with an equilibrium decisiveness [thm], and in the contests computed where a lead discourages the follower, leads compound below a structural gain of one [num].

### 2.1 Success is not contribution

Every selection process favours something, and need not favour the continued functioning of the system in which it occurs. Of two organizations that depend on the same shared infrastructure, one maintains it and the other redirects those resources into expansion while using what others maintain. Neglect is a competitive advantage when maintenance costs are immediate, deterioration is delayed, and those harmed cannot make the expanding organization bear the consequences; the neglectful one need only be more capable at securing the resources that decide survival and expansion, and if those resources let its practices spread, the system selects against some of the behaviour that keeps it functioning. **Success within a selection process is distinct from contribution to the conditions that support it.**

Selection on one dimension does not select the best. It removes the balanced: an agent that spends nothing on what the measure does not count, including the costs it imposes on others and on the future, has more to spend on what it counts, and so outscores on it an agent of equal means with broader concerns. The step is analytic, and the author put the premise in one line: "The agent that only optimiz[es] capital value, and discard[s] all other values, including externalities, will have the highest capital efficiency" [corpus: meta-attractor]. What remains after many rounds is what the filter lets through, not a sample of human nature; how far real populations have been so filtered is an empirical question for Part II.

Nothing here requires that people be or become selfish: practices expand, are imitated or are replaced while many individuals keep their values, and what decides is which practices receive the means to continue. The primitive is selection, not greed.

### 2.2 Three interconnected conditions

**{{!Definition:cond}} (The selection pressure of winning) [def].** *Carriers* are whatever selection acts on: reproducible units that can gain or lose share and carry a way of succeeding from one round to the next; cell lineages, practices, firms, research programmes, members of a federation, states, doctrines and trained AI systems are carriers. A system of carriers is under a *selection pressure of winning* when three conditions hold together, each to a degree.
1. **Relative selection.** What is won is allocated by success relative to the others; a share $\beta$ of it, possibly zero, is divided equally whatever the outcome.
2. **Convertibility.** Part of what is won converts into stocks that raise future winning: capacity, talent, data, standing, rule support. Stocks also come from sources not won in the contest; this *provision not won in the contest* is what the prose calls *provision beneath the game*.
3. **Decisiveness.** Stocks convert into winning by an increasing success function; $r$ measures how decisively.

When control over the selection itself is among the stocks that success can buy, the pressure *closes on itself* (Section 2.3). It needs no intention, no conspiracy and no particular culture; it acts on whatever is reproducible and can be won.

The conditions state formally a loop the author put in one sentence: "if you use what you have to compete for more and what you win is yours, and you use that to keep compet[ing], we will end up racing faster and faster and trade everything for winning" [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. *What you win is yours* is relative selection with nothing shared; *use what you have to compete for more* is convertibility; *keep compet[ing]* is reinvestment that never reaches a point of enough; and *trade everything for winning* is the filter of Section 2.1. They combine in one quantity.

**{{!Proposition:loopgain}} (One loop gain) [thm].** The conditions multiply into a single structural loop gain: equal standing breaks when it exceeds one; discounted, it prices standing while the discounted loop is stable; and when rivals respond, the same form holds with an equilibrium decisiveness.

*Model.* $n$ carriers contest a prize $\Omega$ of fixed size each period. A share $\beta$ is divided equally; the rest goes to carrier $i$ with probability or share $p_i=e^{\sigma_i}/\sum_ke^{\sigma_k}$, where strength $\sigma_i$ increases in $i$'s stocks $X_i^j$ and, except in (4), depends on nothing else. Stock $j$ receives a share $\kappa_j$ of what is won, an inflow $k_{0j}\ge0$ not won in the contest, decays or is corrected at rate $d_j>0$, and diffuses toward the mean at rate $\delta_{f,j}$, with $d_j+\delta_{f,j}\le1$:
$${{!eq:stocks}}\qquad X_i^{j\prime}=(1-d_j)X_i^j+\kappa_j\Omega\big[(1-\beta)p_i+\beta/n\big]+k_{0j}-\delta_{f,j}\big(X_i^j-\bar X^j\big).$$
At equal standing write $r_j=\partial\sigma/\partial\ln X^j\ge0$ for the structural decisiveness of stock $j$, and $\omega_j=\kappa_j\Omega/(\kappa_j\Omega+nk_{0j})$ for the share of stock $j$ that has to be won, set by the stakes against provision not won in the contest. The *structural loop gain* is
$${{!eq:gamma}}\qquad \Gamma=(1-\beta)\sum_j r_j\,\omega_j\,\frac{d_j}{d_j+\delta_{f,j}}.$$
1. *Equal standing is locally asymptotically stable if $\Gamma<1$ and unstable if $\Gamma>1$. The channels add because every stock is fed by the same winnings through one win share: compounding through capacity and the reflexive closure through rule support are terms of one sum, and channels each too weak to open the loop can open it together.*
2. *$\Gamma=0$ if any condition is removed: all of the prize divided equally ($\beta=1$), nothing convertible ($\kappa_j=0$ for all $j$), or no decisiveness ($r_j=0$ for all $j$).*
3. *(The price of standing.) Hold the prize and the others' conduct fixed, and let an actor discount at $\delta$ and reinvest its net payoff at the rates $\kappa_j$ at which winnings convert. Write*
$$\Gamma_\delta=(1-\beta)\sum_jr_j\,\omega_j\,\frac{\delta d_j}{1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})},$$
*which is below $\Gamma$ and tends to it as $\delta\to1$. If $0\le\Gamma_\delta<1$, a unit of relative net payoff today is worth, to first order, $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ units of the actor's own future winnings, the closed-loop gain of the discounted loop, and this price grows without bound as $\Gamma_\delta\to1^-$. If $\Gamma_\delta\ge1$, no first-order price exists: the value of standing is continuous at equal standing with infinite slope.*
4. *(Rivals who respond.) Let strength depend also on an intensity each carrier chooses, and let carriers follow a symmetric Markov equilibrium that is differentiable at equal standing and in which each carrier's equilibrium win share $p^{\rm eq}_i$ rises with each of its own stocks. Write $r^{\rm eq}_j=\frac{n^2}{n-1}\,\partial p^{\rm eq}_i/\partial\ln X^j_i$ at equal standing, the decisiveness read off the equilibrium win share with every intensity responding, and $r^{\rm pr}_j$ for the same quantity with the actor's own intensity held and its rivals' responding. Then (1) and (2) hold with $r^{\rm eq}_j$ in place of $r_j$, which defines the equilibrium gain $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$; and (3) holds with $r^{\rm pr}_j$ in place of $r_j$, which defines $\Gamma^{\rm pr}_\delta$, plus, where intensity imposes a shared loss, the value of the change in that loss that the rivals' responses bring.*

*Scope.* Local, at equal standing, with a fixed prize; {{>standprice}} lets the prize grow. The channels must be reinforcing and fed by the one win share: a countervailing channel is not a negative term of $\Gamma$, and one that answers with delay produces a growing oscillation while the net gain is below one ({{delayosc}}); a stock fed by a separate decision, such as lobbying bought from profits, is not a term of the sum. Part (4) keeps the form and changes the number: where a lead discourages the follower, $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$ exceeds one already at a structural gain of 0.45 in the two-actor contests computed, so equal standing breaks although $\Gamma<1$ [num: MP]; where catch-up is so strong that a carrier's equilibrium win share falls with one of its own stocks, the premise of (4) fails and $\Gamma^{\rm eq}$ alone does not decide. The structural $\Gamma$ is therefore a benchmark, not a bound in either direction; {{eqgain}} gives the magnitudes and two findings held for outside review. *Proof:* S2 [num: LG, PD, MP].

The single-channel case gives the compounding condition in closed form, and with it the formal content of provision beneath the game.

**{{!Proposition:compound}} (When winnings compound, and what enough requires) [thm].** *Let capacity $K_i$ receive a share $\kappa_c$ of what $i$ wins, an inflow $k_0$ not won in the contest, depreciation at rate $d_K$ and diffusion toward the mean at rate $\delta_f$, with $p_i=K_i^r/\sum_jK_j^r$ and a prize of fixed size. Let $K^\ast$ and $S^\ast=nK^\ast$ be individual and total capacity at equal standing, $\omega=\kappa_c\Omega/(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)$ the share of capacity that has to be won, and $\Gamma_0=(1-\beta)r\omega$ the structural gain without diffusion.*
1. *Equal standing is locally stable if $\kappa_c\Omega\big((1-\beta)r-1\big)<nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$ and unstable if the inequality is reversed. Without provision and diffusion the condition for stability is $(1-\beta)r<1$, and $\kappa_c$ sets only the level of capacity. With provision, a smaller convertible share stabilizes equal standing.*
2. *A point of enough. Let every carrier but one stop reinvesting at a cap $\bar K\le K^\ast$ and stay there, and let the remaining one, a laggard, start below the cap. Its net inflow is*
$$\phi(K)=\kappa_c\Omega\Big[(1-\beta)\frac{K^r}{K^r+(n-1)\bar K^r}+\frac\beta n\Big]+k_0-d_KK+\delta_f\,\frac{n-1}n\,(\bar K-K).$$
   *(i) If $\bar K<K^\ast$, a laggard just below the cap returns to it. If $\bar K=K^\ast$, it returns if $\Gamma_0(n-1)/n<1+\delta_f(n-1)/(nd_K)$ and falls away if the inequality is reversed; without diffusion the condition for return is $\Gamma<n/(n-1)$.*
   *(ii) The laggard returns from every start if and only if $\phi>0$ on $(0,\bar K)$. When $r>1$, $\phi(0^+)=\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0+\delta_f(n-1)\bar K/n$, so return from every start needs an inflow not tied to rank: sharing, provision or diffusion. Without any of the three, laggards below a threshold are excluded. Such an inflow is necessary, not sufficient; $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0\ge d_K\bar K$ suffices.*

*Scope.* The prize is fixed; S2 gives the condition of (1) for a prize that stays fixed while capacity grows and for one proportional to capacity, which without provision or diffusion is again $(1-\beta)r>1$, the condition under which looking ahead narrows the working objective ({{>standprice}}). At equality in (1) the linearization is neutral and the proposition is silent. The others stay at the cap of their own accord only when $\bar K\le(\kappa_c\Omega+nk_0)/(nd_K+\delta_f)$; above that bound they are held by assumption, and simulations in which every carrier moves agree with (2)(i) in the ten cases computed [num: CE]. *Proof:* S2 [num: CE, SP].

Each clause of the author's sentence can therefore be removed, each with its condition: share what is won until $(1-\beta)r<1$; make winnings non-convertible, which works only together with provision not won in the contest; or stop at a point of enough, which holds from every start only with an inflow not tied to rank. **Provision beneath the game is not only support for those who fall behind. It is what makes non-convertible rewards work and, with sharing and diffusion, what makes a point of enough hold.** The author's image for it is trees: "Trees are providers" [corpus: shengsheng]. Diffusion, the third inflow, draws a laggard toward the mean, since what one carrier learns others can learn; it is a source, not a guarantee [num: CE]. The author drew the rule from game design: "a winner of the game should be rewarded with anything other than more advantages of the game, otherwise the game will eventually converge and winner will take all. Then the game collapses" [corpus: possibility-space]. Who stops at a cap is a question of objectives, not of the loop ({{>enough}}).

**Which levers lower the gain.** The levers of {{eq:gamma}} act channel by channel ({{chansign}}), by how a channel's decisiveness responds to the level of its stock: not at all in a ratio channel, such as capacity in a contest of shares; in proportion in a level channel, such as rule support that offsets a cost. Provision adds the same to everyone, which dilutes what is won in a ratio channel and changes nothing in a level one. A faster decay of capacity, common to all, is invisible to a contest of ratios, the first instance of what {{>blind}} says of every common term, and raises the discounted gain, since a stock that turns over faster is made more of recent winnings [thm]: making winnings decay faster pushes the wrong lever, and changing what winning converts into pushes the right one. Trees for capacity, correction for rule support, sharing and openness for all: this is "change what winning confers", made exact. Since diffusion lowers the gain in every channel, measures that stop it to keep a lead raise the gain they mean to exploit [thm]; what should diffuse is a question of design for Section 10.

**What the loop gain does not decide.** $\Gamma$ decides whether a relative gap grows, not how fast: the speed is the Perron root $\Lambda^\ast$ of the linearized loop, $1+(d_K+\delta_f)(\Gamma-1)$ for one channel, so the same gain on a slowly turning stock opens leads slowly ({{tdec}}). Two further conditions decide what the loop does to those who live with it, and $\Gamma$ decides neither: whether a lead can become decisive faster than detection and response, a position beyond any sanction, which the loop reaches only through the time a gap needs to become decisive (Section 5.2); and whether capability grows faster than the correction that can contest it (Section 5.3). $\Gamma$ is not estimated for any channel; {{gammarange}} shows at illustrative values what $\Gamma>1$ asks of each parameter, and Section 7.4 registers what can be observed in its place. The pressure is a set of interconnected conditions with one structural loop gain at their core, linked by a few shared parameters and by one shared state, the concentration of standing, which {{>thresholds}} sets out.

### 2.3 The reflexive closure: the meta-attractor

An ordinary adaptive system improves its state $x$ against a fixed rule $\mathcal S$ that scores states. The pressure closes on itself when the rule is endogenous: when what can be won includes influence over the rules, the evaluators and correctors, the formation of the minds that will hold positions, and the language in which success is described, so that the outcome of one round of scoring decides who writes the next round's scorecard.

A research community that allocates funding and positions by publication counts, because reliable knowledge is hard to evaluate directly, funds, staffs and copies the practices that produce more publications; once their practitioners occupy the positions that decide recruitment, training and evaluation, the practices the metric favours shape the institutions that choose and interpret the metric, and newcomers must adopt them to do research at all. The decisive transition is from *being selected by a rule* to *helping reproduce the rule that selects you*; it needs no manipulation, only ordinary decisions about whom to hire, what to teach and which evidence to collect.

The loop need not preserve its winners: an organization can fail while its operating logic passes to its successors; a leader can be replaced by an opponent who finds that holding the position requires the same practices; participants can reject the system's consequences while reproducing the conduct their participation depends on. **The persistent entity can be a way of succeeding**, which is why {{cond}} calls what is selected a carrier. Structural explanation says why intentions alone cannot account for recurrence; it does not erase responsibility, which differs with knowledge, discretion and power.

**{{!Definition:meta}} (Meta-attractor) [def].** Let the state update be $x'=\mathcal F(x,\mathcal S)$ and the rule update $\mathcal S'=\mathcal G(x,\mathcal S)$. Three objects are distinguished; each claim in this paper says which it concerns.
1. **The mechanism: reflexive selection.** A positive loop runs through the rule: the rule moves resources toward some states, and the states so resourced move the rule further in their favour. In {{loopgain}} it is every channel whose stock is control over selection.
2. **An attracting configuration in a specified model:** a compact invariant set of the joint flow, asymptotically stable, with a basin that is a proper subset of the space. A positive loop is necessary for multistability (Thomas's rule; Soulé 2003), not sufficient. The field model of Section 4 exhibits such configurations.
3. **A long transient:** trajectories stay near a region for a long time relative to one round of selection, without the region being attracting.

A *meta-attractor* is the mechanism of (1); wherever this paper calls it attracting, the claim is (2), in the model where it has been shown, and a claim of (2) about the world is a conjecture. The name is kept beside "generator functions" of catastrophic risk, which name the same underlying forces as preconditions; what it adds is the reflexive step in which selection rewrites its own criterion, the persistence that step confers, the layer of minds and language through which it runs, and a generative counterpart, so that one concept covers the mechanism and its generative dual.

**The meta-attractor of winning** is the case in which the pressure of {{cond}} is the rule: success under relative selection converts into control over the selection that judges the next round. The conditional form keeps its conditions in the sentence: *recursive selection creates second-order selection for control over future selection when the resulting benefits are sufficiently appropriable, persistent and large relative to the costs of changing and defending the rules.* Where (2) holds, long-run behaviour maximizes the rule whatever purpose it was introduced to serve (Goodhart 1975; Campbell 1979), and by the time a measure has stopped measuring, the people and institutions it elevated decide whether it is replaced.

The smallest model of the mechanism is a pair, a carrier's success and its favourable control over the rules, each decaying at a rate in $(0,1]$ and feeding the other with a non-negative coefficient, stable exactly when the product of the coefficients is below the product of the rates ({{bench}}) [thm]: Richardson's arms-race condition with control over rules in place of armaments (Richardson 1960), of the same form as {{loopgain}} but not a case of it, each coefficient naming a channel that can be measured.

The pattern is not fated. Positive feedback can saturate, oscillate, split or break; energetic ceilings, heterogeneity, delays, constraints at other scales and reflexive awareness cap it. It is "more like a hurricane than a black hole" [corpus: meta-attractor]: a structure sustained by a flow, which decays when the flow is cut.

**{{!Remark:pathgen}} (Pathological and generative meta-attractors).** Reflexive selection is not itself the problem: science, the rule of law and open-source ecosystems also persist through the conditions they maintain. A meta-attractor is *pathological* to the degree that its reproduction depletes shared conditions that selection does not price, weakens the channels through which such costs register and trigger correction, or makes safe alternatives unreachable within the relevant time, budget and authority; it is *generative* to the degree that its reproduction strengthens independent correction and keeps alternatives reachable, so that success funds correctors it cannot control and what is rewarded is contribution to the shared conditions. That order is not selected among carriers, since correction keyed to no one is selected against among otherwise identical carriers ({{>noinside}}); it has to be built and held by the whole, through coverage and formula funding (Section 10.4). The way out is therefore not the abolition of selection but a change in what is selected for (Section 10); the corpus calls the passage "change the basin, then add speed" [corpus: meta-attractor].

### 2.4 Not bound to any substrate

The pressure needs only relative selection, convertible winnings and decisiveness; its closure needs only that the successful can reshape their selection. Cancer lineages that evade the signals limiting proliferation and the immune response that would remove them (Hanahan and Weinberg 2011; Aktipis et al. 2015), markets with increasing returns that lock in early leads (Arthur 1989) and whose winners buy the rules of the game (Stigler 1971), security competition, federations and training loops all show the same form; X2 gives the instances in full, and X1 the lineage of the ideas. Nothing in {{cond}} refers to a culture, a political system or a species: a civilization on another planet would face the same pressure wherever selection is relative, winnings convert and the successful can edit the selection.

What is specific to AI is not the pressure but how far AI moves its parameters, in either direction. Where AI raises decisiveness, it raises the gain; where it speeds the turnover of stocks, a gain above one opens leads faster ({{tdec}}); where it lowers the cost of control faster than the cost of independent correction, it strengthens the reflexive channels (Section 4); where it raises the growth of hazardous behaviour faster than contestable coverage, it moves the second condition the loop gain does not decide (Section 5.3); where it is shared, diffused or provided beneath the game, it lowers the gain. And trained AI systems are themselves carriers (Section 6).

A structural loop gain says when leads compound, not what those inside the loop can see; Section 3 asks what relative selection makes invisible, and what the discounted gain does to the objective of whoever looks ahead.
