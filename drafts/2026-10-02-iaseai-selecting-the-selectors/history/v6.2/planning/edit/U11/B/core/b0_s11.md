# Part IV. Transition and judgment

Part IV's results on trust, signals, pauses and dwell hold in the models they state; its tracks, readiness dimensions and requirements of a reachable path are definitions, each row with the support it cites; its precedents are observations whose limiting conditions are part of what they show; and its judgment is a recommendation under the standard of Section 8.1.

## 11. A reachable transition

> **Claim.** An end state that every participant would keep is not thereby one they can reach: a path is reachable when it is survivable at every stage, preferred by every participant at every step given its beliefs, and held at every step until the slow variable that step moves has crossed its threshold [def]. In the field model a step released before its slow variable has crossed its threshold is re-absorbed, so graduated steps reach only as a ratchet, and without a recruitable lineage of the alternative the hold a reform needs grows without bound with the age of capture [thm]. Narrow E1 among actors who price in a certain lock, and a costly signal that separates a survival-first actor from a relative one, need no trust; where the first steps are of this kind, trust is an outcome of the transition, not its precondition [thm]. A pause that changes nothing buys time, not survival [thm].
>
> **Why.** Trust is needed to cooperate against a possible defector, to be believed and to stay, and a lower value of winning relaxes all three at once [thm]. Shares lag rule support, so levels improve before safety does [thm]; a prediction carried only by assurances meets the third erosion [thm]. Each commitment moves a slow variable with its own time constant, so the commitments run as parallel tracks, the slowest started first [def].

### 11.1 Trust, and what relaxes it

Trust enters a transition in three places. To cooperate against a possible defector, an actor needs its belief $\varpi$ that the other will cooperate to reach the threshold $g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ of {{assure}}; where the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating, $T>R$, verification has to supply what belief does not. To be believed, a sender needs the receiver not to discount what it says for what it gains by saying it, which relative accounting erodes ({{talk}}). To stay, a participant needs confidence that the arrangement will persist, which the net gain from exit erodes where that gain grows with the capability held in check ({{overhang}}). Confidence in reliable instruments of verification lets parties cooperate without good will, but installing them is itself a first step taken under $\varpi$.

Whatever lowers the value of winning relative to the joint value relaxes all three constraints at once (**{{!Corollary:relaxtrust}}**, S11) [thm]: where $g_C,g_D>0$ it lowers the belief threshold, and it lowers the effective bias of talk and the net gain from exit. Verification relaxes the first where $T-P+F>0$ and $P>S$, and only on what it observes; a sanction relaxes the first and the third only while it can still be imposed, so that against a defector whose position has become decisive it acts only through the bound $\bar p_d$ on the chance of breakout ({{enforce}}(2)) [thm]. Sharing the divisible stakes and provision beneath the game lower $T-R$ and the relative term of exit, and where they also lower the price of standing they widen working objectives, and with them the range of informative talk ({{standprice}}) [thm∣prem]; a common frontier lowers the vulnerability of restraint, $P-S$, by pooling the restrainers' effort ({{commonfrontn}}) [num]. Sanctions relax assurance and exit at the price of escalation, counted in $\Theta$ (Section 8.1).

Trust is mainly an outcome of the transition, not its precondition. It follows from conduct that a lower value of winning makes rational and verification makes visible; the one step it must precede is the installing of instruments, and the first steps of Section 11.2 need none. In the author's words, "Trust is not a prerequisite—it is an outcome. It arises only when we have a shared understanding of reality and can reasonably expect others to act accordingly" [corpus: selfishness-spiral, 2025-03-14].

Not every obstacle is a lack of trust, and the remedy follows the obstacle. A misjudged shared loss calls for evidence; a real conflict of incentives, for changed payoffs and enforcement; a coordination failure, for verification and graduated reciprocation (Osgood 1962); commitment and capture over time, for independent correction and steps each held for its dwell. Graduated steps answer the third only: they do not move a conflict of incentives, and against capture they reach only as a ratchet (Section 11.4).

### 11.2 Prediction, evidence and signals

A prediction about a game whose outcome turns on what the players believe is one of the game's inputs, and it acts through two beliefs. Where an actor misjudges the shared loss, a derivation it can check corrects its own accounting: an actor that holds the prediction, and finds that restraint gives it higher survival whether its rival restrains or races, restrains whatever it believes about the others ({{survassure}}(2)) [thm]. Where racing persists because each expects restraint to be exploited while a cooperative equilibrium exists (Section 5.4), the same derivation moves $\varpi$, and the equilibrium with it. That is counter-performativity, a model of behaviour making the world less like itself (MacKenzie 2006) [obs], and the sense in which this paper's prediction is written to be falsified by being shared (Section 0). It acts only where the outcome turns on a belief the prediction can change, not on what the game selects whatever is believed; and where racing is dominant in a one-shot game, the belief it would correct is simply correct.

Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because the hazard is shared, since a sender gains from the receiver's restraint whatever it intends ({{talk}}(3)): a prediction carried only by assurances meets the third erosion. It has to be carried by what a receiver can check without trusting the sender: a derivation from public premises; verifiable evidence, such as third-party disclosure of incidents and joint evaluation suites; protocols agreed in advance, which commit a sender's disclosures before it knows what they will show (Kamenica and Gentzkow 2011); demonstrations that make a dynamic visible; and costly signals [def]. Shared understanding removes one road to conflict, since under common priors mutual optimism alone cannot produce war (Fey and Ramsay 2007) [thm], but not the others: in an idealized experiment, in exploratory analysis, falling behind drove unsafe choices while the stated level of shared risk changed them little (EV11) [obs]. A change of prediction is therefore the first step, not a sufficient one. The next is conduct a rival can read, which also tips a professed doctrine that few hold in private (Section 5.4); and the payoffs that make falling behind unsurvivable have to change as well, which is what the arrangements below and Section 12 are for.

**{{!Proposition:signal}} (When keeping AI out of one's own decision loops is a signal) [thm].** A step that the lock makes cheap for a survival-first actor and the contest makes costly for a relative one separates them; a step that is cheap to imitate buys restraint only in proportion to what it costs a relative actor. *A sender is survival-first and prices in the lock (type $\mathsf S$, with probability $1-\mu$) or holds a relative objective (type $\mathsf R$, with probability $\mu$). It can take a step $\mathsf E$: a verifiable, durable removal of AI from its own strategic decision loops for a period, open to inspection, which costs type $\mathsf t$ the amount $c_{\mathsf t}$. A receiver restrains iff its belief that the sender is $\mathsf S$ is at least $\varpi^\ast\in(0,1)$; its restraint is worth $\Pi_{\mathsf t}>0$ to type $\mathsf t$, including, for $\mathsf R$, what $\mathsf R$ gains by exploiting it.*
1. *An equilibrium in which $\mathsf S$ takes the step, $\mathsf R$ does not, and the receiver restrains after the step and not otherwise exists iff $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}$ and $c_{\mathsf R}\ge\Pi_{\mathsf R}$.*
2. *If $0\le c_{\mathsf R}<\Pi_{\mathsf R}$, there is no such equilibrium. Where prior trust falls short, $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, the receiver restrains after the step with probability at most $c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ in every equilibrium; where $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ the bound is attained, by an equilibrium in which $\mathsf R$ imitates just often enough to hold the receiver's belief at $\varpi^\ast$.*
3. *A step that is reversible or unverifiable, $c_{\mathsf R}\to0$, is therefore cheap talk: it buys no restraint that prior trust did not already give.*

*Scope.* Whether the step separates is a question of measured costs: where the lock is certain, $c_{\mathsf S}$ can be zero or negative ({{delegatelock}}(1)), while a relative type, which delegates at every speed ({{delegate}}(3)), pays for a durable removal with the speed it forgoes for the period. Where prior trust falls short and the inequalities of (1) are strict, the separating equilibrium is the only one that survives the intuitive criterion. *Proof:* S11 [num: TR].

Unilateral, verifiable narrow E1 is therefore a candidate first move, cheap for an actor that prices in the lock and costly for one that values the speed delegation buys [thm∣prem]: the costly signal by which a security-seeking type reveals itself (Kydd 2000), which only inspection of doctrine and exercises keeps a relative type from declaring without taking (S11).

### 11.3 Four temporal arrangements, and readiness

Keeping AI out of the contest, stopping, slowing and pausing differ in cost, verification and failure. Section 5.3 defined stopping (E2) and slowing (E3) by what they do to the growth channel of risk; here each is stated as an arrangement.

**E1: AI out of the contest.** *The narrow form* keeps human authority over the decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty, strategic and escalatory decisions irreversible within the time available for deliberation, such as the use of force and nuclear command, with jointly verified limits, drilled fallback capacity and an evidence channel independent of AI, so that a human judging an alarm has reasons of their own. Among actors who price in the lock it is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust where the lock is certain, $q_L=1$, and it stays one while the lock fails with a probability below the bound of {{nearlock}} ({{delegatelock}}(1)) [thm]. Since it removes AI from those decisions, not from the detection that answers a breakout, it leaves the chance that a rival's breakout becomes decisive, $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, where it was ({{detectlink}}) [thm]. Where the lock is uncertain, as in tactical domains with a large speed advantage, delegation can be an equilibrium too ({{delegatelock}}(2), (3)): keeping AI out then rests on a commitment held in common, or on the signal of {{signal}} where its costs separate, and bounded, reversible defensive automation falls under the test of O2 (Section 6.6), not under E1. *The broad form*, AI not an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, has its criterion and the three layers of its enforcement in Section 6.6. **Broad E1 is the scope; narrow E1 is the first step** [corpus: alignment-selection, 2026-09-26]. E1 is held in common, since an actor that stays out alone while others race survives less than by joining wherever staying out raises a rival's chance of a decisive position enough ({{survassure}}(3); X11) [thm]; it begins with the decision loops in which the lock makes it self-enforcing, and is extended by agreement.

**The deterrence objection**, at its strongest, is that a commitment that cannot be revoked is worth more than one that can (Schelling 1960), so that semi-automated retaliation would relieve the pressure to launch on warning. Survivable, human-controlled forces give the same relief without removing judgment, and the commitment value of automation is the lock itself, O2 given up in that domain ({{lockin}}). An automated commitment cannot tell a false alarm from an attack, and false alarms have been stopped by human judgment, as in 1983 (Podvig 2002; Hoffman 2009) [obs: EV18]; escalation at machine speed is a common risk that relative selection does not see ({{blind}}), so the contest under-prices it [thm∣prem]. X11 gives the answers in full.

**E2: stop.** Capability of the decisive class is not built while the conditions for governing it are absent: an accurate model of the coupling, the shared prediction, and common custody. Where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage, it is rolled back, which ends exposure fastest ({{freeze}}); for divergences that hide under evaluation, E2 and E3 are what lies beyond admission ({{masked}}). Because E2 applies to the decisive class only, its cost, if AI turns out not to be decisive, is the delay of that class's margin over capable systems that are not decisive, including lives that earlier benefits would have saved. The cost can be large (crux C10) and, for those who bear it, irreversible; but it does not foreclose later choice, whereas if AI is decisive the loss it avoids forecloses choice for everyone, an asymmetry in the option set, not in the size of the losses (Arrow and Fisher 1974). Coverage of E2 comes from consent or from coercion; coercion's risk of escalation is counted in $\Theta$, and the conditions under which a coercive restriction of frontier inputs enters even the comparison of designs are stated once, in Section 12.1.

**E3: develop at the pace of understanding.** In each hazard class, capability grows no faster than contestable coverage can follow, so the uncovered hazardous share stays summable ({{coverage}}) and each hazard mode keeps its robustness margin ({{tempo}}). It binds class by class where coverage falls behind, and it binds a world that has stopped racing (Section 5.3); its carriers are thresholds in capability and in effective compute tied to measured coverage per class (Section 10.4). How much it slows depends on whether AI-assisted oversight raises coverage as fast as hazardous variety grows (crux C3a). Stopping is not slowing: E2 forgoes the decisive class, and E3 paces each hazard class to its coverage.

**A temporary pause**, the fourth arrangement, is valued by what changes during it (**{{!Proposition:pausevalue}}**, S11) [thm]. Where a pause adds no hazard of its own and the same process resumes after it, survival over any number of active rounds is unchanged, and so is its limit, which is zero where the residual hazards are not summable ({{nonsum}}); a pause after which they are summable, where before they were not, keeps survival bounded away from zero; and in the field model, a pause that freezes shares while ordinary rule support keeps accumulating completes capture from every frozen share above $1/G$ ({{pausefield}}). A pause that changes nothing buys time, not survival: time to change verification, residual hazards, human capacity, enforceable limits, shared provision, objectives and institutions. An indefinite stop, a lasting cap, or a pause that produces a different decision process is a different policy. **A pause with no relevant change is not a certificate of readiness to resume**, and readiness has therefore to be defined.

**{{!Definition:readiness}} (Readiness to resume) [def].** *Readiness is assessed along seven dimensions, each with observable indicators; a threshold set by the risk decisions of the domain of use, with the error of measurement inside it; and an assessor whose own row sums are certified and who is insulated from rule-making during the pause. A dimension is met when its indicator has stayed past its threshold for its residence time, at least the time constant of the slow variable on which the dimension rests. Each dimension is decided by a sequential test registered in advance with two error rates: the rate of declaring it met when it is not, set by the protection standard; and the rate of declaring it unmet when it is met, bounded so that the seven together hold back a ready state with no more than a registered probability. A met dimension is re-opened by a test of the same kind, not by a single reading. All seven must be met; none compensates for another.*

| Dimension | Question | Slow variable; the residence time is at least |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Technical reliability | Has the residual hazard of the relevant classes fallen, by evidence independent of the developer and valid against evaluation awareness? | Residual risk per class; the class's manifestation window $\tau_e$ ({{tempo}}) |
| Q2 Shared objectives | Have governing purposes moved from relative advantage toward shared provision? | The self-models that governing positions select; a turnover of those positions |
| Q3 Strategic incentives | Has the value of winning fallen relative to the joint value? | The won share of capacity, $\omega$ ({{scissors}}); a period of growing stakes |
| Q4 Effective correction | Can evaluators obtain evidence, keep independent funding and execute findings? | Rule support in each corrector domain; its dwell ({{dwell}}) |
| Q5 Deployment constraints | Is the tempo of capability and delegation bound to correction capacity? | Coverage against hazardous variety; a generation of capability |
| Q6 Retained human agency | Can people still take over delegated functions? | Human capacity $\mathcal M$; its recovery time after delegation ({{lockin}}) |
| Q7 Custody and verifiability | Is decisive-class capability in common custody, and conduct verifiable faster than breakout? | The capability held in check, and detection latency ({{overhang}}; {{detectlink}}); a period in which the capability grows |

These are dimensions to operationalize, not a checklist proved sufficient; S11 states each residence time in full, and X11 records where each dimension stands today. They do not require that every person's private motives change first: binding institutions can change the effective game while private competitive preferences persist, which is why effective correction is among them. Readiness presupposes rows R1, R2, R7 and R8b of {{require}} ({{crosswalk}}), since no pause supplies them. Residence is required because levels improve before safety does (Section 11.4); the decision rule, because noise and conjunction together bias a fixed reading toward "not ready": seven dimensions, each read once with a 5% chance of missing a met dimension, pass a ready state with probability $0.95^7\approx0.70$ [thm], and a pause held past its evidence raises the cost of staying, so that coverage tips once that cost exceeds the cost of standing outside ({{unravel}}) [thm∣prem]. A sequential test turns noise into delay instead of bias, at error rates fixed in advance ({{readrule}}) [num: TR].

**The order.** By the trust each needs, E1 comes first, since it keeps humans as the players and its narrow form needs none; then E2 or E3, class by class. By verifiability the order reverses: chips, fabs, datacentres and compute can be observed, though thresholds stated in compute erode as algorithms improve (EV9) [obs] and hardware mechanisms for verifying compute use are proposed and prototyped, not deployed (EV17) [obs], while E1 concerns software and internal procedure. E1 is the hardest to verify and the most self-enforcing, which is why it can begin as declared doctrine with inspected exercises (X11 gives the feasibility in full). A third order, by time constant, decides what is started first.

### 11.4 Parallel tracks, dwell and windows

Each commitment of the transition moves a slow variable and holds only if that variable crosses its threshold before the commitment is withdrawn ({{reabsorb}}). The slow variables have different time constants, and one that is not moving when it is needed cannot be moved then: started in the order of the trust they need, the slowest would start last and finish after the window it serves had closed. The commitments are therefore tracks that run in parallel, started in the order of their time constants, slowest first, and binding in the order of the trust they need, from the first track to the fifth [def]. They are a candidate to be tested: civilian services, frontier development and strategic decisions can need different paths (X11).

| Track | Slow variable and its time constant |
|---|---|
| 1. Shared account of hazards | Shared understanding of the hazards, which cannot run ahead of the evidence: for late-manifesting classes, their manifestation window ({{tempo}}) |
| 2. Narrow, testable commitments | Human capacity to take delegated decisions back, which decays at $\delta_h$ under delegation and recovers at $\varrho_h$ without it ({{lockin}}) |
| 3. Fund correction and shared services | Rule support in each corrector domain: several institutional turnovers $1/(\varepsilon d)$, more the deeper the lock-in ({{dwell}}) |
| 4. Delegation, access, custody, decision rights | The capability held in check, which raises the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}); the lead time of custody's installation and verification, which must be shorter than the time to a decisive position |
| 5. Broaden as institutions prove themselves | The record of the arrangements under stress, and the self-models formed inside them: a cycle of stress and recovery, and for minds a generation |

Each track meets a compensating loop of its own, from measurement of hazards turned into measurement of who leads, on the first, to closure into a bloc, on the fifth; X11 gives what each track commits, the mechanisms it acts on, its compensating loop and its indicator of progress. Slowest first means that the third and fourth tracks begin before the trust they will bind on exists, and that the openness of the fifth is built in from the first. That is why Section 12.1 begins its first two tiers together.

**Dwell.** A reform that insulates rule-making from shares lets rule support decay at its rate of correction, but shares answer to rule support and lag it: they keep rising while support stays above break-even, for $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$, so the reform has to be held through that rise and then long enough to repay the lock-in it added; from a captured share above $x_\sigma$, shares are back at their starting level before the state has left the basin of capture [thm]. A rollback judged on share levels is therefore premature (Test D). What decides is where rule support stands against the separatrix, which is not observed directly; near full share, the depth of lock-in cannot be read from the level at all. The hold grows with that depth, and depth with the age of capture, so without a floor under the alternative the hold has no bound [thm]. A lineage of the alternative that persists inside selection, and is recruited when the rule changes, bounds it [thm]: from a captured share of 0.99, with $G=10$ and rule-making ten times slower than adoption, a floor of 0.1% cuts the hold from 10.45 to 3.81 in units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, while a share protected outside selection, which does not recruit, leaves it at 10.44 ({{dwell}}) [num: DW]. These holds take the conversion gain as fixed; where capture has built capacity that goes on converting, the gain stays higher and so does the hold. For real institutions the magnitudes are a conjecture [conj].

That is the mechanism of decay and renewal: in the field model, an order in which forms can fail and be replaced can be reformed in bounded time, and one that has frozen cannot [thm]. In the author's words, "look at the ecosystem, death is not a bug but a feature, changes and mutations are what keeps things alive — create a rich space of systems, not reducing to one rule, one attractor" [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-06-05].

**The ratchet.** In the field model, graduated steps reach the threshold only as a ratchet: a sequence of steps none of which carries its slow variable across its threshold and is held there for its dwell is a sequence of re-absorptions, whatever trust it builds, and a step that crosses on one track does not cross on another ({{ratchet}}) [thm]. A transition is not shown to arrive by small steps that each feel safe; it arrives, if it does, by steps each of which is kept.

**Windows.** Whether frontier development is still the keystone, the domain whose reform would take down the captured domains outside the remaining core, is a conjecture [conj]. The window closes as conversion among the corrector domains grows (Section 4.5), which is why the correction track cannot wait for the trust that custody needs. Arrangements of this kind are often adopted when a crisis opens a window (Gunderson and Holling 2002) [obs]: what a crisis can adopt is a design prepared in advance, with its activation committed, and an emergency closure has to state before adoption what would end it, or it will not afterwards (X11). Those who cannot leave the shared exposure, smaller jurisdictions, users and those who depend on services controlled by others, need standing on every track.

**Where things stand.** The first two tracks have seeds, in an international scientific assessment and a UN scientific panel, and in a bilateral affirmation of human control over nuclear use with an incident channel; narrow E1 has no multilateral document that both leading states now endorse, and the slow tracks, correction and custody, have no seed of comparable standing (EV3, EV7, EV8, EV17; X11) [obs]. The seeds must become binding, verifiable and broader before the relevant losses or lock-in.

The table organizes results proved elsewhere; it does not show that the four can be met together.*

| Requirement | What it asks | Support | Not shown |
|---|---|---|---|

Read as a problem of control, the table asks whether the present state lies in the capture basin of the target, the set from which it can be reached and then held under admissible steps (Aubin 1991); that is proved only in the one-domain field model, where the hold of Section 11.4 is the minimum capture time ({{capturebasin}}).

**Precedents.** The record of restraint among rivals qualifies all of this, and each precedent's limiting condition is where its analogy to AI is weakest (X11, corrected against the sources) [obs]. The 1946 proposals for international ownership of atomic energy failed on sequence and on enforcement against violators: **custody must precede decisiveness**. The Biological Weapons Convention shows that a weapon endangering its holder can be renounced unilaterally where its military value is low and another deterrent is held, and that without verification a covert programme persists; nuclear power after a serious accident, durable safety under unchanged motives where the shared loss returns to its source and no prize is decisive, a change of selection, not of motive; and the Montreal Protocol, coverage made attractive by provision and enforced by conditions of access, once substitutes had made the value of winning low. Rivals survived where nature or balancing supplied saturation (Section 7.2); if advantage in AI is unsaturated, as the racers' own premise holds (crux C5), safety must come from changing what is selected before advantage becomes decisive [thm∣prem].

Section 12 takes from here the release rule of its second tier, readiness held for its residence time and judged against the dwell, and the reason its slowest preparations are warranted now: what binds late has to begin early.
