## S7. The present trajectory

### Proofs of {{branches}}, {{raceselects}} and {{racepremise}}

*{{branches}}.* Each item is the cited result read under its branch's condition.

(1) *Branch I.* As $p_d\to1$ with $D_i\ge C_i$, {{eq:enforceeq}} becomes $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)\le0$, so only profiles with $g_i\le0$, stage equilibria of the objectives held, are sustained ({{enforce}}(4)). The best-response and joining statements are {{join}}; dissipation is {{dissipate}}; delegation is {{delegate}}(1) in the additive model of S6, where $L_e/n>L_c\Delta\psi$ suffices for dominance as speed grows, and {{delegatelock}} with $q_L=1$; the failure of proportional correction at $a\tau_c\ge1$ is {{tempo}}; the motive for preventive attack, under (A8), is {{preventattack}}(1).

(2) *Branch I′.* Above the saturating level further advantage changes no outcome, so a deviation from above it yields no position beyond punishment and $p_d=0$. {{eq:enforceeq}} then reduces to $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-v_i)$, which repetition enforces on the terms of {{restraint}}. The race channel ({{racehazard}}) and the growth channel ({{coverage}}) do not pass through $p_d$, so their hazards remain.

(3) *Branch II.* $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ where punishment is triggered by detection ({{detectlink}}), and {{enforce}}(2) gives enforceability for every actor with $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$. In the bargaining model of S5, with A's chance of prevailing falling from 0.5 to 0 over $N$ periods, $c_A=0.05$ and $\delta=0.8$, attack is rational at the start for $N\le3$ and never for $N\ge4$, by backward induction on $V_t=\max(W_t,\delta V_{t+1})$, since at every date the value of fighting now falls below the value of the next offer [num: legacy v4.4]. Modes with $a\tau_c<1$ admit a stabilizing proportional correction ({{tempo}}). For delegation: in the model of {{delegate}}, when $k$ others delegate the gain from delegating is $L_e[w_i(1,k)-w_i(0,k)]$, increasing in the speed $s$ and proportional to the loss from defeat $L_e$, while its cost $L_c\Delta\psi(1-\psi_0)^{n-1-k}(1-\psi_1)^k$ does not depend on $L_e$. The speed at which gain and cost are equal therefore rises as $L_e/L_c$ falls, and if the limiting gain falls below the cost for some $k$, delegation never becomes dominant. That a defeat answered before it becomes decisive carries a smaller $L_e$ is the premise of the item.

(4) *Branch III.* {{boundary}}(1) gives $e(\theta)=r\Omega(n-1-\theta)/\big(n^2(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, so intensity and shared loss are proportional to $r$; {{dissipate}}(1) gives a dissipated share proportional to $r$; and {{racehazard}} a hazard proportional to $r_t$. With $p_d=0$ no breakout term enters {{eq:enforceeq}}. Low structural decisiveness does not give Branch III, since the channels of {{eq:gamma}} add ({{loopgain}}(1)): a capacity channel with gain 0.8 and a rule-support channel with gain 0.3 give $\Gamma=1.1$, and a relative gap of $10^{-4}$ grows to about 4 in 4,000 periods of the nonlinear model [num: LG]; and in the Markov-perfect contests of {{eqgain}}, the equilibrium gain is 1.11 at a structural gain of 0.45 and 2.83 at 0.6 [num: MP]. $\square$

*{{raceselects}}.* (1) Write $\pi=\Pr(E)\in(0,1)$. Since $\Pr(W)=\Pr(W\mid E)\pi+\Pr(W\mid\neg E)(1-\pi)<\Pr(W\mid E)$ when $\Pr(W\mid E)>\Pr(W\mid\neg E)$, Bayes' rule gives $\Pr(E\mid W)=\Pr(W\mid E)\pi/\Pr(W)>\pi$. Under cumulative payoff-monotone selection, conduct that raises relative success grows in share round by round ({{load}}), so each round's shift starts from a distribution already shifted. (2) If winning depends on $X$ only through $E$, then $\Pr(X\mid W,E)=\Pr(X\mid E)$ and $\Pr(X\mid W,\neg E)=\Pr(X\mid\neg E)$, so
$$\Pr(X\mid W)-\Pr(X)=\big[\Pr(X\mid E)-\Pr(X\mid\neg E)\big]\big[\Pr(E\mid W)-\Pr(E)\big],$$
which is positive under the premise and part (1). Without the premise the inequality for conduct before victory does not carry to conduct after it; without the conditional independence it can fail even with the premise, since winners among those who do not restrain themselves can differ in $X$ from the rest of that group. (3) After a decisive win, a promise to give a fraction $\beta$ of the prize to the others is worth breaking by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$, with no one to sanction it ({{wingives}}): promises about conduct after a shift in power are not credible to the party that will lose (Fearon 1995; Powell 2006), and uncertainty about intentions compounds the problem (Mearsheimer 2001). An actor that decides by its worst case over a stated set of possibilities, and applies that rule to every action (Gilboa and Schmeidler 1989), counts the worst winner; a survival-first actor need not, and the proposition does not need it to. $\square$

*{{racepremise}}.* (1) {{join}} gives that restraint is not a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$; in Branch I, and in Branch II without timely detection, $p_d$ is not small, and by {{enforce}}(2) repetition sustains restraint only for an actor with $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$, which as $p_d\to1$ with $D_i\ge C_i$ admits only stage equilibria ({{enforce}}(4)). (2) {{wingives}} gives the first half: after a decisive win what everyone else receives is set by the winner's objective alone. {{raceselects}}(2) gives the second. (3) The final step is a judgment of value, not a derivation. That a course which leaves every participant's protection to a winner's objective, and selects winners whose objectives give least, cannot be justified as a collective course rests on V1 (no actor's victory is an adequate criterion of success), V2 (O1 and O2 protected first) and V3 (those affected keep a capacity to contest the direction). The form "safe provided the right actor wins" does not escape it: which actor wins is what (2) says the race selects, and after the win its protection of others rests on its objective alone. $\square$

### Near parity

Section 7.2 states that near parity is where standing is priced highest. The support is the two-actor Markov-perfect contests of {{eqgain}}, in which a lead discourages the follower [num: MP; held for outside review]:
- *The price at parity is amplified*: about 1.05 to 7.9 times its structural value across the cases computed ({{eqgain}}(3); Section 3.5).
- *Summed over the two contestants, the price is higher at parity than at a three-to-one split in every case computed.* With large jumps ($r=1$) at $\Gamma=0.3$ the prices at shares 0.75 and 0.25 are 0.2703 and 0.1003, together 0.371, against $2\times0.1934=0.387$ at parity; at $\Gamma=0.6$, 0.6543 and 0.1065 against $2\times1.1762$; with small jumps ($r=4$) at $\Gamma=0.3$, 0.0856 and 0.0093 against $2\times0.1941$.
- *Not every leader's own price is highest at parity.* In the large-jump case at $\Gamma=0.3$ the leader's price at 0.75 exceeds the price at parity (0.2703 against 0.1934, 153% of the structural value). "Highest at parity" holds for the contest as a whole and for the laggard, not for every leader.
- *Effort is highest at parity.* In a tug-of-war contest absorbing at a lead of $\pm K$ ($K=3$, $\delta=0.95$), total effort one step from parity is 0.405 of its value at parity and two steps away 0.013; the laggard's effort is 0.138 and then 0.000 of its value at parity.
- *The laggard stops contesting standing*: its price falls, below zero with a shared loss ({{eqgain}}(6)).

Where a strong motive to catch up makes the laggard try harder, the strategic correction shrinks and in one computed case reverses ({{eqgain}}(5)). The statement of Section 7.2 is therefore about contests in which a lead discourages the follower, and it is held for outside review with the other Markov-perfect results.

### The four loops, formally

Section 7.1 quotes the four loops in the author's words and states their formal correspondence. *Loop by loop.* The first three act on the two properties of the balancing loop of regulator lag ({{countervail}}). Its gain falls under capture, since a captured corrector imposes less and reports less ({{corrrate}}), and under the erosion of the human capacity on which correction rests ({{humancorr}}). Its delay lengthens under acceleration, which shortens the time correction has ({{tempo}}), under masking, which hides the harm that would start it ({{masking}}), and under alienated language, which delays the recognition that would mobilize it ({{talk}}). The delays across which dominance can shift from the balancing loop to the reinforcing ones run from the turnover of capacity, about $1/(d_K+\delta_f)$ periods, through the settling of rule support, about $1/(\varepsilon d)$, to the formation of minds over generations, which no model here contains. The table gives, for each loop, its formal type, the state it runs on, what sets its gain, its delay, where it is shown, and its dual among the strands of Section 10.4.

- **P1, sharing as won.** *Intervention:* a fraction $\beta$ of each period's prize divided equally as it arises. *Signs:* Comp. {{chansign}}(1); Stand. {{standprice}} ($\Gamma_\delta$ scales with $1-\beta$; with a proportional prize the condition is $(1-\beta)r>1$); Enf. {{enforce}}(2), since the gain from deviating falls by the shared part of what a deviation wins in the period; Cov. {{racehazard}}, which scales the contested stakes by $1-\beta$; Talk through $\theta$ and {{talk}}; Del. {{delegate}}, since the loss from defeat $L_e$ scales by $1-\beta$. *Not proved:* that the gain from deviating and the loss from defeat contain the contested prize in proportion is the interface *stocks and stage payoffs* (below).
