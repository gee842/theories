## X10. The design in full

One configuration is developed far enough to expose its assumptions. It is one candidate among those Section 12.1 compares under one range of uncertainty: a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions, a verification agreement together with shared provision, regulated competition meeting the requirements of Section 9, an agreement of the type of Section 8.3(g) and the benign winner are others, and the coercive restriction of frontier inputs enters that comparison only under conditions of its own (Section 12.1). The text does not claim the configuration is superior; which design is best is part of crux C4. It is called here a *compact*: an arrangement for shared AI provision and correction, made of separately held functions rather than one institution. Each clause below is written so that a party under assessment cannot turn it against the assessment, and the hardest functions, execution and the fallback, carry the financing, appointment and certificates that the others carry.

**What the functions carry.** The generative loop of Section 10.4 is carried, in the corpus's list, by funding, education, stewardship and maintenance, shared infrastructure, independent evaluation, conflict resolution, succession, protection against enclosure, real exit and appeal, and the material security that makes restraint survivable [corpus: meta-attractor]. Goodwill is not a carrier. The functions below give those carriers bodies, financing and ends, and each strand of Section 10.4 faces the questions the pathological loops answer: how it keeps obtaining resources, how it meets free riding and the pressure to expand, how it allows itself to be corrected, and how it avoids turning "protecting the whole" into a purpose no one can challenge. The columns of the table are those answers: a rule of financing for the first; formula contributions, the share cap and the levy's wide base for the second; a corrector for the third; and a condition that ends or reverts the body for the fourth, which is the withdrawal question of the guardian test ({{guardian}}).

### Functions, held separately

Each function is held by a different body, separately financed and separately corrected, so that correction lies outside what it corrects. On a planet, "outside" means outside the function: a different source of funds, a different lineage, and certificates anyone can recompute from public data. Every body has a rule of financing, a rule of appointment, a corrector, and a condition that ends or reverts it, so that each can answer the withdrawal question of the guardian test ({{guardian}}), and every body whose findings or decisions have force carries a full inbound row-sum certificate ({{certificate}}(2)), recomputed on a schedule from the data of the disclosure registry. Three funds are fed by formula from the levy below: a *service fund* for provision, a *correction fund* for standard-setting, evaluation, disclosure, the chamber and the appeal, each by its own formula line, and an *execution fund* for execution and custody. The rules body is financed by treaty contributions outside the levy, so that the body that sets the formula receives nothing from it.

**Separation rule.** No body both revises the levy formula and appoints evaluators, and neither of those executes findings. Each has its own row-sum certificate. Supermajorities and the chamber's consent apply to the slow loops, amendment and the formula; the fast loop, restricting a specific capability, stays fast, with review after the fact, because every veto point adds delay, and the robustness margin of {{tempo}} depends on it.

**Monocentric by function, nested by option.** Each function is still held by one body. That is a separation of powers at one scale, monocentric function by function, and not polycentricity in the sense of overlapping jurisdictions at several scales that adjust to one another, which is a different candidate in Section 12.1; "many hands" applies inside evaluation only. The compact can instead be nested: each function held at national, regional and planetary levels, above a common floor of admission, custody and disclosure that no level may lower, with mutual recognition of certificates between levels, and with the fast loop placed at the level where the capability is held. Nesting adds redundancy of correction and local knowledge. It also multiplies the bodies whose certificates must hold and the seams through which a party under assessment can play one level against another, every level is a further conversion target, and the common floor must then be entrenched at every level. Nested governance of a planetary commons is itself an open problem (Stern 2011). The requirements of Section 9 constrain functions, not the number of centres; the choice is open.

**The chamber.** It is a corrector, and Section 4 applies to it. Its members are drawn by a rule no participant controls, they rotate, its inbound row sum is certified like any evaluator's, and its removal conditions are in the table. The regress of guardians ends not at a trusted body but at node-by-node certificates that many parties can recompute from public data; what remains of the regress falls on standard-setting, which has its own row, and on disclosure, without which no certificate can be recomputed.

**Injection is the injector's violation.** Row sums count conversion into an evaluator from the party it assesses ({{certificate}}(2); {{rivalverify}}), so an assessed party could push an evaluator's row sum above one by sending it funds, staff or data, and so strip its findings of executive force; the same move repeated could manufacture the certificate failures that trigger dissolution. The compact closes both. Conversion into an evaluator from a party it assesses, outside the evaluator's disclosed channels, is a violation by that party; it does not suspend findings against that party; the assessment is reassigned to an evaluator of different lineage; and certificate failures attributable to a party under assessment count toward no end condition.

**Recusal, and the clock.** A participant under assessment does not vote on revisions of the purpose under which it is assessed. Assessments are opened by the evaluation network on stated evidence and lapse if not concluded within their deadline. The deadline stops while the assessed party withholds access, data or personnel that the assessment requires, the time withheld is recorded as evidence, and interim measures confined to the capability at issue remain available on the evidence already in hand, so delay by the assessed cannot make an assessment lapse.

**Held-out metrics.** A held-out metric published at registration is no longer held out: it becomes a target (Section 5.4). Metrics are committed in advance by a cryptographic hash and revealed only at evaluation, so that anyone can check that the metric was fixed beforehand and no one can train on it.

**Disclosure.** Certificates are only as good as the data on conversion into correctors, and omission is the cheapest capture: a channel left unreported counts for nothing in a row sum. The disclosure registry keeps mandatory, audited records of funding, staff, data and access flowing into every corrector, from every party, the drafting parties included, with a sanction on the omitting party above its gain from omission ({{blind}}(3)). Without it the certificates of the design could not be recomputed by anyone, so it is the layer on which every other certificate rests, and it gets what every corrector gets: separate funding, an audit of a different lineage, and its own certificate.

**The levy formula is itself a prize.** The levy's *base* is wider than the activity the compact constrains: total compute, or treaty shares set by economic size, with a surcharge on frontier compute above a threshold. A base confined to frontier compute would shrink under exactly the stopping (E2) and slowing (E3) the compact exists to be able to impose, and would make its correctors stakeholders in the activity they constrain; the wider base does not shrink, while the surcharge still prices the frontier. The *correction fund* is indexed to the coverage each hazard class requires, the $d^\ast$ of {{corrrate}}, not to the field's capability and not to perceived harm, so that correctors do not become stakeholders in capability growth and do not lapse after a recovery. Because $d^\ast$ is computed from measured conversion, payers gain by moving influence into channels that are not measured, and correctors who received the fund would gain by overstating it. It is therefore estimated, as a conservative upper bound, by a body that receives none of the correction fund, such as the rules body, whose financing lies outside the levy; checked against direct measurements that do not depend on the conversion data, the correlation of misses on common test sets and the catch rate of planted defects at fixed human hours; and allowed to fall by at most a registered rate per period, so that an apparent recovery lowers the fund only slowly. The formula is fixed for a registered period; revision needs the supermajority and the chamber, and revisers are separated from beneficiaries and carry row-sum certificates, because by {{influence}} the formula is a conversion target.

**Frontier contributions by formula.** In a common frontier, cooperation is an equilibrium only while the pooled effort stays above a threshold that rises steeply with the value of winning ({{commonfrontn}}), and effort in the pool is a good shared by every member, which members choosing one by one under-supply (S10). Contributions to the common frontier are therefore paid by formula from the same base as the levy, not subscribed by each participant.

**Providers: voice without vote.** Providers earn revenue and contracts by usefulness and have no rights to appoint evaluators, set thresholds, exclude entrants or raise their cost of entry, or amend the rules. They have voice: notice and comment on every rule and standard, technical input to evaluation, and standing to contest a finding through the appeal, with their own conversion into every body disclosed. The experience of commons is that users excluded from revising their rules withhold local knowledge and compliance, and here the strongest of them also hold an exit option (Ostrom 1990); the result of Section 4 is that a party able to revise its own assessment captures it ({{influence}}), and in this field the regulated are the largest conversion targets the theory describes. The compact takes the second risk as the larger and draws the line at voice without vote. The tension is real, and it does not end at the line: a voice that is heard more when its holder is larger is itself a channel of conversion, which disclosure and the row sums have to count. The chamber, the appeal and the open channels of comment are where the tension is managed, not dissolved.

**End conditions.** If coverage falls below the participation threshold, or a stated number of the functions' certificates fail for a stated period for reasons not attributable to any party under assessment, the compact dissolves into its separately held functions, each continuing under its own rule. Protections against unilateral domination are entrenched. Every power that confers authority states what would end it ({{guardian}}).

**Against Ostrom's principles.** Boundaries (capability thresholds and hazard classes), monitoring and graduated sanctions are present. Proportionality of benefits and costs is partial: the largest payers have no vote, and strong types take part only for shares near their race payoffs ({{participate}}). Collective choice is answered by voice without vote. Conflict resolution is central, through one appeal, and neither cheap nor local, which nesting would answer. Recognition by an outside authority has no analogue at the scale of the planet; the host jurisdiction's first execution is the design's only link to the sovereignty of its participants. Nesting is the option above. The principles were drawn from commons that lasted at smaller scales (Ostrom 1990), and their transfer to a global commons is itself in question (Stern 2011).

**Other provisions.** A charter with three purposes: useful provision for all participants' populations; protection against O1 and O2; effective correction. Membership open to every state and provider above a capability threshold on non-discriminatory terms, participation made individually rational by access to shared services, a share of the dividend and capacity building ({{participate}}). Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against unilateral domination kept apart (Section 10.3). Baseline access to useful services with continuity guarantees; portable interfaces, escrow of systems providing essential services, and support for each participant to audit, operate and replace them. Narrow E1 first, for decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty: human authorization and a minimum time for deliberation for decisions on the use of force and on nuclear command, drilled parallel capacity, an evidence channel independent of AI, and doctrine open to mutual inspection; broad E1 as the scope (Section 11.3). Admission constraints common to every system, before consequential deployment, internal deployment included, for divergences with common costs and a positive tax, with decision policies retained by performance only inside them; for masked classes, admission by capability threshold with control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation ({{masked}}). Commonly funded, openly published alignment and control research. Pace: no building of the decisive class while readiness is unmet; capability thresholds by evaluation, and input thresholds in effective compute that tighten with algorithmic progress. Graduated remedies: notice; restriction of a specific capability; suspension of specific deployments; never suspension of essential services; emergency powers narrow, time-limited, reviewable and stating what would end them. Bounded competition continues inside: providers compete on usefulness, cost and reliability, and jurisdictions keep plural local purposes.

### Hard cases

**An adverse finding against a weaker participant.** Pending appeal, only the capability at issue may be restricted; essential services continue through alternative providers; the appellant sees the evidence under confidentiality, the panel decides within the deadline, and compensation follows if the finding is overturned. The power to suspend could become containment; the safeguards are the narrow scope of interim measures, the burden of evidence on the compact, and the appellant's standing.

**An adverse finding against the leading provider.** Suspending it could interrupt services for everyone, so formally equal procedures would be unequal in effect. The share cap, escrowed systems and funded standby capacity mean that restricting the leader's hazardous capability need not stop services. The author's book gives the rule for this case: the greater the power, the stricter the regulation it must receive, because it can shape everyone's conditions (力愈大者，受调当愈严，以其能塑众人之境也) [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14 第十四章]. The compact's version is that the leader's row in every certificate is weighted by its share, since the return to influence rises with the square of share ({{influence}}). Execution falls first to the leader's host jurisdiction. If that fails, what remains is the coercive restriction of inputs to the decisive class, the same form whose endorsement Section 8.3 withholds. The design does not settle it: it enters only as the candidate of Section 12.1, under the conditions stated there, with its escalation risk counted in $\Theta$, and the theory does not derive that its net effect is positive. Where neither consent nor such a restriction is available, the case is the hard case of Section 8.3. This is the hardest case, and the design does not dissolve it. It reduces the asymmetry of dependence that would otherwise make enforcement against the strongest impossible, and it gives the body that would execute a restriction a financing, an appointment and a certificate that do not run through the party restricted.

**A coalition of insiders.** Rival elites agree on mutual secrecy and immunity while weakening public correction. Four channels answer to people outside the coalition: the chamber, protected reporting, evaluators funded by formula, and publication of findings by default after a fixed delay. The disclosure registry makes the coalition's own conversion visible, and omission is the violation.

**An unjustified restriction.** The compact is used to contain a developing participant. It has standing to challenge, the burden is on the compact to show evidence of hazard, restrictions carry sunsets, and the compact has obligations to build capacity.

**Succession and withdrawal.** A change of leadership triggers review, not suspension. A withdrawal by a large participant makes the constraints borne by the rest unilateral and can trigger further exits; the participation threshold and the conditions of market access are what keep coverage above it (Section 9, "coverage can unravel"). Because the decisive class was not built and benefits and knowledge were diffused, a withdrawing party starts any race far from decisive capability and with a lower value of winning.

**Loss of an essential service.** Redundancy, escrow and standby capacity carry the service.

Three things could make its hazard of breakdown lower than that of an agreement that keeps the contest underneath: shared benefits lower the value of winning ({{relaxtrust}}; {{share}}); keeping AI out of the strategic loops whose delegation would lock with near certainty is a strict equilibrium among actors who price in the lock ({{delegatelock}}); and its fallback is not a decisive race from parity ({{fallback}}). These are hypotheses about the design, not established results.

### Federations, at both orders

Federations meet both orders of Section 10.2 at once. Competition among member units for capital and residents can become a race to the bottom, the pressure of winning among members, and the strongest members can capture the centre, the selection of the selectors. At the first order, common minimum standards bind every member, and fiscal equalization gives members capacity they do not have to win, both widening the effective self of members that value their absolute payoff and supplying the $k_0$ of the loop gain; for members that act strategically, pooling raises the working self-model rather than only slowing the race ({{share}}(1)). At the second order, equal representation of small members and supermajorities for constitutional change keep the centre from being bought by the largest, a precedent for voting that does not follow compute. A global minimum tax on corporate profits is a recent instance of one rule for all, and of how coverage unravels: the largest participant's groups were exempted from two of its rules in recognition of that participant's own minimum-tax rules (EVIDENCE EV18), the coverage problem of Section 9's participation threshold arising at the participant whose coverage matters most. The same federal form can select for winning or for contribution; what decides is what it rewards.
