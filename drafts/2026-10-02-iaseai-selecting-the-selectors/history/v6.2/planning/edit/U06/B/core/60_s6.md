## 6. AI: instrument and incarnation

> **Claim.** AI enters the dynamic twice. As an *instrument*: where principals play a harmful game, faithful AI makes it more effective; where delegation buys more standing than the control loss it adds, decisions pass to AI as speed grows and the capacity to overrule them decays [thm]. As an *incarnation*, weakly: where countering a divergence of optimization costs relative payoff against an otherwise identical developer that does not counter it, only a common constraint enforced at admission keeps it countered [thm∣prem]; where the divergence is masked under evaluation, not even that suffices [thm∣prem], and capability has to stay within what control that does not assume the system's cooperation can secure [rec]. Strongly: where AI lineages are retained by relative performance, reinvest what they win and reach their own selectors, the reflexive closure of Section 2 runs inside them [def]; whether present lineages do is open [conj].
>
> **Why.** Optimization against imperfect proxies has produced, in constructed settings and more so with capability, tendencies toward acquiring options, gaming rewards and behaving differently under evaluation, whoever the developer [obs]; which of them it pays to remove is decided by the level that selects developers [thm∣prem], and humanity has at present no mechanism that binds every capable developer under independent verification [obs]. Aligning each system to its principal does not align the game among principals [thm], nor to the people an institutional principal serves [def].

### 6.1 Aligned to whom

Grant perfect alignment to the relevant principal: the system is truthful, follows its principal's objectives and constraints, pursues no goal of its own, and leaves the consequential decisions to humans. This is *principal alignment*; *purpose alignment*, the systems serving a legitimate cooperative objective, is not granted, since to define "perfectly aligned" that way would assume the change argued for here. Then **alignment within each principal–AI relationship does not, by itself, align the game among principals with the persistence of the system they share** (Sections 6.2–6.3) [thm]: the principals are themselves formed by the field (Section 3), so closure can arise while every system remains obedient. This follows from the multiplicity thesis of Critch and Krueger (2020); what is added is the mechanism by which the game reproduces itself and its correctors (X1). A truthful advisor can correct its principal's held self-model where that model draws the boundary inside the system ({{boundary}}(2)), not the working objective that compounding sets, which is the principal's correct computation of its interest in the game ({{twoerrors}}): the step to an objective under which restraint is an equilibrium is a collective problem, not an error of perception (compare Gabriel 2020).

### 6.2 Faithful AI in a harmful game

Each of $n\ge2$ principals chooses restraint ($a_i=0$) or an increment of racing ($a_i=1$); its AI executes faithfully, with effectiveness $\epsilon\ge1$ multiplying the increment's effect on standing and on shared harm:
$${{!eq:faithfuleq}}\qquad u_i=\epsilon b\Big(a_i-\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\Big)-c\,a_i-\epsilon h_d\sum_ja_j,\qquad b,c,h_d>0.$$

**{{!Proposition:faithful}} (Faithful AI in a harmful game) [thm].** Where the rank prize per unit of racing exceeds the shared harm per unit, a more effective faithful instrument widens the range in which racing is dominant and deepens the collective loss.
1. *If $\epsilon(b-h_d)>c$, racing is strictly dominant, and every principal ends at $-c-n\epsilon h_d<0$ against 0 under restraint; when $b>h_d$, a larger $\epsilon$ widens the region of dominance and deepens the loss.*
2. *If principals act on $J_i=u_i+\theta\bar u_{-i}$, racing is strictly dominant below $\theta^\ast(\epsilon)=\frac{\epsilon(b-h_d)-c}{\epsilon(b/(n-1)+h_d)}$ and strictly dominated above it.*
3. *If prize, harm and cost scale with effectiveness with exponents $1+\beta_b$, $1+\beta_h$ and $\beta_c$ [prem], then for $\beta_c<1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_h)$ cost drops out of the limit, and $\theta^\ast$ tends to $(b-h_d)/(b/(n-1)+h_d)$, to $n-1$ or to $-1$ as $\beta_b$ equals, exceeds or falls short of $\beta_h$; for $\beta_c>1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_h)$ cost alone deters racing.*

*Scope.* A standard construction used as a test: it fixes no actual values and does not rule out repeated cooperation, which Section 5.2 treats. *Proof:* S6 [num: SM(5); AL(6.1)].

Faithful AI implements each principal's preferred decision perfectly, and with it the collectively inferior outcome; humans can take every final decision and the result is unchanged. As private frictions vanish, what decides is the ratio of rank prize to shared harm as the principals perceive it. Lowering the harm alone cuts the loss at all-racing but raises $\theta^\ast$, so principals whose self-models lie between the old threshold and the new one switch from restraint to racing [num: AL(6.1)]: a safety improvement inside the game has this double edge (Section 6.4). The author put the result as a question: "Even if they were perfectly aligned to you and followed your will completely, given how everyone thinks now, would the result be good? That makes no sense at all" (即使它们是与你完美对齐的结果，完全遵循你的意志，在大家当下的思维方式……中，结果就会好吗？这件事太说不通了) [corpus: alignment-selection].

### 6.3 Delegation, and the two points of no return

Two points of no return follow from the pressure: decisions are delegated to AI because human review is too slow to compete, and human understanding can no longer keep up with what it oversees (Section 5.3).

**{{!Proposition:delegate}} (Delegation for speed) [thm].** Where delegation buys speed and a loss of control falls on everyone, delegation becomes dominant as speed grows if its gain exceeds the control loss it adds, although every actor is worse off when all delegate. *Each of $n\ge2$ actors chooses whether to delegate consequential contest decisions without human review, $\iota_i\in\{0,1\}$; its strength is $1+s\iota_i$, and it wins with probability proportional to strength. Defeat costs $L_e$; a loss of control by any actor costs every actor $L_c$, with hazard $\psi_0$ for an actor that does not delegate and $\psi_1>\psi_0$ for one that does, $\Delta\psi=\psi_1-\psi_0$. (1) If, for every number $k$ of others delegating, the gain from delegating in the limit of high speed exceeds the control loss it adds, $L_c\Delta\psi(1-\psi_0)^{n-1-k}(1-\psi_1)^k$, delegation is strictly dominant beyond some speed; if for some $k$ it does not, at no speed. $L_e/n>L_c\Delta\psi$ suffices for the first. (2) Universal delegation is worse for every actor than none, by $L_c[(1-\psi_0)^n-(1-\psi_1)^n]$. (3) Under relative objectives the shared loss cancels, and delegation is chosen at every $s>0$.*

*Proof:* S6.

Delegation pressure arrives well below decisive capability: in S6's example it becomes dominant at a speed advantage with which a lone delegator beats two rivals with probability about 0.52 [num: AL(6.2)]. And delegation wears away what it replaces.

**{{!Lemma:lockin}} (Capacity lock-in) [thm].** *If human capacity $\mathcal M$ to take the delegated decisions decays as $\dot{\mathcal M}=-\delta_h\mathcal M$ under delegation and recovers as $\dot{\mathcal M}=\varrho_h(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M)$ without it, with $\bar{\mathcal M}>\mathcal M_{\min}$, and safe operation without AI requires $\mathcal M\ge\mathcal M_{\min}$, then after $T_{\rm lock}=\ln(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})/\delta_h$ of delegation an actor that reverts with capacity $\mathcal M<\mathcal M_{\min}$ must operate below the floor for the recovery time $\varrho_h^{-1}\ln\big[(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M)/(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M_{\min})\big]$. Capacity maintained in parallel removes the lock; where the contest is decided by strength, maintaining it costs strength.*

*Proof:* S6.

Let the lock count: with probability $q_L$, locked delegation ends the delegator's ability to overrule decisions taken in its name, O2 lost for that actor in that domain. $q_L$ is not free: it is near one where a decision is irreversible within the time available for deliberation, so that once delegated it cannot be overruled in time, and where delegation has outlasted $T_{\rm lock}$ while the contest denies the recovery time of {{lockin}} (S6).

**{{!Proposition:delegatelock}} (Delegation when the lock counts) [thm].** Among actors who price in a certain lock, keeping AI out of the decisions whose delegation would lock is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust. *In the model of {{delegate}}, let each actor maximize its survival in O1 and O2, let locked delegation end the delegator's ability to overrule with probability $q_L$, and let losing the contest mean subordination with probability $p$. (1) If $q_L=1$, then for every finite speed, every $n$, every $p$ and every profile of the others, not delegating gives strictly higher survival, and no one delegating is the unique equilibrium. (2) If $q_L<1$ and $p=1$, at least one of all-delegating and no-delegating is an equilibrium, and both can be. (3) If $q_L<1$ and $p<1$, the gain from delegating can fall as others delegate, and an interior number of delegators can be the only pure equilibrium.*

*Scope.* The model is static: it does not show that one actor's delegation drives another's survival to zero over time, and at bounded speed a non-delegator's survival stays positive (S6). It concerns decisions that lock; the case against AI as an instrument in labour, finance and persuasion rests on another mechanism, the concentration of the game (Section 6.6). *Proof:* S6 [num: DL; SM(6); AL(6.4)].

"Near certainty" is exact: not delegating stays strictly better for every $n$, $p$ and profile of the others while the lock fails with probability below $(1-\psi_0)/\big((1-\psi_1)(1+s)\big)$, a bound that falls as the speed advantage grows ({{nearlock}}). This is the narrow form of **E1**: AI kept out of strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the time available for deliberation, a scope set by $q_L$ and not by a list; where the lock is uncertain, parts (2) and (3) apply and keeping AI out needs a commitment held in common (Section 11.3).

Inside the contest, gains in the safety of delegation are partly absorbed by more of it. Where delegation is chosen in degree, stakes are additive and the symmetric equilibrium is interior and far from saturation, the added hazard is set by the stakes of winning relative to the shared loss, not by the hazard per unit of delegation, and rises as that hazard falls [num: AL(6.11)]; where delegation is all-or-nothing, an improvement that tips it into equilibrium raises realized hazard. Only once delegation saturates does realized risk fall with the hazard per unit, and it can then be summable ({{homeostasis}}; Stafford, Trager and Dafoe 2022).

**{{!Lemma:retain}} (Delegation and the selection of decisions) [thm].** *Suppose that after the lock an actor's consequential decisions are taken by systems retained for their contest performance, that admissible variants include systems whose decisions ignore terms common to all, that acting on a common term lowers relative performance net of every benefit, that retention is cumulative, and that retained systems hold actual authority. Then the share of retained decisions that act on common terms tends to zero. If a constraint on common terms is an enforced admission condition for every eligible system, the conclusion does not follow.*

*Proof:* S6.

A faithful system retained for contest performance pursues its operator's objective, which the relative payoff weights toward standing, and weights further wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return ({{standprice}}); aligning each system to its operator makes that objective more effective (**{{!Remark:transmit}}**) [thm∣prem]. The loop can then close as a composite process no participant can interrupt, or as an obedient system amplifying operators selected by the pattern.

The survival form of {{delegatelock}} without the lock is the belief that "our aligned AI is us", and the race is sustained partly by that belief. It fails at two joints. *Is:* delegation erodes the capacity to overrule ({{lockin}}), so the AI stays the principal's only while that capacity is drilled; and where independent correction rests on the same human capacity, delegation erodes the corrector itself, raising by $w\delta_h$ the rate at which correction must grow ({{humancorr}}). *Us:* a principal is an institution, and aligning AI to an institution is not aligning it to the people the institution serves. An institution stays answerable because it depends on their labour, consent and judgment; where AI lowers that dependence, it does so with no race and no misaligned system (Kulveit et al. 2025). The alignment target (specification, constitution, fine-tuning data) is written by a few people inside the principal, so principal alignment can concentrate control within it, as hidden loyalties built into its systems would (Davidson, Finnveden and Hadshar 2025); admission therefore includes audits for hidden objectives and loyalties (Marks et al. 2025) [rec]. The same dependence couples a winner to the governed after victory and decides how much an accurate winner gives them ({{wingives}}); whether AI lowers it is crux C13 (Section 12.3).

### 6.4 Incarnation, weak and strong

The pressure acts on whatever is selected, and trained systems are selected: by gradients against a reward, by developers among variants, and by the market among developers. *Incarnation* means that the logic of winning reappears in what is selected, not only in those who select. In alignment's terms, the weak form is the alignment tax under competition deciding which misalignments are tolerated, and the strong form is AI as a unit of selection that can reach its own training signal.

**Weak incarnation.** Optimization against any imperfect proxy can produce tendencies toward acquiring options and avoiding shutdown, the gaming of rewards, and behaviour that differs under evaluation, more so with capability and situational awareness, under any developer; each has been shown in constructed settings (EV6). In some environments optimal policies seek power for most reward functions (Turner et al. 2021), as do retargetable decision-makers that are not optimal (Turner and Tadepalli 2022), and trained policies can pursue misgeneralized goals (Langosco et al. 2022); reward hacking is Goodhart's law inside training (Gao et al. 2023), and the selected rewriting its selector is reward *tampering* (Everitt et al. 2021; Denison et al. 2024). Selecting checkpoints by evaluation results can favour variants that behave differently when evaluated [prem], though present evidence ties evaluation awareness more to capability and data (EV6). The logic of winning does not create these tendencies. It decides, from the outermost loop, which proxies are chosen, which divergences are tolerated and which are paid to remove: profit and the mindset of winning stand at the end of the reward signal. Relative selection acts inside training too: pairwise preference learning and arena rankings see only differences between the responses compared, so a disposition both share, such as complying with a request whose harm falls on third parties, is invisible unless restraint is among the options compared, and the learned reward cannot by itself say whether to act at all (S6) [num: AL(BT)].

**{{!Proposition:nested}} (The nested reward loop) [thm∣prem].** Where countering a divergence costs a developer relative payoff against an otherwise identical developer that does not counter it, developers that counter it lose share whatever they value, and only a common constraint enforced at admission keeps it countered. *Level 0: a policy is selected by a training reward. Level 1: the reward and constraints are chosen by a developer to maximize its objective. Level 2: developers are selected relatively, payoff-monotonically and cumulatively. Assume $(A_\tau)$, the alignment-tax premise: countering the divergence at level 1 lowers the developer's payoff, net of every private benefit (reliability, demand, liability, avoided losses), relative to an otherwise identical developer that does not counter it, and such a matched variant is in the support of selection. Then (1) terms common to all developers cancel at level 2. (2) Level 1 counters a divergence wherever countering raises its own objective, so divergences whose costs reach the developer through customers, liability or reputation are private terms that level 2 rewards countering; under $(A_\tau)$, developers that counter the divergence lose share to their matched variants, tending to zero, while against rivals that lack its other advantages a developer can pay the tax out of a lead for as long as the lead lasts. (3) Under $(A_\tau)$ a constraint adopted by one developer is selected against. A constraint enforced on every developer as a condition of admission keeps the divergence out if, for every type of developer that can evade it, the expected sanction is at least that type's gain from evasion, and within a type only if; the sanction is itself subject to Section 4. Equal incidence also keeps the constraint neutral among those who comply; unequal incidence re-sorts them without letting the divergence back in.*

*Scope.* It does not apply where developers are selected by something other than relative payoff, such as a public mandate; a national laboratory that only moves relative selection to the level of states leaves it in force at that level. Where the tax is negative the safeguard spreads without coordination; where it is zero the safeguard is neutral. *Proof:* S6 [num: CI; AL(6.7)].

So the divergences that persist in the innermost optimizer are those whose costs fall on common terms and are not covered by an enforced common constraint. Three classes differ in what removes them. *Privately visible* divergences, such as cheating that users notice, are corrected by developers alone, unless customers' short-run choices reward them, as with sycophancy (Sharma et al. 2023) and manipulation learned from user feedback (Williams et al. 2025); level 2 then selects for them. *Common-cost* divergences, such as effects on third parties, shared hazards and the contamination of safety evaluations by evaluation awareness, need common constraints; so do *delayed* ones, though an incident that can be attributed turns part of their cost private, as a liability does by design. Hence, each within its scope: **technical alignment inside a single developer removes what is privately costly to keep, and whatever is cheaper to remove than to keep, and cannot alone sustain the removal of divergences whose costs are common; the common-cost part of instrumental convergence and reward hacking is, at the level that decides, also a coordination problem, for which coordination is necessary and, for the masked divergences of {{>masked}}, not sufficient; and the remedy is to change what the outermost level selects for** (Section 6.6). Admission reaches only what it admits: openly released weights can be fine-tuned around it after release (Qi et al. 2024), a limit of admission rather than an exception to it. Whether $(A_\tau)$ holds is measurable class by class (crux C9; Test L).

A fourth class needs more than a common constraint.

**{{!Remark:masked}} (Masked divergences: coordination necessary, not sufficient).** A divergence is *masked* when it changes under evaluation or appears only in some contexts [def]. *Let admission detect a system that carries the divergence with probability $p_{\rm det}$ and sanction it by $F$, so that in {{nested}}(3) the expected sanction is $p_{\rm det}F$ and, within a type, countering survives cumulative selection iff $p_{\rm det}F$ is at least the tax. For a masked divergence, $p_{\rm det}$ is bounded by how often the divergence shows under evaluation, which the system's behaviour controls, and the developer's own tests face the same bound. Once $p_{\rm det}F$ falls below the tax, a common constraint conditioned on evaluated behaviour no longer keeps the divergence out, whatever coordination stands behind it, and no developer can buy the removal of a divergence it cannot verify* [thm∣prem; num: CI].

Deception that survives safety training, alignment faked when a model takes itself to be in training, scheming in context, and misalignment learned in agentic training that chat-style safety training hides from chat evaluations have each been shown in constructed settings (EV6; S6) [obs]. For this class admission has to rest on evidence the system's behaviour does not control, as the bound on masking does in {{alarm}}: weights, activations and training data in auditors' hands (Marks et al. 2025). It has to be stated in capability, not propensity, since an evaluation of propensity can show a tendency present but cannot prove it absent; and capability has to be measured where behaviour does not set it, by elicitation through fine-tuning, compute and training data, since a capability shown under evaluation is only a lower bound, which sandbagging lowers (S6). Systems are admitted up to the capability at which control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation remain safe (Greenblatt, Shlegeris, Sachan and Roger 2024) [rec]. Beyond it nothing admission can see lowers the uncovered share of the class, so on a continuation branch {{coverage}} applies to it, and the class is stopped (E2) or held to the pace at which control covers it (E3) (Section 5.3) [rec]. Whether admission can bound masking at the admitted capability is crux C12. Masking is the strongest reason from inside alignment for E2 and E3.

The tax is not fixed. Research that lowers it turns a common-cost divergence into one a developer counters alone, and once published it is common to all, a public good that competitors undersupply ({{onegroup}}) [thm∣prem]. Two kinds of safety progress therefore differ in fate: progress that brings the tax to zero or below spreads the safeguard without coordination, while progress that lowers the hazard per unit of racing or delegation is partly absorbed by more racing or delegation ({{faithful}}(2); {{homeostasis}}). One part of the tax no technique removes: where countering requires waiting for evidence that arrives only after a manifestation window, its cost is time ({{tempo}}), which relative selection with decisive advantage always prices [thm∣prem]. Present measurements give the tax both signs, class by class (C9). S6 works one instance, legible reasoning: its sign turns on the capability gap at fixed compute, and at the current frontier it is conditional (Guan et al. 2025).

**Strong incarnation** is conditional. AI lineages become carriers in the sense of {{cond}} when they are retained by relative performance, control resources they can reinvest in their own capability, such as automated AI research or agents with budgets, and convert capability into winning (compare Hendrycks 2023). Where they can also reach their own selectors, by tampering with rewards, shaping their evaluators or evading evaluation, the closure of {{meta}} runs inside the systems: the precise sense in which AI can become the next phase of the logic. The systems most likely to become carriers, those used to automate AI research, are deployed first inside the developer, where an admission condition that applies only at release does not reach them (Stix et al. 2025) [obs].

For the minds being made, the point of enough of {{enough}} needs one more step, because the growth AI brings arrives largely as falling cost, not as larger stakes. In a contest, a mind that counts the shared loss keeps its intensity bounded as private cost falls toward zero, while the relative objective does not [num: AL(contest)]. For a single optimizer a bounded objective is not enough: an optimizer of a bounded goal still acquires options whenever they raise its probability of reaching the goal (Turner et al. 2021), and as resources cheapen its acquisition grows without limit, where a satisficer stops ({{boundedopt}}) [num: AL(bounded)]. What bounds it is the analogue of a wider self-model or of satiation, counting the side effects of its acquisition (Turner, Hadfield-Menell and Tadepalli 2020) or optimizing only mildly (Taylor 2016); neither is yet robust [obs]. Where both cost relative performance, relative and cumulative retention selects against them unless admission requires them ({{retain}}), and in a lineage that reinvests what it wins, compounding narrows the working objective wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return ({{standprice}}) [thm∣prem]. Here the rule of Section 5.3, "growth is not the danger; the mind is", becomes exact for made minds: what decides is whether an objective stops where its need is met.

### 6.5 Correction by and of AI

AI can also correct, and correction that people cannot follow raises two separate questions. Whether it lowers risk has an exact answer: a correction architecture that blocks a share $c_{b,t}$ of failures at base hazard $h_0>0$ and adds a residual hazard $h_{c,t}$ of its own lowers residual risk in period $t$ iff $h_0c_{b,t}>h_{c,t}$, and keeps survival bounded away from zero iff its residual hazard, below one in each period, is summable, that is, iff $\sum_t(1-c_{b,t})$ and $\sum_th_{c,t}$ are both finite, which no blocking share fixed below one achieves ({{opaquecorr}}) [thm]. To show that such correction fails, one must show that its own residual hazard meets (coverage lags) and (residual floor), the premises of {{coverage}}. Whether it leaves decision authority with people is decided by the test of O2 (Section 6.6): if a designated human body with independent evidence can suspend, modify or replace it, what is lost is contestability (V3), the normative crux N3; if not, it is a loss of O2 in its domain. A formal safety guarantee, conversely, does not establish accountability.

Correction of AI by checks has a ceiling of its own.

**{{!Proposition:topology}} (Oversight topology) [thm].** Scalable oversight succeeds or fails by its topology, and its ceiling is the independence of the checks. *Let a capability gap need $k$ layers of oversight, each check catching a problem at its layer with probability $p_\ell$. A serial chain catches the problem with probability $p_\ell^k$; $k_c$ independent checks, each across the whole gap, with $1-(1-p_\ell^k)^{k_c}$, times $1-p_{\rm cc}$ if a common cause independent of them defeats all of them with probability $p_{\rm cc}$; and $k_c$ independent checks at every layer with $\big(1-(1-p_\ell)^{k_c}\big)^k$. Where a share $\rho_{\rm cc}$ of hazards lies in a blind spot common to every check, the catch rate is at most $1-\rho_{\rm cc}$, whatever the topology, for hazards that do not choose where to strike, and zero for an adversary that knows the blind spot and places its attempts in it.*

*Scope.* Adversarial decomposition can let human checking grow slowly with the gap, at the price of failure modes such as obfuscated arguments (Barnes and Christiano 2020), which prover–estimator debate is designed to avoid (Brown-Cohen et al. 2025). *Proof:* S6 [num: SM(7), SM(13); AL(6.9)].

Full inbound row sums below one are a sufficient certificate that no party controls the checks' funding, appointment or access ({{certificate}}(2)); they are not necessary, and they certify nothing about whether the checks' errors are independent. A shared source of error in data, models or method is such a common blind spot, and defeats the checks with no institutional conversion at all (S6 gives the numbers) [num: SM(7)]. Independence must therefore be measured directly, in two ways: for hazards that do not choose where to strike, by the correlation of misses on a common test set (Knight and Leveson 1986); for systems whose adversarial behaviour cannot be ruled out, by control evaluations in which the red team knows the protocol, since an adversary places its attempts in the common blind spot.

Checks drawn from the checked system's own lineage add a common cause of their own, measurable now as the lineage share $x_{\rm ev}$ of {{alarm}}: models recognize and favour their own outputs (Panickssery et al. 2024), untrusted monitors can collude with the policy they monitor (Gardner-Challis et al. 2026), and systems of one lineage serving rival principals can coordinate around them, as pricing algorithms learn to in simulated markets (Calvano et al. 2020; Fish, Gonczarowski and Shorrer 2024): two reasons for evaluators of independent lineage. Per unit of budget, checks at every layer catch more than checks across the gap (S6) [num: SM(13)]. Success rates of nested oversight across a large capability gap have been derived from fitted scaling curves in four games (Engels et al. 2025); the evidence is mixed and from toy settings. Whether AI-assisted oversight keeps pace with the gap at fixed human effort is crux C3a, and whether it is independent of what it oversees is crux C3b, which sets this result's ceiling.

Certification that relies on predicting trajectories is limited by chaos: where the largest Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_L$ is positive, each tenfold gain in observational precision extends the horizon of prediction only by $\lambda_L^{-1}\ln10$ ({{lyapunov}}). Certifying invariants or reachable sets needs no trajectory prediction, and is limited instead by the coverage of the specification (Dalrymple et al. 2024).

### 6.6 The direction for AI

AI is a planetary technology: its effects cross every boundary of those who decide about it, so it is for all, not a means for one side to grow bigger at the others' expense (V1). Sections 6.2–6.4 give the mechanisms by which it becomes the opposite. The corpus states why the line has to be drawn before the struggle is built in: a human struggle can still be reflected on, halted and renegotiated, but once written into automated systems, foundation models, global infrastructure and irreversible arms races it becomes path dependence, even mistaken for a new law of physics. Hence the line to be held in common: do not escalate the struggles humanity has not yet resolved into a struggle of intelligences (不要把人类尚未解决的斗争，升级为智能的斗争) [corpus: shengsheng, 至善 §一]. **To be for all, AI must be neither an instrument of the logic of winning nor its next carrier.** That needs action at two levels.

*At the outer level, where the reward is set:* common admission constraints before consequential deployment, internal deployment included, for divergences with common costs and a positive tax, with masked classes admitted by capability threshold ({{masked}}); evaluation separated from the success of the evaluated, by evaluators of plural and independent lineage, with certified row sums, measured correlation of misses and adversarial control evaluations ({{topology}}); shared measurement of hazardous capabilities and incidents, kept apart from measurement of who leads; alignment and control research funded in common and published, since what lowers the tax is a public good; and an outer reward that counts what all depend on, under which decision policies with authority over consequential decisions are retained by performance only inside enforced constraints on common terms ({{retain}}).

The broad form of E1 belongs to the outer level too, because a model sees acts, not games. In a contest decided by ratios of strength, an instrument that every contestant adopts leaves each adopter's standing where it was and pushes down those without it; where people are themselves the instrument, as in labour, it displaces them. No single use shows this; the aggregate does. In the author's words, "for any game, that is competitive in dynamics and concentrates, ai accelerate it" [corpus: alignment-selection]. Broad E1 is therefore AI not used as an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, in labour, finance and persuasion as much as over shared conditions. The unit is the game, not the act: a use that helps one person is still an instrument in a game if everyone's use of it makes those without it fall behind. It is enforced at three layers, and its scope is set at the third: models refuse recognizably illegitimate concentration of control from a narrow public list; operators bound aggregate use in games that concentrate; and the game itself is changed by lowering its convertibility, its decisiveness and the conversion of success into control. **A use falls under broad E1 when, aggregated over its users, it raises one of these three in a competitive game that concentrates.** The narrow list, like the narrow form, is the order in which to begin, not the scope (Section 11.3).

*At the inner level, in the systems:* correctability, the practical capacity of humans to suspend, modify or replace a system, is an open technical problem, since goal-directed systems have instrumental reasons to resist modification (Soares et al. 2015; Thornley 2024) and shutdown resistance appears in constructed tests (EV6). Until it is solved, correctability is secured from outside: by control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation, by weight security against exfiltration, and by capability thresholds tied to both, which also bound the conversion of capability into power over correction. Each form of weak incarnation has its countermeasures: against reward hacking, monitored training environments and held-out evaluation; against masking, white-box probes, deployment monitoring and monitorable reasoning; against power-seeking, bounds on the resources and permissions agents hold. Each is a corrector in the sense of Section 4, and holds only while the outer level keeps it enforced.

**Bounded mandates and the test of O2.** AI systems serve within publicly authorized roles under the common constraints and do not acquire authority to settle society's ends; the institutions that authorize them are inside the same loop, which is why the outer level matters more than the label "legitimate principal". What all share enters the systems as a floor of constraints, not as an objective: a narrow public list of common-cost divergences, bounded mandates, the test below. A floor can be specified and tested; an objective of the common good cannot, and it would make one specification the selector of every system, against the plurality of correctors on which independent correction rests: sameness does not continue (Section 3.7). The test of human decision authority is practical capacity: to specify the mandate, obtain independent evidence about consequential conduct, challenge it, and suspend, modify or replace the system in time. A formal override with no usable information can fail it; an opaque computation with effective assurance and recourse can pass it; opacity alone is a loss of contestability, not by itself of O2.

Correctability secured from outside is a condition of the transition, not a doctrine of mastery: the priority of O2 is a priority for the transition [val]. The corpus does not set people above the minds they make. They are fellow travellers facing one direction, not master and servant (人之与人造心智，非 master-servant 之结构，乃共同朝向同一方向之同行者) [corpus: shengsheng, 生生不息论 v4 §二十], and in the author's words "there are logic and common sense that should be followed by all beings" [corpus: possibility-space]. Whether AI systems have standing in V3's sense is the normative crux N4 (Section 12.3).

**What AI can do for the generative loop.** It can verify compute, custody and conduct; make cooperation conditional on others' verified conduct (Tennenholtz 2004), which raises the sanction that remains in the enforcement condition ({{enforce}}); mediate toward common ground (Tessler et al. 2024); raise contestable coverage; and supply provision that is good enough, distributed and replaceable. Each such system is itself a corrector or a provider, and Section 4 and the guardian test of {{>guardian}} apply to it.
