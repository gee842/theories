## 0. Why this theory

**A prediction, for coordination.** A good theory predicts. This one predicts that, on the present trajectory, the logic of winning carries the participants toward an outcome none of them would choose. In the models of Part I, wherever racing is each actor's best response, each ends worse off than under joint restraint, and the more decisive the advantage, the more of what they contest is consumed in contesting it. If the hazards of capability outgrow the correction that can contest them, as Section 5.3 states and Section 12 treats as the central crux, the same logic endangers whoever would win, since a winner escapes only by ceasing to grow what outgrows correction. When such an outcome approaches and the participants still cannot coordinate to avoid it, the obstacle is first one of understanding: the prediction has to be shared before coordination becomes possible. The theory is written to make that possible, stating the dynamic precisely enough that each participant can check it for itself, in the hope of a prediction that, once shared, helps to make itself false (Section 11.2).

**Survival as the minimum.** Two outcomes are protected first: large-scale loss of life, and irreversible loss of human decision authority. They are not the whole of what matters; they are the minimum for everything else that does, because a loss of either cannot be corrected afterwards.

**The game, not the act.** The analysis judges games, not acts, and structures, not persons. A single use of AI can help one person and still be an instrument in a game whose aggregate concentrates control and makes those without it fall behind, in labour, finance and persuasion as much as in force: adopted by every contestant, an instrument leaves each adopter where it stood and pushes down whoever lacks it (Section 6.6). And where compounding prices standing, an actor that computes its interest correctly weighs standing above what all share (Section 3.5): the error is in the game, and so is the remedy. Responsibility differs with knowledge, discretion and power, and structural explanation does not erase it; but the object of the theory is the game: what it selects, and how that selection can be changed.

**The meaning is affirmative.** We are not only our separate lives: we are the larger process of becoming, going on through us, and that is good. Decay and renewal belong to its growth [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-10-02]. The theory therefore opposes one logic, not growth, competition, technology or any people: the logic by which a part counts only itself and forgets the body it belongs to, as a cancer does, which grows too, but exhausts the body that bears it (Section 5.3). To oppose that logic is to keep the future open for every participant, the minds now being made among them.

## 1. The question and the argument

### 1.1 What is being decided

AI development is pursued, in its leading participants' own declarations, as a contest for decisive advantage through acceleration (Section 7.3), inside institutions whose conditions of evaluation and correction can themselves be reshaped by competitive success. Can continuing along this path credibly deliver safety and a desirable future? If not, what change of course is warranted, and how can it be made?

Behind these questions is a puzzle: actors who would each prefer joint restraint, and who can see the danger, keep racing, and the institutions that could restrain them weaken as the danger grows. The answers are mechanisms, each stated in a model, and they meet in one question, the organizing question of the paper: *can restraint remain incentive-compatible when success changes both the gains from defection and the institutions capable of correcting it?*

### 1.2 The argument in one page

1. **The conditions of winning (Section 2).** Where whatever can gain or lose share (firms, states, research programmes, practices, trained AI systems: *carriers*) is selected by relative success, what is won converts into the capacity to win again, and capacity converts decisively into winning, the carriers are under a selection pressure of winning. Near equal standing, with the prize and conduct fixed, the conditions multiply into one structural loop gain, and leads compound where it exceeds one, and in the contests computed where a lead discourages the follower, already below it. Sharing and diffusion lower the gain in every channel; provision not won in the contest lowers it where decisiveness rises less than in proportion to the stock, as with capacity; correction lowers it where decisiveness rises at least in proportion, as with rule support. Where success can also buy control over the selection itself (the rules, the correctors, the formation of minds, the language), the pressure closes on itself: the meta-attractor of winning. The conditions are interconnected, not a list, and bound to no substrate.
2. **One system (Section 3).** All carriers share one host and are coupled through it, with no outside. Relative selection cannot see what all share, neither the host's decline nor a constraint that costs all alike, and where coupling is real it favours the narrow self, a held self-model drawn inside the system that the coupling punishes. Looking ahead does not cure this: near equal standing and to first order, an actor weighs standing above what all share wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, with a fixed prize whenever the discounted loop gain is positive, and with a prize proportional to capacity, without provision or diffusion, exactly where leads compound. Each actor computes correctly; the game is wrong. Against failures that are global and irreversible, integration must be anticipated on an accurate model of the system: that is why understanding that we are one, one interconnected system with no outside, is a long-term requirement if growth and change are to continue.
3. **Selecting the selectors (Section 4).** Where influence over correctors can be kept by those who buy it, its return grows with the square of share, so it is selected in the largest carriers. Where AI lowers the cost of control, independent correction that starts certified and grows at a stated fraction of that rate keeps evaluation uncaptured; slower correction loses it wherever influence keeps pace and no per-unit cost floor binds; and correction that rests on human capacity eroded by delegation must grow faster. Reforms that leave conversion into the correctors above one are re-absorbed, and where capture precedes the evidence of harm, the host crosses a reachable floor before any averaged alarm fires.
4. **Acceleration and the three erosions (Section 5).** The conditions accelerate the contest and erode three means of restraint. *Enforcement:* restraint is enforceable only while each deviation's chance of becoming decisive stays below a threshold. The share of capacity that has to be won, which grows as the stakes outgrow provision beneath the game, moves both blades of the scissors: where leads compound it raises that chance, and where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return it narrows the objectives that set the threshold, while captured sanctioners and stakes that outgrow cooperation's margin over punishment lower the threshold further. Each arm holds in its own model, and the first two close together in one reduced model; that they close together in the world is conjecture C0. *Correction:* where contestable coverage falls ever further behind hazardous variety and failure is shared, survival tends to zero whoever is racing; an objective not satiated in the stakes has no point of enough: growth is not the danger; the mind is. *Shared understanding:* relative accounting silences talk between two rivals and multiplies a pair's conflict by $n/(n-2)$ among three or more, an effect that fades as actors multiply; blocs are a reading, not a result.
5. **Instrument and incarnation (Section 6).** Where principals play a harmful game, faithful AI makes it more effective, and where delegation buys more standing than the control loss it adds, decisions pass to AI as speed grows and the capacity to overrule decays. Weakly, the logic of winning reappears in the systems: the outermost loop decides which divergences of optimization are paid to remove, and where countering one costs a developer relative payoff against an otherwise identical developer, only a common constraint enforced at admission keeps it countered; for divergences masked under evaluation not even that suffices, and capability has to stay within what control that does not assume the system's cooperation can secure. Strongly and conditionally, AI lineages retained by relative performance that reinvest in their own capability and reach their own selectors are carriers themselves, first inside the developers that use them to build AI, where admission at release does not reach. A principal is not the people it serves, and aligning each system to its principal does not align the game among principals.
6. **The present trajectory (Section 7).** The thresholds of Part I turn on a few shared parameters, above all the share of capacity that has to be won, the convertibility of success into control, and decisiveness ({{>thresholds}}); the trajectory's dynamics are signatures of the conditions, with named gaps in the evidence. Where a deviation can reach a decisive position worth at least cooperation before it is answered, as the premise of the race supposes, repetition enforces only the restraint each would choose anyway, and the race as a collective course leaves everyone's protection after a win to the winner's objective. The premise therefore cannot justify that course; it is a reason to prepare common custody now, while leads are short.
7. **The alternatives and the requirements (Sections 8, 9).** Paths are compared by one standard, irreversible losses first, over one range of uncertainty that includes every path's own failure modes. An arrangement is judged not by its name but by which thresholds it moves, to which side and for whom; that those which constrain conduct and leave the thresholds where they were erode as stakes and speed grow, while those that move them for every capable actor persist, is a registered prediction. Where a race carrier can carry the host below its floor and no single rule-maker reaches every carrier, several requirements cannot be met by any actor alone.
8. **The generative alternative (Section 10).** The same reflexive structure can make contribution fund the conditions of further contribution: success earns reward, not more advantage in the same game and not control over its own assessment, and provision sits beneath the game, like trees. No lever makes the selected intensity zero while a positional prize remains, so that part has to be kept from forming or held in common. Where coverage is complete and correction is financed by formula, the generative order persists through what all share, not through its winners, and its form is derived function by function, not from an axis of centralization; whether it attracts is a conjecture. No better guardian is an answer; a structure in which error cannot grow without limit is.
9. **The transition and the judgment (Sections 11, 12).** Trust is an outcome of a staged transition, not its precondition, where the first steps need none, as narrow E1 among actors who price in a certain lock does; and a step escapes re-absorption only if it is held until its slow variable has crossed its threshold. Under the protection standard a change in what is selected is warranted in three tiers, sorted by the kind of justification each step has: steps that wait on no crux, and steps that stand until a named crux is measured favourable, taken now; stopping the decisive class, common custody and pace, prepared now and activated by a mechanism committed in advance on registered indicators; and whole designs, the coercive restriction of frontier inputs among them, compared and not settled.

The change does not abolish competition: bounded competition in products, science and service continues. It changes what winning confers.

### 1.3 Values and the protected outcomes

The argument rests on five normative commitments, stated so that a reader can reject one and see what follows.
- **V1** AI is a planetary technology: its effects and dependencies cross every boundary of those who decide about it, so it is for all, not a means for one side to grow bigger at the others' expense, and no actor's victory is an adequate criterion of its success. Planetary purpose does not require one culture, one operator or the end of local purposes.
- **V2** Two losses are protected first: large-scale loss of life (O1), and irreversible loss of human decision authority over consequential systems in a defined domain (O2).
- **V3** Those affected by an optimization process should keep a meaningful capacity to contest its direction. Whether AI systems are among those with such standing is left open as the normative crux N4 (Section 12.3); either way, the priority of O2 holds for the transition (Section 6.6).
- **V4** Authority is a productive capacity: it can coordinate long-horizon investment, guarantee useful services and act on shared purposes. Its design is a problem of mechanism in which purpose, competence, learning and misuse are all modelled (X1 gives the tradition the author draws on).
- **V5** Bounded competition is compatible with a generative order where the game does not concentrate control over shared conditions. A winner should be rewarded with something other than more advantage in the same game; recognition for contribution is such a reward, provided it does not convert into control (EV16).

Four outcomes are kept apart: O1 physical catastrophe and O2 irreversible loss of decision authority, protected first; O3 interruption of essential services, protected; and O4 the end of a particular organization's or government's tenure, not protected as such, so that "actor survival" cannot quietly protect incumbency against legitimate replacement. The values do not supply the formal results, and the results do not supply the values: the rejection of "the winner justifies all", and with it the judgment that the premise of the race cannot justify the race as a collective course, rests on V1–V3.

### 1.4 What is claimed, and what is not

The argument moves through three levels, and Parts I–IV follow them. A *mechanism* claim (Part I) says that under stated conditions a selection, capture, delay or incentive result follows, and needs a derivation, its scope and its counterexamples. A *diagnosis* (Part II) says that consequential parts of those conditions characterize the present trajectory, and needs dated evidence with its uncertainty. A *practical judgment* (Parts III, IV) says that a change in what is selected is warranted, and needs a stated standard, a comparison of credible paths and sensitivity to disputed premises. A practical judgment does not need a proof of certain extinction, and cannot follow from naming a possible danger: it must show that the danger is consequential, inadequately controlled on the present path, and better addressed by the proposed change than by credible alternatives. Minds enter in two roles: as what selection shapes, and as the only thing that can redesign the game from within, by understanding it.

**What is not claimed.** The theory does not claim that competition, growth, technology or any people are the danger; bounded competition in products, science and service continues. It does not claim that every path through a race ends at zero survival, that decisive advantage is attainable now, that the thresholds move together in the world (conjecture C0, which the diagnosis tests), that the signatures load on a single factor, or that the structural loop gain has been estimated for any channel. The name is for the mechanism: attraction is shown only in the field model of Section 4, and whether the generative order attracts is a conjecture. It does not claim that one institutional blueprint is uniquely feasible or best: the ranking of whole designs is a stated judgment. It claims nothing about any actor's motives. It does not claim that understanding suffices, since without executive authority it may change nothing; and it does not claim that AI should not be built: stopping (E2) concerns the decisive class only, and slowing (E3) concerns pace.

**What is new.** The theory absorbs a large literature (X1); what it adds is mainly organization, with a few results of its own. The structural loop gain gathers known instabilities of increasing returns and arms races (Arthur 1989; Richardson 1960) into one quantity whose factors are the three conditions, and {{>thresholds}} links the thresholds of Part I through the parameters they share. The price of standing extends the finding that concern for relative gains depends on how far gains convert into future advantage (Powell 1991) by adding the common channel. Selecting the selectors adds to regulatory capture (Stigler 1971; Dal Bó 2006) the selection of the carriers able to use it, its scaling with the square of share, the rate at which independent correction must grow, and the re-absorption of reforms below threshold. Enforcement by repetition, and its failure where power is about to shift, are textbook (Abreu 1988; Fearon 1995; Powell 2006); what is added is the split of the enforcement condition into the breakout probability and its bound, with the comparative statics by which growth moves both. The nested reward loop adds to the multiplicity thesis (Critch and Krueger 2020), under the alignment-tax premise, which divergences the selection of developers keeps: incarnation, weak and strong. And the account of one host says where integration at that scale must be anticipated rather than learned, since, unlike the integrations of evolution (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry 1995), it has no rival whole and no second attempt.
