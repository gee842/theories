## 2. Disagreements decided by derivation

1. **Objective arm.** A states it for any symmetric game (via (7)); B only in the binary game. Derived: $g_i(\theta)=(1+\theta)\bar\Delta+\frac{n-1-\theta}{n-1}(\Delta\pi_i-\bar\Delta)$ falls in θ when $\bar\Delta\le0<\Delta\pi_i-\bar\Delta$; $C_i-v_i=(1+\theta)(\bar\pi_C-\bar\pi_P)$ at symmetric profiles; with $\bar p=(c-\alpha)/(b+c)$, $\partial_\alpha\bar p<0$, $\partial_c\bar p=(1-\bar p)/(b+c)\ge0$, $\partial_b\bar p=-\bar p/(b+c)\le0$. A is right. Added "without breakouts" to A's punishment premise: with breakouts in punishment $v_i$ is not $(1+\theta)\bar\pi_P$. B's case kept as the instance. Fixed-prize sign: $\partial\theta_\Gamma/\partial\Gamma_\delta\propto\theta_0-(n-1)<0$ (checked).
2. **Exemption from the breakout bound.** The temptation less the sanction is $(1-\delta)g-\delta(C-v)+\delta p(D-v)$, linear in $p$; with $D\ge v$ it holds for all $p$ iff it holds at $p=1$, i.e. $(1-\delta)g\le\delta(C-D)$ (iff $\bar p\ge1$ when $D>v$). V6.1's antecedent "$p_d\to1$ and $D\ge C$" (kept by B) misses the case $D<C$ with $(1-\delta)g>\delta(C-D)$, which ES(e) exhibits ($\bar p=0.864$). A's form taken.
3. **Exchangeability in `relpunish`.** The mirror argument needs the swap to map the start state to itself; invariance of payoffs and transitions alone allows an asymmetric start. B's "no state carries a label" is stronger than needed and does not say the start is symmetric; A's (with D2-opus §12's proof) taken.
4. **Stakes arm.** $\bar p(k)=(c-k\alpha)/(kb+c)$ has derivative $-c(\alpha+b)/(kb+c)^2<0$ where $c>0$ and $\bar p<1$. A's "by a common factor relative to $C-v$" is the exact statement; B's "D and g grow faster than C−v" used $D$ where the term is $D-C$.
5. **8.10's $T<R$.** $T<R$ iff $1-\bar p<0$ iff $\bar p>1$ (B wrote $\ge$).
6. **Frontier condition.** From S5's values: destruction pays at θ iff $-c_y-\theta\Delta_y/(n-1)>0$, i.e. war never pays iff $\theta\ge-(n-1)c_y/\Delta_y$, a bound below zero. A (and V6.1) omitted $c_y$ in the switch condition; B's "weight on others reaches the ratio of private cost to relative gain" has the wrong sign and lacks $n-1$. Rewritten.
7. **Talk.** A's ideal points $(b_0+\theta L/(2(n-1)))/(1+\theta/(n-1))$ and B's $(2b_0(n-1)+\theta L)/(2(n-1+\theta))$ are equal; difference $b_0(n-1-\theta)/(n-1+\theta)$; curvature $-2(n-1+\theta)/(n-1)$. TK(e) matches to 1.5e-8.
8. **Strictness of the role punishment.** CR(c),(d): the profile is (deviator X, punisher X, punisher Y) with objectives (−0.5, −0.5, 1.0) and margins (0, 0, 0.2); the indifferent players are the two in the X role, not "the two punishers" (B's S5).
9. **Monotonicity of $\bar p$.** In $C$ the derivative is $1/(b+c)>0$; in $v$ it is $-(1-\bar p)/(b+c)<0$ on $[0,1)$; in $D$ it is $-\bar p/(b+c)$, zero at $\bar p=0$. "Weakly at the edges" (A) narrowed to "weakly in $D_i$ where $\bar p=0$"; ES(g) confirms the zero.
10. **Which joint model.** Both models are in `checks_v62.py` (JX(f) = A, JX(g) = B) and agree that the objective arm does most of the closing. A's taken for the core: its threshold is in the pivot variable (ω* = 0.551; 0.542 with one breakout technology throughout, which is the plan's "one $p_d$ throughout"); every payoff scales with the stakes, so the stakes arm is cleanly off (B's base run holds the stage fixed; its scaled variant scales $b,d$ but not $c$ and so turns part of the stakes arm on, which is why its meeting moves 59 → 47); $T_{\rm dec}$ is nonlinear (TD(b) shows the linearization can miss a gap that never becomes decisive); a 96-setting robustness grid. Meeting times of the two models are never mixed (checks-notes item 9).
11. **Numbers corrected.** PE $1.8\times10^{-9}$ (log 1.83e-9); FS final stock $4\times10^{-9}$ (log 3.96e-9); θ_rep at $p_d=1$ is +0.937 by direct streams (JX(c); BI's closed form prints 0.938).
