# V6.2 tests, cruxes and research programme

Companion to [THEORY.md](THEORY.md) §7.4 and §12, [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md) and [CLAIMS.md](CLAIMS.md). It holds what the core points to: the cruxes in full, with the present evidence and the next measurement for each (§1–§2); the judgment set against the cruxes, item by item (§3); what would redirect the argument (§4); the registered tests, with the rival theories that predict the same or the opposite (§5); the indicators of tier 2 and of the conditional results, with who estimates them and from what (§6); data (§7); the research programme (§8); and the independence of review (§9). Tests name roles ("leading developer", "leading state"), not particular states or firms. What each crux is, and what a measurement of it would change, are stated as in THEORY §12.3.

## 1. Empirical cruxes

Each crux is evaluated on its own; none is a conjunction of the others, since a conjunction would say that regulated competition does better only by becoming this paper's own design. C1 is the central crux: on it turn E3 and the case for E2 (THEORY §5.3).

## 2. Normative cruxes

## 3. The judgment against the cruxes

### 3.1 Tier 1(a): why each item stands on every reading

THEORY §12.1 admits an item to tier 1(a) only if its justification does not depend on how any crux comes out, and names four kinds of justification. **I**, information: the item measures a crux, or is worth most where readings disagree. **L**, lead time: what the item builds takes longer than any warning a crux could give. **E**, equilibrium without trust: the item is best for each actor that holds its premise, whatever the others do. **P**, public good: the item pays on every reading, and competitors undersupply it ({{onegroup}}). Each cell says why the item stands on that crux, in either direction; where a reading strengthens it, the cell says so.

The items: (1) shared measurement of hazards, coverage and residual risk, kept apart from measurement of who leads, with audited disclosure of conversion, certified row sums, miss correlation, control evaluations and the lineage share; (2) verification research and pilots; (3) alignment and control research, commonly funded and openly published; (4) preparation of common custody: its design, institutions, indicators and activation rule registered now, and stress-tested against D1–D8, the commitment to activate being tier 2's (THEORY §12.1); (5) narrow E1.

**Read by column.** Each item survives for a reason of its own kind: measurement by information; verification and the preparation of custody by lead time; research as a public good; narrow E1 as an equilibrium without trust ({{delegatelock}}(1); {{nearlock}}). The cruxes change what each item contains, not whether it is warranted: a favourable C2 withdraws item 6 of tier 1(b), not the measurement that would show it failing again; a favourable C5 lapses activation, not preparation. The normative cruxes are each reader's, and two bear on this tier. Under N2, an actor that does not weigh O2 does not price in the lock, so item 5's equilibrium holds among actors who do. Under N1, a criterion that values speed highly can still accept every item here, since none of them stops or slows anything: the commitment to activate custody and E2, which would, belongs to tier 2, where C4, C10 and N1 bear on it.

## 4. What would redirect the argument

Three outcomes would redirect it, classified before they are seen.
1. **Correction demonstrably scales.** Contestable coverage keeps pace with hazardous variety under present incentives, and residual risk falls. If that requires separating evaluators from success, it is a change of selection; if not, it is a successful continuation, and the theory was wrong about C1.
2. **An implementable safeguard makes continuation adequately safe.** A safeguard installed without moving the thresholds of {{thresholds}} meets the protection standard and stays durable as stakes and speed grow, classified before the outcome by its profile ({{profile}}). This refutes the claim that the conditions must change, and the registered prediction of THEORY §8 that arrangements which constrain conduct while leaving the thresholds where they were erode.
3. **The designed arrangement performs worse.** A stress-tested design fails worse than a credible continuation policy. This rejects that design, not the case for leaving the race.

THEORY §7.4 states the diagnosis's own refutations: signatures that persist unchanged over the dwell time after every threshold has been moved to its safe side for every capable actor; and a shared parameter that, moved, changes only its own signature (Test E).

## 5. Registered tests

Each test states this theory's prediction, the theories that also predict it, and a contrary prediction that discriminates. Only discriminating residues are registered as tests; confirmatory ones are marked. There is no Test H: v6.0's saturation test, leads converting into lasting share and the gap between open and closed models, discriminated no rival and is now an indicator of tier 2 (§6.1).

**Identification for Test Γ.** If actors perceive only a share $\psi$ of the shared harm, the ratio reads $(1+\theta)\psi$, so $\theta$ and $\psi$ are confounded: with half perceived, a true $\theta=0$ reads as $-0.5$ [num: SM(3)]. A third perturbation, which changes the visibility of shared harm without changing its size, or an independent measure of perceived harm, separates them. A second identification sets a change in the shared harm against a rebated levy, whose responses stand in the ratio $n(1+\theta)/(n-1-\theta)$ [num: TH]. SUPPLEMENT S3 gives both.

**Predictions carried in the text.** (i) In deployed delegation, safety improvements reduce risk per useful output while aggregate realized risk falls less, or not at all, until delegation saturates ({{homeostasis}}). (ii) If (coverage lags) holds for a class, catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours fall as capability rises; if they do not, it is refuted for the class measured. (iii) Arrangements that constrain conduct while leaving the thresholds where they were erode as stakes and speed grow, and those that move them for every capable actor persist, each classified by its profile before the outcome is known (THEORY §8) [conj].

## 6. Indicators

### 6.1 Tier 2: activation, and who estimates it

A trigger fires when the estimated time to a decisive position, less the time custody needs to be installed and verified, falls below a margin registered in advance; custody and E2 then activate by a mechanism committed in advance (THEORY §12.1). E3 activates class by class. What the trigger reads is what masking hides and strategic disclosure shades ({{alarm}}; {{detectlink}}), so each quantity is estimated by correctors whose row sums are certified (C2) and whose misses are measured for correlation (C3b), from channels that observe actions directly (verifiable deployments, compute and custody, the performance of contracts; THEORY §4.4), with any bound on masking drawn from evidence the observed control less directly.

### 6.2 Tier 2: release

Release reads indicators of its own, each held for the residence time of the slow variable it rests on, and each decided by a sequential test registered in advance ({{readiness}}; {{readrule}}). Activation's indicators read backwards would release too early: share levels recover before safety does, in the field model [thm] and by conjecture for institutions [conj]; near full share the depth of lock-in cannot be read from the level ({{dwell}}); and capture that makes delegation look safe is harder to undo than to prevent ({{humancorr}}(2)) [num: QS]. As a reform brings inbound conversion toward one, recovery at the captured state slows, so rising autocorrelation in shares indicates progress, and the field model's conversions can be recovered from series of domain shares (SUPPLEMENT S4). Where each of the seven dimensions stands today is read in DISCUSSION X11, *Readiness, read today* and *Where things stand*: none is met, and none is yet measured in the form {{readiness}} asks for.

### 6.3 The conditional results

**The three objects, measured separately.** What actors value: the weight on others and on the host, and the weight on winning (charters, incentive contracts, doctrines; Test Γ's ratio, with its confound $\psi$). What actors believe: about rivals' objectives and capabilities, the shared hazard, and whether they are in a struggle for survival (C8). What institutions reward, permit and protect: convertibility, decisiveness, conversion into evaluators and rules, row sums of correctors, and provision not won in the contest.

**The silent route ({{slowroute}}).** (reach): per harm class, capability with safeguards removed. (a) Cumulative relative selection: capital, compute and talent following rank; an average effective liability equal to the advantage is not enough, since $\tau_{\rm eff}=\nu-1/(1+t)$ has average tending to $\nu$ while the cumulative advantage diverges. (b) Appropriable control: developers' roles in evaluation and standards. (c) The two rates: AI's cheapening of control against that of independent evaluation plus budget growth, raised by $w\delta_h$ where correction rests on human capacity that delegation erodes. (d) Masking: evaluator bias against ground truth at relevant harm levels, and the lineage share $x_{\rm ev}$ wherever models evaluate models. (e) Capture before evidence: release-to-evaluation time against harm-to-detection time; in the joint model, the speed of capacity decides which clock runs out first [num: SR].

**Coverage.** (variety grows): predefined hazard modes per class, and their count per model generation; capture–recapture across independent red teams of different lineage. (coverage lags): coverage measured by the four conditions of contestable correction, legible, overrulable, exercised and resourced, with catch rates of planted errors at fixed human hours and audit coverage. (residual floor): residual-risk estimates per class by independent evaluators, stated as safety cases with numerical bounds. (shared failure): propagation pathways.

**The arrangements.** E1: the share of consequential decisions requiring human authorization with a minimum time for deliberation; drill success of parallel capacity; the rate at which the humans who authorize catch errors planted in AI recommendations, which tells exercised authority from approval by rote where a rate of overrule cannot; an evidence channel independent of AI. E2 and E3: §6.1.

### 6.4 The cruxes added in V6.2

**The tax (C9a–d).** Per class: the capability gap at fixed compute; the compute, latency and labour of monitoring and control; the time lost to manifestation windows; the cost of research that lowers the tax.

**Verifiability (C12).** Independent auditors' rate of detecting planted and emergent hidden objectives at fixed access, across capability levels.

**Dependence on the governed (C13).** The labour share in AI-intensive sectors; the share of consequential decisions taken with no human participation; drill success of parallel human capacity; who writes the alignment target inside a principal, and audits for hidden objectives and loyalties at admission.

**The loop gain.** $\Gamma$ is not estimated for any channel. §8 gives a template for estimating it by channel, and Test Γ reads the one related quantity that can be observed now.

## 8. Research programme

The author's pilot experiment on the mechanism of THEORY §4 ran once with 20 seeds: of its four predictions the first failed and three held, the failure attributed to too few lineages and to drift (EV12). A second design is specified and has not been run. A simulator that rewards capture directly establishes only its programmed mechanism, so each study below needs outcome measures independent of the selector, and real alternatives. None is a prerequisite for the practical judgment, which rests on the protection standard and the evidence; each would strengthen or weaken a named link.

- **Study 1. Selection with an endogenous evaluator.** A fixed selector, a selector that drifts by a matched amount, and a selector shaped by success, with outcome measures independent of the selector, optional capture, costly interference, and room for correction to succeed.
- **Study 2. Capacity and the field, jointly.** *What the joint simulations found* ({{capdyn}}; {{humancorr}}) [num: QS]. Five implementations agree in direction. While the cost of control falls monotonically the quasi-static model is conservative: capacity that starts at or below its selected level stays below it, so the quasi-static failure date is the earliest; adjustment at a constant rate delays failure by $\eta\ln(1+g_q/\kappa_q)/(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})$; gradual selection whose variance does not outgrow capacity stays bounded and certifies in the end; and capture follows the certificate's failure by 8 to 85 periods. It is optimistic in two places. After a reversal, a withdrawn reform must be held 6 to 14 per cent longer than it says, and 17 to 54 per cent where the domain's conversion comes from its own share. Where correction rests on human capacity that delegation erodes, the rate law's threshold rises by $w\delta_h$, and where captured evaluation reports delegation safe, undoing capture takes 1.6 to 2.6 times the rate that would certify evaluation without that loop. *The failure signature, corrected:* slow adaptation of capacity delays failure below the rate law and does not produce failure above it; a certificate that fails at a correction growth rate slightly above the rate law points to a slow variable that erodes correction itself. With capture endogenous, the speed of capacity decides the order of the silent route's two clocks [num: SR]. *Done since V6.1:* the effect of an alternative lineage on dwell. A recruitable lineage with a floor of 0.1% cuts the hold from 10.45 to 3.81 in units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, while a share protected outside selection, which does not recruit, leaves it at 10.44 ({{dwell}}) [num: DW]. *What remains:* capacity selected in several coupled domains at once while the contest moves the shares, so that cascades ({{reabsorb}}) could carry capture between domains; a model that derives the stage games of {{eq:enforceeq}} from the stock dynamics (the interface *stocks and stage payoffs* of S13); the form of human capacity's effect on correction and its elasticity $w$, and whether AI assistance supports that capacity or replaces it (C3a); a slow variable for the share of decision positions held by the winning model, with possible bistability and hysteresis; threshold and vector hosts, with the correctable kernel as the warning condition.
- **Study 3. Governance of correctors and infrastructure.** An early safety improvement crossed with later power to amend evaluation; human procurement and governance of evaluators under success; shared infrastructure under asymmetric dependence, with enforcement against the leading provider, continuity of service and appeal; contributions to a common frontier chosen in equilibrium, to measure the free riding inside the pool that THEORY §10.2 reasons from {{onegroup}} without computing it.
- **Study 4. Endogenous measurement [conj].** The four levels of epistemic closure: capture of measurement, of interpretation, of the concepts in which a failure could be expressed, and of preference. The ASTRA review of the first version, an external AI review the author endorsed (`../reviews/R5-gpt6-astra.md`), proposed further layers of the theory; V6.2 carries selection for the capacity to reshape selection ({{influence}}), the joint dynamics of state and rule ({{meta}}, and the meta-loop toy below), these levels of closure up to the capture of preference (THEORY §4.4), the reachable set ({{kernel}}; {{capturebasin}}) and the distinction between pathological and generative ({{pathgen}}), while endogenous domains and an ecology of practices remain unmodelled and belong to this study.
- **Study 5. Shared prediction and coordination.** Groups of people, and separately of AI agents, play a repeated contest with a shared hazard; treatments vary whether the hazard's derivation is common knowledge, demonstrated in an explorable simulation, or merely asserted, and whether winnings convert into capacity; outcomes are intensity, the informativeness of messages, and the adoption of common constraints. The laboratory race of EV11 varied only a private risk borne by the winner, and its authors note that it did not test collective risk; the treatment with a shared hazard here fills that gap.
- **Study 6. Faithful AI in a harmful game.** Two low-cost experiments on {{faithful}}, proposed at v5.2 and not yet run: human principals advised by a faithful AI in a contest with shared harm, varying the advisor's effectiveness; and AI agents acting faithfully for principals in a simulated commons, varying convertibility. The prediction: where the rank prize per unit of racing exceeds the shared harm per unit, a more effective faithful instrument widens the range in which racing is dominant.
- **Study 7. The strategic models, for outside review.** Every strategic computation so far is a two-actor contest in which a lead discourages the follower. Three questions are open. *Multiplicity:* where the contest runs in many small rounds, a winner-take-all fixed point (a price of 1.392 at $\Gamma=0.2$ and $d=0.05$, about 12.5 times the structural value) stands beside the smooth one (0.1169) ({{eqgain}}) [num: MP; held for outside review]; is it an equilibrium of the continuous-state game and not an artefact of the grid, and do expectations select between them, so that a belief that leads are decisive selects the equilibrium in which they are? *Catch-up contests:* handicaps, learning from the leader, or diminishing returns to effort at the top make the laggard try harder, and in one computed case the strategic correction reverses ($r^{\rm eq}/r=0.993$ at $\Gamma=0.3$); a search for the reverse case would bound the class in which a structural gain below one is necessary and not sufficient for stability. *Beyond two actors and first order:* the Markov-perfect contest with $n\ge3$; the full stochastic game behind the price of standing; the least-value punishment in a stochastic game with an absorbing state, which {{enforce}} asserts for finite stage games.
- **A suggestion: the meta-loop toy.** The closure through what all share can be written as a loop on the loop gain: the discounted gain sets the working objective by {{eq:thetagamma}}, actors acting on that objective make the rules, and the rules set the next period's gain. In a toy with $n=4$ and $\theta_0=0.5$, in which rule-making answers the working objective through a logistic response chosen for the toy, the loop is bistable when that response is steep: at steepness 12 and threshold 0, stable states at $\Gamma_\delta=0.108$ (working $\theta=0.39$) and 0.98 ($\theta=-0.95$), separated by an unstable one at 0.404; under a mild response (steepness 2) there is a single fixed point [num: ML, derived toy]. Rebuilt from the paper's own pieces, (9) for the objective and the rule-makers' contest of {{nounilateral}} or the field model for the response, it would make "does the generative order attract?" a question in a specified model ({{meta}}(2)), and give "change the basin, then add speed" a separatrix. If it cannot be built without an ad hoc response function, it should be dropped. It is the kind of unification the drafting family favours (§9), so it needs review outside that family before anything rests on it, and the core does not cite it.
- **A template for estimating Γ by channel.** $\Gamma$ is not estimated, and the core prints no value of it. The table gives, channel by channel, what sets each factor of {{eq:gamma}} and the only ranges the register now holds, which illustrate and do not estimate (Remark {{#gammarange}}).

## 9. Independence of review

The drafts of this theory, the planning memos that shaped V6.2, the merges, and almost all of its reviews were written by systems of one model family, which share preferences for unified and elegant models, for corpora in which cooperative framings are common, and for absorbing counterexamples into a higher level of the same framework. Their agreement is weaker evidence than its volume suggests. Two of this version's additions are of exactly the kind the family favours, the reduced joint models of the scissors and the meta-loop toy, and both are labelled for outside review. Much of the evidence on the alignment tax and on masked divergences comes from developers, the drafting model's developer among them, which is the arrangement Section 4 describes, so the family's reading of it may be lenient; EV4 records that developer's own political spending.

One model outside the family (Codex) reviewed V6.1. It ran the structure checker and all 25 numerical blocks of that version in a temporary copy, wrote and passed a counterexample script of its own, and reviewed the new formal core and the argument it supports: the loop gain and the price of standing, the cap result, the rate law, equal incidence and the nested loop, mirrored punishments, delegation under the lock, the Price decomposition, the oversight certificate, and the judgment's tiers. It found four errors that the family's reviewers had not: a falling per-unit floor under which the rate law's necessity fails, equal incidence stated as necessary for compliance, a stochastic punishment whose continuation cannot be mirrored, and the condition $p=1$ lost from the delegation result. It did not certify inherited results or unverified references. Its eight boundary cases now run in `checks_v62.py` (blocks PD, CE, FF, CI, ES, DL and PR), and all hold. V6.2 itself has not been reviewed outside the family.

Before any claim is relied on it needs review by people and systems outside that family, above all from structural realism, industrial economics and the history of arms control, and on the new formal results: {{loopgain}}(4) with {{eqgain}}, the strategic price of standing and the multiplicity of Markov equilibria; {{standprice}} with {{growprize}}, including whether the first-order result survives in the full stochastic game; {{chansign}}; {{enforce}}, with the least-value punishment in a game with an absorbing state and the explicit-action check of block ES; {{detectlink}} and {{scissors}}, with {{jointscissors}}; {{relpunish}}; {{blind}}; {{onegroup}}; {{compound}}; {{talk}}; {{corrrate}} with {{capdyn}}, whose parts (2)(b) and (2)(c) are a new derivation; {{humancorr}}, since the simulations of the quasi-static assumption are all the family's; {{nearlock}}; {{signal}}; {{dwell}} and {{readrule}}; and {{require}}. Historians should review the precedent inferences of DISCUSSION X11; the correspondence with Fudenberg and Koh (2026) is read from its abstract and needs the full paper; and several works of 2026 were read through summaries.
