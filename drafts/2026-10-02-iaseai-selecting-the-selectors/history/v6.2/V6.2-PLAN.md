## 1. What the paper is

**The one idea.** Where what is won converts into the capacity to win again, the game manufactures the logic of winning in whoever plays it and lets success buy the institutions that would correct it. Better players do not fix this; a different game does. Change what winning confers.

**The tightening.** The review round's corrections become the spine, not caveats. Each item below names its home.

1. **Compounding prices standing**, wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return. With a fixed prize this always holds. With a prize proportional to capacity it holds exactly where leads compound: $(1-\beta)r>1$ is both conditions. Each actor computes correctly; the game is wrong (`standprice`, `twoerrors`).
2. **The levers act channel by channel** (Prop S2.3 with a one-sentence home in §2's box):
   - sharing and diffusion lower the gain in every channel;
   - provision not won lowers it in channels whose decisiveness rises less than in proportion to the stock (capacity);
   - correction lowers it in channels whose decisiveness rises with the stock's level (rule support).

   Correction that only speeds the decay of capacity does not lower it, because a ratio contest does not see a decay common to all (Lemma 3.3 again). Trees for capacity, correction for rule support, sharing and openness for all: this is "change what winning confers", made exact.
3. **The scissors turn on one pivot**: the share of capacity that has to be won, $\omega$ (stakes relative to provision beneath the game). Each arm is proved. A reduced joint model shows them closing together [num]. In the world, their co-movement is C0 (`scissors`, `detectlink`).
4. **The rate law has two halves**: sufficient at any adaptation speed, necessary when capacity tracks its selected level. Delegation that erodes human capacity raises the threshold by $w\delta_h$; delegation erodes the corrector itself (`corrrate`, `humancorr`).
5. **Masked divergences** make coordination necessary but not sufficient. They are the strongest alignment reason for E2/E3 (`masked`).
6. **The thresholds table** is the paper's integrating figure (Table 7.1, `thresholds`): nine thresholds, one inequality each, with the result that proves it, what pushes toward danger, and the lever that reverses it. A few parameters ($\omega$, $\chi$, $r$) recur across rows. That is the formal sense of "interconnected conditions": not one pressure, but a few parameters on which several thresholds turn. The S7 matrix gives every arrow four entries: the intervention; what is held fixed; which model supplies the sign; which interface is unproved.

**What the paper does not claim.** Use the list in D1-opus §1.3, in §1.4 and mirrored in the guide. It includes the name sentence: "the name is for the mechanism; attraction is shown only in the field model; whether the generative order attracts is a conjecture."

**Spine.** D1-opus §1.1's seven results, plus the four judgment results. The enforcement condition (14) is the organizing inequality of Part I's last step and of the judgment (D1-fable §1.2), and every mechanism in Table 7.1 names the side of (14) it moves where it moves one.
