# Simulated empirical, methodological and policy referee report

**Manuscript:** Theory V7.1, *Changing the AI Trajectory: The Meta-Attractor of Winning and the Generative Alternative*. **Date:** 4 October 2026. **Recommendation:** reject the present integrated research article at a leading field journal; encourage a substantially narrower new submission. A major-revision recommendation would be defensible only if an editor had already accepted a clearly delimited formal or conceptual contribution as the article's center.

This is a fresh AI review in a separate context, not external human peer review. I examined the diagnosis, policy comparison and judgment, the evidence and test registers, the pilot account, and the model-interface inventory. I did not rerun the inherited numerical suite or independently audit every cited primary source. The anchors below refer to the editable source files. This report evaluates the contribution that the evidence supports, rather than treating corrected citations or passing simulations as empirical validation.

## Overall assessment

The manuscript contains a potentially useful mechanism: success can purchase influence over the process that selects future successes. It also does an unusually good job of exposing the premises on which its conclusions depend. But the evidence currently establishes neither the strength of this mechanism in AI institutions nor the coupled trajectory that distinguishes the integrated argument from familiar accounts of competition, regulatory capture, commitment problems, and externalities. The stated preference among whole institutional arrangements is an expressly normative judgment without comparative risk estimates. That is a legitimate position to argue for; it is not yet an identified empirical result or a demonstrated institutional ranking.

This is **not** a demand that every theory paper acquire a dataset. A leading game-theory, mathematical-economics or mathematical journal can accept a paper whose contribution is a sufficiently original, consequential theorem. In that route, the motivating observations need not identify the world, but the main result must carry the paper without the untested diagnosis. Political science also accepts theoretical and interpretive advances; data are not universally required there. It is this manuscript's empirical diagnosis of the current trajectory that needs a defined population, measurements, and an identification strategy, or a systematic comparative/process-tracing design that can discriminate mechanisms. A conceptual systems/cybernetics paper may instead offer a useful synthesis, but must demonstrate the new explanatory or analytical work the synthesis performs. An AI-alignment paper would need a specific advance in understanding or evaluating AI systems or multi-agent interactions; the importance of AI policy alone does not establish that fit. A new algorithm is not required for every AI venue: an informative evaluation, negative result, or theoretical clarification can supply the contribution if the evidence and audience are clear. These routes have different burdens, and the present manuscript tries to collect their advantages at once.

## Ranked substantive obstacles

### 1. The distinctive coupled claim has no identifying evidence

The claim that winning, capture and the invisibility of common harms sustain one another is stated in [§7.3, line 93](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/70_s7.md:93). Yet T1 largely concerns declarations, T2 concerns written commitments rather than exercised choices, T3 has no demonstrated impairment of an entrusted purpose, T4 lacks the predicted scaling and appropriable component, and T5 is unmeasured. These are useful motivating observations, not measurements of a feedback system. The register itself records that no study has tested the interlock: [EVIDENCE, lines 294–308](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/reg/EVIDENCE.md:294).

The distinction matters because a common shock—rapid technical progress, new financing, a change of government, or perceived foreign competition—could generate several signatures together without the proposed causal closure. A co-occurrence result, including a latent-factor result, would not identify the arrows. More narrowly, ordinary capture and race-to-market accounts could explain individual signatures. Honest caveats keep the claims accurate but do not supply the additional scientific contribution.

The issue also exists inside the theory. Stocks-to-stage-payoffs, capture-to-sanctions, correction-to-coverage, and the simultaneous movement of the scissors' arms are open interfaces. See [S13, lines 12, 23–25 and 33](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:12). Evidence for separate links cannot be multiplied into evidence for the integrated system without identifying the joins. Conversely, a new integrated theorem could supply a formal contribution even before this empirical task is solved.

### 2. The key quantities are not operationalized well enough to assign an observed institution to a model regime

The paper says explicitly that no channel's loop gain has been estimated: [TESTS, lines 176 and 194–204](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/reg/TESTS.md:194). A list of possible indicators does not yet identify the elasticities, turnover, diffusion and reinvestment shares on a common scale. The same issue applies to institutional conversion coefficients and row-sum certificates: funding dependence, access restrictions and appointment links identify possible channels, not their dynamical coefficients. A proposed authority or a source of money does not by itself establish a correction rate or timely executive capacity; the manuscript recognizes the missing production relation in [S13, lines 24–25](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:24).

The coverage ratio is particularly consequential. Predefining hazard modes helps, but their count depends on the taxonomy and granularity: splitting one uncovered class into ten can change the ratio without changing actual danger. A usable measure needs rules for sampling and splitting modes, exposure or severity weights where appropriate, and calibration against held-out failures. Capability benchmarks cannot supply that calibration. The definition and residual-floor assumption are distinct in [§5.3, lines 82–86](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/50_s5.md:82), and the register correctly says neither hazardous variety nor residual irreversible risk is measured [EVIDENCE, lines 20 and 294–296](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/reg/EVIDENCE.md:294).

No finite experiment can establish asymptotic summability without extrapolation assumptions. For empirical use, the primary target should therefore be a registered finite-horizon bound in a defined deployment domain, with the extrapolation stated separately. Requiring favorable evidence before releasing a precautionary constraint is an explicit normative choice here; the absence of that favorable evidence must not become positive evidence that the unfavorable mechanism is operating.

### 3. Most proposed tests establish consistency, not discriminating identification

Tests D and G appropriately concede that no matched rival has been derived [TESTS, lines 116, 123 and 126](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/reg/TESTS.md:116). That restraint should govern the other tests as well:

- A competitor release is not an exogenous shock. Rival releases, safety revisions and financing may answer the same technical or market news. Test A needs an assignment mechanism or defensible event variation, not timing alone.
- A steep size–influence gradient in Test B can reflect differing returns to regulation, fixed participation costs, political opportunities or omitted firm scope. A quadratic association by itself does not identify success selecting the capacity to control its own evaluator.
- Capped-return organizations in Test F select into that structure and differ in mission, resources, market access and exposure to rivals. Those differences can produce the proposed contrast without the mechanism.
- Cross-effects in Test E are not exclusive to an interlocked model: a common intervention can affect several otherwise independent outcomes. Disclosure itself can alter reputation, funding and political access, so the proposed conduct-rule comparator is not assured to have only one channel.

These criticisms do not require ruling out entire traditions such as realism or path dependence. They require one specified rival, matched on payoffs, information, resource budgets and opportunities, whose predictions differ on a preselected contrast. The current tests are a research agenda, not yet such a design. Sources: [TESTS, lines 120–132](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/reg/TESTS.md:120); [§7.4, lines 97–104](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/70_s7.md:97).

### 4. The policy ranking is not produced by the stated comparison rule

The protection standard is coherent as a choice of values. The paper nevertheless has neither common finite-horizon risk bounds nor a quantified uncertainty set that permits its rule to rank whole arrangements. It says so directly: [§8.1, lines 13–29](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/80_s8.md:13) and [§12.1, lines 31–39](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/c0_s12.md:31). The fallback lemma assumes the two pathwise hazard inequalities that would make the arrangement safer. Proving their consequence does not establish either premise for common custody.

Naming reversals—recertification, a sunset, standby capacity, independent disposition—does not establish that they execute before an irreversible loss. The manuscript now correctly admits this. Consequently, the broad E1 preference and custody proposal should be presented as a normative institutional proposal with testable requirements, not as a scientific ranking established by the model suite. Tier 1 research and measurement have a plausible option-value argument, but their optimum scale and opportunity costs still require assessment; [§12.1, line 13](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/c0_s12.md:13) already limits the claim accordingly.

The asymmetry is no longer hidden; it remains substantively unresolved. There is more observational material about defects in existing institutions than about failures of the proposed arrangement because the latter does not exist. Applying the same vocabulary to both does not give them equal empirical support. This is most consequential for political-science or political-economy publication, where implementability, endogenous institutional adoption, noncompliance by powerful actors and distributional consequences are part of the explanation.

### 5. The current pilot cannot bear the empirical contribution

The one 20-seed pilot failed P1, has wide intervals, and the revised design is unrun. The pilot is useful for debugging the experimental model. Even a successful rerun would not independently validate an economic or institutional mechanism if its selector, rewards and capacity dynamics already encode that mechanism. Design 2 changes the number of lineages, conversion and quality spread after inspecting the failure, which is transparently reported and reasonable for development, but gives an additional reason to separate development runs from a frozen confirmatory test. See [EV12](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/reg/EVIDENCE.md:202), [TESTS, lines 184–186](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/reg/TESTS.md:184), and [Design 2, lines 5–30](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/experiment/PREDICTIONS-2.md:5).

## One feasible study most likely to change this judgment

**Prioritize Study 1: a preregistered repeated contest with a yoked evaluator control.** It directly targets the distinctive closure, has an immutable external outcome measure, and does not need access to governments or frontier developers. A human laboratory or online experiment with modest, consequential payouts is a stronger first empirical test than another simulator whose behavior is specified by the model. Use safe task performance and monetary common-pool losses; no real hazardous deployment is necessary.

Participants compete over repeated rounds on tasks with an external quality score that they cannot amend. They allocate scarce resources between productive effort and optional, costly influence over the contest's scoring rubric. Do not reward influence directly; participants must choose whether manipulating evaluation pays. Randomly assign groups to a factorial design: (i) winnings can buy future productive capacity versus matched noncontingent capacity provision; (ii) a carrier's success can give it authority over its own evaluator versus evaluator changes replayed from a paired group, with timing and magnitude matched but unrelated to the receiving group's success. The latter is the matched drift control already suggested by the manuscript. Keep expected resource budgets, task difficulty, information, direct lobbying costs and payment rules as comparable as the intervention permits; specify any remaining differences explicitly. Random initial leads help separate feedback amplification from sorting on initial skill. Cross-group interference is restricted to the declared yoking, and analysis respects the pair as a dependency cluster.

The primary endpoint should be independently audited loss of true task quality relative to awarded scores. Secondary endpoints are concentration, spending on influence and the response of subsequent success to randomized initial leads. Freeze the task generator, payoff schedules, correction opportunities, horizon, smallest substantively meaningful effect and analysis before confirmation. Power the number of independent groups using a separate pilot; adding simulated seeds is not a substitute for groups. Permit costly correction to succeed, and avoid tuning parameters until capture is inevitable.

Before the confirmatory run, derive predictions from **two matched models**: the proposed feedback model and a separable contest/rent-seeking model with the same direct effects but no return from a carrier's own success into its future evaluator. Fit nuisance parameters on separate development data and compare out-of-sample trajectories. The decisive contrast is whether enabling own-success feedback produces the predicted additional amplification, beyond matched evaluator drift and ordinary compounding, and whether removing winnings-to-capacity breaks that amplification as predicted. The interaction's sign and size must be derived for the actual design; neither is guaranteed by labeling it a test of the theory.

A positive result would establish a bounded mechanism and materially strengthen a focused political-economy, experimental, or systems paper. A tightly estimated absence of the predicted amplification, or accurate prediction by the separable comparator, would weaken the claimed added explanatory value. If a conventional dynamic capture model predicts the same contrast, the result supports their shared mechanism and cannot establish novelty against that model. Success would **not** identify global C0, show that AI institutions occupy the tested regime, estimate catastrophic residual risk, or rank common custody above regulated competition. Those would remain outside the resulting article's demonstrated scope.

## Confidence and editorial forecast

**High confidence:** the current evidence does not identify the coupled diagnosis or establish the policy ranking; the manuscript's own status tables and gap register make that conclusion unusually secure. **Moderate confidence:** the proposed experiment is the best feasible next empirical investment; access to unusually strong institutional data could change that choice. **Lower confidence:** whether the formal core is sufficiently original and important for a leading theory journal; that requires a theorem-by-theorem novelty judgment and falls outside this report.

I expect rejection of the present omnibus empirical/policy article at leading field journals, but this is an editorial assessment, not a calibrated acceptance probability. I would not attach a numerical chance of acceptance without a named journal, article type, selected central contribution and outcome base rate. The clearest improvement is to choose a contribution whose evidence can actually be completed: one important theorem, one identified feedback experiment, or a shorter conceptual/policy argument that states its institutional preference as a proposal.
