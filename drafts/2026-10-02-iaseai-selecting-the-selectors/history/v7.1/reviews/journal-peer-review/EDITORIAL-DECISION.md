## Verdict and confidence

**V7.1 is defensible as a conditional working paper, but it is not yet a competitive submission as one research article to the leading journals in the requested fields.** The strongest publication prospect is a narrower paper built around Section 4's endogenous influence and correction model. That prospect requires research and comparison, not just shortening and polishing.

The earlier release assessment concerned whether the document could circulate with its premises, limitations and conjectures accurately marked. Journal review asks an additional question: what consequential advance has this article established for this audience? Passing the first test does not establish the second. The present review does not withdraw the narrower working-paper assessment.

These judgments respect different journal standards. [JMLR's author guidance](https://jmlr.org/author-info.html) calls for advances in understanding learning systems, supported by theory or experiments, and clear positioning against predecessors. [TMLR's criteria](https://www.jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html) deliberately do **not** require method novelty or speculative significance; accurate support, audience interest and clear communication are the relevant tests there. It would be wrong to apply a universal “breakthrough or reject” rule to TMLR.

[GEB's scope](https://gametheorysociety.org/games-and-economic-behavior/) includes strategic, axiomatic, behavioral and interdisciplinary contributions; there is no universal dataset requirement. [The Review of Economic Studies](https://www.restud.com/about/) publishes theoretical and applied economics. [APSR's guidance](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials) explicitly recognizes empirical, interpretive and theoretical advances. The criticism here attaches to the claims being made, not to the absence of a particular method.

[SIADS](https://www.siam.org/publications/siam-journals/siam-journal-on-applied-dynamical-systems/) accepts contributions to dynamical-systems theory, understanding through experiments or simulation, and applications of its methods. [IEEE Transactions on Cybernetics](https://www.ieeesmc.org/publications/transactions-on-cybernetics/) emphasizes computational approaches and communication/control across machines, humans and organizations. [Systems Research and Behavioral Science](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/10991743a/homepage/aims.htm) has a broader systems-theory and governance remit. That last fit is an alternative route, not evidence that it has the same editorial threshold as the other venues.

## Why the current article falls short

### 1. Its strongest integrated claim is still a research hypothesis

The manuscript's own [interface register, S13](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md), is decisive. The stock model does not derive the repeated game's stage payoffs. The local effective objective does not establish the full strategic continuation objective. Capture changing sanctions and correction resources producing coverage remain open. The simultaneous movement of the enforcement “scissors” is illustrated in reduced models and proposed as C0, not proved as a general equilibrium result.

This is not an accusation that V7.1 hides those gaps: it now marks them carefully. Nor must every article solve the entire programme. But a paper whose central contribution is the integration must establish the connections on which that integration depends. Alternatively, a narrower article can avoid those claims and stand on one completed mechanism.

### 2. The incremental contribution is plausible but not demonstrated

“Success buys power over future selection” is a valuable organizing idea, but close literature already studies endogenous political power, institutional persistence, relative gains and commitment failure. For example, [Acemoglu and Robinson (2008)](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/persistence%20of%20power,%20elits%20and%20institutions.pdf) models investment in de facto power that offsets institutional reform. [Bombardini (2008)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022199608000275) studies large-firm lobbying participation. These are mostly **already cited**; the missing work is an analytical comparison establishing the new implication, not a longer bibliography.

Section 4's correction-growth threshold and persistent per-unit cost-floor exception remain credible candidates for an original contribution. The review did not locate an identical result in the selected sources, but that does not establish priority. The square-share return follows from two stipulated share channels. Its value depends on what that structure explains and which substantive conclusions survive alternatives, not on the exponent being algebraically correct.

V7.1 already contains important robustness work: size elasticities, regularly varying marginal costs, super-polynomial costs, concave conversion, adaptation lags and transient qualifications. Asking for those same extensions again would be unfair. The remaining question is what follows when strategic rivals and institutional correction respond, and which result differs from the nearest existing account.

### 3. The evidence supports motivation, not the full diagnosis or policy ranking

[Section 7](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/70_s7.md) and [EVIDENCE](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/reg/EVIDENCE.md) distinguish declarations from behavior, favorable institutional changes from demonstrated capture, and capability from decisive advantage. Several crucial quantities are unmeasured; C0 has not been tested. Proposed signatures may also arise from ordinary capture, compounding, common shocks or changing external threats. Tests D and G appropriately admit that a discriminating rival comparison has not been completed.

The reported 239 passing checks are useful computational verification. They do not independently establish a mechanism's prevalence in institutions, originality, or the policy ranking. The preserved negative pilot and unrun revised design likewise cannot serve as empirical confirmation.

The normative protection standard may be argued for on its own terms. But without comparable hazard bounds or an operational uncertainty set, the models do not establish that common custody or the broader generative arrangement dominates regulated competition. The document now acknowledges this. A theorem about the consequences of favorable hazard inequalities does not establish those inequalities for a proposed institution.

A pure theory article can succeed without new data. A political-theory or systems article can succeed through a consequential conceptual argument. Neither route lets the untested real-world diagnosis count as a demonstrated finding.

### 4. The current unit of publication is too broad

The existing assembly log records **63,366 core words**, **66,469 supplement words**, and **22,753 discussion words**, excluding references. This is a book-scale programme, containing multiple possible articles and a policy proposal. Length increases the review burden, but it is not the fundamental objection: cutting the text alone would leave the contribution and interface issues unresolved.

The breadth also creates competing standards. A mathematical reader looks for the central mathematical advance; an economist asks what changes in equilibrium and welfare; an alignment reader asks what is learned about AI; a political scientist asks what is newly explained about institutions. The article needs a primary audience and a result that matters to it.

## What deserves to survive

The review supports preserving four strengths: the distinction between alignment to a principal and the incentives among principals; the separation of concentration, capture, damage and irreversible loss; correction's dual role in dissipating influence and reducing its private return; and the explicit counter-regimes in which independent AI assistance, cost floors or effective constraints prevent the pessimistic outcome. The interface and evidence registers make the work easier to criticize and improve. These are useful foundations, even though they do not establish journal readiness.

## Recommended publication strategy

Keep V7.1 as the synthesis and develop a separate article around **endogenous oversight capture as influence becomes cheaper**. This preserves the broader programme while giving specialists one question they can evaluate.

The core question would be: *When does cheaper influence outrun independent correction, and which institutional arrangements prevent capture rather than merely postpone it?* Retain the conditional correction-rate law, cost-floor distinction, and actual adaptation dynamics. Specify one coherent strategic or evolutionary mechanism rather than treating instantaneous optimization and gradual selection as interchangeable.

Three milestones would materially change this review:

1. **Demonstrate the increment.** Match the closest institutional-power model on primitives and show one new prediction, boundary or intervention ranking. A particularly interesting optional target is to derive when concentration's capture cost reverses its reduction of racing pressure in the same model. Recent AI-race research already predicts safety benefits from consolidation in specified settings; see [Bueno de Mesquita, Dziuda and Polborn (2026)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35276), a working paper already discussed in V7.1. Opposing signs in separate models do not yet establish the tradeoff.
2. **Close the focal interface.** Endogenize influence adjustment and at least one strategically relevant response by competitors or correctors. Derive the dynamic outcome and distinguish eventual recovery from finite-horizon safety. A small complete model may suffice; solving the entire manuscript is unnecessary.
3. **Provide evidence appropriate to the chosen claim.** For a theory article, supply a self-contained proof and matched-model comparison, followed by external specialist scrutiny. For an empirical mechanism article, freeze a design that compares success-controlled evaluation with matched exogenous evaluator drift, uses independent outcome measurement, and tests a specified rival. The [evidence report](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/reviews/journal-peer-review/evidence-referee.md) gives one bounded design. A successful test would support that mechanism in that setting, not certify planetary governance or catastrophic-risk rankings.

This is a research plan for a credible submission, not a promise of acceptance. A shorter conceptual systems article is another plausible route, provided it demonstrates explanatory value over existing frameworks and keeps its institutional proposals distinct from established results.
