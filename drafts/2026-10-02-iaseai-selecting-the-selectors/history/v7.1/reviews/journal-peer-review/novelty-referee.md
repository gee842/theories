# Simulated referee report: originality and literature positioning

Reviewer remit: the strongest incremental contributions in Theory V7.1, assessed against selected primary literature. Review date: 4 October 2026. This is an AI-generated referee simulation, not an actual editorial decision, independent human peer review, or exhaustive priority search. No manuscript files were edited.

**Recommendation: reject the present omnibus submission at a leading research journal; encourage a substantially narrower new paper.** I have high confidence in that recommendation for the manuscript as it stands, moderate confidence that its capture/correction model contains material worth developing into a publishable specialist article, and low confidence in any forecast of eventual acceptance at a particular leading journal. These are judgments about evidential support, not estimated acceptance probabilities.

The work's strongest achievement is a disciplined synthesis: it separates concentration, capture, effective enforcement, and irreversible harm; identifies premises connecting them; and exposes many of those premises to possible refutation. Its own §1.4 and X1 correctly present organization as the main contribution. That is valuable intellectual work. A leading research article nevertheless needs a compact answer to what a competent reader of the closest literature can now prove, predict, explain, or do that they could not previously. The current manuscript offers several candidates but has not yet established one at the requisite level of comparative generality or empirical discrimination.

## Three candidate additions

### 1. Endogenous influence over correctors and the correction-growth threshold

**Exact claim.** Proposition 4.3 derives a local return to appropriable influence proportional to `χ p²`; Proposition 4.5 derives a correction-growth threshold `αcor ≥ αctl/(1+η)` under specified costs and adjustment conditions, with materially different behavior when per-unit cost floors persist. The total cultivated-conversion moment depends on cost rivalry. Proposition 4.6 adds human-capacity erosion. These are substantially more precise claims than “power captures institutions.” See [Proposition 4.3](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/THEORY.md:353), [Proposition 4.5](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/THEORY.md:369), and [Proposition 4.6](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/THEORY.md:379).

**Nearest literature.** Buying favorable policy in a strategic political equilibrium is already central to [Grossman and Helpman, *Protection for Sale*](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4149) (1992 working paper; AER 1994). Firm size selecting political participants is established by [Bombardini (2008)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022199608000275), which derives large-firm lobbying participation under fixed participation costs and tests predictions using firm-level contributions. Most importantly for reform re-absorption, [Acemoglu and Robinson (2008), *Persistence of Power, Elites, and Institutions*](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/persistence%20of%20power,%20elits%20and%20institutions.pdf), pp. 268–270, explicitly endogenizes investment in de facto power, offsetting institutional reform, and institutional persistence. Strong enough democratic institutions can change its equilibrium qualitatively. V7.1 cites this paper at the introduction of equation (10); the issue is insufficient theorem-level comparison, not absence of the citation.

**Increment that remains plausible.** The correction-cost elasticity law, its persistent-floor exception, and its combination with a network of correction domains are credible candidates for a new formal contribution. I did not find an identical result in the sources examined. That does not establish priority. The `p²` result itself is less compelling: the model makes share enter once in producing influence and once in benefiting from it. Multiplying those channels yields a useful mechanism, but its exponent is not a general consequence of endogenous institutions. The manuscript commendably labels it a local gradient rather than a strategic equilibrium. A reviewer will ask whether strategic counter-lobbying, nonlinear influence production, rival responses, and endogenous correction retain a distinctive implication.

**What would discriminate.** Do not test only whether large firms lobby or reforms reverse; the comparators predict these. Estimate whether an intervention changing appropriability, holding the size/cost environment suitably controlled, changes influence investment and the recovery threshold in the model's specified way. Test the elasticity/floor interaction, including conditions under which correction succeeds. For hysteresis, [Test D](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/TESTS.md:123) rightly concedes that thresholds and hysteresis are not sufficient discrimination from path dependence. Its predicted timing of share recovery versus support decay, reform duration, and recruitable alternatives could become distinctive only after the rival model is subjected to the same intervention.

**Assessment:** strongest candidate; moderate confidence in potentially publishable incremental novelty, low confidence that exact novelty or empirical importance is established.

### 2. Compounding induces a local effective objective through relative versus common returns

**Exact claim.** [Proposition 3.9 and equation (9)](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/THEORY.md:268) relate the effective weight on common welfare to the ratio of common and relative continuation returns. Narrowing occurs when the relative return exceeds the common return. Fixed prizes can therefore narrow the effective objective even without unstable concentration; in a specified proportional-prize model the boundary coincides with instability. This distinction is useful and nontrivial as economic interpretation.

**Nearest literature.** [Powell (1991), *Absolute and Relative Gains in International Relations Theory*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/absolute-and-relative-gains-in-international-relations-theory/0018BA1A141FE29A522EEDAA2A75826E), already makes concern for relative gains depend on constraints rather than different primitive preferences. Thus “correct optimization can produce apparently relative objectives” is not the new result. V7.1 acknowledges this accurately. Its proposed addition is the explicit common/relative decomposition, extension beyond force, and provision/diffusion comparative statics. Whether this is a substantial generalization or a tractable local restatement requires a matched formal comparison, not a verbal assertion that more domains are included.

**Limit of the theorem claim.** The reduction holds locally near symmetry in deterministic shares, with opposing conduct fixed and convergence/tail conditions. It is an effective objective representing a marginal calculation, not proof that actors' preferences have changed or that a dynamic strategic equilibrium becomes more selfish. [S13's working-objective interface](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/SUPPLEMENT.md:1139) openly leaves the full strategic continuation derivatives and stochastic reduction unresolved. This is a substantive limit for a game-theory or economics contribution, not merely an exposition problem.

**What would discriminate.** [Test Γ](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/TESTS.md:132) is promising: vary reinvestment/provision and measure responses to shared harm relative to private cost while primitive preferences are held fixed. Account for perceived harm, as its identification paragraph recognizes. But its stated fixed-relative-preference alternative is weaker than Powell's structural account. Derive the comparator's response under the same resource transition, payoffs, information, and intervention. A strategic result identifying when the common channel reverses an established relative-gains prediction would be a stronger contribution than another example of relative incentives.

**Assessment:** useful analytic synthesis and plausible narrow proposition; high confidence that the broad conceptual idea has close precedent, moderate confidence in the proposed incremental distinction, low confidence in priority for the exact formula.

### 3. Absorbing breakout and the coupled enforcement “scissors”

**Exact claim.** [Proposition 5.4](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/THEORY.md:466) decomposes a repeated-game deviation constraint into breakout probability and the maximum sustainable probability; [Proposition 5.17](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/THEORY.md:556) links changes in the won share of capacity to the breakout and objective arms. Quiet high-breakout deviations and a punishment phase that is itself a race are useful observations.

**Nearest literature.** The manuscript itself calls the single-deviation absorbing-breakout incentive condition textbook. [Powell (2006), *War as a Commitment Problem*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/war-as-a-commitment-problem/65DFFF1CD73A16F7ED4EEF6D4F934608), already connects shifting power, first-strike advantages, and bargaining over future power through commitment problems. The [Avenhaus, von Stengel, and Zamir author manuscript of *Inspection Games*](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~zamir/papers/inspection.pdf) includes timeliness games; timely detection is therefore not, by itself, a fresh mechanism. This review verified its bibliographic/section record but did not inspect its full derivations after retrieval timed out.

**Increment and limitation.** Reparameterizing the incentive constraint is helpful for governance diagnosis but unlikely, by itself, to be a major new game-theoretic result. The stronger candidate is deriving both sides' movement from the same endogenous economic state. Yet [S13: stocks and stage payoffs](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/SUPPLEMENT.md:1141) explicitly says no model derives the repeated-game payoffs from the stock dynamics. The arms are proved separately, joint illustrations hold rivals fixed, and real-world interlock is C0. This is a well-marked research program, not yet a unification theorem.

**What would discriminate.** [Test G](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/TESTS.md:126) correctly withdraws a claim that changing objectives distinguishes the model from structural realism. The hump-shaped response in one specified game can test that game, but is not a unique signature without a matched rival derivation. A stronger article would derive endogenous breakout, credible punishment, and objective weights in one strategic environment, then identify an intervention where its equilibrium prediction differs from an established baseline.

**Assessment:** high confidence in useful synthesis; high confidence that the split constraint alone does not establish strong novelty; moderate confidence that the unclosed interface blocks the proposed joint claim.

## Contemporary AI-race comparators

These are primary research comparators, not authorities whose conclusions must be accepted. Their 2026 working-paper status does not imply peer-reviewed certification.

- [Bueno de Mesquita, Dziuda, and Polborn, *The AGI Race and Existential Risk*](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35276), May 2026: firms allocate scarce resources between speed and safety; fragmentation can increase risk, and additional resources have parameter-dependent safety effects. V7.1 already discusses it, including the tension between reduced racing and increased capture under concentration ([S10](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/SUPPLEMENT.md:981)). The valuable next result is an equilibrium condition for when capture reverses concentration's safety benefit. Juxtaposing separately signed mechanisms does not establish that reversal or the best market structure. My independent retrieval reached the NBER abstract and indexed author-paper conclusion, not the full PDF.
- [Fudenberg and Koh, *Cooperating against Catastrophe*](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28291v1), September 2026: the safety frontier is exogenous and common disaster disciplines capability choices. Theorem 1(c) makes pacing an MPE and global racing not an MPE in its stated region; it does not establish uniqueness among all MPE. Proposition 4 gives risk-dependent effects of monitoring lag. V7.1 acknowledges these qualifications. A distinctive contribution would show which endogenous capture or stock-feedback mechanism removes or changes that pacing region under matched primitives. Failed detection alone is not such a result.
- [Choi, Jeon, and Menicucci, *AI Safety and Competition*](https://www.tse-fr.eu/fr/publications/ai-safety-and-competition-0), TSE WP 26-1745, May 2026: the official abstract reports both premature deployment and insufficient entry, with homogenization affecting the race-to-the-bottom region. It illustrates why a competitive-safety theory should specify when its prediction reverses. I did not find this work in V7.1's searched references; only its official abstract and indexed introduction were retrievable here. It is a lead for a full comparison, not a demonstrated theorem-level overlap.

## Acceptance obstacles

1. **The contribution is not yet isolated (high confidence).** A synthesis, several local theorems, a diagnosis, and an institutional program are all offered. Each additional domain increases the comparison burden; breadth alone does not close it. Lead with one estimand or theorem and show the difference from its closest baseline.
2. **Several headline novelties are conditional products of chosen primitives (high confidence).** The square-share return, exponential rate threshold, and effective-objective map may be valuable. Their usefulness must survive meaningful alternatives or explain an otherwise unresolved phenomenon. Labeling a derivation a theorem proves neither priority nor substantive importance.
3. **Strategic joins remain open (high confidence).** Locally fixed opponents, selected influence levels, exogenous response clocks, and externally specified stage payoffs do not collectively establish a strategic equilibrium of the complete process. S13 documents this honestly; a leading formal contribution must close the joins on which its principal result depends or narrow its claim to avoid them.
4. **The proposed distinguishing observations are not yet distinguishing evidence (high confidence).** Tests D and G admit no matched rival comparison. Test Γ has a stronger observable but needs a stronger baseline and perception identification. The §4 pilot described at [TESTS §8](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/TESTS.md:184) is a mechanism simulation with mixed results, not empirical confirmation of actual institutional capture. A theory article need not collect data, but then its new theoretical result must carry the contribution.
5. **Field relevance exceeds demonstrated field contribution (moderate-to-high confidence).** For political economy, a new equilibrium mechanism or credible empirical discrimination is the best route. For game theory, it needs a substantive equilibrium/generalization result. For mathematics or cybernetics, naming familiar stability, threshold, and feedback calculations does not establish a new mathematical method or control result. For technical AI alignment, governance relevance does not substitute for a distinct algorithm, evaluation method, benchmark, or formal alignment guarantee. These judgments concern this manuscript's contribution, not any venue's current submission rules.

## Strongest compact article route

Develop **“Endogenous oversight capture in technology races”** from §4. Keep one clear unit of analysis, one or two correction domains, an explicit entrusted purpose, and independent outcome measurement. Make the core question: *when does a fall in the cost of influencing oversight outrun the ability to keep oversight independent, and when do institutional floors prevent that outcome?*

The necessary new demonstration is a matched model comparison in which actors choose influence and rivals or correctors respond, showing exactly what the elasticity threshold and floor change relative to an endogenous-institutions baseline. Establish the conditions for failure and recovery, including transients; separate capacity optimization from actual evolutionary adjustment. Validate one key production/cost relation or supply a controlled experiment that can falsify it. A particularly strong result would determine when concentration's capture cost overturns its safety benefit in a common model, but that is an optional stronger extension, not something presently demonstrated.

This route has a defensible research question, observable mechanisms, and bounded claims. It does not require proving the whole meta-attractor story. I would seek specialist criticism of this compact contribution before representing it as ready for a leading journal.
