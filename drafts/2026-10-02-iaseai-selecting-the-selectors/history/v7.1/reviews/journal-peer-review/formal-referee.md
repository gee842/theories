# Simulated journal referee report: formal contribution of Theory V7.1

Review date: 4 October 2026. This is a simulated editorial/referee assessment, not an actual journal decision or independent human certification. I read the current source before consulting any earlier review; this report does not rely on earlier AI reviews. It is a bounded contribution review, with spot inspection of important derivations and a targeted primary-literature search. I did not rerun the numerical suite or audit every proof.

**Recommendation: reject in its current form as a research article for leading economic/game-theory or mathematical/control journals; invite a substantially narrower, newly developed paper rather than represent the work required as an ordinary revision.** There is a worthwhile research programme here, with an especially promising capture-and-correction module. I do not presently see a demonstrated contribution of the scale and distinctness needed for JET/GEB/ReStud-style theory publication or leading applied mathematics/control publication. This is a judgment about the submitted work, not a claim that the underlying idea cannot become publishable. Pure theory does not need a dataset by default, and elementary mathematics can support a major contribution. Neither mathematical complexity nor the number of passed checks decides the question.

## What the paper currently establishes

The best description is a **structured collection of conditional mechanism models with several useful connections**, rather than a proved unified theory of a meta-attractor. The manuscript itself correctly states this in [S13:3](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:3) and [S13:56](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:56). Its abstract and scope now avoid claiming that their conjunction is proved. That candour improves scientific reliability; it also makes the gap between the research programme and a unified contribution visible.

## Ranked reasons for the recommendation

**1. The incremental contribution is not yet demonstrated against its closest theoretical alternatives.** The paper says its addition is “mainly organization” at [§1.4:52](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/10_s01.md:52). Organization can be valuable, but the present catalogue does not establish that its central insight was unavailable in existing models or that its new expressions change an important conclusion. A useful test is to remove the terminology and ask which proposition a specialist must now believe that the nearest predecessor could not show.

The bounded literature search found substantive comparators, not merely matching vocabulary:

- [Acemoglu and Robinson, *Persistence of Power, Elites, and Institutions* (AER, 2008)](https://topcat.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.98.1.267) already models de facto power investment offsetting institutional reform and economic institutions persisting after political change. This is directly adjacent to reform re-absorption, though it does not establish the present paper's particular rate law.
- [Acemoglu, Egorov and Sonin, *Dynamics and Stability of Constitutions, Coalitions, and Clubs* (AER, 2012)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.102.4.1446) develops a general framework in which current decisions change future political power and characterizes dynamically stable states. “Selection changes future selection” therefore needs a more specific contribution claim than the reflexive description alone.
- [Jeon and Hwang, *The Emergence and Persistence of Oligarchy* (author working-paper version)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3177771), subsequently published in JET, studies an infinite-horizon bargaining game with endogenous recognition probabilities, voting weights and resource distribution, yielding concentrated long-run political power. This is an appropriate strategic benchmark for what is absent from the current fixed-share influence calculation.
- [Powell, *Absolute and Relative Gains in International Relations Theory* (APSR, 1991)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/absolute-and-relative-gains-in-international-relations-theory/0018BA1A141FE29A522EEDAA2A75826E) derives changing relative-gains concerns from constraints rather than primitive preferences. The current common-versus-relative reinvestment decomposition could sharpen this programme; its original contribution needs a direct model comparison.
- [Bombardini, *Firm Heterogeneity and Lobby Participation* (JIE, 2008)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022199608000275) already derives largest-firm participation in lobbying under fixed participation costs. The current per-unit-floor distinction and exact rate law differ, but “larger carriers cultivate influence” alone is insufficient differentiation.

These comparisons do **not** establish that the exact formulae were previously published. I have not completed a priority search. Some comparators are already cited by the manuscript; the deficiency is not primarily missing citations, but missing analytical comparison demonstrating what the new model adds. In particular, [S4:15](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/04_S4.md:15) says whether a dynamic-constitutions model reproduces Section 4 is open.

**2. The central causal chain is not closed where the substantive economic work occurs.** The problem is not that every paper must model every institution. It is that this paper's main synthesis requires several transfers that the solved models do not provide:

- Stock accumulation does not derive the repeated game's temptation, cooperative value, decisive value or punishment value: [S13:12](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:12).
- The local fixed-opponent effective objective does not supply the common and relative derivatives of a full strategic continuation value: [S13:10](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:10).
- Contest-generated concentration is not connected by a joint theorem to endogenous influence cultivation across domains: [S13:14](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:14) and [S13:20](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:20).
- Capture raising the value of punishment, and correction resources producing effective coverage, remain open: [S13:23–25](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:23).

The “scissors” conjunction is therefore a hypothesis with examples, not an equilibrium comparative static. More seriously for claims that every actor computes correctly yet is worse off, the manuscript offers a symmetric stage comparison and explicitly leaves the dynamic welfare comparison open at [§3.5:84](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/30_s3.md:84). A top economic-theory paper needs the incentive and welfare comparisons evaluated in the same model.

**3. The best new-looking result is still too dependent on imposed technology and adjustment to bear the paper's breadth.** The square-share law comes from one factor of share in support production and another in the decisions receiving the support; it is derived cleanly in [S4:11](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/04_S4.md:11). It is not a general theorem that political influence must scale quadratically. At fixed share, with linear influence benefit and a power cost, the first-order condition gives the selected capacity. Substituting exponentially changing cost and correction gives the rate law: [S4:25–33](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/04_S4.md:25).

The worthwhile insight is correction's double role: it directly dissipates support and reduces the incentive to cultivate it. The floor, lag and initial-state qualifications make that insight more informative. The paper already treats size-cost elasticities, regularly varying marginal costs (with a separate equality caveat), super-polynomial marginal costs, and a concave-conversion variation: [§4.3:49–51](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/40_s4.md:49) and [S4:39](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/04_S4.md:39). These are meaningful robustness results; the objection is not that only a quadratic example exists. But the necessary half depends on capacity tracking its instantaneous selected level; gradual selection can remain bounded and avoid eventual capture even below the advertised rate threshold, as the manuscript explains at [§4.3:51](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/40_s4.md:51). Thus optimizing actors and evolutionary selection are materially different cases. The paper should choose a primitive mechanism and solve it, or make their difference its contribution. Calling the optimum “selected” does not supply the selection process.

**4. The dramatic outcome and proposed alternative are largely conditional classifications, not comparative institutional results.** The survival theorem's important empirical content lies in the residual hazard premise. The theorem does not establish that the proposed governance path satisfies the opposite bound. Failed evaluation also does not supply that floor: [S13:35–37](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/supp/13_S13.md:35). The generative alternative assumes effective common constraints, independent correction, coverage and financing; its attraction remains conjectural at [§10.4:75–77](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/a0_s10.md:75). Those can be acceptable design premises, but they cannot establish the feasibility or dominance of that arrangement over regulated competition without an incentive-compatible model of the institutions themselves.

An operational symptom is the requirement for a structural gain below one at the present distribution of standing in [§10.4:68](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/a0_s10.md:68). The proved scalar gain is local at parity, while the off-parity capture link is open. This is presently a design target needing a defined certificate, not an available general certification procedure.

**5. Leading mathematical/control journals would need a different mathematical centre.** A scalar loop threshold, a convex first-order condition, cooperative-system comparison, a deviation inequality and a hazard product do not become a new general theorem by being put in the same document. The tempo bound itself is attributed to existing control theory at [§5.3:76](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/50_s5.md:76). A credible applied-math route would establish a new result for the coupled system—such as a sharp transient safety/viability characterization with endogenous influence, heterogeneous correction and delayed responses—or demonstrate a consequential new phenomenon with convincing analysis. More technical notation alone would not help.

## Strengths and the strongest standalone prospect

The manuscript makes an important distinction between aligning an AI to its principal and improving the game among principals. It separates concentration, capture, damage and irreversible loss rather than silently identifying them. It also separates a certificate of eventual recovery from present safety, exposes the difference between common and relative reinvestment returns, and gives useful counter-regimes in which AI-assisted correction or cost floors defeat the pessimistic asymptotics. The explicit interface register is unusually helpful.

**My strongest candidate is a focused paper on endogenous influence and the dynamic resilience of correction.** Its concrete question would be: when technology lowers the cost of influencing institutions, how must independent correction adapt, and which interventions prevent capture rather than merely delay it? The double effect of correction, the difference between per-unit and fixed entry costs, splitting under different cost elasticities, and the difference between optimization and gradual adaptation could support a coherent contribution. Its strongest present result is the conditional rate threshold together with those boundaries—not the square-share algebra in isolation.

A second, more difficult candidate is a paper deriving relative-gains incentives and enforcement failure from one dynamic contest. This could connect the continuation-price insight to a genuinely new equilibrium result. It is less mature because the strategic objective reduction and lifetime welfare comparison are still open.

## Three decisive improvements

1. **Select one research question and demonstrate the incremental result against the nearest primary models.** For the recommended capture paper, state in ordinary economic terms one conclusion unavailable from the closest institutional-persistence and lobbying models, then prove it. Give a side-by-side table of primitives, equilibrium/selection concept, results and genuinely different predictions. Move the broad worldview, AI diagnosis and institutional manifesto to a companion synthesis rather than requiring the theorem paper to establish all of them.

2. **Close one load-bearing model interface, not every interface in the programme.** The minimal coherent capture version would endogenize influence investment/adjustment and at least one source of market share or political power, include correction's own resource/incentive constraint, and establish the relevant dynamic outcome and transient safety bound. If the alternative is the standing/enforcement paper, derive its cooperation, deviation, decisive-state and punishment values from the same stock contest and compare lifetime welfare. A strategically complete simple model can be sufficient; a large model is not inherently better.

3. **Produce a discriminating prediction from the completed model and an independently checkable proof package.** Preserve the existing cost-elasticity, regular-variation, super-polynomial-cost, floor and adaptation results; do not merely add harder algebra or repeat those extensions. Test whether the substantive policy ranking survives endogenous strategic responses, changes in institutional authority, and the interaction of market shares with influence. Identify a reversal or a robust necessity/sufficiency boundary that distinguishes this mechanism from the closest predecessor. State how competing mechanisms would be distinguished, with data only where the paper retains claims about actual AI trajectories. Resolve the optimal-penal-code extension flagged at [§5.2:36](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7.1/src/core/50_s5.md:36) if retaining the general enforcement claim. Have a human specialist independently examine the final focal theorem and equilibrium construction.

These are research tasks, not copyediting tasks. Completing them could make a serious journal submission; it would not guarantee acceptance.

## Confidence and limits

- **Internal validity — medium confidence:** the main elementary calculations inspected (rank-one stability/resolvent, influence first-order condition and exponential rate comparison, deviation accounting, conditional hazard bound) look sound. I found no fresh clear counterexample in this bounded review. Confidence is lower for the full collection, the differentiability/envelope extensions, stochastic-game claims and unproved interfaces. I have not certified every theorem.
- **Novelty — medium confidence in close conceptual/technical precedents; low confidence in exact priority:** the broad concepts and several main proof techniques have close predecessors, but priority of the exact correction-rate and standing-price formulas is unresolved. The targeted search establishes adjacency, not absence of originality. A full specialist literature comparison is still needed.
- **Editorial forecast — high confidence about current nonreadiness:** current broad V7.1 is unlikely to be accepted as a leading economic/game-theory or applied-math/control research article without substantial restructuring and additional original research. Confidence about which focused successor could succeed is low, because that depends on results not yet obtained and editorial judgment. No defensible precise acceptance percentage follows from this review.

The 239 reported checks, accumulated corrections and previous AI reviews are useful development evidence. They do not establish originality, significance, model realism or journal acceptance, and none was treated as such here.
