# V6.1 tests, cruxes and research programme

Companion to [THEORY.md](THEORY.md) §7.4 and §12, [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md) and [CLAIMS.md](CLAIMS.md). It holds what the core points to: the cruxes in full, the conditions registered in advance and what each would change, the tests with the rival theories that make the same or the opposite prediction, the indicators of the conditional results, the research programme, and the note on independence of review. Tests name roles ("leading developer", "leading state"), not particular states or firms; data sources are listed at the end.

## 4. What would redirect the argument

Three outcomes would redirect it, classified before they are seen.
1. **Correction demonstrably scales.** Contestable coverage keeps pace with hazardous variety under present incentives, and residual risk falls. If that requires separating evaluators from success, it is a change of selection; if not, it is a successful continuation, and the theory was wrong about C1.
2. **An implementable safeguard makes continuation adequately safe.** A safeguard installed without changing the shared parameters meets the protection standard and stays durable as stakes and speed grow, classified before the outcome by the descriptive profile of THEORY Definition 8.4. This refutes the claim that the conditions must change.
3. **The designed arrangement performs worse.** A stress-tested design fails worse than a credible continuation policy. This rejects that design, not the case for leaving the race.

## 6. Indicators of the conditional results

**The three objects, measured separately.** What actors value: the weight on others and on the host, and the weight on winning (charters, incentive contracts, doctrines, the two-perturbation identification of SUPPLEMENT S3, with the limit that it identifies $(1+\theta)\psi$). What actors believe: about rivals' objectives and capabilities, the shared hazard, and whether they are in a struggle for survival (C8). What institutions reward, permit and protect: convertibility, decisiveness, conversion into evaluators and rules, row sums of correctors, and provision not won in the contest.

**The slow route (THEORY Theorem 4.12).** (reach): per harm class, capability with safeguards removed. (a) cumulative relative selection: capital, compute and talent following rank; an average effective liability equal to the advantage is not enough, since $\tau_{\rm eff}=\nu-1/(1+t)$ has average tending to $\nu$ while the cumulative advantage diverges. (b) appropriable control: developers' roles in evaluation and standards. (c) the two rates: AI's cheapening of control against that of independent evaluation plus budget growth. (d) masking: evaluator bias against ground truth at relevant harm levels. (e) order of events: release-to-evaluation time against harm-to-detection time.

**Enforcement and branches.** (decisive), (fast), (unsaturated) and the breakout probability: persistence of leads and their conversion into lasting share; the open–closed gap; detection latency against the time from breakout to decisive capability (C7); whether punishment would itself be a race (THEORY Proposition 5.4(5)).

**Coverage.** (variety grows): predefined hazard modes per class and their count per model generation; capture–recapture across independent red teams of different lineage. (coverage lags): coverage measured by the four conditions, with catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours, rates of overrule, and audit coverage. (residual floor): residual-risk estimates per class after correction by independent evaluators, stated as safety cases with numerical bounds. (shared failure): propagation pathways.

**The arrangements.** E1: share of consequential decisions requiring human authorization with a minimum time for deliberation; drill success of parallel capacity; rates of human overrule. E2: thresholds for automated AI research, task length and autonomous replication; efficiency-corrected compute caps tightening about two and a half to three times a year at an eight-month efficiency doubling; whether any decisive-class capability exists outside common custody. E3: a dangerous-capability doubling time, with $a\approx\ln2/T_2$ (a seven-month doubling gives about 0.10 a month), and the lag $L$ from appearance to withdrawal, robust when model error is below $e^{-aL}$ (0.55, 0.30 and 0.09 for $L$ of 6, 12 and 24 months).

**Undoing capture.** As a reform brings inbound conversion toward one, recovery at the captured state slows, so rising autocorrelation in shares indicates progress. The field model's conversions can be recovered from series of domain shares (SUPPLEMENT S4).

## 7. Data for the tests

Test A: a corpus of traced changes to developers' safety frameworks (Zhu 2026), with release dates, financing rounds and leadership changes. Test B: lobbying disclosure registers; drafting participation in codes of practice for general-purpose AI; authorship shares of benchmarks and standards. Test C: events separating signals of intent (dialogues, incident channels) from capability shocks. Test D: natural experiments in which rules were withdrawn, evaluation bodies refocused, or obligations delayed. Test E: interventions on formula funding or enforced admission compared with disclosure duties. Test F: organizations with capped or non-convertible returns; public consortia. Test G: multi-agent experiments on shared resources and escalation, with endogenous evaluators. Test I: access terms, funding sources and staff flows of the leading jurisdiction's evaluators. Test J: dates of first race framings against compute allocation and safety staffing. Test K: cross-industry comparison. Test L: release notes, system cards and incident reports, coded in advance. Test M: statutes, funding instruments and enforcement records. Test Γ: variation in public provision of compute, evaluation infrastructure and services across jurisdictions.

## 8. Research programme

The author's pilot experiment on the mechanism of THEORY §4 ran once with 20 seeds: of its four predictions, the first failed and three held, the failure attributed to too few lineages and to drift (EV12). A second design is specified and has not been run.
- **Study 1. Selection with an endogenous evaluator.** A fixed selector, a selector that drifts by a matched amount, and a selector shaped by success, with outcome measures independent of the selector, optional capture, costly interference, and room for correction to succeed.
- **Study 2. Models deferred from the argument.** A joint simulation in which conversion capacity evolves by selection together with the field model, to test the quasi-static assumption of Assumption 4.2 (failure would show as a certificate failing at a correction growth rate slightly above the rate law); a slow variable for the share of decision positions held by the winning model, with possible bistability and hysteresis; threshold and vector hosts with the correctable kernel as the warning condition; and the effect of an alternative lineage on dwell times.
- **Study 3. Governance of correctors and infrastructure.** An early safety improvement crossed with later power to amend evaluation; human procurement and governance of evaluators under success; shared infrastructure under asymmetric dependence, with enforcement against the leading provider, continuity of service and appeal.
- **Study 4. Endogenous measurement [conj].** The four levels of epistemic closure: capture of measurement, of interpretation, of the concepts in which a failure could be expressed, and of preference.
- **Study 5. Shared prediction and coordination.** Groups of people, and separately of AI agents, play a repeated contest with a shared hazard; treatments vary whether the hazard's derivation is common knowledge, demonstrated in an explorable simulation, or merely asserted, and whether winnings convert into capacity; outcomes are intensity, the informativeness of messages, and the adoption of common constraints.

A simulator that rewards capture directly establishes only its programmed mechanism; each study needs independent outcome measures and real alternatives. None is a prerequisite for the practical judgment, which rests on the protection standard and the evidence; each would strengthen or weaken a specific link.

## 9. Independence of review

The drafts of this theory, the planning memos that shaped V6.1 and most of its reviews were written by systems of one model family, which share preferences for unified and elegant models, for corpora in which cooperative framings are common, and for absorbing counterexamples into a higher level of the same framework. Their agreement is weaker evidence than its volume suggests. Before any claim is relied on it needs review by people and systems outside that family, above all from structural realism, industrial economics and the history of arms control, and on the new formal results: Proposition 2.2 (one loop gain) and Proposition 3.9 (the price of standing), including whether the first-order result survives in the full stochastic game; Proposition 5.4 (the enforcement condition), with the least-value punishment in a game with an absorbing state; Corollary 5.5 and whether its counterexample rests on a weak equilibrium; Lemma 3.3 (incidence and evasion); Lemma 3.10; Proposition 2.3 (compounding with provision); Proposition 5.15 (talk among three or more); Proposition 9.1 (requirements by kind); and the quasi-static assumption of Section 4.
