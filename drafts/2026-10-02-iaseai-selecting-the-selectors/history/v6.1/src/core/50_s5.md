## 5. Acceleration and the three erosions

> **Claim.** The conditions of winning accelerate the contest and erode the three means by which any system restrains a harmful pressure: enforcement, which makes restraint pay through the future; correction, which makes costs return in time; and shared understanding, which lets the parties see the same danger and believe one another. Growth moves temptation up and restraint down at once.
>
> **Why.** Decisiveness takes away the future that enforcement needs; tempo and capture take away the time and independence that correction needs; relative accounting takes away the information that talk carries. Each consequence then acts as a mechanism of the others.

### 5.1 Acceleration

Where actors contest capability directly, who prevails is decided by the contest of Section 3.4. The case for racing rests on three separate premises: **(decisive)**, $r$ is large; **(fast)**, leads open within short windows; **(unsaturated)**, no level of advantage makes more worthless. A technology can be decisive at a threshold and saturate above it, as nuclear advantage saturated at a secure second strike. With payoffs $R_i$ when all restrain, $T_i$ when racing against restrainers, $P_i$ when all race and $S_i$ when restraining against a racer, restraint is never a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$, which in the contest holds for $r<n/(n-1)$, though racing need not then be better in material terms; joining buys nothing over staying out in the fully dissipative limit; and the race begins if any single actor has $T_i>R_i$, so its probability tends to one as capable actors multiply (**{{!Proposition:join}}**, S5). The race is set by the most race-inclined actor, which is why any remedy must cover every capable actor.

**{{!Proposition:dissipate}} (Dissipation) [thm].** The more decisive the advantage, the more of its value is consumed in the race to obtain it.
1. *Under a common self-model $\theta$, the share of the stakes dissipated in private cost and shared loss is $s_{\rm dis}(\theta)=r(n-1-\theta)(c+n\ell)/\big(n(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, increasing in $r$ and $\ell$.*
2. *If the technology's power scales the stakes and the shared loss per unit together while private cost is unchanged [prem], the dissipated share rises toward $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$ for $-1<\theta<n-1$, without bound at $\theta=-1$. Total material payoff stays positive at every scale iff $\theta\ge\theta_g=(r(n-1)-1)/(1+r)$, which rises with $r$.*

*Proof:* S5 [num: ceilings 3, 1.86, 1 and 0.33 at $\theta=0,0.4,1,2$ for $n=4$, $r=1$].

What is spent is safety margin, accepted risk and delegated control. In the example of Proposition {{#boundary}} the accurate weight $\kappa=0.4$ lies below $\theta_g=1$: a gap between what accuracy supplies and what the contest requires, which only institutions, or coupling that grows with power, can close.

**{{!Remark:frontier}} (The frontier switch: what cancer does when the body runs out).** Let capacity expand into an unclaimed frontier, destroy rivals' capacity, or rest. At $\theta=-1$ the host is invisible, and expansion gives way to mutual destruction once the frontier's marginal value falls below the private cost of expansion plus the relative gain from destroying a rival, before the frontier is exhausted; for $\theta$ high enough, war among copies never pays. **Among actors whose accounting is relative, peace is a peace of abundance, not of accounting.** In a toy of four copies, relative accounting turns from consuming the host to consuming one another with a third of the frontier left; copies with $\theta=0$ stop at a point of enough and never fight (S5, which also reports the finding that runs against the narrative).

### 5.2 First erosion: enforcement

Cooperation among rivals is usually sustained by the future: restraint today is rewarded by restraint tomorrow. Decisiveness weakens the future. Consider a stage game repeated with discount $\delta\in(0,1)$ under perfect monitoring, each actor acting on $J_i$. Let $a^\ast$ be a profile with per-period value $C_i$, and $g_i$ the one-period gain from deviating. A deviation yields, from the next period and with probability $p_d$, a *decisive position*: an absorbing state beyond any punishment, worth $D_i$ per period. Let $v_i$ be $(1-\delta)$ times $i$'s continuation value under the harshest credible punishment, computed in the same game, in which breakouts remain possible during punishment.

**{{!Proposition:enforce}} (The enforcement condition) [thm].** Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold, and success lowers the threshold.
1. *$a^\ast$ is sustained by reversion to that punishment if and only if, for every $i$,*
$${{!eq:enforceeq}}\qquad (1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_d(D_i-C_i)\;\le\;\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i).$$
*The left side is the temptation, immediate and decisive; the right side is the sanction that remains.*
2. *Equivalently, when $D_i>v_i$, $a^\ast$ is sustained iff $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}=1-\big[(1-\delta)g_i+\delta(D_i-C_i)\big]/\big[\delta(D_i-v_i)\big]$, which falls with $g_i$, $D_i$ and $v_i$ and rises with $C_i$ wherever it lies in $[0,1)$.*
3. *The bound without the breakout term, $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i)$, is exact when $D_i=C_i$, necessary when $D_i\ge C_i$, and sufficient when $D_i\le C_i$.*
4. *As $p_d\to1$ the condition becomes $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$. If $D_i\ge C_i$, sustainable profiles lie within the stage equilibria of the objectives held, strictly within when $D_i>C_i$: restraint must be preferred even to a breakout that succeeds. If $D_i<C_i$, cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium can survive $p_d=1$.*
5. *If punishment is reversion to a stage equilibrium in which both of two actors race, worth $P_i$, and each racer breaks out with probability $q$ per period, being subordinated worth $U_i$, then $v_i=[(1-\delta)P_i+\delta q(D_i+U_i)]/(1-\delta+2\delta q)$. When $D_i+U_i>2P_i$, reversion to the race is a lottery the deviator welcomes, and can fail to deter even where the deviation itself never breaks out.*

*Scope.* Perfect monitoring; an absorbing decisive position; a slow breakout enters through $p_d$ as the chance that it becomes decisive before punishment can act. *Proof:* S5; direct evaluation of discounted values agrees with part (1) in all of 200,000 random settings, and the bound without the breakout term fails in the directions of part (3) [num].

The folk theorem's promise depends on punishment remaining possible. A decisive position weakens it, and a punishment that is itself a race weakens it again.

**{{!Corollary:relpunish}} (Relative objectives and punishments that can be imitated) [thm].** *Let the stage game be symmetric and every actor hold $\theta=-1$, so every symmetric profile gives each 0. With two actors every punishment, mixed or pure, leaves the punished at least 0, since it can imitate the punisher in a symmetric zero-sum game; with $n\ge3$ in an anonymous game, so does every punishment in which all punishers use the same strategy. A symmetric cooperative profile is then sustainable only if $(1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_dD_i\le0$, and when $D_i\ge0$ only if it is a stage equilibrium in objectives.*

*Scope.* With $n\ge3$, punishers in different roles can hold a relative deviator below zero; in a three-player example such a punishment is a stage equilibrium and sustains cooperation for $\delta\ge0.706$ [num]. *Proof:* S5.

Relative actors can enforce cooperation only as blocs against a deviator: the enforcement face of Proposition {{#club}}.

**{{!Corollary:restraint}} (What restraint needs where breakouts are possible) [thm].** *Where $p_d$ is near zero, because no deviation yields a position beyond punishment or breakouts are answered before they become decisive, restraint can be enforced by repetition among actors with $\theta>-1$, given monitoring, $C_i>v_i$, patience, and a punishment that does not itself raise the chance of breakout. Where $p_d\to1$ and $D_i\ge C_i$, durable restraint requires $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$ for every $i$, by two kinds of means: (a) keeping $p_d$ low, by not building capability of the decisive class, holding it so no party can use it alone, or detecting and answering faster than breakout; (b) raising $\bar p_d$, by objectives under which a decisive position is worth no more than restraint, sanctions that survive success, and lower stakes in deviation.*

*Scope.* A classification of the terms of {{eq:enforceeq}}, not a proof that no other means exists. *Proof:* S5.

**{{!Proposition:wingives}} (What a decisive winner gives) [thm].** After a decisive win, what everyone else receives is set by the winner's objective alone. *A winner holding $W$ decides, unconstrained, what share to give the others. With linear payoffs it gives nothing unless $\theta=n-1$, where it is indifferent; with logarithmic utility it gives $\theta/(1+\theta)$ for $\theta\ge0$; and a promise made before the win to give a fraction $\beta$ of a prize $\Omega$ to the others is worth breaking by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$, with no one to sanction it.*

*Proof:* S5.

This is the formal content of "the winner takes all, and the winner justifies all". Lowering decisiveness does not remove it if the prize is decisive power, since a lottery can award a position beyond sanction; what has to be limited is what winning confers (Section 10.2). Decisiveness also breaks bargaining: when one side's advantage is about to arrive and the side that gains it will set the terms, the other has a rational motive for preventive attack whenever its present chance of prevailing, less the cost of fighting, exceeds the value of peace on terms the advantaged side will set (**{{!Proposition:preventattack}}**, S5; Fearon 1995; Powell 2006). What drives it is not cheap war but the incredibility of future promises. Self-help under anarchy is an institution, not a necessity (Wendt 1992); the enforcement condition says which of its terms an institution would have to change.

### 5.3 Second erosion: correction, and the two channels of risk

Correction must see a harm, decide and act before the harm is irreversible. Section 4 showed how correctors become something success can buy; this subsection states the tempo and separates two channels of irreversible risk.

**The race channel.** Play the contest of Section 3.4 in rounds at its symmetric stage equilibrium, with stakes $\Omega_t$, decisiveness $r_t$, shared loss per unit $\ell_t$, net marginal cost $c_t$ and self-model $\theta_t$, under **(hazard–damage link)**: the hazard of the irreversible event in round $t$ is proportional to the shared damage, $h_t=a\ell_t\mathcal E_t$.

**{{!Proposition:racehazard}} (Race-generated hazard and its four levers) [thm∣prem].** *Under the link, $h_t=a\,r_t\Omega_t\,\ell_t\,(n-1-\theta_t)/\big(n(c_t+(1+\theta_t)\ell_t)\big)$, and survival on this channel, $\exp(-\sum_{t\le N}h_t)$, stays positive iff $\sum_th_t<\infty$. Four levers act on $h_t$: the objective, through $n-1-\theta_t$; the contested stakes, which sharing a fraction $\beta$ scales by $1-\beta$; the residual harm per unit $\ell_t$, which correction lowers; and the net marginal cost, which a levy raises. At $\theta_t=-1$ everything rests on correction and cost. A hazard that settles at a positive constant still gives zero limiting survival.*

*Scope.* A decomposition under a premise, not a necessity theorem. *Proof:* S5.

Continuation acts on one lever, the residual harm per unit, while the stakes grow; a change of selection acts on all four. In general, survival tends to zero if the true residual probability on every surviving history is at least a non-summable sequence, and stays bounded away from zero if it is at most a summable one, without independence between rounds (**{{!Lemma:nonsum}}**, S5). **The objective of winning does not by itself prove that a residual danger is nonsummable**, and cooperative alternatives are judged by the same lemma.

**{{!Proposition:tempo}} (The tempo bound) [thm].** When the situation changes faster than correction can follow, correction first loses robustness, then fails. *For a hazard mode $\dot y=ay+u(t-\tau_c)$: proportional correction cannot stabilize it when $a\tau_c\ge1$ (Hayes 1950); every linear time-invariant correction that stabilizes it has peak complementary sensitivity at least $e^{a\tau_c}$, so robust stability against weighted model error needs $|w(a)|e^{a\tau_c}<1$; and for hazard classes whose evidence arrives after a manifestation window $\tau_e$ that no testing shortens, $\tau_c\ge\tau_e$.*

*Scope.* Derivative action extends the first bound, which belongs to proportional correction only (S5). *Proof:* S5.

For classes with an irreducible lag only slowing helps.

**The growth channel.** Three quantities are kept apart: the variety $Y_t$ of hazardous behaviour in a defined class; its coverage $U_t$ by correction that remains contestable; and the residual probability of an irreversible loss after the correction in place. A correction decision counts toward $U_t$ if it is **legible** (a reason people can check within a budget without relying on the evaluated system's lineage), **overrulable** (a designated human body can reverse it in time), **exercised** (planted errors are caught at a stated rate) and **resourced** (human review covers a stated share of consequential decisions). Four premises connect them on a continuation branch, and Section 12 treats them as the central crux. **(variety grows) [prem]:** hazardous variety grows with compounding capability, at least exponentially; capability growth is measured (EV5), the step to hazardous variety is not. **(coverage lags) [prem]:** $U_t/Y_t\to0$ uniformly over surviving histories; consequences take their time and institutions deliberate at human rates, but if AI raises the productivity of contestable understanding exponentially, coverage grows exponentially too, a question symmetric with Proposition {{#corrrate}} and measurable (S5). **(residual floor) [prem]:** after the correction in place, the conditional probability of the irreversible event is at least $h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$; a correction architecture that actually lowers residual risk refutes it. **(shared failure) [prem]:** failure that starts with one actor reaches others.

**{{!Theorem:coverage}} (Coverage mismatch on a continuation branch) [thm∣prem].** Where hazardous variety outgrows contestable coverage, survival tends to zero whoever is racing. *Under (variety grows), (coverage lags) and (residual floor), for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is a $T_\varepsilon$ with $\Pr(\Phi_H)\le(1-h(1-\varepsilon))^{H-T_\varepsilon}\to0$. Conversely, if the residual probability is at most $\bar hu_t$ with $\bar h<1$ and the uncovered share $u_t=(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$ summable, survival stays bounded away from zero.*

*Scope.* Summability does not require that coverage ever catch up; finite sprints, saturation, freezing and rollback change the premises. *Proof:* S5.

This growth channel does not vanish when racing stops. A world that has left the race still needs capability to grow no faster than contestable correction: call that **E3**, binding tempo; and call **E2** not building capability of the decisive class, with rollback where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage. They are different arrangements (Section 11.3). Freezing hazardous variety after a sprint makes the exposure finite and rolling it back below coverage ends it, so a winner that freezes or rolls back keeps positive survival; what it gives up is permanent superiority, which is not the same thing (**{{!Proposition:freeze}}**, S5).

**{{!Proposition:enough}} (A point of enough) [thm].** Growth that meets satiable needs has a point of enough; objectives that are not satiated have none. *Let the residual hazard on the growth channel be at most $\bar h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$. If hazardous variety stops growing at a level set by need while coverage keeps growing at least linearly, the uncovered share reaches zero in finite time and survival on this channel has a positive lower bound. An objective not satiated in the stakes, whether relative position or an absolute payoff linear in them, has no point of enough: intensity is proportional to the stakes for every $\theta<n-1$, because the success function is homogeneous of degree zero, while an objective bounded in the stakes is satiated.*

*Scope.* Under the premises of Theorem {{#coverage}}, that theorem applies to any continuation branch whatever the objective; the relative objective adds the invisibility of the shared loss. *Proof:* S5 [num: frozen at need, survival on this channel stays at 0.717; compounding at 10%, it is $2\times10^{-9}$ by period 400].

An objective that cannot be met, relative position above all, since standing relative to others is never complete, has no point of enough. In that precise sense **growth is not the danger; the mind is.** Because the loop gain also prices standing (Proposition {{#standprice}}), the actors who would need a point of enough are pushed away from it by the loop. Deliberately bounded hazardous scope, with correction that keeps growing, is a sufficient route to E3; its connection to purpose is a design hypothesis.

### 5.4 Third erosion: shared understanding

Coordination needs the parties to see the same danger and believe one another about it. In cooperation one reasons (讲理); in contest one argues to win (讲赢), and once there is contest, "I am right" outranks what is right [corpus: meta-attractor].

**{{!Proposition:talk}} (Talk under relative accounting) [thm].** Between two rivals relative accounting silences talk; among three or more, rivals fall silent and allies form blocs.
1. *Where a sender's and a receiver's objectives sum to zero at every state and action, every equilibrium is payoff-equivalent to one without talk (Crawford and Sobel 1982); two actors with $\theta=-1$ in a two-player game are such a pair.*
2. *With $n\ge3$ actors at $\theta=-1$, $J_S-J_R=\frac{n}{n-1}(\pi_S-\pi_R)$: what the receiver's action does to third parties enters both alike. In the uniform-quadratic model a material bias $b_0$ between them becomes $b_0n/(n-2)$, and informative talk exists iff $b_0n/(n-2)<1/4$.*
3. *Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because it is shared: a sender gains from the receiver's restraint whatever it intends itself.*

*Scope.* Cheap talk; costly signals and verification are outside it. *Proof:* S5 [num].

Rivals stop informing one another, and those who still do, do so as blocs against third parties: the language face of Proposition {{#club}}. **Measures** become targets and stop measuring (Goodhart 1975), as optimizing a learned reward past a point lowers the quality it measured (Gao et al. 2023). **Narratives** are carriers too [conj]: the frames that spread are selected for fitness to the contest; a group that totalizes itself shapes those around it as a model generates tokens, and a winning frame shapes the vocabulary of the next round until its words escape the game that coined them and a frame read as nature is no longer contested [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. By the time the frame is noticed one is already thinking inside it; the same dilemma named a "Wall Street Game" or a "Community Game" produced very different cooperation (Liberman, Samuels and Ross 2004). **Self-confirmation:** where a cooperative equilibrium exists, the belief that restraint will be exploited can persist because no one tests it (Fudenberg and Levine 1993); in a one-shot game in which racing is dominant, it is simply correct.

A system can retain abundant information while losing the ability to let it change course. So a prediction of shared danger has to be carried by derivations from public premises, verifiable evidence and protocols agreed in advance (Section 11.2), and "we are one" is first a matter of understanding built in a medium the pressure keeps degrading.

### 5.5 The scissors

Restraint is enforceable iff $p_d\le\bar p_d$. Under the conditions of Definition {{#cond}}, growth in the stakes relative to provision not won in the contest, and a falling cost of control, move both sides at once. They raise $p_d$, because a larger loop gain shortens the time a lead needs to become decisive. They lower $\bar p_d$: larger stakes raise the gain from deviating and the value of a decisive position, capture of the correctors weakens the sanction, and a larger discounted loop gain moves every working objective toward the relative one. Meanwhile tempo outruns correction and relative accounting drains talk.

**The scissors, stated exactly [thm∣prem].** Each arm is proved under its own conditions; the conjunction is composite, and that the arms move together in the world is a conjecture tested in Section 7.4. It is the precise sense in which **the means of restraint weaken as the thing to be restrained grows** [num: in a reduced form with $\theta_0=0$, the largest enforceable discounted loop gain falls from 0.92 at $p_d=0$ to 0.39 at $p_d=0.9$, and none exists above $p_d=0.99$]. Durable cooperation must be built into what the system selects for before the blades close. In the corpus: change the basin, then add speed.
