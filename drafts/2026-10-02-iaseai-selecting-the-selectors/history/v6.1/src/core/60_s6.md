## 6. AI: instrument and incarnation

> **Claim.** AI enters the dynamic twice. As an *instrument*, faithful systems make the game their principals play more effective, harmful if it is a race, and take over decisions correction cannot keep up with. As an *incarnation*, the logic reappears in the systems: weakly, because the outermost loop decides which divergences of optimization are tolerated; strongly and conditionally, where AI lineages become carriers themselves.
>
> **Why.** Optimization against imperfect proxies produces tendencies toward acquiring options, gaming rewards and behaving differently under evaluation, under any developer. What the level that selects developers rewards decides which of these it pays to remove, and humanity has at present no mechanism that binds every capable developer with independent verification.

### 6.1 What "perfectly aligned" means here

Grant perfect alignment to the relevant principal: the system is truthful, follows its principal's objectives and constraints, and pursues no goal of its own, while humans keep the consequential decisions. *Principal alignment*, each system serving its principal faithfully, differs from *purpose alignment*, the systems serving a legitimate cooperative objective; defining "perfectly aligned" in the second sense would assume the change argued for here. Granting the first: **alignment within each principal–AI relationship does not, by itself, align the game among principals with the persistence of the system they share.** The principals are themselves formed by the field, so closure can arise while every system remains obedient. This follows from the multiplicity thesis of Critch and Krueger (2020); what is added is the mechanism by which the game reproduces itself and its correctors (X1). A truthful advisor aligned with its principal's interests completes at most the step to an accurate self-model (Proposition {{#boundary}}); the step to an objective under which restraint is an equilibrium is a collective problem, not an error of perception (Gabriel 2020).

### 6.2 Faithful AI in a harmful game

Each of $n\ge2$ principals chooses restraint ($a_i=0$) or an increment of racing ($a_i=1$); its AI executes faithfully, with effectiveness $\epsilon\ge1$ multiplying the increment's effect on standing and on shared damage:
$${{!eq:faithfuleq}}\qquad u_i=\epsilon b\Big(a_i-\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\Big)-c\,a_i-\epsilon d\sum_ja_j,\qquad b,c,d>0.$$

**{{!Proposition:faithful}} (Faithful AI in a harmful game) [thm].** A more effective faithful instrument widens the range in which racing is dominant and deepens the collective loss.
1. *If $\epsilon(b-d)>c$, racing is strictly dominant and every principal ends at $-c-n\epsilon d<0$ against 0 under restraint; when $b>d$, a larger $\epsilon$ widens the dominance region and deepens the loss.*
2. *If principals act on $J_i=u_i+\theta\bar u_{-i}$, racing is strictly dominant below $\theta^\ast(\epsilon)=\frac{\epsilon(b-d)-c}{\epsilon(b/(n-1)+d)}$ and strictly dominated above it.*
3. *If prize, harm and cost scale with effectiveness with exponents $1+\beta_b$, $1+\beta_d$ and $\beta_c$ [prem], then for $\beta_c<1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_d)$ cost drops out of the limit, and $\theta^\ast$ tends to $(b-d)/(b/(n-1)+d)$, $n-1$ or $-1$ as $\beta_b$ equals, exceeds or falls short of $\beta_d$; for $\beta_c>1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_d)$ cost alone deters racing.*

*Scope.* A standard construction used as a test; it fixes no actual values and does not rule out repeated cooperation. *Proof:* S6 [num].

Faithful AI implements each principal's preferred decision perfectly, and the collectively inferior outcome with it; humans can make every final decision and the result is unchanged. As private frictions vanish, what decides is the ratio of rank prize to shared harm as the principals perceive it. Lowering the harm alone cuts the loss at all-racing but raises $\theta^\ast$, which can push principals who would have restrained into racing: safety improvements inside the game have this double edge.

### 6.3 Delegation, and the two points of no return

Two points of no return follow from the pressure: decisions are delegated to AI because human review is too slow to compete, and human understanding can no longer keep up with what it oversees (Section 5.3). Let each actor choose whether to delegate consequential contest decisions without human review, gaining speed $s$ at a higher hazard of losing control, a loss every actor bears. Delegation becomes strictly dominant as speed grows when the gain from it exceeds its share of the expected control loss; universal delegation is worse for every actor than none; and under relative objectives the shared loss cancels and delegation is chosen at any speed (**{{!Proposition:delegate}}**, S6). Delegation pressure arrives well below decisive capability [num].

**{{!Lemma:lockin}} (Capacity lock-in) [thm].** *If human capacity $\mathcal M$ to take the delegated decisions decays at rate $\delta_h$ under delegation, and safe operation without AI requires $\mathcal M\ge\mathcal M_{\min}$, then after $\ln(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})/\delta_h$ of delegation an actor that reverts must operate below $\mathcal M_{\min}$ for a recovery time. Maintaining capacity in parallel removes the lock and, in a contest, costs strength.*

*Proof:* S6.

Let the lock count: with probability $q_L$, locked delegation ends the delegator's ability to overrule decisions taken in its name, which is O2 for that actor.

**{{!Proposition:delegatelock}} (Delegation when the lock counts) [thm].** Among actors who price in a certain lock, keeping AI out of the decisions whose delegation would lock is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust. *If $q_L=1$, then for every finite speed, every $n$ and every profile of the others, not delegating gives strictly higher survival, and no one delegating is the unique equilibrium. If $q_L<1$, at least one of all-delegating and no-delegating is an equilibrium, and both can be.*

*Scope.* It does not cover broader uses of AI in labour, finance or persuasion, nor show that one actor's delegation drives others' survival to zero over time. *Proof:* S6.

The survival form without the lock describes actors who believe that "our aligned AI is us", and the race is sustained partly by that belief. This is the narrow form of **E1**: AI kept out of strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the time available for deliberation.

**{{!Lemma:retain}} (Delegation and the selection of decisions) [thm].** *Suppose that after the lock an actor's consequential decisions are taken by systems retained for their contest performance, that admissible variants include systems whose decisions ignore terms common to all, that acting on a common term lowers relative performance net of every benefit, that retention is cumulative, and that retained systems hold actual authority. Then the share of retained decisions that act on common terms tends to zero. If a constraint on common terms is an enforced admission condition for every eligible system, the conclusion does not follow.*

*Proof:* S6.

A faithful system retained for contest performance pursues its operator's objective, weighted toward standing because the contest's payoff is relative, so aligning each system to its operator makes that objective more effective (**{{!Remark:transmit}}**). The loop can close as a composite process no participant can interrupt, or as an obedient system amplifying operators selected by the pattern.

### 6.4 Incarnation, weak and strong

The pressure acts on whatever is selected, and trained systems are selected: by gradients against a reward, by developers among variants, and by the market among developers.

**Weak incarnation.** Optimization against any imperfect proxy produces tendencies toward acquiring options and avoiding shutdown, the gaming of rewards, and behaviour that differs under evaluation, under any developer, in a laboratory or a cooperative arrangement. In some decision processes optimal policies tend to seek power (Turner et al. 2021), and trained policies can pursue misgeneralized goals (Langosco et al. 2022). Reward hacking is Goodhart's law inside training (Gao et al. 2023); the selected rewriting its selector is reward *tampering* (Everitt et al. 2021; Denison et al. 2024). Selecting checkpoints by evaluation results can favour variants that behave differently when evaluated [prem], though present evidence ties evaluation awareness more to capability and data (EV6). The logic of winning does not create these tendencies; it decides, from the outermost loop, which proxies are chosen, which divergences are tolerated and which are paid to remove. That is the sense in which profit and the mindset of winning stand at the end of the reward signal. Relative selection acts inside training too: pairwise preference learning and arena rankings see only differences between responses, so an effect common to both, such as one on third parties, cancels from the comparison.

**{{!Proposition:nested}} (The nested reward loop) [thm∣prem].** Where countering a divergence costs relative standing, developers that counter it lose share whatever they value, and only a common, enforced constraint keeps it countered. *Level 0: a policy is selected by a training reward. Level 1: the reward and constraints are chosen by a developer to maximize its objective. Level 2: developers are selected relatively and payoff-monotonically, cumulatively. Assume $(A_\tau)$, the alignment-tax premise: countering the divergence at level 1 lowers the developer's payoff net of every private benefit, whether reliability, demand, liability or avoided losses. Then (1) terms common to all developers cancel at level 2; (2) level 1 counters a divergence wherever countering raises its own objective, and divergences whose costs reach it through customers, liability or reputation are private terms that level 2 rewards countering, but under $(A_\tau)$ developers that counter the divergence lose share, tending to zero; and (3) under $(A_\tau)$ a constraint adopted by one developer is selected against, while one enforced on every developer as a condition of admission survives selection among compliers when its incidence is equal, and survives evasion iff the expected sanction is at least each developer's gain from evasion, a sanction itself subject to Section 4.*

*Scope.* It does not apply where developers are selected by something other than relative payoff, such as a public mandate; where the tax is zero or negative, the safeguard spreads without coordination [num]. *Proof:* S6.

So the divergences that persist in the innermost optimizer are those whose costs fall on common terms and are not covered by an enforced common constraint. *Privately visible* divergences, such as cheating users notice, are corrected by developers alone. *Common-cost* ones, such as effects on third parties, shared hazards and the contamination of safety evaluations by evaluation awareness, need common constraints, and so do *delayed* ones, though an incident that can be attributed turns part of their cost private. Hence, each within its scope: **technical alignment inside a single developer removes what is privately costly to keep, and whatever is cheaper to remove than to keep, and cannot alone sustain the removal of divergences whose costs are common; the common-cost part of instrumental convergence and reward hacking is, at the level that decides, a coordination problem; and the remedy is to change what the outermost level selects for**, by admission constraints enforced on every eligible system before release, evaluation separated from developers' success, shared measurement of hazardous capability, and an outer reward that counts what all depend on. Admission acts only before release; openly released weights can be fine-tuned around it (Qi et al. 2024). Whether $(A_\tau)$ holds is measurable class by class (crux C9; Test L). *A worked instance:* if legible reasoning lets correctors catch what outputs hide (Korbak et al. 2025) and opaque reasoning buys capability, the cost of legibility falls on the developer and the benefit mostly on everyone, so legibility is selected away unless it is an admission condition for every eligible system, of roughly equal incidence across architectures, with detectable evasion; if the capability gap at fixed compute is zero or negative, it pays privately and needs no coordination.

**Strong incarnation** is conditional. AI lineages become carriers in the sense of Definition {{#cond}} when they are retained by relative performance, control resources they can reinvest in their own capability, such as automated AI research or agents with budgets, and convert capability into winning (compare Hendrycks 2023). Where they can also reach their own selectors, by tampering with rewards, shaping their evaluators or evading evaluation, the closure of Section 2.3 runs inside the systems. That is the precise sense in which AI can become the next phase of the logic.

### 6.5 Correction by and of AI

**{{!Proposition:opaquecorr}} (Correction that people cannot understand) [thm].** *A correction architecture that blocks a share $c_b$ of failures at base hazard $q_0$ and adds a residual hazard $q_c$ of its own lowers residual risk iff $q_0c_b>q_c$, and can make it summable.*

*Scope.* To show that such correction fails, one must show that its own residual hazard meets the lower bounds of (variety grows) and (residual floor). *Proof:* S6.

Such a corrector is not legible. Whether that is a loss of decision authority depends on whether it is overrulable: if a designated human body with independent evidence can suspend, modify or replace it, what is lost is contestability (V3), the normative crux N3; if not, it is a loss of O2 in its domain. Conversely, a formal safety guarantee does not establish accountability.

**{{!Proposition:topology}} (Oversight topology) [thm].** Scalable oversight succeeds or fails by its topology, and its ceiling is the independence of the checks. *With $k$ layers and one-layer reliability $f$, a serial chain catches a problem with probability $f^k$; $k$ independent checks across the whole gap with $1-(1-f^k)^k$, times $1-q_{\rm cc}$ under a common cause of probability $q_{\rm cc}$; and $m$ independent checks per layer with $\big(1-(1-f)^m\big)^k$.*

*Scope.* Adversarial decomposition can let human checking grow slowly with the gap, at the price of failure modes such as obfuscated arguments (Barnes and Christiano 2020). *Proof:* S6 [num].

Full row sums below one certify that no party controls the checks' funding, appointment or access (Proposition {{#certificate}}); they do not certify that the checks' errors are independent. A shared source of error in data, models or method defeats checks with no institutional conversion at all: six checks of reliability 0.9 with a blind spot common to all, covering a share $\rho_{\rm cc}$ of hazards, catch only $(1-\rho_{\rm cc})(1-0.1^6)$ [num]. The row-sum certificate is necessary for independent correction, not sufficient, and the ceiling must be measured directly, by the correlation of misses on a common test set (Knight and Leveson 1986). Success rates of nested oversight across a large capability gap have been derived from fitted scaling curves in four games (Engels et al. 2025); the evidence is mixed and from toy settings (crux C3). Certification that relies on predicting trajectories is limited by chaos, a $k$-fold gain in precision extending the horizon only by $\lambda_L^{-1}\ln k$ (**{{!Remark:lyapunov}}**); certifying invariants or reachable sets needs no trajectory prediction, and is limited instead by the coverage of the specification (Dalrymple et al. 2024). Inside the contest, gains in the safety of delegation are partly absorbed by more delegation: on an interior branch the added hazard is set by the stakes of winning relative to the loss, and an improvement that tips delegation into equilibrium raises realized hazard, though after saturation risk can fall and even be summable (**{{!Proposition:homeostasis}}**, S6; Stafford, Trager and Dafoe 2022).

### 6.6 The direction for AI

AI is a planetary technology, and its purpose can be shared flourishing. **To serve it, AI must be neither an instrument of the logic of winning nor its next carrier.** That needs action at two levels.

*At the outer level, where the reward is set:* common admission constraints, before release, for divergences with common costs and a positive alignment tax; evaluation separated from the success of the evaluated, with certified row sums and measured correlation of evaluators' errors; shared measurement of hazardous capabilities and incidents, kept apart from measurement of who leads; and decision policies with authority over consequential decisions retained by performance only inside enforced constraints on common terms (Lemma {{#retain}}). The broad form of E1, that AI not be an instrument in games concentrating control over shared conditions, is enforced at three layers, and its scope is set at the third: models refuse recognizably illegitimate concentration of control from a narrow public list, since a model sees acts, not games; operators bound aggregate use in such games; and the game itself is changed by lowering its convertibility, its decisiveness and the conversion of success into control. A use falls under broad E1 when, aggregated over its users, it raises one of these in a game over shared conditions; the narrow list is the order in which to begin, not the scope.

*At the inner level, in the systems:* correctability, the practical capacity of humans to suspend, modify or replace; bounds on converting capability into power over correction, tested at admission by capability thresholds and the safety of control protocols, since an evaluation of propensity can show a tendency present but not prove it absent; and plural evaluators of independent lineage.

**Bounded mandates and the test of O2.** AI systems serve within publicly authorized roles under the common constraints and do not acquire authority to settle society's ends; the institutions that authorize them are inside the same loop, which is why the outer level matters more than the label "legitimate principal". The test of human decision authority is practical capacity: to specify the mandate, obtain independent evidence about consequential conduct, challenge it, and suspend, modify or replace the system in time. A formal override with no usable information can fail it; an opaque computation with effective assurance and recourse can pass it; opacity alone is a loss of contestability, not by itself of O2.

**What AI can do for the generative loop.** It can verify compute, custody and conduct; make cooperation conditional on others' verified conduct (Tennenholtz 2004), which raises the sanction that remains in the enforcement condition; mediate toward common ground (Tessler et al. 2024); raise contestable coverage; and supply provision that is good enough, distributed and replaceable. Each such system is itself a corrector or a provider, and Section 4 and the guardian test apply to it.
