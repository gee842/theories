## X2. Substrates

The pressure needs only selection on relative success, convertible winnings and, for its closure, the ability of the successful to reshape their selection. The following are demonstrations; none is the theory's ground.

- **Cells.** A cancer lineage evades the signals that limit proliferation, recruits the surrounding tissue into supporting its expansion, and evades the immune response that would remove it (Hanahan and Weinberg 2011; Aktipis et al. 2015). Growth is not the fault line: large animals, with far more cells, show no higher cancer risk than small ones, because suppression scales with size (Peto's paradox; Caulin and Maley 2011).
- **Firms.** Markets with increasing returns lock in early leads (Arthur 1989). The doctrine that the purpose of business is profit "so long as it stays within the rules of the game" (Friedman 1970) meets its limit where the successful can buy the rules. The same school analysed that possibility (Stigler 1971), and its remedy was to shrink discretionary rule-making that could be captured. The disagreement is about the remedy: a planetary technology with unpriced coupling has no option of making no rules, and where common rules are absent, the winners' systems make them in fact. The doctrine's own argument that managers spending others' money on social goals tax without representation supports public, independent correction, not voluntary self-regulation by firms.
- **States.** In security competition a gain for one is a threat to another, and falling behind is read as fatal.
- **Federations.** Members compete for capital and residents, and a race to the bottom in standards follows; the strongest can capture the centre; common standards and fiscal equalization change what the federation selects for.
- **Science.** Metrics allocate positions, and the positions set the metrics.
- **Training loops.** A policy is selected by a reward a developer sets, and the developer is selected by a contest; pairwise preference comparisons see only differences between responses.
- **Another planet.** Nothing in Definition {{#cond}} refers to a culture, a political system or a species. A civilization on another planet, with other drives and institutions, would face the same pressure wherever selection is relative, winnings convert and the successful can edit the selection.
