## S6. AI: instrument and incarnation

### Proof of Proposition {{#faithful}}

Changing $a_i$ from 0 to 1 changes $u_i$ by $\epsilon b-c-\epsilon d$, independent of the others' choices, and each other $u_j$ by $-\epsilon b/(n-1)-\epsilon d$. Summing, the relative terms cancel, which gives (1); in objectives racing changes $J_i$ by $\epsilon b(1-\frac{\theta}{n-1})-c-\epsilon d(1+\theta)$, which gives (2). For (3), write $\theta^\ast=(T-D-c)/(T/(n-1)+D)$ with $T=\epsilon b$, $D=\epsilon d$ growing with exponents $1+\beta_b$, $1+\beta_d$ and $c$ with $\beta_c$, and take limits. $\square$ *Numerics (SM(5)):* exponents $(0,0,0)$ and $(1,1,0)$ give the limit 1.50; $(0,0.5,0)$ gives $-0.99$; $(0.5,0,0)$ gives 2.998; $(1,0,3)$ and $(0,0,1.5)$ give $-\infty$; at equality, $(0,0,1)$ gives 0.9375 and $(1,0,2)$ gives 2.1. With $b,d,c$ fixed, $\theta^\ast$ is 1.44 at $\epsilon=10$; with $c$ also scaling with $\epsilon$ it stays 0.94. With $n=4$, $b=1$, $c=0.3$, $d=0.2$, $\epsilon=1$, all racing gives each $-1.1$; lowering $d$ to 0.1 and 0.05 cuts the loss to 0.70 and 0.50 while raising $\theta^\ast$ to 1.39 and 1.70. The condition earlier written as $\beta_c<\beta_b=\beta_d$ was neither necessary nor sufficient; the correct one is $\beta_c<1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_d)$.

### Proposition {{#delegate}} in full, and Lemma {{#lockin}}

*Each actor chooses $\iota_i\in\{0,1\}$; strength is $1+s\iota_i$; defeat costs $L_e$; a loss of control by any actor costs every actor $L_c$; the hazard of losing control is $\psi_0$ without delegation and $\psi_1>\psi_0$ with it, $\Delta\psi=\psi_1-\psi_0$; payoffs are $-L_e(1-w_i)-L_c[1-\prod_j(1-\psi_j)]$. (1) If $k$ others delegate, delegating gains $L_e[w_i(1,k)-w_i(0,k)]$, increasing in $s$ and tending to $L_e(1-1/n)$ for $k=0$ and $L_e/(k+1)$ for $k\ge1$, and costs $L_c\Delta\psi(1-\psi_0)^{n-1-k}(1-\psi_1)^k$. (2) Delegation becomes strictly dominant as $s$ grows iff the limiting gain exceeds the cost for every $k$; $L_e/n>L_c\Delta\psi$ suffices. (3) Universal delegation is worse for every actor than none, by $L_c[(1-\psi_0)^n-(1-\psi_1)^n]$. (4) Under relative objectives the shared loss cancels and delegation is chosen at every $s>0$.* *Proof:* direct computation; with all delegating, $w_i=1/n$ as with none and the hazard is higher. With $n=3$, $r=1$, $L_e=L_c$, $\psi_0=0.01$, $\psi_1=0.2$, the dominance threshold is $s^\ast\approx1.16$, where a lone delegator wins with probability about 0.52.

*Lemma {{#lockin}}.* With $\dot{\mathcal M}=-\delta_h\mathcal M$ under delegation and $\dot{\mathcal M}=\varrho_h(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M)$ otherwise, the lock time is $\ln(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})/\delta_h$ and the recovery time $\varrho_h^{-1}\ln[(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M)/(\bar{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M_{\min})]$.

### Proposition {{#delegatelock}} in full, and proof

*Let locked delegation end the delegator's ability to overrule with probability $q_L$; losing the contest mean subordination with probability $p$; $Q=\prod_j(1-\psi_j)$; and, with $r=1$, $\Phi_i=(1-q_L\iota_i)Q[w_i+(1-w_i)(1-p)]$. (1) If $q_L=1$ and $Q>0$, not delegating gives strictly higher survival for every finite $s$, every $n$, every $p$ and every profile of the others. (2) With $k$ others delegating, $p=1$ and $a=(1-\psi_1)/(1-\psi_0)$, $\Phi_D/\Phi_N=(1-q_L)a(1+s)\frac{n+ks}{n+(k+1)s}$; with $A=(1-q_L)a$, all delegating is an equilibrium iff $A(n+(n-1)s)/n\ge1$ and no one delegating iff $An(1+s)/(n+s)\le1$. At least one always holds, since $A(n+(n-1)s)/n-An(1+s)/(n+s)=A(n-1)s^2/[n(n+s)]\ge0$, and both can. (3) At bounded speed one delegator does not drive another's survival to zero: with $p=1$ a non-delegator facing $k$ delegators has $Q_N/(n+ks)>0$.* *Proof.* (1) $\Phi_D=0$ while $\Phi_N\ge Q_Nw_N>0$. (2) By the strengths $1$, $1+s$ and the formula; the identity gives the "at least one". (3) Immediate. $\square$ *Numerics:* part (1) holds for 20,000 random draws (`../v5/checks_v5.py`, DL); in SM(6), over 100,000 draws, "neither" never occurs and "both" occurs 4,068 times. With $n=2$, $q_L=0.99$, $\psi_0=0.01$, $\psi_1=0.05$, all delegating is an equilibrium only for $s\ge206.4$, with survival 0.0045 there.

### Proofs of Lemma {{#retain}}, Remark {{#transmit}} and Proposition {{#nested}}

*Lemma {{#retain}}.* Variants acting on common terms exist and have relative performance lower by at least a positive margin, so under cumulative retention their share tends to zero, as in Proposition {{#load}}; the retained systems' decisions are the actor's. If constraints are an enforced admission condition, no admissible variant violates them. That an actor which cannot overrule decisions taken in its name has lost O2 in that domain is a definition. For a reader who counts survival only as preservation of the unit, delegation acts through the shared hazard instead: delegated systems interacting at machine speed without a human veto raise the probability of shared loss by reciprocal fear of surprise attack (Schelling 1960), inadvertent escalation (Posen 1991) and flash war (Scharre 2018), an argument with cited mechanisms, not a derivation.

*Proposition {{#nested}}.* (1) Level 2 is relative and payoff-monotone, so Lemma {{#blind}}(1) removes every term common to all developers. (2) Level 0 maximizes its reward by definition; level 1 chooses rewards and constraints to maximize the developer's objective, which counts privately visible divergences through customers, liability and reputation; under $(A_\tau)$ countering lowers the developer's relative payoff by a positive margin net of every private benefit, so with cumulative selection countering developers' share tends to zero (Proposition {{#load}}(2), developers as carriers). (3) A constraint borne by one developer lowers its relative payoff; one enforced on all as a condition of admission is a common term among compliers when its incidence is equal, and Lemma {{#blind}}(3) gives the evasion condition. $\square$ *Numerics (CI):* a unilateral safeguard that cuts error from 0.20 to 0.01 at cost 0.01 is selected for (its share tends to one), which is the case $(A_\tau)$ excludes; a safeguard whose benefit is common is selected away.

*Interface.* What level 2 selects on is the developers' relative payoff, of which the trained systems' contribution to relative standing is part. Under a public mandate, or a national laboratory that only moves relative selection to the level of states, the result applies at that level or not at all.

### Proofs for Section 6.5

*Proposition {{#opaquecorr}}.* The residual hazard is $q_0(1-c_b)+q_c$; compare with $q_0$. With base hazard 0.1 a period, a corrector blocking 99% and adding $2^{-(t+2)}$ raises survival to period 200 from $7\times10^{-10}$ to 0.63 (`../v5/checks_v5.py`, OV).

*Proposition {{#topology}}.* Each layer covers $m_\ell$ times the attention of the layer above, so $k=\ln(Y/\mathsf B)/\ln m_\ell$ layers are needed; a serial chain catches a problem only if every link succeeds, $f^k=(Y/\mathsf B)^{\ln f/\ln m_\ell}$. Independent checks across the gap fail together with probability $(1-f^k)^k$; a common cause defeating all with probability $q_{\rm cc}$ leaves at most $1-q_{\rm cc}$. Layered redundancy needs every layer to succeed. With $f=0.9$, $k=6$: the serial chain catches 0.53; six checks across the gap 0.989, or 0.940 under a common cause with $q_{\rm cc}=0.05$; layered redundancy 0.941 with two checks per layer and 0.994 with three. With a blind spot common to all checks covering a share $\rho_{\rm cc}$ of hazards, catch rates are 1.000, 0.950 and 0.700 at $\rho_{\rm cc}=0$, 0.05, 0.3 (SM(7)). Common causes include shared training sources, shared models, shared funders and collusion among untrusted monitors, which the control literature treats as a design variable (Greenblatt et al. 2024).

**Remark {{#lyapunov}} in full.** In a system with largest Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_L>0$, an initial error $\epsilon_{\rm obs}$ reaches a tolerance $\Delta_{\rm tol}$ after about $\lambda_L^{-1}\ln(\Delta_{\rm tol}/\epsilon_{\rm obs})$, so a $k$-fold gain in precision extends the horizon by $\lambda_L^{-1}\ln k$; in the logistic map at full chaos, errors of $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-12}$ reach 0.1 after 7.5 and 37.5 steps on average. This limits safety cases that predict trajectories, such as "the margin we bought will suffice", not certification of invariants, reachable sets or probabilistic bounds. That AI raises the sensitivity of the systems it acts in is a conjecture.

**Proposition {{#homeostasis}} in full.** *In the delegation model with additive stakes and $r=1$, let each actor choose $\iota_i\in[0,1]$, with strength $1+s\iota_i$ and hazard $\psi_0+\Delta\psi\,\iota_i$; write $x=\Delta\psi\,\iota$, $b_s=\Delta\psi/s$, $q=1-\psi_0$, $K_h=(n-1)L_e/(n^2L_c)$. An interior symmetric equilibrium solves $(x+b_s)(q-x)^{n-1}=K_h$. (1) Where $x\ll q$, the added hazard is $nx\approx(n-1)L_e/(nL_cq^{n-1})-n\Delta\psi/s$, nearly independent of $\Delta\psi$, until delegation saturates. (2) There can be several interior equilibria, and along a branch $\partial x/\partial\Delta\psi=-(q-x)/(s[q-nx-(n-1)b_s])$, whose sign changes between branches. (3) After saturation, realized risk falls with $\Delta\psi$ and can be summable under full delegation and growing speed: with $n=3$, $L_e=L_c=1$, $\psi_0=0$, $s_t=2^t$, $\Delta\psi_t=2^{-t}$, full delegation is an equilibrium for $t\ge5$, with total realized risk at most $3/16$ from then on. (4) With all-or-nothing delegation, any equilibrium with $k\ge1$ delegators has realized hazard above the baseline, so an improvement that carries $\Delta\psi$ across the threshold at which delegation becomes an equilibrium raises realized hazard.* *Proof.* At the symmetric point $\partial w_i/\partial\iota_i=s(n-1)/(n^2(1+s\iota))$ and the marginal cost is $L_c\Delta\psi(1-\psi)^{n-1}$; equate and divide by $s$. (1) approximates; (2) differentiates implicitly (two interior roots at $\iota=0.153$, $0.937$ for $n=2$, $s=100$, $L_e=0.5$, $L_c=1$, $\psi_0=0.01$, $\Delta\psi=0.9$, with the corner also an equilibrium; `../v5/checks_v5.py`, HO); (3) the own marginal gain $2s_t/(9(1+s_t))-\Delta\psi_t(1-\Delta\psi_t)^2$ is positive for $t\ge5$ and the objective is concave; (4) $(1-\psi_0-\Delta\psi)^k<(1-\psi_0)^k$. $\square$ With $n=3$, $s=100$, $L_e=1$, $L_c=100$, $\psi_0=0.01$, the added hazard is 0.0011, 0.0053, 0.0062, 0.0065 and 0.0067 as $\Delta\psi$ falls from 0.19 to 0.005, and falls to 0.0030 only at complete delegation (compare Stafford, Trager and Dafoe 2022; Peltzman 1975; Wilde 1982).
