## S4. Selecting the selectors

### Proof of Proposition {{#influence}}, and the effect of scope

Support keyed to the carrier obeys $\dot z^\chi_i=\varepsilon_i(\chi q\sum_j\hat b_{ij}y_j-d_iz^\chi_i)$ from zero; its horizon average is $z_i^{\chi\ast}L_i$ with $z_i^{\chi\ast}=\chi q\sum_j\hat b_{ij}y_j/d_i$ and $L_i=1-(1-e^{-\varepsilon_id_iT_h})/(\varepsilon_id_iT_h)$. Weighting the per-decision advantage $a_iz^\chi_i$ by $y_i$ and summing gives $\chi q\,y^\top My$, with $y=p\bar y$; shared support cancels. Differentiating gives the gradient, and under faithful transmission $\dot{\bar q}\propto\operatorname{Cov}(q,\Delta\pi(q))$ (Price 1970). With $f>0$ and cost $fp^{\xi_f}q$, $\Delta\pi'(0)>0$ iff $\chi p^2\bar y^\top M\bar y>fp^{\xi_f}$, that is, $p>p^\ast$, and for $p<p^\ast$ capacity loses for every $q>0$. The comparative statics follow from $c_iL_i\hat G_{ij}=a_i\hat b_{ij}\varepsilon_iT_h\,\ell_L(\varepsilon_id_iT_h)$ with $\ell_L(v)=[1-(1-e^{-v})/v]/v$ decreasing. $\square$

**{{!Proposition:scope}} (Scope) [thm].** *If the carrier has share $p$ in each domain of a set $\mathcal S$, with $\hat G$ equal to $\hat g$ on the diagonal and $\hat\varphi$ off it and $c_iL_i=cL$, the return is $\chi cL\,p^2|\mathcal S|(\hat g+(|\mathcal S|-1)\hat\varphi)$: superlinear in $|\mathcal S|$ at fixed $p$ when $\hat\varphi>0$, and rising with scope at fixed total size only if $\hat\varphi>\hat g$.* (Sum over $\mathcal S$ and substitute $p=Y/|\mathcal S|$.)

*Relation to earlier work.* Construction of a shared resource is undermined by competition, and monopolizing the constructed niche restores the incentive to build (Krakauer, Page and Erwin 2009); Proposition {{#influence}} has the same appropriability logic, with the selection rule as the object modified, a field of domains, and the opposite valence. Dynamic models of constitutions show present decisions changing future decision rights (Acemoglu, Egorov and Sonin 2012); whether a model of that family reproduces Section 4 is open. Fixed-cost participation in lobbying (Bombardini 2008) is the empirical counterpart of the threshold. Appropriability plays the role relatedness plays in Hamilton's rule.

### Full statement and proof of Proposition {{#noinside}}

*Let carriers differ in an investment $u\ge0$, at private cost $C_u(u)$, that raises the correction rate $d$ for everyone. (1) Among otherwise identical carriers of the same practice, those investing more are selected against under any payoff-monotone rule. (2) Holding the host fixed, correction lowers the payoff of a carrier that holds rule support. (3) A challenger invests only if its own share of the benefit exceeds $C_u'(0)$, so dispersed challengers free-ride. (4) Correction keyed to the corrector yields a return to influence of the form of Proposition {{#influence}}; whether it produces capture depends on whether row sums cross one. (5) If $\Upsilon>1$ and correction makes liability effective, an incumbent can gain from correction applied to all carriers, while correction applied to it alone costs it rent and share.*

Reputation or competition among institutions sustains independent correction only when contributions and outcomes are observed (Panchanathan and Boyd 2004; Sigmund et al. 2010). *Proof.* (1) Two carriers differing only in $u$ are affected identically by the change in $d$; their payoff difference is $C_u(u_l)-C_u(u_k)<0$ for $u_k>u_l$. (2) At fixed $V$ a carrier's support declines in $d$. (3) is the public-goods condition. (4) is immediate. (5) compares $\nu\bar e(\Upsilon-1)$ with the lost rent. $\square$

### Full statement and proof of Proposition {{#corrrate}}

With $L_i=1$, uniform $d$ and $f=0$, $M=\hat A/d$ and $q^\ast=(\chi\hat Y/(\gamma p^{\xi_v}d))^{1/\eta}$, so
$$G_{\rm ev}=\frac{A}{c_{\rm ev}d}+Z_\eta\,(\gamma p^{\xi_v})^{-1/\eta}d^{-(1+\eta)/\eta},$$
strictly decreasing in $d$; this gives (1), and for $\eta=1$, $d^\ast=\big(A+\sqrt{A^2+4c_{\rm ev}Z}\big)/(2c_{\rm ev})$ with $Z=a_{\rm ev}\hat B\chi\hat Y/(\gamma p^{\xi_v})$. Along $\gamma_0e^{-\alpha_{\rm ctl}t}$ and $d_0e^{\alpha_{\rm cor}t}$, the first term is nonincreasing and the second is proportional to $e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$, whatever $\xi_v$, since the size factor $p^{-\xi_v/\eta}$ is constant. Once the captured set holds at least half its decisions, $q^\ast$ grows as $e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$, and integrating rule support gives $a_{\rm ev}z_{\rm ev}(t)\ge c\,e^{(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})t/\eta}$, so $\operatorname{logit}x_{\rm ev}\to\infty$. This gives (2); the rate condition does not repair a certificate invalid at the start. (3): with $f>0$ fixed, $q^\ast=0$ once $d\ge\chi\hat Y/(fp^{\xi_f})$, so the requirement levels off at $\max\{A/c_{\rm ev},\chi\hat Y/(fp^{\xi_f})\}$; under $[H_f]$ that level falls at $\alpha_f$. (4): parts of share $1/k$ have $\hat Y$ divided by $k^2$ and the size factor multiplied by $k^{\xi_v}$, so $q^\ast$ is divided by $k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$ and the cultivated term reaches a given level later by $\Delta t=(2-\xi_v)\ln k/(\alpha_{\rm ctl}-(1+\eta)\alpha_{\rm cor})$; with $f>0$ fixed, parts below the threshold cultivate nothing; and with $q_k^\ast\propto p_k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$ and conversion proportional to $p_kq_k^\ast$, the total is proportional to $\sum_kp_k^{1+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$. $\square$

*Further parts.* If AI lowers the unit cost of independent correction at rate $\alpha_{uc}$ and its budget grows at $\alpha_B$, then $\alpha_{\rm cor}=\alpha_B+\alpha_{uc}$, and eventual failure needs $\alpha_{\rm ctl}>(1+\eta)(\alpha_B+\alpha_{uc})$. With a finite horizon, while $\varepsilon_idT_h\ll1$ the required growth is about $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/\eta$, a pre-asymptotic interval. With concave conversion $\hat bq^{\xi_c}$, the required rate becomes $\xi_c\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$. The model implies that influence spending rises with share as $p^{\xi_v+(1+\eta)(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$, at least quadratically, which is testable.

*Numerics (CS).* The size threshold at $f=0.05$ is 0.2236 per carrier and 0.0500 per decision. Two equal independent carriers give a quarter of the monopoly's cultivated conversion at $\xi_v=0$ and half at $\xi_v=1$; shares $(0.5,0.3,0.2)$ give 0.16 and 0.38 of the monopoly value. With $\eta=1$, $\alpha_{\rm ctl}=0.2$, no correction growth and $k=10$, simulation gives split delays of 23.00 and 11.50 against formula values 23.03 and 11.51. A dominant share of 0.6 with $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0.09$ crosses one for all three elasticities, and with $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0.11$ decays. With $f$ fixed at 0.3 the monopoly crosses at 10.75 and ten parts never do; with $f$ falling at 0.05, the parts begin to cultivate $2\ln10/0.05=92.1$ later. The joint simulation in which capacity evolves with the field has not been run (TESTS, Study 2).

**Mobilization (Remark {{#countervail}}).** Let those harmed mobilize in proportion to perceived harm, $d=d_0+k_B(1-\tilde V)$. Since $1-\tilde V=(1-m)(1-V)$, masking attenuates the feedback by $1-m$ exactly when it is needed, and even with accurate reports $d\le d_0+k_B$, which cannot keep a certificate against a requirement that rises without bound. After a recovery, mobilization falls back to $d_0$ and the certificate can fail again: a regulatory cycle. With fixed costs, enough gain and speed can keep the certificate over a stated interval.

### Proof of Proposition {{#masking}}, and the late alarm

$\tilde V$ increases in $V$ and equals $V_{\rm alarm}$ at $V=(V_{\rm alarm}-m)/(1-m)$. For $V_{\min}\le V\le V_{\rm alarm}$ and $m>m_{\rm fl}$, $\tilde V\ge V_{\min}+m(1-V_{\min})>V_{\rm alarm}$. At $V^\ast$, $\tilde V\ge V^\ast+\bar m(1-V^\ast)>V_{\rm alarm}$. $\square$ If $V\to0$ and eventually $m\le m_1<V_{\rm alarm}$, the alarm fires. If $V$ declines from $V_{\rm alarm}$ as $\dot V=-v_dV$ with constant $m<V_{\rm alarm}$, the alarm is delayed by $v_d^{-1}\ln[(1-m)V_{\rm alarm}/(V_{\rm alarm}-m)]$, which diverges as $m\uparrow V_{\rm alarm}$.

**Remark {{#alarm}} in full.** (1) With an upper bound $m_{\rm in}$ known, an alarm on reports at $V_{\rm target}+m_{\rm in}(1-V_{\rm target})$ fires no later than the true condition reaches $V_{\rm target}$, for every $m\le m_{\rm in}$. (2) A bound estimated inside the evaluated lineage is itself masked. (3) A disjunctive alarm fails silently only if $m_{\rm out}>m_{\rm fl}$. (4) For static, memoryless affine masking $\tilde V=m+(1-m)V$, the correlation coefficient of reports is that of $V$, exactly. *Numerics (AC):* on a stochastic logistic host with load 0.4 and true condition 0.36, masking at $m=0,0.25,0.5$ gives mean reports 0.358, 0.519 and 0.679 with the same lag-one autocorrelation, 0.9814, from which the condition recovered is 0.376; a report filter with memory (exponential smoothing at 0.8) drives the recovered value to 0.042, and the indicator fails. It also needs long series, and once used as an alarm it becomes a target; faking dynamics is nevertheless costlier than faking levels, and the indicator can be cross-checked against channels that observe actions directly.

### The field model

**{{!Lemma:fieldlemma}} (The field is cooperative) [thm].** *$[0,1]^D\times[0,\infty)^D$ is forward invariant under {{eq:field}}, $\limsup_tz_i\le\sum_jb_{ij}/d_i$, and the system is cooperative, so its flow preserves the componentwise order (Hirsch 1985; Smith 1995).*

*Proof of Proposition {{#certificate}}.* (1) At $Z_{\mathcal C}$ the Jacobian is block lower-triangular, with diagonal entries $\mp\zeta_ic_i(G_i(\mathcal C)-1)$ and $-\varepsilon_id_i$. (2) By the lemma, eventually $a_iz_i-c_i\le c_i(\sum_jG_{ij}-1)/2<0$. (3) Take $G_{i1}>1$ for $i\ne1$, $G_{12}>1$, and other off-diagonal gains zero. (4) Pruning, by comparison. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition {{#reabsorb}}.* Comparison and pruning; the re-seeding case is the cascade from the domains that remain captured. **Cascades.** If $x_j\to1$ on a seed, $x_i\to1$ on its cascade hull, the closure of $\mathcal S_{k+1}=\mathcal S_k\cup\{i:x_i(0)>0,\ G_i(\mathcal S_k)>1\}$ (Kempe, Kleinberg and Tardos 2003; Morris 2000), by induction using a lower bound on rule support.

*The keystone example.* In the first version of this model, a liability reform at development unwound capture in every domain while development was a keystone; once the other domains' mutual conversion rose to 1.1, capture survived the complete decoupling of development.

*Proof of Proposition {{#pausefield}}.* The frozen share drives $z\to b_px_p/d$. The equilibria are $(0,0)$, $(1,b/d)$ and the saddle $(1/G,c/a)$. The function $axz-cx-\frac{ad}{2b}z^2$ is nondecreasing along solutions, so every solution converges to an equilibrium and the stable manifold of the saddle separates the basins; the point $(x_p,bx_p/d)$ lies on the nullcline through the saddle, and $x_\sigma>1/G$ by strong cooperativity. $\square$ *Numerics (DW):* by forward integration in logit coordinates, $x_\sigma$ is 0.676 when rule-making is as fast as adoption with $G=2$ and 0.984 when it is ten times slower; dwell times follow in S11. This result also appears as Proposition 10 of the author's paper of 28 September 2026 (LEDGER records the overlap; attribution is the author's to settle).

*What the field model cannot show.* It is cooperative, so it has no attracting cycles and describes lock-in, not reform cycles; a delayed, masked negative loop from the host to correction could produce cycles and is not modelled. *Recovering the parameters.* Integrating {{eq:field}} gives $\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{logit}x_i=\zeta_ic_i[\sum_jG_{ij}(k_i*x_j)(t)-1]$ plus a decaying transient, with kernel $k_i(s)=\varepsilon_id_ie^{-\varepsilon_id_is}$; series for the shares identify the conversions. This is recoverability, not causal identification.

### Proof of Theorem {{#slowroute}}, and an illustration

By (a) and Proposition {{#load}}, load tends to $\bar e$ and $V$ declines toward a limit below $V_{\min}$ unless an alarm triggers liability. By (c) and Proposition {{#corrrate}}(2), $x_{\rm ev}\to1$. By (e), $m(t)>m_{\rm fl}$ throughout the band, so no alarm fires before $V<V_{\min}$ (Proposition {{#masking}}). $\square$ When $h_V\bar e<\rho$ the host cannot reach the alarm before $T_A=(\rho-h_V\bar e)^{-1}\ln[(1-V^\ast/V_0)/(1-V^\ast/V_{\rm alarm})]$ with $V^\ast=1-h_V\bar e/\rho$. *Illustration* ($\rho=0.05$, $h_V=0.08$, $V_{\rm alarm}=0.6$, $V_{\min}=0.3$): independent evaluation raises the alarm at $t\approx37$, sixteen units before the floor is crossed at $t\approx53$; with evaluation captured before the harm (midpoint 20) the alarm fires at $t\approx45$ for $m_{\rm in}=0.3$ and at $t\approx63$, after the floor, for $m_{\rm in}=0.5$; with capture after the harm appears (midpoint 45) it is nearly on time. The two clocks are set exogenously: an illustration of the order of events, not a joint simulation.
