## S11. The transition

### Proofs of Corollary {{#relaxtrust}} and Proposition {{#signal}}

*Corollary {{#relaxtrust}}.* Each item follows from the stated formula: $T-R$ in Proposition {{#assure}}; the bias of messages through the weight on winning in Proposition {{#talk}}; the net exit gain in Proposition {{#overhang}}; Proposition {{#enforce}} for sanctions against a decisive defector; and $P-S$ in the threshold.

**Proposition {{#signal}} in full.** *A sender is survival-first and prices in the lock (type S, probability $1-\mu$) or holds a relative objective (type R, probability $\mu$). It can send $E$: a verifiable, durable removal of AI from its own strategic decision loops for a period, open to inspection, costing type $t$ the amount $k_t$. A receiver restrains iff its posterior that the sender is S is at least its threshold $\varpi^\ast$; the receiver's restraint is worth $G_t$ to type $t$, including, for R, what it gains by exploiting it. (1) A separating equilibrium, S sending, R not, the receiver restraining after $E$ only, exists iff $k_S\le G_S$ and $k_R\ge G_R$, with the belief that a non-sender is R. (2) If $k_R<G_R$, R imitates, no separating equilibrium exists, and the receiver restrains after $E$ only if $1-\mu\ge\varpi^\ast$. (3) Hence $E$ separates only if it costs a relative-objective actor at least what it gains by inducing restraint, which requires it to be durable, verifiable and costly to reverse.* *Proof.* Best responses given the receiver's strategy, and Bayes' rule on path. $\square$ With $k_S=-0.05$, $k_R=0.30$, $G_S=0.40$, $G_R=0.20$ separation holds; if the step can be reversed cheaply ($k_R=0.05$) it does not.

### Proofs of Proposition {{#pausevalue}} and Proposition {{#reachable}}

*Pause.* Shifting the residual sequence leaves the product over any window of the same length unchanged, and the infinite product is zero by Lemma {{#nonsum}}. A pause after which the sequence is summable keeps survival bounded away from zero: with residual hazard 0.01 a round for 20 rounds and $0.01/k^{1.5}$ in the $k$-th round after a change, survival to round 10,000 is 0.80. The field-model part is Proposition {{#pausefield}}.

*Reachable path.* Existence is Proposition {{#commonfrontn}} and Theorem {{#safeguards}}; reachability is Propositions {{#assure}}, {{#survassure}} and {{#delegatelock}}; survival during the transition is Theorem {{#safeguards}}(1) and Lemma {{#lockin}}; persistence is Propositions {{#overhang}}, {{#rivalverify}}, {{#certificate}} and {{#reabsorb}}. $\square$

### Dwell times

In the field model a reform that insulates rule-making from shares must be held at least until rule support falls below break-even, $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$, and then long enough to repay the lock-in accumulated meanwhile. The exact minimum hold, in units of $\varepsilon d$, is 1.89, 5.32 and 10.45 for $G=2$, 5 and 10 when rule-making is ten times slower than adoption, and 5.86, 10.34 and 16.18 when the two are equally fast (DW, forward integration in logit coordinates, reproducing the systems review's check). During the first $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$ the share keeps rising; it is back at its starting level when $G(1-e^{-s})=s$, $s=\varepsilon dt$, that is at $s=1.59$, 4.97 and 10.0, well before withdrawal is safe. A rollback judged on share levels is therefore premature: what decides is where rule support stands relative to the separatrix. For the pause of Proposition {{#pausefield}} with rule-making insulated, the required length is $\ln\big((b/d)/S(x_p)\big)/(\varepsilon d)$, with $S$ the separatrix height at the frozen share, and infinite when $x_p\ge x_\sigma$. Keeping a small alternative lineage shortens the dwell; the size of that effect quoted in the systems review (from 10.45 to 3.81 with a 0.1% lineage) has not been reproduced here and is not used in the core.

*Stages.* The slow variable of Stage 2 is human capacity (Lemma {{#lockin}}), of Stage 4 the capability held in check (Proposition {{#overhang}}). After a recovery, mobilization falls back and certificates can fail again (Remark {{#countervail}}).
