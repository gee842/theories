## S16. Correction through institutional change

### The joint target, and its status

A correction can work when applied and still be displaced by the learning, competition or institutional change it permits. The joint target of Section 10 therefore has two parts: preserve the protected conditions, and preserve an effective means of doing so through the admitted changes. This is a research target, not an additional result established for the full system.

Let a joint state contain the host, actual access and intervention capacity, human corrective capacity, institutional authority, carrier shares and technological capability. Its dynamics must include how those states change the payoffs and admissible actions that select their successors. A protected region specifies limits on harm and losses of agency, together with effective capacities for detection, challenge, intervention and revision. Its ethical commitments come from V1–V5; mathematical persistence alone does not make an institution legitimate. The target is a feasible, non-anticipative institutional strategy that keeps this region invariant over a stated horizon, against a stated class of disturbances and strategic deviations, while permitting legitimate changes of purpose and useful development. The capacity to implement that strategy must be among the protected states rather than an inexhaustible external controller.

Barrier methods provide a language for one part of this target (Ames et al. 2017). If the region is given by $h_j(X)\ge0$ and $\dot X=F(X,u,w)$, an appropriate sufficient boundary condition, when the active constraint gradients describe the tangent cone and the implemented feedback yields well-posed dynamics, is that one admissible feedback choice $u$ makes $\nabla h_j\cdot F\ge0$ at every active boundary, against every allowed disturbance $w$. The same choice must satisfy all active constraints; separate controllers for separate constraints may conflict. Feasibility includes resources, observations, authority and response time. Such a certificate does not show that a controller will retain any of these, or remain selected. Those are properties of the enlarged dynamics to establish, not conclusions supplied by the barrier analogy. Nor does a certificate for one bounded disturbance class cover arbitrary capability growth or unknown forms of failure. The correctable kernel of {{kernel}} remains uncomputed.

### A reduced joint certificate

One smaller join can be made explicitly from the existing equations. It composes the transient support bound of {{certificate}} with load selection and the host boundary; it adds an explicit invariant subset to {{safeguards}}, rather than a new general account of institutional stability. The maintained link is still $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$. Its empirical validity and its extension to actual hazard coverage remain open.

**{{!Proposition:jointcorrection}} (A reduced joint certificate) [thm∣prem].** *Couple the host {{eq:host}} and payoffs {{eq:payoff}} to one rule-making domain of {{eq:field}} through $\tau_{\rm eff}(t)=\tau(t)(1-x_r(t))$. Its field equations are*
$$\dot x_r=\zeta x_r(1-x_r)(az_r-c),\qquad
\dot z_r=\varepsilon\left(\sum_j b_{rj}(t)x_j(t)-d_r(t)z_r\right),$$
*with fixed positive $a,c,\zeta,\varepsilon$. All incoming shares lie in $[0,1]$. The nonnegative conversions, positive correction and nominal liability are continuous and locally bounded. Suppose, for every date under consideration,*
$$a\sum_j b_{rj}(t)\le(1-\sigma)c\,d_r(t),\quad 0<\sigma<1,
\qquad \tau(t)\ge\underline\tau,
\qquad \underline\tau(1-\bar x)\ge\nu+\Delta,$$
*where $0\le\bar x<1$ and $\Delta>0$. Take $0<V_{\min}<1$ and fixed positive $\rho,h_V,\nu$. Carriers have fixed nonnegative loads $e_k$, equal background payoffs $\phi_k(t)=\phi(t)$, and constant-speed replicator selection at speed $s>0$, without mutation or entry. Put $E_*=\rho(1-V_{\min})/h_V$. Then the set*
$$V\in[V_{\min},1],\qquad E=\sum_kp_ke_k\le E_*,\qquad
0\le x_r\le\bar x,\qquad 0\le z_r\le c/a$$
*is forward invariant. Within it the captured rule share and the mean load are nonincreasing, and effective liability is at least $\nu+\Delta$. The other field domains and the incoming conversions may change arbitrarily within the stated bounds.*

*Proof.* At $z_r=0$ the support derivative is nonnegative. At $z_r=c/a$,
$$\dot z_r\le\varepsilon\left(\sum_jb_{rj}-d_rc/a\right)
\le-\varepsilon\sigma d_rc/a<0.$$
Thus support remains in $[0,c/a]$, throughout which $\dot x_r\le0$. The initial bound on $x_r$ persists, and the assumed nominal liability therefore supplies $\tau_{\rm eff}\ge\underline\tau(1-\bar x)\ge\nu+\Delta$. Common background and host payoffs cancel from the replicator equation. Since the loads are fixed,
$$\dot E=s\operatorname{Cov}_p(e,\pi)
=s(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\operatorname{Var}_p(e)
\le-s\Delta\operatorname{Var}_p(e)\le0.$$
Hence the load stays below $E_*$. On the host's lower boundary,
$$\dot V\big|_{V=V_{\min}}=V_{\min}\,[\rho(1-V_{\min})-h_VE]\ge0,$$
and at $V=1$ its derivative is nonpositive. These inward boundary conditions preserve all four constraints. $\square$

*What it establishes.* This is a joint result for effective liability, selection and host viability, including a bound on present correction. It allows time-varying incoming influence and correction and arbitrary capture elsewhere, provided the full incoming bound holds. The rate law of {{corrrate}} can supply such a bound only under its own hypotheses; it does not supply the initial-state or liability bounds. The proposition certifies a subset of viable starting states, not every state from which recovery is possible: $E>E_*$ can still be viable with enough initial host reserve and a sufficiently large liability margin, as {{safeguards}} shows.

*What it assumes.* The nominal liability remains enforceable in exactly the stated reduced form, independent correction stays within its bound, and load traits and background advantages have the stated form. No dynamics here produces the correction budget, preserves outside authority, admits new load traits, or proves that actors cannot remove the intervention itself. With unequal background payoffs the load derivative has the additional term $s\operatorname{Cov}_p(e,\phi)$, which may reverse its sign. The result concerns the forgiving host, not every shared condition or irreversible hazard. It is neither a proof of global institutional uninvadability nor a demonstration that a real arrangement can maintain its premises. Making those premises endogenous is the joint target above.

### Why eventual correction is insufficient

An analytic counterexample uses the same reduced link. Set $\rho=h_V=s=\nu=a=c=d=\zeta=\varepsilon=1$, $b=1/2$, $\tau=2$, and $V_{\min}=1/2$. Two loads, zero and one, start with equal shares; $V(0)=1/2$, $x_r(0)=0.8$ and $z_r(0)=0$. The field gain is $1/2$, so capture eventually disappears by {{certificate}}. Yet initial liability is only $0.4$, and
$$\dot E(0)=(1-0.4)\,\frac14=0.15,\qquad
\dot V(0)=0,\qquad \ddot V(0)=-V(0)\dot E(0)=-0.075.$$
The host crosses its floor immediately. Continuous dependence gives the same failure from sufficiently small positive initial host margins. An eventual certificate therefore cannot substitute for the initial capture, support and load bounds. The numerical block RC checks this trajectory and a time-varying protected case at two integration steps. These are model checks, not measurements of actual institutional capacity.

### Selection tests and feasible intervention

A local anti-correction test asks whether a nearby change that obstructs legitimate correction has positive invasion fitness. It is an informative diagnostic, not the full joint target. For a different fitness function from the fixed concave return in {{eq:infret}}, $s(q)=-q+q^2$ has $s'(0)=-1$, but $s(q)>0$ for $q>1$: small changes are disfavoured while larger changes can spread. A frequency-dependent payoff advantage $-1+2x$ makes an obstructive type decline when rare and grow when its share exceeds one half. Rare-invader resistance thus leaves coordinated entry and finite disturbances to examine. A strategy that pays an initial cost to alter future rules also needs its continuation advantage evaluated; an immediate payoff derivative omits that return.

Conversely, negative fitness for every obstructive strategy is not necessary for protection. A feasible binding constraint can keep even profitable attempts from disabling correction. The target is preservation under the admitted changes; incentive alignment, enforceable constraints and limits on conversion into authority are alternative or complementary ways to achieve it. Neutrality supplies no strict restoring force, and with mutation, drift or noise, deterministic invasion tests must be replaced or supplemented by bounds on failure probability over a stated horizon. None of these tests licenses suppressing legitimate criticism by relabelling it obstruction.

Finally, the partial sensitivity $\nabla_{\log(a,b,c,d)}\log(ab/cd)=(1,1,-1,-1)$ does not identify the effect of an implementable policy. For a differentiable intervention $u$ changing all four positive coefficients, the total derivative is
$$\frac{d\log\mathcal R_{\rm cap}}{du}
=\frac{a'}a+\frac{b'}b-\frac{c'}c-\frac{d'}d.$$
An intervention that raises correction by ten per cent but conversion by twenty per cent increases the gain by $1.2/1.1$, holding the other factors fixed. Costs, displacement and institutional responses can alter the sign; shared-resource constraints can make individually attractive interventions jointly infeasible. This is why a design gradient requires a model of the intervention and its responses. The missing full result is an achievable overlap between protection and its continued implementation, with uncertainty, legitimate revision and selection all inside the analysis.
