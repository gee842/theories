## X10. The design in full

One configuration is developed far enough to expose its assumptions. It is one candidate among those Section 12.1 compares under one range of uncertainty: a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions, a verification agreement together with shared provision, regulated competition meeting the requirements of Section 9, an agreement of the type of Section 8.3(g) and the benign winner are others, and the coercive restriction of frontier inputs enters that comparison only under conditions of its own (Section 12.1). The text does not claim the configuration is superior; which design is best is part of crux C4. It is called here a *compact*: an arrangement for shared AI provision and correction, made of separately held functions rather than one institution. Each clause below aims to prevent a party under assessment from turning it against the assessment, and the hardest functions, execution and the fallback, carry the proposed financing, appointment and certificates that the others carry.

**What the functions carry.** The functions below give the carriers of the generative loop (Section 10.4) bodies, financing and ends. Each strand of Section 10.4 faces the questions the pathological loops answer: how it keeps obtaining resources, how it meets free riding and the pressure to expand, how it allows itself to be corrected, and how it avoids turning "protecting the whole" into a purpose no one can challenge. The columns of the table give proposed answers: a rule of financing for the first; formula contributions, the share cap and the levy's wide base for the second; a corrector for the third; and a condition that ends or reverts the body for the fourth.

**Correction through replacement.** The compact's continuity is not the protected outcome; the people's ability to observe, challenge and correct its consequences is. A replacement of an evaluator, provider, funding rule or decision system therefore needs a transition test: do independent evidence, access, practiced human capacity and effective intervention remain available before, during and after the change? Test N applies such perturbations to the whole correction chain. Appeals and proposals to amend a mistaken purpose are legitimate exercises of correction, not capture merely because they weaken an incumbent. A capture claim still needs the separately documented impairment of {{capture}}. Formula funding and separated appointment are candidate mechanisms; {{jointcorrection}} does not prove that either survives. The unresolved production and authority relations below determine whether the candidate can maintain that result's premises.

### Functions, held separately

Each function is held by a different body, separately financed and separately corrected, so that correction lies outside what it corrects. On a planet, "outside" means outside the function: a different source of funds, a different lineage, and certificates anyone can recompute from public data. Every body has a rule of financing, a rule of appointment, a corrector, and a condition that ends or reverts it, so that each can answer the withdrawal question of the guardian test ({{guardian}}), and every body whose findings or decisions have force carries a full inbound row-sum certificate ({{certificate}}(2)), recomputed on a schedule from the data of the disclosure registry. A valid row bounds eventual capture under {{certificate}}'s premises; it does not certify current decisions. Before executive force is assigned or restored, independent assessment must also bound the body's captured share and inherited rule support against registered tolerances for the relevant residence time, using direct performance and intervention evidence as well as conversion data. Recertification in the table includes both tests. Three funds are fed by formula from the levy below: a *service fund* for provision, a *correction fund* for standard-setting, evaluation, disclosure, the chamber and the appeal, each by its own formula line, and an *execution fund* for execution and custody. The rules body is financed by treaty contributions outside the levy, so that the body that sets the formula receives nothing from it.

**Separation rule.** No body both revises the levy formula and appoints evaluators, and neither of those executes findings. Each has its own row-sum certificate. Supermajorities and the chamber's consent apply to the slow loops, amendment and the formula; the fast loop, restricting a specific capability, stays fast, with review after the fact, because every veto point adds delay, and the robustness margin of {{tempo}} depends on it.

**Monocentric by function, nested by option.** Each function is still held by one body. That is a separation of powers at one scale, monocentric function by function, and not polycentricity in the sense of overlapping jurisdictions at several scales that adjust to one another, which is a different candidate in Section 12.1; "many hands" applies inside evaluation only. The compact can instead be nested: each function held at national, regional and planetary levels, above a common floor of admission, custody and disclosure that no level may lower, with mutual recognition of certificates between levels, and with the fast loop placed at the level where the capability is held. Nesting adds redundancy of correction and local knowledge. It also multiplies the bodies whose certificates must hold and the seams through which a party under assessment can play one level against another, every level is a further conversion target, and the common floor must then be entrenched at every level. Nested governance of a planetary commons is itself an open problem (Stern 2011). The requirements of Section 9 constrain functions, not the number of centres; the choice is open.

**The chamber.** It is a corrector, and Section 4 applies to it. Its members are drawn by a rule no participant controls, they rotate, its inbound row sum is certified like any evaluator's, and its removal conditions are in the table. Node-by-node certificates replace an appeal to a trusted body with conditional requirements that many parties should be able to recompute from independent evidence. They do not end the implementation problem: standard-setting, disclosure, access and the authority to execute a correction can themselves be captured or withdrawn. Their continuation through amendment and replacement is part of C4 and Test N, not a consequence of the certificates. The chamber must also remain answerable to legitimate challenge under V3.

**Injection is the injector's violation.** Row sums count conversion into an evaluator from the party it assesses ({{certificate}}(2); {{rivalverify}}), so an assessed party could push an evaluator's row sum above one by sending it funds, staff or data, and so strip its findings of executive force; the same move repeated could manufacture the certificate failures that trigger dissolution. The compact proposes the following response, subject to independent attribution and appeal: Conversion into an evaluator from a party it assesses, outside the evaluator's disclosed channels, is a violation by that party; it does not suspend findings against that party; the assessment is reassigned to an evaluator of different lineage; and certificate failures attributable to a party under assessment count toward no end condition.

**Recusal, and the clock.** This candidate separates a conflict over an individual enforcement decision from participation in revising general purposes. A participant does not decide its own enforcement case. Opening an assessment does not by itself remove its standing to propose a general amendment, give evidence or seek review. Any additional recusal needs stated conflict grounds and a prompt ruling by an appeal panel independent of the initiating evaluator and assessed party, before the relevant amendment decision; if that review is unavailable in time, the disputed recusal cannot determine the general amendment. Assessments open on stated evidence and lapse at a registered deadline. Tolling for withheld access, data or personnel requires independent confirmation that the demand is necessary, proportionate and feasible, with a registered maximum interval and restoration of participation when the grounds lapse. Disputing a demand is not itself withholding. Repeated assessments cannot renew exclusion without new grounds and review. Urgent measures confined to the capability at issue may act on the evidence already available, with prompt review, without giving the assessed party a veto over its own case. These are proposed safeguards whose timely execution must be demonstrated; the appeals panel is itself subject to the correction and replacement requirements. Test N includes an evaluator using assessment and tolling to obstruct a legitimate reform.

**Held-out metrics.** A held-out metric published at registration is no longer held out: it becomes a target (Section 5.4). Metrics are committed in advance, as a salted, high-entropy test package under a stated commitment scheme, and revealed only at evaluation, so that anyone can check that the metric was fixed beforehand. The commitment does not keep the metric secret from those who hold it: that needs a separate secrecy protocol, tested on its own, with holders apart from the assessed, access recorded, leaks traced and tests rotated after each revelation.

**Disclosure.** Certificates are only as good as the data on conversion into correctors, and omission is the cheapest capture: a channel left unreported counts for nothing in a row sum. The disclosure registry keeps mandatory, audited records of funding, staff, data and access flowing into every corrector, from every party, the drafting parties included, with a sanction on the omitting party above its gain from omission ({{blind}}(3)). Without it the certificates of the design could not be recomputed by anyone, so it is the layer on which every other certificate rests, and it gets what every corrector gets: separate funding, an audit of a different lineage, and its own certificate.

**The levy formula is itself a prize.** The levy's *base* is wider than the activity the compact constrains: total compute, or treaty shares set by economic size, with a surcharge on frontier compute above a threshold. A base confined to frontier compute would shrink under exactly the stopping (E2) and slowing (E3) the compact exists to be able to impose, and would make its correctors stakeholders in the activity they constrain; the wider base does not shrink, while the surcharge still prices the frontier. The *correction fund* has two separately assessed obligations: to sustain institutional independence against conversion, and to provide hazard-specific coverage and timely response. The $d^\ast$ of {{corrrate}} is a required rate of dissipation of institutional rule support; it is neither hazard coverage $U$ nor a budget. The production relations from money, human effort, AI assistance, access and authority to that rate, and separately to coverage and response time, remain open (S13). Provisional budgets must therefore be tested against both obligations, not calculated by identifying them. A body that receives none of the correction fund, such as the rules body, estimates conservative requirements from audited conversion data and direct intervention evidence; independent coverage tests also measure correlated misses and the catch rate of planted defects at fixed human hours. These tests constrain different interfaces and do not by themselves establish a dollar-to-$d$ conversion. Payers gain by hiding conversion and recipients by overstating requirements, so estimates and resource changes are independently audited. Formula funding does not rise mechanically with capability or perceived harm, and may fall by at most a registered rate per period, so that an apparent recovery does not immediately dismantle correction. The formula is fixed for a registered period; revision needs the supermajority and the chamber, and revisers are separated from beneficiaries and carry row-sum certificates, because by {{influence}} the formula is a conversion target.

**Frontier contributions by formula.** In a common frontier, cooperation is an equilibrium only while the effort each cooperator contributes to the pool stays above a threshold that rises steeply with the value of winning ({{commonfrontn}}), and effort in the pool is a good shared by every member, which members choosing one by one under-supply (S10). Contributions to the common frontier are therefore paid by formula from the same base as the levy, not subscribed by each participant.

**Providers: voice without vote.** Providers earn revenue and contracts by usefulness and have no rights to appoint evaluators, set thresholds, exclude entrants or raise their cost of entry, or amend the rules. They have voice: notice and comment on every rule and standard, technical input to evaluation, and standing to contest a finding through the appeal, with their own conversion into every body disclosed. The experience of commons is that users excluded from revising their rules withhold local knowledge and compliance, and here the strongest of them also hold an exit option (Ostrom 1990); Section 4 instead identifies conditions under which appropriable influence over assessment pays ({{influence}}), not a universal implication from voting rights to capture. The compact makes a design judgment that this risk warrants voice without vote for providers in those governing functions. The preference needs comparative testing against arrangements with bounded voting rights and equally effective correction; its necessity is not established. The tension is real, and it does not end at the line: a voice that is heard more when its holder is larger is itself a channel of conversion, which disclosure and the row sums have to count. The chamber, the appeal and the open channels of comment are where the tension is managed, not dissolved.

**End conditions.** If coverage falls below the participation threshold, or a stated number of the functions' certificates fail for a stated period for reasons not attributable to any party under assessment, the compact dissolves into its separately held functions, each continuing under its own rule. Dissolution does not release a constructed decisive class or terminate its custody obligations. Protections against unilateral domination are entrenched, but legal continuity alone is not effective control. Every power that confers authority states what would end it ({{guardian}}), who then controls its assets and how that transfer is enforced.

**Before and after construction.** Before a decisive class is built, withdrawal may leave less physical overhang than a sprint, but diffused knowledge, installed compute and reconstruction capacity can still make a renewed race fast. After construction, that earlier fallback is unavailable. Before any construction is authorized, the candidate therefore requires a registered disposition plan for enrolled systems, copies, compute access and release keys; a separately funded successor custodian and independent executor, with access and authority installed in advance; and D4/D5 drills of the transfer against resistance by the strongest participant. On failure of readiness or participation, release and use authorizations revert to the registered limits, enrolled assets remain in custody or pass to the independently assessed successor, and the executor quarantines them or disables their enrolled frontier use under those limits. Rollback or destruction of inventoried copies is an option only where its safety and completion can be independently checked. Essential services move to nondecisive standby systems. If effective independent actuation cannot be demonstrated, this candidate has not established readiness to construct the class; it returns to the hard case of consent or the separately compared coercive fallback. No disposition erases scientific knowledge, proves that concealed copies do not exist, or prevents reconstruction from all unenrolled resources. Those residuals, the transfer's own risk and the successor's failure risk enter C4; the plan is a testable candidate, not a guarantee of returning to the state in which the class was never built.

**Against Ostrom's principles.** Boundaries (capability thresholds and hazard classes), monitoring and graduated sanctions are present. Proportionality of benefits and costs is partial: the largest payers have no vote, and strong types take part only for shares near their race payoffs ({{participate}}). Collective choice is answered by voice without vote. Conflict resolution is central, through one appeal, and neither cheap nor local, which nesting would answer. Recognition by an outside authority has no analogue at the scale of the planet; the host jurisdiction's first execution is the design's only link to the sovereignty of its participants. Nesting is the option above. The principles were drawn from commons that lasted at smaller scales (Ostrom 1990), and their transfer to a global commons is itself in question (Stern 2011).

**Other provisions.** A charter with three purposes: useful provision for all participants' populations; protection against O1 and O2; effective correction. Membership open to every state and provider above a capability threshold on non-discriminatory terms, participation made individually rational by access to shared services, a share of the dividend and capacity building ({{participate}}). Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against unilateral domination kept apart (Section 10.3). Baseline access to useful services with continuity guarantees; portable interfaces, escrow of systems providing essential services, and support for each participant to audit, operate and replace them. Narrow E1 first, for decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty: human authorization and a minimum time for deliberation for decisions on the use of force and on nuclear command, drilled parallel capacity, an evidence channel independent of AI, doctrine open to mutual inspection, and drills measuring the full detection-and-response delay under human authorization against the breakout bound; broad E1 as the scope (Section 11.3). Admission constraints common to every system, before consequential deployment, internal deployment included, for divergences with common costs and a positive tax, with decision policies retained by performance only inside them; a zero tax removes the positive-tax rationale for a constraint only with evidence that deployed safeguards persist without it, since neutrality does not remove existing unsafe variants; for masked classes, admission by capability threshold with control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation ({{masked}}). Commonly funded, openly published alignment and control research. Pace: no building of the decisive class while its governance is unmet (E2); independently, hazardous growth and deployment stay within demonstrated coverage and control, with suspension or rollback where needed (E3), on every branch including preferred broad E1. Capability thresholds are set by evaluation, and input thresholds in effective compute tighten with algorithmic progress. Graduated remedies: notice; restriction of a specific capability; suspension of specific deployments; never suspension of essential services; emergency powers narrow, time-limited, reviewable and stating what would end them. Bounded competition continues inside: providers compete on usefulness, cost and reliability, and jurisdictions keep plural local purposes.

### Hard cases

**An adverse finding against a weaker participant.** Pending appeal, only the capability at issue may be restricted; essential services continue through alternative providers; the appellant sees the evidence under confidentiality, the panel decides within the deadline, and compensation follows if the finding is overturned. The power to suspend could become containment; the safeguards are the narrow scope of interim measures, the burden of evidence on the compact, and the appellant's standing.

**An adverse finding against the leading provider.** Suspending it could interrupt services for everyone, so formally equal procedures would be unequal in effect. The share cap, escrowed systems and funded standby capacity mean that restricting the leader's hazardous capability need not stop services. The author's book gives the rule for this case: the greater the power, the stricter the regulation it must receive, because it can shape everyone's conditions (力愈大者，受调当愈严，以其能塑众人之境也) [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14 第十四章]. The compact's version is that the leader's row in every certificate is weighted by its share, since the return to influence rises with the square of share ({{influence}}). Execution falls first to the leader's host jurisdiction. If that fails, what remains is the coercive restriction of inputs to the decisive class, the same form whose endorsement Section 8.3 withholds. The design does not settle it: it enters only as the candidate of Section 12.1, under the conditions stated there, with its escalation risk counted in $\Theta$, and the theory does not derive that its net effect is positive. Where neither consent nor such a restriction is available, the case is the hard case of Section 8.3. This is the hardest case, and the design does not dissolve it. It reduces the asymmetry of dependence that would otherwise make enforcement against the strongest impossible, and it gives the body that would execute a restriction a financing, an appointment and a certificate that do not run through the party restricted.

**A coalition of insiders.** Rival elites agree on mutual secrecy and immunity while weakening public correction. Four channels answer to people outside the coalition: the chamber, protected reporting, evaluators funded by formula, and publication of findings by default after a fixed delay. The disclosure registry makes the coalition's own conversion visible, and omission is the violation.

**An unjustified restriction.** The compact is used to contain a developing participant. It has standing to challenge, the burden is on the compact to show evidence of hazard, restrictions carry sunsets, and the compact has obligations to build capacity.

**Succession and withdrawal.** A change of leadership triggers review, not suspension. A withdrawal by a large participant makes the constraints borne by the rest unilateral and can trigger further exits; the participation threshold and the conditions of market access are what keep coverage above it (Section 9, "coverage can unravel"). Before construction, shared benefits may lower the value of winning and the absent class may lower physical overhang, without proving that reconstruction is distant. After construction, withdrawal activates the disposition and independent execution plan above; separately held functions alone do not neutralize built capability. If custody cannot be maintained or disposition verified, the fallback includes the remaining capability and knowledge overhang, and no lower hazard than the compared race has been established.

**Loss of an essential service.** Redundancy, escrow and standby capacity carry the service.

Three things could make its hazard of breakdown lower than that of an agreement that keeps the contest underneath: shared benefits lower the value of winning ({{relaxtrust}}; {{share}}); keeping AI out of the strategic loops whose delegation would lock with near certainty is a strict equilibrium among actors who price in the lock ({{delegatelock}}); and a lifecycle-specific fallback may leave less physical overhang or retain effective custody ({{fallback}}), if its execution and residual hazards support that comparison. These are hypotheses about the design, not established results; the fallback lemma requires the actual pre- or post-construction history to satisfy its hazard bound.

### Federations, at both orders

Federations meet both orders of Section 10.2 at once. Competition among member units for capital and residents can become a race to the bottom, the pressure of winning among members, and the strongest members can capture the centre, the selection of the selectors. At the first order, common minimum standards bind every member, and fiscal equalization gives members capacity they do not have to win, both widening the effective self of members that value their absolute payoff and supplying the $k_0$ of the loop gain; for members that act strategically, pooling raises the working self-model rather than only slowing the race ({{share}}(1)). At the second order, equal representation of small members and supermajorities for constitutional change keep the centre from being bought by the largest, a precedent for voting that does not follow compute. A global minimum tax on corporate profits is a recent instance of one rule for all, and of how coverage unravels: the largest participant's groups were exempted from two of its rules in recognition of that participant's own minimum-tax rules (EVIDENCE EV18), the coverage problem of Section 9's participation threshold arising at the participant whose coverage matters most. The same federal form can select for winning or for contribution; what decides is what it rewards.
