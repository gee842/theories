# Changing what winning confers

*A short entry to Theory V7.2. The full argument, conditions and sources are in [THEORY](THEORY.md), [SUPPLEMENT](SUPPLEMENT.md) and [EVIDENCE](EVIDENCE.md).*

AI can improve each participant's ability to win while making the contest worse for everyone. The question is whether the participants can still restrain that contest when success changes both the reward for breaking restraint and the institutions able to enforce it.

The proposal is to change what winning confers. Reward useful contribution, but prevent its reward from buying unlimited advantage in the same contest or control over the standards by which it is judged. Put enough provision beneath the contest that access to essential capabilities does not depend entirely on winning it. Keep correction independent of the participants whose success it must sometimes reverse.

## Why the pressure persists

The mechanism has three conditions: relative success decides which participants grow or survive; winnings buy capacity; and capacity helps decide future wins. Firms, states, research programmes and AI lineages can all occupy this structure, to different degrees. Where a lead brings enough further advantage, it compounds. When success also buys the rules, the evaluators, or the formation of the next generation's objectives, the feedback reaches the process of selection itself. That is the meta-attractor of winning.

No malicious motive is required. A participant may correctly see that restraint costs it position, and that losing position weakens its ability to protect anything it values. Its rival can reason the same way. Better reasoning inside the game does not necessarily improve the game. Nor does making an AI faithful to each principal align the contest among those principals.

The paper proves parts of this argument in separate models. It does not prove that every real contest has all of them, or that their effects necessarily move together. The link to the present AI trajectory is an empirical diagnosis with named gaps. Declarations of a race, rising capability and attempts to influence rules are evidence relevant to that diagnosis; they do not by themselves measure the strength of the feedback.

## The limits that matter

A few distinctions change what can reasonably follow from the models.

- **Compounding is conditional.** Provision, diffusion, sharing and limits on convertibility can prevent concentration. The effect of a lever depends on the channel in which it acts. The local price of standing is derived under specified dynamics and convergence conditions; it is not a universal formula for strategic or stochastic contests. A stronger relative continuation return can coexist with convergence to equal standing, so it is not itself a concentration test.
- **Recovery is not present safety.** A correction system can be guaranteed to recover eventually and still spend a dangerously long time almost completely captured. Current conditions, accumulated influence, time to recovery and the corrector's ability to act all matter.
- **Failed assurance is not proved catastrophe.** An evaluation that cannot rule out hidden divergence establishes a gap in assurance. The claim that survival tends to zero additionally requires continuing exposure and a lower bound on the risk left uncovered. Those are separate premises to test.
- **Bounded wants need not stop the race.** An objective can be bounded and still strictly prefer every increment. The safer branch needs actual cessation of the hazardous expansion, together with correction that catches up.
- **Detection is only part of response.** A warning helps only if someone can authorize and carry out an effective intervention before the deviation becomes decisive. Keeping AI detection available does not establish that human decision authority preserves that whole clock.

These limits narrow the claims. They also make the proposed change testable: measure the incentive to drop a safeguard against the expected enforceable sanction; the probability of effective response before a breakout; hazardous variety against independent coverage; and the current state and recovery time of correction.

## Keeping correction possible as the system changes

The proposed alternative has a constructive obligation: capability may increase, models may be replaced, and institutions may change, while shared viability and the real means of correction remain protected. A policy that could correct a mistake is insufficient if the people responsible cannot obtain evidence, authorize intervention or carry it out in time. Funding, appointment, access, infrastructure and authority therefore belong inside the system being assessed. What should persist is the ability to learn and revise; no particular institution or doctrine is exempt from legitimate challenge.

One reduced result now connects rule capture, effective liability, selection of harmful activity and the shared environment (SUPPLEMENT S16). It identifies a sufficient region in which both correction and viability remain within their bounds. It assumes that the stated influence bounds and effective liability can be maintained; it does not prove their institutional implementation. A proposed, unrun experiment, Test N, compares arrangements at matched resources while allowing changes to governance and successors. Local incentives against obstruction, eventual recovery and a good audit score are intermediate measurements; the outcome is effective correction before harm while agency and useful service are preserved.

## What follows for action

The judgment starts with values. Large-scale loss of life and irreversible loss of human decision authority are protected first. Those affected should retain a meaningful ability to contest the systems governing them. The standpoint is humankind as one community with a shared fate—人类命运共同体—not the victory of one part viewed from outside the whole.

The preferred branch is **broad E1**: AI is not used as an instrument in contests tending toward dominance or concentration. The unit of judgment is the game across its participants, not whether one isolated use helps its user. This applies to labour, finance and persuasion as well as force, where the concentrating dynamic is shown. **Narrow E1**, keeping decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty in human hands, is a first step. Where broad E1 has not been achieved, **E2** stops the building of decisive capability and **E3** binds hazardous development to the pace of understanding and control. This preserves the author's preference among social arrangements. Independently, every branch must meet the coverage and effective-control conditions: a cooperative programme that develops hazardous capability faster than correction can handle may still need E3. Stopping and slowing are different arrangements.

The paper distinguishes three kinds of justification, not three consecutive stages:

1. **Act now where the case does not wait on the central empirical disputes, and retain protective steps until their named withdrawal conditions are met.** Measure hazards and correction together; develop and test verification; fund shared alignment and control research; maintain independent correction; apply common constraints before consequential deployment, including internal deployment; begin narrow E1 and prepare custody mechanisms.
2. **Prepare now and activate under precommitted conditions.** Stopping the decisive class, common custody and slower development need defined triggers, enough time to take effect, and instruments able to enforce them. A trigger without an institution able to act is a proposal, not a mechanism.
3. **Compare complete designs with their own failures included.** Concentrated custody, distributed arrangements, restrictions on inputs and continued competition must face the same uncertainty and protection standard. Where every worst case exceeds tolerance, their ranking remains a stated judgment. Recovery counts only if an independent corrector can act before the protected outcome is lost. Withdrawal before decisive capability has been built is different from withdrawal afterward; existing capability and knowledge remain part of the latter risk. Durable verified containment is a competing endpoint if its enforcement and coverage conditions can be maintained; a sprint followed by relinquishing superiority differs from a permanent monopoly freeze.

Trust and verification are both necessary. Verification can provide grounds for trust; some prior willingness to treat its findings as information is needed for it to work. Neither is a substitute for the other. The first steps should make reliable conduct observable and preserve the capacity to reverse mistakes.

## What remains open

The central unresolved questions concern the world: whether hazardous variety outgrows correction; whether uncovered hazards retain a material risk floor; whether success buys effective control over evaluators; how quickly a lead could become beyond response; and whether the proposed institutions can retain independent evidence, legitimate authority and the capacity to act in time through their own amendment and replacement. [TESTS](TESTS.md) states the cruxes and what different answers would change. [CLAIMS](CLAIMS.md) separates proved model results, empirical premises, conjectures and recommendations.

The affirmative purpose remains larger than risk reduction. We are participants in an ongoing process of becoming. Competition, plurality, decay and renewal can belong to that process without allowing a part to exhaust the conditions of the whole. A generative order rewards contribution while preserving the conditions for more contribution, including contributions no present winner would choose.
