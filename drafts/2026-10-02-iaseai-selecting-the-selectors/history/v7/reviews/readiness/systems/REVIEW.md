# V7 readiness review: systems, governance and argument

Reviewed 4 October 2026. Fresh adversarial review of START-HERE, THEORY §§1–6 and §§8–12, the relevant §7 contest branches, SUPPLEMENT S8–S11 and S13, and DISCUSSION X8–X11. I formed this assessment from the manuscript rather than prior reviews or repair notes. I did not use the existing numerical suite as assurance and made no manuscript or generated-manuscript edits. Small independent witnesses are saved beside this report.

**Verdict: ready for private specialist circulation as an explicitly unsettled draft; revise before public working-paper release; not publication-ready theory.** The central project remains credible as a collection of conditional mechanisms and a declared institutional preference. The issues below are mainly inconsistencies in the route from those mechanisms to policy and comparison, not proof that the project is false. A conditional working paper need not finish empirical calibration, implement planetary institutions, or prove a joint viability kernel. It does need its own policy branches, alternatives and fallback claims to remain consistent with its models. The repairs below are narrower than a rewrite of the project.

Severity: **P1** changes a central policy/fallback conclusion; **P2** is a material comparative or conceptual overclaim; **P3** is a non-blocking clarification. These priorities concern this manuscript's readiness, not any estimate of real-world AI risk.

## Findings ranked by severity

### 1. P1 — Broad E1 does not, as defined, discharge the independent growth-channel condition

**Anchors.** The definition is [src/core/60_s6.md:103](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/60_s6.md:103) (THEORY 672). The branch is explicit in [src/core/b0_s11.md:56](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/b0_s11.md:56) (THEORY 1112), [src/core/c0_s12.md:9](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/c0_s12.md:9) (THEORY 1163), and START-HERE 33. Contrary conditions appear at [src/core/50_s5.md:88](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/50_s5.md:88) (THEORY 525) and [src/core/b0_s11.md:46](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/b0_s11.md:46) (THEORY 1102). The same E1 exception is applied to masked hazards at [src/core/90_s9.md:27](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/90_s9.md:27) (THEORY 930), THEORY 1228, and DISCUSSION 180.

**Problem.** Broad E1 excludes AI from contests tending toward dominance or concentration. Its three enforcement layers concern illegitimate concentration, aggregate competitive use and the game's convertibility/decisiveness/control. None requires coverage to keep pace with noncompetitive hazardous growth. Yet the preferred branch is presented as broad E1 on one side and E2/E3 warranted where it has not been achieved on the other. The growth-channel theorem explicitly applies regardless of objectives; §§5.3 and 11.3 expressly say E3 still binds a world that has stopped racing. A cooperative programme can satisfy E1 while adding hazardous modes faster than correction covers them, without competing for standing or concentrating control. The example beside this report sets Y_t = 2^t, U_t = t+1 and the theorem's residual floor at 0.01; the uncovered share approaches one and survival tends to zero with no contest in the example.

**Why it matters.** The short entry and judgment can be read to exempt the preferred branch from precisely the independent hazard constraint the manuscript says applies to it. This is not a disagreement with Ren's preference for E1; it is a mismatch between the stated definition and the condition the branch needs.

**Minimal correction.** Preserve the preference. Either define the preferred broad-E1 arrangement to include the independent coverage/control condition, explaining that E1 as currently defined is only one component; or retain broad E1's present meaning and say E3's C1/C3/C12 condition applies on every branch, while E2's decisive-capability trigger is separate. Distinguish a preference among social arrangements from a logical exemption from growth-channel risk. Align START-HERE, abstract, §9, §11 and §12.

**Effect on thesis.** No damage to the race diagnosis. The preferred arrangement needs an additional explicitly independent constraint; E1 alone is not a sufficient escape.

### 2. P1 — The compact's safe-fallback rationale silently assumes that authorized resumption never occurred

**Anchors.** [src/disc/10_X10.md:48](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/10_X10.md:48) (DISCUSSION 400) forbids building the decisive class *while readiness is unmet*. Custody permits release or conversion to plural holding after readiness in DISCUSSION 371. But [src/disc/10_X10.md:60](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/10_X10.md:60) (DISCUSSION 412) says a withdrawing party starts far from decisive capability *because the decisive class was not built*. D1 repeats this at [src/disc/10_X10.md:68](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/10_X10.md:68) (DISCUSSION 420) and [src/disc/08_X8.md:129](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/08_X8.md:129) (DISCUSSION 300). Safe fallback is one of Lemma 8.2's two premises (THEORY 811–815).

**Problem.** The compact is allowed to reach readiness and authorize construction, custody and eventual release. Its D1 account then reasons as though construction has never happened. Dissolving into separately held functions does not by itself remove built models, accessible weights, trained personnel, effective compute or learned methods. The fallback argument is valid as a design intention before construction; the text applies it to the arrangement generally. The explicitly listed catastrophic capture-of-custody branch does not fill this gap: ordinary withdrawal or institutional dissolution after a successful period of authorized operation is a different path.

**Why it matters.** The comparative preference gives weight to lower overhang and a safer dissolution path. That advantage needs to be specified across the entire allowed lifecycle, not only the initial stopping period. The issue exists even if all readiness measurements were correct when made.

**Minimal correction.** Separate pre-construction and post-construction D1 states. For the latter, state the disposition of existing weights, compute, keys and knowledge, who executes it after withdrawal, and what claims remain conditional. If the configuration is intended never to build the decisive class, say so and revise the resumption language. Until then, limit the lower-overhang/no-built-class claim to the pre-construction state and register the later fallback as unresolved.

**Effect on thesis.** Weakens a particular reversibility advantage, not the case for preparing custody. It moves part of C4 from “test this specified fallback” to “specify this missing fallback first.”

### 3. P2 — The finite-sprint alternative switches from self-restraint to a monopoly freeze inside its rebuttal

**Anchors.** [src/core/80_s8.md:64](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/80_s8.md:64) (THEORY 854); [src/supp/08_S8.md:34](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/supp/08_S8.md:34) (SUPPLEMENT 843); DISCUSSION 217–235, especially [src/disc/08_X8.md:51](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/08_X8.md:51). The comparison and durability tables repeat the imposed-freeze reading (THEORY 893; DISCUSSION 296, 325).

**Problem.** The strongest form begins with a winner freezing or rolling back its own hazardous capability, and the text explicitly grants that giving up permanent superiority can preserve survival. The mechanism answer then asserts that “the freeze” is necessarily a stop imposed on every actor except the sprinter. That only follows for a policy of preserving the monopoly, not for the conceded self-freeze/rollback path. A leader can freeze its own stock, allow rivals to catch up and accept loss of superiority. It faces renewed competition and commitment problems, but it is not thereby imposing the “most unequal” stop on others.

**Why it matters.** A central alternative is rejected partly through properties added during the rebuttal. The manuscript's standard promises to compare strongest forms symmetrically. Pointing out that self-restraint is fragile is different from proving that it necessarily has coercive monopoly incidence.

**Minimal correction.** Split the two paths: sprint then relinquish superiority; sprint then enforce a permanent monopoly freeze. Apply peak exposure, prevention, internal-correction and renewed-contest objections to the first. Apply imposed unequal incidence and coercive enforcement to the second. Update the comparison tables. The ranking may remain adverse under the declared values, but should be stated as that judgment.

**Effect on thesis.** The manuscript retains serious objections to sprinting. It loses a purported general mechanism against a broader alternative than that mechanism actually describes.

### 4. P2 — An indefinitely maintained verification bound is ruled out as an endpoint without a result that rules it out

**Anchors.** [src/core/80_s8.md:91](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/80_s8.md:91) (THEORY 881) says the agreement “can be the end only if it lowers the value of winning … rather than only bounding defection.” Compare Corollary 5.7 (THEORY 489–491), Theorem 8.7(1)/(3) (875–879), and Proposition S8.5 (SUPPLEMENT 853).

**Problem.** R4 allows restraint whenever every deviation's breakout probability remains below its enforceable bound. No theorem says that an agreement which preserves relative positions must eventually lose that bound. Theorem 8.7(1) takes the value of deviation to infinity while other payoffs and sanctions stay fixed; that is not unavoidable in the class being compared. Part (3) expressly permits verification to bound the deviation's value. Preserving a motive is not proving that the motive will defeat a durable constraint.

**Independent witness.** In (14), set δ = 0.9, g = 0.1, C = 1, D = 100, v = 0 and p_d = 0.001. The temptation is 0.0991, the available sanction 0.8991, and the bound on p_d is about 0.009889. These primitives can remain unchanged period after period; the cooperation path has no breakout because no deviation is profitable. The gross value of winning remains 100. This is a model-level incentive witness, not a claim that such verification is empirically available. Its existence is enough to defeat the stated necessity; empirical feasibility belongs to C4/C5/C7.

**Minimal correction.** Call the bridge characterization a conditional durability judgment: it is a bridge *if* its verification/custody bound erodes faster than it is maintained, or the net exit gain outgrows enforceable sanctions. Admit durable verified containment as a competing endpoint under the converse conditions. If “value of winning” instead means the net expected gain after enforcement, define it so and avoid implying that G2/G5 are uniquely necessary to lower it.

**Effect on thesis.** Broadens the solution space within the paper's own functional requirements. It does not vindicate today's competition or show the cited agreement feasible.

### 5. P2 — The financing design equates institutional correction with hazard coverage across an explicitly open interface

**Anchors.** [src/disc/10_X10.md:38](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/10_X10.md:38) (DISCUSSION 390) indexes the fund to “the coverage each hazard class requires, the d* of Proposition 4.5.” Contrast THEORY 387 and [src/supp/13_S13.md:24](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/supp/13_S13.md:24) (SUPPLEMENT 1141), which explicitly state that correction rate d and contestable coverage U are different and neither bounds the other.

**Problem.** Proposition 4.5's d* is the decay/correction rate needed to offset the return to cultivating influence over an evaluation domain. It is not a number of hazard modes covered at a catch rate. A class can have low conversion pressure and enormous technical review demand, or high conversion pressure and few hazard modes. Miss correlation and planted-defect catch rates are useful performance measures, but they do not identify the structural conversion parameters or their required budget without the production relation the paper leaves open.

**Why it matters.** Formula funding is one of the concrete devices that makes the generative order's persistence credible. This sentence appears to supply a computable funding rule where the required resource/authority mapping has not been specified.

**Minimal correction.** Split institutional-independence funding from technical coverage and effective-response funding. Treat d* as an input to the first, not as a coverage requirement. State that turning both obligations into a budget needs a separately tested production relation involving labor, AI assistance, access, authority and latency. The fund can remain a candidate rule with conservative buffers.

**Effect on thesis.** Local correction to the institutional proposal. The argument for resources independent of individual winners remains intact; sufficiency of the proposed financing formula is unresolved.

### 6. P3 — Distinguish functional institutional “understanding” from shared cognitive understanding

**Anchors.** [src/core/30_s3.md:94](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/30_s3.md:94) (THEORY 290) makes the two-route claim and calls it the “formal content” of understanding being a long-term requirement. The qualifying scope of Lemma 3.11 is THEORY 288. The necessity recurs in the conclusion, THEORY 1237.

**Problem.** Global irreversible failure cannot be learned from afterward. Lemma 3.11 also shows that a purely common benefit supplies no relative selective advantage. Neither establishes that prior adoption guided by an accurate shared model is the only timely preventive route. The lemma's own scope admits protective traits carried by correlated local benefits. For example, a practice with local benefit b greater than private cost c can spread while also preserving a common condition, even if nobody represents that common condition accurately. Existing robust constraints can prevent a first global failure for local reasons; small reversible experiments can establish partial constraints without a single whole-system account.

**Strongest charitable reading.** [src/core/30_s3.md:105](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/30_s3.md:105) (THEORY 301) explicitly defines a system “knowing” it is one by whether its selection counts what its parts depend on. On that functional definition, a locally selected protective institution can qualify, and the counterexample does not refute the functional claim. This reduces the concern to an ambiguity between that definition and the shared/model-guided cognitive route at source lines 94 and 107 (THEORY 290, 303). It is not a decisive readiness blocker.

**Why it matters.** “We are one” is doing empirical, normative and institutional work separately in §3.7. The narrow selection theorem should not be used to certify the stronger cognitive or historical necessity. Otherwise readers can reasonably reject the overclaim while accepting the model and the shared-fate standpoint.

**Minimal correction.** Retain the common-host premise and V1/V3. State the precise result: the common benefit alone does not select the safeguard, and learning from the protected irreversible loss comes too late. Distinguish functional institutional understanding from the proposal that participants share an accurate model. Present the latter as the proposed route where local incentives, correlated benefits and robust constraints do not already suffice, or as a declared strategic hypothesis.

**Effect on thesis.** Narrows a philosophical/causal necessity claim without weakening the practical case for understanding and coordination.

## What is appropriately open, rather than a readiness defect

The manuscript is unusually explicit that its joint institutional basin is not proved (THEORY 1140–1149; SUPPLEMENT 1103, 1116), its comparative loss bounds are unestimated (THEORY 819, 1189), and its proposed reversals have not been shown to work (THEORY 1190; DISCUSSION 304, 338–347). Those disclosures support a conditional working paper. I have not treated their incompleteness as a theorem error or demanded empirical completion before circulation.

The following are substantive tests, but the paper already identifies them as such: whether hazardous variety outruns independent coverage; whether an uncovered fraction supplies a material residual-hazard floor; whether correctors can measure present capture as well as long-run conversion; whether independent lineages actually decorrelate misses; whether a strongest participant can be constrained in time; whether a custody quorum remains independent; and whether the human authorization step fits the entire detection-and-response clock. No current text establishes an executable global deployment plan, and the paper should not be circulated as one.

On enforcement, the distinction between a registered trigger and installed means of execution is important and present in THEORY 1182. Dormant control of compute/weights or a sanction surviving success is a proposed mechanism; a prior promise alone is expressly insufficient. On corrector independence, THEORY 409 and DISCUSSION 361 appropriately distinguish eventual recovery from current independence and require current-state evidence. On reversibility, the same pre-loss/independent-executor condition is now applied to the design and continuation in DISCUSSION 304–347. These are real strengths of the argument as written, not empirical certificates that the proposed institutions satisfy them.

The remaining design burden is to turn “certified” into identifiable measures and feasible response capacity. Public records of funds, appointment and access are not by themselves estimates of every causal b_ij, c_i and d_i. No present certificate is supplied, as the paper admits. For a working paper, a proposed measurement protocol and candid scope suffice; for implementation, direct performance, uncertainty bounds, adversarial audit and actuation exercises would be indispensable.

## Normative choices, not findings of error

I do not count the priority of O1/O2, the standing of affected persons, the preference for broad E1, or choosing a robust protection standard over discounted expected benefit as proof errors. They are declared commitments. Similarly, one can disagree with voice-without-vote for providers, common custody, or the weighting of forgone medical benefits without refuting any model. Those choices should continue to be labelled as values or institutional judgments. Findings 1–5 concern claims that exceed or conflict with the manuscript's own stated support even while granting its values; finding 6 is an ambiguity with a viable charitable reading.

## Release recommendation

For private specialists, circulate this draft with the findings and request independent mathematical and institutional review. For a public working paper, first repair findings 1–5 and propagate the fixes into the entry, claim boxes, comparison tables and conclusion; clarify finding 6 when convenient. This is a consistency gate, not a requirement to solve C0–C13. For publication as an integrated theory, obtain independent proof review and make the proposed contribution precise enough that conditional mechanisms, empirical diagnosis and the institutional preference cannot be mistaken for one proved end-to-end result.

The central thesis survives these corrections: principal alignment does not settle the game among principals, success can compromise the institutions meant to correct it, and altering incentives, independent correction and timely enforcement are serious candidate interventions. What must narrow is the exclusion of alternative endpoints and the claimed completeness of the preferred branch and its fallback.
