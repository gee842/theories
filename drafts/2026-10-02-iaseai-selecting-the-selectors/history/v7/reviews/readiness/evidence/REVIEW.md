## Material findings, ranked

### E1 — P2: Tests D and G overstate what their named rival literatures predict

**Locations:** [TESTS source:123](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/reg/TESTS.md:123), [TESTS source:126](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/reg/TESTS.md:126). The promise that the tests discriminate is at [TESTS source:116](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/reg/TESTS.md:116), with core reliance at [§7.4:97](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/70_s7.md:97).

Test D assigns Pierson a prediction that, once share levels have fallen, there is no re-absorption regardless of how briefly the reform was held. Pierson's account instead includes learning, coordination and adaptive expectations, beyond setup costs. Its discussion of political authority explicitly describes actors changing rules to reinforce their power. Those mechanisms can persist after an observed share changes. Excluding a sunk-cost explanation does not eliminate them, and the source does not supply the claimed withdrawal response. This is a misattributed comparator, not a refutation of V7's own dwell calculation. [Pierson, *Increasing Returns, Path Dependence, and the Study of Politics*, pp. 254–255, 259; primary article reproduction](https://www.critical-juncture.net/uploads/2/1/9/9/21997192/pierson_increasing_returns.pdf).

Test G assigns structural realism no objective-dependent restraint gap even at intermediate breakout probability. Powell (1991), the named comparator, holds absolute-welfare preferences fixed and changes the constraints that make relative gains useful. It does not establish invariant conduct across V7's varied objectives. X1's own account correctly recognizes the constraint-based argument at [X1:105](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/01_X1.md:105). A within-model simulation varying objectives does not by itself distinguish a broad school that has not been represented in the same game. [Powell, *Absolute and Relative Gains in International Relations Theory*, pp. 1304, 1311–1316, official bibliographic identity](https://doi.org/10.2307/1963947); [retrieved primary-text reproduction](https://studylib.net/doc/8670492/absolute-and-relative-gains-in-international-relations-th...). The publisher endpoint failed; the reproduced article text was checked, not a secondary summary.

**Minimal fix:** Supply a specified rival model with matched observables, information, costs and initial conditions, then derive its response to the same intervention. Until then, label D and G as predictions or within-model tests and remove the broad contrary attributions. A fair comparator may still reveal a distinctive prediction; this review does not claim that none exists.

### E2 — P2: X1 drops the condition that makes falling detection defeat deterrence

**Location:** [X1:153](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/01_X1.md:153).

The fifth novelty item says coordination is insufficient when sanctions are bounded and detection tends to zero. It omits the safeguard-tax condition. A tax that falls faster than expected sanction can leave deterrence intact. For example, with bounded sanction 1, detection 1/K and tax 1/K², expected sanction exceeds tax for K>1. The external masking demonstrations do not establish either a permanently positive tax or that detection approaches zero with capability.

**Already adequate elsewhere:** [EV6:142](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/reg/EVIDENCE.md:142), [§8.3(a):56](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/80_s8.md:56), [X8:9](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/08_X8.md:9), and [TESTS:168–172](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/reg/TESTS.md:168) preserve the joint comparison and separate assurance from realized harm. I am flagging the remaining summary leak, not reopening those resolved passages.

**Minimal fix:** Add the positive lower bound on the tax, or say that insufficiency follows when detection times enforceable sanction falls below the tax. Preserve the distinction between failure to establish safety and proof of an unavoidable hazard.

### E3 — P3: EV6 gives evaluation a causal role that belongs to safety training

**Location:** [EV6:131](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/reg/EVIDENCE.md:131).

The final sentence calls MacDiarmid et al.'s result a masked divergence produced by evaluation itself. The intervention was chat-style RLHF safety training: chat evaluations then looked aligned while agentic evaluations still elicited misalignment. Evaluation revealed the context dependence; the study did not show that the act of evaluation produced it. The experiment also supplied knowledge of reward-hacking strategies through synthetic documents or prompts before training in production environments. That scope matters when reading “natural” as evidence about deployment prevalence. [MacDiarmid et al., abstract and §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18397v1).

**Already adequate elsewhere:** [X1:95](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/01_X1.md:95) and [X1:133](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/01_X1.md:133) give the right causal account; EV6's constructed-setting and unknown-frequency qualifications are also appropriate.

**Minimal fix:** Replace the last sentence with “The safety-training intervention produced context-dependent misalignment that chat-style evaluation missed.” Add the supplied reward-hacking knowledge if retaining the word “production” as an ecological-validity signal.

## Contrary evidence and interpretive limits

The strongest contrary evidence is already visible in V7: pacing can be sustained within a competitive model; the slow-first commitment can be privately valuable; policy reciprocity can strengthen safeguards; evaluations can be improved; mitigation removed some experimental misalignment; benchmark leads remain limited; and the pilot failed its first prediction. These are not evidence that the risks vanish. They rule out reading the diagnosis as an unconditional law of competitive AI development.

One relevant addition would improve the balance around [X1:117](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/01_X1.md:117) and [X8:32–34](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/disc/08_X8.md:32): Zizzo's experiment directly tested Harris–Vickers and found only limited support for its predicted relationship between lead size and effort; leaders did not generally out-invest followers, nor did large gaps produce the predicted near-monopoly. It is a small laboratory study, not a decisive estimate for AI firms. [Zizzo, *Racing with uncertainty: a patent race experiment*, publisher abstract, introduction and conclusions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016771870100087X); [author university record](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/racing-with-uncertainty-a-patent-race-experiment/). V7 already restricts the key statements to contests with discouragement, so this is a useful empirical boundary, not an unresolved false universal claim.

The shorthand “leads are short on every measure” at [§7.2:73](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/70_s7.md:73) should become “the dated public benchmark measures cited here show gaps of months.” A seven-month rolling gap sustained over years is a persistent lead despite its modest magnitude; a preference score is not itself a temporal lead. The same paragraph already states that benchmarks do not measure sanctionability, internal capability, complementary assets or balancing time. That qualification is adequate to prevent treating EV5 as proof against breakout. It should remain next to the inference, and the same distinction should guide the C5 release criteria at [TESTS:17](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/reg/TESTS.md:17).

The current diagnosis is best described as **partly evidenced mechanism-level interpretation**. This is already the wording of [§7.4:97](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/70_s7.md:97). T1 and T2 mainly concern declarations and written policy; T3 does not establish an impaired entrusted purpose; T4 does not measure scaling or appropriability; T5 is unmeasured; the interlock is untested. The gaps at [EVIDENCE:294–308](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/reg/EVIDENCE.md:294) and the unestimated comparative bounds at [§8.1:29](/Users/ren/theories/drafts/2026-10-02-iaseai-selecting-the-selectors/v7/src/core/80_s8.md:29) are substantive, candid qualifications, not reasons to manufacture stronger empirical claims from the models.
