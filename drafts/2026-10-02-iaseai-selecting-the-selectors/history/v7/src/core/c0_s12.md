## 12. The judgment

> **Claim.** Under the protection standard and the present evidence, a change in what the system selects for is warranted in three tiers, sorted by the kind of justification each step has, not by who would accept it [val]. Steps that wait on no crux are taken now, beside steps that stand until a named crux is measured favourable [rec]. Stopping the decisive class (E2), common custody and development at the pace of understanding (E3) are prepared now and activated by a mechanism committed in advance, on registered indicators estimated by correctors certified independent [rec]. Whole designs, the coercive restriction of frontier inputs among them, are compared under one range of uncertainty, each with its own failure modes; no path has yet been shown to meet the tolerance, and without comparative risk bounds their ranking remains a judgment under the standard [val].
>
> **Why.** The first tier keeps options open and measures the cruxes [val]. Custody has to exist before anyone could hold decisive capability alone, and the trigger fires where restraint is least enforceable ({{enforce}}(4)), so an activation decided then would face the condition it is meant to prevent, and one committed in advance escapes it only where its means are in force before the trigger [thm∣prem]. At the third step what can be compared is which failures can be reversed, into what and by whom [val]. One party coming to hold decisive capability is irreversible on every path: the race selects for it, a design built to Section 10 contests it, and that design's other failures, its coercive fallback's escalation apart, have named reversals, none yet shown to work [thm∣prem; val].

### 12.1 The judgment, in tiers

The tiers are kinds of justification, not stages: the first two begin together, and what has the longest time constant starts first (Section 11.4). The author's own order, conditional on whether cooperation can be had, is given in Section 11.3: broad E1 is preferred, and stopping or slowing is warranted where it has not been achieved; tier 2's trigger is where that condition is read mechanically for the decisive class.

What the judgment gives is first a constraint. It says which strategies and which kinds of cooperation cannot reach the goal: those that leave unchanged what winning confers, those that bind conduct without moving thresholds (Section 8.2), those that integrate a part and not the whole ({{club}}), and those that leave the decisive class to one party. And it holds the problem to one standpoint, that of humankind as one community of common destiny (人类命运共同体) [corpus: shengsheng], the one host with no outside of Section 3.6, from which no participant can step. Held together, these narrow the search space greatly. Within what remains the paper also gives solutions, by mechanism: the requirements of Section 9, the generative order of Section 10, the tracks of Section 11 and the tiers below.

**Tier 1(a). Warranted now, on every reading of the empirical cruxes [rec].** A step belongs here only if its justification does not depend on how any empirical crux comes out: measurement that decides the cruxes, preparation whose lead time may exceed the warning, an equilibrium without trust, or research that is a public good. The robustness holds within the values the paper adopts (V1–V3) and the stated assumptions on cost and lead time: it gives each step a reason on every reading, not a proof of its net value at any scale, so each is scaled to the evidence, and preparation stays apart from commitment to a particular activation rule, which is tier 2's. TESTS sets each item against every crux.
1. **Shared measurement** of hazards, coverage and residual risk per class (Section 5.3), kept apart from measurement of who leads, with masked classes measured adversarially; audited disclosure, from every party, of conversion into correctors (funding, appointment, access, the authorship of standards), sanctioned above the gain from omission ({{blind}}(3)); certified row sums for every corrector domain ({{certificate}}(2)); the correlation of evaluators' misses, and control evaluations in which the red team knows the protocol ({{topology}}); and, wherever models evaluate models, the share of evaluation done by the evaluated lineage ({{alarm}}). Every trigger of tier 2 rests on it, and it is worth most where readings disagree.
2. **Verification research and pilots**, of compute, training and custody, and of detection faster than breakout: an instrument must exist before the deviation it is to detect, since detection in time is the enforcement condition seen from verification ({{detectlink}}) [thm].
3. **Alignment and control research, commonly funded and openly published.** What lowers the alignment tax is a public good that competitors undersupply ({{onegroup}}) [thm∣prem]; it pays whatever the tax turns out to be, and where it brings a class's tax below zero, the safeguard spreads without coordination, while at zero it is neutral under selection (Section 6.4).
4. **Preparation of common custody**: its design, institutions, indicators and activation rule registered now, and stress-tested against D1–D8 of {{durability}}; the commitment to activate is tier 2's. If decisive advantage never arrives, this costs only the preparation; if it arrives, custody begun at a warning comes too late wherever building it takes longer than the warning gives (Section 7.2) [thm∣prem]. X10 develops one candidate: a custody body of rotating members from plural lineages and jurisdictions, certified by its row sums, that releases what it holds only by a quorum including non-aligned members.
5. **Narrow E1**, for the decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty, beginning with declared doctrine and inspected exercises and verified wherever verification is possible (Section 11.3). Among actors who price in a certain lock it is best whatever the others do, a strict equilibrium that needs no trust, and "near certainty" has an exact bound ({{delegatelock}}(1); {{nearlock}}) [thm]; taken verifiably by one actor alone, it is a candidate first move, a signal wherever durability makes it cost a relative actor at least what the restraint it induces is worth to that actor ({{signal}}) [thm].

**Tier 1(b). Warranted now; withdrawn where a named crux is measured favourable or the step is shown to fail [rec].** What items 6 and 7 guard against arrives before the evidence that would show it: an alarm averaged over captured evaluators fires late or not at all ({{masking}}); where capture precedes evidence, the host crosses its floor before any alarm ({{slowroute}}); and a masked divergence is detected only as often as it shows under evaluation ({{masked}}) [thm∣prem]. A protection that waits for that evidence waits too long, so under the protection standard the burden of proof lies on withdrawal: each item stands until its crux is measured favourable, and then loses only the part that crux bears [val]. That burden is a precautionary premise of the standard, not a comparison the standard has computed: hazard arriving before evidence is a reason not to wait for the catastrophe, not a bound on the step's own risk. So an item also yields where the step itself is measured to fail or to raise the protected risk, the escape Test M and C4 register. Item 8 stands on every reading, and its cruxes bear only the reliance on it to change conduct.
6. **Independent correction**: formula funding at arm's length, mandated access, rotation, and rule-making insulated from the success of the evaluated, growing at least at the rate the rate law requires, and faster by $w\delta_h$ where it rests on human capacity that delegation erodes ({{corrrate}}(2); {{humancorr}}(1)) [thm]. Withdrawn beyond present institutions where C2 is measured favourable (Test M); the measurement of item 1 continues even then, since a certificate valid today fails later where correction falls below that rate and capacity tracks its selected level [thm].
7. **Common admission constraints** before consequential deployment, internal deployment included, for every class of divergence whose costs fall on common terms: sanctioned at least at every type's gain from evasion, which keeps the divergence from spreading and, where every such inequality is strict, removes it under cumulative selection, and equal in incidence among those who comply, which keeps the constraint neutral (R8a, R8b of {{require}}), with audits for hidden objectives and loyalties (Section 6.3). Withdrawn for a class whose tax is measured negative, since its safeguard then pays privately and spreads (C9); at a measured zero tax the safeguard is only neutral, and variants already deployed without it stay, so the constraint is withdrawn there only on evidence that the safeguard persists without it. Masked classes are admitted by capability threshold, up to where control protocols that do not assume the system's cooperation remain safe, with tier 2 beyond ({{masked}}); this gives way to admission on evidence the system's behaviour controls less directly, up to the capability at which independent auditors are shown to bound masking (C12).
8. **Shared provision of useful services**, good enough, distributed and replaceable, **and the divisible gains of capability shared as they arise**, where they can begin. They realize joint value on every reading of the cruxes (V1, V5) [val]. Capability provided to contestants without being won, sense (a) of Section 2.2, lowers the won share on which the scissors turn ({{scissors}}), and gains shared as they arise, unlike a promise for after victory, do not rest on a winner's restraint ({{wingives}}) [thm]; services provided to persons act through the won share only where they enter a contestant's decisive stock, and otherwise through the drive (C8) [conj], and services from many providers act through caps on dependence (X8, under D5). A measurement can withdraw only the reliance on them to change conduct: if conduct is unchanged under substantial sharing (C6), or provision leaves the weight on winning where it was (C8), that reliance moves to custody and pace, and the sharing stays for what it realizes [rec]. They are not accepted across camps, and the judgment does not claim that they are.

**Tier 2. Prepared now; activated by mechanism on registered indicators [rec].** Here belong the steps whose justification turns on a crux that can be measured in time, and with them the substance of the change: stopping the decisive class (E2), with rollback where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage; common custody of the decisive class, in force; and development at the pace of understanding (E3), per hazard class. Stopping is not slowing, and each is activated on its own indicators (Section 11.3); the race's own premise is the reason to prepare them now (Section 7.2).
- *Trigger.* **A trigger fires when the estimated time to a decisive position, less the time custody needs to be installed and verified, falls below a margin registered in advance**, and custody and E2 activate; waiting until decisiveness is shown would be too late. Its indicators are the persistence of leads, read on every measure, the gap between open and closed models (C5), and detection latency against the time from breakout to decisiveness (C7). E3 activates for a hazard class whose coverage falls behind: residual risk not falling (C1), or catch rates of planted defects (C3a) or, for masked classes, an independently assessed safety case failing at the admitted capability (C12), with detection and enforceable sanctions compared jointly with the changing tax (C9) when deterrence is the proposed control.
- *Estimator.* The time to a decisive position and the timeliness of detection are what masking hides and strategic disclosure shades ({{alarm}}; {{detectlink}}), so they are estimated by correctors whose row sums are certified and whose present independence is assessed through current capture and inherited rule support (C2), with misses measured for correlation (C3b), from channels that observe actions directly (Section 4.4), with any bound on masking drawn from evidence the observed control less directly [rec].
- *Commitment.* **Activation is committed in advance and executed by mechanism, not decided afresh when the trigger fires; the commitment is taken now, conditional on custody passing stress tests registered in advance (D1–D8).** The trigger fires where restraint is least enforceable ({{enforce}}(4)), as the breakout arm of {{scissors}} raises $p_d$, so a collective decision taken there would inherit the enforcement failure it was meant to prevent [thm∣prem]. A commitment made earlier is itself a promise about conduct at the trigger, and the same condition binds it unless its means are in force before the trigger: controls on compute and weights already held by custody, such as dormant hardware limits or escrowed keys, so that the deviation it would stop is no longer available ({{restraint}}(a)), or a sanction that survives success already in place ({{restraint}}(b)) [thm∣prem]. Where neither is installed, activation against the leading party rests on the coercive fallback of tier 3. Installing such dormant means is part of what is prepared now, conditional on their passing D4 and D5, since controls held by custody are themselves the largest conversion target ({{influence}}). With its means in force, preparation shortens the delay of the response from building, deciding and detecting to deciding and detecting, and commitment leaves detecting and executing, with the further delay of an actuation that fails, since commitment removes deliberation, not execution: that is the delay {{tempo}} bounds when the mode to be held is a lead that compounds [thm∣prem].
- *Release.* Release reads indicators of its own, each held for the dwell time of the slow variable it rests on ({{readiness}}; Section 11.4), not those of activation run backwards, which would release too early: share levels recover before safety does, in the field model [thm] and by conjecture for institutions [conj], and capture that makes delegation look safe is harder to undo than to prevent ({{humancorr}}(2)) [num: QS].

**Tier 3. Compared, not settled [val].** The whole designs are the separately held configuration of Section 10.5, developed in X10; a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions; a verification agreement with shared provision; regulated competition that meets {{require}}; an agreement of the type of Section 8.3(g); the benign winner; and the coercive restriction of frontier inputs. They are compared with continuation and with one another on the durability tests of {{durability}}, in one range $\Theta$ that includes each one's own failure modes.

The coercive restriction enters the comparison only under three conditions of its own, two set by mechanisms of Part I and one by value: an equal burden, the restrictor's own frontier work included, since a stop borne unequally re-sorts those it binds and is evaded wherever evasion pays their type ({{blind}}(2), (3)) [thm]; execution by correctors certified independent of every party it restricts and of the restrictor, with full inbound row sums below one and present-state tests of independence, since otherwise the restrictor is the largest conversion target there is ({{influence}}) [thm]; and the consent of the chamber of those affected (V3; R7) [val]. Its escalation hazard is counted in $\Theta$ ({{preventown}}). Even under the three conditions the theory does not derive that its net effect is positive, since denial raises the weight on winning and the trust threshold, the hard case of Section 8.3(f).

Under the rule for $\Theta$ every path's worst case includes an irreversible loss, and no path has a bound on its probability over the protected horizon shown to meet the tolerance, but admitting such a loss does not show that its probability exceeds the tolerance. The standard's third step would prefer the lowest bound that keeps correction open (Section 8.1); it needs estimates on both sides, which the cruxes would supply and do not yet. The ranking is therefore a provisional judgment, stated as one [val]. The preference for changing selection rests on two reasons, read from the failure modes X8 lists path by path, each with its support.
- **(i) Reversibility over every failure mode.** A failure is reversible only if its reversal is named, stops short of O1 and O2, and is executed by someone other than the failing party. Read from its own documents, the design names proposed reversals: dissolution, into separately held functions; capture of one function, by recertification; interruption of an essential service, by funded standby; collusion among its checks, by rotation and plural lineage; overreach of a common constraint, by a sunset and appeal (X8, row by row); none has yet been shown to work, and whether they do is C4 [conj]. The escalation of its coercive fallback, which every coercive path carries, and the failure of (ii) have no such route. For the other failures, late or unsuccessful correction can also cause an irreversible loss: a masked hazard can escape before recertification, and standby can arrive after an essential-service failure has caused catastrophe. These branches enter the same comparison (X8). Continuation's failures are reversible only while no position is decisive and no floor has been crossed, and its worst have no reversal: coverage outgrown and a floor crossed ({{coverage}}), capture before evidence ({{slowroute}}), a decisive position reached ({{wingives}}), the capacity to overrule lost ({{lockin}}) [thm∣prem]; and for hazards that manifest late its premises cannot be measured before they bind ({{tempo}}). The preference gives weight to the design's specified independent routes of correction, whose availability and timely execution must be tested against the alternatives. Their existence does not establish lower loss probabilities: whether correction succeeds turns on cruxes on both sides (C1, C2 and C5 for continuation; C4 for the design), and the ranking's reliance on the design's bounded own risk is C4 (Section 8.1). One condition reads every reversal, on either side: it comes before the protected loss and is executed by an independent corrector that survives. Under it a restrictor's converted correctors are irreversible only where no independent corrector survives, as the design's captured custody is; the capacity to overrule, once lost, returns after a recovery interval ({{lockin}}), so its loss is irreversible where the contest or another hazard denies that interval or an irreversible decision has already been taken; and funded standby prevents loss in an interruption only by arriving before it, since restoration does not reverse what the interruption caused. Keeping options of correction open is staying in the correctable kernel ({{kernel}}): a state from which a named reversal, if it works against every error in $\Theta$, still avoids the protected losses lies inside it, and a failure without one has left it [def].
- **(ii) The irreversible failure every path shares.** One party coming to hold decisive capability has no reversal on any path, whether by a decisive win, a winner's restraint failing, a sprinter's freeze broken, a covert project escaping verification, a restrictor's correctors converted or, for the design, the custody of decisive capability captured (X8). The race produces it by construction and selects for it ({{raceselects}}) [thm∣prem]; the design contests it by separation, plural lineages and the tests D4 and D5, the guardian test of {{guardian}} applying to custody first. The same loss stands on both sides; what differs is whether a path selects for it or is built against it.

A third consideration orders only the futures in which a crux is measured favourable: among paths that measurement brings inside the tolerance, the standard's second step applies, greater useful benefit more fairly distributed, and there the design's case is the joint value it realizes and the race dissipates (Section 10.1). A reader who weighs these reasons differently can reach another ranking; Section 12.3 says which measurements would move it.

**The scope.** Broad E1, AI not an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, is the scope of what has been argued, and narrow E1 its first step (Section 6.6). Between them the game itself is changed, by lowering its convertibility, its decisiveness and the conversion of success into control, and the tiers begin that change.

### 12.2 Why, and where the leverage is

The judgment follows the chain of Section 1.2, each link with its own support, and acts where the chain says the system can be moved. "Root" has two senses. *Causally*, the root is a loop, not a belief: selection shapes minds and institutions, and the minds and institutions it selects reshape selection; neither comes first. *As a point of leverage*, the root is understanding, and it acts in two places, which answer the two errors of {{twoerrors}}. As the transmission through which actors come to count what relative selection makes invisible, the one term in which a benefit common to all can rise without a rule ({{onegroup}}) [thm], it corrects the held boundary. The working objective that compounding sets is no error but each actor's correct computation of its interest, which no argument corrects [thm∣prem]; understanding reaches it only as the way actors agree on which parameters to move, and hold them there for their dwell time.

**The theory of change.** Understanding corrects the held boundary and makes agreement possible; agreement on what winning confers, given executive force and held long enough not to be re-absorbed, changes the game; understanding without executive authority, however far it grows, may change nothing (Section 4.3). Its first step, understanding shared, is the one a paper can take, which is why this one is written as a prediction to be shared, carried by a derivation each actor can check: shared, a prediction of this kind can work against its own fulfilment (Section 11.2).

**The hierarchy and the thresholds.** Meadows ranks twelve places to intervene in a system, from parameters (#12) to the paradigm (#2) and the power to transcend paradigms (#1) (Meadows 1999), and the account here sits inside that ranking. The structural loop gain is a reinforcing loop (#7) and correction a balancing loop (#8); consequences returned and measurement shared are information flows (#6); one rule for all and the separation rule are rules (#5). Selecting the selectors is the reinforcing loop seizing the power over rules and structure (#5, #4), which is why this paper's levers sit on the coupling between them, the conversion of success into control ($\chi$ and the $b_{ij}$; {{influence}}). Changing what winning confers is a change of goal (#3), made by moving parameters, sharing, provision and convertibility, across their thresholds, which is Meadows's own exception, a parameter that is weak until it moves into a range that sets off a place higher on the list: below its threshold a reform is re-absorbed, and across it, it is not ({{reabsorb}}) [thm]. In the corpus's words, "If we don't solve dominant attractors, focusing on minor adjustments won't change the outcome" [corpus: meta-attractor]. The paradigm decides which parameters are moved and whether they are held long enough, and it can change only if the system is understood. Both accounts stand: the paradigm is the highest place, and it acts through thresholds. A lever can also be pushed the wrong way, the common case in Meadows's account; making winnings decay faster is one (Section 2.2).

**Delays and buffers.** The high places move slowest and meet the most resistance, and the path from understanding (#2) to agreement on rules (#5) runs through transmission, which relative selection works against. Two low places, which meet little resistance, buy that path time. Delays (#9): where the delay of correction cannot be shortened, slow the change it must follow until the delay is short against it, as Meadows advises; that is E3 ({{tempo}}). Buffers (#11): a lineage of the alternative that can recruit shortens the hold a change needs before release (Section 11.4) [num: DW], and standby capacity for essential services and the host's margin above its floor lengthen the time a shock can be absorbed before a floor is crossed ({{safeguards}}). Neither changes what winning confers; each buys the dwell time in which agreement can. What would move the judgment is stated next.

### 12.4 Conclusion

Where whatever can gain or lose share is selected by relative success, what is won converts into the capacity to win again, and capacity converts decisively into winning, the carriers are under the selection pressure of winning: as the stakes grow, the gain from defection rises and the sanctions, the correctors and the shared language that could restrain it weaken, each in its own model of Part I, and where the prize grows with what all reinvest, success itself raises the stakes ({{standprice}}); that these move together as the stakes outgrow what does not have to be won is the conjecture the diagnosis tests (C0). Where success can also buy the selectors, the selection itself reproduces the weakening, and that closure is the meta-attractor of winning: as the stakes grow it accelerates the contest, makes it harder and harder to regulate, and alienates language. Inside it each actor computes correctly and the game is wrong: wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return, looking ahead weighs standing above what all share, so the logic of winning is individually rational there, collectively self-defeating, and false as a doctrine about nature (Section 3.8). AI is its most powerful instrument and can become its next carrier, and aligning each system to its principal does not align the game among principals. There is one host and no outside: no rival whole whose competition could drive the next integration, and no second attempt after a failure that cannot be undone. Against such failures integration must be anticipated, which is why understanding that we are one, one interconnected system, is a long-term requirement if growth and change are favoured. Oneness is not sameness; it is the accurate recognition of the system each part belongs to, and the design of a selection that counts it.

The way out is not to abolish competition, growth or selection. It is to change what winning confers, so that the same reflexive structure becomes generative and persists through what all share, not through its winners. A change in that direction has the seven marks of Section 8.2: G1 consequences return to their source; G2 winning confers reward, not more advantage in the same game; G3 success cannot buy control over its own assessment, and correction stays plural, decorrelated and in common hands; G4 decisive capability is held in common or not built; G5 provision of the conditions of a decent life sits beneath the game; G6 measurement and language are shared; G7 pace is bound to understanding. Any arrangement with these marks, whatever it is called, is meant to change the basin in the generative direction, each mark in the model of the row it reverses; that the arrangement's joint basin is viable, reachable and durable is still to be shown (Sections 10.4 and 11.5). Change the basin, then add speed [corpus: meta-attractor].

The meaning is affirmative, and it comes before the commitment. We are not only our separate lives: we are the larger process of becoming, going on through us, and that is good; in the author's words, "we are not just our life. We are the whole process of the universe doing this and itself and this is great" [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-10-02]. Decay and renewal belong to its growth, which is also why an order whose forms can fail and be replaced can still be reformed in bounded time (Section 11.4). Humanity has a great future in it, and so can the minds we are now making, whose correctability is a condition of the transition, not a doctrine of mastery (Section 6.6). What has to be opposed is not growth, competition or any people, but one logic: a part that forgets it belongs to a body. AI is a planetary technology, for all and for the whole world, not for anyone's own interest in fighting to grow bigger (V1). What it is for is medicine, learning, science, competent and answerable authority, provision that, like trees, sustains the system it belongs to without becoming it, and human agency kept in the decisions that matter. What has to be agreed is not who comes first, but what every participant needs in order to go on, and how the means of correction stay in common hands.
