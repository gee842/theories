## 10. The generative alternative

> **Claim.** The reflexive structure that lets success buy control over future selection can instead make contribution fund the conditions of further contribution [def]. The way out of an optimization trap is a change in what winning confers: a better optimization of the same objective does not correct it [thm∣prem], and the abolition of selection, competition or growth is not required [val]. Sharing and provision not won in the contest can stabilize equal standing, and success that cannot buy control over its own assessment leaves the reflexive channels no gain; sharing the divisible prize among all leaves a positive selected intensity while a positional prize remains [thm], so the design keeps that position from forming or holds it in common [rec]. Where carriers differ only in load and one of low enough load is present, four safeguards in force before capture keep the host above its floor throughout iff liability exceeds the private advantage by a margin the host sets [thm]. Where coverage is complete and correction is financed by formula, the generative order persists through what all share, not through its winners [thm∣prem], and its form follows function, not an axis of centralization [def]. Whether it attracts is a conjecture [conj].
>
> **Why.** A term common to all is invisible to relative selection, so an order whose support no carrier can claim is not selected among carriers; it persists as a common constraint does, by coverage, by sanctions that leave no type a gain from evasion, and by correctors financed by formula [thm∣prem]. Each counter-mechanism requires its own form: one rule for all where coverage is the condition, many hands where independence is, common custody where a decisive position would otherwise form [def]. Every remedy for the selection of selectors is itself a selector, so no better guardian is an answer; a structure in which errors cannot grow without limit is [val].

### 10.1 The purpose

Taken out of the contest, capability stops being spent on standing. What the race dissipates, a share of the stakes that rises with decisiveness, with the shared loss and with the narrowness of the working objective ({{dissipate}}(1)), becomes available for need: medicine and public health, education, science, competent administration, infrastructure, and the long-horizon work that fragmented incentives under-supply because its value is common ({{commonfront}}). Cooperation is therefore not only a constraint on danger; its purpose is what AI can do for people when it is not the instrument by which one actor wins the common future. The joint value is large; the race consumes it, and a change in what is selected realizes it (V1).

**Authority as a productive capacity (V4).** Authority can coordinate investment, guarantee services, support learning and act on shared purposes; the ideal the theory draws on justifies it by its responsibility for improving people's lives and the historical trajectory. It enters the design as a purpose to be sustained, not as a premise that it is realized: how institutions select and form officeholders, reward service, hear unwelcome evidence and revise harmful practices are causal variables, and a stated mission is not proof of its implementation. A larger causal radius brings larger responsibility, not larger entitlement; the author's book states the principle as "where power reaches, responsibility follows" (力之所及，责之所在) [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14 第十八章]. With the norm of a shared future (Section 3.7), it is the institutional consequence of the relational self.

**Provision that does not become the system.** Out of the contest, AI can be what infrastructure is: provision of the conditions of a decent life, built to meet need rather than to gain standing. Trees are the image, in the author's words "the lungs of the ecosystem": they provide shelter and fruit, ask no return for it, and do not "become the whole ecosystem" [corpus: shengsheng]. Provision that asks no return keeps no gate, and provision that does not become the whole does not close. Part I gives such provision four properties: *distributed*, many providers below the size at which influence over correctors pays ({{influence}}); *good enough*, stopping growth once a need is met, which keeps its hazard modes within correction and confers no decisive advantage ({{enough}}); *replaceable*, so that human capacity stays above its lock-in floor ({{lockin}}); and *for everyone*, understanding and provision alike, not for a class of guardians (Section 10.4). Contribution earns revenue, contracts and recognition; success does not buy the right to appoint evaluators, set thresholds, exclude entrants or raise their cost of entry, or amend the governing rules (V5), which cuts the conversion of success into rule support (Section 4) and keeps the incentive to contribute. What is open is who builds and funds such provision, not who holds it: provision that does not become the whole is held by no one. Section 10.5 and Discussion X10 develop one candidate answer: provision financed by formula and built by many providers under a cap on any one's share.

### 10.2 What winning confers: sharing, convertibility and provision beneath the game

The prize has a divisible part $\Omega_e$ and a positional part $\Omega_s$ (Section 3.4), and the levers act differently on the two parts and on the channels of the loop ({{chansign}}).

The most direct change takes the frontier out of the contest by pooling effort. Where the value of capability is common to all and concave in total intensity, private incentives give too little intensity, not too much, and the joint optimum must still respect the host's floor ({{commonfront}}); where winning still confers dominance on whoever is outside the pool, what pooling changes is the set of equilibria.

**{{!Proposition:commonfrontn}} (A common frontier) [num].** In the contest below, a frontier that pools effort and confers no dominance among those who pool it makes cooperation an equilibrium beside the race while the value of winning is moderate, and the effort each cooperator must contribute to the pool rises steeply with that value. *Of $n$ actors, cooperators hold effort $e_c$ and racers choose theirs; whoever wins the decisive contest gains $\Omega$, and every loser bears $L_b$. Each actor pays $c$ per unit of its own effort and bears $\ell$ per unit of total intensity, as in Section 3.4. Under separate frontiers each effort enters the contest separately; under a common frontier the $n_c$ cooperators pool their effort into one entity of strength $(n_ce_c)^r$, whose common value $\Omega_c$ is split equally and confers no dominance among them. Trust in a rival is the probability, independent across rivals, that it cooperates. With $n=4$, $\Omega=\Omega_c=L_b=c=1$, a shared loss $\ell=0.3$, $e_c=0.05$ and $r=1.3$: under separate frontiers the only pure equilibrium is all racing; under a common frontier all cooperating and all racing are both strict equilibria, and cooperating is a best response iff trust in each rival exceeds about 0.61; and, with $\Omega_c$ and $L_b$ fixed, the effort each cooperator contributes to the pool, $e_c$, at which racing against cooperators stops paying more than cooperating rises from 0.0054 at $\Omega=0.5$ to 0.035, 0.14 and 0.49 at $\Omega=1$, 2 and 4.*

*Scope.* One parameterization, with racers' intensities at the symmetric fixed point of best responses, each verified as a global best response; cases without a symmetric pure equilibrium are excluded. S10 gives the variations, among them a deviator that best-responds to fixed intensities, which needs more trust. *Proof:* S10 [num: legacy v4.5, N22].

A common frontier does not end the contest. Allocation within a joint project is itself a contest, and the project, as the largest carrier, has the largest return to influence over its correctors ({{influence}}). Effort in the pool benefits every member alike while its cost falls on the contributor, so selection within the pool favours the member that supplies least (compare {{onegroup}}), and Discussion X10 therefore sets contributions by formula. When the value of winning is large, keeping ahead of those outside the pool is the race moved up a level.

**{{!Proposition:share}} (What sharing, pooling and convertibility change) [thm].** Sharing and provision not won in the contest can stabilize equal standing, and cutting the conversion of success into control removes the reflexive channels; sharing the divisible prize among all leaves positive selected intensity while a positional prize remains; pooling complete payoffs and sharing only among the others have different boundary cases. *In the contest of Section 3.4, with prize $\Omega=\Omega_e+\Omega_s$:*
1. *Pooling payoffs, so that each actor receives $1-\beta$ of its own payoff and $\beta$ times the average of all, makes an actor holding $\theta_0\in(-1,n-1)$ act as if it held a self-model that rises strictly with $\beta$ and reaches $n-1$ at $\beta=1$. At $\theta_0=-1$, and under relative selection, pooling scales payoff differences by $1-\beta$: for $\beta<1$ it preserves choices and orderings, while at $\beta=1$ every relative payoff difference is zero, so selection is neutral and every choice is optimal for the relative objective.*
2. *Sharing a fraction $\beta$ of the divisible prize, with the promise kept with probability $\varrho$, is the contest with the effective prize $\Omega_{\rm eff}=(1-\beta\varrho)\Omega_e+\Omega_s$ when the shared part is divided among all $n$, the winner included, and $\Omega_{\rm eff}=\big(1-\beta\varrho\,n/(n-1)\big)\Omega_e+\Omega_s$ when it goes to the $n-1$ others, the convention of {{twochan}}(4) and {{wingives}}. Where $\Omega_{\rm eff}>0$, equilibrium intensity under every common self-model, dissipation and the race hazard are proportional to it, and under relative selection in one field with $r\le1$ the uninvadable intensity is $r\Omega_{\rm eff}/(nc)$. At $\Omega_{\rm eff}=0$, positive costly intensity is selected against; the positive-interior formulas do not cover $\Omega_{\rm eff}<0$. It is positive for every $\beta\varrho<1$ when the share is divided among all, and for every $\beta\varrho<(n-1)/n$ when it goes to the others; with $\Omega_s>0$ it is positive for every $\beta$ under the first convention, since then $\Omega_{\rm eff}\ge\Omega_s$, and under the second iff $\Omega_s>(\varrho n/(n-1)-1)\Omega_e$, reducing to $\Omega_s>\Omega_e/(n-1)$ when the promise is kept with certainty.*
3. *Where capacity receives provision $k_0$ not won in the contest, a smaller convertible share stabilizes equal standing ({{compound}}(1)); without provision the convertible share sets only the level of capacity, with or without diffusion, so winnings made non-convertible stabilize standing only together with provision. Where success cannot be converted into control over correction ($\chi=0$), the reflexive channels of {{loopgain}} carry no gain, the return to influence over correctors is zero ({{influence}}), and the correction that evaluation requires falls to its value without second-order selection, $A/c_{\rm ev}$ ({{corrrate}}(1)).*

*Scope.* Parts (1) and (2) hold in the one-shot contest and its relative-selection version; part (3) collects results stated with their conditions in Sections 2.2 and 4. *Proof and numerics:* S10 [num: SH].

**Three levers, and where each acts.** Pooling widens the effective self of an actor that values its absolute payoff. Under relative selection, partial pooling slows selection without changing its direction; complete pooling makes selection neutral. It therefore does not by itself select a cooperative conduct. Sharing scales every channel of the loop by $1-\beta$ only if it is credible: contemporaneous, escrowed or under shared control, before decisiveness; promised for after a decisive win, it is worth breaking and raises the value of a decisive position instead ({{wingives}}). Changing what winning converts into acts channel by channel (Section 2.2): through capacity only together with provision, which, growing with the stakes, holds still the won share $\omega$ on which the scissors turn ({{scissors}}); through rule support by correction, where the cut $\chi\to0$ removes the reflexive closure outright.

**The positional part cannot be divided.** Toward it a rival's standing is one's own subordination, $\kappa_s\le0$ in {{eq:welfare}}, and relative concern is accurate ({{boundary}}(2)). Sharing does not reach it: however much of the divisible prize is shared, the uninvadable intensity stays at least $r\Omega_s/(nc)$ when the shared part is divided among all (and, when it goes to the others, while $\beta\varrho\le(n-1)/n$), and under the second convention it stays positive for every $\beta$ iff $\Omega_s>(\varrho n/(n-1)-1)\Omega_e$ (part (2)) [thm]. Lowering decisiveness does not remove it, since a lottery can still award a position beyond sanction. It can only be kept from forming, by not building capability of the decisive class and by verified limits on what any winner may do, or held in common custody, so that no party can use it alone ({{restraint}}(a)). That is the crux of design that Section 3.4 located: what a design must limit is what winning confers.

**What should diffuse.** Diffusion is the third inflow not tied to rank and lowers the gain in every channel (Section 2.2), so openness is a lever, within the lines drawn below. It lowers the gain without changing the game, and what diffuses is leverage as well as provision: open models are, in the author's words, "a huge leverage", "both good and bad"; open, they do not settle the problem "as long as there are still this intelligence compounding like dynamics", and with "verification … processes that you cannot fine tune those models or train those models to do other dangerous things" openness is "definitely the direction. But that direction does not mean execution" [corpus: meta-attractor, 2026-10-02, 00:26:09–00:27:19]. The line is drawn by function and by the enforcement and coverage thresholds of {{thresholds}}, not by who leads. Knowledge, useful services and provision diffuse. Capability of the decisive class does not, since every actor it reaches gains a deviation that can become decisive before it is answered and must satisfy {{eq:enforceeq}} on its own ({{enforce}}); nor do the weights of systems in classes that admission constrains, which no admission reaches once released; nor hazardous variety beyond contestable coverage, which raises the uncovered share that survival needs to stay summable ({{coverage}}). What does not diffuse is held in common or not built [def].

**{{!Remark:twoorders}} (Concentration at two orders).** At the first order more contenders make it likelier that one of them starts the race ({{join}}); at the second, cultivated influence over correctors rises with a high moment of shares ({{corrrate}}(4)), which calls for removing common control. The design that serves both merges the frontier and disperses and insulates correction, keeping several lineages inside the merged frontier against common-mode failure ({{topology}}). Neither "centralize" nor "decentralize" is the answer, because the two orders call for opposite forms.

### 10.3 Safeguards, verification and participation

Four safeguards make consequences return on the host of Section 3. **(I1)** A liability above the private advantage of load, enacted in advance for anticipated harms and on the alarm for others, so that effective liability exceeds the advantage once rule-making is uncaptured; for harms beyond the assets of the liable this is regulation in advance rather than liability (Shavell 1984), and near a threshold a quantity does better than a price (Weitzman 1974). **(I2)** Independent correctors in evaluation and rule-making, with $d(t)$ above $d^\ast(t)$ by a margin. **(I3)** Independent evidence: masking kept below its threshold, or a conservative alarm on a bound on masking established independently of the field ({{alarm}}). **(I4)** All three in force before the corrector domains are captured.

**{{!Theorem:safeguards}} (Sufficient safeguards, their margin, and a necessary condition) [thm].** The four safeguards return consequences to their source, and keep the host above its floor throughout iff liability exceeds the private advantage by a margin that the host sets. *Let selection be replicator dynamics among carriers that differ only in load $e\in[e_{\min},e_{\max}]$, with a carrier of load $e_{\min}$ present, on the host {{eq:host}} with floor $V_{\min}$, and let $h_Ve_{\min}<\rho(1-V_{\min})$.*
1. *Sufficiency, with a margin. Under (I1)–(I4), effective liability exceeds the private advantage after a finite time, for unanticipated harms after an alarm that fires above the floor, and the load falls to $e_{\min}$; on the host {{eq:host}} the long-run level $1-h_Ve_{\min}/\rho$ exceeds the floor. From a state above the floor at which a constant margin $\Delta=\tau_{\rm eff}-\nu$ takes force, the trajectory stays above the floor throughout iff $\Delta\ge\Delta_{\min}$; a margin that varies and stays at least $\Delta_{\min}$ suffices, and one that starts below it and rises can also suffice, since the whole path of the margin enters ({{eq:host}}), a threshold set by the host, the floor, that state's composition and the speed of selection. Below it, the host of {{eq:host}} dips under its floor and recovers. A host that regenerates less at every level and degrades by itself below a threshold $V_u$ does not recover, so there the margin must also keep the transient above $V_u$, and the floor must be set no lower than $V_u$ for crossing it to be the first loss.*
2. *Non-redundancy. For each of (I1)–(I3) there are parameters satisfying the other two under which the host crosses its floor, and parameters under which all three come into force only after capture and the host crosses it.*
3. *A necessary condition. Under (reach) and cumulative selection ({{relsel}}), with a race carrier present, a trajectory viable at every horizon keeps the cumulative advantage $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\,ds$ from tending to $+\infty$, and so has long-run average effective liability, the lim sup of its time average, at least the private advantage.*

*Scope.* The margin belongs to the transient, not to the long run, and on a host that degrades by itself a shortfall is permanent. Part (3) is necessary, not sufficient: a time average that tends to the advantage while the cumulative advantage grows without bound lets the host cross its floor. S10 computes the margin on both hosts and the counterexample [num: HO]. The theorem computes, for one fixed strategy, an instance of the boundary of viable parameters, not the correctable kernel of {{kernel}}, which ranges over strategies and disturbances. Enactment is not force: liability enacted while rule-making is captured acts as $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$ ({{nounilateral}}), which is what (I4) is for. *Proof:* S10.

Verification by rivals is correction too, and Section 4 applies to it. A rival's verifier loses capture eventually under the initial-state and uniform-margin conditions of {{rivalverify}}, with its full inbound row counting conversion from the verified party, from its own side and from shared sources such as models, supply chains and professional paradigms. Current independence also needs evidence on inherited rule support and captured share over the transient; rivalry keeps the verified party's contribution near zero and bounds nothing else in the row. Rivalry checks compliance, not judgment, and an accusation between rivals can trigger prevention, so joint resolution precedes any response (S10).

Participation has its own conditions ({{participate}}). Under complete information an arrangement is efficient for a pair of types iff its survival-weighted joint value exceeds the sum of their survival-weighted race payoffs, and an overwhelmingly strong actor takes part only for a share near its race payoff. Under private, independent types, with participation decided before they are revealed and without verification, each type's interim share must be independent of its report, and the arrangement is feasible iff the joint value covers the sum of the types' best race payoffs; it can therefore fail where strong types are rare although cooperation is efficient for every pair [num: legacy v4.5, K7], the mutual optimism that makes bargaining fail under private information (Fearon 1995). Verification that reveals types removes the information rents, and the constraint relaxes as survival in the race falls below survival under the arrangement. Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against unilateral domination are kept apart: the first is made individually rational by shares of value and access to services, the second need not follow capability, and the third is fixed first and not traded for participation. Where participation and the independence of correction cannot both be had, the design is infeasible for those actors and says so, rather than buy participation with control over correction.

### 10.4 The generative loop and its closure

The reflexive structure of Section 2 has no sign of its own: what success converts into decides whether the loop consumes the shared conditions or renews them. The author's book states the two signs as two loops of the same structure [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14 第十四章]. In the *generative loop*, security makes trust possible, trust cooperation, cooperation abundance, abundance sufficiency and slack, and these allow wider care and longer foresight, which make better order and more security (安全→信任→合作→富饶→满足与余裕→更广之仁、更远之智→更好之序与安全). In the *degenerative loop*, fear produces defence, defence struggle, struggle seizure and concentration, and these scarcity and resentment; trust collapses, and fear deepens (恐惧→防御→斗争→掠夺与集中→稀缺与不满→信任之崩→更深之恐惧). The first turns more agents into generators; the second turns more of them into defenders, instruments or exiles. In institutions the generative loop runs from shared capability to distributed agency, to plural observation, criticism, maintenance and repair, to the renewal of the commons, and so to a greater capacity for cooperation and further shared capability. It is carried by funding, education, stewardship and maintenance, shared infrastructure, independent evaluation, conflict resolution, succession, protection against enclosure, real exit and appeal, and the material security that makes restraint survivable [corpus: meta-attractor]. Goodwill is not a carrier.

The criterion is generative or degenerative, not cooperative or competitive. Cooperation on the wrong thing can be worse than competition on it: a cartel is cooperation, and inclusion that stops at a club over-produces the harm it does not bear ({{club}}). Bounded competition in products, science and service continues inside a generative order; what is removed is competition for control over the shared conditions and over the correctors.

Each loop of Section 7.1, and the drive behind them, has a dual: a strand of the generative loop with a carrier, a condition and a test. The five strands are the marks G6, G2, G5, G3 and G7 of Section 8.2 stated as loops; G1 and G4 take the forms of the safeguards of Section 10.3 and of custody in Section 10.5.

For persons, provision beneath the game removes the ground of the belief that one must defeat others first. In the corpus's words, the common foundation does not prescribe how anyone lives; it lets everyone be entitled to live without first defeating others (共同基础不是规定所有人如何生活，而是让所有人不必通过战胜别人，才有资格活着) [corpus: shengsheng, 至善 §三]. For states, service obligations and common custody act on survival itself; for providers, $k_0$ is literally capability not won: a common layer of compute, evaluation infrastructure and services. Exit is free from providers and services, where it is selection with the opposite sign, and not from the common constraints, whose protection depends on there being no outside to them ({{blind}}(2)). How each strand keeps obtaining resources, meets free riding and the pressure to expand, stays correctable and avoids becoming the whole is answered function by function in Discussion X10.

**How the generative order persists.** In the meta-attractor of winning the loop runs through the winners: success buys support for the rule that selected it. In the generative order it cannot, because the second strand cuts that conversion, and support that no carrier can claim is a term common to all, which relative selection cancels ({{blind}}); the order is therefore not selected *among* carriers ({{noinside}}). Carriers inside it are still selected, for usefulness; the rule is not, and it persists as a common constraint does: by coverage, so that no one can step outside the common floor; by financing fixed in formula, so that its correctors depend on no one's success; and by a loop that runs through the whole, not through its winners, in which provision, security and slack make the rule worth keeping to everyone at once, while correctors funded and appointed so that no one keys them keep it correctable. A design in which those rewarded for contribution come to hold the positions that maintain the rule takes the path of the imperial examination, created to dilute hereditary capture and turned over centuries into a selection for the examination's form: an anti-attractor can become an attractor. The corpus counts this the most serious objection to its own prescriptions, every one of which is an institutional damper [corpus: meta-attractor].

**Is the generative order an attractor?** Because it is not selected among carriers, whether it attracts is a question about the whole-level loop and about coverage, and it is a conjecture [conj]; its persistence has to be designed and measured. The same mathematics gives one caution: an order whose loop gain greatly exceeds its damping also resists the correction of its own errors, and a balancing loop that answers late overshoots ({{delayosc}}). Plurality of purposes, correctors and lineages is therefore a requirement, not a decoration (Section 3.7), and it does a second job: an order whose forms can fail and be replaced can be reformed in bounded time, where one that has frozen cannot (Section 11.4).

**The reform dilemma.** Reform needs influence, and the game is what produces influence. The path by which a reformer gains the capacity to change the rules runs through the selection the reform is meant to change, so wherever that selection has closed, it removes the reform before the capacity to make it arrives: those who reach the positions from which rules are changed are those the rules favoured, and they arrive formed by them. This is the guardian problem in motion, and neither of the theory's two answers is a better guardian. Reform can act where the selection has not yet closed: at a keystone while its window is open (Section 4.5), or in a crisis with a design prepared, and its activation committed, in advance (Sections 11.4 and 12.1). And the capacity to reform can be built outside the contest for influence, by correction funded by formula and keyed to no one, so that it does not have to be won.

**{{!Remark:guardian}} (The guardian test).** Every remedy for the selection of selectors is itself a selector. Philosopher-kings, priestly classes, enlightened stewards and new universities have each been proposed as the guardians who would hold the line, and each is a corrector to which Section 4 applies; the author's question to every such proposal is how to resolve "the corruption of the constraining mechanism itself" [corpus: meta-attractor]. No better guardian is an answer. The author's book gives the mechanism and the alternative: regulation can itself become a reinforcing loop, power producing resources and resources proving the power necessary, so it cannot be entrusted to one unconstrained final controller but must divide its powers, publish its reasons, be reviewed on a schedule, receive independent feedback, and allow appeal and exit. The regulators must themselves be regulated (调节者亦须被调节), and real order is not finding people who are always right but building a structure in which error cannot grow without limit (真正之秩序，非得永远正确之人，乃立错误不能无限扩大之构) [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-07-14 第十四章]. Every arrangement and every institution within it, this section's provision, commons of understanding and correctors included, is therefore asked four questions before adoption, since each can become a position that winning reaches. *Capture:* are its correctors' full inbound row sums below one, counting every party and shared source ({{certificate}})? *Withdrawal:* does every closure it makes state what would end it? *Totalization:* can it make itself the whole, so that "protecting the whole" becomes a purpose no one can challenge? *Distribution of understanding:* does understanding go to everyone, or to a class whose position depends on others' not understanding? The author's answer to the last is that "everyone has access to the understanding itself directly" [corpus: alignment-selection].

### 10.5 Design by function

The design question is usually posed along ideological axes, centralization or decentralization, the individual or the collective, markets or states, and the mechanisms support neither pole. Each counter-mechanism of Part I requires its own form: coverage requires one rule for all, independence many hands, and a position beyond sanction must be held in common or not formed. The forms are derived from function, as design principles for commons were derived from the institutions that lasted (Ostrom 1990), and with the same caution about scale: nested governance of a planetary commons is itself an open problem (Stern 2011). Federations are a precedent at both orders, minimum standards and fiscal equalization against the pressure of winning among members, equal representation and supermajorities against the selection of the selectors at the centre; the same federal form can select for winning or for contribution, and what decides is what it rewards (X10).

**A candidate configuration.** Discussion X10 develops one configuration far enough to expose its assumptions, as one of the candidates Section 12.1 compares under one range of uncertainty. It is a set of separately held functions, not one institution: common provision, standard-setting, evaluation, disclosure, rules and the levy formula, execution, custody, a chamber of those affected, and appeal, each held by a different body, separately financed and separately corrected, so that correction lies outside what it corrects. On a planet "outside" means outside the function: a different source of funds, a different lineage, and certificates anyone can recompute from public data. Each function is still held by one body, a separation of powers at one scale and not a polycentric complex of overlapping jurisdictions, which is a different candidate; nesting the functions at several levels above a common floor is an option, not a requirement, since {{require}} constrains functions, not the number of centres. Supermajorities and the chamber's consent govern the slow loops, amendment and the formula; the fast loop, restricting a specific capability, stays fast with review after the fact, since every veto point adds delay and the margin of {{tempo}} depends on it. Providers have voice without vote, since a vote would let success weigh in on its own assessment ({{influence}}), at a cost in local knowledge and compliance that X10 weighs. The configuration states its end, dissolution into its separately held functions, while protections against unilateral domination stay entrenched. By {{influence}} it is the largest conversion target the theory describes, so capture of its own correctors (D4) is its hardest case, not a design row. Where execution through a host jurisdiction fails against the strongest participant (D5), what remains is the coercive restriction of frontier inputs, which the design does not settle: it has one home, the compared-not-settled tier of Section 12.1, under three conditions of its own.

Section 11 takes from here the five strands, the forms and their stated ends, and asks whether such an order can be reached from where the trajectory stands: survivable at every stage, preferred by each participant at each step, and held long enough not to be re-absorbed, with trust an outcome of the transition rather than its precondition.
