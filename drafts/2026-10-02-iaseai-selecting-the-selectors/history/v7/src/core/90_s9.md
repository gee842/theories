## 9. What any adequate arrangement must do

> **Claim.** What every adequate arrangement must do can be stated before designs are compared, and any design, regulated competition included, checked against it: each requirement with its kind of support (necessary in a named model over a named horizon, sufficient, or normative) and with the conditions under which it does not bind [def]. Where (reach) holds with a race carrier present, and no single rule-maker reaches every carrier whose load can carry the host below its floor, several requirements cannot be met by any actor alone, and competition among actors does not supply them [thm].
>
> **Why.** A constraint that one carrier adopts alone is a cost borne by it alone, which relative selection removes while effective liability is below the private advantage; a rule-maker leaves the host to the carriers it does not reach; and rule-makers in a contest of standing, weighing it above a stated level, do not impose a liability alone [thm]. Every sanction, observation and count of coverage that the rows call for is a corrector's act [def], and an enacted liability binds only in the part of rule-making that success has not bought [thm].

Part I proved its results one model at a time, and {{thresholds}} reads them as thresholds, each with a side on which a protected outcome is lost. Read in reverse, such a result is a requirement: an arrangement that does not keep the field on the safe side loses the outcome, in that model and over that horizon. The kind of support decides how a row may be used: a necessary row is a test, which a design that lacks it fails in the row's model within its horizon; a sufficient row is a route, not a test; a normative row rests on a value of Section 1.3 and is declared, not derived. The means by which a design meets a row are not rows; they are the subject of Section 10. Nor are sharing, provision beneath the game and reward that does not convert into control, which the models give as alternatives, any one of which can carry the margin that keeps equal standing stable ({{compound}}) [thm]; they are the levers of Section 10.2, each meeting several rows at once (S9, under the crosswalk). Read together, the rows state with their models the third job of "we are one" (Section 3.7), a selection that counts the system. S9 derives each row, fixes its scope with counterexamples, and maps the rows onto the paper's other lists ({{crosswalk}}).

**The row the others rest on.** Every row but R7 rests on the work of correctors [def]. A liability binds at $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, what captured rule-making leaves of it (Section 4.1); the sanctions that bound breakouts and keep admission constraints are applied by correctors and are themselves subject to capture ({{nested}}(3)); detection and the verification that capacity is kept in practice are theirs; and assessment that success has captured does not count toward coverage. A design whose correctors success can buy meets R1 on paper only, since effective liability falls to zero as rule-making is captured [thm], and loses the other rows through the interfaces that {{thresholds}} marks † [thm∣prem]. R3, correction that success cannot buy, is the row on which the table stands: the closure of {{meta}}, seen from the side of what must be done.

**Where admission does not reach.** Admission reaches only what it admits (Section 6.4), and its limits bound R8a without making it unnecessary. Systems that automate a developer's own research are used first inside it, which is why R8a is stated for internal deployment too. For an openly released class the decision to release is the admission decision for every later use, after which only a liability on use where harm can be attributed remains, the route of R1. For masked classes R8a remains necessary under the alignment-tax premise. With a fixed positive tax, a sanction bounded by exclusion and detection tending to zero cannot enforce it at arbitrarily high capability ({{masked}}); if the tax also falls, the expected sanction must be compared with that tax (C9 and C12 together). Failure of this assurance does not establish the residual hazard floor required by R5. Under the protection standard, the recommendation is admission only up to the capability at which independent control can establish safety, with stopping the decisive class (E2) or holding development to the pace of control (E3) beyond it where broad E1 has not been achieved. S9 gives each limit its mechanism.

**Sufficient routes, and form.** Four routes are sufficient and not necessary: full inbound row sums below one ({{certificate}}(2)); the safeguards (I1)–(I4) of Section 10.3, with the liability margin that the host sets ({{>safeguards}}); not building the decisive class, holding it in common custody, and detecting and answering faster than breakout, which meet R4 ({{restraint}}(a)); and verification whose expected sanction is at least the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}). S9 gives the margins and what each route certifies and does not. The requirements constrain functions, not institutional form: one institution can meet them, and so can mutually recognized institutions of several jurisdictions, a federation or a polycentric complex [def]. Regulated competition meets the rows its rules carry, where those rules reach every capable actor and its correctors are separated from success [def]; what a single rule-maker cannot do is the next result.

**{{!Proposition:nounilateral}} (No unilateral solution) [thm].** Correction and coverage are not supplied by any one carrier, or by any one rule-maker in a contest of standing, and enacting a liability is not enough while rule-making is captured. *Let selection among carriers be cumulative ({{relsel}}), with other advantages equal and a race carrier present.*
1. *While effective liability is below the private advantage, $\tau_{\rm eff}<\nu$, a carrier's unilateral contribution to the safeguards, whether lower load, correction keyed to no one or the cost of verification, lowers its payoff relative to otherwise identical carriers and is selected against.*
2. *Let no single rule-maker reach every carrier whose combined load satisfies (reach). Then no rule-maker alone meets R1 or R2, whatever it imposes. Let the $n_s$ rule-makers be in a contest of standing in which imposing the liability on the carriers one reaches is restraint in the commons of {{wow}}, worth $\Upsilon_s>1$ to the field per unit of its private cost. If each weighs winning by $\lambda_s>(\Upsilon_s-n_s)/(n_s-1)$, no rule-maker imposes the liability alone in equilibrium; whether they impose it jointly is governed by {{assure}}.*
3. *Where captured rule-making leaves effective liability $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, load has a negative private payoff, and selection lowers it, only once $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})>\nu$: enacting $\tau>\nu$ is not enough while $x_{\rm rule}\ge1-\nu/\tau$.*

*Proof:* S9.

**Which rows need more than one party.** R2 does by definition, and so does the shared measurement of R6. R1 does by parts (1) and (2), and part (3) ties it to R3, a second-order public good that competitors do not supply ({{noinside}}). So do the means of R4, R5 under (shared failure), R8a under $(A_\tau)$, and R9 where the contest is decided by strength ({{coverage}}; {{nested}}(3); {{lockin}}); S9 gives the reasons row by row. Where the mechanisms are active, a minimum of cooperation is therefore necessary: binding commitments covering every capable actor, with a layer of correction that no competitor controls [thm∣prem]. This is a result conditional on its models, combined with evidence and a stated standard, not a proof from first principles. Whether the present branch is one on which the mechanisms are active is Part II's diagnosis, and how much cooperation is warranted now is the judgment of Section 12 [val].

**Coverage can unravel.** A common constraint protects only while it covers every capable actor, and coverage is itself an equilibrium that can tip. A participant that leaves becomes a carrier the constraint does not reach (R2), and against it the constraint borne by those who remain is a cost borne by some, which relative selection removes ({{blind}}(2)). The cost of standing outside falls as more stand outside wherever sanctions are funded by participants, shared provision is worth less with fewer inside, or rule-making is captured by those outside. Where it falls from above the cost of staying, when none stand outside, to below it, when all do, staying and leaving are both stable, separated by a tipping share, as compliance and evasion are in {{blind}}(3) ({{unravel}}) [thm∣prem]. One large exit can carry the outside share past it, and the exits that follow need no further shock (compare minimum participation in Barrett 2003); exempting the largest participant lowers coverage with no exit at all (EV18, read in X10) [obs]. An arrangement therefore states its participation threshold, in coverage weighted by share, not in numbers of parties, and type by type, with conditions of access that keep standing outside costlier than staying however many stand outside. A design that cannot state its threshold has not shown that it can last.
