# Part III. Responses: what would change the trajectory

Part III's standard of comparison is a judgment of value, its descriptions of arrangements are definitions, and its formal results hold under the premises each states; which whole design to prefer is left to Part IV.

## 8. Comparing paths, and the alternatives on their strongest terms

> **Claim.** Paths are compared by one standard: irreversible losses first, one range of uncertainty for every path, each path's own failure modes included; no path has yet been shown to meet the tolerance, and without comparative risk bounds the ranking of whole designs remains a judgment under that standard [val]. An arrangement is described by which thresholds of {{thresholds}} it moves, to which side and for whom, and whether its enforcement survives a decisive position [def]. Where an arrangement's hazard is no higher than continuation's, both after it dissolves into its fallback and while it lasts, its own capture and collusion included, it survives at least as well at every horizon, whatever decides its dissolution [thm]. That arrangements which constrain conduct while leaving the thresholds where they were erode as stakes and speed grow, and that those which move them for every capable actor persist, is a registered prediction [conj].
>
> **Why.** Every arrangement that changes the map from conduct to share, resources and standing changes selection, so labels decide nothing [def]. Met at their strongest, the alternatives that keep the contest leave thresholds where they were, each by a mechanism shown in its own model: a lead decays and is judged by its holder; a winner's restraint rests on its need for the governed; a sprint's freeze binds all but its holder, the largest conversion target; verification bounds defection and leaves the value of winning in place [thm∣prem].

### 8.1 The standard of comparison

**The protection standard [val].** Each candidate path is evaluated at common horizons, near (about five years), medium (about twenty) and long (fifty or more), over one justified range $\Theta$ of uncertain parameters applied to every path. First, bound the probability of O1 and O2 within each horizon over $\Theta$. Second, among paths whose bound stays below a stated tolerance, prefer the one with greater useful benefit, more fairly distributed, that keeps more options of correction open. Third, if no path meets the tolerance, prefer the one with the lowest bound that keeps options open. This is robust satisficing on irreversible losses, a normative choice; the criteria it declines rank paths otherwise, and each disagreement is a normative crux. Expected value can favour a small probability of a very large loss when the expected benefit is large. Under discounted expected utility the acceptable existential risk turns on how fast the value of a year of life rises with consumption (Jones 2024; S8 gives his figures) [obs: EV11]; with utility linear in consumption, so that it does not rise, a course is preferred whenever the hazard it adds is below the growth it adds, though it ends in catastrophe with certainty (S8) [thm]. O1 protects catastrophic, common-cause losses of life, which the standard does not discount; deaths from benefits forgone are a cost of the path, not O1, their weight is the normative crux N1, and the margin that only the decisive class could supply is crux C10.

**The range $\Theta$ [val].** A parameter value enters $\Theta$ if it is consistent with the dated evidence of the register and is produced by a stated mechanism whose premises are named, with a precedent or base rate where one exists; a value that needs a premise the register contradicts is excluded. The rule is the same for every path: a rising uncovered share enters on continuation, its premises being named mechanisms the register does not contradict (EV5–EV7), and every other path's failure modes enter on that path, the capture of a common custodian, dissolution, coercive enforcement, interrupted services and collusion among checks on the cooperative one (X8). Every entry cites its source.

**{{!Definition:kernel}} (The correctable kernel) [def].** *The correctable kernel is the set of states of the world from which some non-anticipative strategy of admissible correction avoids O1 and O2 against every sequence of errors in the justified range $\Theta$: a union over strategies of correction and an intersection over sequences of error. In viability theory it is a discriminating, or guaranteed-viability, kernel of a game against disturbances (Cardaliaguet 1996; Cardaliaguet, Quincampoix and Saint-Pierre 1999), not the viability kernel of undisturbed dynamics.*

Leaving the kernel is the loss of guaranteed correctability, a point of no guaranteed return earlier than the losses themselves. The corpus describes openness by four capacities, each a condition of the kernel [corpus: possibility-space]: *not yet exhausted* (未), no decisive end state reached; *still possible* (可), plurality of lineages, purposes and correctors kept; *still responsive* (应), humans able to suspend, modify or replace; *still able to turn back* (转), reversal possible. A closure made to protect openness, such as a defence, a quarantine or an emergency power, can state what would make it withdraw; one that cannot has left the kernel. The corpus names the two: 护闭, closing one place for a time to protect a larger opening, and 成闭, a closing that does not reopen [corpus: possibility-space]. The standard's preference for keeping options of correction open is a preference for staying inside the kernel. This paper computes no kernel; {{>safeguards}} computes a smaller thing, a boundary of viable parameters under one fixed strategy.

**{{!Lemma:fallback}} (When an arrangement cannot do worse) [thm].** Two design conditions guarantee that an arrangement does no worse than continuation at any horizon. *Let an arrangement $A$ and continuation $C$ be driven by the same exogenous world process, the irreversible event on each path occurring at date $t$ when a common uniform draw falls below that path's conditional hazard given the shared history. Suppose that on every history on which both paths have survived to $t$: (safe fallback) once $A$ has dissolved, its hazard is no higher than $C$'s; and (bounded own risk) while $A$ lasts, its hazard, its own hazards of capture, restriction and collusion included, is no higher than $C$'s. Then $A$'s survival probability is at least $C$'s at every horizon, whatever the rule by which $A$ dissolves, rules that depend on the state included.*

*Scope.* Where bounded own risk fails there is no pointwise dominance, and the standard compares worst cases; that is where regulated competition can do better. *Proof:* S8, which also shows that comparing after conditioning on the dissolution date fails when dissolution depends on the state [num: SM(4), SM(14), FD].

Both conditions are read from an arrangement's own documents; the first is the durability test D1 of {{>durability}}. An agreement whose fallback is a short sprint from preserved relative standing violates the first; a design whose own concentration, capture or collusion can exceed continuation's hazards violates the second, and whether the design of Section 10 does is crux C4.

Comparing by survival does not by itself make restraint an equilibrium. Write $\Phi^R$, $\Phi^T$, $\Phi^P$ and $\Phi^S$ for an actor's probability of surviving to a common horizon when all restrain, when it races against restrainers, when all race, and when it restrains against a racer, each counting the hazards of its branch. The signs of $\Phi^R-\Phi^T$ and $\Phi^S-\Phi^P$ make the game in survival terms dominance, a stag hunt, chicken or a prisoner's dilemma; mutual racing is a strict equilibrium at every horizon at which $\Phi^P>\Phi^S$; and where the arrangement's own survival also decays, signs that hold at near and medium horizons can reverse at long ones ({{survassure}}) [thm].

So paths are compared at common finite horizons, not at an infinite one at which both branches of a hazardous world can tend to zero. Under the rule for $\Theta$, continuation with strong correction and a design that changes selection both admit irreversible-loss branches. That fact alone does not bound their probabilities or show that either exceeds the tolerance. No path has yet been shown to meet it, and the bounds needed to apply the standard's third step are unestimated; Section 12.1 therefore states a provisional judgment over the failure modes of every path that X8 lists.

### 8.2 Profiles, not labels

**{{!Definition:profile}} (What an arrangement changes) [def].** *An arrangement is described by a profile: which mechanisms of Part I it neutralizes, weakens, leaves or strengthens, read as the thresholds of {{thresholds}} it moves and the side to which it moves them; its coverage (unilateral, plurilateral, every capable actor); its bindingness (Abbott et al. 2000); whether its enforcement survives a decisive position, and if it prevents one from arriving, by what verified mechanism; whether its beneficiaries can amend it; its fallback and own risk; and its durability. And selection: every arrangement that changes the map from conduct to share, resources and standing changes selection, so what is recorded is the direction, whether it moves the thresholds toward their safe side or away from it, and whether in every capable actor's game or only in rivals'.*

Export controls aimed at preserving a lead change selection, toward a lead: blocking diffusion to rivals raises the structural loop gain in the channels it reaches, the gain the lead is meant to exploit (Section 2.2). At illustrative values, with nothing shared, no provision and diffusion symmetric, blocking it moves the threshold at which leads compound from a decisiveness of about 3 to 6 to any decisiveness above one ({{gammarange}}); targeted controls block diffusion only in part, and to rivals only, which the symmetric computation approximates. X8 profiles the policies now in force.

**The generative direction.** A change of selection in the generative direction has seven marks:
- **G1** consequences return to their source;
- **G2** winning confers reward, not more advantage in the same game;
- **G3** success cannot buy control over its own assessment, and correction stays plural, decorrelated and in common hands;
- **G4** decisive capability is held in common or not built;
- **G5** provision of the conditions of a decent life sits beneath the game;
- **G6** measurement and language are shared;
- **G7** pace is bound to understanding.

Each reverses thresholds of {{thresholds}}, in the model of the row it reverses [thm, row by row]: G1 load, and the alignment tax where admission returns a divergence's common cost to its developer; G2 and G5 together compounding and standing, since non-convertible reward works only with provision not won in the contest, and G5 holds down the won share, the pivot of the scissors ({{scissors}}), where the provision it names enters contestants' decisive stocks, sense (a) of Section 2.2, acting otherwise through the drive (C8) [conj]; G3 capture; G4 enforcement, by keeping $p_d$ small for every deviation ({{restraint}}); G6 talk; G7 coverage and tempo, the capability bound for masked classes, and, applied to decisions, delegation, which narrow E1 keeps within the pace of human correction. G2 is the author's rule of game design (Section 2.2), and the seven are what Section 3.7 asks of a selection that counts the system. What this paper argues for is a change of selection with these marks, covering every capable actor, enforceable and durable, whatever it is called.

**{{!Definition:durability}} (Durability tests) [def].** *An arrangement is tested on its own documents and estimates, not on assumptions added by the tester: D1, its fallback if it dissolves; D2, its estimated hazard of breakdown and what drives it; D3, who keeps informed veto, modification, replacement and exit after any delegation; D4, whether its own correctors have full inbound row sums below one; D5, what happens when its strongest participant violates it; D6, succession and the continuity of essential services; D7, common causes and collusion among its checks and systems; D8, whether membership is open or stops at a club.*

The tests apply to every path, the cooperative design of Section 10 included, by one evidence standard: where an arrangement's documents give no entry, the entry says so (X8). An arrangement with the form of cooperation that fails D1–D3 on its own estimates may be called a *pseudo-solution*; the label summarizes test results and does not replace them. **Registered prediction [conj]:** arrangements that constrain conduct while leaving the thresholds of {{thresholds}} where they were erode as stakes and speed grow, because the mechanisms of Sections 3 to 5 keep acting on them; those that move the thresholds to their safe side for every capable actor persist, because what would erode them has been removed. The classification uses the profile, never the outcome; Section 7.4 states what would refute it.

### 8.3 The alternatives

Each alternative is met in its strongest form, on its merits rather than its prominence, and answered by mechanism: what remains, and which thresholds it leaves. X8 gives each in full, with how it would be installed and what would show that it suffices.

**(a) Technical alignment and AI-assisted correction.** *Strongest form:* control over early transformative systems, used to accelerate alignment and oversight while the window lasts (Greenblatt, Shlegeris, Sachan and Roger 2024); oversight with the right topology ({{topology}}); AI-assisted correction keeping pace with cheapening control ({{corrrate}}); research that drives the alignment tax below zero, class by class, so that the safe variant is also the winning one, or to zero, where it is no longer selected against (Section 6.4); and, in its proponents' own plans, a strong international agreement ranked first. *What remains:* principal alignment does not change the game among principals ({{faithful}}); the window lasts only as long as the race allows; systems are retained for performance ({{retain}}), and progress that lowers the hazard per unit of racing or delegation is partly absorbed by more of both ({{homeostasis}}); redundant oversight is capped by the common causes selection produces, and certified row sums are a sufficient certificate, not a proof of independent errors ({{topology}}); AI-assisted correction is only as independent as its funding, access, appointment and lineage; time is a part of the tax that no technique removes; for masked classes, deterrence fails where detection times the enforceable sanction falls below the tax, including at sufficiently low detection under a fixed positive tax; a falling tax must be compared jointly (C9, C12). Missing assurance supports a capability bound under the protection standard, without proving an unavoidable residual hazard ({{masked}}); and which divergences are tolerated is set at the outermost loop ({{nested}}). Held by correctors separated from success, under constraints common to all, it changes selection, as part of G3 and G7.

**(b) Regulated competition with strong correction.** *Strongest form:* liability and licensing make consequences return, evaluation access is mandated and thresholds are fixed in advance; in a two-developer race with public capability, sufficiently high effective hazard sustains a pacing Markov perfect equilibrium and excludes the everywhere-racing profile, without proving uniqueness or pacing from every initial state (Fudenberg and Koh 2026); civil nuclear power and aviation show durable safety under unchanged commercial motives [obs]. *What remains:* the race is set by the most race-inclined actor ({{join}}); the rule-makers are themselves in a contest of standing ({{>nounilateral}}); liability cannot exceed the assets of the liable (Shavell 1986), so a catastrophic or common harm cannot be turned wholly into a private cost; developers' frameworks condition commitments on rivals [obs: EV2]; the precedents had no decisive prize; and the mechanisms here can weaken the incentives and information on which pacing rests: relative accounting discounts shared harm ({{twochan}}), while quiet deviation, captured evaluation and masking obstruct its assessment ({{enforce}}, {{masking}}, {{masked}}); their mapping into the source model remains to be shown (X8). Regulation that returns consequences and separates evaluators from success, across every capable actor, is G1 and G3 for all, the change this paper argues for, whatever it is called; regulation that constrains conduct while success can still buy the rule-makers is predicted to erode as stakes and speed grow.

**(c) Leadership and a safety margin.** *Strongest form:* a leader spends part of its lead on safety; a large lead can discourage a follower (Harris and Vickers 1987; Konrad and Kovenock 2009); and a developer able to commit first to a slightly slower speed gains, because rivals slow too, a local result that needs no lead (Bueno de Mesquita, Dziuda and Polborn 2026). *What remains* ({{lead}}): a lead shrinks by diffusion even when the follower does not accelerate; spending it gives up the discouragement and returns the contest toward parity, where, in contests in which a lead discourages the follower, standing is priced highest (Section 7.2); for hazard classes whose evidence lag exceeds the margin, it buys nothing ({{tempo}}); whether it suffices is judged inside the contest, by the leader's own evaluators; and a leader whose window is closing has a motive to strike first. It acts on tempo for a time and leaves the objective of winning in place; its best case, a first commitment to slow that rivals answer by slowing, is a first step of the transition (Section 11), not a substitute for it.

**(d) The benign winner: win, bind, share.** *Strongest form:* a coalition seeks a clear advantage, including military superiority and the denial of key inputs to adversaries, then offers its benefits to others in exchange for their support (EV1). It persuades by promising both security and benefits; both rest on the winner's restraint after the fact. By this paper's own results the form is strongest where the winner still needs the governed: under logarithmic utility a winner accurate toward what can be divided gives them $\kappa_W/(1+\kappa_W)$ (Section 5.2). *What remains:* that need after victory is what the race cannot verify beforehand, and what AI can lower (C13); after a decisive win what the others receive is set by the winner's objective alone, and a promise to share is worth breaking, with no one to sanction it ({{wingives}}) [thm]; where conduct before and after victory go together, the race shifts winners toward those that extend dominance ({{raceselects}}) [thm∣prem]; and the winner is itself a field, whose restraint depends on whether its own correctors can be bought by its successes. A winner that binds what victory confers, shares benefits as they arise rather than promising them, and has its own correctors certified independent, with succession rules that survive changes of leadership (Test I), has changed what it selects for.

**(e) A finite sprint, then restraint.** *Strongest form:* sprint to a decisive lead, then freeze or roll back and govern at the tempo of understanding; a winner that freezes keeps positive survival ({{freeze}}), and after a decisive win no contest remains to price standing. *What remains:* the path passes through its greatest exposure, and the rivals' strongest motive for preventive attack ({{preventattack}}), before any restraint begins (C1, C5); and a decisive advantage not used to stop others' growth does not stay decisive, while using it so ends their O2 (V2, V3; {{sprint}}). The paper's own answer is by mechanism: the freeze is a stop one actor imposes on all the others, the most unequal incidence a constraint can have, so it re-sorts those it binds and is evaded wherever evasion pays their type ({{blind}}(2), (3)) [thm], and they read it as a lead being kept; and its holder is the largest conversion target there is, the return to influence over correctors growing with the square of share ({{influence}}) [thm]. E2 and common custody impose the same stop on the decisive class; what separates the sprint from them is incidence and holder. The sprinter's freeze answers to its own correctors and, when the sprinter breaks it, to no one (D4, D5); custody is designed for separated functions under correctors certified independent of every party, its certificates not yet measured (C4; X8 sets the two side by side).

**(f) Deterrence and prevention.** Prevention can be survival-preferred by the preventer.

**{{!Proposition:preventown}} (Prevention under the preventer's own criterion) [thm].** *Let an actor compare courses by its own survival at a common horizon, facing a rival it believes certain to race. (1) It strictly prefers a single strike, which escalates to catastrophe with probability $\varepsilon_P$ and otherwise succeeds with probability $s_P$ in returning the field to something like the arrangement, leaving the race as it was if it fails, to both staying out and joining iff $\varepsilon_P<1-\max(\Phi^S,\Phi^P)/\big(s_P\Phi^R+(1-s_P)\Phi^P\big)$; this bound tends to one as the racing branches $\Phi^S$ and $\Phi^P$ tend to zero, wherever $s_P\Phi^R>0$, and rises when they fall in proportion. (2) Sustained prevention against a target that rebuilds with probability at least $b_P$ a period, each strike escalating with probability at least $\varepsilon_{\min}$, has a per-period hazard of at least $b_P\varepsilon_{\min}$.*

*Scope.* Prevention that permanently ends the target's capacity, or converts into joint monitoring, is a different branch. *Proof:* S8, which also compares sustained prevention with racing at common horizons [num: legacy v5, FS].

The objections are three, and none changes the criterion: normatively, prevention ends or endangers others' O1 and O2 (V2, V3); epistemically, it can make a rival that was in fact survival-first act as the type it was feared to be (Jervis 1976; Kydd 1997); technically, striking at machine speed against unobservable progress brings the delegation of Section 6.3. Deterrence by threatened sabotage (Hendrycks, Schmidt and Wang 2025) acts on $p_d$ by threat, as custody does by holding the decisive class, with war or race as its fallback; its credibility can reverse under survival-first comparisons, its red lines are hard to state and to monitor (X8), and algorithmic progress erodes thresholds stated in compute [obs: EV9].

Insuring against falling behind is not an exit either: the penalty is set by the winner, not the laggard, and insurance accepts the frame of the contest, hedging against losing instead of declining the game ({{insure}}). That is the case the ruling of Section 3.8 closes: against a winner of that logic falling behind cannot be made survivable, so a remedy has to oppose the logic, not hedge inside it. Provision held by no one, beneath the game, lowers the won share for every actor (G5); held by one actor as insurance against losing, it is a position in the contest, which a winner with a relative objective has a motive to destroy.

**The hard case.** A survival-first actor that gives a low probability to a capable rival's holding an unbounded relative objective (Section 3.8) does best to propose verifiable limits and to take narrow E1 verifiably in its own strategic loops, a costly signal ({{>signal}}). With a high probability it compares denial, a coalition that stops the decisive class, and prevention, and coverage of every capable actor then comes from consent or from coercion. Coercive denial raises the weight on winning and the trust threshold and, if it escalates, falls under {{preventown}}. The theory does not derive that its net effect is positive and states the case as hard; Section 12.1 states, once, the conditions under which a coercive restriction of frontier inputs enters even the comparison of designs.

**(g) A verified agreement over a contest that remains.** Rivals agree to slow and to delay decisive capability under verification, with mutually assured destruction of compute if the agreement dissolves (X8). Let the payoffs of mutual restraint, of racing against a restrainer, of mutual racing and of restraining against a racer be $R$, $T$, $P$ and $S$; let verification detect a defection in time with probability $\upsilon$, in which case it is reversed, the defector receiving $P-F$ and the cooperator $P$; let trust $\varpi$ be the belief that the other cooperates; and write $g_C=R-\upsilon(P-F)-(1-\upsilon)T$ for what cooperating gains against a cooperator and $g_D=(1-\upsilon)(P-S)$ for what it loses against a defector.

**{{!Proposition:assure}} (Assurance) [thm].** *In this symmetric two-player game: (1) cooperation is a best response iff $\varpi\ge g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ when both are positive; no trust suffices if $g_C\le0<g_D$; cooperation is dominant if $g_D\le0<g_C$. (2) Where $T-P+F>0$ and the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating, $T>R$, cooperating against a cooperator pays iff $\upsilon>(T-R)/(T-P+F)$; where $T<R$, it pays without verification.*

*Scope.* The threshold of (2) is the deterrence condition of the inspection game (Avenhaus, von Stengel and Zamir 2002); read with its payoffs as continuation values, it is, in the one-period reduction, the enforcement condition seen from verification, $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d}$ ({{detectlink}}). *Proof:* S8, with the $n$-player bounds and the condition under which they fail.

**{{!Theorem:verify}} (Verification and the value of winning) [thm].** Verification is required exactly when, and to the degree that, the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating.
1. *For a fixed sanction, the timely detection that cooperation needs, $(T-R)/(T-P+F)$, tends to one as $T$ grows.*
2. *Detection counts only if it comes before a defection becomes decisive, so that $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$; acceleration, which shortens $T_{\rm dec}$, lowers the timely detection that any given instrument achieves.*
3. *Verification can also lower $T$ by bounding the largest defection that would go undetected, for instance by accounting for compute, which does not need near-certain detection. The bound holds while the verified coalition's advantage in effective compute stays above the ratio at which an undetected project would become decisive [def]; since effective compute is algorithmic efficiency times compute, the coalition's compute advantage must then grow at least as fast as diffusion erodes its algorithmic one. That it does, the premise (advantage keeps pace), is not derived here [conj], and the rate of erosion is observed (EV9).*

*Scope.* Verification does not carry unverifiable information ({{talk}}), its own reliability (Section 4), or future commitments, unless the sanction keeps pace with the net gain from exit, which grows with the capability held in check when leaving with a decisive advantage gains faster than staying with one's share; an agreement that preserves relative positions preserves the relative term of every payoff, and with it the motive to exit ({{overhang}}) [thm]. *Proof:* S8.

One detailed plan of this kind among leading powers, designed to be enforceable without trust (EV10; X8), stands for a position, keep the contest, slow it and stay safe, and the answer above is to the position, not to the plan's figures. It slows and caps, acting on tempo and decisiveness, and keeps relative standing in its enforcement and fallback, and with it the motive to exit. That makes it a bridge, and it can be the end only if it lowers the value of winning, as G2 and G5 do, rather than only bounding defection, as (3) of {{verify}} does.

**(h) Normal technology and resilience.** *Strongest form:* AI's impacts arrive as it diffuses, at the pace of adoption, and policy should seek resilience and reject nonproliferation, which lowers it (Narayanan and Kapoor 2025); great-power advantage from a general-purpose technology comes from diffusing it, not from a lead at the frontier (Ding 2024). *What remains:* the growth channel binds at the pace of deployment, and whether coverage keeps up is measured class by class (C1); whether any advantage is decisive is C5, and where both are measured favourable the activation of tier 2 lapses (Section 12.3); the concentration that custody risks is answered by the two orders of {{>twoorders}} and the guardian test ({{>guardian}}); and the reading's own prescriptions, resilience, diffusion and shared measurement, are G1, G3 and G6 and the diffusion lever of Section 2.2. X8 gives it in full.

The comparison turns on two quantities. If continuation keeps the uncovered hazardous share summable, it can beat a fragile cooperative arrangement; if the uncovered share rises on the continuation path, any cooperative arrangement with a modest hazard of dissolution does better at every horizon past the near term [num: legacy v5, CMP; S8]. The first is crux C1; the second is what Sections 10 and 11 design for and test. X8 gives each path's main residual risk and, path by path, each failure mode in $\Theta$: whether and into what it can be reversed, whether that is derived or only designed, and what would show it under way, the base on which Section 12.1 compares. The analysis does not establish that every cooperative arrangement outperforms regulated competition, and Section 12 does not claim it.

Section 9 states what any adequate arrangement must do to move the thresholds for every capable actor, whatever it is called, taking from here the profile and the marks G1–G7; Section 12 takes the durability tests, the failure modes of X8 and the third step of the standard.
