## S13. Interfaces between the models

The theory is built from models that are each solved on their own: a stock model of compounding (Section 2.2), a load model on one host (Section 3), a field model of correctors (Section 4), a repeated game with breakouts (Section 5.2), survival under coverage (Section 5.3), and the delegation and training games of Section 6. Where a sign or a threshold passes from one model to another, it passes through an *interface*: an assumption about how a quantity of one model moves a quantity of another. Each is listed here with its status. **Proved**: a result of this paper derives the join, under the conditions stated. **Numerical**: only computed models support it, with the block of S15 that computes them. **Open**: it is assumed, and the arrows that cross it are hypotheses. A cell of {{thresholds}} marked † and an entry of the S7 matrix that names an interface cross one of the open joins. No interface is used to bound another.

*Reading.* The proved and closed joins are what let the parameter matrix of S7 be read across models at all; the open ones are where the paper's integration is a hypothesis and not a theorem, and they are why {{thresholds}} is read row by row, each row in its own model, and why the claim that the thresholds move together is C0 [conj]. Two models would close the most. One that derives the stage games of {{eq:enforceeq}} from the stock dynamics would join compounding to enforcement without the stakes arm being assumed (*stocks and stage payoffs*). One that selects capacity in several coupled domains while the contest moves the shares would replace the last quasi-static step of Section 4 (*capacity and the field*). The slowest join, the formation of minds, no model here contains.
