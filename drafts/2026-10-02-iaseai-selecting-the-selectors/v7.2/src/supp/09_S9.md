## S9. Requirements

### Table {{#require}}, row by row

Each row is its cited result read as a condition on arrangements, in that result's model and over its horizon. The derivations, and the examples that fix each row's scope, follow; numbers come from the blocks of S15 named in brackets. The last column of the table holds two kinds of entry, kept apart here: conditions under which a row is not needed, and conditions under which it is met without design, which the table marks as met, not excused.

- **R1, consequences return.** *The two forms, under constant-speed replicator selection.* With other advantages equal and a race carrier present, the exact identity $\ln[p_r(t)/p_k(t)]=\ln[p_r(0)/p_k(0)]+s(\bar e-e_k)B(t)$ holds at either sign of the payoff gap. Hence a cumulative advantage $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\,ds$ that tends to infinity sends the total race share to one and the load to $\bar e$, so $\limsup_tV\le(1-h_V\bar e/\rho)^+$, which (reach) puts below the floor. Viability at every horizon therefore needs $B(t)\not\to\infty$. That implies the form of {{safeguards}}(3), whose proof in S10 takes the same step: if $B(t_k)\le M$ along some $t_k\to\infty$, then $\frac1{t_k}\int_0^{t_k}\tau_{\rm eff}\,ds\ge\nu-M/t_k$, so $\limsup_t\frac1t\int_0^t\tau_{\rm eff}\,ds\ge\nu$. *The average form is necessary and not sufficient:* with $\tau_{\rm eff}=\nu-1/(2\sqrt{t+1})$ the time average of liability tends to $\nu$ while $B(t)=\sqrt{t+1}-1\to\infty$, and on the logistic host with $\rho=0.05$, $h_V=0.08$ and floor 0.3, from a race share of 0.001, the host crosses its floor at $t=89$; with $\tau_{\rm eff}=\nu$ it never does [num: HO]. *Not at a finite horizon:* a trajectory with zero liability need not cross the floor before $H$. With the host starting at full capacity, floor 0.2, $\rho=0.01$, $h_V=0.02$ and zero liability, even full load from the start keeps $V(50)\ge0.435$, and the host crosses the floor only at $t=\ln3/\rho=109.9$, although (reach) holds [num: HO]. At a finite horizon the necessary condition is weaker: liability must keep the cumulative advantage below that of the path that just reaches the floor at $H$, by monotone comparison. *Not needed* either where other advantages correlated with restraint offset the selection of load (the scope of {{load}}).
- **R2, coverage of every actor whose share can grow.** Let a carrier of the race type stand outside the common constraints while the covered carriers bear effective liability $\tau_{\rm eff}$. Its payoff exceeds that of a covered carrier with load $e_l$ by $\nu\bar e-(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})e_l$, positive wherever the constraint costs the covered anything, so its cumulative advantage over every covered carrier grows without bound; where nothing bounds its share, the share tends to one and {{load}} applies, whatever the constraints on the others. Coverage is needed on a second ground: the race begins if any one capable actor prefers racing to restraint, and where those events are independent its probability tends to one as capable actors multiply ({{join}}), so coverage must reach the most race-inclined. *Not needed* where harms stay local; where the uncovered carriers' combined share is bounded at $\bar p_{\rm unc}$ with $h_V[\bar p_{\rm unc}\bar e+(1-\bar p_{\rm unc})\bar e_{\rm cov}]<\rho(1-V_{\min})$, $\bar e_{\rm cov}$ the load the covered carriers still carry (positive, for instance, under the liability margin of {{safeguards}}), so that the load of all cannot carry the host below its floor; or within a horizon shorter than the crossing time.
- **R3a, the rate law.** {{corrrate}}(2) gives both halves in the field model with a seeded evaluation domain, $[H_\gamma]$, a positive cultivation channel and no per-unit floor, and {{capdyn}} gives them off the selected level. *Sufficient at any speed:* with $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and $d_0>d^\ast(\gamma_0)$ the certificate holds at every date, for every adjustment of capacity that starts at or below its selected level and does not raise capacity while above it, since such capacity stays below its selected level and evaluation's row sum below its quasi-static value. *Necessary where capacity tracks:* below the rate law, with $\liminf_tq/q^\ast>0$, the cultivated term keeps a fixed fraction of a quasi-static value that grows without bound, and the certificate fails beyond a finite time. Carriers that optimize track; so does adjustment at a constant rate, which only delays failure [num: QS]; gradual selection tracks only where the variance of capacity rises faster than capacity itself. A requirement wants the sufficient half, the safe design rule; the necessary half says when a slower rate is a real failure and not a delayed one. *Human capacity:* where correction acts at $d\,(\mathcal M/\mathcal M_0)^w$ and delegation erodes $\mathcal M$ at $\delta_h$, both halves hold with the threshold raised by $w\delta_h$; where the erosion stops at a floor $\mathcal M_{\min}$, the rate returns to $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and the required level rises by the factor $(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})^w$ ({{humancorr}}). *Not needed:* with $\chi=0$, or the conversion channels removed, a unit of capacity returns nothing, and the requirement is $A/c_{\rm ev}$, its level without second-order selection ({{corrrate}}(1)); with a per-unit cost floor that AI does not lower, cultivation stops once $d\ge d_f$ and the requirement levels off at $\max\{A/c_{\rm ev},d_f\}$, a level, not a rate; with a floor that falls at $\alpha_f<\alpha_{\rm cor}$, cultivation ends at a finite date and the certificate holds from some date on, though not necessarily before it: with the rate law at 0.1, $f_0=0.1$, $\alpha_f=0.05$ and $\alpha_{\rm cor}=0.051$, it fails at $t=38.4$, while cultivation ends only at $t=588$ [num: FF]; with capacity selected gradually and its variance not rising faster than capacity, capacity stays bounded and correction growing at any positive rate certifies in the end, after a possible transient failure ({{capdyn}}) [num: QS]; with nothing captured; and within a horizon shorter than the failure time. A lump-sum cost of capacity does not belong on this list: it delays cultivation and leaves the requirement unbounded as control cheapens ({{corrrate}}(3)) [num: FF].
- **R3b, no core.** {{certificate}}(4): excluding every locally stable captured configuration needs an empty core of mutually sustaining capture, under the nonunit pruning-gain conditions of {{certificate}}(4). The exact boundary $x_i=1$ is invariant, so a claim of recovery from literally every initial state would be false. *Not needed* if only the states actually reached must be excluded: with a row sum of two, the low initial state $(0.1,0.1)$ stays in the invariant rectangle $[0,0.1]\times[0,0.2]$ and converges to no capture, with $x=3.2\times10^{-10}$ at $t=20$ [num: FA]. A full row sum below one is the stronger certificate, sufficient for eventual recovery from $x_i(0)<1$ and finite initial support ({{certificate}}(2)), without by itself bounding transient capture, and not necessary.
- **R4, breakouts within the bound.** {{enforce}}(2): a deviation with $D_i(a)>v_i$ is deterred iff $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)$, so restraint enforced by repetition needs the inequality for every actor and every such deviation; where punishment is triggered by detection and acts before decisiveness, $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$, and the inequality reads $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})\ge1-\bar p_{d,i}$ ({{detectlink}}). {{restraint}} classifies the means. Where $D_i\le v_i$, a breakout does not raise the temptation net of the sanction: {{eq:enforceeq}} is hardest to meet at $p_d=0$, and the ordinary condition of repetition, $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-v_i)$, decides. *The exception.* {{eq:enforceeq}} is affine in $p_d$, so restraint survives every $p_d\in[0,1]$ iff it holds at $p_d=0$ and at $p_d=1$; at $p_d=1$ it reads $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$, which where $D_i\ge v_i$ implies the condition at $p_d=0$. Directly: under this inequality the left side of {{eq:enforceeq}} is $(1-\delta)g_i-\delta p_d(C_i-D_i)\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-D_i)\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i)$ for every $p_d$, so $\bar p_{d,i}\ge1$. A decisive position worth less than cooperation does not give this by itself: with $C_i=1$, $D_i=0.9$, $v_i=0$, $g_i=2$ and $\delta=0.9$, $\bar p_{d,i}=0.864$, and {{eq:enforceeq}} fails at $p_d=0.87$ and $0.9$; with $g_i=0.5$, so that $(1-\delta)g_i=0.05\le0.09=\delta(C_i-D_i)$, it holds at $p_d=0$, $0.5$, $0.9$ and $1$ [num: ES]. *Not needed* where no deviation reaches a position beyond punishment, so that $p_d=0$: in Branch III, and in Branch I′ above its saturating level ({{branches}}), where the terms of {{restraint}} decide. *Met, not excused,* in Branch II, where detection and response come within the time a breakout needs, by the detection form above.
- **R5, a summable uncovered share.** *Necessary under (residual floor) alone.* If on every surviving history the uncovered share is at least $\underline u_t$, (residual floor) makes the conditional probability of the irreversible event at $t$ at least $h\underline u_t$, and {{nonsum}} gives $\Pr(\Phi_H)\le\prod_{t<H}(1-h\underline u_t)\le\exp\big(-h\sum_{t<H}\underline u_t\big)$, which tends to zero when $\sum_t\underline u_t=\infty$. Survival bounded away from zero therefore needs a summable uncovered share. *Sufficient* under a residual bound below one: if the residual probability is at most $\bar hu_t$ with $\bar h<1$ and $\sum_tu_t<\infty$, survival stays bounded away from zero ({{coverage}}). Under (shared failure) both statements are every actor's, whoever is racing. Neither (coverage lags) nor (variety grows) enters the necessity: (coverage lags) says when R5 fails, and (variety grows) is what makes (coverage lags) expected on a continuation branch, so its failure removes a reason to expect R5 to fail, not the requirement. *Not needed* where (residual floor) fails, as where a correction architecture whose blocking becomes complete makes residual risk summable without coverage ({{opaquecorr}}); at a finite horizon what counts is the exposure up to it, which a freeze after a sprint keeps finite and a rollback below coverage ends ({{freeze}}). *Met, not excused,* where hazardous variety stops growing at a level set by need while coverage keeps growing at least linearly, so that the uncovered share reaches zero in finite time ({{enough}}).
- **R6, independent observation.** {{alarm}}: where an upper bound on masking comes from a channel the field does not control, an alarm on reports set correspondingly higher fires no later than the true condition reaches its target; for masking by the evaluated model, the bound must come from evidence its behaviour controls less directly. {{masking}} shows what fails without it: captured evaluation lowers the level at which a fixed alarm fires, and can silence it through the crossing of the floor. {{talk}}: informative cheap talk needs an effective bias below $\frac14$, which relative accounting raises, and between two rivals at $\theta=-1$ no talk informs, so trust without good will needs stakes on the topic of talk that are not contested, or signals that are costly or verifiable. Each is sufficient, not necessary. The detection on which R4's detection form rests is observation of this kind, and masking hides both $T_{\rm dec}$ and $\upsilon$ (Section 5.2). *Not needed* where channels that observe actions directly, such as verifiable deployments, compute and the performance of contracts, suffice for the purpose at hand, since they do not pass through the masking of {{eq:mask}}.
- **R7, standing.** V3. {{club}}(1) shows that inclusion stopping at the edge of a club over-produces harm to those outside relative to the system optimum whenever $m_o\ell_o>0$; it does not show that O1 or O2 is reached, so the requirement is normative, declared and not derived.
- **R8a, admission constraints that hold.** {{nested}}(3), with {{blind}}(3) type by type: under relative, cumulative selection among developers and $(A_\tau)$, with a matched variant admissible, a divergence whose costs fall on common terms persists unless a constraint enforced on every developer as a condition of admission covers it; the constraint keeps the divergence from spreading if, for every type that can evade it, the expected sanction is at least that type's gain from evasion, removes it under cumulative selection if every such inequality is strict, and within a type only if. Across types the condition is sufficient, not necessary: in one field with equal baselines, compliance takes the field iff the best-paid variant complies, so a type whose evasion pays still loses its evaders to another type's compliers when those earn more. With compliance costs 0.1 and 0.2 and expected sanctions 1.0 and 0.15, the second type's evasion pays and still vanishes, while with 0.05 in place of 0.15 evasion takes the field [num: CI]. The matched variant is load-bearing, since {{nested}}(2) is about variants, not about every pair of developers: a safeguarded developer with baseline 2 and tax 0.1 outgrows an unsafe one with baseline 1, and falls to a share of $2\times10^{-9}$ by $t=200$ against its matched twin; a tax of 0.01 is paid out of a lead of 0.02 for as long as the lead lasts, and not out of a lead of 0.005 [num: CI]. *Not needed* where developers are selected by something other than relative payoff, such as a public mandate, though a national laboratory that only moves relative selection to the level of states leaves it in force there; where the tax is zero or negative for the class, so that the safeguard is neutral or spreads without coordination; and where decisions are not retained by contest performance ({{retain}}). These exceptions remove this selection mechanism, not the independent duties of persistence, coverage and assurance. At zero tax the variants are neutral: a protective variant can persist if present, but it need not spread or remove a hazardous one. A positive residual hazard can remain even with zero tax [num: RP]. *The liability route.* A liability on the developer for the harm of a divergence enters its payoff as a private cost of not countering it. Where the harm can be attributed and the liability collected, and it exceeds the tax, $(A_\tau)$ fails and the safeguard spreads without coordination (the scope of {{nested}}). It reaches only what can be attributed: delayed and masked divergences are where attribution fails, harms beyond the developer's assets call for regulation in advance (Section 10.3), and the liability is itself imposed by correctors (R3). *Where admission does not reach.* Admission reaches only what it admits, and three things lie beyond it. None makes R8a unnecessary, which is why Section 9 states them as limits and not in the table's last column; a design meets each by other means. Systems that automate a developer's own research are used first inside it, where a condition imposed at release cannot see them (Section 6.4); R8a is therefore stated for consequential deployment, internal deployment included, and the means is the row's own scope. Openly released weights can be fine-tuned around any constraint (Section 6.4), so for an openly released class the decision to release is the admission decision for every later use, and what remains afterwards is a liability on use where harm can be attributed, the liability route above, within its limits of attribution. A masked divergence shows under evaluation only as far as the system's behaviour lets it, so admission conditioned on evaluated behaviour keeps it out only while the detection it allows, times the sanction, exceeds the tax ({{masked}}); the developer's own detection-conditioned sanctions face the same incentive comparison. With a fixed positive tax, bounded sanctions and detection tending to zero at fixed auditor access (C12), coordination alone cannot restore deterrence at all capabilities. If the tax also falls, compare expected sanction with the changing tax (C9 and C12 jointly), including the cumulative advantage needed for an asymptotic claim. None of this establishes the residual floor required by R5. Under the protection standard the recommendation is admission up to the capability at which independent control can establish safety, with development bound to the pace of control (E3) on every branch, broad E1 included. Stopping the decisive class (E2) follows its separate governance trigger; the removal of concentrating contests does not by itself supply coverage or an assurance case. Whether admission can bound masking at a given capability remains C12 (Section 12.3).
- **R8b, equal incidence.** R8 is split because its clauses do different work (Section 3.2): the sanction of R8a keeps a constraint, and the equal incidence of R8b keeps it from becoming a weapon against challengers. {{blind}}(2): a constraint that costs every complier the same additive amount is a common term among them and leaves their relative growth unchanged, and one whose cost differs re-sorts them toward those it costs least. Equal incidence is therefore necessary and sufficient for neutrality among compliers, and not needed to keep a divergence out: with compliance costs 0.1 and 0.2 and a sanction of 1, the compliers re-sort to shares 0.88 and 0.12 by $t=20$ while the compliant share is 0.999999975, and with equal costs their relative shares stay at 1.000000 [num: CI]. What it protects against is the second direction of capture, a rule whose cost falls on challengers ({{capture}}); that this protection is required rests on V5, since a rule that bars challengers gives the successful more advantage in the same game [val].
- **R9, human decision authority kept.** R9 is the row for O2. {{lockin}}: with the capacity to take delegated decisions back decaying at $\delta_h$ under delegation and recovering at $\varrho_h$ without it, an actor that has delegated for longer than $T_{\rm lock}=\ln(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})/\delta_h$ and reverts must operate below the floor of safe operation for a recovery time. Where the contest denies that time, the delegator can no longer overrule decisions taken in its name, and O2 is lost in that domain (Section 6.3); where a decision is irreversible within the time available for deliberation, the lock is immediate. Keeping the capacity in practice removes the lock, and where the contest is decided by strength it costs strength, so under relative selection it is not kept alone ({{blind}}(2)). Where delegation is dominant at the speed reached ({{delegate}}(1)), or the lock is uncertain, so that delegation by all can be an equilibrium ({{delegatelock}}(2)), the capacity survives only under a commitment binding every actor [thm∣prem]. *Not needed* where delegation leaves the capacity intact, or where automation is bounded and reversible within the time available, which the test of O2 in Section 6.6 decides. *Met without an arrangement* where every actor prices in a lock it knows to be certain: not delegating is then the unique equilibrium, strict and in need of no trust ({{delegatelock}}(1)), the narrow E1 of Section 11.3.

**Sufficient routes.** Four routes are sufficient and not necessary. Full inbound row sums below one certify eventual recovery of each corrector domain from $x_i(0)<1$ and finite support, against every state of the others ({{certificate}}(2)); they say nothing about whether the correctors' errors are independent ({{topology}}). The safeguards (I1)–(I4) of Section 10.3 give viability once in force, and over the transient when the liability margin is at least $\Delta_{\min}$, a threshold set by the host, the floor, the initial composition and the speed of selection: on the logistic host with the race strategy at 99% of activity, $\Delta_{\min}$ is 0.108, 0.165 and 0.355 for floors 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5; on a host that degrades by itself below a threshold, the margin must also keep the transient above that threshold, 0.26 to 0.51 in the cases computed, and a shortfall there is permanent ({{safeguards}}) [num: HO]. Not building the decisive class, holding it in common custody, and detecting and answering faster than breakout lower $p_d$, and so meet R4, by changing the premise of {{enforce}} rather than its terms ({{restraint}}(a)). Verification with an expected sanction $\upsilon F$ at least the net gain from exit deters irreversible exit from an agreement whose overhang grows ({{overhang}}).

### Proof of {{nounilateral}}

(1) With other advantages equal, a carrier that loads less than another earns $\nu-\tau_{\rm eff}$ less per unit of the difference, so while $\tau_{\rm eff}<\nu$ its share falls relative to the other's ({{load}}). Correction keyed to no one, and the cost of verification, are borne by the carrier that funds them and benefit every carrier, so they are selected against among otherwise identical carriers ({{noinside}}). Each contribution is a cost borne by one carrier, which relative selection removes ({{blind}}(2)).

(2) A liability imposed by one rule-maker leaves the carriers it does not reach uncovered. By hypothesis their combined load satisfies (reach), and the race carriers among them earn the full advantage $\nu$ of load, so their cumulative advantage over every carrier that bears the liability grows without bound and {{load}} applies: the host crosses its floor whatever that rule-maker imposes on its own carriers, and it meets neither R1 nor R2 alone. Imposing the liability lowers the payoffs of the carriers it reaches against those it does not, by (1), and so lowers the rule-maker's standing among the $n_s$ rule-makers, while the benefit, a host kept above its floor, is common to all. Unilateral imposition is therefore restraint in the commons of {{wow}}, with $\Upsilon_s$ the value to the field of a unit of it over its private cost, and no rule-maker imposing is the equilibrium once $\lambda_s>(\Upsilon_s-n_s)/(n_s-1)$. Whether an agreement among the rule-makers is an equilibrium is governed by {{assure}}.

(3) Where captured rule-making leaves $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$ (Section 4.1), the private payoff of a unit of load is $\nu-\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, negative iff $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})>\nu$, that is iff $x_{\rm rule}<1-\nu/\tau$, and only then does the selection of {{load}} reverse. That captured rule-making acts on liability in this form is the join of the field model to the load model, an interface of S13. $\square$

### Why R1 needs the signed-gap identity

General cumulative selection does not make R1 necessary when liability crosses the private advantage. Let $x$ be the share of a race carrier of load 1 against a carrier of load 0, with $\nu=1$ and equal background payoffs, and write $g=1-\tau_{\rm eff}$. The rule $\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{logit}x=g+g^3$ is relative, payoff-monotone and cumulative with $\varkappa=1$: its response to every positive gap is at least that gap, in either carrier order. Repeat a cycle of $\tau_{\rm eff}=0$ for one time unit and $\tau_{\rm eff}=3$ for 0.4 units. Each cycle adds 0.2 to $B$, so $B\to\infty$ and average liability tends to $6/7<\nu$; but it adds $2-4=-2$ to the race logit, so the race share tends to zero. From $x(0)=0.001$, $x(t)\le\operatorname{logistic}(\operatorname{logit}(0.001)+2)=0.00734215$ for all time. On the host with $\rho=0.05$, $h_V=0.08$, $V(0)=1$ and floor 0.3, (reach) holds, yet comparison gives $V(t)\ge1-(0.08/0.05)\,0.00734215=0.98825256>0.3$ for all time. Strong intermittent correction preserves viability although both forms of R1 fail. Smooth short transitions between the liability levels preserve these strict inequalities. Under constant-speed replicator selection on the same liability path, the race logit instead rises by $0.2s$ a cycle and the host eventually crosses its floor. The analytical bounds and independent integrations are in `verification/readiness_checks.py` [num: RP].

The difference is the sign change: on a negative-gap interval the cumulative condition, applied with the carrier order reversed, is an upper bound on the race carrier's relative growth. It cannot be integrated as the positive lower bound used by {{load}}, whose premise keeps the gap positive. R1 uses constant-speed replicator dynamics to avoid that unsupported extension.

### Which rows need more than one party

Section 9 gives the reasons for R1, R2, R3 and R6. R4, R5, R8a and R9 need more than one party under their own premises. Of the means of R4, custody needs more than one party, not building binds only the one that refrains, and detection faster than breakout needs access to the conduct of others. Under (shared failure) each actor's survival turns on the uncovered share of all, so a pace kept by one leaves R5 unmet for every actor ({{coverage}}). Under $(A_\tau)$ an admission constraint adopted by one developer is selected against ({{nested}}(3)). And where the contest is decided by strength, capacity kept in practice costs strength, so R9 kept by one is kept at a loss ({{lockin}}).

### Coverage can unravel

**{{!Remark:unravel}} (The participation threshold) [thm∣prem].** *Let $x$ be the share of capable activity outside an arrangement, weighted by share; let a participant of type $k$ bear a cost $c_k$ of its common constraints; and let the expected cost of standing outside, the sanction, the access forgone and the provision lost, be $s_k(x)$, continuous and strictly decreasing in $x$, because sanctions are funded by participants, shared provision is worth less with fewer inside, or rule-making is captured by those outside. Within a type, under cumulative payoff-monotone selection between staying and leaving, this is {{blind}}(3) with evasion read as exit: if $s_k(0)>c_k>s_k(1)$, staying and leaving are both stable, separated by the tipping share $x_k^\dagger$ at which $s_k(x_k^\dagger)=c_k$; if $s_k(1)\ge c_k$, staying is globally stable; and if $s_k(0)\le c_k$, leaving is, and the arrangement cannot hold the type on these terms. Participants who choose by best response, staying iff $s_k(x)\ge c_k$, have the same stable states and the same tipping share. Across types, where $s_k(0)>c_k$ for every type, every state whose outside share is below $\min_kx_k^\dagger$, the minimum taken over the types that tip, returns to full participation, with the type masses fixed and some participation inside each type. Past it, return is no longer guaranteed, but neither is unravelling: types still below their own tipping shares can return faster than the first type leaves, so the basin is a region in the vector of type shares, and a cascade follows only where the first type's leaving keeps the outside share above the next type's tipping share. The arrangement's participation threshold, the coverage $1-\min_kx_k^\dagger$ set by the type for which leaving first pays, is therefore a conservative sufficient coverage, not the tipping boundary.*

*Proof.* Within type $k$ the leaving variant grows relative to the staying one iff $c_k>s_k(x)$, so the type's outside share moves as $\dot x_k\propto x_k(1-x_k)\big(c_k-s_k(x)\big)$, and the claims for one type are those of {{blind}}(3). Across types, while $x<\min_kx_k^\dagger$ every bracket is negative, so every $x_k$, and with them $x$, falls. $\square$

An exit moves the outside share at once by the leaver's own share, so one large participant can carry it past a type's tipping share, and the exits of that type then follow without a further shock. With a cost of staying of 0.3 and a cost of standing outside of $0.48(1-x)$, the tipping share is 0.375; with $0.6-0.4x$ it is 0.75; with $0.5+0.5(1-x)$, which never falls below 0.5, staying is globally stable; with $0.24(1-x)$, leaving is [num: CI, evasion read as exit]. Conditions of access act on $s_k$: trade and access tied to participation, provision available only inside, and sanctions financed by formula rather than by the participants keep the cost of standing outside from falling as others leave, and once $s_k(1)\ge c_k$ for every type nothing can unravel. What raises the cost of staying moves the threshold the other way. A pause whose end is judged on several conjunctive indicators measured with noise errs toward "not ready" (Section 11.3) [thm], so an arrangement that holds participants longer than its evidence warrants raises the cost of staying and spends its coverage [thm∣prem].

Compounding and Standing are guarded only through R4, by the time a lead needs to become decisive and by the bound the working objective sets; what reverses them directly is not a requirement but a lever, because the models give sharing, provision and non-convertible reward as alternatives, any one of which can carry the margin ({{compound}}). What sets the levers apart from the rows is that each meets several rows at once: provision that grows with the stakes holds the won share still, and with it both sides of R4 ({{scissors}}); reward that does not convert into control, $\chi=0$, brings R3a's requirement down to a level without second-order selection ({{corrrate}}(1)) [thm]. Two of the generative marks of Section 8.2, G2 and G5, name them, and the reading by mark says which rows each meets.

*Read by mark.* G1, G3, G4, G6 and G7 each answer rows directly, G7 both the pace of R5 and, applied to decisions, the delegation of R9. G2 and G5 meet several rows at once rather than one: through the won share they move both sides of R4, and G2 is also what equal incidence protects (R8b), which is why Section 10 argues for them. No mark answers R7: the marks describe a change in what is selected, and standing (V3) is what any selection must leave in place.

*Read by safeguard.* (I1)–(I4) implement R1, R3 and R6 on the host of Section 3, reach R2 through their coverage and R8a through the liability route, and do not touch R4, R5, R8b or R9, which are not mechanisms of load on the host.

*Read by test.* D4, D5, D7 and D8 test rows directly, and D3 tests R7 and R9 after delegation. D1, D2 and D6 test no requirement: they test how an arrangement fails and what it keeps running, and their counterparts are the fallback ({{fallback}}), the participation threshold ({{unravel}}) and the continuity of essential services, O3, which no row carries because the rows concern what the contest selects.

*Read by readiness.* Q1 and Q3–Q7 each ask a row. Q7 asks for common custody or equally effective durable containment, including a verifiable disposition of capability already built; its connection to R4 is the maintained deviation bound, not a unique institutional form. R5 and independent assurance still apply on every branch, including broad E1 and zero alignment tax. Q2 asks none: it measures the Standing threshold and G5 directly, through governing purposes, which requirements do not constrain, since selection acts whatever carriers value ({{load}}). R1, R2, R7 and R8b have no readiness dimension. Readiness is asked of the frontier during a pause and presupposes them; a pause that ends with R1 or R2 unmet resumes a field in which load is selected.

*Other lists.* The four properties of provision in Section 10.1 are rows applied to provision itself: distributed is R3, many providers below the size at which influence over correctors pays ({{influence}}); good enough is R4 and R5, a point of enough that keeps provision within correction and confers no decisive advantage ({{enough}}); replaceable is R9, human capacity kept above its lock-in floor ({{lockin}}); and for everyone is R2 and R7. The outer level of Section 6.6 is R8a, R3 and R6 for AI, and its inner level, correctability secured from outside, is R8a's capability threshold for masked classes together with R9. The function table of Section 10.5 gives the rows their forms. The requirements of {{reachable}} are of the path to an arrangement, not of the arrangement; persistence, the last of them, asks for the verification safeguard, row sums below one and (I4), with each step held for its dwell time.
