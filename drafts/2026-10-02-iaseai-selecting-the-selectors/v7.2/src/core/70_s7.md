# Part II. Diagnosis: the present trajectory

Part II adds no mechanism to Part I. Its claims about the world are observations, with dated rows in the evidence register, read by one standard for every actor and with their gaps named; its formal results classify the contest by the terms of the enforcement condition and say what the premise of the race can justify.

## 7. The present trajectory

> **Claim.** The thresholds of Part I, each shown in its own model, turn on a few shared parameters, above all the share of capacity that has to be won, the convertibility of success into control, and decisiveness [thm]. The present trajectory's dynamics are signatures of the conditions, with named gaps in the evidence [obs]. Where a deviation can reach a decisive position worth at least cooperation before it is answered, the case the premise of the race describes, repetition enforces only the restraint each would choose anyway, and racing is each actor's best response to a racing rival wherever restraint against it pays less than racing [thm]. The race as a collective course leaves everyone's protection after a win to the winner's objective and, where restraint before victory goes with restraint after it and winning turns on the first alone, shifts winners toward those that extend dominance, and so, where extending dominance goes with giving least, toward those whose objectives give least [thm∣prem]; the premise of the race therefore cannot justify that course, and is a reason to prepare custody now [val]. That the thresholds move together as the stakes grow is the conjecture the diagnosis tests [conj].
>
> **Why.** Each threshold is one inequality in a specified model, and a parameter that enters several moves each in that threshold's own model; where a sign crosses models, the interface it needs is named and not proved [thm].

Three parameters recur. Decisiveness and the share of capacity that has to be won set compounding, standing and the breakout probability directly, and reach talk and delegation through the working objective that standing sets; the convertibility of success into control enters every row but delegation, most of them through an interface marked †. That is the formal sense in which the conditions are interconnected: not a single pressure, but a few parameters on which several thresholds turn, so that a change in one moves several [thm, cell by cell]; whether they move together in the world, so that growth moves the thresholds together, is C0 [conj]. S7 gives the full matrix of parameters against thresholds, each sign with its intervention, what is held fixed, the model that supplies it and the interface not proved. Three of its entries carry the argument. **In the fixed-prize capacity model, provision not won in the contest lowers the structural gain and local standing price and, through the time a lead needs to become decisive, the breakout probability** (P4; {{chansign}}, {{standprice}}, {{tdec}}): the formal content of the image of trees, and the pivot of the scissors ({{scissors}}). **Patience cuts both ways**, strengthening repetition and, with a fixed prize, raising the price of standing (P19; Section 3.5). **Promised sharing raises the danger in enforcement**, because a decisive winner can break the promise ({{wingives}}): sharing promised for after victory raises the value of a decisive position by what reneging saves, while sharing as it arises lowers the gain from deviating (P2, P1).

**Four loops.** The author describes the pressure as four loops [corpus: selfishness-spiral], and the rows of {{thresholds}} are their formal parts. *Compounding*: "selfish gets rewarded and it compounds, it out grows other behavior patterns", where a frame is selfish when its ledger counts only its own measures, the narrow self-model of Section 3. *Capture of the rules, and adopt or be out*: the successful reach the positions where rules are written, since "NVFs [narrative-value frames] define rules of the game, and the rules of the game determine the rewards of the players", and the rest must follow: "other agents observe them and understand in order to compete with them they also have to follow their rules". *The naturalized narrative*: "as they accumulate more, they create and slowly dominate the narrative", until the pattern is taken "as 'human nature', as granted, as everyone has to follow this because we can not change it". *Regulator lag*: every society keeps a regulator against compounding, its consensus code of norms, laws and shared understanding, which "has to be able to keep up with the pace of change and complexity of the society … in order to keep selfishness in check. (so basically our understanding of the world has to keep up with reality)"; where the world changes faster, the gap is a window in which the first three run unchecked. Three intensifiers act on all four. Paradigms converge: "more and more agents will also becoming single value optimizers through iteration", which removes the plurality on which decorrelated correction depends ({{topology}}). Harm and capture reinforce each other, so a crisis becomes a new field of accumulation. And as fewer hold more, more compete over less.

**The formal correspondence.** The four are not alike, and they do not run in series. Compounding and capture are reinforcing channels on one state, the concentration of standing: fed by one win share, their gains add in $\Gamma$ ({{loopgain}}(1)) [thm]. "Adopt or be out" is capture's second direction: a rule whose cost falls on challengers picks winners among the compliant ({{blind}}(2), {{capture}}) [thm]. The naturalized narrative is a reinforcing loop through the formation of minds, a conjecture, since frames are carriers only by hypothesis (Section 5.4) [conj]. Regulator lag is not a fourth reinforcing loop but the balancing loop of correction and mobilization ({{countervail}}), and the first three act on its two properties: capture and the erosion of human capacity lower its gain ({{corrrate}}, {{humancorr}}), while acceleration, masking and alienated language lengthen its delay ({{tempo}}, {{masking}}, {{talk}}) [thm, each in its model]. The danger is a shift of dominance from the balancing loop to the reinforcing ones, across delays that run from the turnover of capacity to the formation of minds over generations, which no model here contains (S7 gives the couplings loop by loop). A balancing loop that answers with delay is not a negative term of $\Gamma$ (Section 2.2): at a net gain below one, deviations can still grow, oscillating, so correction that arrives late can destabilize what it is meant to steady ({{delayosc}}) [num: CH].

**The drive, the driver and the substrate.** Behind the loops are drives, above all the belief that one is in a struggle for survival, and the fear of falling behind that it produces, which makes every participant faster. This *drive* is not a factor of $\Gamma$: it raises the weight on winning and shortens the horizon, and so narrows the working objective and weakens the repetition on which enforcement rests. Its mechanism is totalization: an actor that holds the belief subordinates every other value to the contest. The author calls the belief an attractor of the meta-attractor: "如果相信自己在生存状态的文化，attractor大多都会totalize，这是一个meta attractor的attractor" (in a culture that believes it is in a state of survival, most attractors totalize; this is an attractor of the meta-attractor) [corpus: meta-attractor]. Part of the drive is made by the loop: a discounted gain near one narrows the working objective as the belief would, and produces the conduct of a struggle for survival in actors who hold no such belief ({{standprice}}(1)) [thm]; that part is no error of the actor, and only a change of the game removes it ({{twoerrors}}). Part is held, and whether provision beneath the game lowers it is C8 [conj]. Among persons, the pressure channels the desire for status, a fundamental motive (Anderson, Hildreth and Howland 2015), into dominance rather than prestige, the other route to rank (Henrich and Gil-White 2001) [conj]; a generative order can channel it into prestige for contribution, provided prestige does not convert into control over the shared conditions or their correctors, a condition on convertibility that can be measured (V5; EV16; X1). The *driver* is technology, AI above all, which moves the parameters of every row in either direction (Section 2.4), so that what decides is whether it strengthens the loops more than the counter-loops; the *substrate* is the one host that all carriers share (Section 3).

### 7.2 Contest branches: what the premise of the race can and cannot justify

The three parts of the premise of the race (Section 5.1), (decisive), (fast) and (unsaturated), cut the possible contests into four branches by what a deviation can buy. The first two cuts are the two questions of the enforcement condition: whether a position beyond sanction exists, and whether it arrives before it is answered. Saturation is the third.

**{{!Proposition:branches}} (Classification of contest branches) [thm∣prem; each branch tagged].** Each branch is a set of results already proved, read under that branch's condition. *Let AI be an instrument of the contest.*
1. *Branch I: decisive, fast and unsaturated, so that $p_d\to1$. Where a decisive position is worth at least cooperation, repetition enforces no restraint that is not a stage equilibrium of the objectives held ({{enforce}}(4)). Restraint is not a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$, while joining buys nothing materially in the fully dissipative limit ({{join}}); racing dissipates the prize, the more the more decisive the advantage ({{dissipate}}); delegation becomes dominant as speed grows when its gain exceeds its share of the expected control loss, and is never chosen by actors who price in a certain lock ({{delegate}}, {{delegatelock}}); hazard modes with $a\tau_c\ge1$ are beyond proportional correction unless slowed ({{tempo}}); and where the side that gains a coming advantage will set the terms (A8), the side that will lose it has a motive for preventive attack wherever fighting now is worth more than peace on those terms ({{preventattack}}) [thm∣prem].*
2. *Branch I′: decisive but saturating, so that advantage converts decisively up to a level above which further advantage changes no outcome. The contest has an object up to that level and none beyond: above it $p_d=0$, and restraint is enforceable by repetition on the terms of {{restraint}}, while the hazards of development, which do not pass through $p_d$, remain ({{coverage}}, {{racehazard}}) [thm].*
3. *Branch II: decisive but slow, so that a lead can become decisive but detection and response act within the time it needs with probability $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ near one, and $p_d=1-\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ is small ({{detectlink}}). Restraint is enforceable wherever that $p_d$ is at most the bound $\bar p_{d,i}$ of every actor and deviation ({{enforce}}(2)); a shift in advantage spread over enough periods gives no motive for preventive attack ({{preventattack}}); hazard modes with $a\tau_c<1$ stay within proportional correction ({{tempo}}); and delegation becomes dominant only at a higher speed, because the speed at which it dominates rises as the loss from defeat falls against the shared loss of control ({{delegate}}), and a defeat answered before it becomes decisive costs less than one that is not [prem]. Correction and bargaining have time to work [thm∣prem].*
4. *Branch III: not decisive, so that leads do not compound in equilibrium ($\Gamma^{\rm eq}<1$) and no deviation yields a position beyond sanction ($p_d=0$). Intensity, dissipation, shared loss and the race-generated hazard are proportional to decisiveness ({{boundary}}(1), {{dissipate}}(1), {{racehazard}}): what vanishes as decisiveness falls is the object of the race, not every incentive to compete [thm].*

*Scope.* Each item holds in the model of the result it cites. Membership is set by quantities to be measured, not assumed: the decisive level, the detection lag and the saturating level (C5, C7); a technology can move a contest between branches. Low structural decisiveness does not place a contest in Branch III: channels of capacity and rule support add in $\Gamma$ ({{loopgain}}(1)), and where a lead discourages the follower, rivals who respond can lift the equilibrium gain above one from a structural gain below one ({{eqgain}}; S7 gives the cases) [num: LG; MP]. Within Branch I, where a decisive position is worth less than cooperation, cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium can survive $p_d=1$ ({{enforce}}(4)). *Proof:* S7.

Branch I′ is how nuclear rivalry stabilized once a survivable second strike was secure: the contest kept its object up to that level and lost it beyond, while the hazards of development remained (X11) [obs]. Balancing among rivals supplies a second kind of saturation that does not depend on the technology (X1). Whether advantage in AI saturates is not known (Section 7.3), and Section 11.5 states the consequence conditionally.

The race also selects which winner arrives.

**{{!Proposition:raceselects}} (What the race selects) [thm∣prem].** The race selects winners by their conduct before victory and, under one premise, by their conduct after it. *Let actors race for a decisive position; let $W$ be the event that an actor wins, $E$ that it does not restrain itself while ahead, and $X$ that, having won, it extends its dominance rather than limiting it.*
1. *If restraint while ahead lowers the probability of winning, $\Pr(W\mid E)>\Pr(W\mid\neg E)$, then $\Pr(E\mid W)>\Pr(E)$ whenever $0<\Pr(E)<1$: winners are selected for not restraining themselves, and repeated rounds compound the shift ({{load}}) [thm].*
2. *If, in addition, conduct before victory and after it are positively correlated, $\Pr(X\mid E)>\Pr(X\mid\neg E)$ [prem], and winning depends on $X$ only through $E$, then $\Pr(X\mid W)>\Pr(X)$: the race shifts winners toward those that extend dominance [thm∣prem].*
3. *No actor can rely on a winner's stated limits on its use of a decisive position, since after a decisive win no one can sanction a broken promise ({{wingives}}): the commitment problem (Fearon 1995; Powell 2006) [thm].*

*Scope.* The correlation in (2) is a premise, and its base rate is not established (C4; Test I). *Proof:* S7.

A winner is not a point but a field of agencies, firms, laboratories and systems, in which the selection of Sections 2 to 4 continues after victory; Test I measures that field's correctors. Beyond that selection, what restrains a winner is its remaining dependence on the people it governs ({{wingives}}; C13), and an AI aligned to the winner is not thereby aligned to them (Section 6).

**{{!Corollary:racepremise}} (What the premise of the race cannot justify) [thm∣prem; val].**
1. *In Branch I, or in Branch II without timely detection, where $S_i<P_i$, racing is each actor's best response in its objective to a racing rival ({{join}}), and repetition restores restraint only where $p_d(a)\le\bar p_{d,i}(a)$ for every actor $i$ and deviation $a$ ({{enforce}}(2)) [thm].*
2. *The race as a collective course leaves everyone's protection after a win to the winner's objective ({{wingives}}) and, under the premises of {{raceselects}}(2), shifts winners toward those that extend dominance, and so, where extending dominance goes with giving least ({{wingives}}), toward those whose objectives give least [thm∣prem].*
3. *The premise of the race, that a rival might win decisively, therefore cannot justify the race as a collective course, including in the form in which the race is safe provided the right actor wins [val: V1–V3].*

*Scope.* Part (3) is a judgment of value resting on V1 to V3 (Section 1), not a derivation: an actor that rejects them can accept (1) and (2) and reject (3). It does not say that an actor is wrong to race given that others race; it says that what each can rationally do cannot be what all should choose. *Proof:* S7.

Where the premise of the race holds, restraint rests on changing the premise, by not building the decisive class, by custody, or by detection faster than breakout, or on objectives under which restraint is already a stage equilibrium ({{restraint}}); the race supplies neither. The first is the institutional form of the third job of Section 3.7, a selection that counts the system; the second needs objectives wider than accuracy alone supplies (Section 5.1), unless coupling grows with power (C11). In neither case is "we are one" a parameter the result sets. **Oneness is what these requirements are requirements of**: one host, coupled actors, and no outside to absorb the failure. It is a property of the system on every branch; what changes between branches is the institutional form its accurate recognition requires.

The race's own premise is therefore the strongest reason to act before it comes true. Custody, the means it leaves, has to be prepared before a lead can outrun effective response: begun at a warning, it is in place too late wherever building it takes longer than the warning gives. And because the approach of a decisive position is where restraint is least enforceable ({{enforce}}(4)), a decision to activate custody taken then would face the very condition it is meant to prevent: activation has to be committed in advance and executed by mechanism (Section 12.1, tier 2).

The dated public benchmark measures cited here show gaps of months: open models trail closed ones by about four months on average, while algorithmic efficiency diffuses. These observations are consistent with decisive advantage not having arrived; they do not establish that conclusion across unmeasured capabilities or forms of power. Whether the lead has changed hands depends on the measure: on a composite index it has stayed with developers in one state, while on a preference leaderboard it is reported to have been traded [obs: EV5, EV9; C5]. Small benchmark-equivalent gaps cut both ways; a gap maintained over years is still a persistent lead. They are consistent with enforcement: the public measures show no breakout beyond punishment, so enforced cooperation may remain available and common custody can still be a preparation, not an emergency. They do not bound the window: public benchmark leads are not a measure of sanctionability, they miss systems held internally, complementary assets and the time balancing needs, and unmeasured internal capability stays in the range of uncertainty like every other unmeasured failure mode. They also hold the contest near parity, which, in contests in which a lead discourages the follower, is where standing is priced highest: summed over the two contestants, the price of standing is higher at parity than at a three-to-one split in every case computed, and total effort is highest at parity (S7 gives the cases) [num: MP; held for outside review]. The window for custody and the hardest-fought stretch of the contest are the same period.

The nearest neighbour of this account in international relations is defensive realism, which it extends by a hazard in development that endangers its holder, and from which it departs by treating self-help as a selected institution rather than a given (Wendt 1992); where offense has the advantage and is hard to tell from defense, AI sits in the doubly dangerous quadrant (Jervis 1978; X1).

The signatures sustain one another, the loops of Section 7.1 read at the level of what can be observed: W supplies the success that C converts into control; C removes the correction that would make the shared visible; N makes cooperation look naive, which keeps W in place. T6 is not merely a prejudice: where racing is dominant, or among purely relative actors whose punishments treat them alike ({{relpunish}}), restraint that each would not choose anyway really is unenforceable, and the remedy is a change in what is selected, not a better argument. Nor is it simply correct: where a cooperative equilibrium exists, the belief that restraint will be exploited persists because no one tests it (Section 5.4), and where expectations select among equilibria, a belief that leads are decisive selects one in which they are (Section 3.8) [conj]. In the author's words, the belief that others "will also compete and play this winner takes all game, even if we don't ourselves", so that "not participating means losing", is a thinking that "itself creates the trap and reality" [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. The trajectory is not uniform: seeds of cooperation exist, and whether they become binding and broader in time is the question (Section 11.4). The least measured signature is T4, the link to Section 4, since what Section 4 predicts is appropriable support that scales with share. Capability growth is measured, with the longest-horizon series at the limit of its instrument, while the growth of hazardous behaviour and of residual risk is not, which is why Section 12 treats it as the central crux.

### 7.4 Which layer, and how the diagnosis could fail

The diagnosis is at the layer of mechanism ({{meta}}(1)), partly evidenced; whether the present configuration is attracting or a long transient is open (Test D). That the six signatures load on one latent factor is a research question, not a registered prediction. What is registered is what the stated conditions predict, with rival comparisons only where a specified comparator yields a different prediction. Tests D and G currently test within-model responses; confirming them does not distinguish the account from path dependence or constraint-based realism without a matched rival model (TESTS).
- **Test Γ.** $\Gamma$ is not estimated (Section 2.2); in its place, the ratio of conduct's response to shared harm to its response to private cost, which equals $1+\theta$ for the working self-model acted on ({{boundary}}; S3 gives its confound with how much of the harm is perceived), in the fixed-prize deterministic capacity model should rise with provision not won in the contest relative to the stakes, and with diffusion, holding declared objectives fixed, because both lower the discounted gain and so widen the working objective ({{standprice}}, {{chansign}}; S7, P4, gives a computed case) [num: SP]. A fixed relative preference predicts no such interaction.
- **Test E.** Moving one shared parameter should move signatures in more than one group, where a rule aimed at one signature should not. This is the test of C0.
- **Test G.** The gap in restraint between wider and narrower objectives should grow with the breakout probability over the range in which the wider objective still sustains restraint. Where a decisive position is worth more than cooperation it closes as $p_d\to1$ (Section 5.5; S5) [num: JX]. This is a prediction of the specified game. Testing whether it distinguishes the mechanism from a constraint-based account requires deriving the latter's response to the same objective intervention with matched primitives; no general invariance claim about structural realism is assumed.

**What would refute it.** Suppose a system moves every threshold of {{thresholds}} to its safe side for every capable actor, by the marks that Section 8.2 lists. If the signatures then persist unchanged over the dwell time (Section 11.4), the claim fails. If moving one shared parameter changes only its own signature over that time, the claim that the conditions interlock fails for that parameter. Conversely, durable safety obtained by constraining conduct while the thresholds stay where they were, maintained as stakes and speed grow and classified as such before the outcome is known, would refute the claim that the conditions must change.

**The strongest counter-reading.** AI may be a normal technology: its impacts materialize not as capabilities improve but as they are diffused through the economy, at the pace of institutional adoption (Narayanan and Kapoor 2025); and in the history of general-purpose technologies, great-power advantage has come from the capacity to diffuse them, not from a lead at the frontier (Ding 2024). On either reading a lead at the frontier is not decisive in itself. Where deployment is slow enough for contestable coverage to keep up, (coverage lags) fails class by class (C1); where no advantage is decisive, the contest is in Branch III and $p_d=0$ ({{branches}}; C5). Both are measurable, and the judgment registers what follows if they are measured favourable (Section 12.3); the levers of Part I remain, diffusion among them ({{chansign}}). X8 meets the policy side of the reading.

If the diagnosis holds, the question is what would change it. Part III compares paths by one standard, and takes two things from this section: {{thresholds}}, as the test of what a path changes, and the status column of Section 7.3, as the evidence that the range of uncertainty over which every path is judged must respect.
