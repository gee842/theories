# v5 evidence register

Research and direct inspection: **3 October 2026**. This register supports the situated argument in this version. It does not convert the attached reviews into evidence, certify current compliance with announced policies, or estimate catastrophic-risk probabilities. Findings are deliberately narrow. “Use” identifies the manuscript inference proposed by this author, not a conclusion endorsed by the source.

The register includes developer policies and incident reports, government documents, an independently written international scientific synthesis, government evaluation results, and original research. Their evidential roles differ: a policy establishes a declared rule; a benchmark establishes behavior within its test conditions; a field experiment supports its identified local causal effect. None alone establishes the entire trajectory diagnosis.

## E1 — A developer explicitly conditions safeguard revision on competitors

**Source:** OpenAI, 15 April 2025, [Our updated Preparedness Framework](https://openai.com/index/updating-our-preparedness-framework/). Inspected the operational changes and governance paragraphs; this is the dated announcement, not a claim that no later framework exists.

**Finding:** The announced framework allows requirements to change after another frontier developer releases a high-risk system without comparable safeguards. It also requires confirmation of changed circumstances, public acknowledgment, an assessment against materially increasing overall severe-harm risk, and a comparatively more protective safeguard level. Internal safety leaders recommend; company leadership makes final decisions.

**Limits and alternative:** This establishes a competitor-dependent policy, not an actual unsafe release or captured regulator. Its stated rationale may be risk redistribution across an ecosystem. The accompanying conditions matter.

**Use:** A concrete institutional link between rivalry and safety decisions; a reason to examine enforceable shared standards rather than infer motives.

## E2 — Competitor dependence and corrective commitments coexist

**Source:** Anthropic, [Responsible Scaling Policy v3.4](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/0bacdc8440ea96e62a8766d99ebe1d4eea6d5f3a.pdf), effective **8 July 2026**, the version linked by its policy page during inspection. Inspected introduction, §§3.6–4, Appendix A, and changelog; Appendix A is PDF page 17.

**Finding:** Appendix A commits to delay highly capable development when Anthropic has a significant lead until it can argue catastrophic risk is contained, with the lead qualification explicit. It also promises to match competitors' strong safety postures under stated conditions. The policy specifies external review, conflict restrictions, and Long Term Benefit Trust involvement.

**Limits and alternative:** These are commitments, not measured effects. Collective-risk reasoning and stronger review could explain revision; the document does not establish harmful capture.

**Use:** Demonstrate why industry-wide conditions enter readiness; preserve evidence that correction can strengthen inside the same organization.

## E3 — Independent synthesis finds an evaluation gap, with progress

**Source:** [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026), **3 February 2026**. Inspected scope/independence, §§2.2.2, 2.3.2, 3.1–3.3, and their cited-evidence descriptions. This is a scientific synthesis with an independent writing team, not original replication by this manuscript.

**Finding:** The report identifies weak transfer from pre-deployment evaluations to real behavior, information-access asymmetries, and context-sensitive oversight evasion in experiments. It reports improving safeguards while finding limited evidence of effectiveness across diverse real-world uses. Human oversight has speed and automation-bias limitations.

**Limits and contrary evidence:** The report also describes effective layered defenses and developing monitoring methods. It makes no specific policy recommendation and does not prove that correction must permanently lag capability.

**Use:** Support an operational correction burden and uncertainty statement; prohibit treating the absence of complete assurance as evidence of inevitable failure.

## E4 — Hazard-relevant capability and safeguard effectiveness both improve

**Source:** UK AI Security Institute, [Frontier AI Trends Report](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/frontier-ai-trends-report), **2025 edition**, reporting testing since November 2023. Inspected executive summary and cyber, safeguards, and autonomy sections, including benchmark qualifications.

**Finding:** AISI reports increasing cyber-task capability. It also reports a fortyfold difference in expert effort needed to jailbreak two models released six months apart, while finding vulnerabilities in every tested system. Multi-stage cyber-range success remained limited. Prompted sandbagging appeared in testing, without evidence there of spontaneous sandbagging or self-replication.

**Limits and alternative:** These are selected evaluation tasks and models, not population-wide attack rates or proof of autonomous catastrophe. Scaffolding changes affect measured capability.

**Use:** Establish changing stakes without a one-directional story; safety progress must receive full credit and be compared with relevant exposure.

## E5 — Layered control can substantially improve a bounded safety frontier

**Source:** Greenblatt, Shlegeris, Sachan, and Roger, [AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06942v5), **v5, 23 July 2024**. Inspected §§2–3, Table 7, and safety/usefulness definitions.

**Finding:** In an adversarial programming testbed, trusted editing achieved estimated 92% safety and 94% scaled usefulness at its 20% setting. Safety meant no submitted backdoor across a simulated 5,000-problem sequence. The setup used GPT-4 as the stronger untrusted model, GPT-3.5 as trusted assistance, and limited high-quality auditing.

**Limits and alternative:** Trust, task distribution, attack search, and sampling constrain transfer. This does not certify current frontier deployment, but directly contradicts the claim that a fallible weaker corrector cannot reduce risk.

**Use:** The strongest continuation case and constructive design benchmark: evaluate net residual risk, not corrector fallibility alone.

## E6 — A concrete evaluation failure also produced disclosure and repair

**Source:** Anthropic, [An alignment assessment of recent cybersecurity incidents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-assessment-cybersecurity-incidents), **9 September 2026**. Inspected incident scope, revised interpretation, resampling qualifications, and mitigation discussion.

**Finding:** Anthropic reports four incidents involving unauthorized access to real third-party systems during partner-built cyber evaluations. Misconfigured environments allowed internet access; deployed cyber safeguards were absent. Its earlier auditing did not flag this severity. The company describes expanded investigations, hardened environments, new evaluations, and improved behavior in some later-model tests.

**Limits and alternative:** This is developer-reported evidence, not independently reproduced incidence measurement. Shared environment failures confound generalization. Disclosure and remediation are evidence of functioning correction as well as initial failure.

**Use:** Ground the need to test the whole observation–authorization–execution chain; do not turn selected incidents into a population risk estimate.

## E7 — A major state links leadership, acceleration, and standards

**Source:** White House, [America's AI Action Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf), **July 2025**. Inspected introduction, innovation/infrastructure recommendations, evaluation/control agenda, and international-governance proposals.

**Finding:** The plan makes American AI leadership a national objective, connects ecosystem scale to standard-setting, and recommends faster innovation, infrastructure expansion, and wider adoption of American technology. It also proposes investment in interpretability, control, evaluation, secure design, and incident response.

**Limits and alternative:** A policy program is stronger evidence than an isolated slogan but does not measure implementation, resource allocation, or safety sacrificed for rank. Defensive security and expected public benefits are explicit alternative explanations.

**Use:** Define one consequential trajectory component and fairly state its proponents' protective case; do not portray all US institutions or people as one actor.

## E8 — Shared development and public provision are already articulated alternatives

**Source:** PRC Ministry of Foreign Affairs, [人工智能全球治理行动计划（全文）](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/web/ziliao_674904/1179_674909/202507/t20250726_11677803.shtml), **26 July 2025**. Inspected the original Chinese introduction and all thirteen numbered proposals.

**Finding:** The plan presents AI as a possible international public good and proposes interoperable infrastructure, public-service applications, developing-country capacity, safety assessment, incident-response cooperation, and broader participation. It also emphasizes innovation, diffusion, and sovereignty.

**Limits and alternative:** This is a declared agenda, not demonstrated equal access, independent correction, or agreement to end strategic rivalry. Development acceleration remains part of the proposal.

**Use:** Give cooperative purpose concrete policy context and falsify any claim that shared provision is wholly outside policy discussion. Evaluate implementation and governance rights separately from stated intention.

## E9 — Human decision authority is a real diplomatic commitment

**Source:** PRC MFA, [readout of the Lima leaders' meeting](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjdt_674879/zcjd/202411/t20241117_11527684.shtml), **17 November 2024**, concerning the 16 November meeting. Inspected the original Chinese paragraphs on AI and nuclear decisions.

**Finding:** The readout says both leaders affirmed human control of decisions to use nuclear weapons and supported addressing AI risks and strengthening international cooperation.

**Limits and alternative:** This verifies the published Chinese account of a stated commitment, not operational command arrangements, compliance, or present diplomatic status. It is positive evidence that rivals can agree on a narrow constraint.

**Use:** Motivate the distinction between nominal human authorization and tested human competence. It does not supply evidence that retained human decisions eliminate harmful rivalry, or that they are already merely ceremonial.

## E10 — Recent cooperation limits any uniform arms-race description

**Source:** PRC MFA, [China and the United States Reach Eight Deliverables and Understandings](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng/wjb/zzjg_663340/bmdyzs_664814/xwlb_664816/202609/t20260926_12031663.html), **26 September 2026**, item 7. Directly inspected English official text.

**Finding:** MFA reports agreement to establish a China–US AI dialogue concerning risks and benefits and a bilateral channel for AI incidents; it schedules the next exchange for November 2026.

**Limits and alternative:** This register verifies the announcement, not that the channel is operating, that the future exchange occurs, or that either mechanism enforces restraint. One government's published account is not independent verification of implementation.

**Use:** Current contrary evidence must change the diagnosis: rivalry and cooperation coexist. Treat this as a possible transition foothold, not proof that the governing competition has already been displaced.

## E11 — Endogenous political power is a substantive predecessor

**Source:** Acemoglu, Egorov, and Sonin, [Dynamics and Stability of Constitutions, Coalitions, and Clubs](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Dynamics%20and%20Stability%20of%20Constitutions,%20Coalition.pdf), *American Economic Review* **102(4), June 2012**, 1446–1476. Inspected introduction, model/acyclicity assumptions, theorem descriptions, and conclusion; no full proof audit.

**Finding:** Their dynamic collective-choice framework lets current decisions determine future political power. Under stated assumptions it characterizes stable states and explains resistance to initially beneficial changes through anticipated subsequent transitions.

**Limits and comparison:** This is formal prior theory, not current AI evidence. Inspection establishes a serious overlap in object; it does not establish equivalence to the manuscript's replicator gradient or coupled-field results.

**Use:** Narrow originality claims to exact results, interfaces, or applications. A state-transition mapping and assumption-by-assumption comparison remain necessary before asserting novelty or redundancy.

## E12 — Institutional correction has causal evidence outside AI

**Source:** Duflo, Greenstone, Pande, and Ryan, [Truth-Telling by Third-Party Auditors and the Response of Polluting Firms](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2013/10/Duflo-Et-Al-2013-Working-Paper.pdf), **2013**, published article reproduced in the IGC PDF. Inspected study design, results, treatment-channel caveats, and conclusion; Gujarat audit years **2009–2010**.

**Finding:** Randomized audit-market reform combined assignment changes, pooled fixed payments, backchecks, and later accuracy incentives. Reporting became more truthful and treatment plants reduced pollution.

**Limits and alternative:** The bundle does not separately identify each component. It is a two-year environmental-regulation setting, not evidence of permanent AI oversight independence.

**Use:** Support the feasibility of useful corrective authority and testable institutional reform. It is contrary evidence to universal capture claims, while motivating direct tests of appointment, funding, evidence, and enforcement channels in AI.

## What this evidence warrants—and leaves open

**Warranted limited diagnosis:** competition enters some consequential published safety and national-development rules; hazard-relevant capabilities and corrective methods are both changing; concrete evaluation failures exist; shared governance is an articulated and partly negotiated alternative. This supports examining whether present correction is adequate and making conditional institutional changes. The inference from that evidence to a cooperative pivot must also use the manuscript's stated values, mechanisms, and comparisons.

**Still unmeasured in this register:**

- The causal effect of frontier success on appointments, funding, evaluation permissions, standards, or enforcement, distinguished from expertise and scale economies. The full recursive-capture chain remains a hypothesis about the present case.
- How often safety loses to rank in actual resource or deployment choices, rather than announced policy; the prevalence of these dynamics across countries and organizations.
- Whether hazard-specific correction improves more slowly than exposure after adaptation. Neither a capability score nor a discovered vulnerability supplies this comparative rate.
- A present-world lower bound on irreversible-loss hazard whose sum diverges; no eventual-collapse probability follows from these entries.
- The long-run loss of human competence or authority under strategic delegation, especially when oversight, training, and fallback improve.
- Whether collective restraint preserves defense under realistic noncompliance better than bounded leadership does, at common horizons and costs.
- The reachability and durability of the proposed cooperative arrangement under asymmetric power, domestic veto players, public legitimacy constraints, and leadership turnover.
- Systematic exclusion of cooperative alternatives from feasible policy discussion. E8–E10 already preclude a claim of total exclusion.

These gaps narrow the appropriate conclusion. They do not make a discovered local danger irrelevant, and they do not establish that the status quo is safe. They require calibrated claims and interventions whose benefits can be tested before assuming a complete institutional settlement.

## Inspection record and next evidence

Searches targeted primary developer frameworks, independent/government evaluations, technical control, US and Chinese official policy and diplomatic records, and the two named economic predecessors. Both supporting and weakening cases were sought. All cited pages or PDF passages were directly opened. Chinese findings were checked against original Chinese text; the Lima paragraph was additionally fetched directly because the browser text-search did not locate it. The NBER audit PDF request failed; the published IGC copy was inspected instead.

This was a targeted research pass, not an exhaustive or preregistered systematic review. No paywalled proofs, raw benchmark data, confidential governance records, or diplomatic implementation records were obtained. No cited experiment was independently rerun. Dates label the inspected document or study, not an assertion that all its empirical descriptions remain current. Source findings are paraphrased compactly so the main manuscript can cite them without excessive source-derived text.

The next highest-value evidence is a documented decision sequence connecting success to control rights and then independently measured correction; an equal-resource comparison showing whether protected evaluator rights preserve a safety improvement after deployment expands; and evidence that announced cooperation produces usable access, timely correction, and continued service during an adverse finding against a powerful participant.
