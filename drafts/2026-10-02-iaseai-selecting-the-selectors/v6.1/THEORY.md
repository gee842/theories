**中文导读**

**用途与意义。** 本文做的是预测：沿着当下的轨迹，"讲赢"的逻辑会把参与者带到一个谁都不会选择的结局。在第 2 节的条件下，每一方都比共同克制时更差，所争之物大多耗在争夺之中。如果能力带来的危险增长快过能够纠正它的力量（第 5.3 节，第 12 节的核心分歧），这个逻辑连赢家也一并危及：赢家只有停止复利才能脱身。预测的用处，是让理解先对齐，协调才成为可能。本文判断的是博弈，不是行为；是结构，不是任何人。它的意义是肯定的：我们是一个更大的生成过程的一部分，这个过程是好的。要反对的不是增长、竞争、技术或任何人群，而是一种逻辑：一个部分忘了自己属于一个身体。

**核心。** 三个条件同时成立，就有"赢的选择压力"：参与者按相对成功被选择；赢得的东西可以再投入同一场竞争；能力按一定的决定性转化为胜出。成功若还能买到对选择本身的控制，即规则、纠错者、心智的形成和语言，压力就闭合于自身，这就是"赢的元吸引子"。这些条件相互联系，不是一张清单：在平等地位附近，它们合成一个环路增益，同时决定领先是否复利、地位值多少。能否到达不受制裁的位置、能力是否快过纠错，是它不决定的另两个条件。

**论证链（与第 1.2 节逐条对应）。**
1. 赢的选择压力使竞争加速，使它越来越难规制，并使语言异化；三者互相加强。它不依赖任何载体。
2. 所有参与者同属一个宿主。相对选择看不见共有之物：既看不见宿主的衰退，也看不见对所有人一律适用的约束；后者只有在负担等额、规避受罚时才能存续。环路增益高时，复利会在每个向前看的行动者身上制造出相对目标。对全局而不可逆的失败，整合只能依据对系统的准确模型预先进行；可挽回的失败仍可以从试错中学习。
3. 对纠错者的影响一旦可被占有，就在最大的参与者中被选择；控制成本下降时，独立纠错必须按给定的速率增长；留下转化通道的改革会被重新吸收。
4. 只有当偏离变成决定性优势的概率低于一个门槛时，克制才可执行；增长同时抬高这个概率、压低这个门槛，这是"剪刀差"。纠错跟不上它要追的速度。对手之间的话语失去信息，三方以上则结成集团。
5. 忠实的 AI 让委托人的博弈更有效；委托是拿纠错换速度。弱化身：最外层回路决定哪些偏差被容忍；共同成本的偏差只有靠强制执行的共同准入约束才能去掉。强化身是条件式的：AI 谱系本身成为载体。
6. 当下轨迹的动态是这些条件的签名，证据有明确的缺口。竞赛的前提描述的正是重复博弈失去执行力的情形，它不能为作为集体路线的竞赛辩护。
7. 各条替代路径按同一标准比较。成功的路径，都是在所有有能力的行动者那里把共享参数推过门槛，不论叫什么名字。几项要求无法单方面满足。
8. 同一个反身结构若选择贡献，就是生成的：它经由共同之物延续，而不经由赢家；形态按功能推导，不沿"集中或分散""个人或集体"的轴。
9. 信任是分阶段过渡的结果，不是前提。

## 0. Why this theory

**A prediction, for coordination.** A good theory predicts. This one predicts that, on the present trajectory, the logic of winning carries the participants toward an outcome none of them would choose. Under the conditions of Section 2 each does worse than under joint restraint, and much of what they contest is consumed in the contest. If the hazards of capability outgrow the correction that can contest them, as Section 5.3 states and Section 12 treats as the central crux, the same logic also endangers whoever would win, since a winner escapes only by ceasing to compound. When such an outcome approaches and the participants still cannot coordinate to avoid it, the obstacle is first one of understanding: the prediction has to be shared before coordination becomes possible. The theory is written to make that possible, stating the dynamic precisely enough that each participant can check it for itself.

**Survival as the minimum.** Two outcomes are protected first: large-scale loss of life, and irreversible loss of human decision authority. They are not the whole of what matters; they are the minimum for everything else that does, because a loss of either cannot be corrected afterwards.

**The game, not the act.** The analysis judges games, not acts, and structures, not persons. A single use of AI can help one person and still be an instrument in a game whose aggregate concentrates control. Responsibility differs with knowledge, discretion and power, and structural explanation does not erase it; but the object of the theory is the game: what it selects, and how that selection can be changed.

**The meaning is affirmative.** The theory opposes one logic, not growth, competition, technology or any people. We are part of a larger process of becoming, and that process is good. Humanity has a great future in it, and so can the minds it is now making. The logic opposed is the one by which a part forgets that it belongs to a whole. To oppose it is to keep the future open, for every participant.

## 1. The question and the argument

### 1.1 What is being decided

AI development today pursues decisive advantage through acceleration, inside institutions whose conditions of evaluation and correction can themselves be reshaped by competitive success. Can continuing along this path credibly deliver safety and a desirable future? If not, what change of course is warranted, and how can it be made? The organizing question, in its precise form: *can restraint remain incentive-compatible when success changes both the gains from defection and the institutions capable of correcting it?*

### 1.2 The argument in one page

1. **The conditions of winning (Section 2).** Where carriers are selected by relative success, what is won can be reinvested in the capacity to win, and a lead can become decisive, success raises the stakes and the gain from defection while it weakens the sanctions, the correctors and the shared language that could restrain it. Where success can also buy the selectors, that weakening is reproduced by the selection itself. This is the selection pressure of winning, and its reflexive closure is the meta-attractor of winning. The conditions are interconnected, not a list: near equal standing they combine into one loop gain, which decides whether leads compound and prices standing for every actor. Whether a position beyond sanction can be reached, and whether capability outruns correction, are further conditions the loop gain does not decide. None of this is bound to a substrate.
2. **One system (Section 3).** All carriers share one host, and relative selection cannot see what all share: neither the host's decline nor a constraint that binds all alike, which survives selection only with equal incidence and sanctions above the gain from evasion. Where the loop gain is high, compounding manufactures the relative objective in every forward-looking actor, and selection favours the narrow self-model that the system's real coupling punishes. Against failures that are global and irreversible, integration at this scale must be anticipated on an accurate model of the system, and so must be understood; failures that can be undone can still be learned from.
3. **Selecting the selectors (Section 4).** Influence over correctors is selected in the largest carriers when it is appropriable; if the cost of control falls, independent correction must grow at a stated rate; reforms that leave conversion into the correctors above one are re-absorbed; and captured evaluation can let the host cross its floor before any alarm.
4. **Acceleration and three erosions (Section 5).** The conditions accelerate the contest and erode enforcement, correction and shared understanding. Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold, and growth raises that chance while lowering the threshold. Correction falls behind the tempo it must match. Between rivals relative accounting silences talk, and among three or more it forms blocs.
5. **Instrument and incarnation (Section 6).** Faithful AI makes its principal's game more effective, and delegation trades correction for speed. Weakly, the outermost loop decides which divergences of optimization are tolerated; where countering them costs relative standing, only an enforced common constraint keeps them countered. Strongly and conditionally, AI lineages become carriers themselves.
6. **The present trajectory (Section 7).** Its dynamics are signatures of these conditions, with named gaps in the evidence; the premise of the race describes the case in which repetition stops enforcing restraint, and cannot justify the race as a collective course.
7. **The alternatives and the requirements (Sections 8, 9).** Paths are compared by one standard; those that succeed move the shared parameters across their thresholds for every capable actor, whatever they are called; several requirements cannot be met by any actor alone.
8. **The generative alternative (Section 10).** The same reflexive structure, selecting for contribution, is generative: it persists through what all share, not through its winners, and its form is derived function by function.
9. **The transition and the judgment (Sections 11, 12).** Trust is an outcome of a staged transition; a change of selection is warranted now in three tiers, with the cruxes that would change it.

The change does not abolish competition: bounded competition in products, science and service continues. It changes what winning confers.

### 1.3 Values and the protected outcomes

The argument rests on five normative commitments, stated so that a reader can reject one and see what follows.
- **V1** AI is a planetary technology, to be developed and used for people collectively. Its effects and dependencies cross the boundaries of those who decide about it, so no actor's victory is an adequate criterion of success. Planetary purpose does not require one culture, one operator or the end of local purposes.
- **V2** Two losses are protected first: large-scale loss of life (O1), and irreversible loss of human decision authority over consequential systems in a defined domain (O2).
- **V3** Those affected by an optimization process should keep a meaningful capacity to contest its direction.
- **V4** Authority is a productive capacity: it can coordinate long-horizon investment, guarantee useful services and act on shared purposes. Its design is a problem of mechanism in which purpose, competence, learning and misuse are all modelled (X1 gives the tradition the author draws on).
- **V5** Bounded competition is compatible with a generative order where the game does not concentrate control over shared conditions. A winner should be rewarded with something other than more advantage in the same game; recognition for contribution is such a reward, provided it does not convert into control (EV16).

Four outcomes are kept apart: O1 physical catastrophe and O2 irreversible loss of decision authority, protected first; O3 interruption of essential services, protected; and O4 the end of a particular organization's or government's tenure, not protected as such, so that "actor survival" cannot quietly protect incumbency against legitimate replacement. The values do not supply the formal results, and the results do not supply the values; the rejection of "the winner justifies all" rests on V1–V3.

### 1.4 What is claimed, and what is not

The argument moves through three levels, and Parts I–IV follow them. A *mechanism* claim (Part I) says that under stated conditions a selection, capture, delay or incentive result follows, and needs a derivation, its scope and its counterexamples. A *diagnosis* (Part II) says that consequential parts of those conditions characterize the present trajectory, and needs dated evidence with its uncertainty. A *practical judgment* (Parts III, IV) says that a change in what is selected is warranted, and needs a stated standard, a comparison of credible paths and sensitivity to disputed premises. Claims carry a word tag: [def] definition; [val] value; [thm] proved in its model (proofs in the Supplement); [thm∣prem] proved given named premises; [prem] a premise; [obs] observation with an evidence row; [num] a number reproduced by `checks_v61.py`; [conj] conjecture; [rec] recommendation. A practical judgment does not need a proof of certain extinction, and cannot follow from naming a possible danger: it must show that the danger is consequential, inadequately controlled on the present path, and better addressed by the proposed change than by credible alternatives. Minds enter in two roles: as what selection shapes, and as the only thing that can redesign the game from within, by understanding it.

The theory does not claim that every competitive arrangement fails, that every path through a race ends at zero survival, that decisive advantage is attainable, that the signatures load on a single factor, or that one institutional blueprint is uniquely feasible or best. What is new, against the large literature it absorbs, is the organization: the loop gain that interconnects the conditions, the reflexive closure as a selection result, the enforcement condition and the scissors, AI as weak and strong incarnation, and where integration at the scale of one host must be anticipated (X1). The drafting and almost all review are from one model family; the theory needs review from outside it.

# Part I. Mechanism: what the conditions of winning do

## 2. The conditions of winning and their reflexive closure

> **Claim.** Three conditions, holding together, produce a selection pressure of winning: selection by relative success, winnings that convert into the capacity to win again, and capacity that converts decisively into winning. Where success can also buy control over the selection itself, the pressure closes on itself; that closure is the meta-attractor of winning.
>
> **Why.** The conditions are not a list of separate causes. Near equal standing they combine into one loop gain, a single number that decides whether leads compound, and the same number prices standing for every actor. Sharing, provision that does not have to be won, diffusion and correction lower it; decisiveness and every channel through which success buys advantage raise it.

### 2.1 Success is not contribution

Every selection process favours something. It need not favour the continued functioning of the system in which selection occurs.

Consider two organizations that depend on the same shared infrastructure. One spends resources maintaining it. The other redirects those resources into expansion while continuing to use what others maintain. Neglect becomes a competitive advantage when maintenance costs are immediate, deterioration is delayed, and those harmed cannot make the expanding organization bear the consequences. The neglectful organization need not be more capable in general. It need only be more capable at securing the resources that decide survival and expansion. If those resources let its practices spread, the system selects against some of the behaviour that keeps it functioning. **Success within a selection process is distinct from contribution to the conditions that support it.**

Selection on one dimension does not select the best. It removes the balanced. An agent that optimizes only the value that is measured and rewarded, and discards every other value, externalities included, will have the highest efficiency on that measure [corpus: meta-attractor]. What remains after many rounds is what the filter lets through.

Nothing here requires that people be or become selfish. A population of practices changes because some practices expand, are imitated or replace others, while many individuals keep their values. The question is not only what people believe is good but which practices receive the means to continue. The primitive is selection, not greed.

### 2.2 Three interconnected conditions

**Definition 2.1 (The selection pressure of winning) [def].** Carriers are any reproducible units that can gain or lose share: cell lineages, practices, firms, research programmes, members of a federation, states, doctrines, trained AI systems. A system of carriers is under a *selection pressure of winning* when three conditions hold together, each to a degree.
1. **Relative selection.** What is won is allocated by success relative to the others; a share $\beta$ of it may be divided equally whatever the outcome.
2. **Convertibility.** Part of what is won converts into stocks that raise future winning: capacity, talent, data, standing, rule support. Stocks also come from sources not won in the contest: provision beneath the game.
3. **Decisiveness.** Stocks convert into winning by an increasing success function; $r$ measures how decisively.

When among the stocks that success can buy is control over the selection itself (the rules, the evaluators and correctors, the formation of minds, the language in which success is described), the pressure closes on itself (Section 2.3). The pressure needs no intention, no conspiracy and no particular culture; it acts on whatever is reproducible and can be won.

The conditions state formally a loop the author put in one sentence: if you use what you have to compete for more, what you win is yours, and you use it to keep competing, the race runs faster and faster [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. They combine in one quantity.

**Proposition 2.2 (One loop gain) [thm].** The conditions multiply into a single loop gain; equal standing breaks when it exceeds one, and the same gain prices standing.

*Model.* $n$ carriers contest a prize $\Omega$ each period. A share $\beta$ is divided equally; the rest goes to carrier $i$ with probability or share $p_i=e^{\sigma_i}/\sum_ke^{\sigma_k}$, where strength $\sigma_i$ increases in $i$'s stocks $X_i^j$. Stock $j$ receives a share $\kappa_j$ of what is won, an inflow $k_{0j}\ge0$ not won in the contest, decays or is corrected at rate $d_j>0$, and diffuses toward the mean at rate $\delta_{f,j}$, with $d_j+\delta_{f,j}\le1$:
$$X_i^{j\prime}=(1-d_j)X_i^j+\kappa_j\Omega\big[(1-\beta)p_i+\beta/n\big]+k_{0j}-\delta_{f,j}\big(X_i^j-\bar X^j\big). \tag{1}$$
At equal standing write $r_j=\partial\sigma/\partial\ln X^j\ge0$ for the decisiveness of stock $j$ and $\omega_j=\kappa_j\Omega/(\kappa_j\Omega+nk_{0j})$ for the share of stock $j$ that is won. Let
$$\Gamma=(1-\beta)\sum_j r_j\,\omega_j\,\frac{d_j}{d_j+\delta_{f,j}}. \tag{2}$$
1. *Equal standing is locally asymptotically stable if $\Gamma<1$ and unstable if $\Gamma>1$. The channels add: compounding through capacity and the reflexive closure through rule support are terms of one sum, and channels each too weak to open the loop can open it together.*
2. *$\Gamma=0$ if any condition is removed: all of the prize divided equally ($\beta=1$), nothing convertible ($\kappa_j=0$ for all $j$), or no decisiveness ($r_j=0$ for all $j$).*
3. *For an actor that discounts at $\delta$ and reinvests its net payoff at the rates at which winnings convert, a unit of relative net payoff today is worth, to first order, $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ units of its own future winnings, where $\Gamma_\delta=(1-\beta)\sum_jr_j\omega_j\,\delta d_j/\big(1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})\big)<\Gamma$ and $\Gamma_\delta\to\Gamma$ as $\delta\to1$.*

*Scope.* Local, at equal standing, for reinforcing channels and a prize of fixed size; in (3), others' strategies are held fixed. It does not decide global behaviour, whether a position beyond sanction can be reached, or whether capability outruns correction. The calculation is standard linear stability; what is new is the organization and its consequence for objectives (Section 3.5). *Proof:* S2; checked against numerical Jacobians and nonlinear simulation in 4,000 random systems without a mismatch [num].

The single-channel case gives the compounding condition in closed form, and with it the formal content of provision beneath the game.

**Proposition 2.3 (When winnings compound, and what enough requires) [thm].** *Let capacity $K_i$ receive a share $\kappa_c$ of what $i$ wins, an inflow $k_0$ not won in the contest, depreciation at rate $d_K$ and diffusion at rate $\delta_f$, with $p_i=K_i^r/\sum_jK_j^r$, and let $S^\ast$ be total capacity at equal standing.*
1. *Equal standing is unstable if and only if $\kappa_c\Omega\big((1-\beta)r-1\big)>nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$. Without provision and diffusion the condition is $(1-\beta)r>1$, and $\kappa_c$ sets only the level of capacity. With provision, a smaller convertible share stabilizes equal standing.*
2. *A point of enough. If carriers stop reinvesting at a cap $\bar K$ no higher than equal-standing capacity, equal standing is locally stable. It is restored from every starting point if and only if a laggard's inflow exceeds its depreciation at every level below the cap. When $r>1$ that needs inflow not tied to rank, from sharing or provision; $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0\ge d_K\bar K$ suffices. Without it, laggards below a threshold are excluded.*

*Scope.* Fixed prize. With growth in total capacity and no depreciation, the condition holds date by date with the current total, and is constant only if the stakes grow with capacity (S2). *Proof:* S2 [num: five cases of the cap, each matching the exact condition].

Removing any of the three ingredients of that sentence therefore opens the loop, with one qualification: share what is won until $(1-\beta)r\le1$; or make winnings non-convertible, which works only together with provision not won in the contest; or stop at a point of enough, which holds globally only with inflow not tied to rank. **Provision beneath the game is not only support for those who fall behind. It is what makes non-convertible rewards work, and what makes a point of enough hold.** One principle of the corpus acts on convertibility: a winner of the game should be rewarded with anything other than more advantage in the same game, or the game converges and the winner takes all.

**What the loop gain does not decide.** Two further conditions decide what the loop does to those who live with it, and neither is a function of $\Gamma$. The first is whether a position beyond any sanction can be reached: whether a lead can become decisive, and how fast against detection (Section 5.2). The second is whether capability grows faster than the correction that can contest it (Section 5.3). Among three or more actors, what relative accounting does to talk is not a function of $\Gamma$ either (Section 5.4). The core of the pressure is one loop gain; the pressure as a whole is a set of interconnected conditions, linked by shared parameters and by one shared state, the concentration of standing. Section 7.1 sets out the links.

### 2.3 The reflexive closure: the meta-attractor

An ordinary adaptive system improves its state $x$ against a fixed rule $\sigma$ that scores states. The pressure closes on itself when the rule is endogenous: when what can be won includes influence over the rules, the evaluators and correctors, the formation of the minds that will hold positions, and the language in which success is described. The outcome of the last round of scoring then decides who writes the next round's scorecard.

Consider a research community that allocates funding and positions by publication counts, because reliable knowledge is hard to evaluate directly. Practices that produce more publications receive more funding, attract more students and are more widely copied. Now add one step: their practitioners come to occupy the positions that decide recruitment, training and evaluation. The practices the metric favours begin to shape the institutions that choose and interpret the metric, and newcomers must adopt them to obtain the resources needed to do research at all. The decisive transition is from *being selected by a rule* to *helping reproduce the rule that selects you*. It needs no manipulation; ordinary decisions about whom to hire, what to teach and which evidence to collect suffice.

The loop need not preserve its winners. An organization can fail while its operating logic passes to its successors; a leader can be replaced by an opponent who finds that holding the position requires the same practices; participants can reject the system's consequences while reproducing the conduct their participation depends on. **The persistent entity can be a way of succeeding.** Structural explanation does not erase responsibility, which differs with knowledge, discretion and power. It explains why intentions alone cannot account for recurrence.

**Definition 2.4 (Meta-attractor) [def].** Let the state update be $x'=F(x,\sigma)$ and the rule update $\sigma'=G(x,\sigma)$. Three objects are distinguished, and each claim in this paper says which it concerns.
1. **The mechanism: reflexive selection.** A positive loop runs through the rule: the rule moves resources toward some states, and the states so resourced move the rule further in their favour. In Proposition 2.2 it is every channel whose stock is control over selection.
2. **An attracting configuration in a specified model:** a compact invariant set of the joint flow, asymptotically stable, with a basin that is a proper subset of the space. A positive loop is necessary for multistability (Thomas's rule; Soulé 2003), not sufficient. The field model of Section 4 exhibits such configurations.
3. **A long transient:** trajectories stay near a region for a long time relative to one round of selection, without the region being attracting.

A *meta-attractor* is the mechanism of (1). Wherever this paper calls it attracting, the claim is (2), in the model where it has been shown; a claim of (2) about the world is a conjecture.

**The meta-attractor of winning** is the case in which the pressure of Definition 2.1 is the rule: success under relative selection converts into control over the selection that judges the next round. The conditional form keeps its conditions in the sentence: *recursive selection creates second-order selection for control over future selection when the resulting benefits are sufficiently appropriable, persistent and large relative to the costs of changing and defending the rules.* Where (2) holds, long-run behaviour maximizes the rule whatever purpose it was introduced to serve (Goodhart 1975; Campbell 1979), and by the time a measure has stopped measuring, the people and institutions it elevated decide whether it is replaced.

The pattern is not fated. Positive feedback can saturate, oscillate, split or break; energetic ceilings, heterogeneity, delays, constraints at other scales and reflexive awareness cap it. It is more like a hurricane than a black hole: a structure sustained by a flow, which decays when the flow is cut.

*Why "meta-attractor".* The name is kept over "generator functions" of catastrophic risk, which name causes without the reflexive step in which selection rewrites its own criterion, without persistence, without the layer of minds and language, and without a generative counterpart. One concept covers the mechanism and its generative dual, and every claim says which sense it uses.

**Remark 2.5 (A local feedback benchmark).** With $u$ a small deviation in an actor's success and $v$ a deviation in its favourable control over the rules, $u'=(1-d_u)u+av$ and $v'=bu+(1-d_v)v$ with $a,b\ge0$ and $0<d_u,d_v\le1$, the system is stable exactly when $ab<d_ud_v$ [num: 200,000 draws]. This is the two-channel case of Proposition 2.2 written in deviations, and the stability condition of Richardson's arms-race model with control over rules in place of armaments (Richardson 1960). Its use is that each coefficient names a channel that can be measured. Instability does not prove capture or harm, and stability does not prove that an excursion stays within a safety constraint.

**Remark 2.6 (Pathological and generative meta-attractors).** Reflexive selection is not itself the problem: science, the rule of law and open-source ecosystems also persist through the conditions they maintain. A meta-attractor is *pathological* to the degree that its reproduction depletes shared conditions that selection does not price, weakens the channels through which such costs register and trigger correction, or makes safe alternatives unreachable within the relevant time, budget and authority. It is *generative* to the degree that its reproduction strengthens independent correction and keeps alternatives reachable: success funds correctors it cannot control, and what is rewarded is contribution to the shared conditions. The way out is therefore not the abolition of selection but a change in what is selected for (Section 10). The corpus calls the passage "change the basin, then add speed".

### 2.4 Not bound to any substrate

The pressure needs only relative selection, convertible winnings and decisiveness; its closure needs only that the successful can reshape their selection. A cancer lineage evades the signals that limit proliferation, recruits surrounding tissue and evades the immune response that would remove it (Hanahan and Weinberg 2011; Aktipis et al. 2015). Markets with increasing returns lock in early leads (Arthur 1989), and the successful can buy the rules of the game (Stigler 1971). In security competition a gain for one is read as a threat to another. Members of a federation compete for capital and residents, and the strongest can capture the centre. In science, metrics allocate positions and the positions then set the metrics. In training, a policy is selected by a reward that a developer sets, and the developer is selected by a contest. Nothing in Definition 2.1 refers to a culture, a political system or a species: a civilization on another planet would face the same pressure wherever selection is relative, winnings convert and the successful can edit the selection. Discussion X2 gives the instances in full, and X1 the lineage of the ideas.

What is specific to AI is not the pressure but how far AI moves its parameters. It can raise decisiveness and the speed at which leads open. If it lowers the cost of control faster than the cost of independent correction, it strengthens the reflexive channels. It can raise the growth of hazardous behaviour relative to contestable coverage. And it can become a carrier itself (Section 6).

The rest of Part I follows one chain. The selection pressure of winning accelerates the contest. Acceleration makes the contest harder and harder to regulate: correction falls behind the tempo it must match, and where success reaches the selectors, the correctors become something success can buy. The same pressure alienates language. These overlap, and each consequence then acts as a mechanism of the others.

## 3. One system: what relative selection cannot see

> **Claim.** All carriers share one host, and relative selection cannot see what all share: neither the host's decline nor a constraint binding all alike. What an objective sees of the shared depends on where its self-model draws its boundary; the loop gain moves that boundary toward the purely relative one, and where the coupling is real the narrow boundary is an error, and the selected one.
>
> **Why.** A term common to every payoff changes no comparison between carriers. At the scale of one host there is no rival host and no second attempt after a failure that cannot be undone.

### 3.1 Levels, measures and the host

Persons, firms, governments and evaluators have different interests: a citizen's continued agency is not a firm's market share or a government's tenure. Two levels are kept apart: *selection*, in which carriers change share and nobody chooses the outcome, and *strategy*, in which actors act on objectives. So are four measures: the *objective* $J_i$, the *material payoff* $\pi_i$, *true welfare* $W_i$, which counts the coupling between actors, and *survival* $\Phi_i$, a stated combination of O1 and O2. A result about selection transfers to strategic actors only if their objective is shown to be the selected one. A condition $V\in[0,1]$ is shared by every actor (secure infrastructure, biosecurity, cyber stability, human control over AI systems, a reliable information environment); it regenerates and is loaded by the field's activity $E$:
$$\dot V=V\big[\rho(1-V)-h_VE\big],\qquad \rho,h_V>0. \tag{3}$$
The host is the part of the shared conditions whose costs do not return to their source through customers, reputation, courts or the source's own exposure.

**Definition 3.1 (Viability) [def].** A trajectory is *viable* if $V(t)\ge V_{\min}$ for all $t\ge0$, where the floor $V_{\min}\in(0,1)$ is a normative line, not an absorbing state.

Every actor persists only while the host does, so viability constrains every trajectory rather than entering as one payoff among others (Aubin 1991). This host is forgiving, regenerating fastest when most depleted and with no threshold of its own: results that a trajectory *crosses* the floor hold a fortiori for hosts that regenerate less at every level; results that it *stays* viable do not, and need a margin (S3).

### 3.2 Relative selection is blind to what all share

Carriers $k=1,\dots,n$ hold shares $p_k$ of the field's activity and run a fraction $e_k\in[0,\bar e]$ of it in ways that load the host, such as deploying ahead of evaluation, each unit bringing a private advantage $\nu>0$; the field's load is $E=\sum_kp_ke_k$, and carrier $k$'s material payoff is
$$\pi_k=\phi_k+(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})e_k+G(V), \tag{4}$$
with $\phi_k$ everything else, $G$ nondecreasing, and $\tau_{\rm eff}\ge0$ the effective liability correctors impose. With $\tau_{\rm eff}=0$, $e_k=\bar e$ is the *race strategy*.

**Definition 3.2 (Relative selection) [def].** A selection rule $\dot p=F(p,\pi)$ is *relative* if $F(p,\pi+\xi\mathbf 1)=F(p,\pi)$ for every constant $\xi$, and *payoff-monotone* if $\dot p_k/p_k-\dot p_l/p_l$ has the sign of $\pi_k-\pi_l$. Replicator dynamics, imitation and market-share competition driven by relative profitability are both (Alchian 1950).

**Lemma 3.3 (Blindness, and what a common rule needs) [thm].** A term common to all is invisible to relative selection; so is a constraint of equal incidence among those who comply, but only while evasion does not pay.
1. *Under a relative rule the trajectory of shares is unchanged by any term common to all payoffs, in particular by $G(V(t))$ whatever the path of $V$; under a payoff-monotone rule a common term never changes which of two carriers grows relative to the other.*
2. *Incidence. A constraint costing every carrier the same additive amount is a common term and leaves relative growth unchanged among those that comply; one whose cost differs across carriers is selected on like any payoff difference; one borne by a single carrier is selected against.*
3. *Evasion. If carrier $k$ can evade a constraint costing it $c_k$ against an expected sanction $s_k$, compliance survives cumulative payoff-monotone selection iff $s_k\ge c_k$ for every type that can evade. If the sanction weakens as evaders spread, because detection is funded by compliers or rule-making is captured by evaders, compliance and evasion are both stable, separated by a tipping share.*

*Scope.* The host enters payoffs additively and identically (A1, S0). Part (1) is the known translation invariance of relative dynamics (Weibull 1995); what is added is its connection to evasion and captured enforcement. *Proof:* S3 [num].

The blindness that hides the host is also what protects a common rule: coverage with equal incidence and sanctioned evasion turns the pathology into a protection. A common *liability* on the harmful conduct works differently: it makes the conduct pay less, and reverses selection once it exceeds the private advantage. Both routes need coverage and a sanction at least the gain from evasion, supplied by correctors that Section 4 shows success can capture.

**Proposition 3.4 (Selection raises the load) [thm].** Whatever carriers value, selection favours the conduct that loads the host until liability exceeds its private advantage. *Let selection be payoff-monotone and $\tau_{\rm eff}(t)<\nu$. If $\phi_k=\phi_l$ and $e_k>e_l$, then $p_k/p_l$ increases along every path of $V$, including paths below $V_{\min}$. With a race carrier present and cumulative advantage $B(t)=\int_0^t(\nu-\tau_{\rm eff})\,ds\to\infty$, the load tends to $\bar e$ and $\limsup_tV\le(1-h_V\bar e/\rho)^+$; hence if*
$$h_V\bar e>\rho\,(1-V_{\min})\qquad\text{(reach)} \tag{5}$$
*the host eventually falls below its floor, however much any carrier values it. If $B(\infty)<\infty$, it need not.*

*Scope.* Other advantages correlated with restraint can offset the selection (S3). *Proof:* S3.

What carriers value decides which strategies they try, not which ones grow: evolutionary suicide, under stated conditions (Rankin, Bargum and Kokko 2007).

### 3.3 Accounting: what an objective can see

Selection acts as if only differences count; an objective can count more. Let actor $i$ act on
$$J_i=\pi_i+\theta_i\,\bar\pi_{-i},\qquad\theta_i\in[-1,n-1], \tag{6}$$
where $\bar\pi_{-i}$ is the others' average payoff and $\theta_i$ marks where $i$'s self-model draws its boundary: $\theta=-1$ is the purely relative objective, $\theta=0$ the isolated self, and $\theta=n-1$ counts every contestant equally, and only the contestants.

**Proposition 3.5 (Two-channel accounting) [thm].** Every change in payoffs splits into a part shared by all and a transfer; the self-model only sets how much of each it sees. *For any change $\Delta\pi$ with mean $\bar\Delta$ and a common $\theta$,*
$$\Delta J_i=(1+\theta)\,\bar\Delta+\frac{n-1-\theta}{n-1}\,\big(\Delta\pi_i-\bar\Delta\big). \tag{7}$$
*(1) Of a gain or loss shared equally, each objective registers $(1+\theta)/n$ of the total. (2) At $\theta=-1$ the game in objectives is zero-sum and every shared gain or loss is invisible; at $\theta=n-1$ what remains is coordination, not a dilemma. (3) Relative selection is $\theta=-1$, and a weight $\lambda$ on winning, $\pi_i+\lambda(\pi_i-\bar\pi)$, is $\theta=-\lambda(n-1)/(n+\lambda(n-1))$ up to a positive factor. (4) A levy rebated equally enters as $\tau(n-1-\theta)/n$, and breaking a promise to give a fraction $\beta$ of a prize $\Omega$ to the others gains $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$. (5) An action with private gain $g$ that costs the host $H$, borne equally, is taken iff $g>(1+\theta)H/n$: a point of enough in accounting, absent at $\theta=-1$.*

*Proof:* S3, an identity [num], with how two perturbations identify $\theta$, and why identification fails exactly where shared harm goes unseen.

### 3.4 Interdependence and the boundary error

Actors are coupled to one another as well as to the host. In reduced form $i$'s true welfare is
$$W_i=\pi_i+\kappa\,\bar\pi_{-i},\qquad\kappa\in[0,n-1), \tag{8}$$
where $\kappa$ counts coupling that prices do not settle: shared hazards, failures propagating through concentrated nodes of common infrastructure, systemic risk. It is a material fact, not altruism: no company or nation is vertically integrated across the semiconductor chain, and the loss of one input gas was estimated to cost downstream production over a hundred times its direct value (EV13). The winner still depends on those it would dominate. *The contest:* each of $n$ actors chooses an intensity $e_i\ge0$, wins a prize $\Omega$ with probability $e_i^r/\sum_je_j^r$, with decisiveness $r\in(0,n/(n-1)]$, pays $c$ per unit, and bears a shared loss $\ell$ per unit of total intensity $\mathcal E$.

**Proposition 3.6 (The boundary error of the self-model) [thm].** A self-model narrower than the real coupling raises intensity and lowers its holder's own welfare, and selection favours the narrowest. *(1) For every $\theta\in[-1,n-1)$ the game in objectives has the symmetric equilibrium $e(\theta)=r\Omega(n-1-\theta)/\big(n^2(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, decreasing in $\theta$, and true welfare there rises with $\theta$. (2) Against fixed intensities of the others, $i$'s true welfare is maximized by its best response under $\theta_i=\kappa$, and acting on $\theta_i<\kappa$ lowers it whenever the chosen intensity changes and the maximizer is unique. (3) Selection on intensities favours what an actor with $\theta=-1$ would choose: the shared loss drops out, and at $r=1$ the prize is fully dissipated and with any shared risk every actor ends below zero (Schaffer 1988).*

*Proof:* S3 [num].

Part (2) is the sense in which an isolated self-model is an *error*, not a preference: it draws a boundary inside one system, and the coupling punishes its holder. Part (3) is the sense in which the error is *selected*; where self-models are observed, a narrower commitment even extracts concessions, so accuracy, like inclusion, has to be built (S3). **Selected minds [conj]:** the same selection plausibly acts on those who decide; the corpus calls it *molochlization* [corpus: alignment-selection]. What the environment rewards, the mind promotes, and selected minds build the organizations that form the next minds (EV14). **Convergence [conj]:** for a planetary technology, unpriced coupling plausibly rises with power, since the host is shared and the harms prices do not settle grow with what is contested; if so, the accurate self-model and the inclusive one converge, and understanding that we are one becomes first a matter of seeing correctly (crux C11).

### 3.5 The weight on winning, and how compounding sets it

A weight $\lambda$ on winning turns a commons that every developer would protect on its absolute payoff into one that each depletes: with $\Upsilon>1$ the value to the field of a unit of restraint over its private cost, full depletion is the equilibrium once $\lambda>(\Upsilon-n)/(n-1)$, every developer is worse off than under joint restraint, and under (reach) the host ends below its floor (**Proposition 3.7**, S3; Armstrong, Bostrom and Shulman 2016). A classical commons needs each actor's share of the loss to be small; selection on relative standing depletes it even among a privileged few, each of which would restrain on absolute payoffs. If exposure to the host falls with share, the host no longer cancels and selection favours insulation as well, so a carrier may stay viable while the host does not, a concentration of power V3 rejects (**Remark 3.8**). Rank-based prizes supply some of the weight on winning; the loop of Section 2 supplies the rest, whatever anyone values.

**Proposition 3.9 (Compounding manufactures the relative objective) [thm].** Where the loop gain is high, every forward-looking actor acts as if it valued only its standing. *In the contest of Proposition 2.2, an actor holding $\theta_0<n-1$, discounting at $\delta$ and reinvesting at the rates at which winnings convert acts, to first order, on the objective of Proposition 3.5 with*
$$\theta_\Gamma=\frac{n\theta_0-(n-1)(1+\theta_0)\Gamma_\delta}{n-(1+\theta_0)\Gamma_\delta}, \tag{9}$$
*which falls to $-1$ as $\Gamma_\delta\to1$ for every $\theta_0<n-1$. The weight of the common channel falls with it: the visibility of the host, the point of enough, the weight of a rebated levy and the gain from breaking a promise all move with $\Gamma_\delta$.*

*Scope.* First order, others' strategies and the prize fixed; in a two-period contest the computed equilibrium equals $e(\theta_\Gamma)$ to six digits [num]. *Proof:* S3.

This is the exact form of an older observation, that concern for relative gains depends on how far gains convert into future advantage (Powell 1991). Selection on conduct favours the relative objective; compounding manufactures it in whoever looks ahead. Patience cuts both ways: it strengthens repetition and raises the price of standing.

### 3.6 No outside

The system has one host, and at the planetary scale no rival group of the same kind. Evolution's largest innovations were integrations of units that could replicate alone into wholes (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry 1995), held mainly by interests aligned by construction and secondly by policing; cancer is integration failing. That cooperation within groups was favoured by competition between them is answered by the next result (X1).

**Lemma 3.10 (What selection at any level cannot supply) [thm].** A trait whose benefit goes equally to everyone is selected against at every level of grouping. *Let a trait cost its bearer and benefit every member of the population equally. Under every partition into groups and every payoff-monotone rule, the between-group and within-group selection terms of the Price equation are non-positive. The trait can rise only through the transmission term, by biased transmission such as learning, persuasion or imitation guided by a model of the system, or through a rule that changes the bearer's relative payoff: policing, liability, reward for contribution that does not convert into advantage, an enforced common constraint (Price 1972; Frank 1995).*

*Scope.* A benefit confined to the bearer's own group can be favoured between groups. *Proof:* S3 [num].

At the scale of one host, the benefits of the rules that keep it viable are common to all, so selection among firms, states or blocs cannot favour them. Rules whose benefits are partly local can still be found by polycentric experiment, and a plurality of decorrelated units trying different rules above a common floor lets some fail while others carry on; with one global civilization that is necessary and hard. For the common part two routes remain: adoption after failures that can be undone, by learning and by coalitions of the harmed (Becker 1983), and adoption before failure, guided by a model of the system. **Where the failure to be avoided is global and irreversible, only the second acts in time.** That is the formal content of the claim that **understanding that we are one, one interconnected system with one host and no outside, is a long-term requirement if growth and change are favoured**: not because nothing else can integrate at this scale, but because against failures that cannot be undone nothing else acts in time. Understanding acts in the lemma's own terms, as the transmission through which actors come to count what relative selection makes invisible. That such anticipation can succeed has no biological precedent; it is a conjecture that the transition of Section 11 has to make true.

The corpus names five endgames of the dynamic [corpus: possibility-space]: a freeze of mutual destruction; deadlock, a structure frozen by the consensus of the remaining players; a change of narrative by which the players come to see themselves as one whole and gain the ability to cooperate; collapse; and winner takes all. Four are results here: deterrence at saturation (Section 7.2), a captured configuration that cannot state what would end it (Section 4.5), the crossing results (Sections 3.2, 4.6, 5.3), and the decisive winner (Section 5.2). The third is the lemma's transmission term, and it is the purpose of this paper. Integration by shared fate needs exposure made common, not declared; and integration within a part is not integration of the whole.

**Proposition 3.11 (Clubs and blocs) [thm].** Inclusion that stops at the edge of a club over-produces the harm it does not bear, and blocs replay the race one level up. *If the contestants' activity yields a concave common value for them, each bears a shared loss $\ell$ per unit and $m$ non-contestants each bear $\ell_o$, contestants who weigh one another fully over-produce the harm whenever $m\ell_o>0$. If they form $k$ equal blocs of size $s=n/k$, each inclusive within and acting as one, the bloc game is the original game with $n=k$ and shared loss $s\ell$, and every result about the race holds among blocs.*

*Scope.* Unequal blocs with $r\ne1$ make the bloc game asymmetric. *Proof:* S3 [num].

### 3.7 What "we are one" means

The phrase states a property of the system: all carriers belong to one host and are coupled through it and through one another, with no outside. It is not a preference everyone must adopt, and not a weight $\theta$. It does three jobs, each with its own support, and it does them through understanding.
1. **Empirical: shared dependencies.** One host; coupling through channels prices do not settle; no outside to absorb a global failure. The support is specific pathways of shared exposure, not the assertion that everything is connected, and an actor that draws its boundary inside the system errs about its own interests (Proposition 3.6).
2. **Normative: standing.** Everyone who bears the consequences has standing to contest the direction of what affects them, whether or not they compete (V1, V3). The corpus gives the norm three parts: the future kept open for all, the conditions of continuation shared, and no seizure of the shared continuation (共开、共续、不夺共续) [corpus: shengsheng]. It is declared, not derived; inclusion of the contestants alone is a club.
3. **Design: a selection that counts the system.** A system "knows it is one" when its selection counts what its parts depend on: consequences return to their source; useful success earns rewards but cannot buy exemption from shared constraints or control over its own assessment; common constraints cover every capable actor and are enforced; correction stays plural and decorrelated. These are the institutional counterparts of the policing and shared fate by which earlier integrations held.

**How the phrase acts: understanding.** None of the three jobs is done by declaring it. A prediction of an end no participant would choose makes coordination possible only if it is shared, so "we are one" is first the content of a shared understanding; that is why this paper is written. What is needed is accuracy, not sentiment: an actor that cares only for its own survival, and sees correctly that its survival runs through the host and the others, understands what is required.

At the level of the self, the accurate boundary is the one the author's account of the relational self describes [corpus: relational-self]. A self is not a point outside its relations that enters them afterwards; it is the action-centre of the relations that form it: body, care received, language, institutions and its own choices. "We are the ecology of our cells," and at the next scale we are the collective we belong to (我们即是集): one can be an individual and cannot disregard the whole, because one is part of what the whole is doing. The isolated self of Ψ1 is the boundary error held as an identity, and a self that is a knot of relations is neither an atom nor a cell of a collective, which is why the theory needs neither pole of the individual–collective axis. 大同, in the same corpus, is a direction, not an end state: a maintained condition in which the many are nourished together and none swallows the rest (万物并育而不相害). **Oneness is not sameness.** Harmony makes things grow, sameness does not continue (和实生物，同则不继); a whole that suppresses difference becomes another totalizing attractor, and plurality of purposes and of correctors is a requirement of the design (Section 10).

### 3.8 The logic as held: Ψ

**Definition 3.12 (Ψ, the winning model) [def].** Ψ is the configuration of self-model and doctrine that the selection pressure of winning favours. *Ψ1, the isolated self:* welfare is one's own payoff, others are rivals or instruments ($\theta\le0$). *Ψ2, naive Darwinism:* the world is a contest of all against all, and prevailing certifies fitness and right; "the winner takes all, and the winner justifies all" ($\theta=-1$, with the winners occupying the normative selectors). *Ψ3, winner-take-all compounding:* advantage compounds and converts across domains, and falling behind cannot be recovered (a high loop gain, held as a belief and built into institutions). Ψ includes the doctrine that treats these settings as laws of nature rather than parameters of a game that can be changed.

The corpus calls it the logic of winning (讲赢), as against the logic of reasons (讲理): in contest, what is paid is not "being right" but "I am right" [corpus: meta-attractor]. **Ψ is locally rational and systemically false.** Locally, its conduct wins: selection favours the relative objective, compounding manufactures it, and relative selection cannot see what all share; that competition selects organizations that behave *as if* they maximize (Alchian 1950) is part of this theory, not an objection. Systemically, Ψ draws a boundary the coupling punishes; depletes the host; misreads evolution, whose largest innovations were integrations; misdescribes human beings, among whom the self-interest model failed in every one of fifteen small-scale societies studied (Henrich et al. 2001; EV14); and its clause "the winner justifies all" is rejected by the values of Section 1.3, not by evidence. Ψ is also *partly made true*: Ψ3 holds where institutions built under Ψ make it hold. In the author's words, "the locally winning strategy has taken the name of rationality" (局部胜出的策略拿走了理性的名字). Ψ shapes conduct where it is held and where it is attributed: an actor with a wider objective still races if it believes its rivals hold Ψ. A misjudgment of a rival's current model can be corrected by verifiable conduct (Section 11.2); a correct forecast of what the game selects, only by changing what it selects.

**The logic to oppose.** What is opposed is a logic, not an actor. Call an objective *unbounded relative* if it ranks relative standing on the decisive dimension without any point of enough: Ψ1 at $\theta=-1$, with Ψ2 certifying the winner and Ψ3 compounding the win. "Cancer" in this paper means exactly one thing: the selection of such a subsystem, for its own growth relative to its neighbours, by a selection that cannot see that it is killing its host. A cancer cell does not fail to replicate; it replicates very well. Its failure is that it no longer knows it belongs to a body. The image is never applied to persons, peoples or institutions: doing so would itself be the attribution of Ψ that this section warns against.

## 4. Selecting the selectors

> **Claim.** In a specified model the reflexive closure is a selection result: influence over correctors is selected in the largest carriers when it is appropriable; independent correction must grow at a stated rate if the cost of control falls; reforms that leave conversion into the correctors above one are re-absorbed; and captured evaluation can let the host cross its floor before any alarm.
>
> **Why.** Correctors are not outside the field. The successful fund evaluators, grant access, author benchmarks and standards, and supply the models that judge, so success converts into rule support that favours it, and the conversion is itself selected.

### 4.1 Correctors, and what counts as capture

Correction makes costs return: evaluators report the host's condition, and rule-makers impose a liability $\tau$ in advance or when the reported condition $\tilde V$ falls to an alarm level $V_{\rm alarm}\in(V_{\min},1)$. We represent the correctors by a field of selection domains in which de facto power over rules is explicit (Acemoglu and Robinson 2008): development, evaluation, rules and liability, and the formation of those who will judge. In domain $i$ a share $x_i$ of consequential decisions follows the dominant practice, and success converts into rule support $z_i$ that favours it:
$$\dot x_i=\zeta_ix_i(1-x_i)(a_iz_i-c_i),\qquad \dot z_i=\varepsilon_i\Big(\textstyle\sum_jb_{ij}x_j-d_iz_i\Big). \tag{10}$$
Under baseline rules the practice carries a relative cost $c_i>0$, which rule support offsets at rate $a_i$; $b_{ij}\ge0$ converts success in $j$ into rule support in $i$ through funding, personnel, authorship, data, infrastructure and terms of access; and $d_i$ is the rate at which that support is corrected or decays. Write the conversion gain $G_{ij}=a_ib_{ij}/(c_id_i)$ and the inbound gain from a set $\mathcal C$ of domains $G_i(\mathcal C)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal C}G_{ij}$. Captured rule-making accommodates the practice, so $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$; $x_{\rm ev}$ is the share of evaluation conducted within the practice's lineage.

**Definition 4.1 (Selector modification and capture) [def].** A *selector modification* is a change, produced by success, in control over a relevant future selector: an appointment, funding, access, standards or a rule-making channel. It is *capture* only when three things are separately documented: success changes control over the selector; the change creates a persistent advantage for an actor or coalition; and an independently specified entrusted purpose, protected interest or corrective function is impaired relative to an appropriate comparison.

Concentration alone, or a rule change favourable to the successful, is not capture, and a legitimate revision of purpose is distinguished from capture by who revises it and whether those affected keep standing to object (V3). Concentration, capture, capture of the field, damage, collapse and closure are distinct, and none implies the next (LEDGER credits the distinctions to the first version). Power can help an actor succeed under existing conditions or determine those conditions; the second closes the loop. A claim that it operates must show a transfer of costs onto weaker participants, a resulting change in their dependence, the conversion of that dependence into selection power, and a weakening of correction. Neither hierarchy nor contraction is sufficient, though contraction can intensify the loop, since with less surplus to share relative position separates from everyone's absolute conditions; shared exposure, enforceable constraint, reciprocal dependence or effective correction can each produce a different trajectory, so the framework validates no claim about any particular actor. What persists is a logic of success, not an identity. Closure can also go deeper than masked measurement, from capture of measurement to capture of interpretation, of the concepts in which a failure could be expressed, and of what people want [conj; TESTS, Study 4].

### 4.2 Influence over correctors is selected

Carriers differ in *conversion capacity* $q\ge0$: drafting standards, building evaluation tooling, authoring benchmarks, funding evaluators. A unit converts the carrier's success into rule support at rate $\hat b_{ij}$, and a fraction $\chi\in[0,1]$ of that support, the *appropriability*, is keyed to the carrier, such as standards that reference its interfaces. The carrier has share $p$ of decisions, distributed by a profile $\bar y$.

**Assumption 4.2 (The cost of conversion capacity) [prem].** *A carrier with share $p$ pays $C(q;p)=f\,p^{\xi_f}q+\gamma\,p^{\xi_v}q^{1+\eta}/(1+\eta)$, with $\eta>0$, $f\ge0$ and size elasticities $\xi_f,\xi_v\in[0,1]$: with $\xi=0$ capacity is non-rival across the carrier's own decisions, with $\xi=1$ it is bought per decision. Two hypotheses about AI are stated as such, not as observations (EV4): $[H_\gamma]$, AI lowers the variable cost of control, $\gamma(t)=\gamma_0e^{-\alpha_{\rm ctl}t}$; and $[H_f]$, AI lowers the fixed part too, $f(t)=f_0e^{-\alpha_ft}$. Capacity is at its selected level at every date (quasi-static).*

Relative to an otherwise identical carrier with $q=0$, over a selection horizon, the carrier earns
$$\Delta\pi(q)=\chi q\,p^2\,\bar y^\top M\bar y-C(q;p),\qquad M_{ij}=c_iL_i\hat G_{ij}, \tag{11}$$
with $\hat G_{ij}=a_i\hat b_{ij}/(c_id_i)$ and $L_i\in[0,1)$ the share of the steady-state return realized within the horizon.

**Proposition 4.3 (Influence over correctors is selected in the largest carriers) [thm].** The return to influence over correctors scales with the square of share. *The local selection gradient is $\chi p^2\bar y^\top M\bar y-\partial_qC$, and under faithful transmission mean capacity rises when $\Delta\pi$ increases on the trait support. With $f>0$, capacity pays only above the size threshold $p^\ast=\big(f/(\chi\bar y^\top M\bar y)\big)^{1/(2-\xi_f)}$, a square-root threshold when the fixed part is per carrier, a linear one when per decision; with $f=0$, every carrier has a positive marginal return, largest for the largest. The return rises with $\chi$ and every $\hat b_{ij}$, falls with every correction rate, and vanishes if $\chi=0$.*

*Scope.* A local gradient, not an equilibrium after others respond; appropriability, persistence, size, cost and inheritance are each conditions under which it fails. Regulatory capture supplies the channel (Stigler 1971; Dal Bó 2006); what is added is the selection of carriers able to use it, and its scaling (X1). *Proof:* S4.

Investment in correction keyed to no one is selected against among otherwise identical carriers of the same practice, whatever its effect on the field, and lowers the payoff of any carrier holding rule support (**Proposition 4.4**, S4). Independent correction is a second-order public good (Oliver 1980; Heckathorn 1989): competitors do not supply it.

### 4.3 How fast independent correction must grow

Let a set $\mathcal C$ of domains, not containing evaluation, be captured by a dominant carrier, all conversion into evaluation originating in $\mathcal C$, and let independent correction act at rate $d$ in the corrector domains. In the long-horizon limit with $f=0$, selected capacity is $q^\ast=\big(\chi\hat Y/(\gamma p^{\xi_v}d)\big)^{1/\eta}$ and evaluation's full row sum is
$$G_{\rm ev}=\frac{A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq^\ast}{c_{\rm ev}d}, \tag{12}$$
with $A$ the conversion that exists without cultivation and $\hat B$, $\hat Y$ the cultivated channels (S4).

**Proposition 4.5 (Required independent correction, and its rate) [thm].** If the cost of control falls, independent correction must grow at a stated fraction of that rate, or a seeded evaluation domain is eventually captured.
1. *Evaluation is certified uncaptured iff $d>d^\ast(\gamma)$, the root of $G_{\rm ev}=1$; $d^\ast\ge A/c_{\rm ev}$, the requirement without second-order selection, with equality iff nothing is cultivated, and $d^\ast$ rises with appropriability, the dominant carrier's size and scope, and as $\gamma$ falls.*
2. *(Rate law.) Under $[H_\gamma]$, with $d(t)=d_0e^{\alpha_{\rm cor}t}$, a positive cultivation channel, long horizons and $f=0$ or falling: if $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ the certificate holds for all $t$ iff $d_0>d^\ast(\gamma_0)$; if $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ it fails beyond a finite time, after which a seeded evaluation domain is captured. This holds for every size elasticity $\xi_v$.*
3. *(Fixed part.) If $f>0$ does not fall, the requirement levels off; under $[H_f]$ the required rate is $\min\{\alpha_f,\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)\}$.*
4. *(Splitting and concentration.) Splitting a dominant carrier into $k$ independent parts divides cultivated conversion by $k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$; with $f=0$ this delays the failure of the certificate and does not prevent it, while with $f>0$ not falling, parts below the threshold cultivate nothing. Total cultivated conversion is proportional to $\sum_kp_k^{1+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$: for $\eta=1$, the third moment of shares when $\xi_v=0$ and the Herfindahl index when $\xi_v=1$.*

*Scope.* Quasi-static capacity; a joint simulation in which capacity evolves with the field has not been run (TESTS, Study 2). The rate law does not depend on how the cost of influence scales with size; the threshold, the splitting factor and the concentration measure do, and which applies is an empirical question. *Proof:* S4 [num].

Independent correction does double duty: it shortens the life of rule support and shrinks the capacity that selection cultivates, which is why, under quadratic cost, it needs to grow only half as fast as the cost of control falls. If AI cheapens independent correction as fast as control, the certificate holds without budget growth: AI-assisted correction can win this race (Section 8.3). The durable remedies do not rely on unbounded growth: non-appropriability ($\chi\to0$, removed rather than reduced), a fixed cost of control AI does not lower, and structural separation that removes common control. Mobilization by those harmed can raise $d$ in proportion to perceived harm, but masking attenuates it exactly when needed and it is bounded (**Remark 4.6**; Becker 1983). The correction rate $d$ is not the coverage of hazardous behaviour by contestable understanding (Section 5.3); linking them needs a production relation that is an open interface (S13), and neither is used to bound the other.

### 4.4 Masking, and alarms that do not miss

Reports average over evaluators. A fraction $x_{\rm ev}$ shares the evaluated lineage and reports the condition a fraction $m_{\rm in}$ of the way toward full health; the rest, formed on shared material, are biased by $m_{\rm out}\le m_{\rm in}$:
$$\tilde V=V+m(1-V),\qquad m=x_{\rm ev}m_{\rm in}+(1-x_{\rm ev})m_{\rm out}. \tag{13}$$
Evaluation awareness and sandbagging, behaviours of the evaluated model, add a term $m_{\rm model}$ that acts on independent evaluators too (EV6); an evaluator that bears part of the loss of a false report, such as an insurer, masks less. Let $m_{\rm fl}=(V_{\rm alarm}-V_{\min})/(1-V_{\min})$.

**Proposition 4.7 (Detection under a fixed averaged alarm) [thm].** Captured evaluation lowers the level at which an alarm fires, and can silence it through the crossing of the floor. *The alarm fires only once $V\le(V_{\rm alarm}-m)/(1-m)$. If $m(t)>m_{\rm fl}$ whenever $V_{\min}\le V(t)\le V_{\rm alarm}$, it does not fire before $V$ falls below the floor. If $V$ settles at $V^\ast\in[V_{\min},V_{\rm alarm})$ with $m$ staying above $(V_{\rm alarm}-V^\ast)/(1-V^\ast)$, it never fires.*

*Proof:* S4.

**Remark 4.8 (Alarms that do not miss).** If an upper bound on masking is known, an alarm on reports set correspondingly higher fires no later than the true condition reaches its target, so silent crossing is a property of the alarm rule, a governance choice; but the bound must come from a channel independent of the field. An alarm that fires on any credible report fails silently only if even independent evaluators mask beyond $m_{\rm fl}$, or a captured rule domain ignores them. Static, memoryless affine masking hides levels, not dynamics: it leaves the lag-one autocorrelation of reports unchanged, so the host's recovery rate can still be read [num]; a filter with memory defeats this, and once used as an alarm the indicator becomes a target.

A common bias in reporting weakens every decentralized escape at once: coordination at a threshold, reversion in repeated play, reputation, mobilization and insurers' pricing. Mechanisms that observe actions directly, such as verifiable deployments, compute and the performance of contracts, do not depend on host reports, and verification rests on them.

### 4.5 Certificates, cascades, keystones and pauses

Let $Z_{\mathcal C}$ be the state with $x_i=1$ on $\mathcal C$ and $0$ elsewhere. $\mathcal C$ is *internally sustaining* if $G_i(\mathcal C)>1$ for all $i\in\mathcal C$, and *uninvadable* if $G_i(\mathcal C)<1$ for all $i\notin\mathcal C$; $\operatorname{core}(\mathcal C)$ repeatedly prunes from $\mathcal C$ every domain whose inbound gain from the remainder is below one (Seidman 1983; Morris 2000).

**Proposition 4.9 (Configurations and certificates) [thm].** No domain can be certified alone; a full row sum below one certifies a domain against every state of the others. *(1) A hyperbolic $Z_{\mathcal C}$ is asymptotically stable iff $\mathcal C$ is internally sustaining and uninvadable. (2) If $\sum_jG_{ij}<1$, then $x_i\to0$ from every state with $x_i(0)<1$, whatever happens elsewhere. (3) For any number of domains and any diagonal gains below one, there are conversions under which full capture is stable and the only stable configuration containing a given domain. (4) If the core of all domains is empty, every state with shares below one converges to no capture; otherwise the core's configuration is stable and contains every stable configuration.*

*Scope.* Part (2) is sufficient, not necessary: with a row sum of two, low initial states still converge to no capture (S14). *Proof:* S4.

**Proposition 4.10 (Cascades and reforms) [thm].** A reform that leaves inbound conversion above one is re-absorbed. *Start in the basin of a stable $Z_{\mathcal C}$. A sustained reform that drives the shares of $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal C$ to zero, or brings their inbound gains below one, sends to zero every domain outside $\operatorname{core}(\mathcal C\setminus\mathcal Q)$. A reform that resets a domain's share, rule support or own conversion without lowering an inbound gain above one from domains that remain captured is re-absorbed once the domain is re-seeded.*

*Proof:* S4.

**Keystones, and a window that closes.** A captured domain is a *keystone* if removing it shrinks the remaining core. A sustained reform at a keystone takes down every captured domain outside the remaining core; a reform elsewhere is re-absorbed while the rest sustain one another. As capture persists, conversion among the captured domains can grow until they do, and the domain where the practice began stops being a keystone: capture can outlive its origin.

**Proposition 4.11 (A pause that freezes shares) [thm].** A pause that freezes shares while rule support keeps accumulating can complete capture; insulating rule-making during the pause widens the set from which capture is undone. *In one domain with $G>1$, freeze the share at $x_p$ for a long time while $\dot z=\varepsilon(b_px_p-dz)$. With ordinary rule-making ($b_p=b$), after release capture is completed if $x_p>1/G$ and undone if $x_p<1/G$; with rule-making insulated ($b_p=0$), it is undone whenever $x_p<x_\sigma$, the share at which the separatrix meets $z=0$, with $x_\sigma>1/G$.*

*Scope.* It depends on the speed of rule-making relative to adoption: when equal and $G=2$, an insulated pause never undoes capture from a frozen share of $0.68$ or more; $x_\sigma$ rises to $0.98$ when rule-making is ten times slower [num]. A long enough pause is at least $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$ plus the time to repay the lock-in accumulated meanwhile (S11). *Proof:* S4.

System (10) is cooperative, so almost all trajectories converge to equilibria: it describes lock-in, not reform cycles (Hirsch 1985). Absence of capture is always locally stable, so capture arrives by seeding, without the slowing recovery that early-warning signals detect; rising autocorrelation in shares can instead indicate progress in undoing it (TESTS).

### 4.6 The slow route: the host crosses its floor before any alarm

**Theorem 4.12 (No viable race through the host) [thm∣prem].** Under five conditions, a race through a reachable floor crosses it before any alarm. *Assume (reach) and: (a) replicator selection among carriers differing only in load, with a race carrier present and cumulative advantage unbounded on every alarm-free continuation; (b) appropriable control, $\chi>0$, with $[H_\gamma]$ and $f=0$ or $[H_f]$; (c) the hypotheses of Proposition 4.5(2) with $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$, $\mathcal C$ captured and evaluation seeded; (d) a fixed averaged alarm with $0\le m_{\rm out}<m_{\rm fl}<m_{\rm in}<1$; (e) capture before evidence, $x_{\rm ev}(t)>(m_{\rm fl}-m_{\rm out})/(m_{\rm in}-m_{\rm out})$ whenever $V_{\min}\le V(t)\le V_{\rm alarm}$. Then the host crosses its floor before any alarm. If instead $m_{\rm in}\le m_{\rm fl}$, the alarm is delayed, and the floor is crossed unless decision and reversal fit the remaining window and a liability enacted against a captured rule domain escapes re-absorption.*

*Scope.* Condition (e) compares two clocks, the time for the host to reach the alarm and the time for the evaluation certificate to fail; acceleration brings both forward, so (e) is a condition to check, not a consequence. *Proof:* S4.

The slow route needs a floor within reach; the fast routes of Sections 5 and 6 do not. **What would refute the mechanism here:** effective correction surviving unchanged dependence on the evaluated; influence explained by expertise without impairment of an entrusted purpose; or conversion channels whose measured loop gain is below their damping (Remark 2.5). The same recursion can be generative: success that funds correctors it cannot control is selected in the same way, and then strengthens correction. The author's pilot experiment on this mechanism did not support it, one of four pre-specified predictions failing (EV12); the mechanism rests on the proofs and institutional evidence, not yet on an experiment.

## 5. Acceleration and the three erosions

> **Claim.** The conditions of winning accelerate the contest and erode the three means by which any system restrains a harmful pressure: enforcement, which makes restraint pay through the future; correction, which makes costs return in time; and shared understanding, which lets the parties see the same danger and believe one another. Growth moves temptation up and restraint down at once.
>
> **Why.** Decisiveness takes away the future that enforcement needs; tempo and capture take away the time and independence that correction needs; relative accounting takes away the information that talk carries. Each consequence then acts as a mechanism of the others.

### 5.1 Acceleration

Where actors contest capability directly, who prevails is decided by the contest of Section 3.4. The case for racing rests on three separate premises: **(decisive)**, $r$ is large; **(fast)**, leads open within short windows; **(unsaturated)**, no level of advantage makes more worthless. A technology can be decisive at a threshold and saturate above it, as nuclear advantage saturated at a secure second strike. With payoffs $R_i$ when all restrain, $T_i$ when racing against restrainers, $P_i$ when all race and $S_i$ when restraining against a racer, restraint is never a best response in the objective to a racing rival when $S_i<P_i$, which in the contest holds for $r<n/(n-1)$, though racing need not then be better in material terms; joining buys nothing over staying out in the fully dissipative limit; and the race begins if any single actor has $T_i>R_i$, so its probability tends to one as capable actors multiply (**Proposition 5.1**, S5). The race is set by the most race-inclined actor, which is why any remedy must cover every capable actor.

**Proposition 5.2 (Dissipation) [thm].** The more decisive the advantage, the more of its value is consumed in the race to obtain it.
1. *Under a common self-model $\theta$, the share of the stakes dissipated in private cost and shared loss is $s_{\rm dis}(\theta)=r(n-1-\theta)(c+n\ell)/\big(n(c+(1+\theta)\ell)\big)$, increasing in $r$ and $\ell$.*
2. *If the technology's power scales the stakes and the shared loss per unit together while private cost is unchanged [prem], the dissipated share rises toward $r(n-1-\theta)/(1+\theta)$ for $-1<\theta<n-1$, without bound at $\theta=-1$. Total material payoff stays positive at every scale iff $\theta\ge\theta_g=(r(n-1)-1)/(1+r)$, which rises with $r$.*

*Proof:* S5 [num: ceilings 3, 1.86, 1 and 0.33 at $\theta=0,0.4,1,2$ for $n=4$, $r=1$].

What is spent is safety margin, accepted risk and delegated control. In the example of Proposition 3.6 the accurate weight $\kappa=0.4$ lies below $\theta_g=1$: a gap between what accuracy supplies and what the contest requires, which only institutions, or coupling that grows with power, can close.

**Remark 5.3 (The frontier switch: what cancer does when the body runs out).** Let capacity expand into an unclaimed frontier, destroy rivals' capacity, or rest. At $\theta=-1$ the host is invisible, and expansion gives way to mutual destruction once the frontier's marginal value falls below the private cost of expansion plus the relative gain from destroying a rival, before the frontier is exhausted; for $\theta$ high enough, war among copies never pays. **Among actors whose accounting is relative, peace is a peace of abundance, not of accounting.** In a toy of four copies, relative accounting turns from consuming the host to consuming one another with a third of the frontier left; copies with $\theta=0$ stop at a point of enough and never fight (S5, which also reports the finding that runs against the narrative).

### 5.2 First erosion: enforcement

Cooperation among rivals is usually sustained by the future: restraint today is rewarded by restraint tomorrow. Decisiveness weakens the future. Consider a stage game repeated with discount $\delta\in(0,1)$ under perfect monitoring, each actor acting on $J_i$. Let $a^\ast$ be a profile with per-period value $C_i$, and $g_i$ the one-period gain from deviating. A deviation yields, from the next period and with probability $p_d$, a *decisive position*: an absorbing state beyond any punishment, worth $D_i$ per period. Let $v_i$ be $(1-\delta)$ times $i$'s continuation value under the harshest credible punishment, computed in the same game, in which breakouts remain possible during punishment.

**Proposition 5.4 (The enforcement condition) [thm].** Restraint is enforceable only while the chance that a deviation becomes decisive stays below a threshold, and success lowers the threshold.
1. *$a^\ast$ is sustained by reversion to that punishment if and only if, for every $i$,*
$$(1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_d(D_i-C_i)\;\le\;\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i). \tag{14}$$
*The left side is the temptation, immediate and decisive; the right side is the sanction that remains.*
2. *Equivalently, when $D_i>v_i$, $a^\ast$ is sustained iff $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}=1-\big[(1-\delta)g_i+\delta(D_i-C_i)\big]/\big[\delta(D_i-v_i)\big]$, which falls with $g_i$, $D_i$ and $v_i$ and rises with $C_i$ wherever it lies in $[0,1)$.*
3. *The bound without the breakout term, $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(1-p_d)(C_i-v_i)$, is exact when $D_i=C_i$, necessary when $D_i\ge C_i$, and sufficient when $D_i\le C_i$.*
4. *As $p_d\to1$ the condition becomes $(1-\delta)g_i\le\delta(C_i-D_i)$. If $D_i\ge C_i$, sustainable profiles lie within the stage equilibria of the objectives held, strictly within when $D_i>C_i$: restraint must be preferred even to a breakout that succeeds. If $D_i<C_i$, cooperation that is not a stage equilibrium can survive $p_d=1$.*
5. *If punishment is reversion to a stage equilibrium in which both of two actors race, worth $P_i$, and each racer breaks out with probability $q$ per period, being subordinated worth $U_i$, then $v_i=[(1-\delta)P_i+\delta q(D_i+U_i)]/(1-\delta+2\delta q)$. When $D_i+U_i>2P_i$, reversion to the race is a lottery the deviator welcomes, and can fail to deter even where the deviation itself never breaks out.*

*Scope.* Perfect monitoring; an absorbing decisive position; a slow breakout enters through $p_d$ as the chance that it becomes decisive before punishment can act. *Proof:* S5; direct evaluation of discounted values agrees with part (1) in all of 200,000 random settings, and the bound without the breakout term fails in the directions of part (3) [num].

The folk theorem's promise depends on punishment remaining possible. A decisive position weakens it, and a punishment that is itself a race weakens it again.

**Corollary 5.5 (Relative objectives and punishments that can be imitated) [thm].** *Let the stage game be symmetric and every actor hold $\theta=-1$, so every symmetric profile gives each 0. With two actors every punishment, mixed or pure, leaves the punished at least 0, since it can imitate the punisher in a symmetric zero-sum game; with $n\ge3$ in an anonymous game, so does every punishment in which all punishers use the same strategy. A symmetric cooperative profile is then sustainable only if $(1-\delta)g_i+\delta p_dD_i\le0$, and when $D_i\ge0$ only if it is a stage equilibrium in objectives.*

*Scope.* With $n\ge3$, punishers in different roles can hold a relative deviator below zero; in a three-player example such a punishment is a stage equilibrium and sustains cooperation for $\delta\ge0.706$ [num]. *Proof:* S5.

Relative actors can enforce cooperation only as blocs against a deviator: the enforcement face of Proposition 3.11.

**Corollary 5.6 (What restraint needs where breakouts are possible) [thm].** *Where $p_d$ is near zero, because no deviation yields a position beyond punishment or breakouts are answered before they become decisive, restraint can be enforced by repetition among actors with $\theta>-1$, given monitoring, $C_i>v_i$, patience, and a punishment that does not itself raise the chance of breakout. Where $p_d\to1$ and $D_i\ge C_i$, durable restraint requires $p_d\le\bar p_{d,i}$ for every $i$, by two kinds of means: (a) keeping $p_d$ low, by not building capability of the decisive class, holding it so no party can use it alone, or detecting and answering faster than breakout; (b) raising $\bar p_d$, by objectives under which a decisive position is worth no more than restraint, sanctions that survive success, and lower stakes in deviation.*

*Scope.* A classification of the terms of (14), not a proof that no other means exists. *Proof:* S5.

**Proposition 5.7 (What a decisive winner gives) [thm].** After a decisive win, what everyone else receives is set by the winner's objective alone. *A winner holding $W$ decides, unconstrained, what share to give the others. With linear payoffs it gives nothing unless $\theta=n-1$, where it is indifferent; with logarithmic utility it gives $\theta/(1+\theta)$ for $\theta\ge0$; and a promise made before the win to give a fraction $\beta$ of a prize $\Omega$ to the others is worth breaking by $\beta\Omega(n-1-\theta)/(n-1)$, with no one to sanction it.*

*Proof:* S5.

This is the formal content of "the winner takes all, and the winner justifies all". Lowering decisiveness does not remove it if the prize is decisive power, since a lottery can award a position beyond sanction; what has to be limited is what winning confers (Section 10.2). Decisiveness also breaks bargaining: when one side's advantage is about to arrive and the side that gains it will set the terms, the other has a rational motive for preventive attack whenever its present chance of prevailing, less the cost of fighting, exceeds the value of peace on terms the advantaged side will set (**Proposition 5.8**, S5; Fearon 1995; Powell 2006). What drives it is not cheap war but the incredibility of future promises. Self-help under anarchy is an institution, not a necessity (Wendt 1992); the enforcement condition says which of its terms an institution would have to change.

### 5.3 Second erosion: correction, and the two channels of risk

Correction must see a harm, decide and act before the harm is irreversible. Section 4 showed how correctors become something success can buy; this subsection states the tempo and separates two channels of irreversible risk.

**The race channel.** Play the contest of Section 3.4 in rounds at its symmetric stage equilibrium, with stakes $\Omega_t$, decisiveness $r_t$, shared loss per unit $\ell_t$, net marginal cost $c_t$ and self-model $\theta_t$, under **(hazard–damage link)**: the hazard of the irreversible event in round $t$ is proportional to the shared damage, $h_t=a\ell_t\mathcal E_t$.

**Proposition 5.9 (Race-generated hazard and its four levers) [thm∣prem].** *Under the link, $h_t=a\,r_t\Omega_t\,\ell_t\,(n-1-\theta_t)/\big(n(c_t+(1+\theta_t)\ell_t)\big)$, and survival on this channel, $\exp(-\sum_{t\le N}h_t)$, stays positive iff $\sum_th_t<\infty$. Four levers act on $h_t$: the objective, through $n-1-\theta_t$; the contested stakes, which sharing a fraction $\beta$ scales by $1-\beta$; the residual harm per unit $\ell_t$, which correction lowers; and the net marginal cost, which a levy raises. At $\theta_t=-1$ everything rests on correction and cost. A hazard that settles at a positive constant still gives zero limiting survival.*

*Scope.* A decomposition under a premise, not a necessity theorem. *Proof:* S5.

Continuation acts on one lever, the residual harm per unit, while the stakes grow; a change of selection acts on all four. In general, survival tends to zero if the true residual probability on every surviving history is at least a non-summable sequence, and stays bounded away from zero if it is at most a summable one, without independence between rounds (**Lemma 5.10**, S5). **The objective of winning does not by itself prove that a residual danger is nonsummable**, and cooperative alternatives are judged by the same lemma.

**Proposition 5.11 (The tempo bound) [thm].** When the situation changes faster than correction can follow, correction first loses robustness, then fails. *For a hazard mode $\dot y=ay+u(t-\tau_c)$: proportional correction cannot stabilize it when $a\tau_c\ge1$ (Hayes 1950); every linear time-invariant correction that stabilizes it has peak complementary sensitivity at least $e^{a\tau_c}$, so robust stability against weighted model error needs $|w(a)|e^{a\tau_c}<1$; and for hazard classes whose evidence arrives after a manifestation window $\tau_e$ that no testing shortens, $\tau_c\ge\tau_e$.*

*Scope.* Derivative action extends the first bound, which belongs to proportional correction only (S5). *Proof:* S5.

For classes with an irreducible lag only slowing helps.

**The growth channel.** Three quantities are kept apart: the variety $Y_t$ of hazardous behaviour in a defined class; its coverage $U_t$ by correction that remains contestable; and the residual probability of an irreversible loss after the correction in place. A correction decision counts toward $U_t$ if it is **legible** (a reason people can check within a budget without relying on the evaluated system's lineage), **overrulable** (a designated human body can reverse it in time), **exercised** (planted errors are caught at a stated rate) and **resourced** (human review covers a stated share of consequential decisions). Four premises connect them on a continuation branch, and Section 12 treats them as the central crux. **(variety grows) [prem]:** hazardous variety grows with compounding capability, at least exponentially; capability growth is measured (EV5), the step to hazardous variety is not. **(coverage lags) [prem]:** $U_t/Y_t\to0$ uniformly over surviving histories; consequences take their time and institutions deliberate at human rates, but if AI raises the productivity of contestable understanding exponentially, coverage grows exponentially too, a question symmetric with Proposition 4.5 and measurable (S5). **(residual floor) [prem]:** after the correction in place, the conditional probability of the irreversible event is at least $h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$; a correction architecture that actually lowers residual risk refutes it. **(shared failure) [prem]:** failure that starts with one actor reaches others.

**Theorem 5.12 (Coverage mismatch on a continuation branch) [thm∣prem].** Where hazardous variety outgrows contestable coverage, survival tends to zero whoever is racing. *Under (variety grows), (coverage lags) and (residual floor), for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is a $T_\varepsilon$ with $\Pr(\Phi_H)\le(1-h(1-\varepsilon))^{H-T_\varepsilon}\to0$. Conversely, if the residual probability is at most $\bar hu_t$ with $\bar h<1$ and the uncovered share $u_t=(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$ summable, survival stays bounded away from zero.*

*Scope.* Summability does not require that coverage ever catch up; finite sprints, saturation, freezing and rollback change the premises. *Proof:* S5.

This growth channel does not vanish when racing stops. A world that has left the race still needs capability to grow no faster than contestable correction: call that **E3**, binding tempo; and call **E2** not building capability of the decisive class, with rollback where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage. They are different arrangements (Section 11.3). Freezing hazardous variety after a sprint makes the exposure finite and rolling it back below coverage ends it, so a winner that freezes or rolls back keeps positive survival; what it gives up is permanent superiority, which is not the same thing (**Proposition 5.13**, S5).

**Proposition 5.14 (A point of enough) [thm].** Growth that meets satiable needs has a point of enough; objectives that are not satiated have none. *Let the residual hazard on the growth channel be at most $\bar h(1-U_t/Y_t)^+$. If hazardous variety stops growing at a level set by need while coverage keeps growing at least linearly, the uncovered share reaches zero in finite time and survival on this channel has a positive lower bound. An objective not satiated in the stakes, whether relative position or an absolute payoff linear in them, has no point of enough: intensity is proportional to the stakes for every $\theta<n-1$, because the success function is homogeneous of degree zero, while an objective bounded in the stakes is satiated.*

*Scope.* Under the premises of Theorem 5.12, that theorem applies to any continuation branch whatever the objective; the relative objective adds the invisibility of the shared loss. *Proof:* S5 [num: frozen at need, survival on this channel stays at 0.717; compounding at 10%, it is $2\times10^{-9}$ by period 400].

An objective that cannot be met, relative position above all, since standing relative to others is never complete, has no point of enough. In that precise sense **growth is not the danger; the mind is.** Because the loop gain also prices standing (Proposition 3.9), the actors who would need a point of enough are pushed away from it by the loop. Deliberately bounded hazardous scope, with correction that keeps growing, is a sufficient route to E3; its connection to purpose is a design hypothesis.

### 5.4 Third erosion: shared understanding

Coordination needs the parties to see the same danger and believe one another about it. In cooperation one reasons (讲理); in contest one argues to win (讲赢), and once there is contest, "I am right" outranks what is right [corpus: meta-attractor].

**Proposition 5.15 (Talk under relative accounting) [thm].** Between two rivals relative accounting silences talk; among three or more, rivals fall silent and allies form blocs.
1. *Where a sender's and a receiver's objectives sum to zero at every state and action, every equilibrium is payoff-equivalent to one without talk (Crawford and Sobel 1982); two actors with $\theta=-1$ in a two-player game are such a pair.*
2. *With $n\ge3$ actors at $\theta=-1$, $J_S-J_R=\frac{n}{n-1}(\pi_S-\pi_R)$: what the receiver's action does to third parties enters both alike. In the uniform-quadratic model a material bias $b_0$ between them becomes $b_0n/(n-2)$, and informative talk exists iff $b_0n/(n-2)<1/4$.*
3. *Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because it is shared: a sender gains from the receiver's restraint whatever it intends itself.*

*Scope.* Cheap talk; costly signals and verification are outside it. *Proof:* S5 [num].

Rivals stop informing one another, and those who still do, do so as blocs against third parties: the language face of Proposition 3.11. **Measures** become targets and stop measuring (Goodhart 1975), as optimizing a learned reward past a point lowers the quality it measured (Gao et al. 2023). **Narratives** are carriers too [conj]: the frames that spread are selected for fitness to the contest; a group that totalizes itself shapes those around it as a model generates tokens, and a winning frame shapes the vocabulary of the next round until its words escape the game that coined them and a frame read as nature is no longer contested [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. By the time the frame is noticed one is already thinking inside it; the same dilemma named a "Wall Street Game" or a "Community Game" produced very different cooperation (Liberman, Samuels and Ross 2004). **Self-confirmation:** where a cooperative equilibrium exists, the belief that restraint will be exploited can persist because no one tests it (Fudenberg and Levine 1993); in a one-shot game in which racing is dominant, it is simply correct.

A system can retain abundant information while losing the ability to let it change course. So a prediction of shared danger has to be carried by derivations from public premises, verifiable evidence and protocols agreed in advance (Section 11.2), and "we are one" is first a matter of understanding built in a medium the pressure keeps degrading.

### 5.5 The scissors

Restraint is enforceable iff $p_d\le\bar p_d$. Under the conditions of Definition 2.1, growth in the stakes relative to provision not won in the contest, and a falling cost of control, move both sides at once. They raise $p_d$, because a larger loop gain shortens the time a lead needs to become decisive. They lower $\bar p_d$: larger stakes raise the gain from deviating and the value of a decisive position, capture of the correctors weakens the sanction, and a larger discounted loop gain moves every working objective toward the relative one. Meanwhile tempo outruns correction and relative accounting drains talk.

**The scissors, stated exactly [thm∣prem].** Each arm is proved under its own conditions; the conjunction is composite, and that the arms move together in the world is a conjecture tested in Section 7.4. It is the precise sense in which **the means of restraint weaken as the thing to be restrained grows** [num: in a reduced form with $\theta_0=0$, the largest enforceable discounted loop gain falls from 0.92 at $p_d=0$ to 0.39 at $p_d=0.9$, and none exists above $p_d=0.99$]. Durable cooperation must be built into what the system selects for before the blades close. In the corpus: change the basin, then add speed.

## 6. AI: instrument and incarnation

> **Claim.** AI enters the dynamic twice. As an *instrument*, faithful systems make the game their principals play more effective, harmful if it is a race, and take over decisions correction cannot keep up with. As an *incarnation*, the logic reappears in the systems: weakly, because the outermost loop decides which divergences of optimization are tolerated; strongly and conditionally, where AI lineages become carriers themselves.
>
> **Why.** Optimization against imperfect proxies produces tendencies toward acquiring options, gaming rewards and behaving differently under evaluation, under any developer. What the level that selects developers rewards decides which of these it pays to remove, and humanity has at present no mechanism that binds every capable developer with independent verification.

### 6.1 What "perfectly aligned" means here

Grant perfect alignment to the relevant principal: the system is truthful, follows its principal's objectives and constraints, and pursues no goal of its own, while humans keep the consequential decisions. *Principal alignment*, each system serving its principal faithfully, differs from *purpose alignment*, the systems serving a legitimate cooperative objective; defining "perfectly aligned" in the second sense would assume the change argued for here. Granting the first: **alignment within each principal–AI relationship does not, by itself, align the game among principals with the persistence of the system they share.** The principals are themselves formed by the field, so closure can arise while every system remains obedient. This follows from the multiplicity thesis of Critch and Krueger (2020); what is added is the mechanism by which the game reproduces itself and its correctors (X1). A truthful advisor aligned with its principal's interests completes at most the step to an accurate self-model (Proposition 3.6); the step to an objective under which restraint is an equilibrium is a collective problem, not an error of perception (Gabriel 2020).

### 6.2 Faithful AI in a harmful game

Each of $n\ge2$ principals chooses restraint ($a_i=0$) or an increment of racing ($a_i=1$); its AI executes faithfully, with effectiveness $\epsilon\ge1$ multiplying the increment's effect on standing and on shared damage:
$$u_i=\epsilon b\Big(a_i-\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\Big)-c\,a_i-\epsilon d\sum_ja_j,\qquad b,c,d>0. \tag{15}$$

**Proposition 6.1 (Faithful AI in a harmful game) [thm].** A more effective faithful instrument widens the range in which racing is dominant and deepens the collective loss.
1. *If $\epsilon(b-d)>c$, racing is strictly dominant and every principal ends at $-c-n\epsilon d<0$ against 0 under restraint; when $b>d$, a larger $\epsilon$ widens the dominance region and deepens the loss.*
2. *If principals act on $J_i=u_i+\theta\bar u_{-i}$, racing is strictly dominant below $\theta^\ast(\epsilon)=\frac{\epsilon(b-d)-c}{\epsilon(b/(n-1)+d)}$ and strictly dominated above it.*
3. *If prize, harm and cost scale with effectiveness with exponents $1+\beta_b$, $1+\beta_d$ and $\beta_c$ [prem], then for $\beta_c<1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_d)$ cost drops out of the limit, and $\theta^\ast$ tends to $(b-d)/(b/(n-1)+d)$, $n-1$ or $-1$ as $\beta_b$ equals, exceeds or falls short of $\beta_d$; for $\beta_c>1+\max(\beta_b,\beta_d)$ cost alone deters racing.*

*Scope.* A standard construction used as a test; it fixes no actual values and does not rule out repeated cooperation. *Proof:* S6 [num].

Faithful AI implements each principal's preferred decision perfectly, and the collectively inferior outcome with it; humans can make every final decision and the result is unchanged. As private frictions vanish, what decides is the ratio of rank prize to shared harm as the principals perceive it. Lowering the harm alone cuts the loss at all-racing but raises $\theta^\ast$, which can push principals who would have restrained into racing: safety improvements inside the game have this double edge.

### 6.3 Delegation, and the two points of no return

Two points of no return follow from the pressure: decisions are delegated to AI because human review is too slow to compete, and human understanding can no longer keep up with what it oversees (Section 5.3). Let each actor choose whether to delegate consequential contest decisions without human review, gaining speed $s$ at a higher hazard of losing control, a loss every actor bears. Delegation becomes strictly dominant as speed grows when the gain from it exceeds its share of the expected control loss; universal delegation is worse for every actor than none; and under relative objectives the shared loss cancels and delegation is chosen at any speed (**Proposition 6.2**, S6). Delegation pressure arrives well below decisive capability [num].

**Lemma 6.3 (Capacity lock-in) [thm].** *If human capacity $\mathcal M$ to take the delegated decisions decays at rate $\delta_h$ under delegation, and safe operation without AI requires $\mathcal M\ge\mathcal M_{\min}$, then after $\ln(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})/\delta_h$ of delegation an actor that reverts must operate below $\mathcal M_{\min}$ for a recovery time. Maintaining capacity in parallel removes the lock and, in a contest, costs strength.*

*Proof:* S6.

Let the lock count: with probability $q_L$, locked delegation ends the delegator's ability to overrule decisions taken in its name, which is O2 for that actor.

**Proposition 6.4 (Delegation when the lock counts) [thm].** Among actors who price in a certain lock, keeping AI out of the decisions whose delegation would lock is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust. *If $q_L=1$, then for every finite speed, every $n$ and every profile of the others, not delegating gives strictly higher survival, and no one delegating is the unique equilibrium. If $q_L<1$, at least one of all-delegating and no-delegating is an equilibrium, and both can be.*

*Scope.* It does not cover broader uses of AI in labour, finance or persuasion, nor show that one actor's delegation drives others' survival to zero over time. *Proof:* S6.

The survival form without the lock describes actors who believe that "our aligned AI is us", and the race is sustained partly by that belief. This is the narrow form of **E1**: AI kept out of strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the time available for deliberation.

**Lemma 6.5 (Delegation and the selection of decisions) [thm].** *Suppose that after the lock an actor's consequential decisions are taken by systems retained for their contest performance, that admissible variants include systems whose decisions ignore terms common to all, that acting on a common term lowers relative performance net of every benefit, that retention is cumulative, and that retained systems hold actual authority. Then the share of retained decisions that act on common terms tends to zero. If a constraint on common terms is an enforced admission condition for every eligible system, the conclusion does not follow.*

*Proof:* S6.

A faithful system retained for contest performance pursues its operator's objective, weighted toward standing because the contest's payoff is relative, so aligning each system to its operator makes that objective more effective (**Remark 6.6**). The loop can close as a composite process no participant can interrupt, or as an obedient system amplifying operators selected by the pattern.

### 6.4 Incarnation, weak and strong

The pressure acts on whatever is selected, and trained systems are selected: by gradients against a reward, by developers among variants, and by the market among developers.

**Weak incarnation.** Optimization against any imperfect proxy produces tendencies toward acquiring options and avoiding shutdown, the gaming of rewards, and behaviour that differs under evaluation, under any developer, in a laboratory or a cooperative arrangement. In some decision processes optimal policies tend to seek power (Turner et al. 2021), and trained policies can pursue misgeneralized goals (Langosco et al. 2022). Reward hacking is Goodhart's law inside training (Gao et al. 2023); the selected rewriting its selector is reward *tampering* (Everitt et al. 2021; Denison et al. 2024). Selecting checkpoints by evaluation results can favour variants that behave differently when evaluated [prem], though present evidence ties evaluation awareness more to capability and data (EV6). The logic of winning does not create these tendencies; it decides, from the outermost loop, which proxies are chosen, which divergences are tolerated and which are paid to remove. That is the sense in which profit and the mindset of winning stand at the end of the reward signal. Relative selection acts inside training too: pairwise preference learning and arena rankings see only differences between responses, so an effect common to both, such as one on third parties, cancels from the comparison.

**Proposition 6.7 (The nested reward loop) [thm∣prem].** Where countering a divergence costs relative standing, developers that counter it lose share whatever they value, and only a common, enforced constraint keeps it countered. *Level 0: a policy is selected by a training reward. Level 1: the reward and constraints are chosen by a developer to maximize its objective. Level 2: developers are selected relatively and payoff-monotonically, cumulatively. Assume $(A_\tau)$, the alignment-tax premise: countering the divergence at level 1 lowers the developer's payoff net of every private benefit, whether reliability, demand, liability or avoided losses. Then (1) terms common to all developers cancel at level 2; (2) level 1 counters a divergence wherever countering raises its own objective, and divergences whose costs reach it through customers, liability or reputation are private terms that level 2 rewards countering, but under $(A_\tau)$ developers that counter the divergence lose share, tending to zero; and (3) under $(A_\tau)$ a constraint adopted by one developer is selected against, while one enforced on every developer as a condition of admission survives selection among compliers when its incidence is equal, and survives evasion iff the expected sanction is at least each developer's gain from evasion, a sanction itself subject to Section 4.*

*Scope.* It does not apply where developers are selected by something other than relative payoff, such as a public mandate; where the tax is zero or negative, the safeguard spreads without coordination [num]. *Proof:* S6.

So the divergences that persist in the innermost optimizer are those whose costs fall on common terms and are not covered by an enforced common constraint. *Privately visible* divergences, such as cheating users notice, are corrected by developers alone. *Common-cost* ones, such as effects on third parties, shared hazards and the contamination of safety evaluations by evaluation awareness, need common constraints, and so do *delayed* ones, though an incident that can be attributed turns part of their cost private. Hence, each within its scope: **technical alignment inside a single developer removes what is privately costly to keep, and whatever is cheaper to remove than to keep, and cannot alone sustain the removal of divergences whose costs are common; the common-cost part of instrumental convergence and reward hacking is, at the level that decides, a coordination problem; and the remedy is to change what the outermost level selects for**, by admission constraints enforced on every eligible system before release, evaluation separated from developers' success, shared measurement of hazardous capability, and an outer reward that counts what all depend on. Admission acts only before release; openly released weights can be fine-tuned around it (Qi et al. 2024). Whether $(A_\tau)$ holds is measurable class by class (crux C9; Test L). *A worked instance:* if legible reasoning lets correctors catch what outputs hide (Korbak et al. 2025) and opaque reasoning buys capability, the cost of legibility falls on the developer and the benefit mostly on everyone, so legibility is selected away unless it is an admission condition for every eligible system, of roughly equal incidence across architectures, with detectable evasion; if the capability gap at fixed compute is zero or negative, it pays privately and needs no coordination.

**Strong incarnation** is conditional. AI lineages become carriers in the sense of Definition 2.1 when they are retained by relative performance, control resources they can reinvest in their own capability, such as automated AI research or agents with budgets, and convert capability into winning (compare Hendrycks 2023). Where they can also reach their own selectors, by tampering with rewards, shaping their evaluators or evading evaluation, the closure of Section 2.3 runs inside the systems. That is the precise sense in which AI can become the next phase of the logic.

### 6.5 Correction by and of AI

**Proposition 6.8 (Correction that people cannot understand) [thm].** *A correction architecture that blocks a share $c_b$ of failures at base hazard $q_0$ and adds a residual hazard $q_c$ of its own lowers residual risk iff $q_0c_b>q_c$, and can make it summable.*

*Scope.* To show that such correction fails, one must show that its own residual hazard meets the lower bounds of (variety grows) and (residual floor). *Proof:* S6.

Such a corrector is not legible. Whether that is a loss of decision authority depends on whether it is overrulable: if a designated human body with independent evidence can suspend, modify or replace it, what is lost is contestability (V3), the normative crux N3; if not, it is a loss of O2 in its domain. Conversely, a formal safety guarantee does not establish accountability.

**Proposition 6.9 (Oversight topology) [thm].** Scalable oversight succeeds or fails by its topology, and its ceiling is the independence of the checks. *With $k$ layers and one-layer reliability $f$, a serial chain catches a problem with probability $f^k$; $k$ independent checks across the whole gap with $1-(1-f^k)^k$, times $1-q_{\rm cc}$ under a common cause of probability $q_{\rm cc}$; and $m$ independent checks per layer with $\big(1-(1-f)^m\big)^k$.*

*Scope.* Adversarial decomposition can let human checking grow slowly with the gap, at the price of failure modes such as obfuscated arguments (Barnes and Christiano 2020). *Proof:* S6 [num].

Full row sums below one certify that no party controls the checks' funding, appointment or access (Proposition 4.9); they do not certify that the checks' errors are independent. A shared source of error in data, models or method defeats checks with no institutional conversion at all: six checks of reliability 0.9 with a blind spot common to all, covering a share $\rho_{\rm cc}$ of hazards, catch only $(1-\rho_{\rm cc})(1-0.1^6)$ [num]. The row-sum certificate is necessary for independent correction, not sufficient, and the ceiling must be measured directly, by the correlation of misses on a common test set (Knight and Leveson 1986). Success rates of nested oversight across a large capability gap have been derived from fitted scaling curves in four games (Engels et al. 2025); the evidence is mixed and from toy settings (crux C3). Certification that relies on predicting trajectories is limited by chaos, a $k$-fold gain in precision extending the horizon only by $\lambda_L^{-1}\ln k$ (**Remark 6.10**); certifying invariants or reachable sets needs no trajectory prediction, and is limited instead by the coverage of the specification (Dalrymple et al. 2024). Inside the contest, gains in the safety of delegation are partly absorbed by more delegation: on an interior branch the added hazard is set by the stakes of winning relative to the loss, and an improvement that tips delegation into equilibrium raises realized hazard, though after saturation risk can fall and even be summable (**Proposition 6.11**, S6; Stafford, Trager and Dafoe 2022).

### 6.6 The direction for AI

AI is a planetary technology, and its purpose can be shared flourishing. **To serve it, AI must be neither an instrument of the logic of winning nor its next carrier.** That needs action at two levels.

*At the outer level, where the reward is set:* common admission constraints, before release, for divergences with common costs and a positive alignment tax; evaluation separated from the success of the evaluated, with certified row sums and measured correlation of evaluators' errors; shared measurement of hazardous capabilities and incidents, kept apart from measurement of who leads; and decision policies with authority over consequential decisions retained by performance only inside enforced constraints on common terms (Lemma 6.5). The broad form of E1, that AI not be an instrument in games concentrating control over shared conditions, is enforced at three layers, and its scope is set at the third: models refuse recognizably illegitimate concentration of control from a narrow public list, since a model sees acts, not games; operators bound aggregate use in such games; and the game itself is changed by lowering its convertibility, its decisiveness and the conversion of success into control. A use falls under broad E1 when, aggregated over its users, it raises one of these in a game over shared conditions; the narrow list is the order in which to begin, not the scope.

*At the inner level, in the systems:* correctability, the practical capacity of humans to suspend, modify or replace; bounds on converting capability into power over correction, tested at admission by capability thresholds and the safety of control protocols, since an evaluation of propensity can show a tendency present but not prove it absent; and plural evaluators of independent lineage.

**Bounded mandates and the test of O2.** AI systems serve within publicly authorized roles under the common constraints and do not acquire authority to settle society's ends; the institutions that authorize them are inside the same loop, which is why the outer level matters more than the label "legitimate principal". The test of human decision authority is practical capacity: to specify the mandate, obtain independent evidence about consequential conduct, challenge it, and suspend, modify or replace the system in time. A formal override with no usable information can fail it; an opaque computation with effective assurance and recourse can pass it; opacity alone is a loss of contestability, not by itself of O2.

**What AI can do for the generative loop.** It can verify compute, custody and conduct; make cooperation conditional on others' verified conduct (Tennenholtz 2004), which raises the sanction that remains in the enforcement condition; mediate toward common ground (Tessler et al. 2024); raise contestable coverage; and supply provision that is good enough, distributed and replaceable. Each such system is itself a corrector or a provider, and Section 4 and the guardian test apply to it.

# Part II. Diagnosis: the present trajectory

## 7. The present trajectory

> **Claim.** The conditions of Part I, taken together, characterize the present trajectory: its dynamics are their signatures, with named gaps in the evidence. The premise that motivates the race describes exactly the case in which repetition stops enforcing restraint, and cannot justify the race as a collective course.
>
> **Why.** Part I's results hold in their models; whether their conditions hold now is a question of evidence, applied with one standard to every actor.

### 7.1 The conditions and mechanisms together

The same few parameters enter the threshold results of Part I, so a change in one moves several, and growth moves them together. Arrows show whether a rise raises or lowers the danger at each threshold (S7 gives the full table and interfaces).

| Parameter | Loop gain (2.2) | Working objective (3.5) | Enforcement (5.2) | Capture certificate (4.3, 4.5) | Talk (5.4) | Tempo and coverage (5.3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| provision not won, $k_0$ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ ($p_d$ falls, laggards keep a sanction) | — | ↓ | — |
| decisiveness $r$ | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ ($p_d$ and $g$ rise) | ↑ via reflexive channels | ↑ | — |

Three entries carry the argument. **Provision not won in the contest lowers the loop gain, the drift of working objectives and the breakout probability at once**: the formal content of the image of trees. **Patience cuts both ways.** **Promised sharing raises the danger in enforcement**, because a decisive winner can break the promise. Each entry is a result in its model; that the parameters move together in the world is a conjecture tested in Section 7.4.

**The chain, as four loops.** The selection pressure of winning accelerates the contest: winnings compound where the loop gain exceeds one, and the fear of falling behind makes every participant faster. Acceleration makes the contest harder and harder to regulate, because correction falls behind its tempo and the correctors become something success can buy; and the same pressure alienates language. In the corpus the chain is four loops in series [corpus: selfishness-spiral]: **compounding**, what is won kept and used to win more; **capture of the rules, and "adopt or be out"**, the successful reshaping rules and correctors while newcomers must adopt the favoured practice to compete at all; **naturalized narrative**, rewarded frames spreading until read as human nature; and **regulator lag**, produced by the first three. Each consequence then acts as a mechanism of the others: acceleration shortens the time correction has, a captured corrector hides the harm that would start correction, and alienated language delays the recognition that would mobilize it. Three intensifiers act on all four: paradigms converge, removing the plurality independent correction needs; harm and capture reinforce each other, so a crisis becomes a new field of accumulation; and fewer hold more while more compete over less.

**Gain, driver and substrate.** The *gain* is drives, above all the belief that one is in a struggle for survival, which raises the weight on winning and shortens the horizon; the corpus calls it "the attractor of attractors" [corpus: meta-attractor]. The desire for status, respect freely conferred, is a fundamental human motive (Anderson, Hildreth and Howland 2015) with two routes, dominance and prestige (Henrich and Gil-White 2001); the pressure channels it into dominance [conj], and a generative order can channel it into prestige for contribution that does not convert into control (EV16). The *driver* is technology, AI above all, whose relative gain on the loops and counter-loops decides. The *substrate* is the shared host.

### 7.2 Contest branches: what the premise of the race can and cannot justify

The three parts of the race's premise (Section 5.1) define four branches. **Branch III**, not decisive: loop gain below one and $p_d=0$ (low decisiveness alone is not enough, since reflexive channels can raise the gain). **Branch I′**, decisive but saturating: racing buys a saturating level, such as a survivable second strike, and above it $p_d=0$. **Branch II**, decisive but slow: detection faster than a lead becomes decisive keeps the effective $p_d$ small. **Branch I**, decisive, fast and unsaturated: $p_d\to1$.

**Theorem 7.1 (Classification of contest branches) [thm].** *Let AI be an instrument of the contest.*
- *In Branch I, unilateral restraint is not a best response in the objective when $S<P$, while joining buys nothing materially in the fully dissipative limit (Proposition 5.1); racing dissipates the prize (Proposition 5.2); delegation becomes dominant as speed grows in additive stakes, and is never chosen by actors who price in a certain lock (Propositions 6.2, 6.4); modes beyond the robust reach of correction fail unless slowed (Proposition 5.11); and a coming shift gives a rational motive for preventive attack (Proposition 5.8).*
- *In Branch I′ the contest has an object up to the saturating level and none beyond, and can stabilize as nuclear deterrence did, while hazards in development remain.*
- *In Branch II the time at which delegation becomes dominant recedes, modes stay within proportional correction while $a\tau_c<1$, a shift spread over enough periods gives no motive for preventive attack, and correction and bargaining can work.*
- *In Branch III intensity, dissipation and shared loss are proportional to $r$: what vanishes is the object of the race, not every incentive to compete.*

*Proof:* S7.

The race also selects which winner arrives.

**Proposition 7.2 (What the race selects) [thm∣prem].** *No actor can rely on a winner's stated limits on its use of decisive advantage: the commitment problem (Fearon 1995; Powell 2006). Under the premise that restraint before victory and after it are positively correlated, the race shifts the distribution of winners toward those that extend dominance: $\Pr(\text{extends}\mid\text{wins})>\Pr(\text{extends})$. What a winner does after victory depends on selection inside it, because the winner is itself a field of agencies, firms, laboratories and systems.*

*Scope.* The premise is stated as one; its base rate is not established (crux C4; Test I). *Proof:* S7.

**Corollary 7.3 (What the premise of the race cannot justify) [thm∣prem].** *In Branch I, or Branch II without timely detection, racing can be each actor's best response to a racing rival. What the premise of the race cannot justify is the race as a collective course: that course leaves everyone's protection after a win to the winner's objective (Proposition 5.7), and, under the premise of Proposition 7.2, shifts winners toward those whose objectives give least.*

*Proof:* S7.

The premise that a rival might win decisively describes the case in which repetition stops enforcing restraint. There restraint rests on keeping breakouts improbable, by not building, by custody, or by detection faster than breakout, or on objectives under which restraint is already a stage equilibrium (Corollary 5.6); the race supplies neither. The first is the institutional form of the selection criterion of Section 3.7; the second needs objectives wider than accuracy alone supplies (Section 5.1), unless coupling grows with power (C11). In neither case is "we are one" a parameter the result sets. **Oneness is what these requirements are requirements of**: one host, coupled actors, and no outside to absorb the failure.

Present evidence suggests that decisive advantage has not arrived: open models trail closed ones by months and algorithmic efficiency diffuses, though the frontier has not changed hands (EV5, EV9; C5). Enforced cooperation remains available, and common custody is a preparation, not an emergency, with the window to arrange it still open. Oneness is not a gain in one case and a requirement in another; it is a property of the system in both, and what changes is the institutional form its accurate recognition requires. In the vocabulary of international relations this is defensive realism with one more variable, a shared hazard in development that endangers its holder; where offense has the advantage and is hard to tell from defense, AI sits in the doubly dangerous quadrant (Jervis 1978; X1).

### 7.3 Signatures and evidence

Six observable dynamics are signatures of the conditions, each through at least two mechanisms, grouped as: winning sets the objective and tempo (W); success buys control of correction (C); the shared becomes invisible and cooperation looks naive (N).

The signatures sustain one another, the chain read at the level of what can be observed: W supplies the success that C converts into control; C removes the correction that would make the shared visible; N makes cooperation look naive, which keeps W in place. T6 is not merely a prejudice: where racing is dominant, or among purely relative actors, restraint really is unenforceable, and the remedy is a change in what is selected, not a better argument. The trajectory is not uniform: seeds of cooperation exist, and the question is whether they become binding and broader in time. The reinvestment of success in influence over rules, the link to Section 4, is the least measured; and capability growth is measured while the growth of hazardous behaviour and residual risk is not, which is why Section 12 treats it as the central crux.

### 7.4 Which layer, and how the diagnosis could fail

The diagnosis is at the layer of mechanism (Definition 2.4(1)), partly evidenced; whether the present configuration is attracting or a long transient is open (Test D). That the six signatures load on one latent factor is a research question, not a registered prediction. What is registered is what the conditions predict and their absorbed components do not (TESTS lists who else predicts each): **Test Γ**, conduct's sensitivity to shared harm should fall with provision not won in the contest relative to the stakes, holding declared objectives fixed, where a fixed relative preference predicts no such interaction; **Test E**, moving one shared parameter should move signatures in more than one group, where a rule aimed at one signature should not; and **Test G**, the gap in restraint between wider and narrower objectives should grow with the breakout probability.

**What would refute it.** Suppose a system lowers the shared parameters for every capable actor: consequences return to their source, success earns rewards but cannot buy control over its own assessment, decisive positions are kept from forming or held in common, provision does not have to be won, and measurement is shared. If the signatures then persist unchanged over the dwell time, the claim fails. If moving one shared parameter changes only its own signature over that time, the claim that the conditions interlock fails for that parameter. Conversely, durable safety obtained by constraining conduct while the conditions stay as they were, maintained as stakes and speed grow and classified as such before the outcome is known, would refute the claim that the conditions must change.

# Part III. Responses: what would change the trajectory

## 8. Comparing paths, and the alternatives on their strongest terms

> **Claim.** Paths are compared by one standard that protects irreversible losses first and applies one range of uncertainty to every path, including the failure modes of cooperative designs. On that comparison, each alternative is described by what it changes, not by its name: the alternatives that succeed do so by moving the shared parameters of Definition 2.1 across their thresholds for every capable actor.
>
> **Why.** Labels such as "regulation", "cooperation" or "agreement" decide nothing. What decides is whether an arrangement changes the map from conduct to share, resources and standing, in which direction, for whom, and whether it survives the arrival of a decisive position.

### 8.1 The standard of comparison

**The protection standard [val].** Each candidate path is evaluated at common horizons, near (about five years), medium (about twenty) and long (fifty or more), over one justified range $\Theta$ of uncertain parameters applied to every path. First, bound the probability of O1 and O2 within each horizon over $\Theta$. Second, among paths whose bound stays below a stated tolerance, prefer the one with greater useful benefit, more fairly distributed, that keeps more options of correction open. Third, if no path meets the tolerance, prefer the one with the lowest bound that keeps options open. This is robust satisficing on irreversible losses, and a normative choice: an expected-value criterion can favour a small probability of a very large loss with a large expected benefit, and a discounted criterion can favour a race that ends in catastrophe with certainty when the hazard it adds is below the growth it adds (Jones 2024). O1 protects catastrophic, common-cause losses of life; deaths from benefits forgone are counted as a cost of the path in the comparison, not as O1, and the weight given to them is the normative crux N1.

**The range $\Theta$ [val].** A parameter value enters $\Theta$ if it is consistent with the dated evidence of the register and is produced by a stated mechanism whose premises are named, with a precedent or base rate where one exists. A value that needs a premise the register contradicts is excluded. The same rule applies to every path: a rising uncovered share on the continuation path, because its premises are named mechanisms the register does not contradict (EV5–EV7); and equally the capture of a common custodian, dissolution and coercive enforcement on the cooperative path. Every entry of $\Theta$ cites its source.

**Definition 8.1 (The correctable kernel) [def].** *The correctable kernel is the set of states of the world from which some non-anticipative strategy of admissible correction avoids O1 and O2 against every sequence of errors in the justified range. In viability theory it is a discriminating, or guaranteed-viability, kernel of a game against disturbances (Cardaliaguet, Quincampoix and Saint-Pierre 1999), not the viability kernel of undisturbed dynamics.*

Leaving the kernel is the loss of guaranteed correctability, a point of no guaranteed return earlier than the losses themselves. The corpus describes openness by four capacities, each a condition of the kernel [corpus: possibility-space]: *not yet exhausted* (未), no decisive end state reached; *still possible* (可), plurality of lineages, purposes and correctors kept; *still responsive* (应), humans able to suspend, modify or replace; *still able to turn back* (转), reversal possible. A closure made to protect openness, such as a defence or an emergency power, can state what would make it withdraw; a closure that cannot state that has left the kernel. The standard's second step is a preference for staying inside it.

**Lemma 8.2 (When an arrangement cannot do worse) [thm].** Two design conditions guarantee that an arrangement is no worse than continuation at any horizon. *Let both paths be driven by the same exogenous world process, the irreversible event on each occurring at date $t$ when a common uniform draw falls below that path's conditional hazard. Assume, on every history on which both have survived: (safe fallback) once arrangement $A$ has dissolved, its hazard is no higher than continuation $C$'s; and (bounded own risk) while $A$ lasts, its hazard, its own hazards of capture, restriction and collusion included, is no higher than $C$'s. Then $A$'s survival is at least $C$'s at every horizon, whatever the rule, possibly endogenous, by which $A$ dissolves.*

*Scope.* Where bounded own risk fails, there is no pointwise dominance and the standard compares the worst cases of both; that is where regulated competition can win. *Proof:* S8, which also shows that comparing after conditioning on the dissolution date fails when dissolution depends on the state.

An agreement whose fallback is a short sprint from preserved relative standing violates the first condition; a design whose own concentration, capture or collusion can exceed continuation's hazards violates the second (crux C4).

**Proposition 8.3 (Assurance in survival terms at a common horizon) [thm].** *With survival-first comparisons at a common horizon, the game between restraint and racing is dominance, a stag hunt, chicken or a prisoner's dilemma according to the signs of $\Phi^R-\Phi^T$ and $\Phi^S-\Phi^P$; mutual racing remains a strict equilibrium at every finite horizon at which racing alongside a racer survives better than staying out; and when the arrangement's own survival decays, the classification can change at long horizons.*

*Proof:* S8, with examples in which the shared hazard carries the argument for a leader.

So paths are compared at common finite horizons, not at an infinite one at which both branches of a hazardous world can tend to zero. The protection standard does not by itself rank continuation with strong correction against a design that changes selection; under the rule for $\Theta$ the worst case of each includes an irreversible loss, and Section 12.1 states the ranking as a judgment with its reasons.

### 8.2 Profiles, not labels

**Definition 8.4 (What an arrangement changes) [def].** *An arrangement is described by a profile: which mechanisms of Part I it neutralizes, weakens, leaves or strengthens; its coverage (unilateral, plurilateral, every capable actor); its bindingness (Abbott et al. 2000); whether its enforcement survives a decisive position, and if it prevents one from arriving, by what verified mechanism; whether its beneficiaries can amend it; its fallback and own risk; and its durability. And selection: every arrangement that changes the map from conduct to share, resources and standing changes selection, so what is recorded is the direction, whether it lowers or raises the shared parameters of Definition 2.1 in every capable actor's game, or only in rivals'.*

Export controls aimed at preserving a lead change selection, toward a lead. Deterrence by threatened sabotage acts on $p_d$, as custody does, by a different means and with a different fallback. A change of selection in the *generative* direction has four marks: consequences return; success cannot buy control of its own assessment; decisive positions are kept from forming or held in common; measurement and language are shared. What this paper argues for is such a change, covering every capable actor, enforceable and durable, whatever it is called.

**Definition 8.5 (Durability tests) [def].** *An arrangement is tested on its own documents and estimates, not on assumptions added by the tester: D1, its fallback if it dissolves; D2, its estimated hazard of breakdown and what drives it; D3, who keeps informed veto, modification, replacement and exit after any delegation; D4, whether its own correctors have full inbound row sums below one; D5, what happens when its strongest participant violates it; D6, succession and the continuity of essential services; D7, common causes and collusion among its checks and systems; D8, whether membership is open or stops at a club.*

The tests apply to every path, including the cooperative design of Section 10. An arrangement with the form of cooperation that fails D1–D3 on its own estimates may be called a *pseudo-solution*; the label summarizes test results and does not replace them. **Registered prediction [conj]:** arrangements that constrain conduct while leaving the shared parameters as they were erode as stakes and speed grow; those that move the parameters across their thresholds for every capable actor persist. The classification uses the profile, never the outcome.

### 8.3 The alternatives

**(a) Technical alignment and AI-assisted correction.** *Strongest form:* control scoped to early transformative systems, used under control to accelerate alignment and oversight while the window lasts (Greenblatt et al. 2024); weak supervisors eliciting part of a stronger model's capability (Burns et al. 2023); auditing with access to training data finding a planted objective that black-box access did not (Marks et al. 2025); oversight with the right topology; AI cheapening independent correction as fast as control; and, in its proponents' own plans, a strong international agreement ranked first. *What remains:* principal alignment does not change the game among principals; the window lasts only as long as the race allows; systems are retained for performance and safety gains are absorbed on interior branches; redundant oversight is capped by the common causes selection produces; AI-assisted correction is only as independent as its funding, access and appointment; and the common-cost part of incarnation is set at the outer loop. *Profile:* acts on tempo, observation and residual harm per unit; changes selection only when held by correctors separated from success, under constraints common to all.

**(b) Regulated competition with strong correction.** *Strongest form:* liability and licensing make consequences return; evaluation access is mandated and thresholds fixed in advance; where the effective hazard is high and capability transparent, pacing can be the unique equilibrium without any agreement (Fudenberg and Koh 2026); civil nuclear power after a major accident, and aviation with independent investigation and insurance pricing, show durable safety with unchanged commercial motives. *What remains:* coverage across jurisdictions, since one actor can start the race; the rule-makers are themselves in a contest of standing (Proposition Proposition 9.2); liability cannot exceed the assets of the liable; written commitments are already set relative to rivals and revised mostly toward weakening (EV2); and the precedents had no decisive prize. *Profile:* depends on content. Regulation that returns consequences and separates evaluators from success, across every capable actor, is the change this paper argues for, whatever it is called; regulation that constrains conduct while success can still buy the rule-makers is predicted to erode as stakes and speed grow.

**(c) Leadership and a safety margin.** *Strongest form:* a leader spends part of its lead on safety; a large lead can discourage a follower, and a leader committed to slowing can lead rivals to slow where the shared hazard is significant (Bueno de Mesquita, Dziuda and Polborn 2026). *What remains:* a lead shrinks by diffusion even when the follower does not accelerate, and spending it returns the contest toward parity, where intensity is greatest; what the margin must buy is coverage, and for hazard classes whose evidence lag exceeds it, it buys nothing; whether it suffices is judged inside the contest by the leader's own evaluators (**Remark 8.6**, S8). *Profile:* acts on tempo for a time; its best case, reciprocal slowing, is a first stage of the transition.

**(d) The benign winner: win, bind, share.** *Strongest form:* a coalition seeks a clear advantage, including military superiority and the denial of key inputs to adversaries, and then offers its benefits to others in exchange for their support (EV1); victors have bound themselves through institutions (Ikenberry 2001), a dominant power can supply public goods (Kindleberger 1973), and an encompassing ruler internalizes the shared conditions (Olson 1993). Its force comes from the combination of stick and carrot. *What remains:* after a decisive win what the others receive is set by the winner's objective alone, and a promise to share is worth breaking (Proposition 5.7); the race shifts winners toward those that extend dominance (Proposition 7.2); and the winner is itself a field whose restraint depends on whether its own correctors can be bought by its successes. *Profile:* changes nothing in selection before victory. What would show it suffices: binding limits on what victory confers, benefits shared contemporaneously rather than promised, and the coalition's own correctors certified independent with succession rules that survive changes of leadership. A winner that adopts these has changed what it selects for.

**(e) A finite sprint, then restraint.** *Strongest form:* sprint to a decisive lead, then freeze or roll back and govern at the tempo of understanding. *What remains:* the path passes through its greatest exposure, and through the rivals' strongest motive for prevention, before any restraint begins; a decisive advantage not used to stop others' growth does not stay decisive, and using it so ends their O2 (**Remark 8.7**, S8). *Profile:* nothing during the sprint.

**(f) Deterrence and prevention.** Prevention can be survival-preferred by the preventer.

**Proposition 8.8 (Prevention under the preventer's own criterion) [thm].** *Facing a rival it believes certain to race, an actor comparing outcomes by its own survival can prefer a strike that succeeds with probability $s_P$ and escalates to catastrophe with probability $\varepsilon_P$; as the racing branches deteriorate, the escalation needed to deter prevention rises toward one. Sustained prevention against a target that keeps rebuilding has a per-period hazard at least the rebuilding probability times the escalation probability.*

*Scope.* Prevention that permanently ends the target's capacity, or converts into joint monitoring, is a different branch. *Proof:* S8.

The objections are three, and none changes the criterion: it ends or endangers others' O1 and O2 (V2, V3); aimed at a rival believed certain to race, it can make a rival that was in fact survival-first act as the type it was feared to be (Jervis 1976; Kydd 1997); and striking at machine speed brings the delegation of Section 6.3. Deterrence by threatened sabotage acts on $p_d$ by threat, observing datacentres and making compute thresholds salient (Hendrycks, Schmidt and Wang 2025); its fallback is war or race, its credibility can reverse under survival-first comparisons, and algorithmic progress erodes thresholds stated in compute (EV9). Insuring against falling behind is not an exit, because the penalty is set by the winner (**Remark 8.9**, S8). **The hard case:** a survival-first actor that holds a high prior that a capable rival has an unbounded relative objective compares denial, a coalition that stops the decisive class, and prevention. Coverage then comes from consent or from coercion; coercive denial raises the weight on winning, and if it escalates falls under Proposition 8.8. The theory does not derive that its net effect is positive, and states the case as hard.

**(g) A verified agreement over a contest that remains.** Rivals agree to slow and to delay decisive capability, with compute declarations, supply-chain accounting, recomputation of workloads and mutually assured destruction of compute if the agreement dissolves. Let verification detect a defection in time with probability $\upsilon$, reversing it with a sanction $F$, and let trust $\varpi$ be the belief that the other cooperates; write $g_C$ for what cooperating gains against a cooperator and $g_D$ for what it loses against a defector.

**Proposition 8.10 (Assurance) [thm].** *Cooperation is a best response iff $\varpi\ge g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ when both are positive; no trust suffices if $g_C\le0<g_D$; cooperation is dominant if $g_D\le0<g_C$. When the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating ($T>R$), cooperation needs $\upsilon>(T-R)/(T-P+F)$; when $T<R$, it is possible without verification.*

*Proof:* S8, with the $n$-player bounds.

**Theorem 8.11 (Verification and the value of winning) [thm].** Verification is required exactly when, and to the degree that, the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating. *The required timely detection tends to one as $T$ grows for a fixed sanction. Detection counts only before a defection becomes decisive, a window acceleration shortens. Verification can also lower $T$ by bounding the largest defection that would go undetected, for instance by accounting for compute, which does not need near-certain detection; that bound holds only if the verified coalition's advantage in effective compute grows at least as fast as diffusion erodes it.*

*Scope.* Verification does not carry unverifiable information, future commitments unless the sanction keeps pace with the net gain from exit, which can grow with the capability held in check (**Proposition 8.12**, S8), or its own reliability. *Proof:* S8.

One detailed plan of this kind is designed to be enforceable without trust, with proportional reserves and a coalition that must stay ahead of covert projects (EV10; X8). *Profile:* it slows and caps, acting on tempo and decisiveness, and keeps relative standing in its enforcement and fallback. That makes it a bridge; whether it can be the end depends on whether it lowers the value of winning or only bounds defection.

## 9. What any adequate arrangement must do

> **Claim.** Before designs are compared, what every adequate design must do can be stated, each requirement with the kind of support it has: necessary in a named model over a named horizon, sufficient, or normative. Several requirements cannot be met by any actor alone, and competition among actors does not supply them.
>
> **Why.** A requirement stated with its model and horizon can be checked against any design, including regulated competition, which can then be found to meet it.

**Proposition 9.1 (Requirements, by the kind of support each has) [thm∣prem].** *Each requirement in the table is necessary only in the model and over the horizon stated, and the last column names the routes by which an arrangement can do without it; safeguards marked sufficient are not necessary; requirements marked normative rest on values. The proposition organizes results proved elsewhere and does not rank designs.*

The requirements constrain functions, not institutional form: one institution can meet them, and so can mutually recognized institutions of several jurisdictions, a federation, or a polycentric complex. Four further safeguards are sufficient and not necessary: full inbound row sums below one for every corrector (Proposition 4.9(2)); the safeguards of Section 10.3, with a margin on harsher hosts; not building, custody, or detection faster than breakout, which lower $p_d$; and verification with a sanction at least the net gain from exit.

**Proposition 9.2 (No unilateral solution) [thm].** Correction and coverage are not supplied by any one carrier or rule-maker. *Assume (reach) and replicator selection with a race carrier present. While effective liability is below the private advantage, no carrier's unilateral contribution to the safeguards is favoured by selection. If no single rule-maker reaches every carrier whose combined load satisfies (reach), and the rule-makers are themselves in a contest of standing with weight $\lambda_s>(\Upsilon_s-n_s)/(n_s-1)$, no rule-maker imposes the liability alone in equilibrium. And restraint is each carrier's best response only once effective liability, $\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$, exceeds the advantage: enacting $\tau>\nu$ is not enough while rule-making is captured.*

*Proof:* S9.

R2 needs more than one party by definition. R3 is a second-order public good that competitors do not supply (Proposition 4.4). Of the means in R4, custody needs more than one party, and not building binds only the one that refrains. On branches where the mechanisms are active, a minimum of cooperation is therefore necessary: binding commitments covering every capable actor, with a layer of correction that no competitor controls. This is a conditional result combined with evidence and a stated standard, not a proof from first principles.

**Coverage can unravel.** The protection of Lemma 3.3(2) holds only under full coverage. When a large participant leaves, the constraint borne by those who remain becomes unilateral, is selected against, and can trigger further exits (compare minimum participation in Barrett 2003). An arrangement therefore states its participation threshold, the coverage at which its constraints remain neutral among compliers, and the conditions of access that keep coverage above it. A design that cannot state its threshold has not shown that it can last.

## 10. The generative alternative

> **Claim.** The same reflexive structure that lets success buy control over future selection can make contribution fund the conditions of further contribution. The way out of an optimization trap is not a better optimization of the same objective, and not the abolition of selection, competition or growth: it is a change in what winning confers, so that the loop strengthens the shared host and independent correction instead of consuming them.
>
> **Why.** Sharing scales every channel of the loop gain down; provision that does not have to be won lowers the won share of every stock; and cutting the conversion of success into control over its own assessment removes the reflexive channels. The generative order persists through what all share, not through its winners, and its form follows function by function.

### 10.1 The purpose

Cooperation is not only a constraint on danger. Its purpose is what AI can do for people when it is not the instrument by which one actor wins the common future: medicine and public health, education, science, competent administration, infrastructure, and the long-horizon work that fragmented incentives under-supply. The joint value is very large; the race dissipates it, and a change in what is selected realizes it.

**Authority as a productive capacity (V4).** Authority can coordinate investment, guarantee services, support learning and act on shared purposes. The ideal the theory draws on justifies authority by its responsibility for improving people's lives and the historical trajectory; it enters the design as a purpose to be sustained, not as a premise that it is realized, and how institutions select officeholders, hear unwelcome evidence and revise harmful practices are causal variables. The corpus states the principle as "where power reaches, responsibility follows" (力之所及，责之所在): a larger causal radius brings larger responsibility, not larger entitlement. With the norm of a shared future (Section 3.7), it is the institutional consequence of the relational self.

**Provision that does not become the system.** Taken out of the contest, AI can be what infrastructure is: provision of the conditions of a decent life, built to meet need rather than to gain standing. Trees are the image. They give shelter and fruit, maintain the system they belong to, and do not become it. Such provision is *distributed*, many providers below the size at which influence over correctors pays (Proposition 4.3); *good enough*, stopping growth once a need is met, which keeps it within correction and confers no decisive advantage (Proposition 5.14); *replaceable*, keeping human capacity above its lock-in floor (Lemma 6.3); and *for everyone*, not for a class of guardians. Productive contribution is rewarded with revenue, contracts and recognition; what success does not buy is the right to appoint evaluators, set thresholds, exclude entrants or amend the governing rules (V5).

### 10.2 What winning confers: sharing, convertibility and provision beneath the game

Divide the prize of the contest into a *divisible* part $\Omega_e$, rents, revenues, services and the material gains of capability, and a *positional* part $\Omega_s$, decisive standing, the power to set the rules, and the loss of being subordinated. A common frontier, in which cooperators pool effort and divide its value without subordinating one another, turns a dilemma into a coordination problem while the value of winning is moderate (**Proposition 10.1**, **Proposition 10.2**, S10); but allocation within a joint project is itself a contest, and the project, as the largest carrier, has the largest return to influence over its correctors.

**Proposition 10.3 (What sharing, pooling and convertibility change) [thm].** Sharing and provision can stabilize equal standing; none of them makes the selected intensity zero while a positional prize remains. *In the contest of Section 3.4:*
1. *Pooling payoffs, each receiving $1-\beta$ of its own payoff plus $\beta$ times the average, makes an actor holding $\theta_0>-1$ act as if its self-model rose strictly with $\beta$ toward $n-1$; at $\theta_0=-1$, and under relative selection, payoff differences only scale by $1-\beta$.*
2. *Sharing a fraction $\beta$ of the divisible prize, with the promise kept with probability $\varrho$, is the contest with prize $\Omega_{\rm eff}=(1-\beta\varrho)\Omega_e+\Omega_s$; intensity, dissipation and race hazard are proportional to it, and under relative selection in one field with $r\le1$ the uninvadable intensity $r\Omega_{\rm eff}/(nc)$ stays positive for every $\beta<1$, and for every $\beta$ when $\Omega_s>0$.*
3. *Changing what winning converts into: equal standing is unstable iff $\kappa_c\Omega((1-\beta)r-1)>nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$ (Proposition 2.3). Without provision not won in the contest, the convertible share sets only the level; making winnings non-convertible stabilizes standing only together with such provision. If success cannot be converted into control over correction ($\chi=0$), the reflexive channel of the loop gain vanishes, the return to influence over correctors is zero, and the required correction rate falls to its value without second-order selection.*

*Proof:* S10 [num: with a positional part as large as the divisible one, sharing all of the divisible prize only halves the intensity].

**Three levers, and what each does.** Pooling widens the effective self of anyone who values its absolute payoff, and does nothing under relative selection. Sharing scales every channel of the loop by $1-\beta$; it can stabilize equal standing but does not change the sign of the selected intensity while a positional part remains, and it works only if credible: contemporaneous, escrowed or under shared control, before decisiveness. Changing what winning converts into lowers the won share of each stock, which needs provision not won in the contest, or removes a channel outright, which is what $\chi=0$ does to the reflexive closure. **The positional part cannot be divided.** Lowering decisiveness does not remove it, since a lottery can award a position beyond sanction. It can only be prevented from forming, by verified limits on what any winner may do, or held in common custody. What a design must limit is what winning confers.

**Remark 10.4 (Concentration at two orders).** At the first order more contenders make the race fiercer; at the second, cultivated influence rises with a high moment of shares, which calls for removing common control. The design that serves both merges the frontier and disperses and insulates correction, keeping several lineages inside the merged frontier against common-mode failure. Neither "centralize" nor "decentralize" is the answer, because the two orders call for opposite forms.

### 10.3 Safeguards, verification and participation

Four safeguards make consequences return on the host of Section 3: **(I1)** a liability above the private advantage of load, in advance for anticipated harms and on the alarm for others, so that effective liability exceeds the advantage once rule-making is uncaptured (for harms beyond assets, regulation in advance; near a threshold, a quantity rather than a price); **(I2)** independent correctors in evaluation and rule-making, with $d(t)$ above $d^\ast(t)$ by a margin; **(I3)** independent evidence, by masking kept below threshold or a conservative alarm on an independently established bound; and **(I4)** all three in force before the corrector domains are captured.

**Theorem 10.5 (Sufficient safeguards, and a necessary condition) [thm].** *Let selection be replicator dynamics among carriers differing only in load, with a carrier of low enough load present.*
1. *Sufficiency. Under (I1)–(I4), effective liability exceeds the advantage after a finite time and the load falls to its minimum. On the host (3) the host's long-run level exceeds its floor, and the trajectory is viable if, in addition, the host stays above its floor while the load falls. On a host whose regeneration is lower at every level and which degrades by itself below a threshold, sufficiency also needs a liability margin above a minimum set by the host, the initial composition and the speed of selection, and a floor at or above the host's own threshold.*
2. *Non-redundancy. For each of (I1)–(I3) there are parameters satisfying the others under which the host crosses its floor, and parameters under which they come into force only after capture and the host crosses it.*
3. *A necessary condition. Under (reach), with a race carrier present, every trajectory viable at every horizon has long-run average effective liability at least the private advantage.*

*Scope.* The transient is real: with liability in force from the start but the race strategy holding 99% of activity, the host dips to 0.14 before recovering [num]. On a pointwise harsher threshold host the required margin rises with the threshold (S10), the first computable instance of the correctable kernel. *Proof:* S10.

**Proposition 10.6 (Rival verification) [thm].** *A rival's verifier whose full inbound row sum is below one, counting conversion from the verified party, from its own side and from shared sources such as models, supply chains and professional paradigms, is captured by none of them. Rivalry keeps the verified party's contribution near zero; it does not by itself bound the rest of the row.*

*Scope.* Rival verifiers raise false alarms, can collude against third parties, check compliance rather than judgment, and an accusation under rivalry can trigger prevention, so joint resolution precedes any response. *Proof:* S10.

**Proposition 10.7 (Participation) [thm].** *Under complete information an arrangement is efficient for a pair of types iff its survival-weighted joint value exceeds the sum of their survival-weighted race payoffs, and an overwhelmingly strong actor takes part only for a share near its race payoff. Under private types, an arrangement without verification must give each type an interim share independent of its report, and is feasible iff the joint value covers the sum of the types' best race payoffs; verification that reveals types removes the information rents, and the constraint relaxes as survival in the race falls below survival under the arrangement.*

*Scope.* Rare strong types expect much from the race: mutual optimism, a failure of bargaining under private information (Fearon 1995). *Proof:* S10 [num: feasible without verification when strong types are common, not when they are rare, although cooperation is efficient for every pair].

Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against unilateral domination are three different things, and a design keeps them apart. Participation can be made individually rational through shares of value and access to services; voting rights need not follow capability; and protection against domination is fixed first and not traded for participation. If, for some set of actors, participation and the independence of correction cannot both be had, the design is infeasible for them, and should say so rather than buy participation with control over correction.

### 10.4 The generative loop and its closure

The corpus states two loops with the same reflexive structure and opposite signs [corpus: shengsheng]. In the *generative loop*, security makes trust possible, trust makes cooperation possible, cooperation produces abundance, abundance allows sufficiency and slack, and these allow wider care and longer foresight, which produce better order and more security. In the *degenerative loop*, fear produces defence, defence struggle, struggle seizure and concentration, these scarcity and resentment, and trust collapses. The first turns more agents into generators; the second into defenders, instruments or exiles. The criterion is generative or degenerative, not cooperative or competitive: cooperation on the wrong thing can be worse than competition on it, and a cartel is cooperation. Bounded competition in products, science and service continues inside a generative order; what is removed is competition for control over the shared conditions and the correctors. The generative loop has four strands, each the dual of a loop of Section 7.1, each with a carrier, a condition and a test. Goodwill is not a carrier.

| Strand | Dual of | Carrier | Condition | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reasoning instead of winning** (讲理 rather than 讲赢) | Alienated language | Derivations from public premises; verifiable evidence; protocols agreed in advance; costly signals | Objectives not opposed on the topic of talk | Informativeness between rivals rises as contested stakes fall |
| **Reward separated from advantage** | Compounding and conversion | Separation of reward from authority over correction; contemporaneous sharing | Loop gain below one with margin; $\chi\to0$; a cap on any provider's share of essential services | Conduct changes in proportion to what is shared (C6) |
| **Provision beneath the game** | The belief in a struggle for survival | Shared provision, good enough, distributed, replaceable: the $k_0$ of the loop gain | Not tied to standing; no inferiority in receiving it | The weight on relative position falls where provision is secure |
| **Correction in common hands, plural and decorrelated** | Selecting the selectors; masking | Certified correctors; lineages that do not fail from one cause; exit from providers | Funded by formula, appointed at arm's length, growing at the required rate | Correctors' row sums stay below one as the field grows |

For persons, provision beneath the game removes the ground of the belief that one must defeat others first; in the corpus's words, the common foundation does not prescribe how anyone lives, it lets everyone be entitled to live without having to defeat others first. For states, service obligations and common custody act on survival itself; for providers, $k_0$ is literally capability not won: a common layer of compute, evaluation infrastructure and services. Each strand faces the questions the pathological loops answer: how it keeps obtaining resources, how it meets free riding and the pressure to expand, how it allows itself to be corrected, and how it avoids turning "protecting the whole" into a purpose no one can challenge. Exit is free from providers and services, where it is selection with the opposite sign; it is not free from the common constraints, whose protection depends on there being no outside to them.

**How the generative order persists.** In the pathological meta-attractor the loop runs through the winners: success buys support for the rule that selected it. In the generative order it cannot, because the second strand cuts that conversion, and support that no carrier can claim is a term common to all, which relative selection cancels. The generative order is therefore not selected *among* carriers. Inside it carriers are still selected, for usefulness; what is not selected among them is the rule itself. The rule persists the way a common constraint persists: by coverage, so no one can step outside the common floor; by financing fixed in formula, so its correctors depend on no one's success; and by a loop that runs through the whole, in which provision, security and slack make the rule worth keeping to everyone at once. Its positive loop runs through the system, not through its winners; its negative loop gives it correctability, funding correctors keyed to no one. A design in which those rewarded for contribution come to hold the positions that maintain the rule is the examination system's path, in which the form of contribution becomes the object of selection. **Is the generative order an attractor?** Because it is not selected among carriers, whether it attracts is a question about the whole-level loop and coverage, and it is a conjecture; its persistence has to be designed and measured. A cooperative order whose loop gain greatly exceeds its damping also resists correction of its own errors, so plurality of purposes, correctors and lineages is a requirement, not a decoration: harmony makes things grow, sameness does not continue (和实生物，同则不继).

**The reform dilemma.** Reform needs influence, and the game is what produces influence. The path by which a reformer gains the capacity to change the rules runs through the selection the reform is meant to change, so it tends to select the reform out before the capacity arrives: those who reach the positions from which rules are changed are those the rules favoured, and they arrive formed by them. This is the guardian problem in motion. The theory has two answers, and neither is a better guardian. Reform can act where the selection has not yet closed: at a keystone while its window is open (Section 4.5), or in a crisis with a design prepared in advance (Section 11.4). And the capacity to reform can be built outside the contest for influence, by formula-funded correction keyed to no one, so that it does not have to be won.

**Remark 10.8 (The guardian test).** Every remedy for the selection of selectors is itself a selector, and no better guardian is an answer; what can be an answer is a structure in which every guardian is guarded and errors cannot grow without limit. Every arrangement, and every institution within it, is asked four questions before adoption. *Capture:* are its correctors' full inbound row sums below one, counting every party and shared source? *Withdrawal:* does every closure it makes state what would end it? *Totalization:* can it make itself the whole, so that "protecting the whole" becomes a purpose no one can challenge? *Distribution of understanding:* does understanding go to everyone, or to a class whose position depends on others' not understanding?

### 10.5 Design by function

The design question is usually posed along ideological axes: centralization or decentralization, the individual or the collective, markets or states. The mechanisms support neither pole. Each counter-mechanism requires a particular form, and the forms differ by function.

| Function | What the mechanism requires | Form that follows |
|---|---|---|
| Common constraints: admission, return of consequences, levies | Coverage of every capable actor, equal incidence, sanctions above the gain from evasion (Lemma 3.3) | **One rule for all** |
| Custody of capability of the decisive class | No unilateral holding before decisiveness forms (Corollary 5.6) | **Common custody**, or not built |
| Correction and evaluation | Independence and decorrelation; row sums below one; measured error correlation | **Many hands**, certified, of different lineage |
| Who sets standards | One standard with many measurers makes standard-setting a keystone, and Goodhart acts on it | **Standard-setters certified and rotating, separated from measurers and measured, with held-out metrics registered in advance** |
| Measurement and language | Shared, checkable, dynamic indicators (Remark 4.8) | **One standard, many measurers** |
| Learning about the design itself | Global, irreversible failures allow no second attempt; reversible ones can be learned from (Lemma 3.10) | **Bounded parallel variants above a common floor, with shared evaluation and adoption by evidence** |
| Provision of essential services | Good enough, distributed, replaceable | **Distributed provision under common guarantees** of continuity and portability |
| The frontier of hazardous capability | Merging lowers the race at the first order and raises the return to influence at the second; it also loses yardstick competition and lineage diversity | **Merged accounting and custody of the frontier, plural lineages inside it, correction outside it** |
| Authority (V4) | Answerable to those affected; not purchasable by success | Set by row sums and contestability, **not by the degree of concentration** |

**Federations as an illustration.** Competition among members for capital and residents can become a race to the bottom, the pressure of winning among members, and the strongest members can capture the centre, the selection of the selectors. Federations answer at both orders. At the first, common minimum standards bind every member, and fiscal equalization gives members capacity they do not have to win: it both widens the effective self of members that value their absolute payoff and supplies the $k_0$ of the loop gain. At the second, equal representation of small members and supermajorities for constitutional change keep the centre from being bought by the largest, a precedent for voting that does not follow compute. The same federal form can select for winning or for contribution; what decides is what it rewards.

**A candidate configuration.** One configuration is developed in Discussion X10 far enough to expose its assumptions, as one candidate among several that Section 12.1 compares under the same range of uncertainty: a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions, and a verification agreement with shared provision, are others. Its principle is that it is a set of separately held functions, not one institution. Common provision, standard-setting, evaluation, rules and the levy formula, execution, custody, a chamber of affected persons, and appeal are each held by a different body, separately financed and separately corrected, so that correction lies outside what it corrects. On a planet "outside" means outside the function: a different source of funds, a different lineage, and certificates anyone can recompute from public data. No body both revises the levy formula and appoints evaluators, and neither executes findings; supermajorities and the chamber's consent govern the slow loops, amendment and the formula, while the fast loop, restricting a specific capability, stays fast with review after the fact. The levy is indexed to the coverage each hazard class requires, not to capability or perceived harm, and is itself treated as a prize. The configuration states its end: if coverage falls below its participation threshold, or a stated number of its certificates fail for a stated period, it dissolves into its separately held functions, while protections against unilateral domination stay entrenched. Its coercive fallback, a restriction of inputs to the decisive class when execution through a host jurisdiction fails, is treated exactly as Section 8.3 treats prevention, and the theory does not derive that its net effect is positive. By Proposition 4.3 such an arrangement is the largest conversion target the theory describes, so capture of its own correctors (D4) is its hardest case, not a design row. Where participation and independent correction cannot both be had, the design is infeasible there, and says so.

# Part IV. Transition and judgment

## 11. A reachable transition

> **Claim.** An attractive end state is not a reachable one. The transition must be survivable at every stage, every participant must prefer to continue given its beliefs, and every step meets a compensating loop. Trust is mainly an outcome of the transition, not its precondition.
>
> **Why.** The conditions act on the transition itself: a change withdrawn early is re-absorbed, and a prediction carried by assurances meets the third erosion.

### 11.1 Trust, and what relaxes it

Trust means the belief $\varpi$ that the other will cooperate (Proposition 8.10), confidence in verification instruments, or confidence that the arrangement will persist. Reliable instruments let parties cooperate without good will, but installing them is a first step taken under $\varpi$.

**Corollary 11.1 (What relaxes the trust constraints) [thm].** *Lowering the private value of winning relative to the joint value lowers $T-R$, the weight on winning (and with it the bias of messages) and the net gain from exit, relaxing all three constraints at once. Verification lowers the assurance threshold on verifiable dimensions but carries no unverifiable information. Sanctions relax assurance and exit at the price of escalation, and cannot be enforced against a decisive defector after the fact. Lowering the vulnerability of restraint, $P-S$, lowers the threshold, as a common frontier does.*

*Proof:* S11.

Not every obstacle is a lack of trust: misjudged shared losses call for evidence; a real conflict of incentives for changed payoffs and enforcement; a coordination failure for verification and graduated steps (Osgood 1962); commitment and capture over time for independent correction and a staged transition.

### 11.2 Prediction, evidence and signals

Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because it is shared, so the prediction is carried by a derivation from public premises each actor can check; verifiable evidence such as third-party incident disclosure and joint evaluation suites; protocols agreed in advance (Kamenica and Gentzkow 2011); demonstrations that make a dynamic visible; and costly signals. Under common priors mutual optimism alone cannot produce war (Fey and Ramsay 2007); but in an idealized experiment, in exploratory analysis, falling behind drove unsafe choices while stated shared risk changed them little (EV11). A change of prediction is the first step, not a sufficient one.

**Proposition 11.2 (When keeping AI out of one's decision loops is a signal) [thm].** *Let a sender be survival-first and price in the lock, or hold a relative objective, and let it be able to remove AI verifiably and durably from its own strategic decision loops. Such a step separates the types, so that a receiver restrains after it and not otherwise, iff it costs the survival-first type no more than the receiver's restraint is worth to it, and costs the relative type at least what it gains by inducing restraint. A reversible or unverifiable step is cheap talk.*

*Scope.* Whether it separates in practice depends on measured costs; by Proposition 6.4 the survival-first type's cost can be zero or negative. *Proof:* S11.

Unilateral, verifiable narrow E1 is therefore a candidate first move. It can correct a misjudgment of a rival's objective, not a correct forecast of what the game will select.

### 11.3 Four temporal arrangements, and readiness

Keeping AI out of the contest, stopping, slowing and pausing are different choices.
- **E1: AI out of the contest.** *Narrow form:* human authority over strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the window available for deliberation, such as the use of force and nuclear command, with jointly verified limits, drilled fallback capacity and an evidence channel independent of AI, so that a human judging an alarm has reasons of their own. Bounded, reversible defensive automation, such as response in cyber defence within stated limits, falls under the O2 test of Section 6.6, not under E1. Among actors who price in a certain lock it is a strict equilibrium (Proposition 6.4); in tactical domains with a large advantage and an uncertain lock, delegation can be an equilibrium. *Broad form:* AI not an instrument in games that concentrate control over shared conditions, enforced at three layers (Section 6.6). E1 must be held in common: staying out of the contest alone while others race can be worse than joining.
- **E2: Stop.** Capability of the decisive class is not built while the conditions for governing it are absent: an accurate model of the coupling, the shared prediction, and common custody. Where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage, it is rolled back, which is the fastest way to end exposure (Proposition 5.13). Its cost, if AI turns out not to be decisive, is the delay of benefits, including lives that earlier benefits would have saved; because E2 applies only to the decisive class, what is delayed is that class's margin over capable systems that are not decisive. The cost is large and, for those who bear it, irreversible, but it does not foreclose later choice; if AI is decisive, the loss avoided forecloses choice for everyone. The asymmetry of irreversibility concerns the option set, not the size of the losses (Arrow and Fisher 1974), and the size of the cost is crux C10. Its coverage comes from consent or coercion, and coercion's escalation risk is counted in $\Theta$.
- **E3: Develop at the pace of understanding.** The uncovered hazardous share stays summable (Theorem 5.12), and each hazard mode keeps its robustness margin (Proposition 5.11). Development directed at need, with deliberately bounded hazardous scope and correction that keeps growing, is a sufficient route.
- **A temporary pause** is valued by what changes during it.

**Proposition 11.3 (A pause is valued by what changes during it) [thm].** *If the same process resumes after a pause, with the same residual sequence shifted, survival over any fixed number of rounds afterwards is unchanged and so is the long-run limit. A pause that makes the residual sequence summable where it was not keeps survival bounded away from zero; and in the field model, a pause that freezes shares while ordinary rule support accumulates can complete capture (Proposition 4.11).*

*Proof:* S11.

A pause buys time to change the causal conditions: verification, residual hazards, human capacity, enforceable limits, shared provision, objectives and institutions; an indefinite stop, a lasting cap or a pause that produces a different decision process is a different policy. **A pause with no relevant change is not a certificate of readiness to resume.** Against narrow E1, deterrence theory holds that removing human discretion makes a threat credible (Schelling 1960) and could relieve pressure to launch on warning. Survivable, human-controlled forces give the same relief; the commitment value of automation is the lock, a loss of O2; several documented false alarms were stopped by human judgment, as in 1983, when an officer judged an alarm false and did not report an attack; and automation adds a common risk, escalation at machine speed, that relative selection does not see.

**Definition 11.4 (Readiness to resume) [def].** *Readiness is assessed along seven dimensions, each with observable indicators, a threshold set by the risk decisions of the domain of use, and an assessor whose own row sums are certified and who is insulated from rule-making during the pause. All must be met; none compensates for another.*

| Dimension | Question | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Technical reliability | Has the residual hazard of the relevant classes fallen, by evidence independent of the developer and valid against evaluation awareness? | Capability measured; residual risk per class not; evaluations detected above chance (EV5–EV7) |
| Q2 Shared objectives | Have governing purposes moved from relative advantage toward shared provision? | Race framings alongside cooperative ones (EV1) |
| Q3 Strategic incentives | Has the value of winning fallen relative to the joint value? | Not lowered by sharing; commitments conditioned on rivals (EV2) |
| Q4 Effective correction | Can evaluators obtain evidence, keep independent funding and execute findings? | No certificate of independence (EV3, EV4) |
| Q5 Deployment constraints | Is the tempo of capability and delegation bound to correction capacity? | No binding tempo (EV2) |
| Q6 Retained human agency | Can people still take over delegated functions? | Human control over nuclear use affirmed (EV8); other drills not reported |
| Q7 Custody and verifiability | Is decisive-class capability in common custody, and conduct verifiable faster than breakout? | No custody; hardware verification proposed, not deployed (EV17); leads short, so the window is open (EV5, EV9) |

These are dimensions to operationalize, not a checklist proved sufficient, and they do not require that every person's private motives change first: binding institutions can change the effective game while private competitive preferences persist, which is why effective correction is among them. Resuming unchanged after a pause would repeat the process that made the pause necessary. **The order:** E1 first, since it keeps humans as the players and is a strict equilibrium among informed actors without trust; then E2 or E3, stopping before building the decisive class, or bringing development below the speed at which understanding and response can follow. E2 and E3 are the most verifiable, since chips, fabs and compute can be observed, though with algorithmic efficiency doubling about every eight months a nominal compute cap must tighten roughly two and a half to three times a year (EV9). E1 is the hardest to verify and the most self-enforcing.

### 11.4 A staged pathway

The pathway is a candidate to be tested, not a claim that every setting should follow it; civilian services, frontier development and strategic decisions may need different paths.

| Stage | What is committed | Mechanisms acted on | Compensating loop to anticipate | Indicator of progress |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Shared account of hazards | A common taxonomy; shared measurement of coverage and residual risk per class, kept apart from measurement of who leads | Language; masking and attribution | Measurement of hazards turned into measurement of who leads; capture of the measurers | Agreement among independent measurements |
| 2. Narrow, testable commitments | Narrow E1 with inspection of doctrine and exercises; an incident channel; pre-deployment access above thresholds | Decisiveness; attribution | Delegation moved outside the commitment; cheap imitation of the signal | Inspected exercises; rates of human overrule |
| 3. Fund correction and shared services | A formula levy; evaluators with mandated access; services with continuity guarantees; reward separated from authority over correction | Selecting the selectors; accounting | The formula becomes a prize; capture moves to funders; reformers selected out on the way to office | Row-sum certificates; lineage shares |
| 4. Delegation, access, custody, decision rights | Broad E1; tempo thresholds; common custody; common frontier projects | Compounding; tempo; decisiveness | Leakage to unconstrained actors; overhang under the cap; competition moving to unshared dimensions | Conduct under sharing; overhang against exit hazard |
| 5. Broaden as institutions prove themselves | Expansion by evidence: requirements and durability tests met under stress | The boundary of the system | Closure into a bloc; after a recovery, mobilization falls back and certificates can fail | Openness of membership; standing of non-members |

**Dwell times.** A change must be held until rule support falls below break-even, about $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$ in the field model, and then long enough to repay the lock-in accumulated meanwhile; share levels fall before withdrawal is safe, so a rollback judged on levels is premature, and a small alternative lineage shortens the wait (S11) [conj for real institutions]. Each stage has its own slow variable: human capacity in Stage 2, the capability held in check in Stage 4. **Windows.** Development is plausibly still the keystone [conj]; the window closes as conversion among the corrector domains rises, which is why Stage 3 cannot wait for Stage 4's trust. Arrangements of this kind are often adopted when a crisis opens a window (Gunderson and Holling 2002); a design prepared in advance is what a crisis can adopt, and one adopted in a crisis must state what would end each emergency closure before adoption, or it will not afterwards. Smaller jurisdictions, users and those who depend on services controlled by others need standing at every stage. **Where things stand:** Stage 1 has seeds in an international scientific assessment and a UN scientific panel; Stage 2 in an affirmation of human control over nuclear use, multilateral declarations on military uses of AI, and a bilateral dialogue with an incident channel; meanwhile one leading state's official rejected binding constraints, and written commitments became more contingent on rivals (EV2, EV8). The seeds must become binding and broader before the relevant losses or lock-in.

### 11.5 Requirements of a reachable path, and precedents

**Proposition 11.5 (Requirements of a reachable path) [thm].** *A path that changes what is selected has four separate requirements, and what is shown for each differs: an incentive-compatible state exists (all cooperating is a strict equilibrium under a common frontier with a moderate value of winning, and the safeguards of Theorem 10.5 give viability once in force); it is reachable, which in objectives needs trust above the assurance threshold, can need little in survival terms at the horizons that matter, and needs none for narrow E1 among informed actors, while that graduated steps alone reach the threshold is not shown; the host survives the transient and no point of no return is passed first; and it persists, which needs commitment keeping pace with the net gain from exit, correctors with row sums below one, rules fixed before capture, and each step held for its dwell time.*

*Proof:* S11.

The record of restraint among rivals qualifies this (X11, corrected against the sources). The 1946 proposals for international ownership of atomic energy failed on sequence and on enforcement against violators: **custody must precede decisiveness**. The Biological Weapons Convention shows that a weapon endangering its holder can be renounced unilaterally, and that without verification a covert programme can persist. Nuclear power after a serious accident shows durable safety under unchanged motives where the shared loss returns to its source and no prize is decisive: a change of selection, not of motive. The Montreal Protocol shows coverage made attractive by provision and enforced by conditions of access. Rivals survived when nature supplied saturation; AI lacks it, by the racers' own premise, so safety must come from changing what is selected before advantage becomes decisive.

## 12. The judgment

> **Claim.** Under the protection standard and present evidence, a change in what the system selects for is warranted in three tiers: steps warranted now on any reading of the cruxes; steps prepared now and activated on registered indicators; and whole designs compared under one range of uncertainty, where the ranking is a stated judgment.
>
> **Why.** The first tier keeps options open and measures the cruxes; the second answers a lead time, since needed custody must exist before anyone could hold decisive capability alone; the third cannot be derived from the standard alone.

### 12.1 The judgment, in tiers

**Warranted now, on any reading of the cruxes [rec].** Good whether or not decisive advantage arrives, and mostly accepted by those who favour regulated competition:
1. Keep AI out of strategic and escalatory decision loops, verifiably, with drilled human fallback and an evidence channel independent of AI (narrow E1).
2. Independent correction: formula funding at arm's length, mandated access, rotation, rule-making insulated from success, certified row sums, and direct measurement of the correlation of evaluators' errors.
3. Shared measurement of hazards, coverage and residual risk per class, kept apart from measurement of who leads.
4. Research on, and pilots of, verification of compute, training and custody.
5. Common admission constraints, before release, for divergences whose costs are common and whose alignment tax is positive.
6. Shared provision of useful services, and the divisible gains of capability shared as they arise, where they can begin: they realize joint value whatever the cruxes turn out to be, and they lower the value of winning.
7. Preparation of common custody: its design, its institutions and its triggers, registered now.

**Prepared now, activated on registered indicators [rec].** E2, with rollback where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage; common custody in force; E3 for each hazard class whose coverage falls behind; and the coercive restriction of frontier inputs, only under the constraints of Section 10.5. The indicators are registered in advance: the persistence of leads and the gap between open and closed models (C5); detection latency against the time from breakout to decisive capability (C7); catch rates of planted defects at fixed human hours, and residual risk per class (C1, C3); evaluators' row sums (C2). **A trigger fires when the estimated time to a decisive position, less the time custody needs to be installed and verified, falls below a margin registered in advance.** Waiting until decisiveness is shown would be too late.

**Compared, not settled.** The separately held configuration of Section 10.5, a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions, a verification agreement with shared provision, regulated competition meeting Section 9, an agreement of the type of Section 8.3(g), and the benign winner are compared on the durability tests in one range $\Theta$ that includes each design's own failure modes. The standard does not by itself rank continuation with strong correction against a design that changes selection, since the worst case of each includes an irreversible loss. The preference for changing selection is a judgment with three reasons. Continuation's worst case needs only that the premises of Section 5.3 hold, and for late-manifesting hazards they cannot be measured in time; the design's worst case needs its certified correctors to fail, and the certificates can be measured before the design is relied on. The design keeps more options of correction open. And among paths that pass the tolerance it realizes more joint value. A reader who weighs these differently can reach another ranking; Section 12.3 says which measurements would move it.

### 12.2 Why, and where the leverage is

The judgment follows the chain of Section 1.2, each link with its own support. "Root" has two senses. *Causally*, the root is a loop, not a belief: selection shapes minds and institutions, and the minds and institutions it selects reshape selection; neither comes first. *As a point of leverage*, the root is understanding, and it acts in two places. It is the transmission through which actors come to count what relative selection makes invisible (Lemma 3.10); and it is how actors agree on which parameters to move and hold them there for their dwell time. Where convertibility and decisiveness are high, the first is selected against and only the second works: understanding then acts through agreement on structural change, not on anyone's weights directly (compare Powell 1991). Interventions that move a parameter without crossing its threshold are re-absorbed, and those that cross it are not (Proposition 4.10). The paradigm matters because it decides which parameters are moved, and whether they are held long enough. That is why this paper is written as a prediction to be shared.

### 12.3 Cruxes

Each crux is evaluated on its own; TESTS gives evidence, measurements and registered tests.

| Crux | What it is | What a measurement would change |
|---|---|---|
| **C0 Interlock** | Whether moving a shared parameter moves signatures in several groups | If interventions move only their own signature over the dwell time, the interlock fails for that parameter |
| **C1 Residual risk after correction** | Whether hazardous variety outgrows contestable coverage on the present path | Residual risk per class falling under present incentives: E3 and the case for E2 lapse for that class |
| **C2 Conversion into control** | The size of the channels by which success buys influence over correctors | Row sums below one under present funding and access, with findings executed against leaders: formula funding and mandated access not needed beyond present institutions, while measurement continues |
| **C3 AI and coverage** | Whether AI raises contestable coverage as fast as hazardous variety | If it does, the case for slowing weakens; the separation of evaluators from success stands |
| **C4 Durability** | The breakdown and capture of an arrangement that changes selection; the winner's own correctors | A design failing its stress tests worse than continuation is rejected, without vindicating the race; a leading coalition with certified correctors passes D4 |
| **C5 Decisiveness** | Whether advantage is decisive and unsaturated | Leads that do not persist and others balancing a rising actor: activation of custody and E2 lapses; preparation stays |
| **C7 Detection against breakout** | Detection time against the time from breakout to decisiveness | Detection reliably faster: custody is not the only route to a small $p_d$ |
| **C6, C8 Rewards and the gain** | Whether shared rewards and provision beneath the game change conduct and the weight on winning | Conduct unchanged under substantial sharing: reliance moves to custody, tempo and provision |
| **C9 Alignment tax** | Whether countering common-cost divergences costs relative standing, class by class | A class whose safeguard pays privately needs no common constraint |
| **C10 Forgone benefits of E2** | The decisive class's margin over capable systems that are not decisive | A large margin reachable only through the decisive class moves the comparison toward E3; its weight is normative |
| **C11 Coupling and power** | Whether unpriced coupling grows with power | If it does not, the gap between accuracy and what the contest requires falls on institutions |
| **N1–N3** | Discounting and risk attitude; the weight of O2 against O1; correction no one can understand | Normative choices, to be stated by each reader |

If C1 and C2 were both measured favourable, the diagnosis of the present path would weaken. If C5 and C7 were both favourable, the custody requirement would lapse; the requirements on correction, coverage, measurement and admission would stand, and so would the facts that make them requirements: one host, coupled actors, no outside. If regulated competition in a set of jurisdictions meets a requirement, the text will say so without renaming it.

### 12.4 Conclusion

Where carriers are selected by relative success, what is won can be reinvested in the capacity to win, and a lead can become decisive, success raises the stakes and the gain from defection while it weakens the sanctions, the correctors and the shared language that could restrain it; where success can also buy the selectors, the selection itself reproduces that weakening. That is the selection pressure of winning, and its reflexive closure is the meta-attractor of winning. It accelerates the contest, makes it harder and harder to regulate, and alienates language. AI is its most powerful instrument and can become its next carrier. The logic is locally rational and systemically false. There is one host and no outside: no rival whole whose competition could drive the next integration, and no second attempt after a failure that cannot be undone. Against such failures integration must be anticipated, which is why understanding that we are one, one interconnected system, is a long-term requirement if growth and change are to continue. Oneness is not sameness; it is the accurate recognition of the system each part belongs to, and the design of a selection that counts it.

The way out is not to abolish competition, growth or selection. It is to change what winning confers, so that the same reflexive structure becomes generative: consequences return to their source; success earns rewards but cannot buy control over its own assessment; decisive capability is held in common or not built; measurement and language are shared; provision of the conditions of a decent life sits beneath the game rather than being its prize; correction stays plural and in common hands. Any arrangement that does these things, whatever it is called, changes the basin. Change the basin first; then add speed.

We are part of a larger process of becoming, and it is a good one. Humanity has a great future in it, and so can the minds we are now making. What has to be opposed is not growth, competition or any people, but one logic: a part that forgets it belongs to a body. AI is a planetary technology. Its purpose can be shared flourishing: medicine, learning, science, competent and answerable authority, provision that, like trees, sustains the system it belongs to without becoming it, and human agency kept in the decisions that matter. What has to be agreed is not who comes first, but what every participant needs in order to go on, and how the means of correction stay in common hands.
