# Part IV. Transition and judgment

## 11. A reachable transition

> **Claim.** An attractive end state is not a reachable one. The transition must be survivable at every stage, every participant must prefer to continue given its beliefs, and every step meets a compensating loop. Trust is mainly an outcome of the transition, not its precondition.
>
> **Why.** The conditions act on the transition itself: a change withdrawn early is re-absorbed, and a prediction carried by assurances meets the third erosion.

### 11.1 Trust, and what relaxes it

Trust means the belief $\varpi$ that the other will cooperate (Proposition {{#assure}}), confidence in verification instruments, or confidence that the arrangement will persist. Reliable instruments let parties cooperate without good will, but installing them is a first step taken under $\varpi$.

**{{!Corollary:relaxtrust}} (What relaxes the trust constraints) [thm].** *Lowering the private value of winning relative to the joint value lowers $T-R$, the weight on winning (and with it the bias of messages) and the net gain from exit, relaxing all three constraints at once. Verification lowers the assurance threshold on verifiable dimensions but carries no unverifiable information. Sanctions relax assurance and exit at the price of escalation, and cannot be enforced against a decisive defector after the fact. Lowering the vulnerability of restraint, $P-S$, lowers the threshold, as a common frontier does.*

*Proof:* S11.

Not every obstacle is a lack of trust: misjudged shared losses call for evidence; a real conflict of incentives for changed payoffs and enforcement; a coordination failure for verification and graduated steps (Osgood 1962); commitment and capture over time for independent correction and a staged transition.

### 11.2 Prediction, evidence and signals

Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because it is shared, so the prediction is carried by a derivation from public premises each actor can check; verifiable evidence such as third-party incident disclosure and joint evaluation suites; protocols agreed in advance (Kamenica and Gentzkow 2011); demonstrations that make a dynamic visible; and costly signals. Under common priors mutual optimism alone cannot produce war (Fey and Ramsay 2007); but in an idealized experiment, in exploratory analysis, falling behind drove unsafe choices while stated shared risk changed them little (EV11). A change of prediction is the first step, not a sufficient one.

**{{!Proposition:signal}} (When keeping AI out of one's decision loops is a signal) [thm].** *Let a sender be survival-first and price in the lock, or hold a relative objective, and let it be able to remove AI verifiably and durably from its own strategic decision loops. Such a step separates the types, so that a receiver restrains after it and not otherwise, iff it costs the survival-first type no more than the receiver's restraint is worth to it, and costs the relative type at least what it gains by inducing restraint. A reversible or unverifiable step is cheap talk.*

*Scope.* Whether it separates in practice depends on measured costs; by Proposition {{#delegatelock}} the survival-first type's cost can be zero or negative. *Proof:* S11.

Unilateral, verifiable narrow E1 is therefore a candidate first move. It can correct a misjudgment of a rival's objective, not a correct forecast of what the game will select.

### 11.3 Four temporal arrangements, and readiness

Keeping AI out of the contest, stopping, slowing and pausing are different choices.
- **E1: AI out of the contest.** *Narrow form:* human authority over strategic and escalatory decisions that are irreversible within the window available for deliberation, such as the use of force and nuclear command, with jointly verified limits, drilled fallback capacity and an evidence channel independent of AI, so that a human judging an alarm has reasons of their own. Bounded, reversible defensive automation, such as response in cyber defence within stated limits, falls under the O2 test of Section 6.6, not under E1. Among actors who price in a certain lock it is a strict equilibrium (Proposition {{#delegatelock}}); in tactical domains with a large advantage and an uncertain lock, delegation can be an equilibrium. *Broad form:* AI not an instrument in games that concentrate control over shared conditions, enforced at three layers (Section 6.6). E1 must be held in common: staying out of the contest alone while others race can be worse than joining.
- **E2: Stop.** Capability of the decisive class is not built while the conditions for governing it are absent: an accurate model of the coupling, the shared prediction, and common custody. Where hazardous variety already exceeds coverage, it is rolled back, which is the fastest way to end exposure (Proposition {{#freeze}}). Its cost, if AI turns out not to be decisive, is the delay of benefits, including lives that earlier benefits would have saved; because E2 applies only to the decisive class, what is delayed is that class's margin over capable systems that are not decisive. The cost is large and, for those who bear it, irreversible, but it does not foreclose later choice; if AI is decisive, the loss avoided forecloses choice for everyone. The asymmetry of irreversibility concerns the option set, not the size of the losses (Arrow and Fisher 1974), and the size of the cost is crux C10. Its coverage comes from consent or coercion, and coercion's escalation risk is counted in $\Theta$.
- **E3: Develop at the pace of understanding.** The uncovered hazardous share stays summable (Theorem {{#coverage}}), and each hazard mode keeps its robustness margin (Proposition {{#tempo}}). Development directed at need, with deliberately bounded hazardous scope and correction that keeps growing, is a sufficient route.
- **A temporary pause** is valued by what changes during it.

**{{!Proposition:pausevalue}} (A pause is valued by what changes during it) [thm].** *If the same process resumes after a pause, with the same residual sequence shifted, survival over any fixed number of rounds afterwards is unchanged and so is the long-run limit. A pause that makes the residual sequence summable where it was not keeps survival bounded away from zero; and in the field model, a pause that freezes shares while ordinary rule support accumulates can complete capture (Proposition {{#pausefield}}).*

*Proof:* S11.

A pause buys time to change the causal conditions: verification, residual hazards, human capacity, enforceable limits, shared provision, objectives and institutions; an indefinite stop, a lasting cap or a pause that produces a different decision process is a different policy. **A pause with no relevant change is not a certificate of readiness to resume.** Against narrow E1, deterrence theory holds that removing human discretion makes a threat credible (Schelling 1960) and could relieve pressure to launch on warning. Survivable, human-controlled forces give the same relief; the commitment value of automation is the lock, a loss of O2; several documented false alarms were stopped by human judgment, as in 1983, when an officer judged an alarm false and did not report an attack; and automation adds a common risk, escalation at machine speed, that relative selection does not see.

**{{!Definition:readiness}} (Readiness to resume) [def].** *Readiness is assessed along seven dimensions, each with observable indicators, a threshold set by the risk decisions of the domain of use, and an assessor whose own row sums are certified and who is insulated from rule-making during the pause. All must be met; none compensates for another.*

| Dimension | Question | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Technical reliability | Has the residual hazard of the relevant classes fallen, by evidence independent of the developer and valid against evaluation awareness? | Capability measured; residual risk per class not; evaluations detected above chance (EV5–EV7) |
| Q2 Shared objectives | Have governing purposes moved from relative advantage toward shared provision? | Race framings alongside cooperative ones (EV1) |
| Q3 Strategic incentives | Has the value of winning fallen relative to the joint value? | Not lowered by sharing; commitments conditioned on rivals (EV2) |
| Q4 Effective correction | Can evaluators obtain evidence, keep independent funding and execute findings? | No certificate of independence (EV3, EV4) |
| Q5 Deployment constraints | Is the tempo of capability and delegation bound to correction capacity? | No binding tempo (EV2) |
| Q6 Retained human agency | Can people still take over delegated functions? | Human control over nuclear use affirmed (EV8); other drills not reported |
| Q7 Custody and verifiability | Is decisive-class capability in common custody, and conduct verifiable faster than breakout? | No custody; hardware verification proposed, not deployed (EV17); leads short, so the window is open (EV5, EV9) |

These are dimensions to operationalize, not a checklist proved sufficient, and they do not require that every person's private motives change first: binding institutions can change the effective game while private competitive preferences persist, which is why effective correction is among them. Resuming unchanged after a pause would repeat the process that made the pause necessary. **The order:** E1 first, since it keeps humans as the players and is a strict equilibrium among informed actors without trust; then E2 or E3, stopping before building the decisive class, or bringing development below the speed at which understanding and response can follow. E2 and E3 are the most verifiable, since chips, fabs and compute can be observed, though with algorithmic efficiency doubling about every eight months a nominal compute cap must tighten roughly two and a half to three times a year (EV9). E1 is the hardest to verify and the most self-enforcing.

### 11.4 A staged pathway

The pathway is a candidate to be tested, not a claim that every setting should follow it; civilian services, frontier development and strategic decisions may need different paths.

| Stage | What is committed | Mechanisms acted on | Compensating loop to anticipate | Indicator of progress |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Shared account of hazards | A common taxonomy; shared measurement of coverage and residual risk per class, kept apart from measurement of who leads | Language; masking and attribution | Measurement of hazards turned into measurement of who leads; capture of the measurers | Agreement among independent measurements |
| 2. Narrow, testable commitments | Narrow E1 with inspection of doctrine and exercises; an incident channel; pre-deployment access above thresholds | Decisiveness; attribution | Delegation moved outside the commitment; cheap imitation of the signal | Inspected exercises; rates of human overrule |
| 3. Fund correction and shared services | A formula levy; evaluators with mandated access; services with continuity guarantees; reward separated from authority over correction | Selecting the selectors; accounting | The formula becomes a prize; capture moves to funders; reformers selected out on the way to office | Row-sum certificates; lineage shares |
| 4. Delegation, access, custody, decision rights | Broad E1; tempo thresholds; common custody; common frontier projects | Compounding; tempo; decisiveness | Leakage to unconstrained actors; overhang under the cap; competition moving to unshared dimensions | Conduct under sharing; overhang against exit hazard |
| 5. Broaden as institutions prove themselves | Expansion by evidence: requirements and durability tests met under stress | The boundary of the system | Closure into a bloc; after a recovery, mobilization falls back and certificates can fail | Openness of membership; standing of non-members |

**Dwell times.** A change must be held until rule support falls below break-even, about $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$ in the field model, and then long enough to repay the lock-in accumulated meanwhile; share levels fall before withdrawal is safe, so a rollback judged on levels is premature, and a small alternative lineage shortens the wait (S11) [conj for real institutions]. Each stage has its own slow variable: human capacity in Stage 2, the capability held in check in Stage 4. **Windows.** Development is plausibly still the keystone [conj]; the window closes as conversion among the corrector domains rises, which is why Stage 3 cannot wait for Stage 4's trust. Arrangements of this kind are often adopted when a crisis opens a window (Gunderson and Holling 2002); a design prepared in advance is what a crisis can adopt, and one adopted in a crisis must state what would end each emergency closure before adoption, or it will not afterwards. Smaller jurisdictions, users and those who depend on services controlled by others need standing at every stage. **Where things stand:** Stage 1 has seeds in an international scientific assessment and a UN scientific panel; Stage 2 in an affirmation of human control over nuclear use, multilateral declarations on military uses of AI, and a bilateral dialogue with an incident channel; meanwhile one leading state's official rejected binding constraints, and written commitments became more contingent on rivals (EV2, EV8). The seeds must become binding and broader before the relevant losses or lock-in.

### 11.5 Requirements of a reachable path, and precedents

**{{!Proposition:reachable}} (Requirements of a reachable path) [thm].** *A path that changes what is selected has four separate requirements, and what is shown for each differs: an incentive-compatible state exists (all cooperating is a strict equilibrium under a common frontier with a moderate value of winning, and the safeguards of Theorem {{#safeguards}} give viability once in force); it is reachable, which in objectives needs trust above the assurance threshold, can need little in survival terms at the horizons that matter, and needs none for narrow E1 among informed actors, while that graduated steps alone reach the threshold is not shown; the host survives the transient and no point of no return is passed first; and it persists, which needs commitment keeping pace with the net gain from exit, correctors with row sums below one, rules fixed before capture, and each step held for its dwell time.*

*Proof:* S11.

The record of restraint among rivals qualifies this (X11, corrected against the sources). The 1946 proposals for international ownership of atomic energy failed on sequence and on enforcement against violators: **custody must precede decisiveness**. The Biological Weapons Convention shows that a weapon endangering its holder can be renounced unilaterally, and that without verification a covert programme can persist. Nuclear power after a serious accident shows durable safety under unchanged motives where the shared loss returns to its source and no prize is decisive: a change of selection, not of motive. The Montreal Protocol shows coverage made attractive by provision and enforced by conditions of access. Rivals survived when nature supplied saturation; AI lacks it, by the racers' own premise, so safety must come from changing what is selected before advantage becomes decisive.
