# Part I. Mechanism: what the conditions of winning do

## 2. The conditions of winning and their reflexive closure

> **Claim.** Three conditions, holding together, produce a selection pressure of winning: selection by relative success, winnings that convert into the capacity to win again, and capacity that converts decisively into winning. Where success can also buy control over the selection itself, the pressure closes on itself; that closure is the meta-attractor of winning.
>
> **Why.** The conditions are not a list of separate causes. Near equal standing they combine into one loop gain, a single number that decides whether leads compound, and the same number prices standing for every actor. Sharing, provision that does not have to be won, diffusion and correction lower it; decisiveness and every channel through which success buys advantage raise it.

### 2.1 Success is not contribution

Every selection process favours something. It need not favour the continued functioning of the system in which selection occurs.

Consider two organizations that depend on the same shared infrastructure. One spends resources maintaining it. The other redirects those resources into expansion while continuing to use what others maintain. Neglect becomes a competitive advantage when maintenance costs are immediate, deterioration is delayed, and those harmed cannot make the expanding organization bear the consequences. The neglectful organization need not be more capable in general. It need only be more capable at securing the resources that decide survival and expansion. If those resources let its practices spread, the system selects against some of the behaviour that keeps it functioning. **Success within a selection process is distinct from contribution to the conditions that support it.**

Selection on one dimension does not select the best. It removes the balanced. An agent that optimizes only the value that is measured and rewarded, and discards every other value, externalities included, will have the highest efficiency on that measure [corpus: meta-attractor]. What remains after many rounds is what the filter lets through.

Nothing here requires that people be or become selfish. A population of practices changes because some practices expand, are imitated or replace others, while many individuals keep their values. The question is not only what people believe is good but which practices receive the means to continue. The primitive is selection, not greed.

### 2.2 Three interconnected conditions

**{{!Definition:cond}} (The selection pressure of winning) [def].** Carriers are any reproducible units that can gain or lose share: cell lineages, practices, firms, research programmes, members of a federation, states, doctrines, trained AI systems. A system of carriers is under a *selection pressure of winning* when three conditions hold together, each to a degree.
1. **Relative selection.** What is won is allocated by success relative to the others; a share $\beta$ of it may be divided equally whatever the outcome.
2. **Convertibility.** Part of what is won converts into stocks that raise future winning: capacity, talent, data, standing, rule support. Stocks also come from sources not won in the contest: provision beneath the game.
3. **Decisiveness.** Stocks convert into winning by an increasing success function; $r$ measures how decisively.

When among the stocks that success can buy is control over the selection itself (the rules, the evaluators and correctors, the formation of minds, the language in which success is described), the pressure closes on itself (Section 2.3). The pressure needs no intention, no conspiracy and no particular culture; it acts on whatever is reproducible and can be won.

The conditions state formally a loop the author put in one sentence: if you use what you have to compete for more, what you win is yours, and you use it to keep competing, the race runs faster and faster [corpus: selfishness-spiral]. They combine in one quantity.

**{{!Proposition:loopgain}} (One loop gain) [thm].** The conditions multiply into a single loop gain; equal standing breaks when it exceeds one, and the same gain prices standing.

*Model.* $n$ carriers contest a prize $\Omega$ each period. A share $\beta$ is divided equally; the rest goes to carrier $i$ with probability or share $p_i=e^{\sigma_i}/\sum_ke^{\sigma_k}$, where strength $\sigma_i$ increases in $i$'s stocks $X_i^j$. Stock $j$ receives a share $\kappa_j$ of what is won, an inflow $k_{0j}\ge0$ not won in the contest, decays or is corrected at rate $d_j>0$, and diffuses toward the mean at rate $\delta_{f,j}$, with $d_j+\delta_{f,j}\le1$:
$${{!eq:stocks}}\qquad X_i^{j\prime}=(1-d_j)X_i^j+\kappa_j\Omega\big[(1-\beta)p_i+\beta/n\big]+k_{0j}-\delta_{f,j}\big(X_i^j-\bar X^j\big).$$
At equal standing write $r_j=\partial\sigma/\partial\ln X^j\ge0$ for the decisiveness of stock $j$ and $\omega_j=\kappa_j\Omega/(\kappa_j\Omega+nk_{0j})$ for the share of stock $j$ that is won. Let
$${{!eq:gamma}}\qquad \Gamma=(1-\beta)\sum_j r_j\,\omega_j\,\frac{d_j}{d_j+\delta_{f,j}}.$$
1. *Equal standing is locally asymptotically stable if $\Gamma<1$ and unstable if $\Gamma>1$. The channels add: compounding through capacity and the reflexive closure through rule support are terms of one sum, and channels each too weak to open the loop can open it together.*
2. *$\Gamma=0$ if any condition is removed: all of the prize divided equally ($\beta=1$), nothing convertible ($\kappa_j=0$ for all $j$), or no decisiveness ($r_j=0$ for all $j$).*
3. *For an actor that discounts at $\delta$ and reinvests its net payoff at the rates at which winnings convert, a unit of relative net payoff today is worth, to first order, $\Gamma_\delta/(1-\Gamma_\delta)$ units of its own future winnings, where $\Gamma_\delta=(1-\beta)\sum_jr_j\omega_j\,\delta d_j/\big(1-\delta(1-d_j-\delta_{f,j})\big)<\Gamma$ and $\Gamma_\delta\to\Gamma$ as $\delta\to1$.*

*Scope.* Local, at equal standing, for reinforcing channels and a prize of fixed size; in (3), others' strategies are held fixed. It does not decide global behaviour, whether a position beyond sanction can be reached, or whether capability outruns correction. The calculation is standard linear stability; what is new is the organization and its consequence for objectives (Section 3.5). *Proof:* S2; checked against numerical Jacobians and nonlinear simulation in 4,000 random systems without a mismatch [num].

The single-channel case gives the compounding condition in closed form, and with it the formal content of provision beneath the game.

**{{!Proposition:compound}} (When winnings compound, and what enough requires) [thm].** *Let capacity $K_i$ receive a share $\kappa_c$ of what $i$ wins, an inflow $k_0$ not won in the contest, depreciation at rate $d_K$ and diffusion at rate $\delta_f$, with $p_i=K_i^r/\sum_jK_j^r$, and let $S^\ast$ be total capacity at equal standing.*
1. *Equal standing is unstable if and only if $\kappa_c\Omega\big((1-\beta)r-1\big)>nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$. Without provision and diffusion the condition is $(1-\beta)r>1$, and $\kappa_c$ sets only the level of capacity. With provision, a smaller convertible share stabilizes equal standing.*
2. *A point of enough. If carriers stop reinvesting at a cap $\bar K$ no higher than equal-standing capacity, equal standing is locally stable. It is restored from every starting point if and only if a laggard's inflow exceeds its depreciation at every level below the cap. When $r>1$ that needs inflow not tied to rank, from sharing or provision; $\beta\kappa_c\Omega/n+k_0\ge d_K\bar K$ suffices. Without it, laggards below a threshold are excluded.*

*Scope.* Fixed prize. With growth in total capacity and no depreciation, the condition holds date by date with the current total, and is constant only if the stakes grow with capacity (S2). *Proof:* S2 [num: five cases of the cap, each matching the exact condition].

Removing any of the three ingredients of that sentence therefore opens the loop, with one qualification: share what is won until $(1-\beta)r\le1$; or make winnings non-convertible, which works only together with provision not won in the contest; or stop at a point of enough, which holds globally only with inflow not tied to rank. **Provision beneath the game is not only support for those who fall behind. It is what makes non-convertible rewards work, and what makes a point of enough hold.** One principle of the corpus acts on convertibility: a winner of the game should be rewarded with anything other than more advantage in the same game, or the game converges and the winner takes all.

**What the loop gain does not decide.** Two further conditions decide what the loop does to those who live with it, and neither is a function of $\Gamma$. The first is whether a position beyond any sanction can be reached: whether a lead can become decisive, and how fast against detection (Section 5.2). The second is whether capability grows faster than the correction that can contest it (Section 5.3). Among three or more actors, what relative accounting does to talk is not a function of $\Gamma$ either (Section 5.4). The core of the pressure is one loop gain; the pressure as a whole is a set of interconnected conditions, linked by shared parameters and by one shared state, the concentration of standing. Section 7.1 sets out the links.

### 2.3 The reflexive closure: the meta-attractor

An ordinary adaptive system improves its state $x$ against a fixed rule $\sigma$ that scores states. The pressure closes on itself when the rule is endogenous: when what can be won includes influence over the rules, the evaluators and correctors, the formation of the minds that will hold positions, and the language in which success is described. The outcome of the last round of scoring then decides who writes the next round's scorecard.

Consider a research community that allocates funding and positions by publication counts, because reliable knowledge is hard to evaluate directly. Practices that produce more publications receive more funding, attract more students and are more widely copied. Now add one step: their practitioners come to occupy the positions that decide recruitment, training and evaluation. The practices the metric favours begin to shape the institutions that choose and interpret the metric, and newcomers must adopt them to obtain the resources needed to do research at all. The decisive transition is from *being selected by a rule* to *helping reproduce the rule that selects you*. It needs no manipulation; ordinary decisions about whom to hire, what to teach and which evidence to collect suffice.

The loop need not preserve its winners. An organization can fail while its operating logic passes to its successors; a leader can be replaced by an opponent who finds that holding the position requires the same practices; participants can reject the system's consequences while reproducing the conduct their participation depends on. **The persistent entity can be a way of succeeding.** Structural explanation does not erase responsibility, which differs with knowledge, discretion and power. It explains why intentions alone cannot account for recurrence.

**{{!Definition:meta}} (Meta-attractor) [def].** Let the state update be $x'=F(x,\sigma)$ and the rule update $\sigma'=G(x,\sigma)$. Three objects are distinguished, and each claim in this paper says which it concerns.
1. **The mechanism: reflexive selection.** A positive loop runs through the rule: the rule moves resources toward some states, and the states so resourced move the rule further in their favour. In Proposition {{#loopgain}} it is every channel whose stock is control over selection.
2. **An attracting configuration in a specified model:** a compact invariant set of the joint flow, asymptotically stable, with a basin that is a proper subset of the space. A positive loop is necessary for multistability (Thomas's rule; Soulé 2003), not sufficient. The field model of Section 4 exhibits such configurations.
3. **A long transient:** trajectories stay near a region for a long time relative to one round of selection, without the region being attracting.

A *meta-attractor* is the mechanism of (1). Wherever this paper calls it attracting, the claim is (2), in the model where it has been shown; a claim of (2) about the world is a conjecture.

**The meta-attractor of winning** is the case in which the pressure of Definition {{#cond}} is the rule: success under relative selection converts into control over the selection that judges the next round. The conditional form keeps its conditions in the sentence: *recursive selection creates second-order selection for control over future selection when the resulting benefits are sufficiently appropriable, persistent and large relative to the costs of changing and defending the rules.* Where (2) holds, long-run behaviour maximizes the rule whatever purpose it was introduced to serve (Goodhart 1975; Campbell 1979), and by the time a measure has stopped measuring, the people and institutions it elevated decide whether it is replaced.

The pattern is not fated. Positive feedback can saturate, oscillate, split or break; energetic ceilings, heterogeneity, delays, constraints at other scales and reflexive awareness cap it. It is more like a hurricane than a black hole: a structure sustained by a flow, which decays when the flow is cut.

*Why "meta-attractor".* The name is kept over "generator functions" of catastrophic risk, which name causes without the reflexive step in which selection rewrites its own criterion, without persistence, without the layer of minds and language, and without a generative counterpart. One concept covers the mechanism and its generative dual, and every claim says which sense it uses.

**{{!Remark:bench}} (A local feedback benchmark).** With $u$ a small deviation in an actor's success and $v$ a deviation in its favourable control over the rules, $u'=(1-d_u)u+av$ and $v'=bu+(1-d_v)v$ with $a,b\ge0$ and $0<d_u,d_v\le1$, the system is stable exactly when $ab<d_ud_v$ [num: 200,000 draws]. This is the two-channel case of Proposition {{#loopgain}} written in deviations, and the stability condition of Richardson's arms-race model with control over rules in place of armaments (Richardson 1960). Its use is that each coefficient names a channel that can be measured. Instability does not prove capture or harm, and stability does not prove that an excursion stays within a safety constraint.

**{{!Remark:pathgen}} (Pathological and generative meta-attractors).** Reflexive selection is not itself the problem: science, the rule of law and open-source ecosystems also persist through the conditions they maintain. A meta-attractor is *pathological* to the degree that its reproduction depletes shared conditions that selection does not price, weakens the channels through which such costs register and trigger correction, or makes safe alternatives unreachable within the relevant time, budget and authority. It is *generative* to the degree that its reproduction strengthens independent correction and keeps alternatives reachable: success funds correctors it cannot control, and what is rewarded is contribution to the shared conditions. The way out is therefore not the abolition of selection but a change in what is selected for (Section 10). The corpus calls the passage "change the basin, then add speed".

### 2.4 Not bound to any substrate

The pressure needs only relative selection, convertible winnings and decisiveness; its closure needs only that the successful can reshape their selection. A cancer lineage evades the signals that limit proliferation, recruits surrounding tissue and evades the immune response that would remove it (Hanahan and Weinberg 2011; Aktipis et al. 2015). Markets with increasing returns lock in early leads (Arthur 1989), and the successful can buy the rules of the game (Stigler 1971). In security competition a gain for one is read as a threat to another. Members of a federation compete for capital and residents, and the strongest can capture the centre. In science, metrics allocate positions and the positions then set the metrics. In training, a policy is selected by a reward that a developer sets, and the developer is selected by a contest. Nothing in Definition {{#cond}} refers to a culture, a political system or a species: a civilization on another planet would face the same pressure wherever selection is relative, winnings convert and the successful can edit the selection. Discussion X2 gives the instances in full, and X1 the lineage of the ideas.

What is specific to AI is not the pressure but how far AI moves its parameters. It can raise decisiveness and the speed at which leads open. If it lowers the cost of control faster than the cost of independent correction, it strengthens the reflexive channels. It can raise the growth of hazardous behaviour relative to contestable coverage. And it can become a carrier itself (Section 6).

The rest of Part I follows one chain. The selection pressure of winning accelerates the contest. Acceleration makes the contest harder and harder to regulate: correction falls behind the tempo it must match, and where success reaches the selectors, the correctors become something success can buy. The same pressure alienates language. These overlap, and each consequence then acts as a mechanism of the others.
