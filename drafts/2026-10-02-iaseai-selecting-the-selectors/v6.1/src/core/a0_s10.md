## 10. The generative alternative

> **Claim.** The same reflexive structure that lets success buy control over future selection can make contribution fund the conditions of further contribution. The way out of an optimization trap is not a better optimization of the same objective, and not the abolition of selection, competition or growth: it is a change in what winning confers, so that the loop strengthens the shared host and independent correction instead of consuming them.
>
> **Why.** Sharing scales every channel of the loop gain down; provision that does not have to be won lowers the won share of every stock; and cutting the conversion of success into control over its own assessment removes the reflexive channels. The generative order persists through what all share, not through its winners, and its form follows function by function.

### 10.1 The purpose

Cooperation is not only a constraint on danger. Its purpose is what AI can do for people when it is not the instrument by which one actor wins the common future: medicine and public health, education, science, competent administration, infrastructure, and the long-horizon work that fragmented incentives under-supply. The joint value is very large; the race dissipates it, and a change in what is selected realizes it.

**Authority as a productive capacity (V4).** Authority can coordinate investment, guarantee services, support learning and act on shared purposes. The ideal the theory draws on justifies authority by its responsibility for improving people's lives and the historical trajectory; it enters the design as a purpose to be sustained, not as a premise that it is realized, and how institutions select officeholders, hear unwelcome evidence and revise harmful practices are causal variables. The corpus states the principle as "where power reaches, responsibility follows" (力之所及，责之所在): a larger causal radius brings larger responsibility, not larger entitlement. With the norm of a shared future (Section 3.7), it is the institutional consequence of the relational self.

**Provision that does not become the system.** Taken out of the contest, AI can be what infrastructure is: provision of the conditions of a decent life, built to meet need rather than to gain standing. Trees are the image. They give shelter and fruit, maintain the system they belong to, and do not become it. Such provision is *distributed*, many providers below the size at which influence over correctors pays (Proposition {{#influence}}); *good enough*, stopping growth once a need is met, which keeps it within correction and confers no decisive advantage (Proposition {{#enough}}); *replaceable*, keeping human capacity above its lock-in floor (Lemma {{#lockin}}); and *for everyone*, not for a class of guardians. Productive contribution is rewarded with revenue, contracts and recognition; what success does not buy is the right to appoint evaluators, set thresholds, exclude entrants or amend the governing rules (V5).

### 10.2 What winning confers: sharing, convertibility and provision beneath the game

Divide the prize of the contest into a *divisible* part $\Omega_e$, rents, revenues, services and the material gains of capability, and a *positional* part $\Omega_s$, decisive standing, the power to set the rules, and the loss of being subordinated. A common frontier, in which cooperators pool effort and divide its value without subordinating one another, turns a dilemma into a coordination problem while the value of winning is moderate (**{{!Proposition:commonfront}}**, **{{!Proposition:commonfrontn}}**, S10); but allocation within a joint project is itself a contest, and the project, as the largest carrier, has the largest return to influence over its correctors.

**{{!Proposition:share}} (What sharing, pooling and convertibility change) [thm].** Sharing and provision can stabilize equal standing; none of them makes the selected intensity zero while a positional prize remains. *In the contest of Section 3.4:*
1. *Pooling payoffs, each receiving $1-\beta$ of its own payoff plus $\beta$ times the average, makes an actor holding $\theta_0>-1$ act as if its self-model rose strictly with $\beta$ toward $n-1$; at $\theta_0=-1$, and under relative selection, payoff differences only scale by $1-\beta$.*
2. *Sharing a fraction $\beta$ of the divisible prize, with the promise kept with probability $\varrho$, is the contest with prize $\Omega_{\rm eff}=(1-\beta\varrho)\Omega_e+\Omega_s$; intensity, dissipation and race hazard are proportional to it, and under relative selection in one field with $r\le1$ the uninvadable intensity $r\Omega_{\rm eff}/(nc)$ stays positive for every $\beta<1$, and for every $\beta$ when $\Omega_s>0$.*
3. *Changing what winning converts into: equal standing is unstable iff $\kappa_c\Omega((1-\beta)r-1)>nk_0+\delta_fS^\ast$ (Proposition {{#compound}}). Without provision not won in the contest, the convertible share sets only the level; making winnings non-convertible stabilizes standing only together with such provision. If success cannot be converted into control over correction ($\chi=0$), the reflexive channel of the loop gain vanishes, the return to influence over correctors is zero, and the required correction rate falls to its value without second-order selection.*

*Proof:* S10 [num: with a positional part as large as the divisible one, sharing all of the divisible prize only halves the intensity].

**Three levers, and what each does.** Pooling widens the effective self of anyone who values its absolute payoff, and does nothing under relative selection. Sharing scales every channel of the loop by $1-\beta$; it can stabilize equal standing but does not change the sign of the selected intensity while a positional part remains, and it works only if credible: contemporaneous, escrowed or under shared control, before decisiveness. Changing what winning converts into lowers the won share of each stock, which needs provision not won in the contest, or removes a channel outright, which is what $\chi=0$ does to the reflexive closure. **The positional part cannot be divided.** Lowering decisiveness does not remove it, since a lottery can award a position beyond sanction. It can only be prevented from forming, by verified limits on what any winner may do, or held in common custody. What a design must limit is what winning confers.

**{{!Remark:twoorders}} (Concentration at two orders).** At the first order more contenders make the race fiercer; at the second, cultivated influence rises with a high moment of shares, which calls for removing common control. The design that serves both merges the frontier and disperses and insulates correction, keeping several lineages inside the merged frontier against common-mode failure. Neither "centralize" nor "decentralize" is the answer, because the two orders call for opposite forms.

### 10.3 Safeguards, verification and participation

Four safeguards make consequences return on the host of Section 3: **(I1)** a liability above the private advantage of load, in advance for anticipated harms and on the alarm for others, so that effective liability exceeds the advantage once rule-making is uncaptured (for harms beyond assets, regulation in advance; near a threshold, a quantity rather than a price); **(I2)** independent correctors in evaluation and rule-making, with $d(t)$ above $d^\ast(t)$ by a margin; **(I3)** independent evidence, by masking kept below threshold or a conservative alarm on an independently established bound; and **(I4)** all three in force before the corrector domains are captured.

**{{!Theorem:safeguards}} (Sufficient safeguards, and a necessary condition) [thm].** *Let selection be replicator dynamics among carriers differing only in load, with a carrier of low enough load present.*
1. *Sufficiency. Under (I1)–(I4), effective liability exceeds the advantage after a finite time and the load falls to its minimum. On the host {{eq:host}} the host's long-run level exceeds its floor, and the trajectory is viable if, in addition, the host stays above its floor while the load falls. On a host whose regeneration is lower at every level and which degrades by itself below a threshold, sufficiency also needs a liability margin above a minimum set by the host, the initial composition and the speed of selection, and a floor at or above the host's own threshold.*
2. *Non-redundancy. For each of (I1)–(I3) there are parameters satisfying the others under which the host crosses its floor, and parameters under which they come into force only after capture and the host crosses it.*
3. *A necessary condition. Under (reach), with a race carrier present, every trajectory viable at every horizon has long-run average effective liability at least the private advantage.*

*Scope.* The transient is real: with liability in force from the start but the race strategy holding 99% of activity, the host dips to 0.14 before recovering [num]. On a pointwise harsher threshold host the required margin rises with the threshold (S10), the first computable instance of the correctable kernel. *Proof:* S10.

**{{!Proposition:rivalverify}} (Rival verification) [thm].** *A rival's verifier whose full inbound row sum is below one, counting conversion from the verified party, from its own side and from shared sources such as models, supply chains and professional paradigms, is captured by none of them. Rivalry keeps the verified party's contribution near zero; it does not by itself bound the rest of the row.*

*Scope.* Rival verifiers raise false alarms, can collude against third parties, check compliance rather than judgment, and an accusation under rivalry can trigger prevention, so joint resolution precedes any response. *Proof:* S10.

**{{!Proposition:participate}} (Participation) [thm].** *Under complete information an arrangement is efficient for a pair of types iff its survival-weighted joint value exceeds the sum of their survival-weighted race payoffs, and an overwhelmingly strong actor takes part only for a share near its race payoff. Under private types, an arrangement without verification must give each type an interim share independent of its report, and is feasible iff the joint value covers the sum of the types' best race payoffs; verification that reveals types removes the information rents, and the constraint relaxes as survival in the race falls below survival under the arrangement.*

*Scope.* Rare strong types expect much from the race: mutual optimism, a failure of bargaining under private information (Fearon 1995). *Proof:* S10 [num: feasible without verification when strong types are common, not when they are rare, although cooperation is efficient for every pair].

Participation benefits, voting rights and protection against unilateral domination are three different things, and a design keeps them apart. Participation can be made individually rational through shares of value and access to services; voting rights need not follow capability; and protection against domination is fixed first and not traded for participation. If, for some set of actors, participation and the independence of correction cannot both be had, the design is infeasible for them, and should say so rather than buy participation with control over correction.

### 10.4 The generative loop and its closure

The corpus states two loops with the same reflexive structure and opposite signs [corpus: shengsheng]. In the *generative loop*, security makes trust possible, trust makes cooperation possible, cooperation produces abundance, abundance allows sufficiency and slack, and these allow wider care and longer foresight, which produce better order and more security. In the *degenerative loop*, fear produces defence, defence struggle, struggle seizure and concentration, these scarcity and resentment, and trust collapses. The first turns more agents into generators; the second into defenders, instruments or exiles. The criterion is generative or degenerative, not cooperative or competitive: cooperation on the wrong thing can be worse than competition on it, and a cartel is cooperation. Bounded competition in products, science and service continues inside a generative order; what is removed is competition for control over the shared conditions and the correctors. The generative loop has four strands, each the dual of a loop of Section 7.1, each with a carrier, a condition and a test. Goodwill is not a carrier.

| Strand | Dual of | Carrier | Condition | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reasoning instead of winning** (讲理 rather than 讲赢) | Alienated language | Derivations from public premises; verifiable evidence; protocols agreed in advance; costly signals | Objectives not opposed on the topic of talk | Informativeness between rivals rises as contested stakes fall |
| **Reward separated from advantage** | Compounding and conversion | Separation of reward from authority over correction; contemporaneous sharing | Loop gain below one with margin; $\chi\to0$; a cap on any provider's share of essential services | Conduct changes in proportion to what is shared (C6) |
| **Provision beneath the game** | The belief in a struggle for survival | Shared provision, good enough, distributed, replaceable: the $k_0$ of the loop gain | Not tied to standing; no inferiority in receiving it | The weight on relative position falls where provision is secure |
| **Correction in common hands, plural and decorrelated** | Selecting the selectors; masking | Certified correctors; lineages that do not fail from one cause; exit from providers | Funded by formula, appointed at arm's length, growing at the required rate | Correctors' row sums stay below one as the field grows |

For persons, provision beneath the game removes the ground of the belief that one must defeat others first; in the corpus's words, the common foundation does not prescribe how anyone lives, it lets everyone be entitled to live without having to defeat others first. For states, service obligations and common custody act on survival itself; for providers, $k_0$ is literally capability not won: a common layer of compute, evaluation infrastructure and services. Each strand faces the questions the pathological loops answer: how it keeps obtaining resources, how it meets free riding and the pressure to expand, how it allows itself to be corrected, and how it avoids turning "protecting the whole" into a purpose no one can challenge. Exit is free from providers and services, where it is selection with the opposite sign; it is not free from the common constraints, whose protection depends on there being no outside to them.

**How the generative order persists.** In the pathological meta-attractor the loop runs through the winners: success buys support for the rule that selected it. In the generative order it cannot, because the second strand cuts that conversion, and support that no carrier can claim is a term common to all, which relative selection cancels. The generative order is therefore not selected *among* carriers. Inside it carriers are still selected, for usefulness; what is not selected among them is the rule itself. The rule persists the way a common constraint persists: by coverage, so no one can step outside the common floor; by financing fixed in formula, so its correctors depend on no one's success; and by a loop that runs through the whole, in which provision, security and slack make the rule worth keeping to everyone at once. Its positive loop runs through the system, not through its winners; its negative loop gives it correctability, funding correctors keyed to no one. A design in which those rewarded for contribution come to hold the positions that maintain the rule is the examination system's path, in which the form of contribution becomes the object of selection. **Is the generative order an attractor?** Because it is not selected among carriers, whether it attracts is a question about the whole-level loop and coverage, and it is a conjecture; its persistence has to be designed and measured. A cooperative order whose loop gain greatly exceeds its damping also resists correction of its own errors, so plurality of purposes, correctors and lineages is a requirement, not a decoration: harmony makes things grow, sameness does not continue (和实生物，同则不继).

**The reform dilemma.** Reform needs influence, and the game is what produces influence. The path by which a reformer gains the capacity to change the rules runs through the selection the reform is meant to change, so it tends to select the reform out before the capacity arrives: those who reach the positions from which rules are changed are those the rules favoured, and they arrive formed by them. This is the guardian problem in motion. The theory has two answers, and neither is a better guardian. Reform can act where the selection has not yet closed: at a keystone while its window is open (Section 4.5), or in a crisis with a design prepared in advance (Section 11.4). And the capacity to reform can be built outside the contest for influence, by formula-funded correction keyed to no one, so that it does not have to be won.

**{{!Remark:guardian}} (The guardian test).** Every remedy for the selection of selectors is itself a selector, and no better guardian is an answer; what can be an answer is a structure in which every guardian is guarded and errors cannot grow without limit. Every arrangement, and every institution within it, is asked four questions before adoption. *Capture:* are its correctors' full inbound row sums below one, counting every party and shared source? *Withdrawal:* does every closure it makes state what would end it? *Totalization:* can it make itself the whole, so that "protecting the whole" becomes a purpose no one can challenge? *Distribution of understanding:* does understanding go to everyone, or to a class whose position depends on others' not understanding?

### 10.5 Design by function

The design question is usually posed along ideological axes: centralization or decentralization, the individual or the collective, markets or states. The mechanisms support neither pole. Each counter-mechanism requires a particular form, and the forms differ by function.

| Function | What the mechanism requires | Form that follows |
|---|---|---|
| Common constraints: admission, return of consequences, levies | Coverage of every capable actor, equal incidence, sanctions above the gain from evasion (Lemma {{#blind}}) | **One rule for all** |
| Custody of capability of the decisive class | No unilateral holding before decisiveness forms (Corollary {{#restraint}}) | **Common custody**, or not built |
| Correction and evaluation | Independence and decorrelation; row sums below one; measured error correlation | **Many hands**, certified, of different lineage |
| Who sets standards | One standard with many measurers makes standard-setting a keystone, and Goodhart acts on it | **Standard-setters certified and rotating, separated from measurers and measured, with held-out metrics registered in advance** |
| Measurement and language | Shared, checkable, dynamic indicators (Remark {{#alarm}}) | **One standard, many measurers** |
| Learning about the design itself | Global, irreversible failures allow no second attempt; reversible ones can be learned from (Lemma {{#onegroup}}) | **Bounded parallel variants above a common floor, with shared evaluation and adoption by evidence** |
| Provision of essential services | Good enough, distributed, replaceable | **Distributed provision under common guarantees** of continuity and portability |
| The frontier of hazardous capability | Merging lowers the race at the first order and raises the return to influence at the second; it also loses yardstick competition and lineage diversity | **Merged accounting and custody of the frontier, plural lineages inside it, correction outside it** |
| Authority (V4) | Answerable to those affected; not purchasable by success | Set by row sums and contestability, **not by the degree of concentration** |

**Federations as an illustration.** Competition among members for capital and residents can become a race to the bottom, the pressure of winning among members, and the strongest members can capture the centre, the selection of the selectors. Federations answer at both orders. At the first, common minimum standards bind every member, and fiscal equalization gives members capacity they do not have to win: it both widens the effective self of members that value their absolute payoff and supplies the $k_0$ of the loop gain. At the second, equal representation of small members and supermajorities for constitutional change keep the centre from being bought by the largest, a precedent for voting that does not follow compute. The same federal form can select for winning or for contribution; what decides is what it rewards.

**A candidate configuration.** One configuration is developed in Discussion X10 far enough to expose its assumptions, as one candidate among several that Section 12.1 compares under the same range of uncertainty: a polycentric complex of mutually recognized institutions, and a verification agreement with shared provision, are others. Its principle is that it is a set of separately held functions, not one institution. Common provision, standard-setting, evaluation, rules and the levy formula, execution, custody, a chamber of affected persons, and appeal are each held by a different body, separately financed and separately corrected, so that correction lies outside what it corrects. On a planet "outside" means outside the function: a different source of funds, a different lineage, and certificates anyone can recompute from public data. No body both revises the levy formula and appoints evaluators, and neither executes findings; supermajorities and the chamber's consent govern the slow loops, amendment and the formula, while the fast loop, restricting a specific capability, stays fast with review after the fact. The levy is indexed to the coverage each hazard class requires, not to capability or perceived harm, and is itself treated as a prize. The configuration states its end: if coverage falls below its participation threshold, or a stated number of its certificates fail for a stated period, it dissolves into its separately held functions, while protections against unilateral domination stay entrenched. Its coercive fallback, a restriction of inputs to the decisive class when execution through a host jurisdiction fails, is treated exactly as Section 8.3 treats prevention, and the theory does not derive that its net effect is positive. By Proposition {{#influence}} such an arrangement is the largest conversion target the theory describes, so capture of its own correctors (D4) is its hardest case, not a design row. Where participation and independent correction cannot both be had, the design is infeasible there, and says so.
