## S8. Comparing paths

### Proof of Lemma {{#fallback}}

Couple the two paths by a common uniform draw $U_t$ each period: the event occurs on a path at $t$ when $U_t$ falls below that path's conditional hazard given the shared world history. On every history on which both have survived to $t$, the two conditions give $A$'s hazard at most $C$'s, so $\{A\text{ fails at }t\}\subseteq\{C\text{ fails at }t\}$. By induction the event "$C$ survives to $H$" is contained in "$A$ survives to $H$", path by path, whatever the rule by which $A$ dissolves, so $\Phi^A(H)\ge\Phi^C(H)$. $\square$

*A step that fails.* Comparing after conditioning on the dissolution date, $\Phi^A(H\mid\tau)\ge\Phi^C(H)$ for every $\tau$, is not valid when dissolution depends on the state: in a two-state Markov world in which bad states make dissolution more likely, the conditional comparison fails (0.291 against 0.324 in SM(4)), while the coupled comparison holds at horizons 10, 30 and 100. If $C$'s hazard is a deterministic schedule, the two conditions need only be stated on $A$'s histories. *Example (FD):* with dissolution at 2% a period and a fallback that restarts continuation ten periods later, the arrangement survives with probability 0.976, 0.864 and 0.211 at horizons 10, 30 and 100, against 0.875, 0.575 and 0.026 for continuation.

### Proposition {{#survassure}} in full

*Survival is computed with per-period conditional probabilities over a common horizon. Let $h_R$ be the hazard under restraint, $q_{\rm I}$ the loss of the ability to overrule by delegation, $q_{\rm II}$ the shared hazard, $q_W$ the hazard of preventive attack, $p_{\rm dec}$ and $p^S_{\rm dec}$ the probability that a racer's advantage becomes decisive when all race and when the actor stays out, and $p_{\rm ext}$ the actor's belief that a decisive winner would extend its dominance over it. With independence, $\Phi^R=1-h_R$, $\Phi^T=(1-q_{\rm I})(1-q^T_{\rm II})(1-q_W)$, $\Phi^P=(1-q_{\rm I})(1-q^P_{\rm II})(1-q_W)(1-p_{\rm dec}\frac{n-1}n)$, $\Phi^S=(1-q^S_{\rm II})(1-p^S_{\rm dec}p_{\rm ext})$. (1) The advantage of cooperating is $\varpi(\Phi^R-\Phi^T)+(1-\varpi)(\Phi^S-\Phi^P)$: dominance, stag hunt with threshold $(\Phi^P-\Phi^S)/((\Phi^R-\Phi^T)+(\Phi^P-\Phi^S))$, chicken, or prisoner's dilemma by the signs. (2) Where $\Phi^S\ge\Phi^P$ and $\Phi^R\ge\Phi^T$ an actor that holds the prediction restrains whatever it believes. (3) Mutual racing is a strict equilibrium at every horizon with $\Phi^P_H>\Phi^S_H$. (4) For constant hazards, $\Phi^B(H)/\Phi^R(H)=((1-q_B)/(1-q_R))^H$. (5) If an actor gives the premises of Section 5.3 probability $p_{\rm prem}$, the classification at horizon $H$ uses mixtures, and when the arrangement's own survival also decays the signs can fail at long horizons.* *Proof:* linear in $\varpi$; substitution. $\square$ *Numbers* ($n=2$, $p_{\rm ext}=1$): with $q_{\rm I}=0.6$, shared hazards 0.3 (0.4 when all race), $q_W=0.2$, $p_{\rm dec}=0.6$, $h_R=0.1$, survivals are 0.90, 0.22, 0.13 and 0.28 and cooperation is dominant; with low delegation the game is a stag hunt with threshold 0.16; for a leader that wins with probability 0.8 if it races, the threshold is 0.42 when the shared hazard is 0.3 and 0.11 when it is 0.8: for the leader, the shared hazard carries the argument. With $p_{\rm prem}=0.99$, the arrangement's survival $e^{-0.005H}$ and race branches decaying at 0.05 when the premises hold, the arrangement beats the mixed race at $H=10$, 100 and 600 but not at 1,000 (`../v5/checks_v5.py`, SR).

*Discounting.* Under a discounted objective with constant race hazard $h$ and growth rates $g_r$ racing and $g_p$ paced, a race is preferred iff $h<g_r-g_p$, though it ends in catastrophe with certainty (Jones 2024); a rising hazard reverses the preference only if it comes to exceed the growth differential. Jones (2024) also finds that falling mortality raises the acceptable risk, which belongs to crux C10.

### The alternatives: results stated briefly in the core

**Remark {{#lead}} (What a lead buys).** (1) With diffusion at rate $\delta_f$, a lead $\Delta_0$ held by a leader that pauses lasts $\delta_f^{-1}\ln(1+\delta_f\Delta_0)<\Delta_0$: a six-month lead diffusing at 0.1 a month lasts 4.7 months, a two-year lead about a year. (2) The margin is time, but what it must buy is coverage, which rises by about $\dot U\Delta$ while uncovered variety grows; for hazard classes with an evidence lag above $\Delta$ it buys nothing. (3) Whether it suffices is judged by the leader's own evaluators under time pressure. (4) In a static contest the follower raises effort when the leader slows (Dixit 1987); in dynamic races a large lead discourages it, and a leader seeing its window close has a motive to strike first (Van Evera 1999). On their own median estimates, the planners of one detailed scenario judge that spending part of a lead buys about a third of the reduction in takeover risk that a coordinated slowdown buys (EV10). *Proof of (1):* $\dot\Delta=-\delta_f\Delta-1$ (`../v4/checks_v45.py`, LD).

**Remark {{#sprint}} (The finite sprint as a trade-off).** A finite sprint trades four quantities: exposure during and after it, zero after a freeze if the hazardous ratio built is at most one (Proposition {{#freeze}}); erosion of the frozen edge as rivals developing within understanding catch up; rivals' responses, which cannot verify which kind of winner they face; and maintenance of the advantage, which requires stopping others' growth, ending their O2. It can preserve the sprinter's survival while giving up permanent superiority; excluding it as a deviation from a cooperative arrangement needs its net deviation payoff to be negative (Proposition {{#participate}}).

**Proposition {{#preventown}} in full.** *Facing a rival it believes certain to race, comparing by its own survival: (1) a single strike that succeeds with probability $s_P$, returning the field to something like the arrangement, and escalates to catastrophe with probability $\varepsilon_P$, gives $(1-\varepsilon_P)[s_P\Phi^R+(1-s_P)\Phi^P]$, and beats staying out and joining unless $\varepsilon_P>1-\max(\Phi^S,\Phi^P)/(s_P\Phi^R+(1-s_P)\Phi^P)$; (2) sustained prevention against a target that rebuilds with probability at least $b_P$ a period, each strike escalating with probability at least $\varepsilon_{\min}$, has per-period hazard at least $b_P\varepsilon_{\min}$; (3) at common horizons, sustained prevention with hazard 0.01 beats racing with 0.05 (0.60 against 0.08 at $H=50$).* *Proof:* compare and solve; Lemma {{#nonsum}}; $0.99^H>0.95^H$. $\square$ With $\Phi^R=1$, $s_P=0.7$, $\varepsilon_P=0.2$ and race branches near zero it gives 0.56. Shifts that cannot be observed raise the chance of mistaken prevention (Debs and Monteiro 2014); verification that supports deterrence can also supply targets (Coe and Vaynman 2020). An actor that manipulates shared risk as an instrument (Schelling 1966) plays chicken in survival terms, with mixed equilibria and wars of attrition. When the prior that a capable rival holds an unbounded relative objective is low, a survival-first actor's best course is to propose verifiable limits and take narrow E1 verifiably in its own loops, the costly signal of Proposition {{#signal}}.

**Remark {{#insure}} (Why insuring against falling behind is not an exit).** Bounding the penalty for falling behind with autonomous, good-enough provision and defence does not leave the contest: the penalty is set by the winner, not the laggard, so the bound is as reliable as the belief that a winner would not extend dominance; a winner with a relative objective has a motive to destroy what the laggard secured; and it accepts the frame of the contest. Defence that makes decisive advantage unattainable for everyone lowers $r$ for all and belongs to E2, if it stays under human oversight. Shared provision belongs to a world that has left the contest.

### Proofs for the verified agreement

**Proposition {{#assure}} in full.** *Payoffs $R,T,P,S$; a defection detected in time is reversed, the defector receiving $P-F$ and the cooperator $P$; undetected defection yields $T$ and $S$. With $T_\upsilon=\upsilon(P-F)+(1-\upsilon)T$, $g_C=R-T_\upsilon$ and $g_D=(1-\upsilon)(P-S)$: (1) the advantage of cooperating is $\varpi g_C-(1-\varpi)g_D$, with the four cases of the core and, if both are negative, chicken; (2) the verification and trust conditions as stated; (3) cooperation is risk-dominant iff $g_C>g_D$, a property of payoffs selected under global games and adaptive play (Carlsson and van Damme 1993; Kandori, Mailath and Rob 1993; Young 1993); (4) with $n$ players and independent belief $\varpi_1$ about each rival, $\varpi_1^{n-1}\ge\varpi^\ast$ suffices only if a cooperator gets at least $S$ when some but not all others defect and the advantage of defecting in any such state is at most $g_D$.* *Proof:* (1) cooperating yields $\varpi R+(1-\varpi)(\upsilon P+(1-\upsilon)S)$, defecting $\varpi T_\upsilon+(1-\varpi)P$; (4) bound the intermediate states. Without the second bound the condition fails: with advantages $(1,-10,-1)$ by number of defecting others in a three-player game, each rival cooperating with probability $\sqrt{0.5}$ gives $-3.73$ although all others cooperate with probability 0.5 (`../v5/checks_v5.py`, N13). $\square$

**Proposition {{#overhang}} (Commitment under a growing overhang) [thm].** *Let $\mathcal O(t)$ be the capability an agreement holds in check, $X$ and $X_{\rm stay}$ the values of exiting first and of staying, exit occur at hazard $\mathcal H$, a fraction $\mu_x$ of the overhang be destroyed on exit, and the expected loss from exit be $\mathcal L=\mathcal H(1-\mu_x)\Lambda_x(\mathcal O)$ with $\Lambda_x$ increasing. (1) Deterring an irreversible, public exit requires $\upsilon F\ge X-X_{\rm stay}$; deterring a reversible covert preparation requires $\upsilon F\ge(1-\upsilon)(X-X_{\rm stay})$; the net exit gain grows with the overhang when leaving with a decisive advantage gains faster than staying with one's share. (2) $\dot{\mathcal L}/\mathcal L=\dot{\mathcal H}/\mathcal H-\dot\mu_x/(1-\mu_x)+\varepsilon_\Lambda\dot{\mathcal O}/\mathcal O$, so growth of the overhang faster than the decline of the exit hazard is not sufficient for the loss to rise. (3) An agreement that preserves relative positions preserves the relative term of every payoff, and with it the motive in $X$.* *Proof:* expected values; differentiate $\ln\mathcal L$ (counter-case: $\mathcal O=e^{2t}$, $\mathcal H=e^{-1.5t}$, $\Lambda_x=\sqrt{\mathcal O}$, $\mu_x=0$ give $\mathcal L=e^{-0.5t}$); proportional caps leave each $w_i$ unchanged. $\square$

*Theorem {{#verify}}.* (1) Rearrange $R>T_\upsilon$. (2) Timely detection is detection before decisiveness. (3) collects Propositions {{#talk}}, {{#overhang}} and Section 4. (4) Bounding the largest undetected defection bounds $T$; the rate condition compares the coalition's advantage with its erosion by diffusion; revealing types is the information role of Proposition {{#participate}}. $\square$

*An instance.* One detailed plan among leading powers to slow development and delay superintelligence to about 2040 combines compute declarations, supply-chain accounting, inference-only retrofits and recomputation of workloads, research transparency, mutually assured destruction of compute if the deal declines, and hardened reserves of about two per cent of pre-deal compute; it bounds covert projects rather than requiring near-perfect detection, the second route of Theorem {{#verify}}. One author's scenario judgment puts decline at about four per cent a year in years five to ten, cumulatively near half over ten years (EV10; X8 gives the durability table).

### An illustration of the two cruxes

Let the per-period hazard on a path be 0.05 times its uncovered share plus a background 0.001, and on the cooperative path add a dissolution hazard times the loss its fallback carries. Survival at horizons 10, 30 and 100: continuation with the uncovered share rising from 0.2 by 0.01 a period, 0.871, 0.567, 0.028; continuation with a summable uncovered share, 0.966, 0.941, 0.873; cooperation with a summable share and dissolution 2% a period with fallback loss 0.5, 0.873, 0.696, 0.319; cooperation with dissolution 0.2%, 0.956, 0.913, 0.789. The numbers are illustrative; they show that a well-corrected continuation can beat a fragile arrangement, and that a rising uncovered share makes any modestly durable arrangement better past the near term.
