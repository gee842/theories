## S7. The present trajectory

### Proofs of Theorem {{#branches}}, Proposition {{#raceselects}} and Corollary {{#racepremise}}

*Theorem {{#branches}}.* Each item of Branch I is the cited result under its condition. Branch I′: above the saturating level further advantage changes no outcome, so $p_d=0$ there. Branch II: backward induction in the multi-period bargaining model of S5 shows that a shift spread over enough periods gives no motive for attack; modes with $a\tau_c<1$ are within proportional correction; and $p_d=\Pr(T_{\rm dec}<\tau)$ is small when detection and response are faster. Branch III: intensity, dissipation and shared loss are proportional to $r$ (Proposition {{#boundary}}(1)). That low decisiveness is not enough for Branch III follows from Proposition {{#loopgain}}: with $r=0.8$ a reflexive channel lifts $\Gamma$ to 1.1 (LG). $\square$

*Proposition {{#raceselects}}.* The first part is the commitment problem (Fearon 1995; Powell 2006), with uncertainty about intentions (Mearsheimer 2001); an actor that decides by its worst case over a stated set of possibilities, and applies that rule to every action (Gilboa and Schmeidler 1989), counts the worst, while a survival-first actor need not. For the second: if restraint while ahead lowers the probability of winning, Bayes' rule gives $\Pr(\text{extends}\mid\text{wins})>\Pr(\text{extends})$ for conduct before victory, and repeated selection compounds it (Proposition {{#load}}); carrying the inequality to conduct after victory needs the stated correlation. $\square$

*Corollary {{#racepremise}}.* Proposition {{#join}}(1) gives the individual best response; Propositions {{#wingives}} and {{#raceselects}} give the collective course. $\square$
