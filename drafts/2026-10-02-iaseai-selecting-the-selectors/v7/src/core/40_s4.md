## 4. Selecting the selectors

> **Claim.** In the models of this section the reflexive closure is a selection result: where influence over correctors can be kept by those who buy it, its return grows with the square of share, so it is selected in the largest carriers [thm]. Where AI lowers the cost of control, independent correction whose initial inbound bound is certified and which grows at a stated fraction of that rate, half under quadratic cost, keeps that certificate in force at any speed of adaptation covered by the rate law; preventing transient capture additionally needs initial-state and support bounds [thm]; without a per-unit cost floor, correction that grows more slowly loses evaluation to carriers whose influence tracks its selected level [thm]. Where correction rests on human capacity that delegation erodes, the required rate rises by the weighted rate of erosion [thm]. Reforms that leave conversion into the correctors above one are re-absorbed [thm]. Where the floor is within reach and capture of evaluation precedes the evidence of harm, a race crosses the host's floor before any averaged alarm fires [thm∣prem].
>
> **Why.** Correctors are not outside the field: the successful fund evaluators, grant access, author benchmarks and standards, and supply the models that judge [obs]. The capacity to convert success into rule support is a trait like any other, selected by the success it brings [thm].

### 4.1 Correctors, and what counts as capture

Correction makes costs return: evaluators report the host's condition, and rule-makers impose a liability $\tau$ in advance or when the reported condition $\tilde V$ falls to an alarm level $V_{\rm alarm}\in(V_{\min},1)$. The correctors are represented by a field of selection domains in which de facto power over rules is explicit (Acemoglu and Robinson 2008): development, evaluation, rules and liability, and the formation of those who will judge. In domain $i$ a share $x_i$ of consequential decisions follows the dominant practice, and success converts into rule support $z_i$ that favours it:
$${{!eq:field}}\qquad \dot x_i=\zeta_ix_i(1-x_i)(a_iz_i-c_i),\qquad \dot z_i=\varepsilon_i\Big(\textstyle\sum_jb_{ij}x_j-d_iz_i\Big).$$
Under baseline rules the practice carries a relative cost $c_i>0$, which rule support offsets at rate $a_i$; $b_{ij}\ge0$ converts success in $j$ into rule support in $i$ through funding, personnel, authorship, data, infrastructure and terms of access; and $d_i$ is the rate at which that support is corrected or decays. Write the conversion gain $G_{ij}=a_ib_{ij}/(c_id_i)$ and the inbound gain from a set $\mathcal C$ of domains $G_i(\mathcal C)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal C}G_{ij}$. A domain whose full inbound row sum $\sum_jG_{ij}$ is below one is *certified for eventual recovery*: from $x_i(0)<1$ and finite initial support, its share tends to zero whatever the other domains do ({{>certificate}}(2)). Captured rule-making accommodates the practice, so $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau(1-x_{\rm rule})$; $x_{\rm ev}$ is the share of evaluation conducted within the practice's lineage.

**{{!Definition:capture}} (Selector modification and capture) [def].** A *selector modification* is a change, produced by success, in control over a relevant future selector: an appointment, funding, access, standards or a rule-making channel. It is *capture* only when three things are separately documented: success changes control over the selector; the change creates a persistent advantage for an actor or coalition; and an independently specified entrusted purpose, protected interest or corrective function is impaired relative to an appropriate comparison. Capture runs in two directions: toward *accommodation*, where the rule bends toward the practice, and toward *barriers*, where the rule stands and its incidence falls on the practice's challengers.

Because selection is relative, relief of the practice and a burden on its challengers move the same difference of payoffs ({{blind}}(1)), so the rule support $z_i$ of {{eq:field}} stands for either direction. They differ in what shows them: accommodation in enforcement that softens; a barrier in correction's own form, a rule that stands and is enforced, which is why it is the easier to overlook. Since unequal incidence re-sorts the compliant ({{blind}}(2)), a captured rule-maker need not exempt the incumbent when it can impose on all a cost the incumbent bears most easily, such as a compliance cost fixed per carrier, which weighs most, per decision, on entrants and the smallest carriers (EV4). A rule that raises rivals' costs is still not by itself capture, which also asks for an impaired corrective function; what marks the channel is appropriability (S4 gives the forms each direction takes, and the test).

Concentration alone, or a rule change favourable to the successful, is not capture, and a legitimate revision of purpose is distinguished from capture by who revises it and whether those affected keep standing to object (V3). Concentration, capture, capture of the field, damage, collapse and closure are distinct, and none implies the next (LEDGER credits the distinctions to the first version). Power closes the loop when it determines the conditions of success rather than helping an actor succeed under them, and a claim that it does must show a transfer of costs onto weaker participants, a resulting change in their dependence, the conversion of that dependence into selection power, and a weakening of correction. Neither hierarchy nor contraction suffices, though contraction can intensify the loop (S4); shared exposure, enforceable constraint, reciprocal dependence or effective correction can each produce a different trajectory, so the framework validates no claim about any particular actor. What persists is a way of succeeding, not an identity (Section 2.3).

### 4.2 Influence over correctors is selected

Carriers differ in *conversion capacity* $q\ge0$: drafting standards, building evaluation tooling, authoring benchmarks, funding evaluators. A unit converts the carrier's success into rule support at rate $\hat b_{ij}$, and a fraction $\chi\in[0,1]$ of that support, the *appropriability*, is keyed to the carrier, such as standards that reference its interfaces or compliance costs that fall on its challengers. The carrier has share $p$ of decisions, distributed by a profile $\bar y$.

**{{!Assumption:costq}} (The cost of conversion capacity) [prem].** *A carrier with share $p$ pays $\mathrm{Cost}(q;p)=f\,p^{\xi_f}q+\gamma\,p^{\xi_v}q^{1+\eta}/(1+\eta)$, with $\eta>0$, a variable cost $\gamma>0$, a per-unit cost floor $f\ge0$ and size elasticities $\xi_f,\xi_v\in[0,1]$: with $\xi=0$ capacity is non-rival across the carrier's own decisions, with $\xi=1$ it is bought per decision. The floor is a marginal cost that every unit of capacity pays, such as a statutory procedure for each item of influence; it is not a lump sum. Two hypotheses about AI are stated as such, not as observations (EV4): $[H_\gamma]$, AI lowers the variable cost of control, $\gamma(t)=\gamma_0e^{-\alpha_{\rm ctl}t}$; and $[H_f]$, AI lowers the floor too, $f(t)=f_0e^{-\alpha_ft}$.*

Relative to an otherwise identical carrier with $q=0$, over a selection horizon, the carrier earns
$${{!eq:infret}}\qquad \Delta\pi(q)=\chi q\,p^2\,\bar y^\top M\bar y-\mathrm{Cost}(q;p),\qquad M_{ij}=c_iL_i\hat G_{ij},$$
with $\hat G_{ij}=a_i\hat b_{ij}/(c_id_i)$ and $L_i\in[0,1)$ the share of the steady-state return realized within the horizon.

**{{!Proposition:influence}} (Influence over correctors is selected in the largest carriers) [thm].** The return to influence over correctors scales with the square of share. *The local selection gradient is $\chi p^2\bar y^\top M\bar y-\partial_q\mathrm{Cost}$, and under faithful transmission mean capacity rises when $\Delta\pi$ increases on the trait support. With a floor $f>0$, capacity pays only above the size threshold $p^\ast=\big(f/(\chi\bar y^\top M\bar y)\big)^{1/(2-\xi_f)}$; with $f=0$, every carrier has a positive marginal return, largest for the largest. The return rises with $\chi$ and every $\hat b_{ij}$, falls with every correction rate, and vanishes if $\chi=0$.*

*Scope.* A local gradient, not an equilibrium after others respond; appropriability, persistence, size, cost and inheritance are each conditions under which it fails. Regulatory capture supplies the channel (Stigler 1971; Dal Bó 2006); what is added is the selection of carriers able to use it, and its scaling (S4; X1). *Proof:* S4.

Share enters twice, once in how much success the carrier converts into rule support and once in how many of its own decisions that support then favours: hence the square, and hence the largest carriers are the ones selected to shape their correctors. The corpus names the transition in one line: the selected begins to select the selectors (被选择的，开始选择选择者) [corpus: meta-attractor].

Investment in correction keyed to no one is selected against among otherwise identical carriers of the same practice, whatever its effect on the field, and lowers the payoff of any carrier holding rule support (**{{!Proposition:noinside}}**, S4). Independent correction is a second-order public good (Oliver 1980; Heckathorn 1989): competitors do not supply it.

### 4.3 How fast independent correction must grow

Let a set $\mathcal C$ of domains, not containing evaluation, be captured by a dominant carrier, with all conversion into evaluation originating in $\mathcal C$, and let independent correction act at rate $d$ in the corrector domains. Write $\hat A_{ij}=a_i\hat b_{ij}$; $\hat Y=p^2\bar y^\top\hat A\bar y$, so that in the long-horizon limit, with $L_i=1$ and uniform $d$, a unit of capacity returns $\chi\hat Y/d$ to the carrier; and $\hat B=p\sum_j\hat b_{\mathrm{ev},j}\bar y_j$, the cultivated conversion into evaluation per unit of capacity. The *selected level* of capacity, where the gradient of {{eq:infret}} vanishes, is then $q^\ast=\big[(\chi\hat Y/d-fp^{\xi_f})_+/(\gamma p^{\xi_v})\big]^{1/\eta}$, and with capacity $q$ held, evaluation's full row sum is
$${{!eq:rowsum}}\qquad G_{\rm ev}=\frac{A+a_{\rm ev}\hat Bq}{c_{\rm ev}d},$$
with $A$ the conversion into evaluation that exists without cultivation.

Independent correction does double duty: it shortens the life of rule support, and it lowers the return $\chi\hat Y/d$ to a unit of capacity, so selection cultivates less. That is why it need not keep up with the cost of control: under quadratic cost ($\eta=1$), half the rate suffices.

**{{!Proposition:corrrate}} (Required independent correction, and its rate) [thm].** Where the cost of control falls, independent correction must grow at a stated fraction of that rate, or a seeded evaluation domain is captured by carriers whose influence keeps up with its selected level.
1. *(Requirement.) With capacity at its selected level, evaluation is certified iff $d>d^\ast(\gamma)$, the root of $G_{\rm ev}=1$. Then $d^\ast\ge A/c_{\rm ev}$, the requirement without second-order selection, with equality iff nothing is cultivated, and $d^\ast$ rises with appropriability, with the dominant carrier's size and scope, and as $\gamma$ falls.*
2. *(Rate law.) Assume $[H_\gamma]$, no floor ($f=0$), $d(t)=d_0e^{\alpha_{\rm cor}t}$, a positive cultivation channel and long horizons. Sufficient at any speed of adaptation: if $\alpha_{\rm cor}\ge\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and $d_0>d^\ast(\gamma_0)$, the certificate holds at every date, for every adjustment of capacity that starts at or below its selected level and does not raise capacity while it is above that level. Necessary where capacity tracks its selected level: if $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and capacity keeps a positive fraction of its selected level ($\liminf_tq/q^\ast>0$), the certificate fails beyond a finite time, after which a seeded evaluation domain is captured. Both halves hold for every size elasticity $\xi_v$.*
3. *(A per-unit floor.) If $f>0$, capacity is cultivated only while $d<d_f=\chi\hat Y/(fp^{\xi_f})$, so a floor that does not fall levels the requirement off at $\max\{A/c_{\rm ev},d_f\}$. Under $[H_f]$, $d_f$ rises at rate $\alpha_f$, and three cases decide whether the certificate holds eventually. If $\alpha_{\rm cor}>\alpha_f$, cultivation ends at a finite date and the certificate holds from some date on, though not necessarily before it, since cultivation that ends can carry the row sum above one on the way [num: FF]. If $\alpha_{\rm cor}=\alpha_f$ and $d_0\ge d_f(0)$, nothing is ever cultivated. Otherwise the rate law of (2) decides, with selected capacity a fixed fraction of its level without the floor ($\alpha_{\rm cor}=\alpha_f$) or approaching that level ($\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_f$). A lump-sum cost of capacity, paid once if any is held, only delays cultivation and leaves the requirement unbounded as control cheapens (S4 gives its size threshold).*
4. *(Splitting and concentration.) Splitting a dominant carrier into $k$ independent parts divides $\hat Y$ by $k^2$ and cultivated conversion by $k^{(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$, which with $f=0$ delays the failure of the certificate without preventing it; under a floor $f>0$ that does not fall, parts below the size threshold cultivate nothing. Total cultivated conversion is proportional to $\sum_kp_k^{1+(2-\xi_v)/\eta}$, a high moment of shares: for $\eta=1$, the third when $\xi_v=0$ and the Herfindahl index when $\xi_v=1$.*

*Scope.* How the cost of influence scales with size, on which the threshold, the splitting factor and the concentration measure depend, is an empirical question. The power law of {{costq}} is not needed for the threshold of the rate law: where the marginal cost of influence is regularly varying in capacity with index $\eta$, correction growing faster than $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ keeps the certificate from some date on, and slower correction loses it where capacity tracks its selected level, with $\eta$ that asymptotic elasticity; at equality the slowly varying factor decides. Where the marginal cost rises faster than any power, as an exponential does, correction growing at any positive rate certifies in the end, possibly after a transient failure (S4) [thm]. The elasticity of the marginal cost of influence is therefore the empirical target. Below the rate law, capacity that lags its selected level stays below it, so the quasi-static failure date is the earliest ({{capdyn}}). The necessary half is a result about carriers that optimize or adjust at a rate that does not fall, the case a requirement should be designed against: gradual selection, pushed only by the marginal return $\chi\hat Y/d$, keeps capacity bounded unless its variance rises faster than capacity, and correction growing at any positive rate then certifies in the end, after a possible transient failure. The quasi-static model is optimistic after a reform is withdrawn, because capacity is a stock [num: QS], and where correction rests on a slow variable that the contest erodes ({{>humancorr}}). *Proof:* S4 [num: CS, FF, QS].

Correction is work done by people: reading systems, testing them, judging what the tests show. Where correctors delegate that work to the systems they correct, or to the lineage that built them, the human capacity $\mathcal M$ on which correction rests decays, as decision-makers' capacity does under delegation ({{>lockin}}).

**{{!Proposition:humancorr}} (Delegation erodes the corrector) [thm; num].** Where correction rests on human capacity that delegation erodes, the threshold of the rate law rises by the weighted rate of erosion, and capture that makes delegation look safe is harder to undo than to prevent. *Let correction act at the effective rate $d_{\rm eff}=d\,(\mathcal M/\mathcal M_0)^w$, with $w>0$ the elasticity of correction in the human capacity $\mathcal M$.*
1. *If delegating corrective work erodes that capacity at rate $\delta_h$, $\mathcal M(t)=\mathcal M_0e^{-\delta_ht}$, both halves of {{corrrate}}(2) hold with $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ replaced by $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)+w\delta_h$ [thm].*
2. *If captured evaluation reports delegation to be safe, so that delegation rises with $x_{\rm ev}$, capture is hysteretic: in one domain, with the cost of control constant and capacity at its selected level, undoing capture from the captured state takes a permanent correction rate 1.6 to 2.6 times the rate that would certify evaluation without this loop, rising with the strength of the coupling, at the parameters computed [num: QS].*

*Scope.* The form of $d_{\rm eff}$ is a modelling choice inside the open interface between correction and the capacity, resources and authority behind it (S13). Where erosion stops at a floor $\mathcal M_{\min}>0$, the required rate returns to $\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$ and the required level of correction rises by the factor $(\mathcal M_0/\mathcal M_{\min})^w$. Part (2) is one simulation of one loop. *Proof:* S4.

The contest thus moves both sides of the rate law: it cheapens control, and through delegation it erodes the capacity that corrects, a slow variable that capture itself accelerates. Human capacity kept in practice where correction depends on it, and narrow E1 (Section 11.3), act on this term.

The rate of independent correction is the growth of its budget plus the rate at which AI lowers its unit cost (S4). If AI cheapens independent correction at least $1/(1+\eta)$ as fast as it cheapens control, the certificate holds without any growth of budget: AI-assisted correction can keep pace (Section 8.3(a)). It does so only where it is independent of the evaluated lineage, since a corrector of that lineage raises $x_{\rm ev}$ and with it the masking of Section 4.4, and where it assists the human capacity of {{humancorr}} rather than replacing it. The durable remedies do not rely on unbounded growth: non-appropriability ($\chi\to0$, removed rather than reduced); a per-unit cost of capacity that AI does not lower, or lowers more slowly than correction grows; and structural separation that removes common control. Mobilization by those harmed can raise $d$ in proportion to perceived harm, but masking attenuates it exactly when needed and it is bounded (**{{!Remark:countervail}}**, S4; Becker 1983).

The correction rate $d$ is not the coverage of hazardous behaviour by contestable understanding (Section 5.3). A fixed capacity to certify or to correct can make some kinds of erroneous support decay faster; growing understanding without executive authority may change nothing. Linking the two needs a production relation in which authority stands beside understanding, resources and delay, an open interface (S13), and neither is used to bound the other.

### 4.4 Masking, and alarms that do not miss

Reports average over evaluators. A fraction $x_{\rm ev}$ shares the evaluated lineage and reports the condition a fraction $m_{\rm in}$ of the way toward full health; the rest, formed on shared material, are biased by $m_{\rm out}\le m_{\rm in}$:
$${{!eq:mask}}\qquad \tilde V=V+m(1-V),\qquad m=x_{\rm ev}m_{\rm in}+(1-x_{\rm ev})m_{\rm out}.$$
Evaluation awareness and sandbagging, behaviours of the evaluated model, add a term $m_{\rm model}$ that acts on independent evaluators too (EV6; van der Weij et al. 2024); an evaluator that bears part of the loss of a false report, such as an insurer, masks less. Let $m_{\rm fl}=(V_{\rm alarm}-V_{\min})/(1-V_{\min})$.

**{{!Proposition:masking}} (Detection under a fixed averaged alarm) [thm].** Captured evaluation lowers the level at which an alarm fires, and silences it through the crossing of the floor when masking stays high enough. *The alarm fires only once $V\le(V_{\rm alarm}-m)/(1-m)$. If $m(t)>m_{\rm fl}$ whenever $V_{\min}\le V(t)\le V_{\rm alarm}$, it does not fire before $V$ falls below the floor. If $V$ settles at $V^\ast\in[V_{\min},V_{\rm alarm})$ with $m$ staying above $(V_{\rm alarm}-V^\ast)/(1-V^\ast)$, it never fires.*

*Proof:* S4.

**{{!Remark:alarm}} (Alarms that do not miss).** Where an upper bound on masking is known, an alarm on reports set correspondingly higher fires no later than the true condition reaches its target, so silent crossing is a property of the alarm rule, a governance choice. The bound must come from a channel the field does not control, since a bound estimated inside the evaluated lineage is itself masked. For masking by the evaluated model, independence of funding and appointment is not enough, since the behaviour under evaluation is the model's: the bound on $m_{\rm model}$ must come from channels that behaviour controls less directly: weights and activations, which linear probes read for strategic deception though not yet robustly (Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025) and which optimization against a probe, or a model trained to evade monitors, can obfuscate (Bailey et al. 2024; McGuinness et al. 2025); training data; and elicitation by fine-tuning, which recovers withheld capability given good demonstrations and may fail where the hidden capability exceeds the demonstrators' (Greenblatt, Roger, Krasheninnikov and Krueger 2024). How far these channels bound masking at a given capability is crux C12. Where models evaluate models, $x_{\rm ev}$ is the share of that evaluation done by the evaluated lineage, a quantity observable today (EV4, EV6). An alarm that fires on any credible report fails silently only if even independent evaluators mask beyond $m_{\rm fl}$, or a captured rule domain ignores them. Static affine masking hides levels, not dynamics: the autocorrelation of reports still reads the host's recovery rate, until reports pass through a filter with memory or the indicator, once an alarm, becomes a target (S4) [num: AC].

A common bias in reporting weakens every decentralized escape at once: coordination at a threshold, reversion in repeated play, reputation, mobilization and insurers' pricing. Mechanisms that observe actions directly, such as verifiable deployments, compute and the performance of contracts, do not depend on host reports; verification rests on them, and so does any trigger committed in advance (Section 12.1). Closure can also go deeper than masked measurement, from capture of measurement to capture of interpretation, of the concepts in which a failure could be expressed, and of what people want [conj; TESTS, Study 4].

### 4.5 Certificates, cascades, keystones and pauses

Let $Z_{\mathcal C}$ be the state with $x_i=1$ on $\mathcal C$ and $0$ elsewhere. $\mathcal C$ is *internally sustaining* if $G_i(\mathcal C)>1$ for all $i\in\mathcal C$, and *uninvadable* if $G_i(\mathcal C)<1$ for all $i\notin\mathcal C$; $\operatorname{core}(\mathcal C)$ repeatedly prunes from $\mathcal C$ every domain whose inbound gain from the remainder is below one (Seidman 1983; Morris 2000).

**{{!Proposition:certificate}} (Configurations and certificates) [thm].** No domain can be certified alone; a full row sum below one certifies a domain against every state of the others. *(1) A hyperbolic $Z_{\mathcal C}$ is asymptotically stable iff $\mathcal C$ is internally sustaining and uninvadable. (2) If $\sum_jG_{ij}<1$, then $x_i\to0$ from every state with $x_i(0)<1$, whatever happens elsewhere. (3) For any number of domains and any diagonal gains below one, there are conversions under which full capture is stable and the only stable configuration containing a given domain. (4) If the core of all domains is empty, every state with shares below one converges to no capture; otherwise, where no inbound gain in the pruning equals one, the core's configuration is stable and contains every stable configuration.*

*Scope.* Part (2) is sufficient, not necessary: with a row sum of two, low initial states still converge to no capture (S14). It certifies eventual removal, not present independence: from a start near full capture the captured share can rise for a long time before it falls, beyond 0.999999 for fifty periods in one case with a row sum of one half, and at $x_i(0)=1$ it never falls (S14) [num: CX]. One sufficient transient bound in this field model is $a_i z_i(0)\le c_i$, with $a_i,c_i$ fixed and the full inbound gain below one at every date: at $z_i=c_i/a_i$ the support derivative is negative, so support cannot cross that boundary and $x_i$ cannot increase. An initially acceptable captured share therefore remains acceptable. In what follows, a corrector *certified* for use in a decision therefore has, besides a full row sum below one, a bound on its present captured share and support, a residence time past that bound, and evidence of actual access, appointment and authority. At a gain exactly one, part (4) needs a separate argument: pruning keeps such a domain, and its configuration can be unstable (S14) [num: CX]. *Proof:* S4.

**{{!Proposition:reabsorb}} (Cascades and reforms) [thm].** A reform that leaves inbound conversion above one is re-absorbed. *Start in the basin of a stable $Z_{\mathcal C}$. A sustained reform that drives the shares of $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal C$ to zero, or brings their inbound gains below one, sends to zero every domain outside $\operatorname{core}(\mathcal C\setminus\mathcal Q)$. A reform that resets a domain's share, rule support or own conversion without lowering an inbound gain above one from domains that remain captured is re-absorbed once the domain is re-seeded.*

*Proof:* S4.

**Keystones, and a window that closes.** A captured domain is a *keystone* if removing it shrinks the remaining core: a sustained reform there takes down every captured domain outside that core, while a reform elsewhere is re-absorbed as the rest sustain one another. As capture persists, conversion among the captured domains can grow until they do, and the domain where the practice began stops being a keystone: capture can outlive its origin.

**{{!Proposition:pausefield}} (A pause that freezes shares) [thm].** A pause that freezes shares while rule support keeps accumulating can complete capture; insulating rule-making during the pause widens the set from which capture is undone. *In one domain with $G>1$, freeze the share at $x_p$ until rule support has settled, while $\dot z=\varepsilon(b_px_p-dz)$. With ordinary rule-making ($b_p=b$), after release capture is completed if $x_p>1/G$ and undone if $x_p<1/G$; with rule-making insulated ($b_p=0$), it is undone whenever $x_p<x_\sigma$, the share at which the separatrix meets $z=0$, with $x_\sigma>1/G$.*

*Scope.* The threshold $x_\sigma$ rises as rule-making slows relative to adoption [num: DW]. From rule support at its captured level $b/d$, an insulated pause must last $\ln\big(G/S(x_p)\big)/(\varepsilon d)$, where $S(x_p)$ is the separatrix's height at the frozen share in units of the break-even support $c/a$; none suffices from $x_p\ge x_\sigma$ (S4 gives the lengths). A frozen share accumulates no lock-in; a reform that insulates rule-making without freezing shares needs the longer hold of Section 11.4. *Proof:* S4.

System {{eq:field}} is cooperative, so almost all trajectories converge to equilibria: it describes lock-in, not reform cycles (Hirsch 1985; S4). Absence of capture is always locally stable, so capture arrives by seeding, without the slowing recovery that early-warning signals detect; rising autocorrelation in shares can instead indicate progress in undoing it (TESTS).

### 4.6 The slow route: silent crossing

**{{!Proposition:slowroute}} (Silent crossing when capture precedes evidence) [thm∣prem].** Where evaluation is captured before the host's decline reaches the alarm band, a race through a reachable floor crosses it before any averaged alarm fires. *Assume (reach) and:*
- *(a) replicator selection among carriers differing only in load, with a race carrier present and cumulative advantage unbounded on every alarm-free continuation;*
- *(d) a fixed averaged alarm with $0\le m_{\rm out}<m_{\rm fl}<m_{\rm in}<1$;*
- *(e) capture before evidence: $x_{\rm ev}(t)>(m_{\rm fl}-m_{\rm out})/(m_{\rm in}-m_{\rm out})$ whenever $V_{\min}\le V(t)\le V_{\rm alarm}$.*

*Then the host crosses its floor before any alarm. If instead $m_{\rm in}\le m_{\rm fl}$, the alarm is delayed, not silenced. The conclusion also holds with (e) replaced by three conditions: (b) appropriable control, $\chi>0$, under $[H_\gamma]$; (c) correction growing at $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_{\rm ctl}/(1+\eta)$, with no floor or under $[H_f]$ with $\alpha_{\rm cor}<\alpha_f$, the other hypotheses of {{corrrate}}(2), capacity tracking its selected level, $\mathcal C$ captured and evaluation seeded; and (clock) $T_{\rm cap}<T_A$, where $T_{\rm cap}$ is the date after which $x_{\rm ev}$ stays above the threshold of (e) and $T_A$ the first date at which $V$ reaches $V_{\rm alarm}$.*

*Scope.* Condition (e) carries the conclusion. Conditions (b) and (c) supply the mechanism behind it, capture of evaluation in finite time where the cost of control falls faster than correction can follow, but they do not imply (e), which compares two clocks, the time for the host to reach the alarm band under the race load and the time for capture to pass the masking threshold. Acceleration brings both forward, and in the joint model either can come first, according to how fast correction grows and how fast capacity adjusts [num: QS, SR], so (e) is a condition to check (TESTS, Study 2). *Proof:* S4.

The slow route needs a floor within reach; the fast routes of Sections 5 and 6 do not. Where the alarm is only delayed, the floor is still crossed unless decision and reversal fit the remaining window and a liability enacted against a captured rule domain escapes re-absorption ({{masking}}, {{reabsorb}}). **What would refute the mechanism here:** effective correction surviving unchanged dependence on the evaluated; influence explained by expertise without impairment of an entrusted purpose; conversion channels whose measured loop gain is below their damping ({{bench}}); or support by carriers for correction keyed to no one at net cost to themselves (TESTS, Test B). The author's pilot experiment did not support the mechanism: its first prediction, that conversion capacity rises only where the loop is closed and appropriable, failed, while the other three held (EV12); the mechanism rests on the proofs and institutional evidence, not yet on an experiment.

Captured correction, which charges less and sees later, is one of three means of restraint that winning erodes; Section 5 takes the three together.
