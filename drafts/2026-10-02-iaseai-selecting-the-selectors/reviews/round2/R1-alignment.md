# Review: "Selecting the Selectors: Recursive Selection and the Evolution of Control over AI Evaluation" (IASEAI'27 archival track)

Reviewer role: senior technical alignment researcher (scalable oversight, reward modelling, LLM-as-judge, AI control, multi-agent/cooperative AI, structural risk).

Method note. I read the PDF and the LaTeX source, checked every proposition in Appendix A by hand, re-implemented the six-domain field from the Appendix B parameters in pure Python (no access to the authors' script), and opened or searched for the cited sources for every empirical claim in Sections 1 and 7 plus the prior-art candidates below.

## 1. Summary

The paper models n "selection domains" (development, evaluation, standards, procurement, formation, information) in which a practice's share x_i follows a rule-support-driven switch (Eq. 1) and rule support r_i accumulates from success elsewhere through a conversion matrix B (Eq. 2). Stable configurations are sets of domains that are internally sustaining and uninvadable under the normalized conversion R_ij (Prop. 1); capture spreads through a cascade hull and is certified by k-core-style pruning (Props. 4–5). The central addition (Section 4) lets carriers of a practice vary heritably in "conversion capacity" q; the selection gradient on q is χ·yᵀMy − C′(q) (Prop. 2), where χ is the appropriable fraction of cultivated rule support, L_i a horizon-persistence weight, and M a kernel built from cultivated cross-domain conversion. Selection on q and capture reinforce each other (Prop. 3, "ratchet"). Section 6 adds a shared condition V depleted by the practice and a masking model of evaluation (Eq. 6) giving a detection-delay law that diverges at β x̄ = V_alarm/K (Prop. 6). Sections 7–9 map this to AI oversight (AI judges, scalable oversight, obedience vs field alignment), propose discriminating experiments, and derive governance principles, notably "lower the appropriability of rule-making" and "firewall the selectors in control units".

## 3. Strengths

1. **The mathematics is correct and the numerics reproduce exactly.** I verified Lemma 1, Props. 1–6 and Cors. 1–3 line by line (details in Section 4 where I found anything to say). My independent RK4 re-implementation from Appendix B alone reproduces the paper's numbers to the stated precision: capture times P 42.5 / E,I 55.3 / S 66.7 / F 105.4 (paper ≈43/56/67/106); t_A = 74.7; t_det at β = 0,…,0.55 = 74.7, 87.7, 106.4, 135.5, 186.9, 305.7, 462.1 and "never" at 0.6 (identical to Table 2); minimum V after reform 0.535/0.498/0.451/0.389/0.306/0.186/0.104 (paper 0.54/0.50/0.45/0.39/0.31/0.19/0.10); the ψ block and μ block of Table 2 reproduce exactly once β = 0 is assumed (see Weakness M-6 on the caption); firewalled E,F never cross 0.5 and detection is at 74.7 for every β; the standards reset is re-absorbed in ~36 time units (paper ≈38); the keystone threshold (1.3+5ψ)/2 < 1 bites exactly between ψ = 0.13 and 0.15. The closed-form delay in the proof of Prop. 6(iii) (logistic case) also checks by hand (60.8 at β = 0.3, 231.0 at 0.5, 387.7 at 0.55). This degree of reproducibility from a stated parameter table is rare and should be preserved in the camera-ready.
2. **Honest scoping.** The Statement of Contributions says the field-level results are continuous-time analogues of threshold-cascade/k-core results; Section 10 says "The numbers illustrate the propositions and estimate nothing"; Section 7 frames the AI claims as "hypotheses about magnitudes". Pathology (P1–P3) is kept separate from the dynamics, and Corollary 1 states when control is *not* selected. The "When the account loses force" paragraph (Section 8) is a genuine falsifier list.
3. **One genuinely new and actionable lever.** "Lower the appropriability of rule-making" (Section 9) follows from Prop. 2 and is not visible in fixed-conversion entrenchment models. Interface-neutral standards, rotating held-out benchmark sets, outcome-based procurement and conflict-of-interest rules are mapped to a single parameter χ. Likewise "measure rows, certify jointly" (Prop. 4b–d) gives regulators a conservative, decentralized test that does not require a joint model of the field, and the keystone/re-absorption results (Prop. 5, Table 2 middle block) explain why single-domain reforms relapse.
4. **The "control units" idea (Remark 2; "A hundred models trained on the same data count as one unit") is the sharpest point for an alignment audience** and is well supported by the cited correlated-error literature (Kim et al. 2025; Goel et al. 2025, both verified).
5. The empirical citations on LLM judges are accurate (checked): Awuni et al. 2026 (arXiv:2609.17857) does report a 3.4–8.4 pp same-family lift, so "a few percentage points" is fair; Żatuchin 2026 (arXiv:2610.00369, posted 30 Sept 2026) does reanalyse Laurito et al.'s matrices and finds no detectable own-model premium while confirming the AI-vs-human preference; Li et al. "Preference Leakage" is accepted at ICLR 2026 and does show judges favouring related student models; Kim et al. (ICML 2025) report that larger, more accurate models have highly correlated errors "even with distinct architectures and providers"; Goel et al. (ICML 2025) report errors growing more similar with capability and judges favouring similar models.
6. Clean, economical prose; the figures are legible and consistent with the text.

## 4. Weaknesses (most serious first)

**[MAJOR] W-1. Two of the five characterizations in "Relation to scalable oversight" (Section 7, p. 7) are inaccurate, and this paragraph is the paper's main bridge to the alignment audience.**
(a) "AI control monitors an untrusted model with a trusted one of different provenance (Greenblatt et al. 2024), a firewall in our terms." In Greenblatt et al. the untrusted model is GPT-4 and the trusted model is GPT-3.5, the *same* provider; "trusted" means believed not to be intentionally subverting because it is too weak to scheme, not independent lineage. The firewall analogy can be rescued (trust via a capability gap rather than via lineage) but as written the citation is misattributed, and the correct reading actually sharpens the paper's own point: a same-provider trusted monitor shares formation with the monitored model, so by Remark 3 its β is not zero.
(b) "Debate keeps a non-independent judge useful through adversarial structure (Irving et al. 2018)." In Irving et al. the judge is a human; the judge's limitation is weakness, not non-independence. Work with LLM judges in debate (e.g. Kenton et al. 2024, "On scalable oversight with weak LLMs judging strong LLMs"; Khan et al. 2024) would be the right citation for the claim being made.
(c) "Weak-to-strong generalization … build[s] each overseer from earlier winners": in Burns et al. the supervisor is an earlier, weaker model; "earlier generation" is accurate, "winners" is a stretch. Recursive reward modeling and IDA are described fairly; constitutional AI/self-rewarding and Gao et al. are described accurately.

**[MAJOR] W-2. The novelty claim for the central result is overstated relative to known precedents, and the related-work positioning names the wrong "closest precedent".**
Section 2 calls Gerlee & Anderson (2015) "the closest precedent for our appropriability condition". The inequality "a costly environment-modifying trait is selected iff its privately appropriated, persistence-discounted benefit exceeds marginal cost" is the niche-construction dilemma literature: Krakauer, Page & Erwin (2009, *Am. Nat.* 173:26–40, "Diversity, Dilemmas, and Monopolies of Niche Construction") show that shared niche benefits produce a tragedy of the commons eliminating niche construction unless constructors *monopolize* the benefit — exactly the paper's χ; Lehmann (2008, *Evolution* 62:549–566) gives an inclusive-fitness (Hamilton's-rule) analysis of niche-constructing traits whose effects persist into future generations, i.e. relatedness × persistence-weighted benefit > cost, the structure the paper describes in the paragraph after Prop. 2 (see also Kylafis & Loreau 2008, *Ecol. Lett.*). Gerlee & Anderson is downstream of these. In economics the appropriability condition on investment in rule-shaping is Olson (1965) — concentrated/appropriable benefits produce lobbying, diffuse ones produce free-riding — which Stigler (1971, cited) builds on; Bombardini & Trebbi (2012, *J. Int. Econ.* 87:18–26) test firm-specific versus collective lobbying directly; "appropriability regimes" is Teece (1986); and Acemoglu & Robinson (2008, cited only for persistence) already model elites *choosing* costly investment in de facto power. None of this makes the paper unoriginal — the object (the selection environment itself), the quadratic cross-domain return (Cor. 2), and the ratchet coupling to k-core capture thresholds (Prop. 3) are new — but the Statement of Contributions and Section 2 must say so, or an evolutionary-theory or political-economy reviewer will treat the omission as a reason to reject.

**[MAJOR] W-3. The alignment claim is stated as a result but is a one-step consequence of a modelling assumption.** Abstract/Introduction: "Aligning each system to its operator does not bound any of this." In the model "alignment" is not a variable; the claim reduces to Corollary 3's premise that assistance divides C′ by 1+α. That is a reasonable premise and the "Obedience is not field alignment" paragraph (p. 7) is honest that the thesis is inherited (Critch & Krueger; Critch 2021; Kulveit et al.). But the paper's own second sentence of Cor. 3 shows the sign is not determined: assistance available to evaluators raises d_i and can lower β. The intro presents only one direction. The precise supportable statement is: "operator-alignment enters the model only through the cost of conversion; lowering that cost raises selected capacity (Cor. 3) and thereby, weakly, the captured set (Prop. 3); nothing in operator-alignment per se lowers χ or raises d_i." Say that, and the claim is both correct and sharper.

**[MAJOR] W-4. Apparent inconsistency between Eq. (3) and the Appendix B simulation of Fig. 2 (the central illustration).** Eq. (3) and Prop. 2 define the appropriable stock from the carrier's *own* success profile y ("its share of decisions in each domain"). Appendix B states ζ̇_ik = ε_i(χ q_k Σ_j b̂_ij x_j − d_i ζ_ik) with x_j the *whole practice's* share "(representative carriers)". Under this convention the high-capacity type appropriates conversion generated by the low-capacity type's success. The quoted threshold "χ = 0.25 at which capacity starts to pay in development" follows from 0.8·χ·… wait, from χ·0.2·x_D > 0.05 with x_D ≈ 1; with own-share accounting (type-1 share ≈ 0.45 initially) the threshold would be ≈0.56 and the χ = 0.3 run of Fig. 2 would likely not take off. Either the convention is intended (then it must be stated in the main text and reconciled with Eq. 3) or Fig. 2's χ = 0.3 case is an artefact. The multi-type entry and mutation terms are also unspecified, so Fig. 2 is the one result I could not reproduce from the text.

**[MINOR] W-5. Corollary 1 is imprecise under the paper's own convex-cost assumption.** With C(q) = γq²/2 (Prop. 2 iv), C′(0) = 0, so any positive return selects *for* capacity at the margin; conditions (b)–(d) then lower q*, they do not make capacity "selected against". The statement needs C′(0) > 0 (as in the simulation's linear cost 0.05q) or the phrasing "selected to a lower level / not selected above zero when C′(0) exceeds the return". Part (e) ("offsets") asserts rather than shows that the transmission term dominates.

**[MINOR] W-6. Table 2 caption omits that the middle (ψ) and bottom (μ) blocks are computed at β = 0.** My reproduction matches those blocks only at β = 0 (e.g. min V 0.535/0.524/0.500/0.461 and 0.546/0.535/0.516/0.498); at the β = 0.3 used elsewhere the values are 0.389/0.379/0.356/0.299 and 0.396/0.389/0.377/0.365. One phrase fixes it.

**[MINOR] W-7. The bridge from self-preference measurements to β is undefined.** β (Eq. 6) is the fraction of the gap to full health by which a same-lineage evaluator inflates a *scalar report of a shared condition*; the cited studies measure pairwise *preference lifts* in output comparisons. Appendix C proposes an operationalization ("in-family minus out-of-family score on items with fixed ground truth"); it should appear in one sentence in Section 7, and the claim "for a single lineage the realistic concern is delay rather than silence" should be marked as resting on identifying a pp lift with β. Also, x_E is redefined in Section 6 from "share of consequential evaluation decisions following the practice" to "fraction of the panel sharing the lineage"; say that this is a reinterpretation.

**[MINOR] W-8. Remark 1 is titled more strongly than what it proves.** If panel composition x_E and β are known, Eq. (6) inverts exactly, so the alarm *can* be recalibrated; the remark shows only that it cannot be recalibrated from the report alone when composition is unknown. Retitle accordingly. The genuinely hard case (β unknown) is not addressed.

**[MINOR] W-9. Model-structure limitations that bear on the AI mapping and are not listed in Section 10.** (i) Because the payoff difference in Eq. (1) is frequency-independent, Prop. 1(ii) rules out *any* stable partial adoption; real selection environments have network effects and congestion that can stabilize interior shares, which would soften the all-or-nothing capture picture. (ii) The abstract says the return's "kernel is the cross-domain conversion that also decides whether capture is stable"; strictly, stability depends on B⁰ + q̄B̂ while the return depends on B̂ alone — the same object only in the cultivated part. (iii) Props. 1, 4, 5 need c_i > 0 for R_ij to be defined, but the text discusses c_i ≤ 0 (p. 3); state the standing assumption. (iv) Prop. 5(ii)'s "re-absorbed from states near Z_C" is vacuous at μ = 0 (faces invariant); the content is for μ > 0 and should be stated as such. (v) Prop. 4(c) proves more than it states (every share tends to zero from any state with shares below one when core(N) = ∅).

**[MINOR] W-10. Missing alignment-side literature that directly supports or sharpens claims.** Eisenstein et al. (2023, "Helping or Herding?") show reward-model ensembles share errors when they share pretraining — the direct precedent for "panels of diverse models help only when their errors are decorrelated" and for control units; Turner et al. (2021) / Carlsmith (2022) on power-seeking should be positioned against the "any heritable trait whose effect on later selection is appropriable" generalization (p. 4), which is a population-selection analogue of instrumental convergence; Koch et al. (2021, dataset concentration) and Dehghani et al. (2021, "Benchmark Lottery") belong in Table 1's evaluation/formation rows; Greif & Laitin (2004) on self-reinforcing vs self-undermining institutions is the political-science precedent for the generative/pathological distinction.

**[MINOR] W-11. Clarity/density.** Ten sections, six propositions, three corollaries, four remarks, a test programme and nine governance principles in 8.6 pages. The "Illustration" paragraphs (pp. 4–6) are strings of numbers that only make sense with Appendix B open; Fig. 2 is very small; the "meta-attractor"/"three levels" apparatus of Section 3 is used once (Def. 1) and could be cut by half to buy space for W-1 and W-2.

**[MINOR] W-12. Production issues.** Fig. 3(d): the "never" label is clipped at the right edge ("nev"). Laurito et al. 2025: Ada Böhm is missing from the author list; add vol./issue 122(31). Appendix D: the δ = 0.8 and χ = 0.1 numbers (0.59, 0.38) use T = 20, not the T = 5 baseline just stated — say so. Section 8 identification requires the kernel decay ε_i d_i to be known or estimated; note it.

## 5. Readiness

As it stands the paper is well above the median submission in rigour and polish, and its main text is within the 10-page limit, self-contained, double-blind and carries the required Statement of Contributions. Its exposure is to three kinds of reviewer: an alignment researcher who knows that AI control's trusted model is GPT-3.5 (W-1); an evolutionary theorist or political economist who knows the niche-construction-dilemma and collective-action literature (W-2); and a governance reviewer who wants at least one estimated quantity or one run experiment.

- **Estimated probability of archival acceptance as is: ~35%** (base rate ~19%).
- **Must-fix before submission (any could be used to reject):** W-1 (rewrite the oversight paragraph; one hour), W-2 (add the five precedents and reword the novelty claim; two hours), W-3 (reword abstract/intro; thirty minutes), W-4 (state and reconcile the representative-carrier convention; report whether Fig. 2's χ = 0.3 case survives own-share accounting), W-5 and W-6 (precision fixes).
- **What would most raise the probability within three days:** after the must-fixes (~50%), the single highest-value addition is running even a reduced version of the Appendix C arm-1/2/3 simulation (e.g. 100 carriers, 40 rounds, 10 seeds) and reporting mean capacity and hysteresis per arm in a half-column figure — it turns Section 8 from a proposal into a result and pre-empts the "no experiments" objection (~60%). If that is not feasible, add Eisenstein et al. and a one-paragraph worked control-unit example for an actual LLM oversight pipeline (judge, reward model and benchmark author sharing a pretraining corpus) to Remark 2.

## 6. Could this paper, in its current version, be an important paper that advances alignment?

Not yet in its current version; potentially yes after revision. The ingredients of an important paper are here: a mechanism (selection on appropriable control of selection) that explains why evaluator capture is produced rather than accidental; a crisp formal statement of why independence of overseers must be counted in control units over funding, data, lineage and authority rather than by provider name; and a lever (appropriability) that current entrenchment analyses cannot see. For scalable oversight the uncomfortable implication is real: since every frontier overseer shares pretraining formation with what it oversees, the paper's own Remark 3 says the firewall condition (row sum below one and β near zero) is nearly unattainable for LLM-on-LLM oversight, and the binding constraint is the shared corpus, not the provider. That is a thesis alignment researchers should engage with.

What stops it being important now: the mapping to oversight is by analogy and in two places factually wrong (W-1); none of R_ij, χ, β is estimated or even operationalized in the main text (W-7); the model has no agents, so "alignment" appears only as a cost parameter (W-3); and the field-level results are acknowledged re-derivations. The paper currently advances the *vocabulary* of structural risk in evaluation ecosystems; it would advance alignment *practice* once Prop. 6's delay law is tested against same-family/cross-family/shared-corpus panels on a scalar ground-truth condition, or once the control-unit cut is computed for a real oversight stack.

## 7. Questions for the authors

1. In Appendix B, ζ̇_ik is driven by the practice share x_j rather than type k's own share x_jk. Is this intended? Under own-share accounting, what are the χ thresholds, and does the χ = 0.3 run in Fig. 2 still cross the cascade threshold? Please also specify the multi-type entry and mutation terms.
2. How should β be estimated from a pairwise preference lift of 3.4–8.4 pp? What β does that imply, and hence what delay in Prop. 6(iii) units?
3. Is anything about alignment represented in the model other than through C′? Could a system aligned to a broader target (refusing conversion work, assisting correction) be represented as raising C′ or d_i, and what then is the field-level claim?
4. In "Relation to scalable oversight", do you intend "trusted" in AI control to mean independent lineage? Greenblatt et al. use GPT-3.5 (same provider); the trust is via a capability gap. How does a capability-gap firewall map to row sums and β?
5. How does the capacity result differ from Krakauer–Page–Erwin (2009), Lehmann (2008), Olson (1965) and Bombardini–Trebbi (2012)? Please state precisely what is new.
6. Corollary 1 under C(q) = γq²/2: is "selected against" meant as "selected to a lower level"?
7. Table 2: at what β were the ψ and μ blocks computed? (My reproduction matches them only at β = 0.)
8. Prop. 4(b–d) assume rows are measured exactly. How robust are the certificates to mis-measured or partially observed R_ij, and can a row certificate be made conservative under interval uncertainty?
9. Which of the field results survive competition among several practices within a domain (loss of cooperativity), even numerically?
10. Will the pure-Python script be released (anonymized) with the submission? I could reproduce the field numbers but not Fig. 2.
