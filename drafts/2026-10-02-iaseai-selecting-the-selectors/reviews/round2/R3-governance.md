# Review (R3: AI governance / political economy) — "Selecting the Selectors: Recursive Selection and the Evolution of Control over AI Evaluation" (IASEAI'27 archival track)

## 1. Summary

The paper models "recursive selection": a practice (a reproducible way of succeeding, e.g. releasing before independent evaluation concludes) spreads in n selection domains (development, evaluation, standards, procurement, formation, information) according to rule support r_i, which is generated by the practice's success in other domains through a conversion matrix B (Eqs. 1–2, p. 3). Stable configurations of captured domains are characterised by threshold / k-core-type conditions (Prop. 1, p. 3; Prop. 4–5, p. 5). The central addition is heritable "conversion capacity" q: by a Price-equation argument, capacity is selected iff the appropriable, horizon-weighted return χ y^T M y exceeds marginal cost (Prop. 2 and Cor. 1–3, p. 4), with a ratchet between capacity and capture thresholds (Prop. 3, p. 4). Section 6 adds a shared condition V depleted by the practice and a masking model in which in-lineage evaluators over-report its health, yielding a detection-delay law that diverges at β x̄ → V_alarm/K (Prop. 6, p. 6). Section 7 maps the domains and channels to frontier AI (Table 1, p. 7), Section 8 proposes discriminating experiments, field predictions and an identification sketch, and Section 9 derives or asserts governance principles. The main text ends mid-page 9 (about 8.4 pages), leaving roughly 1.5 pages of headroom under the 10-page limit.

## 3. Strengths

1. **A genuinely prior question with a crisp answer.** Most capture and entrenchment work takes influence as a by-product of success; the paper asks when the *capacity* to shape selection is itself selected, and gives a Hamilton-like condition (Prop. 2) plus explicit conditions under which control is *not* selected (Cor. 1). That discipline (counter-conditions, "When the account loses force", p. 8) is rare in this literature and will be appreciated by policy readers.
2. **Careful separation of description, pathology and norms.** The meta-attractor is defined value-neutrally (Def. 1, p. 3); pathology is a separate set of conditions P1–P3 (p. 4); the three normative premises are stated explicitly (Limitations, p. 9); the "Test closures" principle is labelled "not derived from the model" (p. 9); and the account is applied reflexively to the regulator ("Apply the analysis to the regime", p. 9; "Treating objections as evidence that the objector is captured would reproduce the loss of correction", p. 8). No firm or state is named; the variables are practices, not actors.
3. **Field-level results with real governance content.** Prop. 4(a) (fallacy of composition: domain-by-domain audits cannot certify a coupled field), Prop. 4(b)/(d) (a decentralised, conservative row certificate and a robust firewall for the evaluator), and above all Prop. 5(ii)–(iii) (a reform that resets a domain's state or personnel without lowering inbound conversion is re-absorbed) are sharp, falsifiable (Field prediction (c), p. 8) and policy-relevant: replace the people, keep the channels, and the configuration returns.
4. **A quantitative reason to care about evaluator provenance.** Prop. 6 and Remark 1 turn "independence of evaluators" into a measurable object (β x_E) with a divergence point. The move from independence "in name" to independence "in control units" over funding, data, lineage and authority (Remark 2; Sec. 9) is a useful contribution to the evaluator-independence debate, and the observation that EU AI Act Art. 31 covers notified bodies and their personnel but not tooling, AI judges or formation (p. 9) is correct (I verified Art. 31(4)–(5)) and non-obvious.
5. **AI-channel evidence is accurately cited and honestly framed.** I verified Panickssery et al. 2024 (NeurIPS 2024), Li et al. 2026 (ICLR 2026), Awuni et al. 2026 (arXiv 2609.17857, 3.4–8.4 pp same-family lift), Kim et al. 2025 and Goel et al. 2025 (ICML 2025), Laurito et al. 2025 (PNAS), Żatuchin 2026 (arXiv 2610.00369), Singh et al. 2025 and Longpre et al. 2024; each is used for what it shows. Table 1 says plainly that its entries "are illustrative, not measurements".
6. **Internal numerical consistency.** I recomputed the row sums (0.94 after cutting θ to 0.2; 1.64 and 0.64 for the standards reset; 0.74 without development), the keystone condition (1.3+5ψ)/2 < 1, the hysteresis threshold 1−R_ii−4φ = 0.26, the appropriability threshold χ = 0.25 at which capacity pays in development, the 0.10 → 0.51 advantage after capture, and the β = 0.3 detection delay from the logistic analogue of Prop. 6(iii) (60.8 time units). All match the text and Table 2.

## 4. Weaknesses (most serious first)

**[MAJOR] W1. Positioning gaps in the political-economy and sociological literatures that bear directly on the novelty claim (Sec. 2, Sec. 10 "Is this regulatory capture renamed?").**
- *Collective action.* Cor. 1(a) ("carriers that invest are displaced by those that free-ride although the practice as a whole gains") is Olson (1965). The missing bridge matters for competition policy: fewer carriers means less free-riding and a higher effective χ, so concentration raises the selected capacity. That is Stigler's own argument about why concentrated industries capture, and it is exactly the link a competition authority would want.
- *Certification intermediaries and ratings.* The closest formal precedents for "evaluators funded or chosen by the evaluated" are absent: Lizzeri 1999 (RAND J. Econ. 30(2)); Strausz 2005, "Honest certification and the threat of capture" (IJIO 23(1–2): 45–62), which derives when reputation lets certifiers resist capture; Bolton, Freixas & Shapiro 2012 and Skreta & Veldkamp 2009 on ratings shopping; and Lerner & Tirole 2006, "A Model of Forum Shopping" (AER 96(4): 1091–1113), in which sponsors literally choose their certifier. The title "Selecting the Selectors" invites this comparison, and the paper's mechanism (conversion) does not cover the most documented one (the evaluated *choosing* lenient evaluators or jurisdictions). A paragraph should say so and state what the field model adds.
- *Endogenous institutional change.* Greif & Laitin 2004 (APSR 98(4): 633–652: self-reinforcing vs self-undermining institutions via quasi-parameters) is nearly isomorphic to the meta-attractor / generative-vs-pathological distinction; Mahoney & Thelen 2010 (gradual change: drift, layering, *conversion*) is missing, and their "conversion" means something different from the paper's, which will confuse institutionalist readers.
- *Field theory and isomorphism.* The paper uses "field" and "conversion" without Bourdieu (1986, conversion of capital across fields, which is precisely the matrix B), Fligstein & McAdam 2012 (incumbents, challengers and internal governance units that typically serve incumbents), or DiMaggio & Powell 1983 (normative isomorphism through professionalisation, which is the paper's "formation" domain). Espeland & Sauder 2007 (rankings reshape the fields they rank) and Power 1997 (audit society) are the closest precedents for benchmarks and leaderboards as selectors.
- *Capture theory proper.* Laffont & Tirole 1991 (formal capture under asymmetric information); Carpenter 2010 (reputation as the evaluator's defence); Che 1995 (revolving door vs expertise).
The differentiating claims in Sec. 10 (field unit; loss of correction as a field property) have precedents in field theory. The real novelty is the formal selection condition on capacity, the threshold/certificate machinery and the detection-delay law. Say that.

**[MAJOR] W2. The expertise–independence trade-off is absent.** Every principle in Sec. 9 pushes inbound conversion into evaluation down, but the model has no term in which inbound conversion also carries evaluator competence (frontier access, compute, know-how). In the capture literature this trade-off is the central dilemma (Che 1995; Dal Bó 2006; Carpenter & Moss 2013), and it is the actual reason evaluators and safety institutes depend on developers. Without it, "structural separation ... applied here to evaluation" (p. 8) and "row sum below one" look costless. At minimum acknowledge; better, add an evaluation-precision term increasing in access and show the frontier.

**[MAJOR] W3. Actionability gap between indicators and model quantities.** The decentralised certificate (Prop. 4b) requires R_ij = a_i b_ij/(c_i d_i), which contains the counterfactual cost c_i that the paper itself calls unobservable (Identification, p. 8). So no authority can compute its row sum from Table 1. Table 1 lists channels and indicators but never connects them to R, χ, d_i, L_i or β, and χ, d_i and V are given no proxies at all. What a regulator, safety institute, standards body or evaluator would *do differently* is derivable from the paper but never stated: (i) audit your own inbound channels in control units (funding share from evaluated firms, access by consent, judge-lineage share, tooling dependence); (ii) prefer channel-changing reforms to state- or personnel-resetting ones (Prop. 5iii); (iii) write provenance diversity for automated judges into evaluation standards; (iv) run the sharp test jointly across authorities; (v) sunset rule stocks; (vi) overshoot because of hysteresis. One worked example (a stylised safety-institute row with illustrative numbers) and a parameter → proxy → data source → status (measurable / estimable under the model / latent) table would close most of this gap.

**[MAJOR] W4. The identification paragraph (Sec. 8, p. 8) is mathematically correct but econometrically thin.** I verified the derivation: for μ = 0, d/dt logit x_i = κ_i c_i[Σ_j R_ij (k_i ∗ x_j)(t) − 1] plus a decaying transient, so R_ij = −(lag coefficient)/(intercept). But: the kernel rate ε_i d_i must be estimated jointly (Koyck-type nonlinear estimation); x_j depends on r_j, which depends on x_i, so there is simultaneity; common shocks (a technology jump raising all x) bias R upward; six slow domains give essentially one trajectory per field; x_i ("share of consequential decisions following the practice") is itself hard to measure; the μ term is dropped. "Identified when shares vary non-collinearly" is recoverability under the model, not causal identification. The paper lists competing explanations but offers no strategy. It should present the paragraph as recoverability, and point to the natural experiments the model itself defines: margin reforms (safe-harbour adoption, notified-body rules, benchmark deprecation or rotation, access-policy changes) are exogenous shifts in b_ij or d_i usable in difference-in-differences on Field predictions (b)–(c).

**[MAJOR] W5. Evidence-to-parameter conflation in Sec. 6 (p. 6, Illustration).** "Measured same-family preferences of language-model judges are a few percentage points (Awuni et al. 2026), so for a single lineage the realistic concern is delay rather than silence" treats a 3–8 pp pairwise preference lift as the masking fraction β (the share of the way toward full health that a report is pushed). These are different quantities on different scales; nothing licenses the inference. Appendix C already proposes the right estimator (in-family minus out-of-family score on fixed-ground-truth items). Either state the sentence as an analogy or drop the quantitative conclusion. Related: two of the load-bearing empirical sources are preprints from 15 and 30 September 2026 (Awuni; Żatuchin, posted two days before this draft, single author). Reviewers cannot assess them; hedge ("recent, unreviewed").

**[MAJOR] W6. Derived vs mapped vs asserted is only partly marked in Sec. 9.** The paper labels "Test closures" as normative, but the other principles mix a derived lever with asserted instrument mappings: "benchmarks with rotating held-out sets" reduce contamination/Goodhart rather than appropriability χ per se; "conflict-of-interest rules for those who draft standards" cut conversion b, not χ; "sunsets act as stocks decay" is a mapping; "structural separation ... applied here to evaluation" is a large policy proposal in half a clause with no cost discussion; "Keep exit cheap" rests on Remark 4, whose dependence mechanism is itself an assumption. A compact table (principle → model object/result → derived / mapped / normative → existing instrument → what the model adds) would make the paper far more usable and would pre-empt the "capture renamed" objection.

**[MINOR] W7. Existing instruments beyond Art. 31 and PDUFA.** For IASEAI's audience the omission is costly: Sarbanes–Oxley §201/§203 and the PCAOB (non-audit-service bans, partner rotation, and an institutional answer to "who audits the auditors", which the paper cites only as a question via Costanza-Chock et al.); issuer-pays reforms for credit ratings (Dodd–Frank §939; the Franken amendment's random assignment is "pooled funding allocated at arm's length"); regulatory markets (Clark & Hadfield 2019), where the model directly predicts how licensed private regulators would be captured; joint regulator forums (UK DRCF) as the instrument for "certify jointly"; the AI Act's harmonised standards drafted in CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 and the GPAI Code of Practice as live instances of the Standards channel; and the International Network of AI Safety Institutes, whose pre-deployment access by agreement is a live instance of the Evaluation row.

**[MINOR] W8. Misdescriptions in the oversight mapping (p. 7).** AI control (Greenblatt et al. 2024) monitors untrusted GPT-4 with a *weaker* trusted GPT-3.5 from the same lab; trust comes from incapacity to scheme, not "different provenance", so it is not a firewall "in your terms" without further argument. In debate the judge is less capable, not "non-independent" in the paper's sense.

**[MINOR] W9. Terminology and uncited-but-present sources.** "Conversion" (Mahoney–Thelen), "field" (Bourdieu; Fligstein–McAdam) and "formation" (which merges human education with model training data: a bold choice that should be flagged) all need a sentence. The .bib already contains Shumailov et al. 2024 and Taori & Hashimoto 2023 (self-consuming loops: belongs at Remark 3), Bachrach & Baratz 1962 (belongs at "representation and agenda", p. 6), Kleinberg & Raghavan 2021 (belongs at "shared models homogenize outcomes", p. 7) and Hofbauer & Sigmund 1998 (belongs at Prop. 2ii), none of which is cited.

**[MINOR] W10. Remark 1's premise is weak.** Panel composition (x_E) is usually the one observable thing; what is unknown is β. Reframe around unknown or adaptive β.

**[MINOR] W11. Within-domain network effects are assumed away** ("a switch driven by rule support, not a game among adopters", p. 3). For standards and procurement, direct network externalities are the main entrenchment mechanism (Farrell & Saloner; Katz & Shapiro); with frequency dependence, bistability arises without cross-domain conversion, which affects the "field-level" definition R_ii < 1. Say why the abstraction is made and what changes.

**[MINOR] W12. χ is treated as a policy lever but is also a trait.** The paper's own generalisation lists "controlling interfaces" as an appropriable trait, i.e. investment in raising χ. "Lower the appropriability of rule-making" is therefore contested terrain, and Prop. 5(ii)-style re-absorption applies to it.

**[MINOR] W13. Normative and actor scope.** Generally careful, but two places read as thesis-by-implication: the Introduction's first paragraph (a string of empirical findings about frontier developers) and the title of Sec. 7. One sentence noting that the same channels are documented in pharmaceuticals, finance, accounting and standard setting would make clear that AI is a case, not an indictment. "Education ministries" among the authorities that "see only in part" (p. 8) implies formation is a regulatory object; clarify.

**[MINOR] W14. The shared condition V** ("integrity of a critical service or independent research capacity", p. 5) is too abstract to measure. Give one operationalisation (e.g. the share of independent evaluation capacity that does not depend on firm-granted access).

**[MINOR] W15. Presentation and bibliography.** Figure 3(d): the "never" annotation is clipped at the axis edge and reads "nev" (fig-field.tex line 42; add clip=false or move the node). Figures 2–4 are small for a two-column page. Table 1's indicator column mixes measurable indicators with citations. Bibliographic details: see Section 5. Appendix B says the cascade threshold is "mean capacity above 0.89", whereas the hull condition R_iD = 0.6 + 0.5 q̄ > 1 gives 0.8; reconcile.

## 7. Could this paper, in its current version, be an important paper that advances alignment?

Potentially yes, but not yet in this version. The core idea, that selection can favour the capacity to shape selection, with a condition in appropriability, persistence and correction speed, is a real contribution to the structural-risk and gradual-disempowerment line (Zwetsloot & Dafoe; Critch; Kulveit et al.): it converts a worry into conditions and levers (lower χ, keep d_i high, firewall in control units) and tells you when the worry does not apply. The detection-delay law and the "field-level independence" reading of oversight are genuinely useful to practitioners who rely on AI judges and reward models, and the re-absorption result is a non-obvious warning for anyone designing evaluator reforms. What limits its importance now: the oversight mapping is one paragraph with a misdescription of AI control; the one place where evidence is quantitatively tied to a model parameter (β) is unjustified; no experiment is run, only protocols; and the policy content stops one step short of being actionable. As a theory paper that sets up a measurable research programme, it could become important if the Appendix C experiments are run and if β is measured as the paper itself proposes. It advances the conceptual frontier of alignment-as-field-design more than the technical frontier.

## 8. Questions for the authors

1. Appendix B gives the cascade threshold as mean capacity above 0.89; the hull condition R_iD = 0.6 + 0.5 q̄ > 1 gives 0.8. Which quantity is the 0.89 (does it include the 0.05 capacity cost or the χ-dependent split between appropriable and shared support)?
2. How would a safety institute compute its row sum (Prop. 4b) without the counterfactual cost c_i? Please give a worked example with the indicators of Table 1.
3. Prop. 2(ii) is within-practice selection. With χ = 0, cultivated support still raises the practice's shared stock, so between-practice selection (the x_i dynamics) could favour capacity even when within-practice selection removes it (a multilevel Price decomposition). Figure 2 at χ = 0.1 suggests the high-capacity type is simply displaced; is that general, or parameter-dependent?
4. Does inbound conversion carry any benefit for evaluation quality (access, compute, expertise)? If not, how should a regulator weigh a firewall against competence loss?
5. Is χ exogenous? What stops carriers from investing in appropriability itself (proprietary interfaces, tooling lock-in), and how does "lower the appropriability of rule-making" survive the re-absorption logic of Prop. 5(ii)?
6. Why exclude within-domain frequency dependence (network effects) for standards and procurement, and how do Prop. 1 and Def. 1 ("field-level" iff R_ii < 1) change if it is included?
7. Why is AI control a firewall "in your terms" when the trusted monitor shares the lab and lineage of the untrusted model?
8. What is the mapping between a measured same-family preference lift (percentage points on pairwise judgments) and the masking fraction β?
9. How does the framework handle the evaluated choosing their selector (forum shopping, ratings shopping, jurisdictional competition), the most documented mechanism in certification markets?
10. Formation merges human education with model training data; does Remark 3's conclusion (mixed panels stop restoring independence) survive if β^1 differs across lineages, i.e. if lineages are formed on different material?
11. For the Information domain, what is the mechanism by which rule support r_I (coverage sourced from developer announcements) converts into adoption of the practice at rate a_I?
12. The Statement of Contributions mentions informal essays as conceptual antecedents of Section 3. Do they contain Definition 1 or P1–P3, and how does that affect the novelty claimed for contribution (1)?
