REVIEW: "Selecting the Selectors: Recursive Selection, Cross-Domain Capture, and the Answerability of AI Development" (IASEAI'27 submission)

Reviewer profile applied: senior technical alignment researcher (scalable oversight, reward modeling, LLM-as-judge, multi-agent/cooperative AI, structural-risk literature). I read the full LaTeX source and the PDF text, checked every proof in Appendix A by hand, re-implemented the Appendix B simulation from the stated parameters alone, and checked prior art on the web. I did not read anything else in the repository.

## 1. Summary

The paper isolates one structural mechanism, "success that converts into influence over the conditions of future success", and formalizes it as a field of n selection domains, each a bistable replicator-type unit (practice share x_i, rule-support stock r_i), coupled by a conversion matrix B that turns adoption in domain j into rule support in domain i. It characterizes the stable "capture configurations" E_S (a set S is stable iff internally sustaining and externally closed in the normalized conversion R), derives a cascade closure cl(S) and its dual core(C) (keystone domains), and adds a shared condition V that captured evaluation masks, so that correction can be silenced if the cascade reaches the evaluation domain before the true condition hits the alarm level. It then argues that general-purpose AI plausibly raises the three decisive quantities (cross-domain coupling, dependence of evaluators on the evaluated, adoption speed relative to rule revision), proposes falsifiers (convertibility, inbound-over-local, re-absorption, selector divergence, early warning), and offers design principles (measure conversion not concentration; audit fields not sectors; firewall the selectors; price at the margin, sunset the stock, overshoot; reform keystones while they are keystones). The six-domain numerics illustrate cascade, closure, re-absorption and hysteresis.

## 2. Scores

- Relevance: 8/10 (frontier-risk governance, oversight/evaluation; speaks directly to AI-as-evaluator)
- Quality: 6/10 (mathematics correct and self-contained; model minimal with exogenous conversion; the alignment-specific claims are asserted, not derived; numerics appendix incomplete)
- Clarity: 7/10 (well written; dense; two-page essayistic Section 3; several terms used in two senses; one corollary stated more strongly than what is proven and in tension with the design section)
- Originality: 5/10 (field-level framing and treating the selectors as domains is a modest genuine step; the core idea and most formal results have close antecedents the paper does not cite)
- Significance: 5/10 (potentially high; currently realized as a position paper with a toy model and no empirical content)

Overall recommendation (archival track): BORDERLINE. Under a strict novelty bar this is a weak reject; at a venue that values carefully argued governance framing with formal scaffolding it is a weak accept. I would not fight for it in its current version; I would support it after the three-day revisions in Section 7, and I would champion it if any one of the longer-term items were delivered.

Confidence: 4/5.

## 3. Strengths

S1. Conceptual hygiene. Table 1 (concentration, capture, field-level capture, damage, collapse, closure as separate, non-implying conditions) is useful and will be quoted. The insistence that concentration is not capture, and that what must be measured is conversion, is a real corrective to how AI-governance discussions usually run.

S2. The mathematics is correct. I verified: the n=1 Lyapunov function and saddle; Prop 1 (block-triangular Jacobian; Metzler/M-matrix argument for (ii)); Prop 2 (comparison plus induction); Corollaries 1-3; Prop 3 (order preservation plus pruning); Cor 4; Prop 4; the dependence remark. All numerical claims in Section 4.5 reproduce from Appendix B (crossing times 48/60/77/125, floor at t~210, alarm at t~75, recapture at t~42, hysteresis thresholds 1.0 up and 0.3 down, survival of capture at theta=0 for phi=0.2), with one caveat noted in W6.

S3. The unravelling dual and its consequences are the most useful formal content: keystones, "reform keystones while they are keystones", "overshoot because capture is hysteretic", and "a reform confined to one domain is re-absorbed while its inbound conversion from the captured set exceeds one" are crisp, policy-relevant statements that a regulator could act on.

S4. "Selecting the selectors" names a real and under-theorized failure mode for AI evaluation infrastructure, and the paper correctly connects it to self-preference in LLM judges and correlated errors across models.

S5. Honest scoping. The paper states its normative premises, lists when the account loses force, warns against treating objections as evidence of capture, and applies the analysis to any regime that would implement it. This reflexivity is rare and welcome.

S6. The writing is clear and economical sentence by sentence; figures use distinct dash patterns as well as color.

## 4. Weaknesses (most serious first)

**W1 [MAJOR] The alignment-relevant claims are not in the model.** The paper's pitch to this audience rests on two statements: (a) per-system (intent) alignment does not bound field-level capture, and the principals that aligned systems serve are themselves formed by the field; (b) AI-assisted evaluation helps only if the evaluator's provenance is independent of the success of what it evaluates. Neither appears in the formalism. There are no principals, no agents, no alignment variable, and the conversion matrix B is exogenous and constant. The model therefore describes the consequences of conversion but says nothing about why or when success gets converted, which is exactly where alignment would enter (an intent-aligned, capable agent lowers its principal's cost of drafting standards, building dependence, designing evaluations). "Obedience is not field alignment" is one paragraph of prose (Section 5) with no result behind it. The same holds for the claim that general-purpose AI raises the b_ij; the paper rightly calls these "hypotheses about magnitudes", but then the paper's contribution to alignment is a hypothesis, not a finding.

Focus question 2 answered directly: this claim is not new and is already presupposed in the field. Kulveit et al. (2025) build gradual disempowerment on exactly the premise that each system can be aligned to its principal while the economy, culture and state drift, and their "misaligned culture produces misaligned politics produces misaligned regulation" loop is the paper's "principals formed by the field". Critch's "What Multipolar Failure Looks Like, and Robust Agent-Agnostic Processes" (2021, Alignment Forum) states carrier-independence (processes that persist regardless of which agents carry them) and the erosion of correction almost verbatim to Sections 3.2-3.3; TASRA's Type 1 reuses the Production Web story. Christiano's WFLL Part I and Hendrycks (2023) are also antecedents. What the paper adds is the location of the failure in conversion rather than in loss of human dependence (Kulveit) or in conflict (Hammond et al.). That is a legitimate refinement of emphasis, but it is stated rather than shown, and the paper should say plainly that the thesis is inherited and the framing is what is new.

**W2 [MAJOR] Prior art that bears on originality is missing, and the Statement of Contributions overclaims as a result.**
- Critch (2021) RAAPs, as above: the persistence-across-carriers claim and the "way of succeeding" language are already there.
- Kwak, "Cultural Capture and the Financial Crisis" (2013), in the Carpenter and Moss volume the paper already cites: regulators formed by the regulated through identification, status deference and networks is precisely "epistemic closure" and the formation channel. The systematic review by Chesterfield (Regulation & Governance, 2025) shows the concept is now a literature.
- Krueger, Maharaj and Leike (2020), "Hidden incentives for auto-induced distributional shift"; Perdomo et al. (2020), performative prediction; Hardt, Jagadeesan and Mendler-Dünner (2022), "Performative Power" (NeurIPS). Performative power is a causal, measurable notion of a firm's ability to steer the population that evaluates it, identifiable with minimal market knowledge. It is the closest existing formal object to the paper's "conversion", and "measure conversion, not concentration" is essentially the performative-power programme transposed to rule stocks. Not citing it is a significant gap.
- Coupled tipping elements: Brummitt, Barnett and D'Souza (2015), "Coupled catastrophes"; Klose et al. (2020); Wunderling et al. (2021). These derive the same result that coupling tips subcritical elements before their own thresholds are crossed. The field model is a coupled-tipping-element model with a particular bilinear coupling.
- Threshold cascades and cohesion: cl(S) is the activation closure of the linear threshold model (Kempe, Kleinberg and Tardos 2003; Granovetter is cited but this is the operative formalism) and core(C) is the cohesive-set / k-core pruning of Morris (2000, "Contagion"). Propositions 2 and 3 are these combinatorial facts lifted into a cooperative ODE via comparison. Calling them a "cascade theorem" and "unravelling theorem" and the stability classification a "complete characterization" (it covers neither the boundary cases s_i(S)=1 nor nonlinear stability, and Prop 1(ii) concerns linear asymptotic stability only) oversells.
- Given the title, selectorate theory (Bueno de Mesquita et al. 2003) should be acknowledged; given the word "field", organizational field theory (DiMaggio and Powell 1983; Fligstein and McAdam 2012) and Merton's cumulative advantage (1968) should be.

None of this is fatal: the specific combination (selectors as domains in the same field, with a shared-condition mask) is the paper's own. But the related-work section currently lets a reader believe more is new than is.

**W3 [MAJOR] The AI-evaluating-AI claim is not actionable because it never meets the scalable-oversight literature (focus question 3).** The paper's beta is a single scalar that conflates three different kinds of dependence: statistical (shared lineage and data, hence correlated errors), capability (judge weaker or stronger than judged) and institutional (funding, access). Existing techniques address these differently and the paper engages with none of them: debate (Irving, Christiano and Amodei 2018) is designed so that a weaker, non-independent judge can still extract truth through adversarial structure; AI control (Greenblatt et al. 2023) uses a trusted weaker model of different provenance as monitor, which is the paper's "firewall the selectors" at the per-system level; weak-to-strong (Burns et al. 2023) and recursive reward modeling / IDA (Leike et al. 2018; Christiano et al. 2018) are recursive selection by construction; Constitutional AI / RLAIF (Bai et al. 2022) and self-rewarding LMs (Yuan et al. 2024) are the limiting case of total lineage overlap in the evaluator; reward models are routinely initialized from the policy's base model, and Gao et al. (2023) document over-optimization against them. The paper's "only if" (independence of provenance) is asserted, not derived; the mask y = V + beta x_E (K - V) is a linear interpolation with no micro-foundation and no adversarial or strategic component, although strategic evaluation gaming is the alignment community's main concern about AI evaluators.

The paper also misses the strongest implication of its own model. If the formation domain F is captured, mixed-lineage evaluator panels do not restore independence, because every lineage is formed on the same outputs. This is consistent with Kim et al. (2025), who find error correlation across providers and architectures, and with recent work the paper could cite: Christian et al. (2026, arXiv:2601.20838) show reward models inherit value biases from their pretrained base regardless of identical preference data; Awuni et al. (2026, arXiv:2609.17857) measure a 3.4-8.4 point same-family lift in LLM-judge panels that persists under mixed-family panels and a human anchor; Mancoridis and Hitzig (2026, arXiv:2606.30653) find higher generator-evaluator agreement predicts more mistakes. The paper's recommendation "no evaluator drawn solely from the evaluated system's lineage" is therefore too weak by its own logic, and it would be a sharper, more useful paper if it said so.

What is missing for practice: a definition of a measurable provenance-overlap index for an evaluation pipeline (fraction of judging decisions whose judge shares base model, pretraining data source, provider, or funder with the judged), a stated functional dependence of beta on it, and a concrete selector-divergence protocol (same-lineage minus other-lineage score as a function of the judged model's share).

**W4 [MAJOR] No empirical content.** Table 2 is "illustrative"; the "Agents" paragraph is an uncontrolled anecdote ("one software project ... that we observed") that cannot carry the weight of "evaluation captured by the evaluated with no actor intending it"; the convertibility experiment, which the paper itself describes as inexpensive with language-model agents, is proposed but not run. For a conference whose reviewers score significance, a paper whose only non-formal content is a six-domain illustration with made-up parameters will read as a position paper.

**W5 [MAJOR, easily fixed] Corollary 1 is stated more strongly than what is proven and conflicts with Section 7.** The text says "no test of domains one at a time can certify a field" and the table says field-level capture "does not follow from any domain-level audit". But a domain-level audit of domain i's full inbound row does give a sufficient local certificate: if sum_j R_ij < 1 then limsup r_i <= sum_j b_ij / d_i, so a_i r_i - c_i is eventually negative and x_i -> 0 from any x_i(0) < 1, whatever happens elsewhere. What fails is certification from domain-internal incentives (R_ii) alone, or certification of the whole field from one domain. This matters because the design principle "measure conversion, not only concentration" is exactly a per-domain inbound audit, and "audit fields, not sectors" should be qualified: a sector regulator can certify its own sector if it measures all inbound channels, but cannot know which inbound channels are live without field-level information, and the conservative certificate uses the whole row.

**W6 [MINOR, reproducibility] Appendix B is incomplete.** It states a floor of 1e-4 on shares but no ceiling. Integrating the stated algorithm (RK4, step 0.05, floor only), x_D saturates to exactly 1.0 in double precision within the first 85 time units, after which x(1-x)=0 and the liability reform in Fig. 2d cannot act; the downward hysteresis sweep in Fig. 3a likewise keeps all five domains captured down to theta=0. With a symmetric clamp at 1e-4 everything reproduces qualitatively, but V bottoms at ~0.51 rather than the reported 0.46, so the reported minimum depends on an unstated numerical choice (the unwinding delay is proportional to the logit distance the share has to travel). State the clamp and its value, release the script, and consider noting the substantive point hiding here: in the pure ODE the delay before a margin reform bites grows without bound with time spent captured, which is an "entrenchment" effect the paper could use rather than cap.

**W7 [MINOR] Internal inconsistencies and loose terminology.**
- Section 5 says Corollary 2 "explains why reforms elsewhere are re-absorbed while [formation] remains captured". In the numerics F's outbound conversion is phi = 0.1, so F contributes 0.1 to any inbound sum; re-absorption there is driven by D (theta = 1.2). The prose claim needs a parameterization that supports it.
- "Evaluation and formation are keystones of epistemic closure (Proposition 4)": Prop 4 contains no F, and "keystone" now has two meanings (Cor 4's structural keystone versus "keystone of closure").
- Eq. (1) is called "replicator dynamics on relative payoff", but the payoff difference a_i r_i - c_i is frequency-independent; the x-dynamics are a bistable switch driven by r, not a game. Say so; it avoids the impression that strategic interaction among adopters is modelled.
- "Meta-attractor" is a neologism the paper does not need: E_S are ordinary attractors of the joint (x, r) system since rules are state variables. Either define once and use sparingly or drop in favour of "capture configuration".
- The race in Fig. 2c is a near-tie by construction (silencing at t~68, alarm would fire at t~75); the text should say the parameters were chosen to exhibit the race.

**W8 [MINOR] Allocation of pages.** Section 3 runs about two pages of essay before any model, including the cancer analogy and "Power's Two Roles" (Strange, Lukes, Farrell and Newman). At an AI safety venue this reads as digression; compressing it by 40 percent would fund the oversight paragraph (W3) or a minimal experiment (W4).

## 5. Direct answer: "Could this paper, in its current version, be an important paper that advances alignment?"

NOT YET.

Focus question 1 answered directly: in its current version it will not change what alignment researchers work on, and it changes how they think only at the margin. Its central object is a relabeling at the conceptual level (regulatory and cultural capture, cumulative advantage, path dependence, niche construction, RAAPs, performative power, and gradual disempowerment's culture loop all describe success shaping the conditions of future success). Its distinct move is real but modest: treating the selectors (evaluation, formation) as domains inside the same selection field, giving threshold conditions under which a field tips although no domain would on its own, and a dual for unwinding. The formal results are correct but are standard cascade and cohesion combinatorics in ODE form, and they do not contain any alignment variable, so they cannot support the paper's headline claims (W1). The one idea with the potential to change practice, evaluator provenance as a measurable safety variable, is one paragraph and is not connected to the oversight techniques it would have to inform (W3). There is no evidence (W4).

What it would take to become an important paper, any one of which would suffice:
- (a) Formalize the alignment layer. Add principals who choose conversion investment at a cost that falls with the capability of an intent-aligned agent, and prove that intent alignment weakly raises every b_ij and so lowers every capture threshold. "Aligning each system to its operator strictly increases field-level capture risk, ceteris paribus" is a theorem this model is one step away from, and it would be the paper's headline.
- (b) Run the convertibility experiment the paper proposes. Multi-round LLM-agent tournament with a fixed hidden cost oracle; treatment arm lets winners edit the rubric or seed the judge's few-shot set; measure persistence of a costly practice and hysteresis when conversion is later removed. Persistence and hysteresis only in the treatment arm is the signature the model predicts and no existing paper has shown.
- (c) Estimate one row of the conversion matrix for the AI evaluation domain: judge-lineage overlap on major LLM-judge leaderboards (MT-Bench, AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard variants), selector divergence as a function of the judged model's share, and the funding/compute/access dependence of third-party evaluators on developers. Even a descriptive table turns Table 2 from illustration into measurement.

## 6. Questions for the authors

- Q1. How are shares kept away from 1 in your code, and what exactly produces V_min = 0.46 in Fig. 2d? With a symmetric 1e-4 clamp I obtain 0.51; with a floor only, the reform does not act.
- Q2. Do you agree that sum_j R_ij < 1 is a sufficient domain-level certificate for domain i? If so, how should Corollary 1 and the "no domain-level audit" entry of Table 1 be restated, and how does "audit fields, not sectors" survive?
- Q3. In what model-internal sense does intent alignment raise the b_ij? Can you state and prove it, even in a two-domain version?
- Q4. beta conflates lineage, capability and institutional dependence. Which does the Panickssery / Kim evidence support, and what functional form do you posit for beta as a function of lineage overlap?
- Q5. Does your model imply that mixed-lineage judge panels are insufficient once formation is captured? If so, why does Section 7 recommend them as a sufficient remedy, and how do you read Kim et al.'s cross-provider correlations and Awuni et al.'s persistence under panels?
- Q6. Beyond formalization, how does the account differ from Critch's RAAPs (carrier-independence, erosion of correction) and from Kwak's cultural capture (regulators formed by the regulated)?
- Q7. What is the "Agents" anecdote evidence for, given no controls and no data? Could it be rerun as a controlled experiment, or should it be reduced to a sentence?
- Q8. Why describe Eq. (1) as replicator dynamics when the payoff difference is frequency-independent? Is any strategic interaction among adopters intended?
- Q9. Is "complete characterization" meant to cover the boundary cases s_i(S) = 1 and Lyapunov (as opposed to linear) stability?
- Q10. What observable data would identify R_ij, and what is the minimal panel an auditor would need?

- L2.
