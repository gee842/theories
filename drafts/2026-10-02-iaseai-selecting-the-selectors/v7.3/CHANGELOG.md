## V7.2, 4 October 2026

This edition follows Ren's request to integrate the proposal on correction through institutional change, after a team considered its mathematical, conceptual and empirical implications. [V7.1](../v7.1/README.md) and [its change record](../v7.1/CHANGELOG.md) remain unchanged. ### The constructive argument

The opening, bilingual argument guide, alignment discussion, generative alternative, transition and conclusion now share one explicit target: preserve shared viability and the real capacity to discover and correct errors as capability, successors and institutions change. Section 10.4 is its principal home. Preserving a corrective function is distinguished from protecting its present officeholders or rules from legitimate replacement.

The correctable-kernel definition now makes clear that a policy's availability depends on information, resources, authority and time. Formula financing, appointment rules and access rights are candidate mechanisms whose continued execution must be established. The review also distinguishes case-specific recusal from participation in general reform, requires prompt independent review and bounded tolling, and treats preferred institutional forms as candidates rather than uniquely derived necessities. The manuscript no longer treats a common institution as necessarily outside every process of selection, or a local attractor as proof of scalar maximization. The same constructive burden applies to every institutional alternative.

### The reduced result and its limits

S16 adds one proposition, `jointcorrection`, composing the field model with load selection and the forgiving host through a maintained relation between capture and effective liability. With safe initial capture, support, load and host condition, a uniform incoming-gain bound and sufficient effective liability, the protected region is forward invariant. The proposition permits specified time variation but does not derive the funds, authority or access that maintain its bounds. It assumes fixed load traits, equal background payoffs and constant-speed replicator selection, without mutation or entry.

An analytic counterexample and numerical witness show eventual elimination of capture alongside a prior crossing of the host's floor. Local incentive and coalition examples distinguish a negative marginal return from resistance to large or coordinated changes. The intervention discussion distinguishes parameter sensitivity from the total effect of a feasible reform.

This is a limited composition, not a claim to have solved durable institutional correction, established the residual-hazard floor, or proved the present-path interlock. Those gaps remain visible in S13 and the claims register.

### Evidence and testing

Test N and protocol §6.5 specify a proposed, unrun comparison of institutions with matched resources and initial performance, permitting changes in funding, appointments, access, infrastructure, metrics and successors. Outcomes include the complete detection–authorization–execution chain before a harm deadline, legitimate appeal, service continuity and cost. Local gradients and audit scores are intermediate diagnostics. A baseline that performs as well supplies no evidence for the proposed arrangement's superiority or necessity.

EV19 records the component literature on corrigibility, human control, mutual policing and control barriers, with retrieval scope and limits. The existing Frank reference on evolutionary genetics is distinguished as 1995a from the mutual-policing paper, 1995b. CLAIMS §11 records this integration without attributing new mathematical formulations to Ren as verbatim statements; historical §§8–10 remain unchanged.
