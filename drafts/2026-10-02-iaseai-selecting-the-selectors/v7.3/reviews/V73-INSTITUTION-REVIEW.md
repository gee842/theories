## Readiness judgment

The institutional contribution is substantial enough for a **conditional working paper**. The three findings below were repaired and checked in the final source; no institutional release blocker remains within this review's scope. The addition supplies a sequence from a current bottleneck: verify existing powers, fund a finite transition, obtain usable records, practice an alternative, connect findings to executed corrections, permit timely appeal, and exercise succession under ordinary resources. It does not require the agency to possess the full compact's powers first. A missing general theory of political adoption or final incorruptible reviewer should not block this bounded contribution.

The proposed test programme can reject the claimed package advantage. Random assignment to offers, retention of failed adoption, a resource-matched alternative, joint outcomes and a successor phase are substantive improvements over V7.2. The 18-team pilot is candidly a feasibility study; a later confirmatory sample and useful-effect margin must be fixed prospectively. Absence of precision is inconclusive, not confirmation. The comparison estimates a package's effect in the specified simulated setting, not the separate effect of every component or its real-world political availability.

## Findings

These record problems in the first reviewed text and their verified resolutions. Locators refer to the corresponding paragraphs in the final reviewed source.

### IR1 — Medium: scope reduction needs a fixed service denominator

**Locators:** `src/disc/10_X10.md:19` (narrowing task and volume), `:25` (negotiating task scope), `:27` (smaller service scope); `src/reg/TESTS.md:191` (fixed denominator), `:199` (90% of legitimate requests and joint challenge rate), `:201` (primary comparison).

The route sensibly permits a smaller AI deployment when fallback is insufficient. But the initial text does not explicitly prevent the revised scope from removing difficult requests or challenge episodes from the success denominator. Matched baseline performance cannot correct treatment-induced task selection. An agency could show better joint correction among retained tasks while serving fewer people; the stated floor would then be ambiguous rather than protective.

**Action:** Fix eligibility, workload and challenge denominators before assignment. Keep dropped or redirected work in the original service accounting, with manual completion credited and unmet/delayed work counted as service loss. Report comparisons at reduced scope separately as secondary. X10 should distinguish reducing AI use from reducing the public-service obligation. This is a small reporting clarification, not a demand to prohibit useful partial adoption.

**Resolution verified:** X10 now distinguishes reduced AI scope from the original service obligation. Test N fixes eligibility, workload and challenge denominators at assignment; dropped, rejected and redirected requests remain counted, and reduced-scope comparisons are secondary. Resolved.

### IR2 — Low: the destination row retains a unanimity condition that the new route rejects

**Locator:** `src/core/b0_s11.md:88`, the Existence row beside the revised adoption row at `:89`.

An institution can lawfully bind a participant who would prefer weaker constraints. Requiring universal preference for the destination conflicts with the revised distinction between voluntary coordination and legitimate binding intervention. The cited voluntary equilibrium results remain useful support for that subclass, but are not the general definition of a viable institutional destination.

**Action:** Describe a viable destination sustained by the necessary incentives or legitimate binding arrangements; qualify the equilibrium evidence as the voluntary case. Do not imply that every supplier or officeholder must welcome correction.

**Resolution verified:** The destination is now “viable once in force, with incentives or legitimate binding constraints sufficient to preserve the assessed protection.” The voluntary equilibrium results remain bounded examples. Resolved.

### IR3 — Low / before pilot execution: specify the simulated adoption game

**Locators:** `src/reg/TESTS.md:191` (institutional teams), `:195` (votes and participant choices), `:197` (new decision rights and incentives), `:201` (implementability under the tested coalition).

The scenario fixes authority, assent and funding, but should also commit actor-specific objectives/payoffs, information and the supplier's resistance opportunities. Otherwise successful adoption may primarily measure cooperative role interpretation, while “implementation under the tested coalition” sounds stronger. Randomization removes treatment allocation bias; it does not make an underspecified political game representative.

**Action:** Add these scenario parameters to preregistration, including who controls refusal, costs and alternative offers. State that adoption findings concern that elicited incentive and authority structure; real institutional adoption requires a subsequent field assessment. This is a protocol-development requirement, not a reason to delay a conditional paper until a field study is complete.

**Resolution verified:** The added paragraph specifies assent, refusal and reversal powers, conflicting role scores, a finite supplier resistance budget and prospective information conditions. These are labelled simulation assumptions to vary before transfer to institutions. Calibration and a complete preregistration still precede execution; they need not precede publication of this proposed study. Resolved for paper readiness.

## What survives the adversarial reading

- **Authority and adoption:** X10 names the initiators and distinguishes a proposal, appropriation and enforceable commitment. It correctly excludes unilateral rewriting of a current contract and authority over unenrolled systems. Failure to obtain a prerequisite is recorded as failure at the tested scope.
- **Dependence and tradeoffs:** A nominal export is insufficient; operator practice, standby, privacy, workload and forgone productivity count. Funding and personnel independence are examined rather than inferred from organizational labels. The S17 analogy is explicitly not a calibration of contract terms or spending into equation parameters.
- **Appeal and succession:** The pilot requires prompt restoration after a valid challenge, exposes the reviewer to dependence and replacement, and keeps correction of affected cases distinct from merely disconnecting credentials. Successors inherit actual resources and records; the outgoing institution's continuity is not the protected outcome.
- **Rejection and bounds:** Failed adoption, delayed execution, a service-floor breach or defeated valid appeal can defeat success. A sufficiently precise result below the useful-effect margin can reject comparative efficacy; no result is presently claimed. The bundled shocks test the joint handoff, without separately identifying why it fails. That is adequate for the stated package-level question.

The remaining real-world unknowns—supplier acceptance, coalition durability, actual recurring cost, common infrastructure failures, valid appeal performance and continued service after replacement—are explicit empirical work. They should limit the paper's claims, not be converted into a universal impossibility conclusion.
