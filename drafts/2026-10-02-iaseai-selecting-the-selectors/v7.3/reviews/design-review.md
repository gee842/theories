# V7.2 independent conceptual and institutional review

Reviewed 4 October 2026. Fresh reading of the named V7.2 material; previous review conclusions were not treated as authority. No manuscript changes made.

## Judgment

The new constructive target is appropriately larger than the reduced result: preserve shared viability **and the executable means of correction** through admitted learning, changes in capability and successors, and institutional amendment. S16 does not claim to solve that target. Test N is clearly proposed and unrun, allows coordinated institutional changes, compares credible alternatives with matched resources, and distinguishes detection, authorization and execution. Its outcome rule expressly allows regulated competition to succeed and rejects stability purchased by suppressing legitimate appeal.

There is one claim-level release blocker and three narrower issues. Most arise where inherited language has not been reconciled with the stronger qualifications added in V7.2. They do not invalidate the reduced composition.

## Findings

### D1 — High; release blocker: the opening of §10 still asserts the institutional persistence now left open

**Exact locations:** `src/core/a0_s10.md:3` and `:5`; compare `:75`, `:77`, `:79`, `src/disc/10_X10.md:7`, `:40`, and `src/supp/16_S16.md:40`.

The opening Claim says, “Where coverage is complete and correction is financed by formula, the generative order persists through what all share, not through its winners [thm∣prem].” The Why says the order “persists as a common constraint does” through coverage, sanctions and formula-financed correctors. These are stronger conclusions than the new account supports. Equal incidence can remove a relative payoff disadvantage, and an enforceable sanction can deter a specified deviation; neither derives continuing appropriation, appointments, evidence access, authority or execution through successor and rule changes. Formula funding is a rule that those changes can disable.

The added paragraphs correctly call these candidate means and explicitly leave their effective operation open. S16 also explicitly assumes, rather than produces, executable liability and correction. The summary therefore still offers the circular answer the new target rejects: an order persists because its supporting constraint and correction persist. The `[thm∣prem]` label does not repair this when the prose lists coverage and financing as though they were the relevant sufficient premises.

**Required resolution:** Recast the two summary sentences as a design hypothesis or conditional requirement and point to the open implementation problem. If retaining a theorem claim, name the maintained executable-control premises and restrict the claim to the specified model; do not call that institutional persistence. This is a consistency correction, not a demand to solve C4 before publication.

### D2 — Medium; substantive design gap: assessment and recusal can suppress legitimate constitutional correction

**Exact locations:** `src/disc/10_X10.md:34`; compare `:7`, `:24`, `:44`, `:56`, and `TESTS.md:183–185`.

The new text rightly protects proposals to amend mistaken purposes. The operational recusal clause nevertheless removes a participant's vote on such revisions as soon as the evaluation network opens an assessment. The deadline then stops while the assessed party withholds anything the assessment “requires.” There is no stated independent, timely ruling on whether opening the assessment, demanding particular access or tolling the deadline is justified, or when amendment rights are restored. The appeal provisions chiefly describe contesting a finding and restrictions, not this prior deprivation of participation.

A concrete adverse case is an incumbent evaluator opening an assessment of a participant proposing a legitimate change to its mandate, demanding disputed or unavailable material, and maintaining recusal while treating the dispute as withholding. The new definition of capture does not itself give that participant a remedy before the decision it wished to contest. General rights to comment or appeal are insufficient if institutional amendment can pass while the participant is excluded.

**Resolution:** Either specify prompt independent review of assessment scope, recusal and tolling, including restoration of participation and protection against repeated exclusion, or explicitly register this as an unresolved overreach mechanism. Add this concrete adversarial case to Test N's legitimate-appeal and false-intervention arm. The paper need not confer a right to decide one's individual enforcement case; it does need to distinguish that conflict from blanket exclusion from revising general purposes.

### D3 — Medium; overclaim: recomputable certificates do not end the guardian regress

**Exact location:** `src/disc/10_X10.md:30`; compare `:11`, `:38`, `:40`, `src/core/40_s4.md:87`, and `src/supp/16_S16.md:7–9`.

The chamber paragraph says, “The regress of guardians ends not at a trusted body but at node-by-node certificates that many parties can recompute from public data.” The adjoining caveat sends what remains to standard-setting and disclosure, but the constructive target also requires someone able to obtain the data, contest the model or standard, authorize intervention and implement a remedy. Recomputability can distribute mathematical checking; it does not produce these powers or secure their continuity. Even complete and accurate row sums provide a model-conditional capture certificate, not a certificate of just purposes or all forms of effective correction.

The paper elsewhere recognizes these distinctions unusually clearly. This sentence nevertheless implies a completed institutional foundation that the new enlarged-state target expressly leaves open.

**Resolution:** Say that distributed certificates replace reliance on one trusted guardian with explicit, testable conditions. Keep the survival of the evidence and execution chain, and the legitimate revision of its standards, as continuing C4/Test N obligations. No extra permanent meta-controller should be inserted to make this claim true.

### D4 — Medium; residual necessity overstatement: the design table derives particular forms from functional constraints

**Exact locations:** `src/core/a0_s10.md:89`, `:91–109`, especially `:95`, `:100`, and `:105`; `src/disc/10_X10.md:44`; compare `src/core/80_s8.md:93`, `src/core/c0_s12.md:11`, and `src/core/40_s4.md:29–31`.

The new comparison correctly permits safe endpoints with continuing competition and does not require negative fitness for every obstructive preference. But §10.5 still calls its third column “Form that follows,” says each counter-mechanism “requires its own form,” and concludes the forms “are derived from function.” The rows then choose formula funding, a particular chamber, and broad E1 as the scope from premises that establish functional constraints, sufficient certificates or the much narrower delegation equilibrium. Those premises do not uniquely require these organizational forms.

The same slippage is explicit in X10's rationale for excluding provider votes: “the result of Section 4 is that a party able to revise its own assessment captures it.” The cited influence proposition establishes a **conditional local return**, with cost thresholds and other failure conditions, not that every such voting right results in capture. X10 can prefer voice without vote as a design judgment; it cannot derive its necessity from that proposition.

**Resolution:** Label these as candidate forms addressing the functional requirements and label the provider-vote exclusion as a risk judgment. Preserve the new distinction between required protection and preferred mechanisms. This does not require weakening the stated preference for broad E1 or denying that unrestricted self-assessment is hazardous.

## What this review did not find

- No new claim that the reduced S16 invariant region is the correctable kernel, an attracting basin, an implementable general controller, or proof of institutional uninvadability. `src/core/a0_s10.md:79`, `src/core/b0_s11.md:88,95`, and S16 are explicit about the maintained bounds and restricted initial states.
- No claim in the new Test N protocol that a local anti-obstruction gradient is necessary or sufficient for the constructive target. It includes coalitions, institutional amendment and successors, allows a successful baseline to defeat superiority claims, and counts failed authorization or late execution as failure.
- No substitution of the new composition for the open empirical causal joins or C1's residual-risk premise. `src/core/c0_s12.md:13` directly prevents that inference.
- No hidden claim of demonstrated superiority in the new X8 introduction, START-HERE's reduced-result paragraph, or §12's revised conclusion. The remaining derived-form language is D4, rather than a reason to disregard those explicit qualifications.

## Blockers and scope

**Release blocker:** D1 should be corrected before presenting V7.2 as having consistently integrated the constructive target. D2–D4 need a correction or an explicit unresolved status; none requires completing an institutional implementation or running Test N before publishing a bounded research proposal. D2 is a blocker to treating the compact as already operationally compliant with its own contestation standard, not to describing it as a candidate.

This review read `src/core/20_s2.md`, `80_s8.md`, `a0_s10.md`, `b0_s11.md`, `c0_s12.md`, `src/disc/08_X8.md`, `10_X10.md`, START-HERE, C4 and Test N in TESTS, with supporting checks of the capture/influence definitions and S16. V7.1 diffs were used to distinguish new qualifications from inherited language. It did not audit all proofs, reproduce numerical checks, verify external empirical sources, estimate actual institutional hazard bounds, or judge the author's values for the author. Test N's independent research scoring instrument is an acknowledged experimental boundary, not an identified circular proof; any empirical conclusion remains limited to its registered envelope.
