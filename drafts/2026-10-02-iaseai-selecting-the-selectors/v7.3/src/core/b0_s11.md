# Part IV. Transition and judgment

Part IV's results on trust, signals, pauses and dwell hold in the models they state; its tracks, readiness dimensions and requirements of a reachable path are definitions, each row with the support it cites; its precedents are observations whose limiting conditions are part of what they show; and its judgment is a recommendation under the standard of Section 8.1.

## 11. A reachable transition

> **Claim.** An end state that every participant would keep is not thereby one they can reach: a path is reachable when it is survivable at every stage, preferred by every participant at every step given its beliefs, and held at every step until the slow variable that step moves has crossed its threshold [def]. In the field model, a step that does not carry its slow variable across its threshold, or is released on share levels before its dwell, is re-absorbed, so graduated steps reach only as a ratchet; and unless a lineage of the alternative persists that can be recruited when the rule changes, the hold a reform needs grows without bound with the age of capture [thm]. Narrow E1 among actors who price in a certain lock, and a costly signal that separates a survival-first actor from a relative one, need no trust; where the first steps are of this kind, trust is an outcome of the transition, not its precondition [thm]. A pause that changes nothing buys time, not survival [thm].
>
> **Why.** Trust is needed to cooperate against a possible defector, to be believed and to stay; payoff changes that raise restraint relative to temptation, narrow the bias of talk and lower the exit premium relax these respective constraints [thm]. Rule support falls only at the rate at which it is corrected, while shares lag it, so levels improve before safety does [thm]; a prediction carried only by assurances meets the third erosion [thm]. Each commitment moves a slow variable with its own time constant, so the commitments run as parallel tracks, the slowest started first [def].

### 11.1 Trust, and what relaxes it

Trust enters a transition in three places. To cooperate against a possible defector, an actor needs its belief $\varpi$ that the other will cooperate to reach the threshold $g_D/(g_C+g_D)$ of {{assure}}; where the value of winning exceeds the value of cooperating, $T>R$, verification has to supply what belief does not. To be believed, a sender needs the receiver not to discount what it says for what it gains by saying it, which relative accounting erodes ({{talk}}). To stay, a participant needs confidence that the arrangement will persist, which the net gain from exit erodes where that gain grows with the capability held in check ({{overhang}}). Confidence in reliable instruments of verification lets parties cooperate without good will about what verification can see, and only once some belief has let them install it: installing them is itself a first step taken under $\varpi$.

The constraints have separate comparative statics ({{relaxtrust}}): raising $R$ or lowering $T$ with the other stage-game primitives fixed lowers the belief threshold where $g_C,g_D>0$; a wider working objective lowers the bias of talk; and a smaller positive relative premium in exit lowers its net gain. A common intervention relaxes all three only where it makes all three changes [thm∣prem]. Verification relaxes the first where $T-P+F>0$ and $P>S$, and only on what it observes; a sanction relaxes the first and the third only while it can still be imposed, so that against a defector whose position has become decisive it acts only through the bound $\bar p_d$ on the chance of breakout ({{enforce}}(2)) [thm]. Sharing and provision beneath the game can act on these payoffs, and where they lower the price of standing they widen working objectives and the range of informative talk ({{standprice}}); their net effect on trust must be checked using every stage payoff they change, not $T-R$ alone [thm∣prem]; a common frontier lowers the vulnerability of restraint, $P-S$, by pooling the restrainers' effort ({{commonfrontn}}) [num]; and the price of sanctions is escalation, which the comparison counts in $\Theta$ (Section 8.1).

Trust and verification are both conditions the transition has to meet, and they do not conflict: neither substitutes for the other, and each makes the other work. Trust grows from conduct that a lower value of winning makes rational and verification makes visible; verification works only where some trust reads its reports as information rather than as moves. The first steps of Section 11.2 need no prior trust to begin, so trust can grow as an outcome of the transition as well as be a condition of its later steps. In the corpus's words, "Trust is not a prerequisite—it is an outcome. It arises only when we have a shared understanding of reality and can reasonably expect others to act accordingly" [corpus: selfishness-spiral, 2025-03-14]. The author counts trust among the bottlenecks, the belief that the other side's messages are credible and its commitments will be kept, and how much it matters (这里面有几个bottomneck - 而这个来自于信任（相信其他方的消息可信并可守承诺），以及这个的重要性) [corpus: possibility-space, 2026-10-03]. And, here in an edited translation of mixed Chinese and English speech: whether one builds verification or raises transparency, without trust it is all in vain (无论你是建立核查机制（verification），还是提高透明度（transparency），只要没有信任，这些都白搭) [47:25]; so why decide from the start that the other side cannot be trusted? Let us first try making contact (所以，为什么一开始就认定对方不可信？我们先尝试接触) [41:57] [corpus: possibility-space, 2026-10-04]. Installing instruments, and reading their reports as information rather than as moves, already needs some prior belief, so the first step is to stop presuming the other's type.

Not every obstacle is a lack of trust, and the remedy follows the obstacle. A misjudged shared loss calls for evidence; a real conflict of incentives, for changed payoffs and enforcement; a coordination failure, for verification and graduated reciprocation (Osgood 1962); commitment and capture over time, for independent correction and steps each held for its dwell. Graduated steps answer the third only, and against capture they reach only as a ratchet (Section 11.4).

### 11.2 Prediction, evidence and signals

A prediction about a game whose outcome turns on what the players believe is one of the game's inputs, and it acts through two beliefs. Where an actor misjudges the shared loss, a derivation it can check corrects its own accounting: an actor that holds the prediction, and finds that restraint gives it higher survival whether its rival restrains or races, restrains whatever it believes about the others ({{survassure}}(2)) [thm]. Where racing persists because each expects restraint to be exploited while a cooperative equilibrium exists (Section 5.4), the same derivation moves $\varpi$, and the equilibrium with it. That is counter-performativity, a model of behaviour making the world less like itself (MacKenzie 2006) [obs], and the sense in which this paper's prediction is written to be falsified by being shared (Section 0). It acts only where the outcome turns on a belief the prediction can change: it does not change what the game selects whatever is believed, and where racing is dominant in a one-shot game the belief it would correct is simply correct.

Messages about a shared hazard are not credible merely because the hazard is shared, since a sender gains from the receiver's restraint whatever it intends ({{talk}}(3)): a prediction carried only by assurances meets the third erosion. It has to be carried by what a receiver can check without trusting the sender: a derivation from public premises; verifiable evidence, such as third-party disclosure of incidents and joint evaluation suites; protocols agreed in advance, which commit a sender's disclosures before it knows what they will show (Kamenica and Gentzkow 2011); demonstrations that make a dynamic visible; and costly signals [def]. Shared understanding removes one road to conflict, since under common priors mutual optimism alone cannot produce war (Fey and Ramsay 2007) [thm], but not the others: in an idealized laboratory race in which unsafe play carried a private risk, borne by the winner, of losing one's winnings, choices tracked the state of the race, falling behind and a rival's unsafe move, more than risk preferences; the design did not test shared risk, as its authors note (EV11) [obs]. A change of prediction is the first step, not a sufficient one. The next is conduct a rival can read, which also tips a professed doctrine that few hold in private (Section 5.4); and the payoffs that make falling behind unsurvivable have to change as well, which the arrangements below and Section 12 are for.

**{{!Proposition:signal}} (When keeping AI out of one's own decision loops is a signal) [thm].** A step that the lock makes cheap for a survival-first actor and the contest makes costly for a relative one separates them; a step that is cheap to imitate buys restraint only in proportion to what it costs a relative actor. *A sender is survival-first and prices in the lock (type $\mathsf S$, with probability $1-\mu$) or holds a relative objective (type $\mathsf R$, with probability $\mu$). It can take a step $\mathsf E$: a verifiable, durable removal of AI from its own strategic decision loops for a period, which costs type $\mathsf t$ the amount $c_{\mathsf t}$. A receiver restrains when its belief that the sender is $\mathsf S$ is above $\varpi^\ast\in(0,1)$, races when it is below, and is indifferent at $\varpi^\ast$, where it may mix; its restraint is worth $\Pi_{\mathsf t}>0$ to type $\mathsf t$, including, for $\mathsf R$, what $\mathsf R$ gains by exploiting it.*
1. *An equilibrium in which $\mathsf S$ takes the step, $\mathsf R$ does not, and the receiver restrains after the step and not otherwise exists iff $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}$ and $c_{\mathsf R}\ge\Pi_{\mathsf R}$.*
2. *If $0\le c_{\mathsf R}<\Pi_{\mathsf R}$, there is no such equilibrium. Where prior trust falls short, $1-\mu<\varpi^\ast$, the receiver restrains after the step with probability at most $c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ in every equilibrium; where $c_{\mathsf S}\le\Pi_{\mathsf S}c_{\mathsf R}/\Pi_{\mathsf R}$ the bound is attained, by an equilibrium in which $\mathsf R$ imitates just often enough to hold the receiver's belief at $\varpi^\ast$.*
3. *A step that is reversible or unverifiable, $c_{\mathsf R}\to0$, is therefore cheap talk: it buys no restraint that prior trust did not already give.*

*Scope.* Whether the step separates is a question of measured costs: where the lock is certain, keeping AI out raises the survival-first type's own survival, so $c_{\mathsf S}$ can be zero or negative ({{delegatelock}}(1)), while a relative type, which delegates at every speed ({{delegate}}(3)), pays for a durable removal with the speed it forgoes, the more the longer the period. Where prior trust falls short and the inequalities of (1) are strict, the separating equilibrium is the only one that survives the intuitive criterion. *Proof:* S11 [num: TR].

Unilateral, verifiable narrow E1 is therefore a candidate first move, cheap for an actor that prices in the lock and costly for one that values the speed delegation buys [thm∣prem]: the costly signal by which a security-seeking type reveals itself (Kydd 2000), which only inspection of doctrine and exercises keeps a relative type from declaring without taking (S11).

### 11.3 Four temporal arrangements, and readiness

Keeping AI out of the contest, stopping, slowing and pausing differ in cost, in verification and in how they fail. Section 5.3 defined stopping (E2) and slowing (E3) by what they do to the growth channel of risk; here each is stated as an arrangement.

**E1: AI out of the contest.** *The narrow form* keeps human authority over the decisions whose delegation would lock with near certainty: strategic and escalatory decisions irreversible within the time available for deliberation, such as the use of force and nuclear command. It comes with jointly verified limits, drilled fallback capacity and an evidence channel independent of AI, so that a human judging an alarm has reasons of their own. Among actors who price in the lock it is a strict equilibrium that needs no trust where the lock is certain, $q_L=1$, and it stays one while the lock fails with a probability below the bound of {{nearlock}} ({{delegatelock}}(1)) [thm]. It leaves detection available, but $\upsilon(T_{\rm dec})$ measures detection and effective response together: $p_d$ is unchanged only where narrow E1 preserves that complete clock. Where human authorization adds response time, C7 must measure it and verify the enforcement bound with it ({{detectlink}}) [thm∣prem]. Where the lock is uncertain, as in tactical domains with a large speed advantage, delegation can be an equilibrium too ({{delegatelock}}(2), (3)): keeping AI out then rests on a commitment held in common, or on the signal of {{signal}} where its costs separate, and bounded, reversible defensive automation falls under the test of O2 (Section 6.6; X11), not under E1. *The broad form*, AI not an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, judged by the game and not by the act, has its criterion and the three layers of its enforcement in Section 6.6; it is the scope, and the narrow form the first step. E1 is held in common: an actor that stays out alone while others race survives less than by joining wherever staying out raises a rival's chance of a decisive position enough ({{survassure}}(3); X11) [thm], so E1 begins with the decision loops in which the lock makes it self-enforcing, and is extended by agreement.

**The deterrence objection**, at its strongest, is that a commitment that cannot be revoked is worth more than one that can (Schelling 1960), so that semi-automated retaliation would relieve the pressure to launch on warning. Survivable, human-controlled forces give the same relief without removing judgment, and the commitment value of automation is the lock itself, O2 given up in that domain ({{lockin}}). An automated commitment cannot tell a false alarm from an attack, and false alarms have been stopped by human judgment, as in 1983 (Podvig 2002; Hoffman 2009) [obs: EV18]; escalation at machine speed is a common risk that relative selection does not see ({{blind}}), so the contest under-prices it [thm∣prem]. X11 gives the answers in full.

**E2: stop.** Capability of the decisive class is not built while the applicable conditions for governing it are absent: independent evidence adequate to bound the relevant coupling and hazards, binding purposes and constraints, and effective custody or equally effective durable containment that keeps every relevant deviation within the enforceable bound. The shared-prediction and common-custody arrangement is the preferred route proposed here; it is not the only functional way to satisfy this gate. Any permitted construction also requires the post-construction disposition and independent execution plan assessed under Q7 below. Where the decisive class also exceeds independent coverage, rollback can end that exposure fastest ({{freeze}}). The independent coverage and control condition applies to non-decisive classes too, under E3; divergences that hide under evaluation are one reason for it ({{masked}}). Because E2 applies to the decisive class only, its cost, if AI turns out not to be decisive, is the delay of that class's margin over capable systems that are not decisive, including lives that earlier benefits would have saved. The cost can be large (crux C10) and, for those who bear it, irreversible; but it does not foreclose later choice, whereas if AI is decisive the loss it avoids forecloses choice for everyone, an asymmetry in the option set, not in the size of the losses (Arrow and Fisher 1974). Coverage of E2 comes from consent or from coercion, whose risk of escalation is counted in $\Theta$ and whose conditions are stated once, in Section 12.1.

**E3: develop at the pace of understanding.** In each hazard class, capability grows no faster than contestable coverage can follow, so the uncovered hazardous share stays summable ({{coverage}}) and each hazard mode keeps its robustness margin ({{tempo}}). It binds class by class, where coverage or an independent safety case falls short, on every branch including the preferred broad E1 arrangement (Section 5.3); its carriers are thresholds in capability and in effective compute tied to measured coverage per class (Section 10.4). How much it slows depends on whether AI-assisted oversight raises coverage as fast as hazardous variety grows (crux C3a). Stopping is not slowing: E2 forgoes the decisive class, and E3 paces each hazard class to its coverage, including suspension or rollback where pacing alone cannot restore adequate control. Broad E1 changes the social game; it does not establish the residual-risk or coverage premises for a noncompetitive programme.

**A temporary pause**, the fourth arrangement, is valued by what changes during it ({{pausevalue}}): where it adds no hazard of its own and the same process resumes after it, survival over any number of active rounds is unchanged, and so is its limit, which is zero where the residual hazards are not summable ({{nonsum}}); a pause after which they are summable, where before they were not, keeps survival bounded away from zero; and in the field model a pause that freezes shares while ordinary rule support keeps accumulating completes capture from every frozen share above $1/G$ ({{pausefield}}) [thm]. A pause that changes nothing buys time, not survival, and the time is for changing the causal conditions: verification, residual hazards, human capacity, enforceable limits, shared provision, objectives and institutions. An indefinite stop, a lasting cap, or a pause that produces a different decision process is a different policy. **A pause with no relevant change is not a certificate of readiness to resume**, and readiness has therefore to be defined.

**{{!Definition:readiness}} (Readiness to resume) [def].** *Readiness is assessed along seven dimensions: technical reliability (Q1), shared objectives (Q2), strategic incentives (Q3), effective correction (Q4), deployment constraints (Q5), retained human agency (Q6), and custody and verifiability (Q7). Each has observable indicators, a threshold set by the risk decisions of the domain of use with the error of measurement inside it, and an assessor whose own row sums are certified and who is insulated from rule-making during the pause. A dimension is met when its indicator has stayed past its threshold for its residence time, at least the time constant of the slow variable on which the dimension rests. Each dimension is decided by a sequential test registered in advance, with a model of the noise in its readings, two levels, the lower at its threshold and the upper above it by a registered indifference band, within which the pause stays, and two error rates: the rate of declaring it met when it stands at or below its threshold, set by the protection standard; and the rate of declaring it unmet when it stands at or above the upper level, bounded so that the seven together hold back a ready state with no more than a registered probability. A met dimension is re-opened by a test of the same kind, not by a single reading. All seven must be met; none compensates for another.*

Before resumption that permits construction of the decisive class, Q7 also requires a registered disposition of the built capability if participation or custody later fails, with an executor independent of the failing party; X10 gives a candidate and its limits. This is a design requirement, not a proof of safe fallback. These are dimensions to operationalize, not a checklist proved sufficient; S11 gives the question each asks and the slow variable on which it rests, and X11 records where each stands today. They do not require that every person's private motives or relative position change first: binding institutions can change the effective game while private competitive preferences persist. Q2 therefore concerns effective purposes and rules, and Q3 the net incentive bound for all relevant deviations; reducing the value of winning is the preferred route, not a prerequisite when durable enforcement meets the bound. Q7 permits common custody or equally effective verified containment, each with the applicable lifecycle plan. Readiness presupposes rows R1, R2, R7 and R8b of {{require}} ({{crosswalk}}), since no pause supplies them. Residence is required because levels move before slow variables cross (Section 11.4); the decision rule, because noise and conjunction together bias a fixed reading toward "not ready": seven dimensions, each read once with a 5% chance of missing a met dimension, pass a ready state with probability $0.95^7\approx0.70$ [thm], and a pause held past its evidence raises the cost of staying, so that coverage tips once that cost exceeds the cost of standing outside ({{unravel}}) [thm∣prem]. A sequential test turns noise into delay instead of bias, at error rates fixed in advance ({{readrule}}) [num: TR].

**The order.** By the trust each needs, E1 comes first, since it keeps humans as the players and, in its narrow form, is a strict equilibrium among actors who price in the lock; then E2 or E3, class by class. By verifiability the order reverses. E2 and E3 are the most verifiable among rivals, since compute and its hardware can be observed, though thresholds stated in compute erode as algorithmic efficiency improves, a rate condition that verification must meet (EV9) [obs], and hardware mechanisms for verifying compute use are proposed and prototyped, not deployed (EV17) [obs]. E1 is the hardest to verify, since it concerns software and internal procedure, and the most self-enforcing among actors who price in the lock, which is why it can begin as declared doctrine with inspected exercises (X11). A third order, by time constant, decides what is started first (Section 11.4).

The author's own order is conditional, and comes before these. If cooperation cannot be had, the first choice is that neither AI nor its use is built into the logic of struggle, AI neither existing as struggle nor used as its instrument (broad E1); failing even that, ASI and recursive self-improvement are not built, since the social and cognitive conditions are not met though the technical ones are, until cooperation becomes possible (E2), or development is slowed below the pace at which understanding can respond (E3) (如果不能合作：优先选择是不把ai本身和对ai的使用嵌入或者变成这种逻辑，即ai不应该以斗争的方式存在，也不能被当成斗争的工具……其次，如果连这个都做不到，我们人类应该有自知之明，先别搞asi，rsi，我们虽然技术条件达到了，但是社会和意识条件依然未达到，应该先停下来，让合作变得可能……要么就把发展速度降低到认知以及通过认知可以做出应当应对的速度以下) [corpus: possibility-space, 2026-10-03]. His closing judgment, that every other path is a dead end for humanity (其他都是人类绝路啊), is his value judgment [val]. He put the same order on 4 October, here in an edited translation of mixed Chinese and English speech: AI need not necessarily be stopped; at least first create a logic of cooperation in which AI does not join the struggle (其实也不一定非要把 AI 停下来。至少先创造一种合作的逻辑，让 AI 不加入斗争) [45:45]; if even that cannot be done, let us not build it yet, and first raise our own level of consciousness (如果连这个都做不到……那我们就先别造，先提升我们自己的意识水平) [46:02]; there is no way to win safely like this (没有办法这样安全地赢) [46:20] [corpus: alignment-selection, 2026-10-04]. This is a preference among social arrangements [val]: broad E1 is preferred to managing a continuing contest by stopping its decisive class or slowing it, and those measures are warranted where E1 has not been achieved and their respective triggers bind. It is not an exemption from the independent growth-channel condition. Even on the preferred branch, hazardous development must satisfy the coverage and control condition of E3, class by class; meeting E1 alone does not show that it does. Tier 2 therefore has separate triggers: the approach of a unilateral decisive position activates E2 and custody, while a failure of coverage or independent control activates E3 on any branch. The first trigger is evidence that the social condition sought by broad E1 is not secure; the second can bind without a contest.

### 11.4 Parallel tracks, dwell and windows

Each commitment of the transition moves a slow variable and holds only if that variable crosses its threshold before the commitment is withdrawn ({{reabsorb}}). The slow variables have different time constants, and one that is not moving when it is needed cannot be moved then: started one after another in the order of the trust they need, the slowest would start last and finish after the window it serves had closed. The commitments are therefore tracks that run in parallel, started in the order of their time constants, slowest first, and binding in the order of the trust they need, from the first track to the fifth [def]. The tracks are a candidate to be tested, not a claim that every setting follows them (X11).

| Track | Slow variable and its time constant |
|---|---|
| 1. Shared account of hazards | Shared understanding of the hazards, which cannot run ahead of the evidence: for late-manifesting classes, their manifestation window ({{tempo}}) |
| 2. Narrow, testable commitments | Human capacity to take delegated decisions back, which decays at $\delta_h$ under delegation and recovers at $\varrho_h$ without it ({{lockin}}) |
| 3. Fund correction and shared services | Rule support in each corrector domain: several institutional turnovers $1/(\varepsilon d)$, more the deeper the lock-in ({{dwell}}) |
| 4. Delegation, access, custody, decision rights | The capability held in check, which raises the net gain from exit ({{overhang}}); the lead time of custody's installation and verification, which must be shorter than the time to a decisive position |
| 5. Broaden as institutions prove themselves | The record of the arrangements under stress, and the self-models formed inside them: a cycle of stress and recovery, and for minds a generation |

Each track meets a compensating loop of its own, from measurement of hazards turned into measurement of who leads, on the first, to closure into a bloc, on the fifth; X11 gives what each track commits, the mechanisms it acts on, its compensating loop and its indicator of progress. Slowest first means that the third and fourth tracks begin before the trust they will bind on exists, and that the openness of the fifth is built in from the first. That is why Section 12.1 begins its first two tiers together.

**Dwell.** A reform that insulates rule-making from shares lets rule support decay at its rate of correction, but shares answer to rule support and lag it: they keep rising while support stays above break-even, for $\ln G/(\varepsilon d)$, so the reform has to be held through that rise and then long enough to repay the lock-in it added; from a captured share above $x_\sigma$, shares are back at their starting level before the state has left the basin of capture [thm]. A rollback judged on share levels is therefore premature (Test D); release exactly at the dwell boundary approaches the saddle, so the hold must exceed it by a margin. What decides is where rule support stands against the separatrix, which is not observed directly; near full share, the depth of lock-in cannot be read from the level at all. The hold grows with that depth, and depth with the age of capture, so without a floor under the alternative the hold has no bound [thm]. A lineage of the alternative that persists inside selection, and is recruited when the rule changes, bounds it [thm]: from a captured share of 0.99, with $G=10$ and rule-making ten times slower than adoption, a floor of 0.1% cuts the hold from 10.45 to 3.81 in units of $1/(\varepsilon d)$, while a share protected outside selection, which does not recruit, leaves it at 10.44 ({{dwell}}, with the holds by gain, speed and depth) [num: DW]. Without the floor the alternative's share falls during the hold to about $10^{-31}$ at these settings, extinct in any finite population, so the hold is then unbounded and the floored values are the meaningful magnitudes [num: VF]. These holds take the conversion gain as fixed; where capture has built capacity that goes on converting, the gain stays higher and so does the hold. For real institutions the magnitudes are a conjecture [conj].

That is the mechanism of decay and renewal: in the field model, an order in which forms can fail and be replaced can be reformed in bounded time, and one that has frozen cannot [thm]. In the author's words, "look at the ecosystem, death is not a bug but a feature, changes and mutations are what keeps things alive — create a rich space of systems, not reducing to one rule, one attractor" [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-06-05].

**The ratchet.** In the field model, graduated steps reach the threshold only as a ratchet: a sequence of steps none of which carries its slow variable across its threshold and is held there strictly beyond its dwell boundary cannot be certified to escape capture, whatever trust it builds, and a step that crosses on one track does not cross on another ({{ratchet}}) [thm]. A transition is not shown to arrive by small steps that each feel safe; it arrives, if it does, by steps each of which is kept.

**Windows.** Whether frontier development is still the keystone (Section 4.5) is a conjecture [conj]. The window closes as conversion among the corrector domains grows, which is why the correction track cannot wait for the trust that custody needs. Arrangements of this kind are often adopted when a crisis opens a window (Gunderson and Holling 2002) [obs]: what a crisis can adopt is a design prepared in advance, with its activation committed, and an emergency closure has to state before adoption what would end it, or it will not afterwards. Those who cannot leave the shared exposure need standing on every track (X11).

**Where things stand.** The first two tracks have seeds, in an international scientific assessment and a UN scientific panel whose appointment one leading state opposed, and in an affirmation by two rival states of human control over nuclear use, with a dialogue and an incident channel between them; narrow E1 has no multilateral document that the leading states now endorse, and its only shared basis is that affirmation (EV7, EV8) [obs]. The slow tracks, correction and custody, have no seed of comparable standing (EV3, EV17) [obs]. The seeds must become binding, verifiable and broader before the relevant losses or lock-in; X11 gives the record.

Read as a problem of control, the table asks whether the present state lies in the capture basin of the target, the set from which it can be reached and then held under admissible steps (Aubin 1991); the one-domain field model supplies the dwell boundary of Section 11.4, the infimum time for entering the basin of absence, with release strictly later and a margin ({{capturebasin}}). Section 11.6 adds a bounded path joining correction, selected load and host viability. Physical reachability under available controls and political adoption of those controls are separate requirements.

The constructive target of Section 10.4 also asks whether the means of taking the next corrective step survive each preceding one. {{jointcorrection}} preserves its joint region once the initial state and maintained premises meet its bounds; it does not itself supply a path into that region or the institutions that keep its controls available. The conditional path of {{reformpath}} addresses the former for a reduced class; the latter remains an institutional task. Replacement of a provider, evaluator or rule must therefore be tested for continued access, authority and response time, not inferred safe from continuity of its name or charter.

**Precedents.** The record of restraint among rivals qualifies all of this, and each precedent's limiting condition is where its analogy to AI is weakest (X11, corrected against the sources) [obs]. The 1946 proposals for international ownership of atomic energy failed on sequence and on enforcement against violators: **custody must precede decisiveness**. The biological-weapons record shows that a weapon endangering its holder can be renounced unilaterally, as one party did before the 1972 Convention, where its military value was low and the renouncer held another deterrent, and, under a convention without verification, that a covert programme can persist. Nuclear power after a serious accident shows durable safety under unchanged motives where the shared loss returns to its source and no prize is decisive, with later lapses: a change of selection, not of motive. The Montreal Protocol shows coverage made attractive by provision and enforced by conditions of access, once substitutes existed and the value of winning was low. Rivals survived where nature or balancing supplied saturation (Section 7.2); if advantage in AI is unsaturated, as the racers' own premise holds (crux C5), safety must come from changing what is selected before advantage becomes decisive [thm∣prem].

### 11.6 Improving institutions from where they are

An actionable theory must identify changes an existing institution can make, the protection it needs while making them, and the capacity its successor must retain. The relevant negative result is bounded: a specified arrangement cannot meet a specified obligation while retaining an identified constraint. That directs attention to changing the constraint. Failure of a sufficient certificate alone establishes neither incapacity nor the necessity of the preferred reform. Successful correction by an existing institution is evidence for a route that works, not a reason to redefine it as outside the theory.

**A finite transition to a maintained destination.** {{reformpath}} in S17 composes a construction delay, a temporary increase in correction, a structural reduction in incoming influence and ordinary continuing maintenance [thm∣prem]. Its initial state need not satisfy the joint protected region of {{jointcorrection}}. Explicit bounds determine how long the temporary intervention must be available, the transition resources and host reserve sufficient to reach that region, and the continuing correction sufficient after the temporary increase ends. The proof uses comparison bounds and the earlier invariant set. Reaching a protected set while avoiding an unsafe one is an established control problem (Meng et al. 2021); the contribution here is the specified composition and its institutional interpretation, not a new general control method.

The construction distinguishes the work of reform from the work it leaves behind. Removing a temporary increase is justified by the resulting state and maintained operating conditions, not by an elapsed announcement period or an eventual-recovery guarantee. A reform whose construction takes longer consumes more reserve before its protection takes effect. A stronger temporary effort may shorten part of that wait but raises the resource rate; its total cost depends on the resulting duration. An improvement in one parameter can be outweighed by the dependencies through which it is supplied (S16). No monotone improvement in every indicator is required; the whole path must respect its protected floors.

The assumptions matter as much as the calculation. A sponsor must be able to authorize and pay for the temporary intervention, the reduction in incoming influence must actually occur and remain bounded, and ordinary maintenance and effective liability must remain executable. Those are maintained capacities in the reduced model. The result does not explain how an institution wins those powers, keeps them through unrestricted amendment or provides technically complete coverage of future AI hazards. Its sufficient reserve and timing bounds do not identify the best reform or prove failure when they are missed.

**One operational route.** X10 develops a bounded illustration in an existing procurement programme: improve evidence access and continuity, connect a justified finding to an authorized and resourced response, exercise that response against a leading enrolled supplier, and test whether the capacities survive succession. Work starts with an actor's existing authority over a specified contract and service; it does not assume authority over unenrolled developers or other jurisdictions. Participation, migration costs, resistance and errors by the corrector enter the assessment. A supplier's market success can remain rewarded while its power to withhold the evidence or service needed for correction is reduced. The analogy to lower conversion and stronger correction is a hypothesis about mechanisms, not a calibration of the field equations.

**What counts as moving in the right direction.** At matched resources, a candidate must improve the timely correction of consequential mistakes while maintaining useful service, legitimate challenge and the means to correct the next mistake. Detecting more problems without authorization or execution does not meet this criterion. Nor does making the regulator impossible to challenge. Test N includes failed adoption, switching and standby costs, contested findings, valid appeals and succession. Improvement in a bounded pilot supports that reform within the tested setting; it does not establish global coverage, an irreversible-risk bound or superiority over every institutional alternative.

Section 12 takes from here the release rule of its second tier, readiness held for its residence time and judged against the dwell, and the reason its slowest preparations are warranted now: what binds late has to begin early.
