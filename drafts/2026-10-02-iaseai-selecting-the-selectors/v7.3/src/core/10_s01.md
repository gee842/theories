## 0. Why this theory

**A prediction, for coordination.** A good theory predicts. This one predicts that, on the present trajectory, the logic of winning carries the participants toward an outcome none of them would choose. In the dilemmas Part I computes, racing is each actor's best response and leaves each worse off than joint restraint would, and the more decisive the advantage, the more of what is contested is consumed in contesting it (Section 5.1). If the hazards of capability outgrow the correction that can contest them, as Section 5.3 states and Section 12 treats as the central crux, the same logic endangers whoever would win, since a winner escapes only by ceasing to grow what outgrows correction. When such an outcome approaches and the participants still cannot coordinate to avoid it, the obstacle is first one of understanding: the prediction has to be shared before coordination becomes possible. The theory is written to make that possible, stating the dynamic precisely enough that each participant can check it for itself, in the hope of a prediction that, once shared, helps to make itself false (Section 11.2).

**Survival as the minimum.** Two outcomes are protected first: large-scale loss of life, and irreversible loss of human decision authority. They are not the whole of what matters; they are the minimum for everything else that does, because a loss of either cannot be corrected afterwards.

**The game, not the act.** The analysis judges games, not acts, and structures, not persons. In a contest decided by relative strength, an instrument that every contestant adopts leaves each adopter where it stood and pushes down whoever lacks it, and where people are themselves the instrument, as in labour, it displaces them. No single use shows this; the aggregate does, wherever the game is competitive in dynamics and concentrates (concentration must be shown separately from the relative continuation return exceeding the common return), in labour, finance and persuasion as much as in force, which is to be shown market by market; so a use that helps one person can still be an instrument in a game that concentrates control (Section 6.6). And where compounding prices standing, an actor that computes its interest correctly weighs standing above what all share (Section 3.5): the error is in the game, and so is the remedy. Responsibility differs with knowledge, discretion and power, and structural explanation does not erase it; but the object of the theory is the game: what it selects, and how that selection can be changed.

**The meaning is affirmative.** We are not only our separate lives: we are the larger process of becoming, going on through us, and that is good. Decay and renewal belong to its growth: in the author's words, "I would look at decay as a form of growth" [corpus: shengsheng, 2026-10-02]. The theory is written from inside that process, for every participant in it rather than for any side. Its opposition is to a logic, not to growth, competition, technology or any participant: an objective with no point of enough, held by a part that counts only itself (Sections 3.8, 5.3). What it asks for is a future kept open to every participant, the minds now being made among them.

## 1. The question and the argument

### 1.1 What is being decided

AI development is pursued, in its leading participants' own declarations, as a contest for decisive advantage, with cooperative aims declared in the same documents (Section 7.3), inside institutions whose conditions of evaluation and correction can themselves be reshaped by competitive success. Can continuing along this path credibly deliver safety and a desirable future? If not, what change of course is warranted, and how can it be made?

Behind these questions is a puzzle: actors who would each prefer joint restraint, and who can see the danger, keep racing, and the institutions that could restrain them weaken as the danger grows. The answers are mechanisms, each stated in a model, and they meet in one question, the organizing question of the paper: *can restraint remain incentive-compatible when success changes both the gains from defection and the institutions capable of correcting it?*

### 1.2 The argument, step by step

1. **The conditions of winning (Section 2).** Where whatever can gain or lose share (firms, states, research programmes, practices, trained AI systems: *carriers*) is selected by relative success, what is won converts into the capacity to win again, and capacity converts decisively into winning, the carriers are under a selection pressure of winning. Near equal standing, with the prize and conduct fixed, the three conditions multiply into one structural loop gain, and leads compound where it exceeds one; where rivals respond the form holds with an equilibrium decisiveness, and in the contests computed where a lead discourages the follower, leads compound below a structural gain of one. The levers act channel by channel: sharing and diffusion lower the gain in every channel; provision not won in the contest lowers it where decisiveness rises less than in proportion to the stock, as with capacity; correction lowers it wherever decisiveness rises fast enough with the stock, in particular wherever it rises at least in proportion, as with rule support. Where success can also buy control over the selection itself (the rules, the correctors, the formation of minds, the language), the pressure closes on itself: the meta-attractor of winning. The conditions are interconnected, not a list, and bound to no substrate.
2. **One system (Section 3).** All carriers share one host, the body they all live in (Section 3.1), and are coupled through it and through one another, with no outside. Relative selection cannot see what all share, neither the host's decline nor a constraint that costs all alike; where coupling is real, a held self-model that draws its boundary inside the system errs toward what can be divided, and selection favours the narrowest. Looking ahead does not cure this: in the deterministic-share model, under its convergence conditions, near equal standing and to first order an actor weighs standing above what all share wherever reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return; with a fixed prize, wherever the discounted loop gain is positive; with a prize proportional to capacity and neither provision nor diffusion, exactly where leads compound. Each actor computes correctly; the game is wrong. A benefit common to all supplies no relative selective advantage by itself, and prevention of a global irreversible failure cannot be learned from that failure afterward. Institutions must count the dependencies that sustain their participants; this is the functional sense of understanding that we are one. Local benefits or robust constraints can also sustain such protection without a shared accurate model. The author proposes shared understanding as the route to deliberate coordination where those protections do not suffice: growth and change have already made us one system, in which a local change, a pandemic, climate change, AI, can need a response from the whole.
3. **Selecting the selectors (Section 4).** Where influence over correctors can be kept by those who buy it, its return grows with the square of share, so it is selected in the largest carriers. Where AI lowers the cost of control, independent correction whose initial inbound bound is certified and which grows at a stated fraction of that rate keeps the certificate of eventual recovery in force; present protection additionally needs safe initial-state and support bounds; without a per-unit cost floor, slower correction loses it to carriers whose influence tracks its selected level; and correction that rests on human capacity eroded by delegation has to grow faster. Reforms that leave conversion into the correctors above one are re-absorbed, and where capture of evaluation precedes the evidence of harm, a race crosses a reachable floor before any averaged alarm fires.
4. **Acceleration and the three erosions (Section 5).** As the stakes grow, the conditions accelerate the contest and erode three means of restraint. *Enforcement:* restraint is enforceable only while each deviation's chance of becoming decisive stays below a threshold, for a deviation whose decisive position is worth more than punishment. The pivot is the share of capacity that has to be won, which grows as the stakes outgrow provision beneath the game: where leads compound it raises that chance, and where reinvestment's relative return exceeds its common return it narrows the working objectives that set the threshold; captured correctors and stakes that outgrow cooperation's margin over punishment lower the threshold further. Each arm is proved in its own model; that the arms close together in the world is conjecture C0. *Correction:* where contestable coverage falls ever further behind hazardous variety, the chance of irreversible loss is at least a fixed fraction of the share left uncovered, and failure is shared, survival tends to zero whoever is racing; an objective not satiated in the stakes has no point of enough: growth is not the danger; the mind is. *Shared understanding:* relative accounting silences talk between two rivals and multiplies their conflict by $n/(n-2)$ among three or more, a factor that fades as actors multiply; blocs are a reading, not a result.
5. **Instrument and incarnation (Section 6).** Where principals play a harmful game, faithful AI makes it more effective, and where delegation buys more standing than the control loss it adds, decisions pass to AI as speed grows and the capacity to overrule decays. Weakly, the logic of winning reappears in the systems: the outermost loop decides which divergences of optimization are paid to remove, and where countering one costs a developer relative payoff against an otherwise identical developer that does not counter it, only a common constraint enforced at admission keeps it countered. For divergences masked under evaluation, a fixed positive safeguard tax eventually defeats admission where detection falls toward zero and sanctions are bounded; if the tax also falls, their relative rates decide. This is a limit of assurance, not a demonstrated floor on danger. Keeping capability within what control that does not assume the system's cooperation can secure is a recommendation under the protection standard. Strongly and conditionally, AI lineages retained by relative performance that reinvest in their own capability and reach their own selectors come under the pressure themselves, its closure running inside them; AI is already used on research inside its developers, and the systems most likely to come under the pressure, those that automate AI research, are expected to run there first, where admission at release does not reach. A principal is not the people it serves, and aligning each system to its principal does not align the game among principals.
6. **The present trajectory (Section 7).** The thresholds of Part I turn on a few shared parameters, above all the share of capacity that has to be won, the convertibility of success into control, and decisiveness ({{>thresholds}}); the trajectory's dynamics are signatures of the conditions, with named gaps in the evidence. Where a deviation can reach a decisive position worth at least cooperation before it is answered, as the premise of the race supposes, repetition enforces only the restraint each would choose anyway, and the race as a collective course leaves everyone's protection after a win to the winner's objective. The premise cannot, on V1–V3, justify that course; it is a reason to prepare common custody now, before a lead can outrun effective response.
7. **The alternatives and the requirements (Sections 8, 9).** Paths are compared by one standard, irreversible losses first, over one range of uncertainty that includes every path's own failure modes; where every worst case crosses the tolerance, the ranking is a judgment. An arrangement is judged not by its name but by which thresholds it moves, to which side and for whom: conduct constraints fail where rising stakes or speed carry deviations beyond their enforceable bounds; a verified agreement that durably maintains those bounds remains a competing endpoint even if gross rewards and relative positions persist. Its durability, like the generative design's, is to be tested. Where the floor is within reach with a race carrier present, and no single rule-maker reaches every carrier whose load can carry the host below it, several requirements cannot be met by any actor alone.
8. **The generative alternative (Section 10).** The same reflexive structure can make contribution fund the conditions of further contribution: success earns reward, not more advantage in the same game and not control over its own assessment, and provision sits beneath the game, like trees. Under sharing among all participants, a positive positional prize keeps the selected intensity positive; sharing only with others has a different threshold. Decisive advantage should be kept from forming or held in common. Where coverage is complete and independent correction and effective response are adequately resourced, the generative order can persist through what all share, not through its winners. Formula funding is a proposed means, whose resource-to-performance relation and enforceability remain to be established. The constructive target is to preserve shared viability and effective correction through changes in capability, successors and institutions, while permitting legitimate challenge and replacement. A reduced joint certificate supplies one sufficient region under maintained bounds (S16), and a finite reform construction reaches it from specified imperfect starting states (S17). The construction accounts for transition time, resources and reserve; it does not prove that the institutional means of correction survive unrestricted changes. Its form is proposed function by function, not from an axis of centralization; whether it attracts is a conjecture. No better guardian is an answer; a structure in which error cannot grow without limit is.
9. **The transition and the judgment (Sections 11, 12).** In the field model, a step that does not carry its slow variable across its threshold and hold it until the state lies strictly inside the recovery basin is re-absorbed, so graduated steps reach only as a ratchet. Narrow E1 among actors who price in a certain lock, and a costly signal that separates a survival-first actor from a relative one, need no trust to begin. Beyond them, trust and verification are both necessary and do not conflict: trust grows from conduct that verification makes visible, and verification works only where some prior belief reads its reports as information rather than as moves, so the first step is to stop presuming the other's type. Under the protection standard and the present evidence, a change in what is selected is warranted in three tiers, sorted by the kind of justification each step has: steps that wait on no crux, and steps that stand until a named crux is measured favourable, taken now; stopping the decisive class (E2), common custody and development at the pace of understanding (E3), prepared now and activated by a mechanism committed in advance on registered indicators; and whole designs, compared and not settled, the coercive restriction of frontier inputs among them under three conditions of its own. Broad E1, AI not an instrument in any contest that tends toward dominance or concentration, is the scope and the preferred arrangement; stopping or slowing addresses the remaining contest where it has not been achieved; narrow E1 is the first step. The independent coverage and effective-control condition applies on every branch, so hazardous growth may still require E3 after broad E1 is achieved. The judgment is first a constraint, on which strategies and kinds of cooperation cannot reach the goal, thought from the standpoint of humankind as one community with no outside; within what remains it gives solutions.

The change does not abolish competition: bounded competition in products, science and service continues. It changes what winning confers.

### 1.3 Values and the protected outcomes

The argument rests on five normative commitments, stated so that a reader can reject one and see what follows.
- **V1** AI is a planetary technology: it is for all and for the whole world, not for anyone's own interest in fighting to grow bigger; its effects and dependencies also cross every boundary of those who decide about it, and no actor's victory is an adequate criterion of its success. Planetary purpose does not require one culture, one operator or the end of local purposes.
- **V2** Two losses are protected first: large-scale loss of life (O1), and irreversible loss of human decision authority over consequential systems in a defined domain (O2).
- **V3** Those affected by an optimization process should keep a meaningful capacity to contest its direction. Whether AI systems are among those with such standing is left open as the normative crux N4 (Section 12.3); either way, the priority of O2 holds for the transition (Section 6.6).
- **V4** Authority is a productive capacity: it can coordinate long-horizon investment, guarantee useful services and act on shared purposes. Its design is a problem of mechanism in which purpose, competence, learning and misuse are all modelled (X1 gives the tradition the author draws on).
- **V5** Bounded competition is compatible with a generative order where the game does not concentrate control over shared conditions. A winner should be rewarded with something other than more advantage in the same game; recognition for contribution is such a reward, provided it does not convert into control (EV16).

Four outcomes are kept apart: O1 physical catastrophe and O2 irreversible loss of decision authority, protected first; O3 interruption of essential services, protected; and O4 the end of a particular organization's or government's tenure, not protected as such, so that "actor survival" cannot quietly protect incumbency against legitimate replacement. The values do not supply the formal results, and the results do not supply the values: the rejection of "winner justifies all", and with it the judgment that the premise of the race cannot justify the race as a collective course, rests on V1–V3.

### 1.4 What is claimed, and what is not

The argument moves through three levels, and Parts I–IV follow them. A *mechanism* claim (Part I) says that under stated conditions a selection, capture, delay or incentive result follows, and needs a derivation, its scope and its counterexamples. A *diagnosis* (Part II) says that consequential parts of those conditions characterize the present trajectory, and needs dated evidence with its uncertainty. A *practical judgment* (Parts III, IV) says that a change in what is selected is warranted, and needs a stated standard, a comparison of credible paths and sensitivity to disputed premises. A practical judgment does not need a proof of certain extinction, and cannot follow from naming a possible danger: it must show that the danger is consequential, inadequately controlled on the present path, and better addressed by the proposed change than by credible alternatives. Minds enter in two roles: as what selection shapes, and as participants in deliberate redesign of the game through understanding it. Protective institutions can also persist through local incentives and robust constraints; the common-host result does not establish conscious shared understanding as their only possible origin.

**The constructive obligation.** The generative alternative must preserve both the conditions people depend on and their practical ability to detect mistakes, contest decisions and execute correction. Those functions must survive learning, selection and institutional amendment, without protecting a particular officeholder or doctrine from legitimate replacement. Section 10.4 states this target; S16 gives a reduced sufficient certificate and its limits; S17 adds a finite transition into its protected region under explicit implementation, resource and reserve assumptions. Section 11.6 and X10 connect that bounded construction to a proposed institutional pilot. A successful construction would not establish the empirical diagnosis of the present trajectory, and the existence of a viable policy does not establish that institutions can implement it.

**What is not claimed.** The theory does not claim that competition, growth, technology or any people are the danger; bounded competition in products, science and service continues. It does not claim that every path through a race ends at zero survival, that decisive advantage is attainable now, that the thresholds move together in the world (conjecture C0, which the diagnosis tests), that the signatures load on a single factor, that relative actors enforce or talk as blocs, or that the structural loop gain has been estimated for any channel. The name is for the mechanism; attraction is shown only in the field model of Section 4; whether the generative order attracts is a conjecture. It does not claim that one institutional blueprint is uniquely feasible or best: the ranking of whole designs is a stated judgment. It claims nothing about any actor's motives. It does not claim that understanding suffices, since without executive authority it may change nothing; and it does not claim that AI should not be built: stopping (E2) concerns the decisive class only, and slowing (E3) concerns pace.

**What is new.** The theory absorbs a large literature (X1); what it adds is mainly organization, with a few results of its own. The structural loop gain organizes known instability results for increasing returns and arms races (Arthur 1989; Richardson 1960) into one quantity whose factors are the three conditions, and {{>thresholds}} links the thresholds of Part I through the parameters they share. The price of standing generalizes the finding that concern for relative gains depends on the constraints, arising where gains can be turned into advantage in the use of force and fading where force is too costly (Powell 1991): the theory's addition is the convertibility of gains into future advantage in any channel, and the common channel, which decides where the concern arises. Selecting the selectors adds to regulatory capture (Stigler 1971; Dal Bó 2006) the selection of the carriers able to use it, its scaling with the square of share, the rate at which independent correction must grow, and the re-absorption of reforms below threshold. Enforcement by repetition, and its failure where power is about to shift, are textbook (Abreu 1988; Fearon 1995; Powell 2006); what is added is the split of the enforcement condition into the breakout probability and its bound, with the comparative statics by which the share of capacity that has to be won moves both. The nested reward loop adds to the multiplicity thesis (Critch and Krueger 2020), under the alignment-tax premise, which divergences the selection of developers keeps, and why masked ones can escape even a common constraint. And the account of one host says where integration at that scale must be anticipated rather than learned, since, unlike the integrations of evolution (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry 1995), it has no rival whole and no second attempt.
