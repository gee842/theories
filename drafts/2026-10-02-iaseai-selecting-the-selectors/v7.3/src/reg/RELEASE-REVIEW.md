# V7.3 release assessment

*5 October 2026. Accepted as V7.3 of the conditional working paper after development, three fresh bounded reviews and completed verification. This assessment is not empirical validation or independent human certification.*

## Why this warrants a new edition

V7.2 supplied a protected region under maintained capture, liability and host conditions. V7.3 adds an explicit finite path into that region from some imperfect starting states, rather than assuming the institution starts protected. The new construction joins installation delay, temporary correction, structural change, sufficient reserve and full resource accounting with ordinary continuation. It is an elementary comparison proof with a nonempty example, not a new general control method.

The practical addition begins with the powers of an existing public-service procurement programme. It specifies the decisions and costs needed to acquire usable evidence, practice a fallback, execute a justified correction, hear a valid appeal and transfer the functions to successors. Test N now offers a bounded comparison in which failed adoption, extra resources alone, lost service and failed appeals can defeat the proposed advantage. The pilot is proposed and unrun.

Together these additions move the work from a protected destination toward a conditional transition and an operational research programme. That is a defensible version advance. It does not establish the whole causal story, a real institution's durability, or the superiority of the larger institutional compact.

## What the mathematical result establishes

[SUPPLEMENT S17](SUPPLEMENT.md#s17-a-funded-transition-into-the-protected-region) constructs a specified policy: build correction during a finite installation period, reduce incoming conversion, hold temporary support for a computed duration, then return to ordinary correction. The sufficient entry deadline and resource requirement are explicit. Current capture, accumulated influence and harmful load may initially exceed S16's bounds.

The illustrative case begins with capture 0.9 against a target bound of 0.3, support 1.8 against the S16 ceiling of 1, and load 0.765 against 0.7. The scheduled release is 1.643742 and the conservative entry deadline 2.606503. The analytic worst-load host bound is 0.321999, above the floor of 0.3. The full transition costs 10.630740 resource units and ordinary operation 0.6 per time unit. These dimensionless numbers are not estimates for real institutions. Independent integration gives an actual model minimum near 0.665465.

The tests preserve limits as well as successes. A long installation crosses the floor before eventual recovery. A short pulse without structural repair returns to capture. Conversely, a slower capture response misses the conservative reserve certificate while remaining safe in the actual model; removal of maintenance after sufficiently deep suppression also need not cause recapture in the self-coupled witness. The theorem's bounds are sufficient, not necessary, and no result labels every failed certificate an impossible transition.

## What remains open

The construction assumes authority and access for its intervention, an enforceable liability relation, a continuing conversion bound and ordinary funding under a stated cost relation. It does not explain how a political coalition acquires or keeps these conditions through unrestricted evolution. It covers a fixed two-load model and one correction domain, with no mutation or entry and a forgiving host. It does not itself prove human agency, legitimate appeal or complete technical hazard coverage.

The procurement illustration and proposed simulation investigate operational counterparts; they are not a calibration of the equations. An actual institution's adoption, dependence, response speed, costs and durability still need evidence. The full correctable kernel, the present trajectory's joint causal diagnosis and the residual irreversible-risk premise remain unestablished. No public distribution, field experiment or external deployment was performed.
