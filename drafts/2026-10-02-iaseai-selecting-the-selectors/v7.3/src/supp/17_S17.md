## S17. A funded transition into the protected region

S16 certifies continuation from one protected set. This section constructs a finite path into that set from imperfect correction, while preserving the host's floor. Reaching a target and staying there while avoiding an unsafe set is a standard control problem (Meng et al. 2021); the construction here uses direct comparison equations. Its additional content is an explicit installation delay, a temporary correction pulse, a maintained reduction in incoming influence after the pulse, and separate finite and recurring resource requirements. It does not establish political adoption or the indefinite survival of reform authority.

### Model and implementation premises

Use one domain of {{eq:field}}, two fixed loads $0$ and $e>0$ in {{eq:payoff}}, equal background payoffs and constant-speed replicator selection. Write $x$ for the captured rule share, $z$ for its support and $y$ for the high-load carrier's share. Maintain the reduced liability link of S16:
$$\dot x=\zeta x(1-x)(az-c),\qquad
\dot z=\varepsilon[b(t)x-d(t)z],\qquad
\dot y=se\,[\nu-\tau(1-x)]y(1-y),\qquad
\dot V=V[\rho(1-V)-h_Vey].$$
All coefficients other than $b,d$ are fixed and positive. There is no entry, mutation, new load trait or other payoff advantage. The nominal liability $\tau$ is enforceable in the stated reduced form. The initial shares satisfy $0<x_0,y_0<1$, support $z_0\ge0$, and $0<V_{\min}<V_0\le1$. Put $E_*=\rho(1-V_{\min})/h_V$ and assume $0<E_*<e$, so full race load would not preserve the floor.

An authorized reform can install a persistent cut of incoming conversion from $b_0\ge0$ to $b_1\in[0,b_0]$ in time $L>0$. During installation it builds correction linearly from $d_0>0$ to a booster $d_B\ge\max(d_0,d_\infty)$; after installation it holds the booster until a computed release time and then deactivates it to ordinary correction $d_\infty>0$. The admissible controls permit this downward deactivation and the conversion change at installation: coefficients are piecewise continuous and states remain continuous. A maximum build rate $r_d>0$ applies to upward construction, not to deactivation; peak capacity is $\bar d>0$. Installation authority and access last for the prescribed programme. The cut $b_1$, nominal liability and ordinary correction remain executable afterwards.

Maintaining an effective correction rate $d$ costs $k_dd$ per unit time, with $k_d>0$. A separate rate $m\ge0$ pays the programme's other ongoing implementation costs, including maintenance of its rule and separation arrangements; installation costs $C_I\ge0$ upfront. This specified production-and-cost relation is a model premise, not a dollar-to-correction estimate for existing institutions. A finite budget $B\ge0$, available to spend from the start, pays all operations until entry, including baseline operations; after entry a recurring revenue flow $R\ge0$ is available. These are resources under legitimate, executable authority, not promises from parties the arrangement may need to constrain. Their political creation and survival remain outside this model.

### Explicit construction

Choose $\bar x\in(0,1)$ and $z_\dagger$ such that
$$\Delta=\tau(1-\bar x)-\nu>0,\qquad
\frac{b_1}{d_\infty}<z_\dagger<\frac ca.$$
The first is a liability margin; the second makes support below $z_\dagger$ sustainable even after the booster ends. For $\ell(q)=\log[q/(1-q)]$, define
$$Z_0(t)=\frac{b_0}{d_0}+\left(z_0-\frac{b_0}{d_0}\right)e^{-\varepsilon d_0t},\qquad
I_0=\frac{b_0L}{d_0}+\frac{z_0-b_0/d_0}{\varepsilon d_0}(1-e^{-\varepsilon d_0L}).$$
Write $Z_L=Z_0(L)$ and $z_B=b_1/d_B$. The time to drive the support bound below $z_\dagger$ is
$$T_z=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon d_B}\log\frac{Z_L-z_B}{z_\dagger-z_B},&Z_L>z_\dagger,\\
0,&Z_L\le z_\dagger.
\end{cases}$$
The integral of that bound during this phase is
$$I_B=z_BT_z+\frac{Z_L-z_B}{\varepsilon d_B}(1-e^{-\varepsilon d_BT_z}).$$
Now set
$$T_x=\frac{\left[\ell(x_0)+\zeta\{a(I_0+I_B)-c(L+T_z)\}-\ell(\bar x)\right]_+}{\zeta(c-az_\dagger)},\qquad T_F=L+T_z+T_x,$$
$$y_*=E_*/e,\qquad
T_y=\frac{[\ell(y_0)+se\nu T_F-\ell(y_*)]_+}{se\Delta},\qquad
T_*=T_F+T_y.$$
The actual policy is
$$b(t)=\begin{cases}b_0,&t<L,\\b_1,&t\ge L,\end{cases}\qquad
d(t)=\begin{cases}
d_0+(d_B-d_0)t/L,&0\le t<L,\\
d_B,&L\le t<T_F,\\
d_\infty,&t\ge T_F.
\end{cases}$$
Thus $T_F$ is the scheduled end of temporary correction, and $T_*$ a conservative deadline for entering the joint protected region. Neither is inferred from a single favourable reading of capture.

For $A=\rho-h_Ve$, the host exposed to maximum load throughout has trajectory
$$\underline V(t)=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\left[\frac{e^{-At}}{V_0}+\frac{\rho}{A}(1-e^{-At})\right]^{-1},&A\ne0,\\
\displaystyle\left[V_0^{-1}+\rho t\right]^{-1},&A=0.
\end{cases}$$
The full transition cost and recurring requirement are
$$C_*=C_I+k_d\left[\frac{d_0+d_B}{2}L+d_B(T_z+T_x)+d_\infty T_y\right]+mT_*,\qquad C_\infty=k_dd_\infty+m.$$

**{{!Proposition:reformpath}} (A finite funded reform path) [thm∣prem].** *Under the stated model and implementation premises, suppose*
$$d_B\le\bar d,\qquad \frac{d_B-d_0}{L}\le r_d,\qquad
B\ge C_*,\qquad R\ge C_\infty,\qquad
\underline V(T_*)\ge V_{\min}.$$
*Then the constructed policy is feasible within the stated capacity, build-rate and resource constraints. The host stays above its floor during the whole transition. At $T_F$, $x\le\bar x$ and $z\le z_\dagger$; these bounds persist after the booster is withdrawn. At $T_*$, $ey\le E_*$. From that time the trajectory lies in the protected region of {{jointcorrection}} and remains there under ordinary correction $d_\infty$, with $x$ and $ey$ nonincreasing. The starting state need not be in that region.*

*Proof.* The equations preserve $0<x,y<1$, $z\ge0$ and $0<V\le1$ at every finite time. During installation $d(t)\ge d_0$ and $x\le1$, so the comparison equation $\dot Z=\varepsilon(b_0-d_0Z)$ gives $z(t)\le Z_0(t)$ and $\int_0^Lz(t)\,dt\le I_0$. After installation, while the booster operates, $\dot z\le\varepsilon(b_1-d_Bz)$. Thus
$$z(L+t)\le z_B+(Z_L-z_B)e^{-\varepsilon d_Bt},$$
whose integral to $T_z$ is $I_B$ and whose value is at most $z_\dagger$ from $T_z$ onwards. The exact capture logit identity $d\ell(x)/dt=\zeta(az-c)$ therefore bounds its value at $L+T_z$ by the numerator's expression before subtracting $\ell(\bar x)$. Thereafter it decreases at least at rate $\zeta(c-az_\dagger)>0$. The definition of $T_x$ gives $x(T_F)\le\bar x$.

On withdrawal, the boundary $z=z_\dagger$ has derivative at most $\varepsilon(b_1-d_\infty z_\dagger)<0$, so $z\le z_\dagger$ and $\dot x\le0$ continue without the booster. In particular, $\tau(1-x)\ge\nu+\Delta$. Before $T_F$ the load logit grows no faster than $se\nu$, since effective liability is nonnegative; afterwards it decreases at least at rate $se\Delta$. This proves $y(T_*)\le y_*$.

At every date $ey\le e$, so scalar comparison gives $V(t)\ge\underline V(t)$. Because $E_*<e$ and $V_0>V_{\min}$, the maximum-load trajectory decreases while above the floor. Consequently its terminal bound at $T_*$ protects the entire transition. After $T_*$, support and capture are already bounded, liability has its positive margin, and load is compatible with the host boundary. Also $ab_1/(cd_\infty)<1$, so {{jointcorrection}} supplies invariant continuation. Integrating the prescribed correction schedule gives exactly the operating term of $C_*$; the capital, ongoing rule cost and later recurring requirement are separately included. The peak and upward build-rate inequalities make the schedule admissible under the stated constraints. $\square$

### A nonempty case and its limits

Block PT checks an illustrative, dimensionless case with $a=c=\nu=\rho=h_V=k_d=1$, $\varepsilon=6$, $\zeta=10$, $s=6$, $e=0.9$, $\tau=4$, $d_0=0.6$, $d_B=5$, $d_\infty=0.5$, $b_0=1.2$, $b_1=0.2$, $L=0.15$, $\bar x=0.3$ and $z_\dagger=0.7$. Initially $(x,z,y,V)=(0.9,1.8,0.85,0.95)$ and $V_{\min}=0.3$: the field gain is two, liability $0.4<\nu$, support exceeds $c/a$, and load $0.765>E_*=0.7$. None of those unfavourable starting conditions is replaced by an assumption of present protection.

The certificate releases the booster at $T_F=1.643742$ and enters the region by $T_*=2.606503$, with a conservative host lower bound of $0.321999$. With $C_I=2$ and $m=0.1$, full transition cost is $10.630740$ and recurring cost $0.6$: a finite budget of 11 and recurring revenue of 0.7 suffice, with peak capacity 5 and an upward build rate at least $29.333334$. Ordinary maintenance retains a field gain of $0.4$. Joint RK4 and a separate exponential-midpoint calculation agree that the actual model host stays above about $0.66546$; the proof intentionally uses the much weaker maximum-load bound. None of these numbers estimates a real institution's costs or speed.

The boundary cases are part of the result's meaning. With $\zeta=8$ the conservative host bound falls to $0.296525$, below the floor, but the actual model path stays viable: failure of this sufficient certificate is not impossibility. A short pulse ending at installation while the original conversion channel remains returns the system to capture and crosses the host floor; this combined omission does not establish either sufficient condition as separately necessary. Extending installation to $L=5$, with the full computed pulse and durable cut eventually performed, lowers the actual host to about $0.294035$: the same destination can arrive too late. A destination sustainable after reform therefore does not certify its transition. Conversely, losing a sufficient maintenance certificate does not by itself prove recapture: in a self-coupled domain a sufficiently suppressed source may decay before accumulating damaging support. A recurring funding failure makes the stated policy unavailable; actual failure needs its own dynamics.

This closes one reduced transition join, with explicit costs and release, rather than the full institutional one. It does not show who gains the authority to perform the cut, whether its funding or liability survives strategic institutional change, or how real budgets produce the assumed correction rate. It protects the modeled host and restores a bounded rule domain; it does not by itself establish human agency, legitimate participation, multi-domain hazard coverage or safe post-construction fallback. Those remain outcomes and mechanisms to test in a reform pilot. The construction's value is that installation, transient protection, affordability and ordinary continuation are assessed together, and a permanent booster is not the explanation of success.
