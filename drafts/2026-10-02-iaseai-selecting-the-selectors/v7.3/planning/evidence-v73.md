## Test N: one operational transition

TESTS §6.6 supplies a worked preregistration template for the agency-service example in DISCUSSION X10: synthetic routine-request routing and drafting with staff retaining final authority. The proposed feasibility sample is 18 institutional teams, six matched triplets and 12 decision rounds. It is not an external preregistration, empirical dataset, adequately powered confirmatory study or estimate of real transition time. Public registration, recruitment, calibration and execution remain work to do.

The comparison separates current continuation, an equal-resource offer for additional auditing/training, and an equal-resource offer that changes access and executable authority while temporarily supporting migration and continuity. Required adoption decisions, failed offers, expenditure, construction delay and bargaining are observed. Participants know the stage schedule and possible successor powers. A temporary reserve ends before the succession phase; ordinary budget, actual access and practiced fallback then carry the arrangement.

Primary outcomes join timely correction to service continuity and valid appeal. The proposed two-round correction deadline, next-round appeal restoration and 90% service-completion floor are explicit exercise settings, not public-service norms or safety thresholds for frontier AI. Independent research adjudication is an experimental boundary; adoption and operational governance are not held successful by assumption. Eligibility and the workload denominator are fixed at assignment; withdrawn, refused or narrowed-away work remains visible as service loss, with matched-scope analysis secondary. The protocol also states illustrative conflicting actor payoffs, assent and transfer powers, supplier resistance costs and participant information. These are experimentally assigned incentives, not estimates of real officials. Inference is at the institution-team level. The pilot estimates feasibility and variance; a separate, prospectively registered confirmatory sample must establish any claimed improvement margin.

A rejected reform is an adoption failure in that scenario. Improved detection without action, service-floor breach, defeated valid appeal, or loss of the effect after temporary support ends blocks the corresponding success claim. A successful baseline remains a successful baseline. The study therefore tests movement from a current bottleneck toward better executable correction, not a preselected conclusion that all existing institutions fail.
