# Direction frontier: formation of cooperation and the limits of an inclusive trend

Research note, 2026-10-05. Targeted review of five original scientific studies; an interpretive bridge to the unified theory, not a systematic review or a new empirical result.

## Assessment

The defensible claim is conditional: some environments let heritable organization, costly cooperation, and institutions that sustain cooperation form and spread. The evidence below does **not** establish a general tendency toward wisdom, universal inclusion, or progressively wider moral concern. “More inclusive” must distinguish a larger coordinated unit from more equal standing for everyone affected by it. None of these studies tests whether protection naturally extends to an outsider whose loss does not diminish the successful coalition's prospects.

For the theory, track at least four separate properties: continued regenerative capacity; accuracy and usefulness of information; effective opportunities to challenge and repair decisions; and the distribution of standing and protection among affected parties. Their conjunction is a proposed standard of wisdom, not an empirical identity.

## Five primary sources

### 1. A new cooperative unit can form under explicit selection

William C. Ratcliff, R. Ford Denison, Mark Borrello, Michael Travisano (2012), “Experimental evolution of multicellularity,” *PNAS* 109:1595–1600. DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1115323109](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1115323109). [Full primary paper and supplement](https://www.icts.res.in/sites/default/files/Ratcliff@Travisano_2012.pdf).

**Finding and formation mechanism.** Gravity-based selection produced snowflake yeast clusters in all ten initial populations after 60 transfers. Mother–daughter adhesion generated related clusters with reproductive life histories; increased apoptosis later accompanied smaller propagules. Heritable changes, relatedness, and selection on collective performance provided a route toward a new reproducing unit.

**Limit.** Settling selection and laboratory resources were supplied; cluster advantage depended on that regime. This demonstrates neither universal convergence nor moral improvement. Collective integration can involve constituent sacrifice. Increased organization therefore does not itself establish better protection for every member or outsider, or explain how a favorable selection regime arises naturally.

### 2. People can select and finance an institution that corrects second-order free riding

Christian Hilbe, Arne Traulsen, Torsten Röhl, Manfred Milinski (2014), “Democratic decisions establish stable authorities that overcome the paradox of second-order punishment,” *PNAS* 111:752–756. DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1315273111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1315273111). [Author-hosted primary paper](https://christian-hilbe.github.io/files/2014_hilbe.pdf).

**Finding and formation mechanism.** Among 125 students in 25 groups, 12 of 15 groups using binding majority choice selected punishment of both free riding and failure to fund enforcement. Individual migration initially favored the alternative in eight of ten groups, although several changed later. Reciprocal commitment helped finance correction itself.

**Limit.** The institution menu, enforcement, and constitution were supplied. Decision method and commitment duration changed together, preventing attribution to democracy alone. Minority rights and outsider harms were unmeasured. The study neither explains institutional invention nor establishes that only voting can sustain authority; later movement under migration also matters.

### 3. Voluntary sorting can favor sanctioning institutions, with replicated boundaries

Sergio Lo Iacono, Wojtek Przepiorka, Vincent Buskens, Rense Corten, Marcel van Assen, Arnout van de Rijt (2023), “The competitive advantage of sanctioning institutions revisited: A multilab replication,” *PNAS Nexus* 2:pgad091. DOI: [10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad091](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad091). [Primary research record and abstract](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/the-competitive-advantage-of-sanctioning-institutions-revisited-a/); [published paper](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/173302646/pgad091.pdf).

**Finding and formation mechanism.** A replication with 1,008 participants in seven laboratories allowed movement between groups with and without peer sanctions. Five laboratories met every preregistered replication criterion: sanctioning groups attracted a majority and achieved higher cooperation and profits. Two gave weaker but favorable results. Sorting and experienced returns can support adoption.

**Limit.** The robustness claim is bounded to the European context. Choosing supplied institutions does not demonstrate enforcement's invention, indefinite persistence, or inclusive protection. This and source 2 compare different institutional alternatives and commitment arrangements; they do not establish unconditional superiority of either migration or binding voting.

### 4. The same correction capacity can be used against cooperators

Benedikt Herrmann, Christian Thöni, Simon Gächter (2008), “Antisocial Punishment Across Societies,” *Science* 319:1362–1367. DOI: [10.1126/science.1153808](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1153808). [Primary paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kraemer/antisocial-punishment.pdf).

**Finding and countermechanism.** Among 1,120 participants in sixteen student pools, punishment sometimes targeted equally or more cooperative contributors. In some pools, this offset punishment's cooperative gains. Effective sanctions and contribution information can therefore support suppression as well as correction. Associations with civic norms and rule-of-law measures were correlational.

**Limit.** University samples were not nationally representative. The experiment identifies neither a sufficient cultural cause nor universal harm from punishment. For the theory, feedback reaching someone able to act is insufficient: the interpretation of error, interests guiding sanctions, and opportunities to contest misuse matter. More enforcement capacity need not mean more wisdom.

### 5. Strategic responsiveness can sustain exploitative cooperation

William H. Press, Freeman J. Dyson (2012), “Iterated Prisoner’s Dilemma contains strategies that dominate any evolutionary opponent,” *PNAS* 109:10409–10413. DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1206569109](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1206569109). [Primary paper](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~dturaev/project2-literature.pdf).

**Formal result and countermechanism.** Certain memory-one strategies in the long-run iterated prisoner's dilemma impose linear payoff relations. Feasible extortionate strategies secure unequal gains above mutual defection: greater cooperation can raise both players' payoffs while disproportionately benefiting the extorter. Responsiveness and cooperation can thus coexist with asymmetric extraction.

**Limit.** The payoff and interaction assumptions are essential. This mathematical result does not establish that intelligence generally produces exploitation or that extortion is evolutionarily stable across populations. Cognition, morality, and institutions were not experimentally measured. It is a countermodel to equating strategic improvement and greater cooperation with wiser, fairer relations.

## What these results permit the synthesis to claim

The following is an **inference across the studies**, not a theorem attributed to them:

> More extensive cooperation can form when variation generates workable arrangements, consequences make their shared benefits legible, the arrangements can finance their own maintenance, and selection or collective choice retains them. Whether the resulting organization becomes more inclusive depends additionally on whose consequences enter those processes and who can challenge the resulting rules.

This formulation gives formation its own explanatory burden. A model that supplies accurate harm detection, perfect enforcement, or a morally preferred objective has demonstrated performance conditional on those provisions. It has not yet explained their origin, their maintenance against counterselection, or their extension to people initially excluded.

Three distinctions should remain explicit in the next theory extension:

| Distinction | Consequence for the direction claim |
|---|---|
| Enlarging the coordinated unit versus enlarging protected standing | A group may become more capable while its internal distribution or external conduct worsens. |
| Selecting among supplied rules versus generating a new rule | Endogenous adoption is real but weaker than endogenous institutional invention. |
| Benefit that returns to decision-makers versus harm borne only by outsiders | A survival-based advantage can explain repair of the former without explaining concern for the latter. |

The current synthesis can treat wider, accountable correction as a **conditional evolutionary possibility and explicit normative commitment**. These sources do not warrant upgrading it to an unconditional law of reality. Nor do their counterexamples refute every statistical tendency: a tendency would need a defined population of environments, direction measure, time scale, and comparison baseline.

## A discriminating next experiment

Test whether a population can **create and maintain financed protection for a harmed party with little initial leverage**, rather than rewarding agents for following an already supplied inclusive rule.

Allow finite-cost proposals for monitoring, hearing claims, compensation, enforcement, and revision. Vary separately: (1) whether outside harm later returns to the coalition; (2) whether affected parties gain voice or exit; (3) whether enforcement can be challenged or captured; and (4) whether institutions and their actual costs transmit between generations. Include conditions in which outsiders remain causally dispensable to the coalition. If all inclusive proposals are generated only by an experimenter-provided privileged policy, label that assistance rather than describing invention as fully endogenous.

Measure separately persistence, net renewal, distribution of regenerative capacity, information calibration, successful correction, inclusion of initially excluded parties, and the cost of sustaining each institution. A rise in group payoff is not sufficient evidence for a rise in wisdom.

**Candidate falsifiable conjecture:** reliable feedback from affected consequences, affordable and accountable enforcement, and transmissible institutional variation increase the probability of durable expansion of protected standing relative to controls. The stronger claim—that protected standing expands even without consequential dependence, inherited moral preference, or externally imposed reward—remains open and is the crucial discriminator for the proposed universal direction.
