# Replacement, establishment, and the overlap needed for a safe handoff

Research memo, 5 October 2026. AI-generated derivations for the integrated selection–renewal project. The model below is deliberately solvable. Its assumptions and numerical example are illustrative, not fitted biological, institutional, or AI data. Invasion, evolutionary rescue, maturation delays, and occupancy-time accounting have substantial prior art; no originality claim is made for their ingredients or their broad connection.

## The distinction this memo adds

A practice can be favored when rare, become widespread, and ultimately provide better renewal while still failing to replace its predecessor without destroying an indispensable function during the transition. Three different events therefore need separate definitions:

1. **Selective invasion:** the new practice increases from a small initial share under the specified selection or adoption mechanism.
2. **Demographic establishment:** a finite lineage escapes early stochastic loss. This meaning is common in evolutionary biology; it is not the same as fixation.
3. **Functional establishment:** enough of the new practice is actually operational to supply the required function, with a feasible transition that has respected the protected constraints.

The [existing foundation](../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-04-selection-and-generative-conditions.md) already separates favorable composition from absolute recovery. This memo identifies an additional mechanism: the predecessor is retired according to adoption, but the successor delivers its function only after maturation. The resulting capacity gap is generated by the replacement process itself; it is not merely an externally imposed repair deadline.

“Better” has two independent meanings here. A positive adoption advantage says what spreads; greater mature productivity says what renews more effectively. Neither implies the other. The example grants both advantages to the new practice and still produces transition failure.

## A selected replacement with delayed useful function

There is a fixed reference population of functional positions. Let \(x(t)\) be the fraction that has adopted a new practice. Under immediate retirement of predecessors, this is also its share of occupied positions. Later, when a predecessor and trainee overlap, \(x\) remains a fraction of the reference positions, not a share of every simultaneously active carrier.

Assume constant selective advantage \(s>0\):

\[
\dot x=sx(1-x),\qquad x(0)=\varepsilon\in(0,1),\qquad
x(t)=\frac{qe^{st}}{1+qe^{st}},\quad q=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}.
\tag{1}
\]

This can represent a specified pairwise adoption mechanism. It is not derived from the renewal flow below, and constant advantage is assumed while the support floor has not been breached. The new practice therefore has positive rare growth and tends toward complete adoption in the unconstrained dynamics.

The initial seed fraction \(\varepsilon\) is already functional. Every subsequent adoption requires a fixed duration \(L>0\) before becoming functional. No one fails or leaves training in this first model. The mature fraction is

\[
m(t)=
\begin{cases}
\varepsilon,&0\le t<L,\\
x(t-L),&t\ge L.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

Thus all comparisons of selection speed can start with the same seed and maturity state; they do not silently change an unobserved prehistory.

Let \(\kappa>0\) be the absolute renewal flow supplied by one full reference population of mature predecessors. Continuing operation consumes \(\kappa\) units of a support stock per unit time. Mature successors supply \(\eta\kappa\) per full reference population, where \(\eta>1\). Unmatured successors supply none of this particular indispensable function. Under immediate predecessor retirement, the support balance is

\[
\dot E=\kappa[(1-x)+\eta m]-\kappa
=\kappa(\eta m-x),\qquad E(0)=E_0.
\tag{3}
\]

The protected floor is \(E_{\min}\). Equations (1)–(3) apply until that floor is crossed. Crossing is assumed to irreversibly invalidate the transition, for example because the training or renewal process loses an indispensable input. The mathematical continuation below the floor is only a device for detecting that event. If temporary floor violations are recoverable, a different protected set and transition model are required.

The flow coefficients are net of ordinary operating inputs, with those inputs assumed feasible. Duplicate-operation cost will be added separately. This model assumes no upper storage cap through the transition minimum and no other failing support condition. Selection pressure, maturation reliability, demand, and intervention authority are not endogenized here.

## Exact result: positive invasion need not produce functional establishment

Write \(k=e^{sL}\). If \(\eta\varepsilon\ge1\), the mature seed alone can supply the entire demand and no support deficit occurs. Consider the nontrivial case \(\eta\varepsilon<1\).

For \(0\le t<L\), the net flow is \(\kappa(\eta\varepsilon-x(t))\). For \(t\ge L\), put \(w=qe^{st}\). Then

\[
\frac{\dot E}{\kappa}
=\frac{\eta w}{k+w}-\frac{w}{1+w}
=\frac{w[(\eta-k)+(\eta-1)w]}{(k+w)(1+w)}.
\tag{4}
\]

**Proposition 1 — onset and duration of a transition deficit.** A negative renewal-flow interval occurs if and only if

\[
\boxed{k>k_c:=\frac{\eta(1-\varepsilon)}{1-\eta\varepsilon}.}
\tag{5}
\]

Otherwise \(E\) is nondecreasing. When (5) holds, its derivative is first positive, then negative, then positive again. The negative interval begins at

\[
t_{\mathrm{down}}=\frac{\log k_c}{s}<L
\tag{6}
\]

and ends at

\[
w_*:=\frac{k-\eta}{\eta-1},\qquad
t_*:=\frac1s\log\frac{w_*}{q}>L.
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** Before \(L\), \(x\) increases, so a negative flow arises exactly when \(x(L)>\eta\varepsilon\); solving that inequality gives (5). After \(L\), the sign in (4) is determined by an affine increasing function of \(w\). Under (5), its unique zero occurs after \(L\), at (7). Without (5), it is nonnegative from \(L\) onward as well as before \(L\). Solving \(x(t)=\eta\varepsilon\) gives (6). ∎

For a rare seed, the threshold approaches \(sL>\log\eta\). The exact finite-seed threshold is (5). This is a comparison between the speed of replacement, the delay in useful function, and mature productivity. It is not a universal maximum speed of innovation.

The reserve change is explicit. Let \(F(t)=E(t)-E_0\). For \(0\le t<L\),

\[
F(t)=\kappa\left[\eta\varepsilon t-
\frac1s\log\frac{1+qe^{st}}{1+q}\right].
\tag{8}
\]

For \(t\ge L\),

\[
F(t)=\kappa\left[\eta\varepsilon L+
\frac\eta s\log\frac{1+qe^{s(t-L)}}{1+q}
-\frac1s\log\frac{1+qe^{st}}{1+q}\right].
\tag{9}
\]

**Proposition 2 — exact transition reserve.** Under (5), define

\[
R_{\mathrm{required}}=\max\{0,-F(t_*)\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Then the immediate-retirement transition respects \(E(t)\ge E_{\min}\) for all times if and only if

\[
\boxed{E_0-E_{\min}\ge R_{\mathrm{required}}.}
\tag{11}
\]

When (5) fails, the required reserve is zero. This is an exact equivalence for the stated deterministic model with a closed protected floor. Strictly positive reserve margin is needed if touching the floor itself destroys functionality.

**Proof.** Equations (8)–(9) integrate (3). The derivative structure established above leaves only the initial value and the later local minimum at \(t_*\) as candidates for the all-time minimum. Hence \(\min_{t\ge0}E(t)=E_0+\min\{0,F(t_*)\}\). Crossing before that minimum invalidates the transition; after the minimum the unconstrained reserve is nondecreasing. ∎

A negative flow interval alone does not prove that initial reserve is needed: the initial positive flow can have built sufficient surplus. The maximum with zero in (10) matters. Formula (11) also does not establish feasibility of omitted resources or the legitimacy of the protected function.

The successor alone meets demand once \(\eta m\ge1\), at

\[
T_{\mathrm{est}}=L+\frac1s\log\frac{1}{(\eta-1)q},
\qquad\text{when }\eta\varepsilon<1.
\tag{12}
\]

This is an absolute functional threshold, not fixation. Before it, remaining predecessor service may still be essential. Surviving until the flow minimum and surviving to successor-only establishment are distinct descriptions, although (11) ensures both in this model because support is increasing after \(t_*\).

## Worked counterexample

Set \(\varepsilon=.01\), \(\eta=2\), \(L=2\), \(\kappa=1\), and \(E_{\min}=0\). Both the adoption advantage and the eventual productivity advantage favor the successor.

| Selection rate | Later minimum of support stock | Required initial reserve | Interpretation |
|---:|---:|---:|:---|
| \(s=.2\) | No declining interval | \(0\) | Maturation keeps ahead of retirement |
| \(s=1\) | \(t_*=6.279490\) | \(0.729169\) | A positive invasion needs a transition buffer |
| \(s=2\) | \(t_*=4.278900\) | \(1.281121\) | This faster replacement demands a larger buffer |

For \(s=1\), successor-only establishment would occur at \(T_{\mathrm{est}}=6.595120\). At the support minimum, the adopted fraction is already approximately \(.843482\), while the functional successor fraction is only \(.421741\).

Starting with reserve \(E_0=.5\), the support floor is crossed at approximately \(t=4.937242\), before establishment. At that crossing, adoption is approximately \(.584706\) and useful successor capacity is only \(.160047\). The beneficial practice has become a majority, but the physical transition fails. Starting with reserve \(E_0=.8\) keeps the same adoption and maturation trajectory above the floor, with minimum reserve approximately \(.070831\).

The listed values come from the closed expressions; derivative checks against (3) and a dense time-grid minimum check agreed. They can be reproduced directly from (7)–(12). These calculations establish properties of the example, not that faster adoption is generally harmful. Faster replacement can be necessary when the old practice is itself failing, and changing retirement rules changes the result.

## A constructive replacement policy with paid overlap

Immediate retirement ties predecessor service to the adoption count. A different policy retires each predecessor only when its successor becomes functional. Its predecessor fraction is then

\[
o(t)=1-m(t),\qquad
I(t)=x(t)-m(t)\ge0,
\tag{13}
\]

where \(I\) is the pending trainee fraction and also the extra predecessor fraction retained compared with immediate retirement. The total concurrently occupied capacity, in reference-population units, is \(1+I\). Keeping this capacity is a real requirement.

With that predecessor service,

\[
\dot E=\kappa[o+\eta m-1]
=\kappa(\eta-1)m\ge0.
\tag{14}
\]

Thus the specified support stock does not decline during replacement. The old practice still disappears as \(m\to1\). The policy preserves the function while changing its carrier.

To avoid making overlap free, let \(B\) be an identified stock of resources usable for the extra service and training, with incremental cost \(c>0\) per unit of overlapping capacity per unit time:

\[
\dot B=-cI(t),\qquad B\ge B_{\min}.
\tag{15}
\]

Assume this expenditure covers the relevant extra inputs, does not secretly deplete \(E\) or another protected stock, and does not change the stated maturation delay. Sufficient physical capacity must also exist to accommodate \(1+I(t)\). Under these assumptions,

\[
\boxed{B_0-B_{\min}\ge cL(1-\varepsilon)}
\tag{16}
\]

funds the entire one-predecessor-per-trainee handoff. Along with the capacity limit, \(E_0\ge E_{\min}\), and maintained implementation authority, it is a constructive sufficiency certificate. It is not necessary for every possible safe replacement policy.

For the logistic trajectory, if \(q\le e^{-sL/2}\), the peak extra capacity is

\[
I_{\max}=\frac{e^{sL/2}-1}{e^{sL/2}+1}
=\tanh(sL/4).
\tag{17}
\]

It occurs after \(L\), where \(qe^{st}=\sqrt{k}\). If \(q>e^{-sL/2}\), the post-delay difference is already decreasing at \(L\), so the peak is \(I(L)=x(L)-\varepsilon\). Before \(L\), \(I=x-\varepsilon\) is increasing. These facts follow by differentiating \(w(k-1)/[(1+w)(k+w)]\) after \(L\).

In the \(s=1\) example, the full-overlap policy requires peak extra capacity approximately \(.462117\) times the reference population and cumulative budget \(1.98c\). It succeeds with the thin \(.5\) support reserve, provided those extra resources and implementation conditions hold.

## A conditional accounting invariant for replacement

Let \(a(u)=\dot x(u)\ge0\) denote adoption flow for any monotone schedule that starts with mature seed \(\varepsilon\) and eventually replaces the rest. The pending population is

\[
I(t)=\int_{\max(0,t-L)}^t a(u)\,du.
\tag{18}
\]

Each new cohort remains pending for exactly \(L\). Tonelli's theorem therefore yields

\[
\boxed{\int_0^\infty I(t)\,dt
=\int_0^\infty\!a(u)\!\int_u^{u+L}\!dt\,du
=L(1-\varepsilon).}
\tag{19}
\]

The total pending-capacity time is independent of the pace of adoption. Under the full predecessor-retention policy it equals total duplicate-service time. Pace changes when this load occurs and how much simultaneous capacity it requires. It does not remove the stipulated maturation work.

This is established occupancy-time accounting, closely related to the accounting underlying [Little's queueing law](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.9.3.383?journalCode=opre), not a newly discovered universal invariant. The finite rollout identity here follows directly by integration and requires no stationarity assumption; it should not be confused with the particular stationary assumptions of Little's original theorem.

The identity extends to heterogeneous completion times. For cohort amount \(dA(u)\) that remains pending for duration \(\ell(u)\),

\[
\int_0^\infty I(t)\,dt=\int_0^\infty\ell(u)\,dA(u).
\tag{20}
\]

For a fixed adoption schedule and random cohort durations having common conditional mean \(\bar L<\infty\), expected pending-capacity time is \(\bar L(1-\varepsilon)\). No independence between different cohort durations is needed for this expectation. If delay depends on congestion, has infinite mean, or is correlated with cohort size without the stated conditional-mean property, the simplified expression does not follow. With abandonment, the identity still counts actual residence times, but completing replacement is a separate condition.

Full predecessor retention is deliberately conservative when \(\eta>1\). If mature productivity is measured and divisible, the pointwise minimum extra predecessor fraction that prevents the support flow from becoming negative is

\[
z_{\min}(t)=[x(t)-\eta m(t)]_+\le I(t).
\tag{21}
\]

Retaining this amount supplies \(\dot E\ge0\) without one-to-one overlap everywhere. Other policies can spend existing reserves or exploit substitutions. Consequently (19) is an invariant of pending work and of the specified full-retention protocol; it is not the minimum budget required by every viable replacement. Nor does a finite integral by itself establish enough peak staffing, independent assessment, authority, or rights protection.

## Finite lineages add another establishment hurdle

The deterministic adoption equation presupposes that a nonzero seed remains. In a continuous-time linear birth–death process with per-carrier birth rate \(b\), disappearance rate \(d<b\), and \(j\) initial carriers, the probability of avoiding eventual lineage extinction is

\[
1-(d/b)^j<1\qquad\text{when }d>0.
\tag{22}
\]

This follows from the extinction fixed point for one lineage, \(q=d/(b+d)+bq^2/(b+d)\), taking the smaller root, and independence of the initial lineages. It assumes a constant, unlimited branching environment and says nothing by itself about reaching the required functional scale before support is lost. In the opposite idealization with pure birth, one initial carrier, and rate \(b\), the exact probability of reaching \(M\) carriers by time \(T\) is \((1-e^{-bT})^{M-1}\). Even certain eventual expansion then does not imply timely establishment. These are standard branching-process facts, not results particular to this theory.

For time-dependent environments, growth opportunities and stochastic establishment must be calculated together. [Uecker and Hermisson (2011)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3176092/) develop branching-process treatments of beneficial alleles in variable environments. [Orr and Unckless (2014)](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1004551) explicitly couple rescue probability with population decline, rebound timing, and population minima. Those precedents prevent claiming that the distinction between selective advantage and successful rescue is itself new.

## Implications and remaining closure problem

The model yields a concrete comparison of mechanisms: retire by adoption count, retire by demonstrated successor function, or permit retirement against a measured reserve and a verified completion trajectory. The relevant observations are initial mature seed, adoption speed, the distribution of maturation times, functional output per mature carrier, demand, stored support, peak training load, and who can finance and authorize overlap.

In human or AI systems, rollout share, profitability, benchmark improvement, or formal appointment need not equal operating competence, independent evaluative capacity, or effective authority. The model does not establish that any particular deployed system has this structure; that requires observing the retirement and maturation mechanisms. Biological mappings likewise require the actual life cycle, inheritance, and maturation constraints.

Temporary support for transition is already studied in evolutionary rescue. [Ashander, Thompson, Sanchirico, and Baskett (2019)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/66z123wg) model investment that prevents a demographic trough from crossing a dangerous threshold while adaptation catches up. Their result supports the relevance of transition finance, not the particular cohort model or formulas here.

The remaining theoretical task is to explain why the paid overlap policy is adopted and maintained under selection, including whether incumbents can obstruct replacement and whether successors can evade maturation requirements. The present construction proves that a viable handoff exists under explicit resources and authority; it does not assume that an evolving institution will implement it. Functional protection must permit capable successors to take over, and its ethical legitimacy cannot be inferred from the persistence of either incumbent or successor.
