# Paid control of the allocation that renews access to support

5 October 2026. AI-assisted mathematical development for Ren. This proposes an explicitly different binding action in the existing shared-renewal reaction network. The original [G/GF/YG model](../../../history/2026-10-05-support-and-shared-generativity/completion/PHYSICAL-REGULATION.md), its protocol, and its [negative results](../../../history/2026-10-05-support-and-shared-generativity/completion/REGULATION-REVIEW.md) remain unchanged.

**Result.** A regulator that blocks Y from consuming the last precursor for C can preserve a real productive opportunity: restoring C and capturing a fresh packet while Y remains. This is first proved locally, and then as a paid net comparison from a common state with no regulator. Construction consumes one R and one F; finite binding, dissociation, wear and competing reactions are included. A finite parameter class and deadline give a strictly higher joint delivery probability than the absent-regulator process.

This is a theorem within the declared mesoscopic reaction law. The binding chemistry, fabrication reaction and admissible rate values remain supplied assumptions, not identified molecules or a derivation from fundamental physics. It proves neither spontaneous architecture discovery nor an advantage from every preparation.

## 1. The reaction that binding must actually change

Use the previous unit-volume, unit-packet setting and keep state order

\[
x=(R,C,Y,D,F,G,GF,YG),\qquad
N=R+C+Y+D+G+GF+2YG=3.
\]

Retain every event in the original gate table, except introduce a declared bound-reproduction coefficient \(\zeta\in[0,1]\):

\[
\boxed{R+YG\longrightarrow Y+YG
\quad\text{at propensity }\zeta\beta R\,YG.}
\tag{1}
\]

The original architecture is \(\zeta=1\). The proposed allocation gate uses \(\zeta=0\); intermediate values allow partial blocking. Free Y still reproduces at propensity \(\beta RY\). Bound Y still cannot recycle, as in the original table. Binding must therefore obstruct the bound constituent's access to the reproduction reaction; changing only its recycling activity does not implement this mechanism.

This is a comparison of distinct supplied architectures. It does not change the old model retrospectively, nor give an actor a costless switch between chemistries. Whether an actual molecule can deliver this specificity and the required fabrication rates is an empirical identification question.

Paid construction remains \(R+F\to G\), propensity \(\kappa RF\). Association \(G+Y\to YG\), propensity \(\mu GY\), and dissociation, propensity \(\nu YG\), take finite time. Each Y constituent wears at rate d whether free or bound; each gate constituent wears at rate \(\delta\) in every form. Fuel binding, fuel leakage and both original recycling channels retain their specified laws.

The change in (1) preserves every material and packet ledger. With

\[
U=F+GF,\quad L=Y+YG,\quad T=G+GF+YG,
\quad W=N-D+U,
\]

construction still has \(\Delta U=-1\), \(\Delta W=-1\), and converts one usable plant unit into gate material. Binding is a zero-increment event in this reduced ledger, not a claim of zero physical binding free energy. The changed allocation drifts are

\[
\mathcal L_\zeta R=J-aR-\beta R(Y+\zeta YG)-\kappa RF,
\]

\[
\mathcal L_\zeta L=\beta R(Y+\zeta YG)-dL,
\quad J=kFD(C+\theta Y).
\tag{2}
\]

The W drift remains the old work identity. The intervention changes which structure receives R; it does not create work by changing its label.

## 2. A productive conflict in an actual reachable state

Consider

\[
x_\dagger=(1,0,1,0,0,1,0,0).
\tag{3}
\]

There is one remaining R, one Y and one already constructed gate, but neither C nor a free or bound packet. Until C forms, capture, recycling, further gate construction and fuel binding are all disabled. Dissociation cannot release a packet because GF is absent.

If Y consumes this last R, the successor has \(R=C=F=GF=0\). Neither reproduction, wear, gate dissociation nor incident support can recreate R or C through the stated repertoire: capture requires C, and recycling requires a packet. Future capture is therefore impossible. This is a stoichiometric loss of productive access, not an assigned unfavorable transition.

Conversely, \(R\to C\) restores a capture channel at rate g. Blocking the competing allocation can preserve the route by which Y's surroundings obtain further usable input. No recycling is sacrificed during this particular episode because fuel is already absent. Outside this episode, withholding recycling can still be costly.

## 3. Exact local theorem, including wear and dissociation

Assume finite \(a>0\), \(\beta,\mu,g>0\), and nonnegative finite remaining rates, with the positive wear assumptions of the main model permitted. First require C to assemble before the last R is consumed by reproduction or the sole Y wears. Then require a newly captured packet before that C or Y wears.

Before assembly, the only relevant gate configurations are free G/Y, bound YG, and Y with the gate gone. If Y wears, this joint test fails; Y is not reintroduced by another reaction. If the gate wears, its material becomes D and the ungated allocation race remains. Define

\[
p_B=\frac{a}{a+\beta+d},\qquad v=a+d+\delta.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(p_U^\zeta\) be the protected assembly probability starting with free G/Y, and \(p_I^\zeta\) the corresponding probability starting bound. Their first-step equations are exactly

\[
(v+\beta+\mu)p_U^\zeta
=a+\delta p_B+\mu p_I^\zeta,
\]

\[
(v+\zeta\beta+\nu)p_I^\zeta
=a+\delta p_B+\nu p_U^\zeta.
\tag{5}
\]

These are a reduction of actual physical states in (3), not assigned module rates. Y wear is a failure term; it is not credited with helping C by removing competition. Gate wear is included through \(\delta p_B\).

Since \(a+\delta p_B=p_B(v+\beta)\), set

\[
D_\zeta=(v+\beta+\mu)(v+\nu+\zeta\beta)-\mu\nu>0.
\]

Solving gives

\[
p_U^\zeta=
\frac{p_B(v+\beta)(v+\nu+\zeta\beta+\mu)}{D_\zeta},
\]

\[
\boxed{p_U^\zeta-p_B
=\frac{p_B\mu\beta(1-\zeta)}{D_\zeta}.}
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, the original \(\zeta=1\) gate has exactly \(p_U^1=p_B\) in this episode: binding does not change the allocation race. For \(\zeta<1\), the probability improves strictly.

After successful assembly, R and F are zero and C and total Y are each one. Gate binding, dissociation and gate wear can continue, but they do not alter the competing capture, C-wear and total-Y-wear intensities g,b,d. Thus the probability of restoring capture while preserving Y is

\[
\boxed{q_\zeta(x_\dagger)
=p_U^\zeta\frac{g}{g+b+d}.}
\tag{7}
\]

The success state has \(C\ge1\), \(Y+YG\ge1\), and a newly captured free packet. This is a delivered access-to-support event, not the persistence of G by itself.

### Finite horizons

The local comparison holds at every positive deadline, not only eventually. Couple the same G/Y association, dissociation and gate-wear history across architectures, with Y wear excluded from this background process. Let \(I_t\) indicate that Y is bound in that background history. C assembly has its independent rate a; Y wear its independent rate d, accounted for separately below; reproduction kills the opportunity at intensity \(\beta[1-(1-\zeta)I_t]\). Consequently the protected-assembly density is

\[
f_\zeta(s)=a e^{-(a+d+\beta)s}
\mathbb E\!\left[
e^{\beta(1-\zeta)\int_0^s I_t\,dt}\right].
\tag{8}
\]

The probability of a fresh protected capture by H is

\[
q_\zeta(H;x_\dagger)
=\int_0^H f_\zeta(s)
\frac{g}{g+b+d}
\left[1-e^{-(g+b+d)(H-s)}\right]ds.
\tag{9}
\]

Decreasing \(\zeta\) increases both integrands. Binding occupies a positive duration with positive probability for every \(s>0\) when \(\mu>0\), so the improvement is strict for every \(H>0\) if \(\zeta<1\) and \(a,\beta,\mu,g>0\). All delay and wear rates remain finite. Fuel-dependent occupancy is not the source of this particular theorem: F is zero, so the useful effect is the blocked allocation branch itself.

## 4. A paid comparison beginning with no regulator

The local theorem alone does not repay construction. For that comparison use the common preparation

\[
\boxed{S=(2,0,1,0,1,0,0,0).}
\tag{10}
\]

Both arms begin with the same material, packet, source and rate constants, and no regulator form. The absent arm has construction disabled. The proposed arm enables \(R+F\to G\) and has \(\zeta=0\). Binding laws can be defined identically in the absent arm; they remain inaccessible. Neither arm receives free material or fuel during construction.

For a common deadline H, define the joint target

\[
\mathcal E_H=
\{\text{a first fresh capture occurs by }H
\text{ with }C\ge1\text{ and }Y+YG\ge1\text{ at that capture}\}.
\tag{11}
\]

The initially supplied packet does not count as fresh capture. At completion there is a new free packet and both productive forms coexist. This is a hitting/delivery event; later consumption or wear can remove that state before H. It does not assert terminal readiness at H or indefinite retention. The following success path preserves the original single Y without wear; the common event itself allows any history that meets (11).

### The same paid architecture has an early disadvantage

For fixed finite rates, the two success probabilities from S satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm gate,S}(\mathcal E_h)-\Pr_{\rm absent,S}(\mathcal E_h)
=-\frac{\kappa a g}{3}h^3+O(h^4).
}
\tag{11a}
\]

**Proof.** Stop the process at its first fresh capture, with separate success/failure absorbing flags according to whether total Y is positive. Y extinction is irreversible in this repertoire. Before this stopping event, U never exceeds the initial one packet, so the material-and-flag process is finite. Ordinary generator Taylor expansions therefore apply.

Let f be the success flag, let \(Q_0\) retain the proposed binding laws but disable construction, and let \(Q_1=Q_0+B_G\), where

\[
B_Gv(x)=\kappa RF[v(T_Gx)-v(x)].
\]

On pre-capture states \(Q_0f=gC\mathbf1_{\{L\ge1\}}\). Construction changes neither C nor L, so \(B_Gf=B_GQ_0f=0\) everywhere, including the absorbing flags. Differences through second order vanish. At third order the only remaining term is \(B_GQ_0^2f\). At S and its construction successor \(x_\dagger\), respectively,

\[
Q_0^2f(S)=2ag,\qquad Q_0^2f(x_\dagger)=ag.
\]

There is no C yet; assembly is the only one-jump way to create positive capture intensity. Since construction has propensity \(2\kappa\) at S,

\[
(Q_1^3-Q_0^3)f(S)=2\kappa(ag-2ag)=-2\kappa ag.
\]

Dividing by \(3!\) proves (11a). The finite-rate statement includes the construction waiting-time penalty; it does not simply add a favorable construction path to the baseline paths. ∎

For positive \(\kappa,a,g\), construction therefore hurts this very same joint target at sufficiently early deadlines. The later strict gain established below is a time-dependent reversal within one architecture and preparation. Continuity gives at least one crossover; uniqueness or monotonicity of the difference is not established. It is not an all-times improvement.

### An upper bound for the absent arm

An exact failure route is two reproductions followed by leakage:

\[
(R,Y,F)=(2,1,1)\to(1,2,1)\to(0,3,1)\to(0,3,0).
\]

C and D remain zero throughout. The endpoint can never capture again. The probability that these are the first three events is

\[
B=
\frac{2\beta}{2a+2\beta+d+\ell}
\frac{2\beta}{a+2\beta+2d+\ell}
\frac{\ell}{3d+\ell}.
\tag{12}
\]

Every active competing propensity is included in these denominators. Therefore, for every H,

\[
\boxed{\Pr_{\rm absent,S}(\mathcal E_H)\le1-B.}
\tag{13}
\]

This uses a subset of eventual failures to bound success; it does not assume that all other paths succeed.

### A lower bound for the constructed arm

A successful paid route is

\[
S\xrightarrow{\text{build}}x_\dagger
\xrightarrow{\text{bind Y}}(1,0,0,0,0,0,0,1)
\xrightarrow{R\to C}(0,1,0,0,0,0,0,1),
\]

followed by a fresh capture before C or Y wears. The first event spends one R and the sole F. There is no further fuel or construction opportunity before the fresh capture. The probability of this route, allowing harmless gate-state changes in the final phase, is

\[
L_*=
\frac{2\kappa}{2a+2\beta+d+\ell+2\kappa}
\frac{\mu}{a+\beta+d+\delta+\mu}
\frac{a}{a+d+\delta+\nu}
\frac{g}{g+b+d}.
\tag{14}
\]

The third factor requires assembly before dissociation or either constituent's wear; later second chances are conservatively excluded. The last factor permits gate wear and dissociation because they leave C and total Y unchanged. It is an aggregate competing clock, not the full microscopic exit rate.

All four phases take time. Set

\[
\lambda_1=2a+2\beta+d+\ell+2\kappa,\quad
\lambda_2=a+\beta+d+\delta+\mu,
\]

\[
\lambda_3=a+d+\delta+\nu,\qquad
\lambda_4=g+b+d,\qquad
\lambda_{\min}=\min_j\lambda_j.
\]

Requiring each selected phase to finish within \(H/4\) gives the valid finite-horizon bound

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm gate,S}(\mathcal E_H)
\ge L_*\prod_{j=1}^4(1-e^{-\lambda_jH/4}).
}
\tag{15}
\]

The factorization follows from the Markov property and the first-event exponential races. In the final phase, the constant relevant hazards g,b,d justify the same race formula despite intervening gate-state transitions.

## 5. A nonempty finite-rate class with strict net gain

Fix positive finite \(a,b,d,\delta,\nu,g\). Define

\[
q=\frac{a}{a+d+\delta+\nu}\frac{g}{g+b+d}>0,
\qquad \epsilon=\frac q8,\qquad c_\epsilon=\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}.
\tag{16}
\]

Choose finite rates successively satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\ell&\ge3d\,c_\epsilon,\\
2\beta&\ge c_\epsilon\max\{2a+d+\ell,\ a+2d+\ell\},\\
2\kappa&\ge c_\epsilon(2a+2\beta+d+\ell),\\
\mu&\ge c_\epsilon(a+\beta+d+\delta).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{17}
\]

Each right-hand side is finite and depends only on already fixed rates, so the class is nonempty. The same \(\beta,\ell,g\) and other plant constants are used in both arms. The hierarchy is strong competition, a rapidly lost unused packet, and still faster paid fabrication and binding. It is a sufficient class, not a necessary or calibrated range.

Each factor of B is at least \(1-\epsilon\), while the first two factors of \(L_*\) are at least \(1-\epsilon\). Since \(q\le1\), \(\epsilon\le1/8\), and hence

\[
\Pr_{\rm absent,S}(\mathcal E_H)
\le1-(1-\epsilon)^3\le3\epsilon=\frac{3q}{8},
\]

\[
L_*\ge(1-\epsilon)^2q\ge\frac{49q}{64}.
\tag{18}
\]

Choose the finite deadline

\[
H\ge\frac{4\log16}{\lambda_{\min}}.
\tag{19}
\]

Each phase-completion factor in (15) is at least \(15/16\), and their product is at least \(3/4\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm gate,S}(\mathcal E_H)
\ge\frac{147q}{256}
>\frac{96q}{256}
\ge\Pr_{\rm absent,S}(\mathcal E_H).
}
\tag{20}
\]

This proves a paid net improvement for one genuine joint delivery from the same regulator-free preparation. Neither an assigned favorable value function nor a regulator present from time zero is used. It does not prove inclusion of a whole capability region.

### An exact illustration, chosen from the hierarchy rather than searched

Take

\[
a=b=d=\delta=\nu=1,\quad g=4,
\quad\ell=141,\quad\beta=3384,
\quad\kappa=162432,\quad\mu=159189.
\tag{21}
\]

Other unchanged laws can use \(k=1,\theta=4,\alpha_b=4,\rho=1\), with bound leakage equal to free leakage. These values are obtained by setting (17) to equality: \(q=1/6\), \(\epsilon=1/48\), \(c_\epsilon=47\). No parameter search was performed. Every factor in B and the first two factors in \(L_*\) equals \(47/48\).

Here \(\lambda_{\min}=4\), so H=3 suffices: \(e^3>16\), already certified by the first five terms of its exponential series. The more direct rational bounds are

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm absent,S}(\mathcal E_3)
\le\frac{6769}{110592}<\frac1{10},
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm gate,S}(\mathcal E_3)
\ge\frac34\frac{2209}{13824}
=\frac{6627}{55296}>\frac1{10}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

Thus a common reliability threshold of 0.1 separates this one installed joint profile. These are analytical bounds, not simulated point estimates. The large finite constants establish compatibility of the inequalities, not plausibility of a specific chemistry. Known molecular or transport limits could exclude this hierarchy; none has been identified here.

## 6. How the preparation can be reached—and what that does not prove

The original preparation is \(x_0=(0,1,1,1,1,0,0,0)\). With \(g,b,k,\theta>0\), a supported path to S is

\[
\text{capture, capture, C wear, Y-catalyzed recycling, Y-catalyzed recycling}.
\tag{23}
\]

After the two captures F=3. C wear produces a second D. The two recyclings produce R=2 and leave F=1, giving exactly S with no gate. The product of successive rates on this particular five-event route is

\[
g\cdot g\cdot b\cdot(6k\theta)\cdot(2k\theta)
=12bk^2\theta^2g^2.
\tag{24}
\]

Requiring these events and excluding all competitors has strictly positive probability on every positive interval under finite rates. A construction event appended at S has propensity \(2\kappa\), yielding \(x_\dagger\) and six-event rate product \(24b\kappa k^2\theta^2g^2\).

Across that six-event preparation, the initial packet and two newly captured packets are spent on two recyclings and one construction. One C wears, and one recycled R becomes G. The gate is not inserted as free initial material.

Nevertheless, the net theorem (20) compares processes **starting at S**. In the enabled process, fabrication can compete during the route from \(x_0\), so the probability and duration of reaching S differ between arms. Reachability does not permit conditioning away that earlier cost or claiming a net advantage from \(x_0\). That comparison would require its full initial-state law. S is a declared, physically supported preparation; the theorem is not an origin from no resources or no reaction repertoire.

## 7. What this bridge closes, and its remaining limits

The [opportunity audit](REGULATION-OPPORTUNITY-AUDIT.md) identified that retaining packets was not yet a demonstrated productive benefit in the old regulator model. Here the contested input is the last precursor for capture machinery. The harmful branch and the protected branch are explicit reactions. A successful gate commits one precursor and packet to regulation, preserves another precursor, assembles C, and enables a new packet while Y remains. The gains are probabilities of actual joint delivery, with fabrication and finite kinetics inside the comparison.

The exact positive claim is limited to the changed architecture, the common preparation, the stated parameter class, the deadline, and event (11). The old model's negative comparisons remain intact. A gate with \(\zeta=1\) cannot inherit this proof; a gate that fails the rate hierarchy may still lose; different targets can favor different arrangements. On the paid success route, construction spends the only F, and the assay ends at the first fresh capture. GF never forms along that route. The theorem therefore establishes useful paid allocation inhibition, not the necessity or advantage of fuel-responsive switching over a constant inhibitor.

The material/work ledger and event law are explicit. They do not identify chemical potentials, molecular structures, achievable association constants or thermodynamic reverse pathways. The architecture and reaction repertoire are supplied. Their discovery, selection, inheritance and long-run maintenance remain distinct questions. Positive wear and finite material also retain the earlier eventual-absorption boundary.

The formal advance is narrower and concrete: **a paid change in a specified interaction can preserve an otherwise irreversibly lost opportunity to renew shared access to support, and can improve a joint finite-time delivery from an equal regulator-free state.** This is one conditional mechanism within the proposed theory, not a universal tendency or an ethical conclusion.
