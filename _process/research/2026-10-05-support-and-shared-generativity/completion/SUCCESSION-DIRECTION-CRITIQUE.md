# Useful direction when the scaffold itself must be replaced

5 October 2026. Independent mathematical and model review following commit `37f580edadeecd5182d9effbd876414852fb243d`.

**Finding.** Paid access to a stored packet removes the earlier impossibility of scaffold replacement. It also removes the reason that suppressing bound reproduction was uniformly beneficial. The same reaction can destroy the only route back to capture, or produce the daughter required for succession while a capture carrier remains intact. Both effects occur at physically reachable states of the supplied CF extension. The declared complete-task calculation finds a real positive comparison at H=10, together with the opposite short-time sign. A narrow, explicit neighborhood of elementary rate constants preserves the positive H=10 comparison. Neither result establishes architectural prevalence or indefinite generativity.

This note concerns the specific common-law model and supplied source. It does not replace the positive objective with a definition of successful systems.

## 1. The extension and the boundary of the old proof

Retain the [CF storage model](USEFUL-RETENTION-MECHANISM.md) and add precisely the channels in the [declared paid-access protocol](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/paid-access/PROTOCOL.md):

\[
D+CF\xrightarrow{h}R+C,\qquad
R+CF\xrightarrow{s_i}A_i+C,\qquad
A_i+R+CF\xrightarrow{q_i}A_i+G_i+C.
\tag{1}
\]

Their propensities are h D CF, s_i R CF and q_i A_i R CF. The protocol excludes bound-packet copying and the previously optional CF-catalyzed *free*-packet recycling channel. Both fabricator/gate types remain available. Source capture C -> CF, wear, leakage, all original free-packet channels, and binding remain active where reactants permit them.

The earlier nine-state proof stops at the first A/G/Y scaffold wear. Within those nine states, all three new channels are disabled, so that proof remains exact. However, after A wear from

\[
P_i:\quad A_i=CF=YG_i=1,
\]

bound recycling produces

\[
S_i:\quad R=C=YG_i=1,
\quad A_i=CF=F=0.
\tag{2}
\]

The two-event route P_i -> A_i wear -> bound recycling has positive probability, including its competing hazards. At S_i, source access survives the consumption of R. Bound reproduction therefore is no longer a transition into the old absorbing failure set. Suppressing it cannot be represented globally by deleting only a killing clock.

The new access is physically substantive. The old availability count V=R+C+CF+F+sum(GF_i) increases by one in D+CF -> R+C, while the work count W=N-D+F+CF+sum(GF_i) is conserved. A captured packet becomes an accessible precursor while the capture carrier remains. More work and more accessible construction combinations are different quantities. This increase in V opens alternatives; V alone does not rank their consequences for the whole organization.

## 2. Exact opposed consequences from the same material preparation

Compare the mapped states S_0 and S_1, with identical counts and common reaction constants but the two supplied gate structures. This is equality of declared material preparation, not identity of their complete physical microstates. Let time t be small and positive, and tag the original Y constituent in YG.

For the common assay **the original Y has worn, a descendant remains, and C+CF>0**, the nonblocking gate has the two-event route

\[
R+YG_1\to Y+YG_1,
\qquad YG_1\to G_1+D
\]

where the second event is the original Y's wear, at rate d. The capture carrier remains C throughout. The blocking gate must first dissociate, then perform free reproduction, then lose the original Y: at least three events. Hence

\[
p_{Y,1}(t)-p_{Y,0}(t)
=\frac{\beta d}{2}t^2+O(t^3)>0
\quad\text{for sufficiently small }t.
\tag{3}
\]

Conversely, for **at least one fresh R -> C assembly by t**, both processes initially have rate a. Bound reproduction removes that sole R; its first effect is to remove this assembly opportunity. A marked-event generator calculation gives

\[
p_{C,0}(t)-p_{C,1}(t)
=\frac{a\beta}{2}t^2+O(t^3)>0.
\tag{4}
\]

One can verify (4) without discarding any other hazard. Reproduction does not directly change the assembly-success flag, so its generator difference annihilates that flag. Applied to the first derivative, its contribution is beta times the change in assembly hazard, beta(0-a). All common competing events cancel at this order.

These statements hold for any fixed finite positive a,beta,d and other finite rates. They are not produced by choosing a favorable parameter search. They prove a genuine tradeoff in the two success probabilities at the same small deadline. They do **not** by themselves prove incomparability of capability regions defined using one common reliability threshold alpha; that additional threshold comparison must actually be checked.

### A joint installed-organization succession task

The favorable succession effect can also complete an organization rather than merely leave a daughter. From S_i, require arrival by t at some A_j=CF=YG_i=1, with the original Y replaced and the original C and G never having worn. Either A type is accepted by the common target. All initial and final material counts are N=4; the event pays for its new fabricator.

For type 1, a shortest success has eight events: bound Y reproduction B; original-Y wear W; bound recycling T; bound A formation A; daughter binding M; and three captures c1,c2,c3. Their constraints are

\[
B<W<T<c2<A<c3,\qquad c1<T,\qquad W<M.
\]

There are exactly 16 orders: c1 can precede B, lie between B and W, or follow W; inserting M after W gives respectively 5,5,6 possibilities. Each selected rate product is beta d h(s_0+s_1) mu g^3. No holding-time hazard is removed; competing hazards affect the next order in t. Thus

\[
p_{{\rm installed},1}(t)
=\frac{\beta d h(s_0+s_1)\mu g^3}{2520}t^8+O(t^9),
\qquad
p_{{\rm installed},0}(t)=O(t^9).
\tag{5}
\]

To check minimality, initial W=4 and final W=5. Y wear and A formation cost one each, so at least three captures are necessary. A daughter, original-Y wear, recycling, A formation and rebinding require five further events. At eight events there is no allowance for dissociation, leakage or extra births/wear. Blocking therefore needs at least a ninth event. Retaining the original C and G prevents a wear/rebuild route from evading that count. After any bounded number of jumps the reachable state set is finite, so the stated Taylor remainders are controlled by a finite local exit-rate bound.

This is an analytical positive succession result for a declared joint task and a reachable preparation. It is not the complete four-original-carrier assay, and it does not claim that type 1 dominates its other futures.

## 3. The sign in a complete task is a derived balance

On a finite stopped assay state space, include every lineage mark, entry condition, success flag and failure boundary needed by the target. Let Q_zeta interpolate the bound-reproduction rate in such a generator, with all other channels fixed. This is a mathematical comparison of supplied laws or assay generators, not an external controller changing a trajectory's chemistry. Write

\[
(Bv)(x)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal B}b_r(x)
[v(T_rx)-v(x)],\qquad Q_\zeta=Q_0+\zeta B,
\tag{6}
\]

where r specifies a bound-reproduction channel, including its marked constituent choices, and T_r is its actual state update. In a valid unmarked lumping, this becomes beta R YG times the single successor-value difference; the marked generator must retain the sum when successors differ.

With a declared initial subprobability measure mu and terminal success flag f, put u_zeta(t)=exp(t Q_zeta)f. The exact response is

\[
\frac{d}{d\zeta}\mu e^{HQ_\zeta}f
=\int_0^H\sum_x p_\zeta(s,x)\sum_{r\in\mathcal B}b_r(x)
[u_\zeta(H-s,T_rx)-u_\zeta(H-s,x)]\,ds.
\tag{7}
\]

The nine-state theorem was a special case in which the successor had zero continuation value for every tested productive task. After scaffold wear, that premise fails. States with no remaining capture carrier or packet still give negative repair contributions. States such as S_i can give positive succession contributions. The sign also depends on the remaining deadline and which carriers have already been replaced.

Equation (7) is an exact diagnostic, not a substantive class definition by itself: defining a favorable class as 'the integral is positive' would simply restate success. An explanatory result must derive its sign from independently specified reactions, rate bounds, source conditions and an entry ensemble, as in the local proofs above or the complete-task bound below. It must not infer that every positive contribution in a full task comes from one selected reaction route.

## 4. A positive complete-task result, its reversal, and a primitive-rate class

The prespecified [complete-task calculation](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/paid-access/results.json) starts from R3,Y1,F2 with no A or G. At the first fresh capture it requires A_i=CF=YG_j=1. At the seventh fresh capture it requires the same count/type state with all four original entry carriers worn. J_i(H) is the unconditional contribution for first forming A_i. The target is common, and failed first events and preparations remain in the law.

The [directed integer certificate](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/paid-access/certificate.json) reports, at all declared positive rates equal to one and H=10,

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_0(10)&\in[278831198712280953,278831198712280954]/10^{30},\\
J_1(10)&\in[269173570450682085,269173570450682086]/10^{30}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

The certified difference is positive and at least 9657628261598867/10^30. Both probabilities are approximately 2.7 times 10^-13. This is a nonzero moderate-rate comparison, not likely operation.

The same calculation has the exact early expansion

\[
J_0(H)-J_1(H)
=-\frac{1934532}{24!}H^{24}+O(H^{25}).
\tag{9}
\]

Thus even this *single common complete task*, under one fixed source and rate setting, has opposite short- and later-deadline comparisons. Continuity proves at least one crossing. It proves neither a unique crossing nor monotonic improvement beyond it. The positive result at H=10 is retained; the stronger claim of positive comparison at every horizon is false.

### A derived neighborhood specified by reaction rates

The positive result is not confined to an isolated equality of parameters. Preserve the reaction vocabulary, zero channels, preparation, source form and H=10 task. Allow every positive **elementary propensity coefficient** to differ from its unit value by at most

\[
\varepsilon=1/50000=0.00002.
\tag{10}
\]

Keep the architectural choices zeta0=0 and zeta1=1 fixed. If coefficients share a physical parameter, keep that linkage; this result does not assert independent chemical tunability. A product such as k theta is an elementary reaction coefficient for this statement, rather than permission to vary each factor by epsilon and ignore their product. The width in (10) was chosen after the unit-rate result by the analytic tolerance proof below; it was not an empirically preregistered validation class.

On the exact pruned assay graph, the unit-law exit rate is at most 21. For a path with at most 512 jumps, its perturbed/reference likelihood ratio lies between

\[
(1-\varepsilon)^{512}e^{-210\varepsilon}
\quad\text{and}\quad
(1+\varepsilon)^{512}e^{210\varepsilon}.
\]

The lower bound is at least c=1-722 epsilon=24639/25000, and the upper bound is at most 1/c. The latter follows from exp(x)<=1/(1-x) for 0<=x<1. The maximum perturbed exit rate is 21(1+epsilon), so at H=10 the dominating Poisson mean is below 211. The probability of more than 512 jumps is bounded by

\[
\tau<e^{211}/2^{512}<3^{211}/2^{512}<10^{-50};
\]

the last inequality is an integer comparison. This includes real jumps by domination; null uniformization steps need not be observed.

Let L0 be the lower endpoint for J0 and U1 the upper endpoint for J1 in (8). Applying the path bound separately to both unnormalized branch events gives, throughout this entire primitive-rate box,

\[
\boxed{J_0'(10)-J_1'(10)
\ge c(L0-10^{-50})-U1/c-10^{-50}
>1.6\times10^{-15}>0.}
\tag{11}
\]

This inequality was checked by exact rational arithmetic. It allows the perturbations to favor the competing branch; no favorable selection weights are assigned. It is a derived robustness neighborhood around the independently prespecified unit-rate model, not a new prospective experimental validation. Its width, 0.002 percent per coefficient, is conservative and narrow. No chemical feasibility, measurement precision, or biological reliability follows from it.

## 5. Continued illumination cannot remove this finite-population loss boundary

Let L=Y+sum(YG_i) be total Y lineage size. Under precisely the stated channels, L=0 is invariant, N is fixed, every Y constituent wears at rate d>0, and all Y births are free or bound reproduction. For 0<=zeta_i<=1,

\[
\lambda_{Y\text{-birth}}\le\beta R L
\le B:=\beta\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor.
\tag{12}
\]

The source and packet reserve do not change that bound. For N=4, B=4 beta. During an interval T, couple potential births to a rate-B Poisson clock independent of the constituent wear clocks. With probability at least

\[
p_T=e^{-BT}(1-e^{-dT})^N>0,
\tag{13}
\]

no birth clock rings and all the at most N currently present Y constituents wear. Binding and unbinding do not alter their individual wear rate. Other reactions cannot create Y in this no-birth event. The bound is uniform over all material configurations, source states and packet reserves allowed by this law.

By the Markov property, the lineage-loss time T_Y therefore obeys

\[
\Pr(T_Y>mT)\le(1-p_T)^m\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]

This excludes indefinite Y-dependent generativity almost surely in this fixed finite-population model, even under continued illumination. It does not imply immediate failure, deny a useful metastable operating interval, or show that every non-Y capability disappears. Immigration, reverse Y-wear, growth of the material population, or an additional template-free Y formation channel changes the premise and requires a new accounting. A long-run claim cannot silently import such a restart.

## 6. The strongest next positive target

The broad claim 'the useful architecture wins for all preparations, deadlines, tasks and maintained supports' is obstructed by actual states and tasks of the current law, not merely by a generic philosophical objection. Equations (3)–(5) give opposed local consequences; (8)–(9) give a reversal for one complete task; and (14) limits infinite continuation. None of these obstructions completes the originally desired positive account of direction.

A substantive next theorem can retain that aim with explicit physical scope:

- Derive the **entire unnormalized entry measure** from the common unformed preparation and environmental process. Include failures and all ways of reaching the declared organizational family; do not renormalize selected survivors or only a favorable formation route.
- Derive a **full successor kernel** that follows replacement of all carriers, its actual endpoint/environment, elapsed time, captured work and failed exits. Return counts alone must not erase a changed support state.
- State a primitive source/material/rate class before validation, or explicitly label a mathematically derived neighborhood such as (10). Prove a useful joint-task or occupation comparison from those conditions. Do not define the class by having the desired answer.
- Test the resulting prediction on independently measured transitions and an independently supplied entrance ensemble. Changing the source, reserve, binding or wear rates should give predicted failures and sign reversals, not simply invite relabeling the outcome as nongenerative.

For an organization occupying only four conserved material units, this is presently a theory of formation and finite renewal, not multiplication of whole organizations. Propagation would require an explicit additional material and association mechanism. The new positive work is that a charged carrier can finance construction while retaining capture access, and a fully paid joint task already has a proved favorable region. What remains is to show when that coupling governs the actual ensemble of generated organizations strongly enough to produce the proposed wider direction.
