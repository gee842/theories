# Acquired prediction, correction and paid action in one formed organization

6 October 2026. AI-authored mathematics for the named connection **formed, revisable machinery → acquired consequence prediction → correction after dependency change → action using that retained prediction**. This joins the [actual updater construction](UPDATER-FORMATION-MATH.md) to an explicitly priced predictive capability. It responds to the interface left open by the [paid teaching study](CONSEQUENCE-TO-LEARNING-MATH.md) and respects the [controlled-refinement criterion](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/CONTROLLED-REFINEMENT.md).

The construction is conditional. A paid retrofit supplies new sensing, decoding, finite memory, updating and response-routing functions. The energy and material that pay for it come from actual preceding formation events. Paying a declared retrofit does not derive its physical functionality or identify its chemistry. The result is a joined realization with explicit assumptions and failure cases, not a claim that arbitrary prediction or concern follows from energy alone.

## 1. A physical prefix, not a fresh learner preparation

Use the previous study's original A/H formation law, its two-material precursor stock and paid adapter. The new original stocks are declared **before preparation**: common fuel 24, source energy 121, no A/H and a damaged site. In the same zero-loss preparation law, a fraction 2/9 reaches the paid adapter; all other terminal histories and their unspent resources remain in unconditional results.

Every successful old preparation has common fuel 20, local fuel one and source energy 120. The adapter pays one common unit and mobilizes the actual local unit, leaving reserve 20 and twelve ten-unit source packets. Its two recyclable backing slots come from actual formed A and H. This is the existing coarse material interface; the new circuit is not silently substituted for a consumed-parts model.

From that state, the old updater is actually formed at cost g=2, and its old selector is actually assembled at cost c=1 with a fair first orientation. The remaining reserve is 17. A separate retrofit costs b=3, leaving reserve 14. The retrofit preserves the current updater's type, its lifetime, the actual selector orientation, source, material and all previous expenditure. It installs a finite predictive circuit and changes how the updater is used:

- A retrofitted R-type module implements **Slow** writing.
- A retrofitted D-type module implements **Fast** writing.

These are declared new functionalities. The earlier R/D random-restart and opposite-selector algorithms are not claimed to be the same algorithms as Slow/Fast prediction. The paid retrofit is the explicit functional interface between them.

The circuit holds q∈{0,1,2,3,4} and the last executed orientation ℓ∈{0,1}; this fixed finite capacity is part of the supplied recipe. Initially q=2 is an uninformed prior, while ℓ is the orientation actually assembled by the old selector. It is not a target acquired from the environment. A gate replacement can preserve this memory because q and ℓ remain physically in the circuit slot. Circuit destruction and rebuilding instead lose it, repay the base/retrofit bills and install a fresh prior.

## 2. Accessible record, prediction and decision

The plant has hidden useful orientation z. A trial choosing action a captures its packet exactly when a=z and the source opportunity is available. The controller does not receive z, the reversal time or future availability. It receives the chosen action and its actual capture/no-capture receipt C. In the primary model availability is one.

The local decoder produces

\[
Y(a,C)=\begin{cases}a,&C=1,\\1-a,&C=0.\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]

Under the declared exhaustive, noiseless two-orientation plant, Y=z. Equivalently, it identifies whether orientation one would capture an available packet under the current condition. This follows from the physical compatibility law and the action-tagged receipt; the analyst does not send z as a learning target.

The retained q is read as a forecast \(\hat p_1=q/4\) for capture when choosing orientation one. The forecast for an actual issued action is

\[
\hat p(a,q)=\begin{cases}q/4,&a=1,\\1-q/4,&a=0.\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

This is a supplied decoder for a finite learned register. Being in [0,1] does not establish statistical calibration. The Slow rule is a clipped finite counter, not the unbiased exponential estimator from the earlier teaching study. We assess its forecasts on actual scored trials, with coverage reported separately.

The normal command is

\[
\pi(q,\ell)=\begin{cases}0,&q<2,\\1,&q>2,\\\ell,&q=2.\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

A paid write implements one of the following:

\[
q^+_{\rm Fast}=4Y,
\qquad
q^+_{\rm Slow}=\begin{cases}\min(4,q+1),&Y=1,\\\max(0,q-1),&Y=0.\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]

The write rule uses only the acquired record, current module and existing register. A hidden-condition change alone does not modify q or ℓ. The retained prediction can therefore be useful before a change and wrong afterward.

The causal action claim is stronger than finding a nonzero coefficient. Holding the physical state and actual last action fixed, setting q=0 issues action zero and setting q=4 issues action one. In the concrete correction witness below, a paid write changes q from two to zero while keeping the just-executed action one fixed; the subsequent normal command changes from one to zero. Thus the command change is due to the written prediction, not a tie being broken by a different `last` value.

## 3. Sequential costs and actual continuation

The primary costs and lifetimes are

\[
g=2,\ c=1,\ b=3,\ h=1,\ d=3,\ w=1,
\quad r+v=2,\ G=10,\ L=6,\ J=2.
\tag{5}
\]

Here h pays circuit memory retention for an exposure, d pays response work and observation, w pays writing, r and v pay circuit and updater renewal, G is a source packet's energy, and L,J are circuit/updater lifetimes.

After any affordable hardware formation or rebuilding, an exposure first pays h. It then chooses (3), records the forecast (2), and prepays d before the physical outcome. Capture transfers G from the inherited source and pays r+v. The acquired record can then pay for a write if w is affordable; otherwise q stays unchanged. The last-action field records the actual action. Finally, capture restores both lifetimes and failure decrements them. The record is transient and does not become free persistent memory.

Every source opportunity consumes one packet whether captured, wasted by a failed action or unused by inactive hardware. Failure to afford retention destroys accessible circuit memory. An unaffordable later stage never refunds an earlier formation payment. These full exposure, skipped-write, destruction and rebuilding rules belong to the [executable protocol](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/selectors/updaters/prediction/PROTOCOL.md).

The Lean relation separately represents **rent → work/receipt → paid write → wear/renewal**. It proves the deterministic-capture, affordable-write subprocess, including the continuing correction witness. Its generic ledger accounts for each represented phase. It does not claim a refinement of all skipped-write, circuit-rebuild, inactive-expiry or stochastic-outage program branches.

For the represented subprocess, every finite history satisfies

\[
\mathcal E_{\rm final}+\text{paid formation/retention/work/write/renewal}
+\text{wasted source}=\mathcal E_{\rm initial}.
\tag{6}
\]

The actual old formation trace, old adapter, actual old updater/selector formation, paid retrofit and predictive continuation also satisfy one combined ledger. Both material backing slots remain in that history. No subsystem receives its original allocation again after it has spent it.

## 4. A sharp acquisition and correction budget

From a commissioned entrance with neither old updater nor selector installed, the first predictive trial requires sequential prepayment

\[
\boxed{F\ge g+c+b+h+d=10.}
\tag{7}
\]

To guarantee that a **failed** first trial can also write its acquired evidence requires one further unit:

\[
\boxed{F\ge g+c+b+h+d+w=11.}
\tag{8}
\]

A productive return could pay the write, but cannot pay for the already required startup. In the primary original preparation, the adapter entrance reserve equals original common stock minus four. Thus fourteen original common units can fund the first trial, while fifteen are required to fund its first failure-contingent write. This is not a guarantee of subsequent action: that write may leave no reserve for using the result.

Now consider a declared correction episode starting from q=4, last action one, full updater lifetime J=2 and a circuit with more than three remaining uses. The plant has changed to z=0, remains fixed, and captures are noiseless. Slow writing produces q=3 after one failed action and q=2 after two. The tie rule retains action one. The third negative write produces q=1, finally issuing action zero. Fast writing would produce q=0 after the first failure.

For the three-write route, each unproductive exposure costs h+d+w before any usable return. After the second failure the updater must be reconstructed at cost g. Then the corrected action must prepay h+d. Consequently

\[
\boxed{B_{\rm correct}=3(h+d+w)+g+h+d=21.}
\tag{9}
\]

This threshold also applies to the displayed route that changes from Slow to Fast after the first two failures: from q=2, the third failed observation is written as q=0 instead of q=1, but the same payments precede the first corrected trial. It is conditional on those updater choices and no intervening circuit destruction, not a claim about every randomly formed updater history.

At F=20 this route can perform the three corrective writes and pay another memory rent, yet cannot afford the corrected trial. At F=21 it can initiate that trial exactly. Its subsequent productive packet can fund both renewal and writing because G≥r+v+w in the primary law. The finite source also needs at least four opportunities; the circuit must retain its acquired memory through the three failures. A correct register alone is not an affordable action.

The corresponding one-write Fast route costs h+d+w+h+d=9 when the current updater remains active for the corrected trial. It is a different architecture-specific route, not a universal lower bound on learning. A direct immediate response, another available action repertoire, different renewal timing or additional inflows can alter these bounds.

More generally, n informative failures with no intervening return, K required updater formations and a final paid trial require

\[
F\ge n(h+d+w)+Kg+h+d.
\tag{10}
\]

For a full updater of lifetime J, replaced immediately on expiry, K=⌊n/J⌋ through the final trial. This count assumes the circuit persists and the stated n-write decision lag applies. The lag and validity of the observations are separate premises; the budget formula cannot establish them. The Lean sequential-payment theorem checks the exact primary three-write prefix and its threshold, retaining every intermediate charge.

## 6. Why the same no-return signal can become misleading

The decoder's success depends on the plant class. Compare two worlds while executing action zero:

| Hidden world | Observed action-zero receipt | Would action one capture? |
|---|---|---|
| z=1, source available | No return | Yes |
| z=0, source unavailable | No return | No |

The action and receipt are identical. The prepaid work, failed fuel balance and lost packet can also be identical. Yet the counterfactual orientation-one target differs. No function of that record alone can identify the target over this expanded class. An additional identifiable measurement or paid discriminating intervention is needed; calling the record a reward does not resolve the ambiguity.

In the second world, decoder (1) produces Y=1 and may strengthen a false orientation-one prediction. Noisy/outage comparisons retain that actual decoder and record the resulting errors. They do not secretly supply corrected z to the writer. Since actions depend on prior memory and resources, the observations are also action-dependent and censored by affordability. An unconditional expectation recurrence from the earlier calibrated-teacher model cannot simply be imported.

This distinction is central to the research objective: an operationally acquired signal is a stronger explanation than an externally assigned success score, but it still requires a valid relation to the consequence being predicted. Correction of a wrong action under one plant does not establish universally reliable inference.

## 7. Exact comparison and the controlled-refinement boundary

The conventional comparator uses the same finite state, actual receipts, updater choices, costs and timing. The intended equality is equality of the physical transition kernel, not merely one favorable average. The same material mechanism expressed as a conventional finite-state predictor has no performance difference by its name.

Fixed Fast and fixed Slow keep the same prices while testing the choice of updating rule. The direct-record arm uses the latest decoded bit with the same paid retention, writing and work, but a cheaper declared retrofit for its simpler register/router. It therefore has an explicit architectural price difference; it is not a free-memory baseline. Fixed Fast remains the equal-price comparison. Paid sham writes, inverted acquired records and inverted actions separate the memory-writing and action-routing couplings while leaving physical returns real.

The [controlled-refinement result](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/CONTROLLED-REFINEMENT.md) explains what would justify moving a learner between descriptions: exact world projection/lifting, compatible complete observations and the same inherited memory. Here the old-to-new step is a **paid change of functioning machinery**, not an exact observational relabeling of the old R/D controller. The formal result is an exact old-prefix embedding, an actual paid interface, and a concrete predictive continuation with its account. It does not assert that several rent/work/write events are one zero-cost abstract update, or that all Python stochastic histories have been refined into Lean.

The [formal package](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/selectors/updaters/prediction/formal/README.md) checks the finite register bounds, physical ledger, inherited-state interfaces, noiseless decoder, outage ambiguity, memory-dependent command, sequential cost obstruction and joined correction witness. The supplied recipe's physical origin, empirical identification, open-ended prediction, reliable transfer between centers and the shared evaluative routing needed for concern remain separate obligations. A local return predictor does not establish another center's standing or an ethical reason to protect it.
