# How concern can revise its own boundaries

**Latest continuation:** the [integrated settlement](../../../theories/becoming/chapters/REVISABLE-CONCERN-SETTLEMENT.md) now joins paid revision, retained grounds, effective protection and succession in one conditional process, with defended ethical grounds, finite decision results and an evidence audit. Its [study record](settlement/README.md) preserves proofs, independent internal reviews and what remains unresolved. The chapter below introduces the earlier scope and access results on which it builds.

The new advance is an account of how a process can change **who and what its reasons reach**, and how that change can resist convenient suppression.

The previous concern theory explained learned valuation, effective action, correction, and renewal. This continuation adds a traceable challenge to the rules governing inclusion. If the process accepts a reason in one case and a warranted comparison exposes an excluded equivalent case, it must extend the reason, establish a relevant difference, or openly revise its prior commitment. Refusing or silencing the challenge is a different outcome.

Three distinctions carry the argument:

1. **A valid claim is different from a claim that gets heard.** Cutting a channel can block influence without removing standing or refuting the reason.
2. **A retained concern is different from its current delivery channel.** Valid records can preserve it through obstruction. A defeated justification, however, requires checking what was learned from it.
3. **Eventual recognition is different from timely protection.** A procedure can converge correctly after the opportunity to help has passed.

The central strategic result is simple. Imagine two new claims, each costing an authority two units to honor. Each seems secure because blocking either route costs three. But if both routes share a channel that can be blocked for three, excluding both saves four. The claims are vulnerable together even though they pass separate checks. A genuinely independent, funded route can change that result; more information alone may not.

The formal work gives an explicit finite admission rule, learning and deadline conditions, retention and retraction counterexamples, and exact joint suppression thresholds. It preserves an essential boundary: equal recognition of one relevant reason does not require identical treatment of whole persons, erase special duties, or make standing depend on familiarity or usefulness. Unfamiliar claims need direct intake as well as analogy.

This is a constructive extension of the candidate theory. Its positive anchors, relevance criteria, procedural commitments, and available institutions remain declared premises. It does not prove that reality inevitably develops wider concern.
