# Settling ethical scope and acting through remaining disagreement

5 October 2026. AI-assisted continuation of Ren's research, not a quotation or attribution of these arguments to Ren. This chapter develops the [ethical foundation](../../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md) and [revisable concern](../../THEORY.md). It offers a defended ethical kernel, exact consequences, and a decision procedure for cases that the kernel does not uniquely rank.

**A substantial ethical settlement is possible without a complete ranking of every conceivable life.** Avoidable suffering can supply a reason independently of usefulness; bare identity cannot cancel that reason; and a process imposing consequential decisions owes affected parties a practicable response. These commitments rule out specific exclusions now. Where independently defensible claims conflict, their continued existence constrains the decision even when they do not determine a unique outcome.

The new formal contribution is a finite rule for preserving that distinction. It rejects actions that are no better under any retained reason model and worse under at least one, keeps the remaining options visible, and characterizes the circumstances in which equal claims require an impartial lottery. Its proofs settle those conditional questions. The argument for the commitments is philosophical and revisable; it is neither a physical inevitability nor merely a report of someone's preferences.

## Why the positive reason is more than a preference

Take a deliberately narrow starting case: severe, unwanted suffering, avoidable without imposing any other relevant cost, violating consent, or frustrating an independent good. The subject has a reason for relief because of what this condition is like for that subject. The reason is not exhausted by an observer's desire for relief, nor by a reward the subject can offer. A person who ceases to command sympathy does not thereby cease to undergo the injury.

The proposed first commitment is that this fact supplies at least a reason against causing or maintaining that condition in deliberation about shared action. Its defense is substantive. A theory that denies any such reason treats the difference between a subject's severe distress and its costless relief as normatively empty. It must explain why the subject-relative loss supplies no consideration at all, rather than explaining it away by low productivity, silence, or distance. Those are facts about the observer's incentives or access, not facts removing the loss. This is a reason to reject the denial, not a deductive demonstration from nonnormative premises that every skeptic must accept.

An egoist can accept a reason to relieve their own suffering while denying other-directed reasons. Our second commitment challenges the relevant distinction. If the acknowledged ground is suffering's character for its subject, replacing the bearer while preserving that ground does not remove it. An additional relationship or responsibility may add a reason; its existence does not automatically reduce another's reason to zero. The egoist may instead say that only the relation to themselves ever mattered. That is a changed or more explicit normative position, whose omission of the other subject's loss still needs defense. It is not refuted by symmetry alone.

The proposal therefore does not sneak impartial concern out of self-interest. It defends **subject-relative harm as a consideration in shared judgment**, then defends consistency about that consideration. The defense is open to counterargument, but disagreement does not make every answer equally supported. A convenient refusal to acknowledge a reason is not evidence against it.

Three verdicts must stay distinct. An actor who accepts the positive reason and the relevant comparison but retains a bare identity gate is inconsistent with those commitments. An actor who rejects the bridge can remain formally consistent while, on the argument defended here, making a substantive ethical mistake about what the other's loss supplies. Either actor may remain unmotivated. A causal explanation of how motivation forms answers a different question from the justification of the reason; success or failure of that causal process does not decide the ethical argument.

This first commitment is sufficient, not an exhaustive account of value. Enjoyment, attachment, freedom, understanding, rest, and meaningful activity can also matter. The narrow harm case supplies an anchor without reducing a life to suffering avoidance.

## Why agency and answerability add reasons

Relief alone does not settle how someone should live. A competent person's informed, uncoerced refusal supplies a distinct consideration because choosing, revising, and authorizing their own projects are part of their practical authorship. Treating that person solely as a site at which an administrator maximizes comfort can destroy the agency the intervention purports to serve. This supports a strong presumption against overriding an informed refusal in a matter principally concerning that person's life.

The presumption is a defended additional commitment. It is not a theorem derived from suffering alone. Emergencies, impaired decision capacity, and harms to others can change the reasons. The burden then includes specifying the exception, examining a less intrusive feasible response, and providing representation and review. Children, unconscious people, and beings unable to formulate objections retain their welfare claims; inability to authorize action does not authorize arbitrary action by others.

Answerability follows from another substantive judgment: exercised power over others requires justification responsive to the claims it affects. Private certainty that an intervention is beneficial cannot substitute for examining the affected person's reasons. An objection may ultimately be outweighed, but the objection must be allowed to alter the judgment when it reveals a relevant mistake. Otherwise the announced justification is insulated from the very facts it claims to answer.

Effective answerability has causal requirements: independent intake or representation, intelligible grounds, protection against retaliation where necessary, authority to amend conduct, and resources appropriate to the promised protection. These are the connection to the theory's access, liquidity, succession, and obstruction results. The [joint obstruction analysis](../../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/JOINT-OBSTRUCTIONS.md) explains why concern or correction need not select itself. Its failure to arise does not negate the reasons supporting it.

## What these grounds already settle

The kernel settles the following judgments relative to its defended commitments and accurate case descriptions.

| Question | Determinate consequence | Grounds still needed in an application |
|---|---|---|
| Can a dispensable subject be ignored solely because helping them contributes nothing to production? | No. Usefulness does not cancel an independently established harm reason. | Evidence of the subject and harm; actual competing claims and costs. |
| Can a label or jurisdiction remove an otherwise unchanged reason? | No. A changed conclusion needs a relevant changed ground. | The warranted comparison; whether a relationship actually supplies an additional reason. |
| Does scarce capacity make an unfulfilled claim disappear? | No. Scarcity restricts feasible satisfaction, not the existence of the claim. | Justification of allocation and of who created or could relieve scarcity. |
| Does silence establish absence of a claim? | No. It may reflect incapacity, exclusion, or missing evidence. | An appropriate investigation or representative route, proportionate to stakes. |
| Is greater measured comfort enough to justify overriding informed refusal? | No. Agency supplies an additional reason requiring an answer. | Capacity, information, voluntariness, external harms, and any justified exception. |
| Must every affected objection prevail? | No. Standing provides consideration and answerability, not an unlimited veto. | The actual conflicting claims and the grounds for the eventual choice. |

The word “settled” here means that the conclusion follows under the stated grounds and survives the specified counterexamples. It does not mean that a new fact cannot defeat a case description, or that the ethical commitments have become unrevisable dogma.

## Evidence of patienthood and uncertainty

A proposed patienthood judgment has two parts: a criterion explaining why the candidate's condition could supply a reason, and evidence that this candidate meets it. Suffering-based concern needs evidence of valenced experience, not mere complexity, continued existence, intelligence, economic contribution, linguistic fluency, or goal optimization. Evidence can be indirect without being worthless. The required explanation of why experience exists is also distinct from the evidence that a particular being has it.

The 2021 primary review by Birch and colleagues supplies an example of an explicit, revisable evidence framework. It combines neural and behavioral indicators, warns that no one indicator is conclusive, and distinguishes weak research coverage from evidence against sentience. Its confidence grades are not numeric probabilities of consciousness. This supports the methodological distinction between uncertain attribution and settled absence; it does not establish patienthood for every system with superficially similar behavior. [Original report, executive summary and sections 1.6–1.7](https://www.lse.ac.uk/asset-library/news/sentience-in-cephalopod-molluscs-and-decapod-crustaceans-final-report-november-2021.pdf).

The authors' updated cephalopod assessment, published on 20 January 2026 and covering evidence through 2024, uses eight criteria and reports strong evidence for octopuses and cuttlefish, substantial evidence for squid, and an unresolved case for nautiluses because research is sparse. The authors explicitly leave room for expert judgment rather than presenting an automatic consciousness detector. These are the review's scoped assessments, not proof of the metaphysical origin of experience. [Schnell and colleagues, abstract and sections I–II](https://doi.org/10.1002/brv.70125).

The proposed decision discipline is to maintain separately: supported inclusion; a credible unresolved claim requiring precaution or further inquiry; and a currently unsupported claim. This is an evidential status, not a permanent ranking of kinds of beings. A claim should enter the unresolved set through a stated argument or relevant evidence, not merely because any possibility can be imagined. Excluding or retaining it must be answerable to counterevidence; the standard must not be changed opportunistically with the convenience of the candidate.

Uncertainty does not always determine a unique precaution. If costless relief helps on a live patienthood account and is no worse on the alternatives, the finite dominance result below supports relief without a precise probability. If precaution itself causes competing serious harms, that premise fails. Probabilities, severity comparisons, rights, or another defended decision rule may then be necessary. Neither “uncertain, therefore zero” nor “conceivable, therefore overriding” follows from the kernel.

## A finite procedure for unresolved scope and conflict

The following is a proposed procedure, not an empirically validated institution. Its smallest mathematically complete component is proved in [decision mathematics](decision-math.md).

1. **State the decision and its deadline.** Include inaction, delay, information gathering, less intrusive alternatives, and available changes to the constraint. Identify actual resources, authority, and irreversible consequences. A delay is an action with its own costs.
2. **Record claims before ranking outcomes.** Identify each bearer or represented claimant, the specific reason, supporting and defeating evidence, consent, and relevant relationships. Preserve unmet claims. Do not infer absence from inability to bargain, speak, or help the system survive.
3. **Construct the retained model set.** Each model combines a live factual account with a reasoned normative ordering that respects the defended kernel. Distinguish factual uncertainty from ethical disagreement in the record. A model's membership requires grounds; no finite set can certify its own completeness.
4. **Check mandatory protections and feasibility.** If protections are accepted as inviolable for this decision, restrict to actions satisfying them across the retained models. If none exists, report that conflict and reconsider the assumptions, means, or priority rule. Do not quietly erase a protection or execute an empty choice set. The dominance theorem assumes a nonempty feasible set.
5. **Remove robustly dominated options.** Reject an option when another is at least as defensible under every retained model and strictly more defensible under at least one. Preserve the witness and each model's reasons. Remaining options are not thereby proven ethically right; they survived this particular comparison.
6. **Resolve the remaining choice explicitly.** Seek discriminating evidence or a justified priority if time allows. Where genuinely equal claims compete for an indivisible benefit and fair randomization is appropriate, use an impartial lottery. Where claims are incomparable, report the reason for the chosen procedure and its contested tradeoff. An emergency decision can be necessary before a unique ranking is established.
7. **Make the decision answerable after action.** Record who bears loss, the remaining grounds of disagreement, the conditions triggering revision, and the responsible route for correction. Unmet claims may support acknowledgment, mitigation, or repair; the particular duty needs its own argument, and a justified unavoidable loss is not automatically culpable wrongdoing.

An actual completed decision is a chosen action plus an intelligible justification, represented claims, implementation, and revision conditions. A maximal set alone is not a decision. This procedure guarantees only the finite comparison results under its input assumptions, not correct model selection, just authority, or successful implementation.

## Three exact results that make the procedure useful

Let A be a finite nonempty set of feasible actions. Every retained model m supplies a reflexive, transitive relation a ≥m b meaning that a is at least as defensible as b under that model. A model may leave pairs incomparable. Write a ≻ b when a ≥m b for all m and, for at least one model, b is not ≥m a.

**A nonempty remainder exists.** The relation ≻ is a strict partial order, so the undominated set is nonempty. Every excluded action has a retained dominating witness, possibly reached through a chain. The result needs neither a total ranking nor a shared numerical unit across persons or theories.

**Some uncertainty can be settled without a probability.** Suppose protection and injury differ only in a harm that exists under one warranted patienthood model. Every other retained model regards protection as no worse. Protection then dominates injury. Uncertainty about patienthood does not rescue the gratuitously harmful option. A cost, consent issue, other patient's loss, or evidentially live benefit of the alternative defeats the “no worse everywhere” premise and must be considered.

**Equal claims can require randomization.** If the full relevant structure is unchanged by swapping two equally situated claimants, a deterministic rule choosing exactly one cannot remain invariant under that swap. An equal lottery can. For n wholly symmetric claimants competing for exactly k places, invariance forces each claimant's marginal selection probability to be k/n. Under the full permutation symmetry, the unique invariant distribution over k-person subsets is uniform. This is a result about equal procedural chances, conditional on randomization's permissibility; it does not prove all scarce benefits should be allocated by lottery.

The proof is ordinary finite order and symmetry reasoning. Its advance within this theory is an explicit way to connect preserved, uncertain, plural claims to constrained decisions without pretending that propagation scores or market strength provide moral comparability.

## Counterexamples the settlement survives

**Impartial indifference.** Assign no reason to any being's suffering. The evaluator can be perfectly consistent under every renaming. This defeats an attempted derivation of positive concern from consistency alone. It does not refute the defended positive reason; it rejects that reason. The confrontation is ethical, not a graph problem.

**Paternalistic comfort.** An administrator improves a competent person's measured comfort by permanently removing informed refusal and independent projects. A welfare-only comparison might endorse the act. The agency commitment requires another coordinate; domination on comfort alone is therefore not robust domination over the whole relevant profile. This does not establish an absolute ban on every intervention without consent.

**Incomparable rescue.** One feasible action saves A and another saves B; the retained grounds establish each person's serious claim but no relevant priority. The kernel rules out treating the unchosen person as valueless. It does not produce a ranking by summing invented units. In the exactly symmetric version the lottery result applies; in an asymmetric version it does not decide the tradeoff without further reasons.

**Nonexperiencing nature.** Consider a stipulated uninhabited, nonexperiencing landscape with no indirect effect on any present or future subject. The suffering and agency premises do not decide whether destroying it for no purpose is wrong. Add an ecological integrity or natural-value premise and there can be a direct reason for preservation; retain only subject-directed premises and that direct reason is unestablished. Both extensions fit the kernel. Real ecosystems frequently have subject-directed and intergenerational stakes, but this logical example removes them deliberately. Relational dependency can ground duties concerning nature without establishing that a whole ecosystem is an experiencing patient.

**Unbounded precaution.** Protecting every merely imaginable patient at unlimited cost can obstruct protection of well-supported patients. The model set requires evidential and argumentative admission; significant competing burdens destroy the costless-relief comparison. The proposal retains precaution without a theorem that every speculative claim must dominate established ones.

**Identical observations and different patienthood.** If two possible underlying states give every available observation the same distribution while differing in patienthood, no method using only those observations can identify the state with certainty. This is a conditional information limit, not proof that actual patienthood evidence is always insufficient. It explains why action under uncertainty is a positive part of the theory rather than a temporary failure to think hard enough.

## The achieved settlement

We can now say more than “ethical foundations remain assumptions.” The kernel has explicit grounds, recognizable objections, and determinate consequences. It rejects usefulness-based erasure, arbitrary identity gates, silence-as-disproof, and the disappearance of claims through scarcity. It gives agency and answerability independent roles. It also supplies an exact finite comparison and an exact symmetry result for decisions made before every uncertainty is resolved.

The same analysis settles the limits of what this kernel entails. It permits multiple reasoned extensions on nonexperiencing nature and on conflicts lacking a priority relation. Their existence is a countermodel to a claim that these matters already follow from the kernel. It is not evidence that further ethical argument is impossible. The proper next step is to defend or defeat the needed additional ground, rather than repeat the entire foundational uncertainty.

The strongest present formulation is: **take a being's independently supported good or harm as a reason; carry that reason wherever its grounds continue to apply; keep conflicts and uncertainty visible; and make the resulting judgment answerable in action.** This is a substantive candidate foundation for justified concern. Its universal acceptance, complete moral truth, and historical prevalence have not been proved by the finite results.
