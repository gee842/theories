# Action-dependent futures: independent assumption and proof review

6 October 2026. Named connection: **justified protection → strategic stability when present action changes future opportunities or power**. This note initially records an independent audit before reviewing the mechanism draft. It supplies no new completion criterion, executable controller, empirical frequency estimate or claim of ethical value formation.

## Exact existing boundary

The [settlement report](REPORT.md), §1 and R2's persistent-opponent proof, explicitly holds future contexts and commercial opportunities independent of earlier harms, admissions and records. Temporary deviations remain in a funded region with equal candidate operator continuations. That equality is what permits current safeguard savings and attack prices to settle the comparison. Its R4 already prices a permanent intervention over the full discounted horizon, subject to no outside windfall. A counterexample that drops these premises does not refute the original theorem.

The [actual reason-protection construction](REASON-PROTECTION-MATH.md) already solves a finite game with action-dependent resource feasibility, preserves voluntary extra safeguards and includes future external inflows that can disappear after failed acquisition. It would be inaccurate to call the new connection the repository's first endogenous continuation result. The [development proposal](../../../2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/GOVERNANCE-CONTINUATION-GAP.md) identifies a narrower question: what replaces the older equal-continuation proof when actions also change future opportunities, oversight or control?

## Exact operator criterion and proof obligation

Fix all other actors' complete contingent policies. Let the public state be sufficient for legal actions, paid prefixes, transition probabilities and the operator's receipts; include resources, authority and relevant history where they affect those quantities. Let one transition cover one original discount period. An episode action must then represent a full feasible contingent operator plan inside that episode, rather than omit a later attack after an earlier suppression decision. Alternatively, retain the within-period decision tree explicitly without incorrectly discounting every internal move by the full per-period factor.

For candidate operator policy \(\pi\), use its actual discounted value \(V^\pi\), evaluated under those same dynamics and opponent policies. At a state \(x\) where \(\pi(x)=p\) prescribes protection, that choice is a best response to deviation \(a\) precisely when

\[
r(x,p)-r(x,a)\;\ge\;
\delta\bigl(P_aV^\pi-P_pV^\pi\bigr)(x).
\]

This local equivalence compares a one-period deviation followed by \(\pi\). To establish optimality against arbitrary longer strategies, require the Bellman inequalities at every state reachable under feasible deviations, with equality for \(\pi\). They cannot be checked only along the proposed protective path. Bounded receipts and \(0\le\delta<1\) make \(V^\pi\) bounded. Iterating the inequalities for any history-dependent deviation policy gives

\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\delta^t r_t\right]
\le V^\pi(x_0)-\delta^N\mathbb E[V^\pi(x_N)].
\]

The final term tends to zero, proving the operator best-response claim. This is a standard verification argument, not a novelty claim. A favorable number assigned to an off-path continuation is not its evaluation. A certified upper bound on deviation advantage can suffice, but its validity must cover the actual deviation-successor states and relevant opponent reactions.

Strict inequalities exclude the specified deviations; weak ones allow ties. Neither statement alone identifies which equilibrium is selected. State sufficiency, payoff interpretation and transition validity remain supplied premises in a finite application. If a hidden record, private signal or unknown rent changes behavior, it cannot be silently erased by calling the displayed state sufficient.

## Three exact diagnostic counterexamples

These are authored arithmetic examples, not fitted governance models or new experimental outcomes.

### 1. A present loss can purchase a better future

At ordinary state \(S\), protection costs 4 before receiving 7 and leaves \(S\) unchanged. An attack costs 5 before receiving 7, disables only the current safeguard and moves to state \(C\). There the sole continuing action protects at cost 4 before receiving 8. Starting cash 5 funds every prefix; every closing account increases. Above that level, the account does not change any action's availability or net receipt, so the two-state strategic description is sufficient. The net receipts are therefore 3, 2 and 4, with no disappearing future actuator or audit capacity. The current protective margin is 1, but

\[
V^\pi(S)=\frac3{1-\delta},\qquad
V^\pi(C)=\frac4{1-\delta},\qquad
Q^\pi(S,\text{attack})-V^\pi(S)
=-1+\frac\delta{1-\delta}.
\]

The attack is profitable for \(\delta>1/2\); at \(\delta=3/4\), it gives 14 against 12. Both paths remain fully funded. The extra future receipt is an explicit changed opportunity, not an unexplained reward for the desired action. This can represent a current intervention whose enforcement effect ends while its effect on later commercial access persists. Calling the physical cut temporary does not make its entire continuation temporary. A longer horizon can increase the attraction of control just as it can increase the value of cooperation.

For comparison with R4, if permanent capture saves \(C\) per period and additionally produces rent \(w\) only from the following period, its total gain before the capture price is \(C/(1-\delta)+\delta w/(1-\delta)\). The original no-windfall bound covers the first term. Repeating it while omitting the second would change its scope.

### 2. Checking the protective path misses a multi-period deviation

Let \(\delta=1/2\). Candidate \(\pi\) stays at \(S\), earning 1 per period, so \(V^\pi(S)=2\). A zero-receipt move reaches \(A\); candidate \(\pi\) then returns to \(S\), again for zero, giving \(V^\pi(A)=1\). The one-period move out of \(S\), followed by \(\pi\), is unattractive: its value is \(1/2\).

But at \(A\) another feasible zero-receipt action reaches \(C\), with receipt 10 forever. The two-step deviation has value \((1/2)^2\,20=5>2\). The missing inequality is at the off-path state \(A\). A complete Bellman certificate rejects the purported protection policy there. No repeated search over new gadgets is needed: the declared feasible state graph already contains the error.

### 3. An optimal response to an incredible threat is not an equilibrium

In a two-stage game, the operator can respect the arrangement and end with payoffs \((2,2)\), or attack. After attack, the trustee chooses punishment \((0,0)\) or tolerance \((3,1)\), with operator payoff listed first. Against a fixed punishment policy, respecting is optimal. At the actual later node the trustee strictly prefers tolerance, and the operator then strictly prefers attack. All actions can be feasible. The fixed-policy operator certificate is correct about its assumption but cannot supply that policy's credibility.

The same issue applies to promised future audits, withdrawal or enforcement. Their response must be feasible and optimal for the actor who actually chooses it under that actor's dated preferences. A binding commitment can change the game, but its existence, authority, cost and persistence must then be supplied and charged. A mathematical continuation policy is not itself a commitment technology.

## Whole-game and normative acceptance conditions

An operator certificate holds the other actors' policies fixed. A whole-game subgame-perfect claim additionally needs a single coherent strategy profile: cold and warm dated trustees optimize at their actual nodes, the persistent operator optimizes at all its nodes, and these choices induce the same transition/value system used in the certificate. Current withdrawal, alternative products, post-deviation repairs and denial cannot be dismissed by assuming that the intended protective continuation occurs regardless. For a finite per-episode tree, the supplied dated-preference structure can support backward induction within the episode and the discounted-tail argument across episodes. Imperfect information would require an explicitly different equilibrium treatment.

Protection also requires a feasible maintained path. A sufficiently unattractive attack does not fund the next audit; adequate funds do not make an attack unattractive. If a deviation destroys the ability to audit or act, either include that failure state in the value comparison or establish a valid bound that already covers it. Do not require that every imaginable deviation preserve viability merely to prove equilibrium protection: an unprofitable exit from the region may be excluded strategically. Conversely, an invariant only under the selected policy cannot silently certify protection after arbitrary imposed interference.

These incentive and feasibility checks retain the independently justified reason, standing, factual interpretation and legitimate authority. They do not derive those from cash flow, persistence or the observer's approval. The result can establish a conditional regime where supplied justified protection is stable, and delimit one where it is strategically undermined. It does not show that protecting a system's own future generally gives others adequate influence, nor that every failure of profitability defeats a valid ethical responsibility.
