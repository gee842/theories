# Review and verification

Prepared 5 October 2026. This records internal AI-assisted review, not external peer review.

## Completed work

- The mathematical review checked the simultaneous maintenance schedule, the conditions for translating calendar time into available work, branchwise generation, validation and installation costs, and the finite confidence bound. It found no blocking error under the stated assumptions.
- Independent exact checks reproduced 36 support-frontier comparisons and nine candidate outcome trees. The verifier also compares 37 deterministic fixed-batch protocols, including shorter attempt limits and no inquiry. Results are in [VERIFICATION.json](VERIFICATION.json), with the verifier's source hash.
- The source review checked the distinction between empirical findings, computational examples and conceptual frameworks. It requested a qualified opening: present usefulness is not generally sufficient for retaining access to future capabilities, rather than asserting that present usefulness is insufficient in every setting. This correction is included.
- The synthesis explicitly distinguishes unconditional bad-installation probability from the fraction of installations that are bad. The illustrative two-percent limit constrains the former.
- Existing project work was audited so that paid generation, validation, revision and inquiry maintenance are not presented as new discoveries of this continuation.

## Limits that remain

The candidate-quality distribution, observation laws, costs, viability floors and acceptance objective are supplied. The model does not generate new functions or purposes. The work-clock result assumes divisible, resumable work, immediate outcomes on completion and no unmodeled calendar-time restrictions. Spending inherited capacity down to its floor is a deterministic optimum within that model, not advice to eliminate real reserves.

The construction-and-succession study described in [ANSWER.md](ANSWER.md) is a proposed next experiment; it has not been run. Continued discoverability is a candidate cross-domain mechanism. Neither the calculation nor the cited studies proves a universal invariant of health, resolves whose futures should count, or shows that retaining every uncertain possibility is beneficial.
