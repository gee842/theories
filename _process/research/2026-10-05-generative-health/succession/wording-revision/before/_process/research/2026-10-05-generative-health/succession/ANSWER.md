# How inherited means preserve or obstruct further generation

Prepared 5 October 2026 for Yongxu Ren. This AI-assisted continuation completes a bounded construction-and-succession experiment, a matched-material access intervention, and a conditional mathematical analysis. It revises the previous [discovery account](../discovery/ANSWER.md) in response to contrary as well as supportive results.

The result constrains a particular arrangement-only score. It does not settle whether significance and a universal evaluative criterion can be explained within the activity itself. The [previous text](ranking-correction/ANSWER-before-correction.md), calculations and adverse results are preserved.

The strongest current answer is **the continued renewal of effective capacity to respond and reorganize, including the capacity to revise how that renewal happens**. The new finding sharpens “effective.” Preserving more material or more completed answers does not by itself preserve more capacity for further construction. What survives must be usable under the successor's actual resources and methods, and its manner of use may itself need to change.

This is a proposed causal core, not a proved universal definition of health. The calculations establish exact conditional resource constraints. The experiment establishes effects inside a supplied finite construction system. It does not derive the significance of its targets or the standing of affected beings. Their formation and justification remain questions within the wider generative account; this local omission does not place significance outside generation.

## A successful product can already contain productive means

The earlier contrast between passing on a discovery and passing on discoverability needs qualification. A finished product can expose how to make or modify it. A readable program contains components a successor can extract. Whether inherited material is generative depends on what the successor can recover and do with it, rather than on whether we call it a product or process. Human transmission experiments have found improvement from access to completed products and their performance alone under the tested conditions. [Caldwell and Millen 2009](https://cognitionandculture.net/wp-content/uploads/Social_Learning_Mechanisms_and_Cumulativ.pdf).

Accordingly, the new baseline receives the complete successful program and can reuse every subcomponent. Additional arms inherit intermediate constructions outside that source. They receive the same initial endowment and external resource flows, and all pay for what they retain, construct, verify and install. No arm receives a future answer or a better synthesis algorithm.

Every generation starts from the serialized package actually left by its predecessor. The earlier private search state is gone. Four changed-task episodes test whether successive learners can construct and install responses while carrying resources and material onward. A failed new task does not magically kill the old operating artifact or erase later opportunities to recover.

## Retention helps some responses and impedes others

The [completed experiment](../../../../experiments/constructive-succession/README.md) uses a small circuit language to separate material, construction, checking and inheritance. Its 7,740 configuration trajectories comprise 30,960 successor episodes; randomized comparisons use 64 paired task-sequence seeds, not thousands of independent real-world observations. Repeated, locally changed and independent target families are reported separately.

One instructive comparison uses related tasks and the same reserve rule. With a work allowance of 300 per generation, source-only inheritance completes 26 of 256 tasks; retaining candidates resembling the preceding task completes 42. But the latter makes only two new compositions, compared with eleven for source only. Its advantage is largely that a later answer is already available.

With allowance 1,200, the same comparison reverses. Source only completes 62 tasks, including 47 new compositions; similar-candidate inheritance completes 45 tasks, including only three new compositions. More ready answers coexist with fewer successful constructions. Different arms face different numbers of tasks absent from their inherited repertoire, so this decomposition alone does not isolate a causal effect on new construction.

The complete grid prevents a one-sided reading. Some other inheritance settings improve construction as well as retrieval. In a related-task setting with higher archive price and no reserved search fraction, diverse inheritance makes 62 new compositions against the baseline's 48. The higher price changes the retained package and subsequent search, so this is not a recommendation to increase preservation costs. On repeated tasks every arm succeeds and extra retention can consume resources without any corresponding task benefit.

The tested retention and access policies change relative performance across the declared conditions. These exploratory outcomes challenge a stronger and tempting hypothesis: that giving a successor more useful-looking means should automatically improve its capacity to generate another answer.

## Preserving material and activating it are different operations

The next intervention holds inherited source, cached computations and starting resources fixed. It changes only whether additional inherited components automatically enter combinatorial search. Both versions can retrieve an exact answer from the same archive. Dormant components can also be reconstructed through the active repertoire; the primitive construction interface remains available.

For one diverse package, under the same budget, exhaustive testing of all 256 possible next functions gives:

| Outcome | Number of target functions |
|---|---:|
| Both access rules solve the task | 50 |
| Only activating all extras solves it | 17 |
| Only leaving extras dormant solves it | 15 |
| Neither solves it | 174 |

These are exact finite results for those algorithms and costs. The two methods inherit the same material but make different constructions reachable before their budgets run out. Automatic use can provide a shortcut or delay a useful construction behind many other combinations. Neither rule dominates; a nearly equal total can conceal very different reachable functions.

The full access study checks 3,072 matched pairs across three packages, two work allowances and two reserve rules. It changes access for one episode; the onward packages can subsequently diverge. It does not supply a learner that knows in advance which mode will succeed.

This supports a more precise distinction: **stored availability, active use, successful execution, and onward transmission are separate causal stages**. Their relationship can matter as much as the amount retained. Related program-learning research distinguishes retaining definitions from admitting all of them into search; the present calculation is a small mechanism test, not a discovery of that general issue. [Cropper 2020](https://andrewcropper.com/pubs/aaai20-forgetgol.pdf).

The apparent paradox does not overturn the value of genuinely optional, free capabilities. If a system can keep using every old policy at the same cost and gains more choices, its feasible policy set cannot shrink. The experiment changes a fixed policy's enumeration or pays for storage. It therefore separates what a richer system could do from what its actual organization makes it do. This distinction was already present in the [root foundation](../../2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/ROOT-FOUNDATION.md).

## The invariant concerns delivered continuation

The mathematical companion asks a stricter question than how many tasks one successor can solve. How much reserve is needed to answer a task, pay the entire transition, and leave another successor capable of doing the same?

Let `z` record the actually delivered productive configuration: tools, valid evidence, interfaces and relevant environmental conditions. For a supplied task `q` and feasible response policy `a`, let `c` be the peak resource draw, `d` the net expenditure after real receipts, and `f` the delivered next configuration. With the task announced before the response is chosen, the exact minimum reserve satisfies

\[
H_0(z)=0,\qquad
H_{n+1}(z)=\max_q\min_a\max\{c(z,q,a),\ d(z,q,a)+H_n(f(z,q,a)),\ 0\}.
\]

The first constraint pays for the present episode; the second finances the successor's remaining obligations. For the declared finite model, the increasing limit `H_infinity` gives an indefinite-succession certificate. The [proof](MATHEMATICAL-CORE.md) is an application of established viability and resource dynamic programming, not a new mathematical foundation invented here.

A simple constructed example makes its meaning concrete. Two processes give the same current answer, start with nine resource units, and spend five on their first response and handoff. Each completed episode subsequently receives three units. One leaves successors that each need five units again. It completes only three episodes before its reserve is too small. The other first handoff leaves a productive interface that makes every later supplied task and handoff cost at most three. It can continue indefinitely in this model. Their first answer, first cost and external provision match; what differs is what the first handoff makes possible afterward.

Those costs are stipulated to exhibit the distinction. They are not fitted circuit results. The infinite conclusion follows from a checked inductive certificate, not from observing several successes. Independent enumeration checked 49,356 contingent policies and 394,008 task rollouts for the general recurrence, plus rational-cost cases and 2,100 stationary comparisons with both pre-work and post-work receipts. All passed. [Verification record](RESERVE-VERIFICATION.json).

## What this says about the different lenses

Your original distinction between compression and lenses becomes clearer here. The lenses ask different questions about the same proposed continuing relationship. They need not expose the same variables, and they are not interchangeable measures.

| Lens | What it brings into view |
|---|---|
| Maintenance | Whether indispensable supports remain available before their loss matters |
| Learning and correction | Whether consequences can change the distinctions and responses guiding action |
| Construction | Whether actual means can make a previously unavailable response executable |
| Memory and inheritance | What successors can recover, reuse, question and afford |
| Selection and authority | Which available changes actually receive support and enter operation |
| Shared health | Whose continuation and standing are protected, and who bears the costs |

The candidate common relationship is that activity contributes to the conditions for further activity, including changes in how those conditions are organized. A faithful lens preserves the intervention questions it claims to answer. A present-performance lens need not preserve the information required by a construction or succession lens. This is why their differences should not all be compressed away.

The access experiment gives a precise limit on a fixed arrangement-only score required to reproduce every task-success ranking. Let `S_A` and `S_D` be the sets of tasks solved by the active and dormant policies at the same budget. We observe a task in `S_A` but not `S_D`, and another in `S_D` but not `S_A`. If the next-task distribution puts all its probability on the first, A has higher success probability; concentrating it on the second reverses the ranking. Therefore no fixed strict ordering of these two policies can reproduce both comparisons while ignoring the task distribution. Declaring them tied also erases those strict differences. A universal criterion applied to whole situations could yield different judgments when the actual relations change. The result neither refutes nor establishes such a criterion.

The shared generative relationship must be investigated through what the lenses preserve and omit, rather than inferred from the rank reversal. In this experiment, candidate construction, acquired tables, installed source and paid inheritance already belong to one history. The target actually regulates search and installation, but why satisfying it matters to an organization or recipient is not explained; incoming work is supplied independently of task fulfillment. The [reconstruction](NONDUAL-EVALUATION.md) distinguishes that representational omission from a foundational failure and connects it to earlier work in which the same consequence participates in acquired understanding and renewed support. Core and tree are differentiated articulations of that activity, and branch findings can revise the core's articulation.

The new experiment also blocks a shortcut from this relationship to maximizing openness. More simultaneous active possibilities can exhaust a search budget; a temporary restriction can make further response achievable. Conversely, permanent restrictions can eliminate the needed route. The relevant question is what remains practically recoverable and revisable under the actual circumstances, rather than whether every possible component is active at once.

## The current answer and the remaining boundary

The proposed core can now be expressed as **activity renewing effective conditions of further becoming, with those conditions themselves open to consequential revision**. Its content lies in identifiable causal relations: what supplies support, what is retained, how it becomes accessible, how alternatives are assessed and executed, and what successors actually receive.

Calling a continuation healthy requires an adequate account of affected futures. An inventive, self-renewing process can still dominate or exhaust others. This finite mechanism does not establish which interests count or how their significance is formed and justified. The wider inquiry investigates those relations within generative activity while preserving actual differentiated consequences. A particular institution may end while its obligations continue through other arrangements; the account must explain who and what actually continue.

The construction study completes a bounded part of the earlier proposed test. It establishes paid construction and transfer through four successors and isolates an access mechanism. It does not reproduce the base learner, resource source or authority structure, which remain supplied. It does not create new purposes or prove response to unrestricted novelty. Those are specific remaining explanatory boundaries, not reasons to call finite success a universal law.

The immediate theoretical correction is nevertheless substantive: **a future is maintained through workable, revisable relations among means, activity and successors. Neither the successful product, the preserved archive, nor the number of nominal alternatives can stand in for that relationship.**
