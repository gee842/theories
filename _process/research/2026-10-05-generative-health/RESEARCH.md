# Primary evidence: the organization of generative health

**Continuation, 6 October 2026:** [Recipient consequence and supported continuation](succession/RECIPIENT-CONTINUATION.md) grounds a conditional enabling claim in an independently identified recipient activity. The [subsequent correction analysis](succession/CONTINUITY-AND-CORRECTION.md) proves a source-level prefix invariant for the existing plasticity history and derives when selection among its routes cannot preserve the specified continuation. It credits the earlier liquidity and protocol results, adds a prospective buffer condition, and distinguishes adequacy failure, ethical warrant and implemented revision.

Prepared 5 October 2026. Scope: causal arrangements that sustain adaptive and generative capacity while their implementations change. This is a focused evidence memo, not a systematic review or a claim to have established a universal law.

## Strongest supported answer

The strongest candidate is **the continuing production of the capacity to renew and revise the conditions of continuation**. The relevant invariant would be a maintained causal relationship, rather than fixed components, a fixed identity, maximal options, or an ever-growing quantity.

A more mechanistic conjecture is: **supported freedom to vary, consequential correction, and incorporation of workable changes into the support network**. The system must keep producing the resources and organization that make these operations possible. The conjunction is the proposed synthesis here; the sources below establish or argue for different parts, under different scopes.

## Primary-source ledger

### S1. Mutual maintenance can persist while its particular realization changes

Montévil, M. and Mossio, M. (2015), *Biological organisation as closure of constraints*, Journal of Theoretical Biology 372:179–191. [Author full text](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2015-mm-organisation-closure-constraints/). Relevant locations: §§2, 5–7; especially the conclusion on invariance and variation.

**Source claim and status:** Their theoretical framework distinguishes material processes from relatively stable constraints that channel them. Closure consists in constraints contributing to one another's maintenance. Stability belongs to this reciprocal organization, while specific functions can be acquired, modified, or lost. Their proposed invariant is closure across relevant time intervals, not one permanently fixed network. Thermodynamic openness remains essential. Functional novelty must enter maintainable relationships if it is to endure.

**Limit:** This is a proposed biological characterization, not a proof that any directed cycle is productive, every organism satisfies the account, or every healthy social system must instantiate it.

### S2. Continued existence does not imply the capacity to counter deterioration

Di Paolo, E. A. (2005), *Autopoiesis, adaptivity, teleology, agency*, Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences 4:429–452. [Author PDF](https://ezequieldipaolo.net/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/autopoiesis_teleology_2005.pdf). Relevant locations: pp. 435–440 and 441–443.

**Source claim and status:** Di Paolo argues that autopoiesis alone admits persistence without active correction. Adaptivity distinguishes tendencies toward or away from viability limits and acts on them. Recursion includes responding before emergency regulation is needed and adjusting the regulators themselves. Fatigue, depletion of adaptive resources, and conflicting regulation can therefore be failures while the organism remains alive. Recovery can change the organism's viable relations rather than restore its previous form.

**Limit:** This conceptual account supplies graded functional norms, not a universal optimum or an ethical theory. Its explicit adaptivity definition is qualified to some circumstances; it does not promise survival against arbitrary perturbations.

### S3. A concrete architecture for change without continually disrupting the production core

Bich, L., Mossio, M., Ruiz-Mirazo, K. and Moreno, A. (2016; online 2015), *Biological regulation: controlling the system from within*, Biology & Philosophy 31:237–265. [Author PDF](https://leonardobich.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/bich-mossio-ruiz-mirazo-moreno-2015-biological_regulation-controlling-the-system-from-within-biology-philosophy.pdf). Relevant locations: author manuscript pp. 13–19, 20–23; published DOI [10.1007/s10539-015-9497-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-015-9497-8).

**Source claim and status:** Their regulatory subsystem is produced by constitutive processes but partially decoupled from their immediate dynamics. Specific perturbations activate it to switch between viable operating regimes. This separation introduces degrees of freedom for changing control without each change requiring wholesale core reorganization. Decoupling alone is insufficient: regulation must contribute to the system that supports it. The authors illustrate this account with chemotaxis and the lac operon, then argue that selective control of novelty enables greater organizational complexity.

**Limit:** The broader claim about open-ended complexity is theoretical. This architecture covers their restricted definition of biological regulation, not every possible realization of adaptation or every social institution.

### S4. Variation and maintenance are mutually enabling, with destructive cases

Montévil, M., Mossio, M., Pocheville, A. and Longo, G. (2016), *Theoretical principles for biology: variation*, Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology 122:36–50. [Author full text](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2016-mmp-theoretical-principles-variation/). Relevant locations: §§4.1–4.4 and 6.

**Source claim and status:** Their proposed principles treat organisms as historical and context-dependent. Closure makes constraints sufficiently stable for activity, but its validity is not tied to a fixed inventory of constraints. Existing organization may erase a local variation, incorporate it, or amplify destabilization. Organization supports variation; variation can support continued organization under changing conditions. They explicitly leave the full account of simultaneously restricting and undergoing variation as a scientific challenge.

**Limit:** Their account is a theoretical program, not a demonstrated impossibility theorem for fixed-state models. It does not imply that more variation is better or that every innovation preserves organization.

### S5. Preserving the existing regime can conflict with preserving a worthwhile future

Walker, B., Holling, C. S., Carpenter, S. R. and Kinzig, A. (2004), *Resilience, adaptability and transformability in social–ecological systems*, Ecology and Society 9(2):5. [Full paper](https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol9/iss2/art5/main.html). Relevant locations: “Components of Adaptability,” “Transformability,” and “Closing Comments.”

**Source claim and status:** They distinguish remaining within an existing regime, influencing its resilience, and creating a different system when the old one becomes untenable. Transformation may change the defining variables and scale. Resilience can preserve an undesirable trap, so reducing it may be beneficial. They propose diversity, trust, institutional variety, and communication across scales as possible supports for transformation, explicitly calling these suggestions speculative.

**Limit:** The stability landscape is often a metaphor, and the authors acknowledge limited formal measurability. Their perspective supports distinguishing continuation from transformation; it does not establish a sufficient universal mechanism for successful transformation.

### S6. A viable present and an attainable viable future are distinct

Martin, S. (2004), *The cost of restoration as a way of defining resilience: a viability approach applied to a model of lake eutrophication*, Ecology and Society 9(2):8. [Full paper](https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol9/iss2/art8/main.html). Relevant locations: “The viability kernel,” “Viability niches,” “Disturbance cost and resilience,” and discussion.

**Source claim and status:** In the specified model, states meeting current constraints can nevertheless have no admissible trajectory that keeps meeting them. The viability kernel isolates states with at least one such trajectory over the chosen horizon. Available controls alter this set; recovery costs add a graded resilience measure. Merely observing acceptable present output can therefore miss future infeasibility.

**Limit:** These are mathematical and model-specific results conditional on chosen constraints, dynamics, controls, and horizon. Existence of a trajectory is weaker than implementation, robustness to unmodeled disturbance, or demonstrated generative capacity.

### S7. Maintaining futures can operate through inherited environmental changes

Laland, K. N., Odling-Smee, F. J. and Feldman, M. W. (1999), *Evolutionary consequences of niche construction and their implications for ecology*, PNAS 96:10242–10247. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC17873/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.96.18.10242). Relevant retrieved sections: introduction and discussion; model results checked against the authors' abstract and discussion, not independently rederived.

**Source claim and status:** Their two-locus population-genetic model lets niche construction interact with independent resource renewal and depletion. Environmental modifications feed back on selection, altering trajectories and equilibria, including creating or removing polymorphisms. The analysis gives a causal route by which organisms shape conditions encountered by descendants, beyond direct genetic transmission.

**Limit:** Environmental construction is not necessarily beneficial to every participant or scale. These conditional model results do not identify a universal norm of health or guarantee adaptation under arbitrary external change. Full PMC retrieval was intermittent; no claim here depends on independently checking the paper's algebra.

## Synthesis into causal conditions

The following are proposed application criteria, not seven universally necessary biological laws. Each needs an independently specified system, timescale, set of relevant disturbances, and account of whose continuation matters.

| Candidate condition | Causal question an application must answer | Support |
|---|---|---|
| Recurrent provision | What replaces what is consumed, damaged, lost, or forgotten, including the providers themselves? | S1 |
| Corrective access | Which consequential differences can affect the selection of an effective response soon enough? | S2 |
| Supported alternative organization | What can vary or be switched without immediately destroying the activity that supports the change? | S3 |
| Retention through support | How does a useful change acquire the resources, dependencies, and reproduction required to persist? | S1, S4 |
| Revision of correction | What detects exhausted, misleading, or conflicting regulation and changes it? | S2 |
| Reachable transition | Can the system get from the present arrangement to a workable successor under its actual limits? | S5, S6 |
| Environmental continuation | Which enabling conditions are inherited, replenished, or degraded beyond the selected boundary? | S7 |

“Freedom plus correction” alone is incomplete: a trial can reveal a better arrangement after the means of reaching it have been exhausted. “Renewal plus feedback” is also incomplete: a maintained controller can reliably restore a harmful regime. The hypothesis needs both continuing realizability and a justified account of what is being protected. That normative choice cannot be recovered solely from the existence of a maintenance loop.

Three important distinctions follow. First, **available futures and the production of future availability differ**: a stock of options can coexist with loss of the ability to regenerate it. Second, **growth and generativity differ**: producing more output can erode the conditions of later production. Third, **a change of embodiment need not be loss of the relevant capacity**, but the bearer of that capacity must be specified. An institution dissolving into successor institutions and a person surviving bodily change are not automatically the same kind of continuity.

## What would strengthen or falsify the proposed answer

Compare systems with similar present outputs and resource stocks but different support for correction and revision. Apply novel disturbances, then measure retained functional performance, restoration of reserves, successful changes of operating regime, and performance under a later distinct disturbance. Match exposure, external subsidy, and time horizon. A genuine generative advantage should reappear after turnover and further change; a one-time buffer or emergency rescue is not the same result.

The proposed mechanism is weakened if continued adaptive performance is explained entirely by outside replacement, fixed reserves, or an unchanged preprogrammed repertoire when endogenous revision was claimed. Conversely, a successful application would not prove universal necessity. It would establish a particular causal realization and help identify which parts of the synthesis are indispensable in that domain.

The reviewed sources do not derive moral goodness from persistence, demonstrate a universal scalar health measure, or prove that all these perspectives are equivalent. They make the research question more concrete: **what organization keeps the means of correction and reorganization available, effective, and supported through the changes they help bring about?**
