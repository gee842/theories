## What was missing

The [recipient-correction study](../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/RECIPIENT-CORRECTION.md) acquires separate actor and recipient correspondences. Its noiseless teaching rule labels a failed action's opposite as correct. When an opportunity can be absent, a faithfully observed zero cannot distinguish the wrong action from an unavailable opportunity. Better recording alone does not resolve that ambiguity.

The new [protocol](../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/selectors/updaters/prediction/recipient-correction/identification/PROTOCOL.md) makes a narrower inference. A zero leaves the recipient correspondence unknown and changes the next exploration action. A positive recipient receipt qualifies the action just performed, under a stable binary target and a plant that cannot return support for the wrong action. The same qualified record then governs a later paid action. Missing evidence leaves the record unchanged and is distinct from an acquired zero.

This does not derive the relevant sensor or select its architecture through a private reward. It supplies an identified inference under explicit plant and channel laws, and shows why the stronger inference from every failure is unsound.

## Results and their limits

All primary probabilities include failed preparation and ineligible endpoints. With recipient availability three quarters, the positive rule's strict history—unknown after failure, then positive identification, then productive qualified use—has probability `1/32`; its noiseless counterpart is `1/18`. These are finite fixture probabilities, not empirical frequencies or universal rates.

The positive rule and the failure-as-complement comparator have the **same** primary expected recipient captures, `11/96`, and the same private resource accounts. Their difference is epistemic: final false qualification has probability zero for the positive rule and `7/288` for the failure-label rule; expected wrong qualified recipient commands are zero and `1/72`. The latter's zero count under the strict history predicate is partly definitional: it labels the first failure as qualified. It must not be used to claim that comparator never helps.

A conventional finite-state table matches the positive rule exactly. In the same-target case, keeping the initial candidate without exploration gets `5/24` expected recipient captures against the explorer's `3/16`. An initially correct action can be delayed by exploration following an outage. The expensive-write control can identify a response but cannot afford its later qualified use. A target change after identification produces stale qualified commands. No opportunity and missing channels leave the positive rule unqualified.

No finite all-zero record supports total zero-error identification: both target orientations can generate it during outages. A conditional opportunity floor gives a tail bound on remaining unqualified after completed matching trials, but the trials' funding and completion remain separate obligations. Finite stocks do not license an asymptotic guarantee.

The [recording-fidelity result](RECORDING-FIDELITY-INTEGRATION.md) and this result have different inference targets. Selectively retaining successes is generally invalid for estimating a Bernoulli success frequency as though the retained sample were complete. Here a positive receipt certifies a deterministic orientation under a stipulated no-false-positive law. It estimates neither availability nor benefit magnitude. There is no contradiction, and the two storage implementations have not thereby been composed.
