# Prospective independent review of the retrospective analysis

6 October 2026. Accepted for the declared computation, before either new outcome aggregation. This is an AI-authored independent review within this repository, not a review independent of prior knowledge of the published paper. Final numerical and source-correspondence acceptance remains pending.

The reviewed [protocol](../../../../experiments/recipient-value-learning/PROTOCOL.md) fixes Experiment 3, the author-eligible sample, participant-level target-liked selections divided by 32 offered trials, equal weighting of the two current-contingency strata, and participant-cell Welch–Satterthwaite uncertainty. It fixes three sensitivities before computation. Missing responses are no liked selection, not disliked choices; the response fractions remain visible. Individual participants, not repeated trials, supply the uncertainty units.

The [identification argument](IDENTIFICATION.md) correctly separates the selected-sample training contrast from a full randomized population effect. Eligibility uses post-assignment response and attention measures. Including all 385 saved respondents tests sensitivity to those exclusions but cannot recover unsaved or unobserved participants. Awareness is measured after choice and is restricted to descriptive frequencies; it does not select the primary population. No nonsignificant result is an equivalence result.

The offered-denominator coding identity is correct: personally optimal responding equals liked responding when current reward favors the liked option, and response fraction minus liked responding otherwise. Thus the equally standardized unchanged-minus-reversed optimal contrast is the primary liked-response contrast minus one half of the incongruent-current response-rate contrast. The answered-denominator identity requires nonzero individual denominators. Any optimal-choice interval must use the participant's actual optimal-response summaries directly, preserving the covariance between response and liked rates. Neither identity reproduces the paper's mixed-logistic coefficient.

The identification note supplies a valid finite-history observational-equivalence counterexample. A change in the target-value term can be exchanged for a change in unobserved private-payoff expectations while retaining identical conditional choice probabilities, the response process and task feedback law. The bounded-expectancy version requires the stated interior slack. This defeats unique identification within that model class; it does not establish which mechanism humans use. Fixed objective payoffs are not fixed subjective knowledge, and recorded realized rewards do not directly measure expectations.

The source inspection supports independent A/B and C/D assignments and a shuffled fixed 24/8 feedback allocation. The new analysis must not substitute independent per-trial reward draws. It concerns the same trained target in new scenarios, not new-recipient generalization or valuation arising from zero. The described recipient gift and own lottery incentives are instructions, not a verified fulfillment ledger. A behavioral effect would not itself justify an ethical ground, demonstrate felt concern, or establish a universally favorable direction.

The source metadata audit reconstructs 333 eligible participants among 385 saved complete respondents without the new choice aggregation. Its provisional clean-ID map must still be verified against the actual raw/clean trial records. The independent checker will reconstruct eligibility, trial coding and all fixed participant contrasts without importing the producer. Every unexpected source discrepancy is to remain visible; agreement with a target count is not permission to change a rule.
