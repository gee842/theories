# How others come to matter: an explanatory architecture for learned, widening, sustainable concern

Research memo, 5 October 2026. Prepared for the concern-theory investigation. This is a constructive architecture, two elementary formal discriminators, and four selected empirical anchors. It is not a claim that a complete empirical or metaphysical theory has already been established. No canonical files are edited.

## 1. The central advance

The theory should explain three achievements separately and connect their mechanisms:

1. **Another's condition acquires motivational force.** It can influence action when improving it produces no current private benefit.
2. **That concern becomes portable.** It extends to an untrained recipient because of features of their situation that the learner treats as reasons, rather than because the recipient happens to share a familiar identity.
3. **Concern becomes answerable and renewable.** The learner can revise what helping means, respect another's agency, resolve competing claims, and sustain these capacities under cost, changing conditions, and succession.

A learned association can contribute to the first. Generalization across situations can contribute to the second without completing it. Neither alone supplies justice or durable correction. This separation makes a positive theory possible: each bridge becomes an identifiable learning or organizational problem rather than an unexplained increase in a scalar called moral value.

The explanatory subject remains the differentiated situation. A center's capacity to perceive, value, and act develops through bodily regulation, interaction, shared practices, and material support. It is nevertheless useful to locate a causal mechanism in a particular nervous system, relationship, or institution. Locating a mechanism does not make that center self-sufficient or collapse the others into it.

## 2. What “complete” can responsibly mean

A complete architecture for a specified class of systems must leave no indispensable transition disguised as a favorable label. For each transition, it should name its inputs, carrier, update process, costs, output, failure modes, and an observation that distinguishes it from its nearest rival. It must say which parts are established, which are model assumptions, and which are open hypotheses.

That is **explanatory and interface completeness**, not omniscience. A proposed architecture can be complete in this sense while its mechanisms remain only partially confirmed. A fully established theory additionally needs evidence for those mechanisms, scope conditions, predictive adequacy, and competing explanations. Neither kind of completeness licenses a claim to have solved consciousness or proved every moral judgment.

There are at least six distinct closure obligations:

| Obligation | What must be explained | What would fail to close it |
|---|---|---|
| Existence of stakes | What can go better or worse for a center, and whether any of it is experienced | Calling every self-maintaining process a suffering subject |
| Access to another | How the center distinguishes another's condition and preferences from its own projections | Treating its own distress or a welfare proxy as the other's whole reality |
| Acquisition of concern | Why an independently measured other-outcome begins changing action value | Inserting a positive coefficient for that outcome and declaring the result explained |
| Portability and reasons | Why concern transfers to a new recipient or why its scope is revised | Testing new situations involving only the same already-valued individual |
| Justification and conflict | Which claims deserve standing, how reasons are assessed, and how incompatible claims are handled | Equating reinforcement, social agreement, consistency, or persistence with moral correctness |
| Reproduction and correction | How the embodied and social carriers are funded, retained, taught, challenged, and repaired | Treating lifelong stability or succession as a free consequence of one successful choice |

The wider generativity theory already supplies important parts of the last obligation: source dependence, maintained carriers, reserves, deadlines, authority, and consequences that can actually change production. The present task adds the formation and revision of what those carriers are concerned about.

## 3. Four meanings of mattering, with two necessary intermediates

**Functional stakes** concern conditions for an organization to continue functioning. Their existence is relative to a specified organization and timescale. A controller can regulate a functional variable without that variable being felt. Functional dependence gives a causal reason for a system to respond; it does not establish the moral status of everything on which it depends.

**Felt stakes** concern what is experienced as good, bad, threatening, painful, welcome, or significant. The presence and character of experience cannot be read straight off a stock variable or a success score. In the present theory, felt life is a possible natural realization of stakes, not a metaphysical exception to nature. Its occurrence and scope remain independent explanatory and evidential questions.

**Recognized stakes** are another center's conditions as represented by a learner. Recognition can be accurate enough to support rescue, manipulation, or indifference. It can also be mistaken: what the observer calls relief may be silencing, restraint, or an unwanted gift.

**Direct concern** is a motivational relation in which the other's actual condition can count in favor of or against an action apart from its expected effects on the actor's private resources, reputation, or sensory comfort. This is a causal and counterfactual characterization, not a claim to read motives from one act. It does not require that helping feel unpleasant or that concern have no rewarding developmental origin. A historically acquired end can become a present end.

**Reason-responsive concern** can distinguish an appearance of benefit from a benefit, change its understanding of the recipient, and revise an exclusion or priority when presented with relevant considerations. Reasons are not merely whatever produces the largest affective response. The person can understand a consideration without being motivated by it, or act on it while feeling little sympathy.

**Justice** concerns standing, permissible treatment, distribution, procedure, responsibility, and authority when centers' claims interact or conflict. It is not simply a greater quantity of direct concern. A person can intensely care for someone while denying their voice; a fair rule can protect someone whom its administrator does not personally like. A just arrangement may limit one person's project to protect another, including the caregiver's own legitimate needs.

These are distinctions within one world. A causal history of a judgment and an argument for its validity answer different questions. Neither requires that the reasons or the reasoning exist outside nature.

## 4. A generative account with explicit inputs and transformations

The following is a proposed recurrent architecture, not a universal developmental ladder. Different routes can begin at different points, and later achievements can alter earlier ones.

### A. Relational anchoring

The starting conditions include a living or otherwise specified organization, primitive evaluative dispositions, capacities for perception and learning, and a surrounding practice of interaction. In human development, the empirical question is how existing bodily and social dispositions participate in the formation of helping and concern. It is not necessary to posit a newborn with a fully articulated impartial morality, nor is it legitimate to assume an entirely unevaluative learner and conceal the required learning signal.

A candidate mechanism is repeated joint regulation: another's actions alter what is possible for the learner; the learner discovers that its own actions alter another's activity; some such interactions acquire affective significance. Scaffolding can make an otherwise obscure need, action, or outcome learnable. This contributes an actual history of formation. It does not show that all concern must originate in expected personal gain.

The evolutionary origins of the initial dispositions remain a separate modeling obligation. Declaring them “evolved” names a historical process but does not identify the selection, construction, drift, or developmental mechanisms that produced their particular scope.

### B. Distinguishing the recipient from the cue

The learner builds a model connecting visible signs, testimony, circumstances, and actions to the recipient's condition. It must learn that a sign is evidence, not the valued object itself. A crying person can become quiet because they are relieved, afraid, unconscious, or no longer able to express themselves. Silence alone cannot discriminate these possibilities.

This model must also represent preference differences. Increasing the strength of concern while retaining a mistaken model can increase unwanted intervention. An account of concern therefore needs both valuation learning and recipient-model correction; neither is a substitute for the other.

### C. Acquisition of an other-directed end

Several routes are possible: affective coupling, learned associations, observing others' care, participation in a shared activity, identification with a recipient, or reflective adoption of a commitment. This memo does not assert that they reduce to one algorithm. Its primary empirical anchor below isolates one narrower associative route.

The conceptual point is that an initially instrumental or incidental relation can generate a value-bearing representation that subsequently influences action without the original benefit being expected. This can be a genuine change in what currently matters to the learner. It should not be redescribed as present egoism merely because its history included the learner's rewards. Conversely, a cached cue response is not yet demonstrated concern for the recipient's actual condition: revaluation and cue-versus-outcome tests are needed.

### D. Abstraction and widening

The learner can form a more abstract representation: the relevant feature may be relief of distress, an unfulfilled need, loss of opportunity, or a person's capacity to choose. The abstraction enables transfer beyond the original situation. But it may still include a gate such as “only family,” “only members,” or “only people who resemble me.” Cross-situation abstraction and cross-recipient standing are therefore different achievements.

A candidate widening mechanism combines varied encounters, accurate models of previously excluded centers, analogical comparison, and challenges to the exclusions in the learner's own explanations. When the learner says that a condition matters because it hurts, another case can expose whether the real operative rule is about pain, familiarity, rank, or affiliation. Revision can change the rule rather than merely intensify the original response.

This mechanism has direction only under stated conditions. Encounters can also train contempt or competitive delight in another's loss. Reasoning can defend a group boundary, and analogies can be rejected. Evidence must track which features actually control generalization.

### E. Reflective commitment and justice

A learner may endorse a reason even when it conflicts with immediate appetite or local approval. It can then use an articulated commitment to direct attention, plan, build habits, invite correction, and constrain opportunistic exceptions. The commitment becomes causally effective through those mechanisms; naming an endorsement does not make it effective by definition.

The justice question begins when the learner asks whose claims deserve consideration, which distinctions justify differential treatment, whose knowledge should correct its model, and who may decide. Other people are then possible sources of correction and practical claims, not merely inputs to a benevolent optimization.

Affected expression is indispensable evidence but cannot be the sole test of standing: infants, nonverbal beings, future persons, and people prevented from speaking cannot be excluded for failing to participate. Representation on their behalf needs revisable evidence, accountable interpretation, and an explicit account of uncertainty. Nor does having a voice automatically settle every conflict among voices.

### F. Sustainable reproduction

An acquired concern can fail through fatigue, insecurity, distorted information, loss of practical skill, peer pressure, institutional incentives, or replacement by successors who never acquired it. Sustainability therefore requires maintaining both the learner and the routes through which reality can correct the learner.

The relevant resources include attention, time, rest, material means, access to affected people, training, memory, and legitimate ability to act. Budgets should count the costs of care and correction instead of assuming inexhaustible virtue. Maintenance is not maximization of sacrifice: preserving the carer's capacities, permitting refusal and exit, and distributing work can be conditions of sustained concern.

At succession, one must test what was transmitted: compliance with a familiar script, an affiliation, a manipulable reward rule, a causal model, or portable and revisable reasons. Copies of behavior do not establish inheritance of the same capacity.

## 5. Two elementary formal results that expose rather than conceal the missing bridges

### 5.1 Acquiring a value without installing universal concern

Consider a deliberately minimal associative learner. Let x in {−1,+1} encode the represented affective valence of a particular recipient's condition, balanced across training trials. Let r be the learner's independently measured reward, with E[r | x] = κx. Fit the predictor θx with squared-error learning:

\[
\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\eta x_t(r_t-\theta_t x_t).
\]

For independent training draws, its conditional expected increment is

\[
E[\Delta\theta_t\mid\theta_t]=\eta(\kappa-\theta_t).
\]

Thus, for constant 0 < η < 2, the mean converges geometrically to κ from any finite initial mean. This is a statement about the mean; persistent stochastic noise need not disappear. If the acquired value θx subsequently enters choice even when the old reward contingency is absent, then positive training covariance can produce an acquired preference for that recipient's positive state. Reversing κ reverses the predicted acquired preference.

This is an illustrative construction, not the model estimated in the studies below. It isolates what has and has not been generated. The target coefficient need not be supplied initially. Nevertheless, the architecture must already permit representation of the recipient, association, and transfer from acquired value to action. Its transfer and persistence are testable assumptions. If the learner instead recomputes every choice solely from current private reward, no enduring other-directed end follows. If later updating simply erases the association, the account predicts loss, not moral retention.

The primitive reward here is not universal moral concern. But neither the origin of primitive evaluation nor the entire developmental acquisition of the representation is explained by this small model. Those remain declared inputs at this scale, with further mechanisms required at other scales.

### 5.2 Why familiar-recipient learning cannot identify wider standing

Let w describe an independently defined recipient outcome, and let g(j) be 1 for an in-group recipient j and 0 otherwise. Consider the learned value

\[
V(j,w)=\theta_Uw+\theta_Gg(j)w.
\]

If every training recipient has g(j)=1, all training predictions depend only on θU+θG. Any pair with the same sum produces exactly the same predictions and fitting loss. A wholly portable rule (s,0), a wholly group-gated rule (0,s), and mixtures are observationally indistinguishable on that training support. For a new recipient with g(j)=0, their values are respectively sw, 0, and θUw.

Therefore success with familiar recipients, even across many situations, does not identify portable concern. The conclusion requires no claim that people literally fit this linear model; it exhibits the general problem of hypotheses agreeing on training support but disagreeing outside it.

A regularizer or simplicity preference can select one solution, but then the inductive bias is part of the explanation. Widening can be investigated by exposing new recipients, varying irrelevant identities while holding relevant stakes fixed, and testing whether reason-giving changes the generalization rule. A model that builds recipient-invariance into its features may study the consequences of that commitment; it has not explained its acquisition.

## 6. Moral reason acquisition is more than preference updating

At least four changes can look like “learning to care more”:

1. **Factual correction:** I already oppose causing pain but learn that this practice causes it.
2. **Conceptual recognition:** I learn that an unfamiliar manifestation instantiates a concern I already accept.
3. **Scope revision:** I abandon a restriction that previously blocked an otherwise applicable reason.
4. **Evaluative or priority revision:** I come to accept a consideration, or change its weight against other considerations, after examining a conflict.

The first two need not increase the underlying moral weight. The third changes whom or what an existing reason can reach. The fourth can change the commitments themselves. Conflating these changes would make both the mechanism and the evidence opaque.

An account of acquisition should specify how alternatives are generated, which facts and examples are considered, how conflicts are represented, how criticism changes the judgment, and how the new judgment influences later action. It should also separate **being caused to accept a reason** from **having a good reason to accept it**. Persuasion by status, punishment, or propaganda can produce sincere acceptance. Sincerity is not its own justification.

The present normative proposal is that the reality of another's stakes cannot be annulled by a bare change of identity; differences in treatment require relevant differences, and decisions about another must remain answerable to evidence of their situation and agency. This is a substantive account of standing and relevance, not a theorem of associative learning. It does not require identical treatment, equal affect, or a single additive welfare function.

The proposal can be defended by arguments about consistency of the reasons one already offers, recognition of another's independent perspective, avoidance of arbitrary privilege, and the requirements of living together under shared constraints. These arguments are not identical, and no one makes every conceivable amoralist's disagreement logically impossible. A theory should present their strengths and contested premises rather than call agreement a deductive necessity.

This is compatible with moral reasons being real and discoverable. Distinguishing justification from learning history does not imply that reasons are arbitrary preferences. It prevents a causal finding about what humans can learn from being mistaken for a verdict about what everyone ought to value.

## 7. Four primary empirical anchors, with narrow claims

The following four studies support pieces of the architecture. They do not establish the whole architecture or a universal moral trajectory. Source descriptions are deliberately brief; the proposed mechanisms elsewhere in this memo are our reconstruction, not results attributed to these papers.

**Development through participation — Dahl, Goeltz, and Brownell (2022).** In a longitudinal experiment, 83 infants initially aged 6–9 months participated in up to ten weekly visits. Explicit encouragement and praise increased object-retrieval helping relative to control; the reported effect was small (η²=.02), with an additional age-dependent cleaning effect. This supports a causal contribution of scaffolding to emerging helping. It does not distinguish all possible motivations, demonstrate universal moral standing, or establish that social input creates concern from nothing. [Primary abstract and publication details](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34779506/).

**Internalization is not equivalent to added payment — Warneken and Tomasello (2008).** Twenty-month-olds previously given a material reward helped less in a later test than children given praise or no reward. This makes “more external reward always creates more retained helping” an inadequate account. The experiment supports a distinction between maintained helping and dependence on material reward. It does not prove that the initial motivation was unlearned, establish a complete motive diagnosis, or show that rewards always undermine care. [Original paper](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/AmericanPsychologicalAss/Warneken_Extrinsic_DevPsych_2008_1554561.pdf).

**A representation-to-value bridge — Zhang and Hackel (2025).** Four adult experiments associated a target's abstract affective states with participants' monetary outcomes. Reported empathy transferred to novel scenarios involving the trained target; manipulations had relatively small effects. Preference-based stimuli helped distinguish the target's state from the observer's own likes. In Experiment 3, the preregistered overall effect of contingency reversal on choice was nonsignificant (p=.131). An analysis specified as exploratory found a choice effect among participants aware of the learned contingency, with acknowledged measurement limitations. This supplies evidence for modulation of acquired target valuation, not a proof of its origin from zero, durable moral commitment, or transfer to untrained powerless recipients. The mechanism can also associate value with another's bad outcomes. [Original paper, especially pp. 15–19](https://www.hackellab.org/media/zhang-hackel-2025-psych-science.pdf).

**Subsequent raw-data qualification, 6 October 2026:** the [independently reviewed Experiment 3 reanalysis](../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/REWARD-LEARNING-AND-RECIPIENT-CHOICE.md) narrows the preceding interpretation for this choice task. Its 333-participant primary contrast is +0.50 percentage points, approximate 95% interval [−4.25, +5.24], with all three fixed sensitivity intervals spanning zero. This establishes neither equivalence nor uniquely acquired target value. A changed unmeasured private-payoff expectation can reproduce the same choice law in the declared countermodel class. The original account above remains as genealogy; a strong value-acquisition reading of Experiment 3 alone is not supported by this audit. Other experiments and measures in the paper were not reanalyzed. [Exact sources](../../../sources/32-mental-state-reward-learning-2026-10-06/README.md) and [integration](reward-association/INTEGRATION.md) preserve the scope and one unresolved awareness-count discrepancy.

**Revision through disagreement — Li and Tomasello (2022).** In a factorial study, 129 children aged 4–5.5 discussed allocation scenarios with a puppet. Disagreement increased context-sensitive equitable allocation in training and the subsequent same-session test. Requests for justification helped during training without a carryover effect. Evidence for improved allocation accompanied by valid articulated reasons was more limited. The study supports a role for interaction in changing judgments, not the conclusion that discussion necessarily produces justice. Its fairness tasks, artificial interlocutor, short timescale, and use of prevailing common-ground values limit stronger inferences. [Original paper, especially pp. 10–11](https://sites.duke.edu/tomasellolabduke/files/2023/09/Li-L.-Tomasello-M.-2022.pdf).

These findings are compatible without proposing a single reward law. Scaffolding can make an activity intelligible, payment can change what the activity is taken to be for, an abstract state can acquire affective value, and disagreement can expose a limitation in a rule. Their relative contribution and interaction remain empirical questions.

## 8. Rival pathways and the observations that separate them

| Apparent advance | Rival explanation | Discriminating observation or intervention |
|---|---|---|
| More helping | Expected reciprocity, punishment avoidance, reputation | Remove credible private return and audience while checking beliefs; test an actually costly choice |
| Less distress in the observer | Avoidance of an aversive cue | Make cue removal and actual relief separable; test preference for relief when concealment is cheaper |
| Concern learned through a shared activity | Affection for one partner or compliance with a script | Test another recipient, unfamiliar action, absent trainer, and a changed context separately |
| Generalized empathy | Same-target generalization with an identity gate | Vary recipient identity independently of independently assessed need and inability to repay |
| Helping persists without payment | Habit or frozen cue value | Change what actually helps; test revaluation and correction, not only repetition |
| Moral reasons are articulated | Fluent rationalization or social conformity | Test novel counterexamples, changed authorities, private choices, and willingness to correct a favored rule |
| High concern for welfare | Paternalistic projection | Give reliable evidence of different preferences or refusal; inspect how the intervention changes |
| Consistent impartial rule | Blindness to relevant differences | Vary needs, responsibility, agency, and feasible alternatives while preserving identity symmetry |
| Strong organizational retention | Enforced conformity or silenced dissent | Preserve costly challenge and track whether affected evidence can change the rule itself |
| Reduced harm per interaction | More total harmful activity | Measure interaction volume, distribution, severity, and cumulative harm as well as averages |

No single row identifies a motive infallibly. Convergent interventions, independently assessed outcomes, and explicit belief checks are more informative than reading concern directly from a donation, an emotion rating, or a fitted coefficient.

The most informative widening test crosses four changes: **new recipient, absent private return, meaningful cost, and contrary recipient evidence**. Success on the first three without the fourth may be generous paternalism. Success on the fourth without the first three may be accurate strategic management. A further delayed or succession test is needed for durability.

## 9. Paternalism is a structural failure, not insufficient intensity

Suppose an actor assigns high value to another's welfare but uses a false model of what benefits them. Increasing concern then strengthens the wrong intervention. Even an accurate prediction of one benefit may omit another person's agency, privacy, attachments, or conception of a worthwhile life. The repair may require changing the model, the dimensions counted, the authority to decide, or the available action set.

This is why the theory should not identify wisdom with maximizing a supplied welfare score. An affected center participates in determining what its situation means. That participation may be supported through testimony, choice, advocates, or careful evidence where direct expression is unavailable. It can fail through intimidation or control of the categories in which claims must be made.

Respect for agency does not give a frictionless solution to every emergency, incapacity, or conflict. The theory needs explicit burdens of evidence, least-intrusive alternatives, proportionality, and later review for exceptional interventions. The key architectural point is that a benevolent actor cannot certify the adequacy of its own concern solely by the strength of its benevolent intention.

## 10. What this adds to a theory of conscience

Conscience can be understood as a maintained capacity to register that an action or rule fails a claim the center recognizes, reconsider the reasons, and alter conduct. It need not be a supernatural judge or an infallible feeling. Its operative components can include attention, emotion, models of consequences, remembered commitments, comparison of cases, practical control, and social correction.

Its maturation has at least three distinguishable forms: more accurate recognition of stakes, less arbitrary restriction of their standing, and greater ability to enact and revise the resulting commitments. Progress in one can coexist with regression in another. Emotional intensity can rise while models worsen; moral vocabulary can become richer while actual correction is blocked.

The strongest positive hypothesis is therefore that **participation in a shared situation can transform what a center is responsive to, and reflective participation can transform the rules by which that responsiveness generalizes**. Those transformations can in turn preserve other centers' capacities to express, teach, create, and correct. This gives the larger regenerative theory a possible recursive mechanism: concern helps reproduce the encounters and capacities through which concern can become more adequate.

The same recursion can narrow. Affiliation can reproduce exclusion; reward coupling can reproduce hostility; authority can reproduce obedience; institutions can make corrective speech unaffordable. The favorable loop needs enabling conditions that can be measured independently of calling its outcome wise. Their formation, competition, and maintenance are the proper subject of a direction hypothesis.

## 11. Gap ledger and a concrete research program

| Bridge | Present explanatory position | Next decisive evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Functional to felt stakes | Distinguished, not solved by renewal dynamics | Independent theory and evidence for experience; do not infer it from persistence alone |
| Scaffolding to helping | Limited causal developmental support | Separate skill, understood goal, approval, and recipient benefit; follow persistence after scaffold withdrawal |
| Representation to acquired value | A concrete associative route and restricted adult evidence | Replicate with independent outcome measures and compare instrumental, cached, and outcome-directed models |
| Acquired value to direct concern | Mechanistically possible; not identified by value transfer alone | Actual relief versus equally effective cue suppression, with private returns controlled |
| Familiar concern to portable standing | Formal underidentification exposed | New identities and powerless recipients, with irrelevant features independently varied |
| Social challenge to revised reasons | Limited short-term developmental support | Distinguish compliance from reason transfer; test justified dissent from the trainer or majority |
| Concern to justice | An explicit normative and organizational proposal | Defend standing and conflict principles; test agency, refusal, distribution, and correction separately |
| Individual learning to durable inheritance | Requires material and social carrier model | Longitudinal costs, perturbations, succession, restored capacity after disruption |
| Favorable local mechanism to universal direction | Open | Specified environments and measures; compare formation, loss, prevalence, and excluded harm against rivals |

A useful first study or simulation should not try to solve all rows with one scalar. It should compare explicit learner architectures under the same training history, then cross scenario transfer with recipient transfer, reward withdrawal, private cost, evidence correction, and delayed retention. Record both actual recipient outcomes and the learner's account of its reasons. A result that only improves trained-target affect would still be informative, but it would close a different gap from universal inclusion.

The theory becomes more complete by explaining how concern can be born, transformed, lost, and renewed under stated conditions. It becomes less complete if every success is called wisdom and every failure is assigned to insufficient wisdom after the fact.
