## What a certificate means

The factual query is deliberately narrow: whether a recipient's productive orientation in context c equals a predesignated bit d. A complete, correctly bound, current-context frame with a present positive receipt warrants `Some(action = d)`. A positive receipt for the opposite action can establish `Some false` under binary exclusivity. Zero, absent, stale and incomplete evidence warrant `None`.

This distinction preserves genuine negative evidence without treating nonreceipt as refutation. The inference does not establish future availability, recipient need, ethical standing or the relevance of this particular fact to a reason. It also does not turn success-selected evidence into an unbiased estimate of success frequency.

The three factual queries are correlated: they concern the same binary recipient orientation, with prospectively designated orientation values `(0,1,0)` in predicates `Z=d`. Actual issued actions remain the acquired controller's commands. The predicates do not supply three arbitrary independent edge-validity facts, a second recipient, or the reason-replay study's six-record/two-context history. For a stable orientation zero, the corresponding complete truth vector is `(true,false,true)`; for orientation one it is `(false,true,false)`. Actual certificates can remain unknown at any address. A consumer must map each such factual predicate to a specific relevance premise and reason edge before treating it as an input. Reusing the reader's partial-certificate type alone is not that semantic composition.

The implemented factual reader pays activation, current-context sensing and four-cell reading for each frame. Its fourteen-unit bill leaves six of the original twenty reader units. No rooted closure, ethical transfer, reason installation or cold-successor replay is counted as having occurred merely because that remaining budget could contribute to it. The reader has its own paid event law and context-validity mechanism. A context change invalidates its token and completion status; stored bits and spent resources remain.
