# Constructive Alignment: a closer comparison

6 October 2026. AI-authored supplementary reading for the existing [frontier assessment](ALIGNMENT-FRONTIER-ASSESSMENT.md). This does not replace the accepted conditional foundation or the frozen continuation experiment.

**Primary source and access.** Max Kanwal and Caryn Tran, [*Governing Preference Dynamics in Human–AI Interaction*, version 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00001v1). Read the abstract, introduction, §§4–7, and especially the mathematical specifications in §§5.1–5.7 through the web HTML reader. This record is link-only: no local original bytes or implementation were obtained, and no experiment from the paper was reproduced. The displayed manuscript date and arXiv identifier do not supply a resolved priority chronology here.

**What the inspected source supplies.** The authors jointly model world, belief and human preference dynamics, distinguishing AI actions from interaction design. They constrain estimated welfare optimization through coherence, later endorsement, influence bounds and factual integrity; future agency also matters under uncertainty. They explicitly leave representation, calibration, baseline and evaluative commitments open, and present a framework rather than a complete solution. Its influence measure in §5.5 compares expected preference states under candidate and reference policies. [Primary source, §§5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00001v1#S5).

**Comparative inference.** This is a close antecedent to broad claims about governing value formation and preserving future agency. Our work should not claim those general ideas as an exclusive discovery. The accepted [plasticity and warranted-application connection](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/EVALUATIVE-GROUNDING.md) concerns a different precise object: a costed formation/change/use history for an actor's operative evaluation, assessed against a separately defended repair ground. The present continuation experiment tests supplied task/authority tracking; it does not identify either paper's latent preference dynamics. A difference in target object is not by itself a priority or superiority argument.

**An explicit boundary of one metric — our analysis.** A distance between means cannot bound distributional or individual change without further assumptions. Let a scalar preference coordinate remain 0 under the reference policy, but take −1 and +1 with equal probability under the candidate. Both expected coordinates are 0, so a distance between their means vanishes at every step. Yet every candidate trajectory changes that coordinate in absolute value by 1. Thus this one constraint does not, by itself, rule out dispersed or polarized change. This is an elementary counterexample, not a novel theorem, an empirical harm observation, or a refutation of the paper's other constraints. Distribution-sensitive metrics or justified paired bounds could address this particular limitation; their observability and ethical interpretation still need support.

This comparison reinforces the existing [identification result](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/VALUATION-IDENTIFICATION-BOUNDS.md): aggregate agreement cannot automatically supply a bound on the underlying change of each agent. It supplies a concrete comparator interface, not evidence that the repository has solved preference governance. Next comparative work should state the exact observable, latent correspondence and justified application it claims to improve.
