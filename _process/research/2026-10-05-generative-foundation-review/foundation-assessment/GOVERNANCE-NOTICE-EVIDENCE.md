## What this adds to the existing result

The earlier study follows a reason from an affected person into an institution and asks whether it revises shared decision grounds. This extension follows the correction outward. An institution's records and an affected person's practical information can diverge. Correcting the former does not by itself update the latter. A theoretically sufficient handoff must identify whose existing decisions depend on the superseded message, what correction they can access and understand, and whether the relevant action remains possible.

Three endpoints must therefore be recorded separately: the current institutional rule, affected people's accessible information, and delivered redress. A refund may answer a financial claim without correcting every outstanding instruction. A correction letter may improve information without delivering money. Neither entails full restoration of welfare. This separation is compatible with meaningful partial progress and does not require delaying a justified partial remedy until every endpoint is complete.

A successful contact with a caller is not evidence that non-callers have the same access. Conversely, a person's silence is not evidence of either informed acceptance or continued error. Avoiding this selection problem requires a justified coverage frame and evidence about attempted delivery, receipt, comprehension and unresolved barriers. These are distinct observations, not interchangeable meanings of “notified.”

The reported percentage is debt-based and subgroup-specific; the inspected passage supplies no underlying count numerator or denominator. The money amounts use different dates and administrative categories. We derive no completion fraction, population coverage estimate or cost-effectiveness result. These reports do not provide the recipient-level ledger needed to independently reconcile all payments, link callers to non-callers, or attribute changed behavior to corrected notices. An official accounting report is evidence of the agency's reporting, not our independent bank-receipt verification.
