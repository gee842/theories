## 3. Necessary distinctions in the foundational claim

**Dependence is conditional on a boundary, task and horizon.** A process needs whatever support its actual laws require. More informative law constraints can establish resource, information or reconstruction bounds; the bare statement that necessary support is necessary is not the explanatory achievement. If all situations consistent with accessible information permit a common adequate action, new distinguishing evidence is unnecessary for that decision. If adequate responses are incompatible, reliable selection needs usable distinctions, which may be acquired now, retained or embodied in inherited organization. Continuous external sensing and explicit thoughts about interdependence are not universal prerequisites for every act of cognition.

**Necessary support is not a sufficient construction.** Separate resource, information and authority conditions need compatible timing and a feasible joint history. Conversely, a successful supplied construction proves a possibility under its law, not that its particular recipe is universally necessary. More detailed physical implementation is warranted when it discharges an input actually used by the claim, not whenever a lower-level question can be asked.

**Causal significance is not justified standing.** An actor may model another accurately and exclude it, or protect its own future by increasing control. Interdependence can support cooperation or domination. The ethical arguments already supplied must be assessed on their merits; they are not absent merely because indifference is logically consistent. Neither favorable behavior nor a learned number completes the warrant. A local plasticity law may legitimately be a physical premise; an unexplained benevolent evaluator cannot silently be both a premise and the capacity said to have been derived.

**Universal explanatory scope differs from an unconditional favorable guarantee.** The framework must cover destructive regimes without presenting them as failures of reality to obey its definition. Arbitrarily powerful interference, infeasible claims or insufficient information can defeat a particular guarantee. Demonstrating that limit narrows the guarantee with reasons; it does not automatically settle which workable protections, authority or remedies should replace it. No finite run can establish unrestricted empirical validity, but completion also does not require resolving every imaginable history or coercing agreement from every possible moral skeptic.
