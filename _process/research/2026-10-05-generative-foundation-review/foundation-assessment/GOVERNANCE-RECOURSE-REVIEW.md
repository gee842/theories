## The inference that actually needs testing

Three different grounds for change must remain separate:

| Evidence or objection | Warranted scope to investigate | What does not follow automatically |
|---|---|---|
| A case record has the wrong person, period or earnings | Correct that record and any cases sharing the demonstrated defect | Every use of the decision rule is invalid |
| A common evidentiary premise fails for a defined class | Review that premise, its use in action, the affected class and any necessary interim protection | Every complaint is true, or every case has the same final outcome |
| The governing legal or ethical standard is inadequate or misapplied | Examine the standard, authority, interpretation and permissible remedy | More accurate execution of the current rule supplies the missing justification |

These are analytical distinctions, not classifications inferred solely from the number of successful appeals. An individual correction may reveal a general defect, but its reason must be examined. Conversely, a challenge need not establish the correct outcome of every affected case to undermine a shared inference. Where several underlying histories produce the same aggregate record but require different decisions, an assumed completion of the missing history does not become established evidence merely because a calculation using it is internally consistent.

The relevant uptake therefore preserves **what the evidence defeats**. Better intake, a clearer explanation, more responsive case handling and revision of an evidentiary decision rule are different outcomes. They can coexist; success at one level is not evidence of success at every level. A general revision can also be real without being adequate: changed software, changed staff guidance, corrected prior cases and actual cessation of the defective practice require separate observations.

## Locate the break without inventing a motive

The same eventual harm is compatible with several mechanisms:

1. **Missing or distorted observation.** Affected people cannot obtain records, articulate the issue or reach the relevant process; information is miscoded or lost. Better access or faithful transmission could address this particular break.
2. **A classification failure.** The organization receives a reason but treats it only as a local exception or service complaint. A resolved case can disappear from the evidence available for reviewing the general rule.
3. **A failure of authority or incentives.** The relevant challenge reaches a decision maker but cannot, or does not, alter the practice. This requires evidence about powers, decisions and constraints; organizational self-preservation is a hypothesis, not a motive established by persistence alone.
4. **A failure to carry through an accepted change.** A revision is authorized but does not reach operational rules, staff, contractors or earlier affected cases. Formal acceptance is not equivalent to implemented protection or restored loss.

These alternatives can occur together. Their distinctions prevent a diagnosis of “insufficient listening” from prescribing further observation when an already understood defect remains operative. They also prevent attributing deliberate disregard to an actor who never received the relevant material. Better evidence access does not itself allocate authority, justify a legal interpretation or establish whose losses have moral standing.

## Minimal discriminating historical audit

For each concrete challenge, trace its original content and date, who received it, its stated basis, the decision maker's available authority, the response and the subsequent operational scope. Separate what contemporaneous records establish from what later findings establish about that history. A later conclusion cannot by itself show exactly what every earlier participant knew. A review outcome may be probative of a shared defect without creating binding general precedent; binding force and evidential relevance are separate questions.

The informative comparison is between the **scope of the defect identified** and the **scope of the action actually changed**, with the time between them retained. Include explanations for narrower action, unresolved uncertainty and evidence that would defeat the challenge. Do not treat a promise, new form, written policy or successful individual remedy as a substitute for checking the affected class. Include people who did not complain: appeal outcomes are selected observations, and low complaint counts cannot independently establish a low underlying error or harm rate.

This process trace can substantiate the existence and location of a failed connection. It cannot identify the counterfactual effect of a proposed governance arrangement as though authority, incentives or feedback channels had been randomized. Nor does eventual policy reversal isolate which campaign, official, ruling or organizational change caused it. Avoid both claims that correction was absent and claims that eventual reversal fully restored people's futures.

## Consequence for the central account

Ren's external-dependence clarification motivates looking beyond an institution's own representation of its task. The substantive test is whether externally grounded reasons can revise consequential action at the scope they warrant. A system may maintain itself, improve administration and correct cases while leaving a challenged common premise in use. Mere continuation and feedback responsiveness therefore do not complete the bridge to warranted governance.

The smallest useful advancement here is an identified mismatch between the generality of a recognized reason and the generality of the resulting correction, with the causal location of the break supported by documents. If the evidence instead shows only a case-specific error, the general-rule inference must be withdrawn. If the bottleneck is authority or implementation, additional sensing alone is inadequate. If the remaining conflict concerns the governing value or legal ground, faithful factual uptake leaves that further justification explicit. None of these results uniquely confirms this theory over established accounts of administrative learning and accountability.
