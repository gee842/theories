## Our assessment: the discriminating connection

A correction has at least three independently checkable objects: a particular person's decision, the reason supporting a shared rule, and the implementation that produces or repairs outcomes. An institution may change one without changing the others. Counting complaints, successful appeals or new guidance therefore leaves the proposed connection unidentified.

The relevant transfer is a **reason with justified scope**. A corrected personal fact warrants the matching personal correction. A defect in an inference shared across cases warrants identifying dependent cases and withdrawing that inference as sufficient support within its actual domain. Alternative valid support must remain available. Evidence insufficient to establish a debt does not establish that no debt could exist; the applicable burden of justification determines what the institution may assert or enforce in the meantime.

For empirical identification, seek a linked record of:

1. A challenge, its evidence, the adjudicated reason, uncertainty and date.
2. The operative rule and the decisions dependent on the challenged premise.
3. The official empowered to investigate, revise, pause or remedy it.
4. The instruction and version actually implemented, with resources and effective date.
5. Subsequent nonappellant decisions and actual delivery of ordered remedies.

A favorable outcome for an appellant alone leaves steps 2–5 open. A transmission log establishes receipt, while reasoned disposition and operational change test uptake. An institution's endurance, revenue or reduced complaint count cannot substitute for those checks. Silence may reflect barriers to challenge. Publication, escalation and authority also remain separate variables: a public reason can lack an effective decision route; an authorized office can receive a reason and decline warranted revision.

This case is useful as an adverse process trace and an existence test of a gap in institutional correction. It cannot estimate what a specified reform would have caused, establish a general failure rate across institutions, or demonstrate that every possible affected case has been identified. No new aggregate has been calculated. Any proposed repair needs its own implementation and outcome evidence.

The connection to Ren's question is conditional: an institution's continued capacity to act can depend on evidence supplied by people outside its decision boundary, while its existing internal objectives can obstruct revising itself in response. Keeping external evidence available and making warranted revision effective are distinct requirements. This supports a research interface for governance; it does not derive ethical concern, legitimate authority or a universal favorable direction from dependence alone.
