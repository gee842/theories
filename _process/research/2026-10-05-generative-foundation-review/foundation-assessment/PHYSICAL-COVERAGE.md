# Physical coverage of the proposed generative foundation

Specialist assessment, 5 October 2026. Research direction: Yongxu Ren. AI-assisted mathematical and literature audit. Scope: the physical premises in the [root foundation](../root-foundation/ROOT-FOUNDATION.md), [turnover theorem](../root-foundation/TURNOVER-AND-CLOSURE.md), [information theorem](../root-foundation/INFORMATION-AND-RENEWAL.md), and [invariance memo](../history/root-foundation/INVARIANCE-AND-PRIOR-ART.md). This report preserves those documents and proposes an extension rather than silently changing their claims.

## 1. Decisive answer

The present premises do **not** cover every physical case. They define an important class of systems, not a physical law forcing every persistent process into selection–renewal dynamics. Three separations are decisive:

1. Finite mean lifetime is not a finite maximum lifetime. Ordinary stochastic degradation can have an unbounded survival tail, even though every particular instance eventually fails with probability one.
2. Continued activity does not imply cumulative degradation of every enabling constraint. Equilibrium transport and passive physical protection require separate physical accounts; positive turnover cannot be inferred merely from the passage of time.
3. Functional organization can arise from prepared precursors without an already functioning instance of the resulting organization. The live-parent premise is therefore a substantive claim about the chosen support interface.

These are not objections to the conditional proofs. They identify where a theorem's assumptions must be measured or replaced. The strongest constructive improvement is a **stochastic support theorem** below: finite types, vulnerable instances, and bounded parent-dependent production imply an explicit survival bound when the dependency graph is acyclic. It replaces the deterministic maximum-lifetime assumption with loss hazards and remains exact for dependent, history-sensitive production processes. An ordinary serial-replacement process attains the bound.

The defensible domain is broader than one institution, technology, or biological mechanism: **situated systems whose declared functions depend on vulnerable, replenishable capacities, with a causally justified support interface and specified demands, horizons, and failure tolerances**. Selection is an additional mechanism within that domain. A theory of alignment can be general across that domain without explaining equilibrium physics through the same mechanism.

## 2. Coverage matrix

“Conditional fit” means that the indicated interface and rates must be established; it does not mean that their validity has already been demonstrated in all systems of that kind.

| Physical or organizational class | Present result that applies | Missing or violated premise | Defensible treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engineered components with enforced expiry dates and no restoration after expiry | Deterministic lifetime bound | Requires an actual maximum functioning duration, including every repair and bypass | Direct fit when expiry is physically or operationally enforced |
| Molecular populations with first-order degradation | Balance result; stochastic extension below | Exponential lifetimes have no finite maximum | Use loss hazards and a probabilistic survival bound |
| Proteins with age-dependent stabilization | Stochastic extension only if a positive lower hazard can be justified | Constant-rate degradation need not hold; a global positive lower bound may also fail | Measure age/state-dependent hazards; retain the resulting qualification |
| Cells or biochemical organizations with measured synthesis and turnover | Renewal and information branches can apply to identified capacities | Dependency, causal production, loss, and external provision all need calibration | Strong candidate domain; a measured turnover rate alone does not prove full closure |
| Autonomous error-correcting hardware | Information/provision branch, with its own channel and correction laws | Erasure channel and shared effort equation are not generic physical laws | Model measured noise and correction; include control fields and reservoirs |
| Equilibrium persistent currents or passively protected states | Operational response representation | Positive support turnover is not entailed by equilibrium activity | Treat equilibrium constraints and actual environmental vulnerabilities explicitly |
| Spontaneous folding or self-assembly from prepared precursors | Operational representation; a fuller precursor model | A previously functioning product need not be a parent | Include nonfunctional precursors and spontaneous transitions; do not infer organizational reproduction from state cycling |
| Organizations importing personnel, controllers, materials, or corrective information | External-provision alternative | Internal closure is absent or unestablished | Retain the supplier boundary instead of calling all support internally reproduced |
| Evolution with continually changing causal roles | Local finite models or a larger process model | One finite exact type vocabulary over an unlimited future is unestablished | Validate finite horizons and aggregation errors; do not merge novelty away to force a cycle |
| A fixed predictable task, or different contexts admitting the same safe response | Operational feasibility result | Information-discrimination demand may be absent | An open-loop response can be adequate; no positive information threshold follows |
| Conflicting, changing human purposes | Response profiles with plural acceptance requirements | Physics does not select the acceptable requirement set | Preserve explicit affected parties, conflicts, amendments, and participation |

The table distinguishes the formal scope of a representation from the scope of an explanatory mechanism. Describing all rows by intervention-response profiles does not establish that all rows have the same loss, repair, selection, or information equations.

## 3. Actual physical counterclasses and what they establish

### 3.1 Stochastic lifetimes are the immediate biological correction

For a single instance with first-order degradation rate \(\lambda>0\),

\[
\Pr(T>t)=e^{-\lambda t}.
\tag{P1}
\]

The mean is finite and \(\Pr(T<\infty)=1\), but \(\Pr(T>\tau)>0\) for every finite \(\tau\). Therefore a deterministic support deadline obtained by substituting a half-life or mean lifetime for a maximum lifetime is invalid. A measured half-life cannot be used as the \(\tau_i\) in the original lifetime theorem.

This is not an exotic hypothetical. Shahrezaei and Swain's primary stochastic gene-expression model uses first-order molecular degradation and explicitly derives an exponential mRNA lifetime. Their analysis also shows that similar observable protein distributions can arise from different underlying expression mechanisms, reinforcing the need for intervention-sensitive identification. [Shahrezaei and Swain, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582303/).

Nor is exponential degradation universal. Metabolic pulse-chase measurements by McShane and colleagues found non-exponential degradation for more than ten percent of the measured proteins, with stability changing with molecular age. The appropriate repair is not to declare a universal exponential law; it is to use whichever hazard bounds are actually supported. [McShane et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27720452/).

Broad turnover is nevertheless measurable: a tissue atlas estimated half-lives for more than 3,000 unique proteins. Such measurements establish a promising empirical interface for renewal hypotheses, without establishing a maximum lifetime or a universal dependency graph. [Rolfs et al., 2021, *An atlas of protein turnover rates in mouse tissues*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26842-3).

### 3.2 Equilibrium activity is not a theorem of wear

Measurements of persistent currents in normal-metal rings agree with a model in which an equilibrium state supports a dissipationless current. This is a real physical class in which ongoing microscopic activity cannot simply be assigned a positive replacement expenditure because it is activity. [Bleszynski-Jayich et al., 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.4780).

The experiment does not prove that a ring, cryostat, or laboratory lasts forever. It establishes a narrower but decisive point: the loss and replacement of a support must come from a physical mechanism, not from the assertion that all motion is impairment. An enlarged experimental boundary can contain vulnerable apparatus, but its existence does not prove that the current's equilibrium law is itself a selection–renewal law.

At the model level, a stationary equilibrium distribution can preserve a macroscopic response without continual reproduction of that response by selected alternatives. At the apparatus level, maintenance may be indispensable. These are different claims at different interfaces. Measuring the latter cannot retroactively turn the former into the same causal mechanism.

### 3.3 Functional return need not require a live functional parent

Single-molecule experiments show RNA structures hopping between folded and unfolded states at equilibrium under constant force. They permit a physical description in which a folded instance returns after a period with no folded instance present. [Liphardt et al., 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11326101/).

A minimal model is

\[
U\xrightleftharpoons[k_-]{k_+}F,
\qquad k_+,k_->0.
\tag{P2}
\]

If \(F\) is the declared functional conformation, its creation rate can remain positive while \(N_F=0\). The functional population's zero state is not absorbing. The stationary functional occupancy is \(k_+/(k_++k_-)\), and repeated returns do not require an existing functional copy.

One can include the unfolded molecule as a precursor type. That is a legitimate broader physical model, but the molecule's continuing availability is then a separate support condition; the claim that every relevant support is productively vulnerable still needs evidence. A cycle of microscopic conformations is also not automatically a cycle of organizational reproduction or differential selection.

Anfinsen's refolding work gives the related lesson that a biologically active conformation can reappear from a denatured molecule under suitable conditions. This does not erase the history or informational specificity of the precursor sequence. It shows why a functioning-parent claim must distinguish precursor organization, current function, and evolutionary history. [Anfinsen's 1972 Nobel lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/anfinsen-lecture.pdf).

### 3.4 Information has physical costs, but not one universal positive cost per correct answer

The work cost of information processing depends on the logical process, the available side information, the physical state, and the thermodynamic implementation. Faist and colleagues derive costs tied to information discarded conditional on the output; this does not provide a constant positive price for every inference or every true proposition. [Faist et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.1037).

Accordingly, the positive \(q\), loss \(\delta\), and shared allocation constraint in the root information model are application assumptions. They can describe a useful physical technology without being deduced from Landauer's principle. Renewable energy availability, parallel provision, reversible processing, slower demands, additional observations, or persistent knowledge can change the feasible set.

A relevant positive example is autonomous quantum error correction: Gertler and colleagues experimentally improved the coherence time of an encoded qubit using continuous control fields and engineered corrective dissipation. The correction mechanism and its supporting apparatus are explicit. Its quantum channel is not the root model's classical erasure channel, and its observed improvement does not establish unlimited lifetime. [Gertler et al., 2021; primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09322).

These examples defeat an inference from “physically implemented” to the specific scalar maintenance law. They support the broader program of measuring a technology's noise, provision, and attainable correction together.

## 4. A stochastic replacement for the deterministic lifetime theorem

The finite-lifetime theorem should be retained for genuine expiry bounds. The following extension is more suitable when functioning instances fail stochastically.

### 4.1 Assumptions

Let \(N(t)\in\mathbb N^n\) count active support instances of finitely many independently specified types. Let \(m_0=\mathbb E N(0)\) be finite. A declared function requires at least one active tracked instance; losing all tracked instances therefore makes that function unavailable. Individual demands can of course fail earlier.

Write \(N_i(t)=N_i(0)+C_i(t)-D_i(t)\), where \(C_i,D_i\) count unit creations and losses. Their predictable compensator rates, with respect to the full model history, are \(b_i(t),d_i(t)\). Jointly marked events are permitted: one event may remove a parent and create a successor, provided the parent is active immediately before the event. This does not require independent instances, independent channels, a Markov population process, or an uninformed controller.

Assume nonexplosion, finite first moments and integrable compensators on each finite interval, and the history-wise bounds

\[
b_i(t)\le\sum_{j=1}^n B_{ij}N_j(t-),
\qquad
d_i(t)\ge\lambda_iN_i(t-),
\quad \lambda_i>0,
\tag{P3}
\]

where \(B\) is a finite nonnegative matrix. If arbitrary allowed interventions are to be covered, the bounds must hold uniformly over those interventions. They are not bounds only under a fitted baseline policy.

The first bound excludes spontaneous or external creation when every tracked parent is absent. It also bounds productive throughput per active parent. The second supplies an aggregate loss hazard, not a deterministic expiration date. Constant individual hazards are sufficient but unnecessary. Age-dependent or history-dependent hazards are allowed when they satisfy the stated lower bound.

The moment conditions hold for the usual nonexplosive counting models with finite initial mean and linearly bounded creation. More generally they may be established by stopping at population thresholds, applying the expectation inequalities to the stopped compensators, and verifying the required integrability before removing the stopping. No assumption about unbounded-time interchange of expectations is needed in the finite-time comparison below.

Let \(\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)\). Define \(T_{\rm ext}\) as the first time at which all counts are zero. Under (P3), zero is absorbing.

### Theorem P1: acyclic stochastic support has a computable survival tail

For all finite \(t\),

\[
\mathbb E N(t)\le e^{(B-\Lambda)t}m_0
\tag{P4}
\]

componentwise. If the support graph \(j\to i\) when \(B_{ij}>0\) is acyclic, set \(\lambda=\min_i\lambda_i>0\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(T_{\rm ext}>t)
\le \min\!\left\{1,
e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\frac{t^k}{k!}\,\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}B^k m_0
\right\}.}
\tag{P5}
\]

In particular, \(T_{\rm ext}<\infty\) almost surely and

\[
\mathbb E T_{\rm ext}
\le\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\frac{\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}B^k m_0}{\lambda^{k+1}}<\infty.
\tag{P6}
\]

**Proof.** Integrability of the compensators gives an absolutely continuous mean \(m(t)=\mathbb E N(t)\) satisfying \(m'(t)\le (B-\Lambda)m(t)\) almost everywhere. Put \(A=B-\Lambda\). This is a Metzler matrix, so \(e^{At}\) is entrywise nonnegative. Variation of constants applied to the nonnegative deficit \(Am-m'\) gives (P4). No stochastic independence enters this argument.

Since \(A\le B-\lambda I\) entrywise and both are Metzler, their positive semigroups satisfy \(e^{At}\le e^{(B-\lambda I)t}\). Acyclicity makes \(B\) permutation-similar to a strictly triangular matrix; hence \(B^n=0\). Thus

\[
e^{(B-\lambda I)t}
=e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{t^kB^k}{k!}.
\tag{P7}
\]

Because zero is absorbing and the total population is integer-valued,
\(\Pr(T_{\rm ext}>t)=\Pr(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}N(t)>0)
\le\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}m(t)\).
This proves (P5). Its right side tends to zero, proving almost-sure extinction. Integrating the nonnegative tail bound and using \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}t^k/k!\,dt=\lambda^{-(k+1)}\) proves (P6). ∎

The survival upper bound also bounds the probability that a function requiring those supports remains available through time \(t\). It does not guarantee availability before extinction; conjunctive requirements and missed deadlines can make service fail sooner.

### 4.2 An exactly sharp witness

Start with one active instance of type 1. At rate \(\lambda\), type \(i<n\) expires and creates exactly one type-\(i+1\) successor in a jointly marked event. The type-\(n\) instance expires at rate \(\lambda\) without a successor. Let the successive holding times be independent exponential variables. There is exactly one active instance until extinction.

Here \(m_0=e_1\), \(B_{i+1,i}=\lambda\), every \(\lambda_i=\lambda\), and all other entries of \(B\) vanish. The graph is an acyclic serial chain. The extinction time has an Erlang distribution:

\[
\Pr(T_{\rm ext}>t)
=e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(\lambda t)^k}{k!},
\qquad
\mathbb E T_{\rm ext}=n/\lambda.
\tag{P8}
\]

Equations (P5) and (P6) are both attained. No finite deterministic deadline exists: the survival probability is positive at every finite time. Nevertheless, the stochastic deadline at any prescribed failure tolerance is computable. For three serial types with \(\lambda=1\), the survival upper bound is exactly \(e^{-t}(1+t+t^2/2)\).

This witness shows that removing deterministic lifetime bounds need not remove predictive power. It changes the kind of warranted prediction from an absolute deadline to an explicit probability and an expected duration.

### 4.3 Weak cycles and time-varying rates

Acyclicity is sufficient for \(A=B-\Lambda\) to have all eigenvalues with negative real parts. More generally, (P4) yields extinction when \(A\) is Hurwitz, even if \(B\) has cycles. Equivalently for nonnegative \(B\) and positive diagonal \(\Lambda\),

\[
\rho(\Lambda^{-1}B)<1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_{\rm ext}<\infty\ \text{almost surely}.
\tag{P9}
\]

This is the standard positive-system/subcriticality criterion. Its role here is to distinguish the presence of a renewal route from rates capable of defeating the extinction comparison. It is not a sufficient condition for survival when reversed: bounds may be loose, and critical or capacity-limited processes can die out despite cycles.

If physical bounds depend on time, replace \(B-\Lambda\) by a measurable Metzler matrix \(A(t)\) with a well-defined positive evolution operator \(\Phi(t,0)\). The same argument yields \(\mathbb E N(t)\le\Phi(t,0)m_0\). Decay must then be checked for the actual evolution operator. The union of all permitted support edges may be cyclic even though no instantaneous graph is cyclic; instantaneous acyclicity alone does not imply (P5). This matters for alternating teams, ecological succession, and periodically scheduled maintenance.

The proof is a direct application of established counting-process expectations, positive systems, and branching-process comparisons. It is a mathematical extension of this project's domain, not a claim to have originated those methods. Related continuous-time multitype extinction work includes [Windridge, 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.4768); that paper's specific epidemic branching model is not being asserted identical to (P3).

## 5. Indefinite feasibility and noisy physical continuation are different claims

A deterministic replenishment model can have a safe equilibrium forever. A finite stochastic implementation may nevertheless fail eventually.

Suppose there is a time block \(\Delta>0\) and \(p>0\) such that, conditional on every surviving history and under every permitted continuation policy, permanent failure during the next block has probability at least \(p\). Then

\[
\Pr(T_{\rm fail}>k\Delta)\le(1-p)^k.
\tag{P10}
\]

**Proof.** Apply the conditional bound to the event of surviving each successive block and use conditional expectation inductively. ∎

Thus indefinite survival has probability zero, including in systems with active renewal. A finite-state continuous-time chain with an absorbing failure state reachable from every other state provides a familiar model class with an appropriate block bound, when rates are fixed and the reachability conditions hold. The controlled statement additionally requires a bound uniform over allowed policies; reachability under only one policy is insufficient.

This does not make maintenance useless. It makes the operational question sharper: **how much can an intervention improve the probability of meeting specified functions over a declared horizon?** A long expected lifetime, small failure probability over a century, and survival with probability one for infinite time are different claims.

For physical applications, the recommended target is therefore a horizon \(H\) and tolerated failure \(\varepsilon\), with deterministic infinite-horizon feasibility treated as an informative idealization where warranted. This connects directly to the existing evaluation specification; its horizon and failure fields have concrete mathematical work to do.

## 6. The information premise can be broadened without asserting universal epistemic demand

The root theorem uses independent fair contexts to prevent memory from supplying the missing distinction. That is a clean constructed case, not a necessary assumption for all information bounds.

At a particular history \(h\), let \(p_h=\Pr(\theta=1\mid h)\) and let \(P_0^h,P_1^h\) describe the genuinely available next observation on a finite or countable alphabet. If the two contexts require opposite binary responses, the conditional optimal error is

\[
e^*(h)=\sum_y\min\{(1-p_h)P_0^h(y),p_hP_1^h(y)\}.
\tag{P11}
\]

This follows by pointwise minimization of the response error. It includes information already contained in history. When \(p_h=1/2\), it reduces to the existing total-variation formula. When history makes one context certain, no fresh observation is needed to distinguish it. When both contexts permit a common acceptable action, the binary opposite-response bound does not apply at all.

For multiple goals and observations, retain the attainable response-profile set instead of assuming that all information is ordered by one scalar reliability \(c\). Different experiments can be incomparable for different tasks. Maintenance should then act on the actual observation and execution interface; a universal scalar “gradient toward alignment” does not follow from physics.

A similar distinction applies to quantum systems. Classical distributions of recorded outcomes under specified physical instruments can be represented operationally. The finite classical hidden-state transport proof does not by itself establish a finite classical ontological model of an arbitrary quantum process, or allow incompatible observations to be read simultaneously without disturbance. Generality of the operational interface must preserve physical instrument restrictions.

## 7. What remains universal, and what should become a domain claim

There are three nested levels.

**Operational necessity.** A response requirement is feasible only if some implementable policy attains it. Faithful transport of the physical interface preserves feasibility. This is a general mathematical consistency statement, compatible with very different physical mechanisms.

**Vulnerable-support necessity.** Under measured loss and provision constraints, future functioning depends on replenishment, substitution, reduced dependence, or acceptable revision. Finite support networks admit exact graph, rate, reserve, and survival bounds. The original deterministic theorem and Theorem P1 are different useful members of this family.

**Endogenous selection–renewal dynamics.** When current practices affect which practices continue and how their supporting capacities are reproduced, the resulting feedback can select adequate or inadequate regimes. This requires the corresponding causal links. It is neither a consequence of the word change nor a necessary property of every physical process.

The broadest defensible central statement is:

> For a specified system, task family, physical interface, and horizon, alignment requires attainable acceptable responses. When those responses depend on vulnerable capacities, the loss, provision, information, and execution laws constrain how long the responses remain attainable. If practices also change which capacities and practices persist, those changes create endogenous feedback governing future alignment. The applicable laws and feedbacks must be established at the chosen interface; no universal objective or universal renewal mechanism is assumed.

This statement is not one theorem until an application supplies a model. It identifies the common mathematical architecture and the physical conditions under which the substantive theorems constrain it. The architecture can accommodate heterogeneous mechanisms without asserting that they have identical equations.

## 8. Evidence that would change the coverage assessment

Exponential lifetimes limit the deterministic lifetime branch; they do not by themselves exclude a process from the whole framework, because the cumulative-balance branch or Theorem P1 may cover it. The stronger claim that every physical process realizes the same vulnerable-support and live-parent mechanism encounters the equilibrium and parent-free functional-return classes in Section 3. Their applicability at a chosen boundary must be assessed physically rather than settled by renaming every precursor or constraint as renewal. A broader, explicitly conditional domain claim could become much better supported through the following evidence.

| Claim under examination | Evidence that supports it | Evidence that forces revision |
|---|---|---|
| No spontaneous or imported functioning support | Depletion/restart experiments and source tracing show all new instances require a tracked live parent | Reliable formation when all alleged parents are absent, or an omitted external source |
| Positive support loss under all relevant interventions | Direct hazard measurements, including age, dormancy, shielding, and low-demand regimes | A permitted regime with losses below the claimed lower bound |
| Finite useful type description | Stable intervention-response predictions and checked error bounds across substitutions and succession | Novel roles or aggregated distinctions change intervention outcomes |
| Bound on parent-dependent creation | Controlled production measurements covering the allowed operating region | A bypass, burst mechanism, or alternative process exceeds the bound |
| Continuing discrimination is necessary | Tasks contain genuinely incompatible appropriate responses, and all available information is represented | A common safe response, predictive history, or an omitted observation bypasses the supposed information threshold |
| Shared resource competition limits correction | Factorial interventions separately calibrate information maintenance, execution, and their coupling | Separate provision or technological substitution removes the proposed coupling |
| Selection changes renewal and future correction | Interventions on selection alter renewal, and renewal interventions alter independently measured later correction | Only a shared cause, contemporaneous score effect, or unmeasured external provision explains the association |

A positive result in each domain would establish a broad empirically supported family. It would not make the assumptions true by definition. Conversely, a persistent current or a spontaneously refolding molecule does not refute a well-specified theory of vulnerable institutional correction; it refutes an overextended claim that one mechanism is compulsory for all physical processes.

No new physical experiment or empirical parameter fit was conducted for this report. The new stochastic result is a proof, the serial witness is a constructed exactly soluble process, and the cited experiments establish physical scope and counterclasses. External specialist review should scrutinize the application bounds before this is used to forecast a measured system.
