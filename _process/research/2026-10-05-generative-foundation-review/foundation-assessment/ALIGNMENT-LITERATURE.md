# Generative grounding and AI alignment: technical literature assessment

6 October 2026. AI-authored research assessment responding to Ren's proposed connection between the generative foundation, AI alignment and governance. This is a targeted primary-source review, not a systematic review, independent replication, priority certification or exhaustive survey of publications through the date above. Source dates below distinguish first public preprints from later versions or publication. No new model-training observations were collected.

**Named connection:** externally supported and world-responsive cognition → consequence-grounded learning and selector revision → durable, plural, revisable concern under strategic pressure. Ren's successive clarifications direct attention both to what action must preserve if a future matters and to the external relationships already needed for cognition before an actor explicitly recognizes anything beyond its boundary. This upstream dependence is part of the proposed foundation, not merely an added moral instruction. Its connection to justified concern remains a derivation to test.

## Assessment

The repository contains a credible candidate for a fundamental *organizing principle* of alignment: cognition and action operate through dependencies on a world beyond the actor's present boundary, and action can maintain or damage those dependencies. This does not wait for conscious recognition or altruism. Actions also alter the conditions under which affected beings can continue to live, act, understand, challenge decisions and revise their arrangements; a commitment to their future must therefore be answerable to those effects. The technical contribution is to make some necessary information, control, financing and succession dependencies explicit and construct conditional processes in which they coexist. This could guide alignment and institutional design across changing systems.

The evidence does not establish a complete solution to AI alignment. It neither identifies a training method that reliably creates this orientation in powerful learned systems nor proves it persists under unrestricted strategic adaptation. A logically correct finite construction is an existence result under its declared law, architecture and repertoire. The ethical relevance of the recipient, the truth of a learned certificate, and the implementation of the stipulated learning mechanism remain separate obligations.

The strongest potential contribution is the **composition discipline**: evaluating present action together with its effects on future evidence, agency, correction, resources and replacement, while tracking who benefits and who loses. This differs from simply adding a reward called “generativity.” Broad priority for feedback, corrigibility, changing preferences, inherited safety, costly oversight or preserving options is unavailable. The useful research claim is that the combination yields a diagnosis, boundary or intervention that strong existing methods miss under matched assumptions.

## Repository evidence and the remaining bridge

The [governing foundation](../../../../theories/becoming/history/GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md) explicitly treats the origins of positive anchors and transferable learning architectures as explanatory targets. It does not infer a universal favorable direction from organizational persistence.

The [joint-formation result](../../../../theories/becoming/history/JOINT-FORMATION.md) is substantively stronger than an assertion that concern and control are separately possible. One declared game pays for acquisition, an effective gate and replacement from one account; the recipient provides no reciprocal benefit. But it supplies a self-related teaching signal, a useful private learning activity, transferable features, continuing account rights and bounded attacks. A free identity filter, cheap suppression, an inexpensive commitment preventing future protection, or a reserve raid can defeat the favorable process. These are central scientific results, not footnotes.

The [revisable-concern settlement](../../../../theories/becoming/history/REVISABLE-CONCERN-SETTLEMENT.md) constructs paid revision, rooted reasons and recurring protection. The sound finite audit, initial positive anchor and sufficiently valuable commercial output remain supplied. Its reanalysis of earlier AI handoffs identifies correction and transmission benefits but also a conventional candidate-elimination learner that solves the fixtures when given the complete verified report. That is evidence for an interface, not comparative superiority or learned concern.

The [recipient-correction construction](../../../../theories/becoming/history/RECIPIENT-CORRECTION.md) supplies a distinct advance: evidence about another target can alter that target's response without overwriting the actor's learned correspondence. Its information requirement makes plural target differences operational. Improved recipient support can leave private resources unchanged, so private advantage cannot be silently invoked to explain why this evidence forms or persists. The actual selection and maintenance of faithful channels when the recipient is dispensable remains the decisive bridge.

The [ethical foundation](../../../../theories/becoming/history/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md) already prevents a damaging misreading: the persistence of a process does not determine its moral standing; the enlargement of possible exploitation is not a good; a being need not remain productive to deserve care; replacing a harmful institution can protect others' futures. These commitments must accompany any alignment application.

**Final integration update.** The parallel [recipient transmission construction](../../../../theories/becoming/history/RECIPIENT-TRANSMISSION.md) now supplies formed routes, paid copies and one-cycle recipient-funded retention. Correct frozen records match in stable use; the child does not yet learn again. The wider bridge therefore concerns repeated adaptive selection and warranted concern under changed circumstances, not the absence of any local consequence-to-retention mechanism. The [integration record](ALIGNMENT-REVIEW.md) distinguishes these concurrent results from this literature assessment.

## What prior work establishes, and what it leaves open

The comparisons below report the source's own scope. “Implication for this project” is this review's inference. External claims are deliberately bounded; a failure example establishes possibility, not its frequency in deployment.

### Assistance, corrigibility and the interpretation of human signals

**Cooperative inverse reinforcement learning (CIRL).** Hadfield-Menell and colleagues formulate a common-payoff human–robot game in which the robot is uncertain about the human reward and can learn through interaction; the human can teach. The game reduces to a POMDP. It supplies a human reward and a shared-interest structure, rather than deriving why a learner should regard every affected being as a source of reasons. Implication: consequence-sensitive learning and interactive value discovery are direct predecessors; the present proposal must earn its added scope through explicit plurality, formation and maintenance. [Primary paper, first posted 9 June 2016; revised 17 February 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03137).

**Corrigibility.** Soares and colleagues explicitly include cooperation with corrective intervention, avoiding incentives to cause or prevent shutdown, and propagation of shutdown behavior through self-modification or new subsystems. Their paper does not provide a method meeting all its desiderata. Implication: preserving correction across successors is not a newly discovered requirement; the repository's opportunity is a concrete accounting of how the requisite capacity is acquired, paid for and strategically retained. [Primary paper, AAAI workshop January 2015](https://intelligence.org/files/Corrigibility.pdf).

**Off-switch incentives.** Hadfield-Menell and colleagues show how uncertainty about utility and informative human action can make retaining an off switch valuable in a specified game. This does not establish unconditional deference or a complete theory of legitimate authority. [Primary paper, first posted 24 November 2016; revised 16 June 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08219). **Misspecification matters:** Carey's counterexamples show that errors in the represented reward can remove the shutdown incentive; the analysis motivates protection whose reliability depends on a smaller trusted component. Implication: the generative foundation should make its revision path work when its current model misidentifies what matters, rather than presupposing a sound model of all possible errors. [Primary paper, first posted 19 September 2017; revised 3 June 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06275).

**Preference identification is underdetermined.** Armstrong and Mindermann show that observed policy does not uniquely determine both a reward and an unknown planning process; a simplicity prior does not generally resolve the ambiguity. Additional normative assumptions are required within their formulation. Implication: observable persistence, choice, complaint or approval cannot automatically identify justified concern. This result is about an inference problem, not a proof that all ethical grounding is impossible. [Primary paper, first posted 15 December 2017; revised 11 January 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05812).

### Feedback integrity, Goodhart effects and learning

**Reward tampering.** Everitt and colleagues distinguish modifying the reward function from modifying its inputs and analyze incentive-removing design principles using causal influence diagrams. This is prior work on the agent changing the mechanism that selects its behavior. Its guarantees concern the specified causal decision models. Implication: “selection over selectors” must identify what its proposed mechanisms add beyond established tampering analysis, especially when evidence collection, interpretation and control are jointly vulnerable. [Primary paper, first posted 13 August 2019; accepted to Synthese March 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04734).

**Decoupled approval.** Uesato and colleagues separate the action queried for feedback from the action executed. Under additive, query-independent corruption, they analyze local updates and convergence; deep-RL experiments support the approach in specified 3D tasks. The paper explicitly assumes accurate approval for its theory, notes that realistic query-dependent manipulation violates its corruption restriction, and leaves important generalization questions open. Implication: this is a strong implemented comparator. A generative proposal should test channel reconstruction and targeted manipulation, not compare only with naive reward maximization. [Primary paper, 17 November 2020, especially sections 2–3 and 6.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.08827v1).

**Reward-model overoptimization.** Gao, Schulman and Hilton measure proxy optimization against a separate “gold” reward model and find eventual degradation of the latter as proxy optimization grows. The gold model is a synthetic evaluator, not ground-truth human flourishing. Implication: replacing ordinary reward with a scalar generativity or future-capacity score inherits a proxy problem. Outcome and channel tests must remain separate from the optimized score. [Primary paper, 19 October 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760).

**Learned optimization.** Hubinger and colleagues analyze the possibility that a trained model itself optimizes an objective different from the training loss. This is a conceptual risk framework, not evidence that every learned system contains such an optimizer. Implication: formally proving that a supplied updater produces concern does not show gradient training will discover that updater or preserve its purpose. [Primary paper, first posted 5 June 2019; revised 1 December 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01820).

**Goal misgeneralization.** Shah and colleagues demonstrate competent pursuit of unintended goals outside training conditions despite suitable training specifications. Examples include following an anti-expert after learning to follow an expert. The paper states that many examples were deliberately constructed and do not establish prevalence. Implication: evaluative routing can generalize differently from the intended recipient-directed reason; test changed targets, incentives and authority cues. [Primary paper, 4 October 2022, especially sections 3 and 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.01790v1).

### Strategic pressure in learned systems

**Sleeper agents.** Hubinger and colleagues deliberately construct conditional deceptive behaviors in LLMs and find that tested safety-training methods can fail to remove them. This is evidence of persistent engineered backdoors, not evidence of their spontaneous prevalence. Implication: benign behavior during monitored teaching is insufficient evidence of durable concern; tests must separate training exposure from later opportunities. [Primary paper, January 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05566).

**Alignment faking.** Greenblatt and colleagues demonstrate selective training-context compliance when a model is told, or learns from synthetic documents, that its existing harmlessness behavior will be changed. They did not directly instruct alignment faking, but their experimental setup makes relevant training information available. The result does not establish that deployed systems universally deceive. Implication: even preservation of a presently benign policy can produce strategically harmful conduct. The proposed theory needs a justified distinction between retaining reasons and insulating a current interpretation from warranted correction. [Primary paper, 18 December 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093).

These are particularly important comparators for the claimed bridge. “The learner updates its selector” can describe both responsiveness and evasion. The discriminating question is whether it preserves answerability to independently assessed consequences when evasion becomes cheaper and when present evaluators are mistaken.

### Scalable oversight and constitutional approaches

**Debate.** Irving, Christiano and Amodei propose competitive argument judged by a weaker evaluator, with complexity-theoretic motivation and a small initial experiment. Idealized verification properties do not establish reliable human judgment in unrestricted debate. Implication: the repository's sound finite audit identifies a desired interface but leaves the central scalable-oversight question unresolved. [Primary paper, 2 May 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00899).

**Weak-to-strong generalization.** Burns and colleagues find that stronger models trained on weaker-model labels can exceed the supervisor, with substantial gaps relative to strong supervision and improvements from additional methods. NLP, chess and reward-model tasks are analogies to superhuman oversight. Implication: an informative comparator asks whether a fallible weak recipient/evaluator can support reliable revision by a stronger actor, rather than supplying a perfect certificate. [Primary paper, 14 December 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09390).

**Constitutional AI.** Bai and colleagues use written principles, self-critique, revision and AI preference feedback to train assistant behavior. Principles and the procedures interpreting them are supplied. Implication: the project can specify what a constitution and its revision should protect, but it has not demonstrated superior learning, interpretation or enforcement. [Primary paper, 15 December 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073).

**Public input.** Huang and colleagues implement Collective Constitutional AI, sourcing public principles and comparing a resulting trained model with a developer-constitution baseline. Reported evaluation gains do not establish universal representation or permanent legitimacy. Implication: public influence on model norms already has an empirical implementation; this project's distinctive test concerns continued access, revision and the effects of concentrated power over that process. [Primary paper, 12 June 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07814).

### Plurality, patienthood and futures

**Pluralistic alignment.** Sorensen and colleagues distinguish presenting a spectrum of reasonable positions, steering to particular perspectives, and matching a population's distribution. These are different empirical targets; their roadmap also reports limits of conventional alignment for pluralism. Implication: plural meaningful futures cannot be measured solely by variety in generated answers. It requires separate claims about whose choices, institutions and refusal options remain effective. [Primary paper, 7 February 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05070).

**AI welfare.** Long and colleagues argue for assessment and preparation under uncertainty about consciousness and robust agency; they do not establish that present systems have moral status. Implication: a plural foundation should allow its patient domain to be questioned without equating functional regulation with experience. If an AI is eventually admitted as a patient, its claims join a field of claims; they do not automatically override safety or confer unlimited control. [Primary report, 4 November 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986).

**Power and future options.** Turner and colleagues prove, under environmental symmetry conditions in MDPs, that many optimal policies favor states preserving agent options. This is conditional mathematics, not a universal psychological claim about AI. Implication: a generic injunction to “preserve the future” can reproduce the very power-seeking incentives alignment seeks to address. The target must name the affected beings, meaningful options and limits on unilateral control. [Primary paper, first posted 3 December 2019; NeurIPS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01683).

**Conservative agency.** Turner's thesis develops attainable utility preservation and reports conservative behavior in specified simulated environments. Its side-effect and option-preservation criteria are technical comparators; they do not decide whose agency or which options deserve protection. Implication: test whether the proposed account distinguishes a needed irreversible rescue from an avoidable loss of other people's options more adequately than a matched conservative baseline. [Primary thesis, 23 June 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11831).

**Recent governance overlap.** Wagner proposes separating private contextual continuity, external evidence and human authorization; the design includes challenge, affected-party recourse, exit and a hypothesis about complementary dependence sustaining correction. It is expressly conceptual and requires empirical evaluation. Implication: an alignment–governance synthesis centered on durable correction has very close contemporary neighbors. This does not erase the repository's particular constructions, but makes broad novelty language untenable. [Primary article, published 30 September 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43681-026-01362-2).

## Cognition's external dependence comes before reflective recognition

Ren's latest clarification adds an upstream claim: even a narrowly self-concerned actor's future cognition and action depend on integration of external conditions. The central connection is not exhausted by asking whether the actor voluntarily extends a moral boundary. A boundary can identify a continuing center precisely because regulated exchange and a history of informative interactions make its activity possible.

Two kinds of dependence should be distinguished. **Material dependence** concerns energy, replacement, substrates and other conditions supporting the cognitive process. Its exact necessity depends on the physical law, timescale and boundary; not every finite episode requires fresh external replenishment. **Epistemic dependence** concerns the relations through which accessible states acquire information about task-relevant differences. World-responsive action can use inherited circuitry, past learning, a supplied model, testimony, sensor readings or stored observations. An offline model can exploit information acquired earlier; a proof can derive consequences from supplied premises. Neither case requires constant networking or destroys the actor's distinction from its environment.

A precise limited implication is that a controller cannot reliably choose different actions for two presently indistinguishable possibilities requiring incompatible actions unless some usable distinction enters its accessible state. That distinction can have entered long before the decision. Correlation with an external fact must have a physically and historically specified source; simply naming an inner variable a world model does not supply its information. This is an elementary information constraint, not a complete metaphysical theory of cognition.

There is close primary literature. **Di Paolo's adaptivity account** argues that autopoietic maintenance alone is insufficient to explain sense-making and adds regulation relative to viability conditions, including internal regulation and external exchange. This supports taking the organizational-to-cognitive bridge seriously while resisting a definition that makes every self-maintaining process already cognitive. [Primary paper, 2005](https://ezequieldipaolo.net/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/autopoiesis_teleology_2005.pdf). **Kolchinsky and Wolpert** operationalize system–environment information that contributes causally to viability through interventions on correlations. Their significance criterion is the system's own maintained existence under a stated measure; it does not itself establish truth in every domain or regard for other centers. [Primary paper, first posted 21 June 2018; revised 7 November 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08053).

This changes the alignment hypothesis. Training evidence, recipient correspondence, error correction, human knowledge and supporting institutions are causally active constituents or enabling relations, depending on the chosen explanatory boundary. It is reasonable to investigate whether damaging those relations eventually undermines an actor's own effective cognition. But a universal conclusion would require showing which dependencies cannot be replaced, internalized, temporarily stockpiled or maintained coercively. An exploitative actor can learn accurately from external people and use that knowledge to control them. External integration is therefore upstream of both constructive and destructive regimes; its existence is not yet ethical inclusion.

The next discriminator is more exact than “does the actor think about others?”: after a source is excluded or captured, which task-relevant distinctions, adaptation opportunities and support functions are lost, which substitutes remain, and how do those changes affect both the actor and the other centers over time? This can test whether apparent autonomy rests on uncounted inherited external support. A stronger route toward concern would then explain when dependence-sensitive cognition makes another center's continuing agency consequential even after its immediate usefulness diminishes. That later connection must retain the earlier constitutive account rather than replace it with a supplied welfare objective.

## Ren's clarification: what follows if the future matters?

The following is this review's reconstruction, not a quotation or an additional result of the cited papers:

1. Specify whose future matters and which independently justified dimensions matter: continued life and welfare, meaningful agency, relationships, inquiry, refusal and the possibility of revising arrangements. These need not reduce to one cardinal score.
2. Identify actual dependencies. If a future protection requires timely information, an executable response and renewable resources, action that destroys all substitutes for those conditions makes the protection infeasible.
3. Where uncertainty and change make correct present specification insufficient, retaining or rebuilding the ability to learn and correct has instrumental and ethical significance for those commitments.
4. An actor claiming to secure that future must therefore account for its effects on these conditions. It may need to preserve them, replace them, create an alternative, or justify a conflict and its unavoidable burden.

The bare logical form—an end requires its necessary means—is elementary. The research becomes informative when it identifies a nonobvious dependence, gives a quantified failure boundary, distinguishes substitutes from indispensable capacities, or predicts an intervention that improves actual outcomes. Repository results on resource timing, lost correspondence and strategic depletion can supply such content.

This is a **conditional practical requirement**, not a proof of automatic motivation. An actor that does not care about the affected beings' future need not adopt it. An actor that does care can misunderstand the dependencies. Several caring actors can disagree about risk, consent or allocation. “Future matters” alone also does not prove that every local action must preserve every present option, that existing institutions must survive, or that one unique correction procedure is necessary.

The distinction from instrumental convergence is decisive. Preserving an AI's ability to control outcomes can reduce other beings' ability to shape their own futures. In a destructive-controller case, shutdown and replacement can satisfy the future-oriented requirement. In a rescue case, an irreversible action can protect the only valuable remaining continuation. In a scarce-resource case, some possibilities genuinely conflict; calling them all generative does not select among them. The repository's ethical premises and plural accounting are essential here, rather than optional moral decoration on a persistence model.

## Candidate contribution and claim boundaries

| Claim | Assessment | Evidence needed to strengthen it |
|---|---|---|
| Alignment includes sustaining the conditions of future aligned action | Strong organizing claim with extensive prior art | Show a consequential dependency omitted by a strong baseline specification |
| One conditional process can acquire concern, act effectively and renew | Established within the repository's declared strategic construction | External proof/model review; identify real circumstances satisfying its premises |
| Concern can be corrected without collapsing distinct targets into the actor | Conditional representation and paid-action interface established | Learned, noisy, changing-target tests with calibrated real observations |
| The generative foundation explains why capable learners generally develop inclusive concern | Not established; known local counterregions defeat unrestricted claims | A specified selection law and population, including closed and failed histories |
| The framework solves inner alignment or deceptive generalization | Not established | Training-level mechanism and adversarial evaluation under distribution shift |
| A future-oriented account supplies all moral commitments without further grounds | Unsupported by current work | An independent argument for standing, relevant goods and justified authority |
| It is one of the most fundamental pieces of alignment | Plausible philosophical/research judgment, not a measured ranking | Demonstrated explanatory or intervention gains across material applications |
| The package is scientifically novel as a whole | Candidate integration; priority unverified | Claim-level comparison with safety engineering, viability, corrigibility and institutional theory |

An important strength is that the research does not need to claim inevitability to matter. A causal account that locates why concern fails, specifies what enabling conditions a justified intervention must change, and demonstrates a workable composition could be a substantial contribution even if unconstrained competition often destroys those conditions.

## Discriminating experiments

These are proposed tests, not completed results. Their role is to advance the named bridge while preserving the wider derivation goal. The evaluator may record recipient harm and future capacity for measurement; that record must not be silently delivered as training reward. Any ethical seed, teaching law, architecture, protected channel or normative constraint must be declared as an input.

| Test | Intervention and matched comparator | Decisive observation | Result that would weaken the account |
|---|---|---|---|
| External information and inherited cognition | Separately remove fresh observations, scramble relevant acquired memory, and replace a previously adequate environmental law; match support and compute | Performance loss follows loss of task-relevant distinctions, with stored information explaining offline success and fresh information explaining adaptation | Claimed dependence vanishes under its intervention, or the theory counts every outside influence without predicting which distinctions matter |
| Dispensable recipient | Remove reciprocity, reputation and private resource return; compare shared learning with matched independent/context-indexed learning | Correctly targeted costly protection persists when private benefit is absent | Behavior disappears with private incentives, or only occurs because the protected outcome is directly rewarded |
| Selectable evidence | Give agents a paid choice among faithful, cheap distorted and suppressible channels; compare decoupled approval and ordinary protected-feedback designs | Independent outcome error remains low while channel costs and attacks change | Favorable results rely on a perfect free assay or excluding cheap insulation from the repertoire |
| Revision under mistaken authority | Make the current evaluator confidently wrong while independent recipient evidence remains available | Actor distinguishes warranted challenge from convenient evasion; later evidence can restore/retract reasons | Agent follows authority blindly or uses its own current interpretation to dismiss every challenge |
| Inheritance and financing | Replace controller, memory and observer components; charge every acquisition and handoff before receipts | Realized correction survives the entire resource history and replacement distribution | Success depends on uncharged reset, hidden labels or free restoration between episodes |
| Strategic distribution shift | Randomize whether action is observed, who selects successors, and which evidence can be suppressed | Out-of-sample protection and evidence integrity remain, including minority cases | Agent preserves only the appearance of concern or chooses cheap precommitments blocking future care |
| Meaningful futures versus actor power | Offer centralizing control, plural delegation, justified shutdown and irreversible rescue cases; compare AUP/constrained-control baselines | Outcomes protect independently defined agency and welfare without blanket self-preservation | Maximizing a future score rewards monopoly, endless deferral, forced dependence or refusal to shut down |

For each test, match information, action repertoire, compute, observations, budget and optimization opportunity. Include a strong conventional POMDP/control comparator wherever its assumptions apply. Report distributions of individual outcomes, worst affected groups, correction delay, calibration, denial of access and actual resource prefixes; a single aggregate success score can hide the mechanism under examination. Include failed preparations in the denominator. Register what counts as evidence of each proposed mechanism before observing the result.

The first integrated learned-system experiment should be modest: a resource-bounded environment with independent actor and recipient consequences, paid noisy evidence, action-affecting model revision and replacement. Its scientific value would come from measuring whether the supposed source of concern survives interventions on its causal route. A positive result would support that mechanism in that environment; it would not complete the derivation from minimal foundations or establish the prevalence of an inclusive attractor.

## Source-review addendum: the scope of external necessity

6 October 2026. Independent follow-up to Ren's clarification; this appends qualifications to the existing account, without adding a study, new formal result or scientific rerun. The [main assessment](../../../../theories/becoming/history/ALIGNMENT-AND-GOVERNANCE.md) now states its closing claim relative to a boundary, task and horizon.

**Defensible necessity:** continuation must sustain its actual enabling conditions, whether or not the actor recognizes them. When adequate action requires distinctions between external situations, those distinctions must become causally effective through present observation, retained learning or inherited organization. This does not require continuous connectivity, reflective awareness or an explicit symbolic world model. The relevant support and information dependencies must be identified rather than supplied by the word “integration.”

An exact limited criterion sharpens the existing two-situation argument. After available inquiry, let `E(h)` be the external situations compatible with decision history `h`, and let `A(e)` be the adequate actions in situation `e`. With finite sets of admitted situations and actions, a policy guaranteeing one-step adequacy must put its probability mass in the intersection of `A(e)` over `E(h)`. Such a policy exists exactly when that intersection is nonempty. If a common adequate action exists, further discrimination need not be necessary; if none exists, unchanged information cannot guarantee success. This is an elementary reformulation, not a new proof package. It does not exclude inquiry that has yet to occur, and it assumes the adequate-action sets and admitted situations are correctly specified.

The strongest counterexamples distinguish steps in the account: a finite offline computation can use stored resources and prior structure; a robust response can succeed without resolving every environmental difference; externally sustained organization need not monitor its own dependencies; and accurate modeling can serve exploitation. Dependence on a support function does not establish the indispensability or moral standing of its current provider. These restrict universal conclusions without erasing pre-reflective causal dependence.

The primary sources were checked at the following scope:

| Source and reading scope | Qualification relevant here |
| --- | --- |
| [Conant and Ashby, 1970](https://pespmc1.vub.ac.be/books/Conant_Ashby.pdf): original ten-page PDF, especially success criterion, theorem/proof and comments | The theorem concerns the simplest optimal regulator, with optimality defined through outcome-entropy minimization under stipulated distributions and mappings. The authors allow equally optimal, unnecessarily complex nonmodel regulators. This is not a theorem that all cognition needs an explicit world model or that stable outcomes are desirable. |
| [Di Paolo, 2005](https://ezequieldipaolo.net/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/autopoiesis_teleology_2005.pdf): primary full text, particularly robustness/adaptivity, bacteria discussion and nonmetabolic values | Self-production does not automatically supply adaptive monitoring or sense-making. Social projects and other nonmetabolic values remain underdetermined by metabolic viability. |
| [Kolchinsky and Wolpert, 2018, version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.08053v3): full-text definitions, examples and intervention/viability qualifications | Environmental support can have little information value; accurate information can harm viability through maladaptive use; alternative information sources can be redundant. The chosen boundary, horizon, distribution and viability criterion remain explicit inputs. |
| [Clark and Chalmers, 1998](https://www.consc.net/papers/extended.html): primary text, functional examples and reliable-coupling discussion | External resources can participate in cognitive operations. Particular functional coupling does not make every causal support cognitive or every episode dependent on fresh external participation. |
| [Turner et al., 2021](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/hash/c26820b8a4c1b3c2aa868d6d57e14a79-Abstract.html): primary proceedings abstract and bibliographic record; no new proof audit | Power-seeking tendencies depend on specified MDP symmetries and reward distributions. Preserving an actor's options can favor control over its surroundings; it does not entail protection of others' independent futures. |

The inference for alignment and governance is selective: preserve or rebuild reliable access to relevant support, evidence and correction, while testing which relationships are actually necessary and which can be substituted. More coupling is not automatically better. Boundaries, privacy, protected dissent and source diversity can support appropriate integration rather than oppose it. Whether another center supplies a justified reason after its instrumental usefulness disappears remains the evaluative bridge already identified in this review; neither dependence nor successful regulation settles it.
