# Alignment as the Reproduction of Its Conditions

## A generative foundation for situated agency and coordination

## 1 Abstract and central claim

This dossier develops a general generative foundation for alignment and coordination: processes produce, reproduce, learn, and select forms that alter the conditions of further production, action, and selection. Where continued activity depends on exhaustible or vulnerable enabling functions, continuation requires that those functions remain accessible through maintenance, replacement, transformation, or supported inputs. Processes of observation, interpretation, correction, participation, and succession have enabling conditions of their own. They belong inside an explanation of durable alignment whenever their continuation is consequential.

The foundation is situated. It permits plural and changing goals, incomplete observation, contested commitments, changing representations, multiple interacting levels, and transformations that replace the original carriers of a function. It requires neither a global scalar objective nor a universal gradient. A biological process need not possess represented purposes; a human institution cannot be evaluated solely by persistence; an AI can faithfully implement a principal's harmful objective.

The mathematical argument has several connected parts. An event model derives selection and its contribution to changing renewal. Balance inequalities constrain the duration of trajectories consuming finite support. Partial-observation recursion characterizes feasible safe action. Explicit participation constraints distinguish collective attainability from continued implementation. Controlled probabilistic reduction and local dependency closure characterize representations that preserve these distinctions. A finite installed-process theorem then separates actual safety and recurring renewal from the existence of a successful alternative policy.

The central claim submitted for review is:

> A general explanatory foundation for durable alignment must account for how consequential activity changes the conditions of its own continuation and revision. Within declared model classes, this requirement yields exact constraints on support, information, executable action, succession, and participation. Present success, selective advantage, or the existence of a corrective policy does not establish that those conditions will continue to be available.

“General” refers to structural applicability across different implementations satisfying explicit premises. “Generative” refers both to mechanisms producing trajectories and to mechanisms changing what can subsequently be produced, interpreted, selected, or revised. “Foundation” identifies a common explanatory structure with mathematical consequences. It does not assert a unique ontology, an exhaustive equation for every phenomenon, or a derivation of all ethical obligations from persistence.

The dossier is complete as a statement of the argument submitted for review: it supplies definitions, principal derivations, an integrated finite theorem, counterexamples, evidence status, prior-art positioning, and a map to the wider results. A complete exposition must also identify where the theory remains incomplete.

## 2 The claim and its epistemic status

Three propositions should be distinguished.

**Structural proposition.** Activities and their enabling conditions can be causally coupled across time. A successful action may replenish or deplete capacities needed for later actions; actors may change the rules that determine future success; those rules may shape later actors and their purposes. This supplies a generative explanatory schema. A particular application must identify the relevant dependencies independently of its eventual outcome.

**Conditional mathematical proposition.** Once events, dependencies, information, feasible actions, boundary processes, and protected requirements are specified, continuation and correction face restrictions that can be proved. These restrictions are universal over their stated classes. Some are necessary conditions, some equivalences, and some sufficient constructions.

**Substantive scientific proposition.** The schema identifies causally important mechanisms in actual AI systems, institutions, or biological systems, and improves prediction or intervention beyond simpler alternatives. That claim requires operational definitions, measurements, comparisons, and evidence. The existing studies supply bounded support and counterexamples; they do not establish unrestricted transport across domains.

The recognition of a generative root does not require proving that its coordinates or vocabulary are uniquely possible. Any finite tuple can be encoded in one variable. Meaningful irreducibility requires restrictions on locality, manipulability, accessibility, description cost, or modular structure. Conversely, an arbitrary transition function can encode a great deal while explaining little. The explanatory burden lies in identifying and constraining its mechanisms.

## 3 The generative argument

### 3.1 Activity has conditions and consequences

An enabling function is a causally specified contribution without which a declared activity, at a declared level and over a declared horizon, cannot proceed through the represented routes. Examples include usable energy, a functioning measurement channel, a trained interpreter, access to a remedy, a maintained habitat, or the continued participation of someone whose work is required.

Indispensability is relative to available substitutes and the system boundary. It must not be defined retrospectively as “whatever was present when the system survived.” A dependency claim should support an intervention contrast: removing or changing the function alters the attainable continuation unless a represented substitute supplies it.

Actions can change these conditions. Training changes later skill; repeated extraction changes a support stock; deployment changes the data encountered by later models; an appointment changes who interprets future evidence; an institutional rule changes which experiences become publicly actionable. These effects can reinforce or undermine the activity that produced them.

### 3.2 Selection and formation are inside the process

Differential persistence can operate without explicit optimization. Reproduction, adoption, retention, replacement, and resource allocation are distinct mechanisms that can make some forms more prevalent. Their prevalence may change the environment, encounter structure, available representations, or institutional rules. Those altered conditions can change subsequent selection.

The cognitive extension is reciprocal: experience and institutions help form agents' representations and purposes; agents acting through those representations reproduce or change their institutions. Cognition is therefore not necessarily an externally fixed policy choosing within an unchanged game. This does not imply that every cognitive or institutional change follows one selection equation.

### 3.3 Continued activity imposes conditions on the conditions

For an activity requiring a vulnerable enabling function, a description of present availability is insufficient to explain future availability. The explanation must specify preservation, regeneration, replacement, substitution, or continued provision across its boundary. If a corrector is essential, its information, implementation channel, competence, and succession require the same treatment.

This recursion need not introduce an infinite hierarchy of supervisors. A finite dependency cycle can contain mutually maintaining processes. An open system can also rely on declared external provision. The relevant question is whether the claimed boundary and horizon include the dependencies on which the conclusion actually relies.

### 3.4 The constitutive argument for continued agency

A goal whose achievement requires continuing inquiry presupposes the possibility of future judgment. Let a specified class of trajectories require infinitely many judgment events. If a transition necessarily reaches, in finite time, a region where no further judgment is possible, no policy taking that transition can realize that trajectory requirement. The proof is direct incompatibility between the required event sequence and the transition's consequence.

This is a conditional constitutive necessity. It neither proves that every person must choose indefinite inquiry nor derives obligations to others from an agent's interest in its own continuation. Finite projects and legitimate endings remain possible. The earlier 生生 proof programme explicitly distinguishes this constructivist strength from an unconditional moral theorem. In the present account, shared agency and legitimate revision are openly declared commitments.

The foundational synthesis is thus a relation among activity, its effects on future enabling conditions, and the continuation requirements of the activity or commitments under consideration. Particular identities, institutions, practices, and goals may change while relevant functions continue. Preserving every existing form is neither necessary nor sufficient.

## 4 System specification and evaluation

Let a sufficiently informative system state be

\[
z=(x,e,c,a,m,\phi,\psi,r).
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(x\) records current activities and carriers; \(e\) environmental or material conditions; \(c\) functional capacities; \(a\) access, relations, and operative authority; \(m\) memory; \(\phi\) representations and interpreters; \(\psi\) represented evaluative commitments where applicable; and \(r\) installed rules for action, selection, learning, and revision. These are semantic roles, not eight universal substances. A justified application can omit, combine, or expand them.

Specify a transition mechanism, observation maps \(O_i\), action permissions \(U_i\), and actual decision rules \(\pi_i\). A decision rule may depend on the participant's observation history and installed memory; it may not use unobserved analyst state. Joint actions, environmental inputs, shared disturbances, and event timing determine the next state. A rule is endogenous only if modeled events can change it and those changes alter later transitions.

An evaluation specification is

\[
\mathcal E=(A,\mathcal D,\mathcal T,\mathcal K,\mathcal U,\mathcal B,H,\varepsilon).
\tag{2}
\]

\(A\) identifies affected parties or biological reference systems; \(\mathcal D\) disturbances; \(\mathcal T\) functions to preserve, recover, or establish; \(\mathcal K\) protected constraints; \(\mathcal U\) permitted interventions and information; \(\mathcal B\) available resources and dated budgets; \(H\) horizons and deadlines; and \(\varepsilon\) tolerated failure levels. The specification can contain incompatible concerns and can remain incomplete. Explicit amendments create a revised specification rather than retroactively changing the meaning of recorded outcomes.

Let \(\iota\) denote the actual initial information state, including a specified prior or a set of compatible states and the observations already available. Define the situated capacity profile

\[
\mathcal C_{\mathcal E}(\iota)
=\{\tau\in\mathcal T:
\text{some permitted policy accomplishes test }\tau
\text{ from }\iota\text{ within its constraints}\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Each test retains its beneficiary, uncertainty, budget, information, horizon, and reliability requirements. A robust test requires one observation-admissible policy to work across all its compatible initial states; a Bayesian test uses its declared prior. Choosing a different policy after privately revealing each hidden state would answer a different question. Inclusion of these sets expresses improvement only under a common specification and model. Many information states are incomparable. Counting labels, options, novelty, or institutional survival is not an adequate substitute.

Separate this possible capacity from actual performance under installed rules. The gap between an available policy and the process that selects, funds, authorizes, and executes it is a substantive object of explanation. A planner's policy witness does not supply that process.

The environment is not presumed fully observable, stationary, or benevolent. Different results below adopt different restrictions. In particular, an exact finite-state model requires sufficient represented memory; a deterministic population equation requires a justified approximation; a normative comparison requires a declared standpoint. No unproved interchange among these assumptions is used. Notation is local to each model; reuse of a letter does not identify different domains' quantities.

## 5 From elementary events to selection and renewal

### 5.1 The event model

Let \(X_i\) be integer counts of type \(i\), \(E\) enabling conditions, and \(S\) selector conditions. For state \(Y=(X,E,S)\), specify three event families:

| Event | Count change | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Type \(i\) produces type \(j\) | \(X\mapsto X+e_j\) | \(X_i b_i(Y)Q_{ij}(Y)\) |
| Type \(i\) disappears | \(X\mapsto X-e_i\) | \(X_i d_i(Y)\) |
| An existing type \(i\) becomes \(j\) | \(X\mapsto X-e_i+e_j\) | \(X_i k_{ij}(Y)\) |

Rates are nonnegative, \(Q\) is row stochastic, and \(k_{ii}=0\). Birth with inherited variation differs from within-carrier learning or switching. Immigration, recombination, simultaneous interactions, merging, and splitting require additional explicit events.

Between jumps, let \(\dot E=F(Y,u,w)\) and \(\dot S=G(Y,u,w)\). For a suitable test function \(f\), the generator is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Lf={}&F\cdot\nabla_E f+G\cdot\nabla_S f\\
&+\sum_{ij}X_i b_iQ_{ij}[f(X+e_j,E,S)-f(Y)]\\
&+\sum_iX_i d_i[f(X-e_i,E,S)-f(Y)]\\
&+\sum_{ij}X_i k_{ij}[f(X-e_i+e_j,E,S)-f(Y)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

This defines a process after domains, regularity, nonexplosion, boundary behavior, and initial conditions are supplied. It is a generating mechanism, but arbitrary choices of \(F,G\), and event rates are not independently explained by writing the generator.

Under a justified large-population scaling, deterministic abundances obey

\[
\dot n_i=\sum_j n_j b_jQ_{ji}-n_i d_i+
\sum_j(n_jk_{ji}-n_i k_{ij}).
\tag{5}
\]

For nonlinear rates this is generally not an exact equation for expected finite-population counts. Moment closure and limiting assumptions must be checked. The derivation belongs to established population-process mathematics.

### 5.2 Exact selection and renewal identities

Within (5), with no external entry, put \(N=\sum_i n_i>0\), \(p_i=n_i/N\), \(r_i=b_i-d_i\), and \(\bar r=\sum_i p_i r_i\). Summation and the quotient rule give

\[
\dot N=N\bar r,
\tag{6}
\]

\[
\dot p_i=p_i(r_i-\bar r)
+\sum_jp_jb_j(Q_{ji}-\delta_{ji})
+\sum_j(p_jk_{ji}-p_i k_{ij}).
\tag{7}
\]

The first term is selection; the others distinguish inherited variation and within-carrier change. None requires a system-wide optimizing agent.

Let \(q_i(Y,t)\) be an independently specified per-carrier net contribution to one support function. Write

\[
\dot E=R(E,t)+N\bar q,\qquad \bar q=\sum_i p_i q_i.
\tag{8}
\]

Define

\[
(\mathcal Tq)_i=\sum_j(b_iQ_{ij}+k_{ij})(q_j-q_i).
\]

**Proposition 1 Selection–renewal decomposition.**

\[
\frac{d\bar q}{dt}
=\operatorname{Cov}_p(q,r)
+\mathbb E_p[\mathcal Tq]
+\mathbb E_p[\dot q_i],
\tag{9}
\]

\[
\frac{d(N\bar q)}{dt}
=N\left[\bar r\bar q+\operatorname{Cov}_p(q,r)
+\mathbb E_p[\mathcal Tq]+\mathbb E_p[\dot q_i]\right].
\tag{10}
\]

**Proof.** Differentiate \(\sum_i p_iq_i\), substitute (7), and collect selection terms into covariance. Exchange summation indices in the inherited-variation and switching terms to obtain \(\mathcal Tq\). The remaining derivative follows the changing full state and any explicit time dependence. Multiply by \(N\) and use (6). ∎

This is a Price-style balance specialized to renewal, not a new general identity. It supplies an interpretable decomposition: what selection favors, what transmission changes, how contributions change with circumstances, and how absolute activity scale changes total support.

Neither covariance sign alone diagnoses alignment. Selection can favor the least damaging type while all types deplete indispensable support; negative covariance can be offset by background renewal or other terms. Allocating an interaction's contribution to individual types may be nonunique. A causal estimate requires a defensible process model, not an accounting convention chosen after observing survival.

### 5.3 What relative success cannot identify

**Proposition 2 Relative-success ambiguity.** In pure selection \(\dot n_i=r_i n_i\), the transformation

\[
\widetilde n_i=a(t)n_i,\qquad
\widetilde r_i=r_i+\frac{d}{dt}\log a(t),
\quad a(t)>0,
\tag{11}
\]

leaves shares \(p_i\) and relative growth differences unchanged while changing absolute abundance.

**Proof.** Differentiating \(a n_i\) gives the stated transformed growth rate; the common factor cancels from every share. ∎

For example, rates \((1,0)\) and \((-1,-2)\) give identical share trajectories from the same composition while total abundance respectively grows or vanishes when the first type is present. Thus those observations alone cannot universally certify absolute viability over this class.

This is an identification limit, not the claim that no success indicator can be informative. In a fixed coupled ecological model, changed abundance can feed back into relative rates and eventually break the equivalence. The permitted model class and observed channel must be stated.

## 6 Support limits and timely correction

Let an independently specified support stock obey

\[
\dot B=R-L,\qquad B\ge B_{\min}.
\]

**Proposition 3 Support balance and duration.** Every trajectory respecting the floor through time \(T\) satisfies

\[
\int_0^t[L(s)-R(s)]\,ds\le B(0)-B_{\min}
\quad\text{for every }0\le t\le T.
\tag{12}
\]

If the net deficit remains at least \(d>0\) until the floor is reached, the available margin lasts at most \([B(0)-B_{\min}]/d\).

**Proof.** Integrate the balance and rearrange the floor inequality. The constant-deficit case follows by comparison. ∎

For a vector of stocks, if \(w\ge0\) and every admissible continuation at the required activity level satisfies \(w^\top\dot B\le-d<0\), its duration is at most \(w^\top[B(0)-B_{\min}]/d\). The weights certify a shortage; they do not define a social welfare function. Alternative supplies and substitutions must be included before this argument excludes all continuations.

The restriction permits finite projects consuming reserves, external supply, changing dependencies, and dormancy with finite total consumption. An ideal lossless support need not be continuously replaced. Trust, authority, or knowledge must not be assigned fictitious material conservation laws.

If correction becomes effective only at time \(\tau\), and every relevant pre-correction action leaves deficit at least \(d_-(t)\ge0\), a necessary condition is

\[
\int_0^\tau d_-(t)\,dt\le B(0)-B_{\min}.
\tag{13}
\]

Adequate information, implementation, and safe continuation after correction remain additional requirements. Sequential delays add; overlapping stages require their actual critical path.

A concrete witness has two indistinguishable faults requiring incompatible irreversible repairs. Reserve lasts ten time units, repair takes three, and diagnosis arrives at time eight. Commitment is required by seven. Each fault is recoverable under full information, but the available information cannot guarantee recovery. Diagnosis at seven, one additional time unit of reserve, or a repair taking two time units restores feasibility under the declared convention that correction completed exactly at the floor succeeds.

## 7 Feedback across levels and possible regimes

### 7.1 Levels are causal roles

The game level concerns actions under current rules, information, and capacities. A meta level concerns changes to those conditions. The same event can act at both levels: a deployment solves a task while changing later data; an appointment supplies work while changing who can revise policy.

There is no presumption that meta dynamics are always slower, deliberate, or controlled by a single actor. Representing their update laws in the same state avoids treating them as an unexplained external governor. In this dossier, meta-attractor denotes a specified attracting or persistent regime of those coupled dynamics, rather than merely any feedback loop. This is a local terminology convention; the v7.3 manuscript also uses the term for a broader recurring mechanism of selector reproduction.

### 7.2 Local stability can fail through coupling

For a declared two-dimensional linearization,

\[
\dot u=-\alpha u+\beta v,\qquad
\dot v=\gamma u-\delta v,
\quad \alpha,\delta>0,\quad\beta,\gamma\ge0,
\tag{14}
\]

the trace is negative and determinant is \(\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma\). Therefore the origin is locally asymptotically stable for the exact linear system if \(\beta\gamma<\alpha\delta\), and unstable if the inequality reverses. At equality a zero eigenvalue prevents asymptotic stability of the linear system; nonlinear terms decide a nonlinear model's behavior.

Each isolated component can restore itself while the coupled loop amplifies disturbances. This is an established stability result and a useful diagnostic, not a universal phase-transition formula. Delays, saturation, discontinuities, stochastic forcing, and additional dimensions can change the criterion.

### 7.3 A complete selection–support example

Let \(x\) be the share of maintainers, \(N\) absolute activity scale, and \(E\) a functional support stock:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot x&=x(1-x)(\theta-c),\\
\dot N&=N[aE-m-\gamma N-cx],\\
\dot E&=\rho Nx(1-E/K)-\delta E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

The state domain is \(0\le x\le1\), \(N,E\ge0\). All parameters except \(\theta\ge0\) are positive. A maintainer pays capacity cost \(c\); \(\theta\) changes its relative return through a balanced transfer mechanism. Maintenance production depends on the absolute number \(Nx\). Physical feedstock and implementation of the transfer mechanism are outside this particular reduced model and must be supplied in an application.

If \(\theta<c\), \(N_0>0\), and \(x_0<1\), then \(x\to0\), \(E\to0\), and \(N\to0\). To see this, support is bounded by \(\max(E_0,K)\), logistic comparison bounds \(N\), and decaying \(x\) makes maintenance \(Nx\) vanish. The support equation then forces \(E\to0\), after which \(\dot N\le-mN/2\). The all-maintainer boundary is an exception because this version has no mutation or invasion.

If \(\theta>c\), maintainers increase when present, but recovery is not guaranteed. On \(x=1\), put \(M=m+c\) and \(D=\delta K/\rho\). Two distinct positive equilibria exist exactly when

\[
aK>(\sqrt M+\sqrt{\gamma D})^2.
\tag{16}
\]

Indeed the equilibrium equation is
\(\gamma N^2+(M+\gamma D-aK)N+MD=0\).
The smaller positive equilibrium is a saddle in the \(N,E\) subsystem; the larger is locally attracting there. With \(\theta>c\), the full local composition eigenvalue at \(x=1\) is negative. The extinction regime can still attract depleted initial conditions. Equality in (16) gives the double-root boundary; it does not itself establish the full global basin geometry.

This example derives collapse, a regenerative regime, and failure of rescue despite favorable selection within one supplied mechanism. It does not show that every competition depletes support or that every cooperation preserves it.

### 7.4 Selection of the selectors

The wider meta-attractor work also models a trait that changes future correction or selection. A representative fixed-horizon result uses conversion capacity \(q\ge0\), carrier share \(0<p\le1\), fixed nonnegative domain exposure profile \(\bar y\), appropriable fraction \(\chi\in[0,1]\), and a return matrix \(M\). To expose the timing premise, let the carrier's support begin at zero and obey
\[
\dot z_i^\chi=\epsilon_i\left(\chi q\sum_j\widehat b_{ij}p\bar y_j-d_i z_i^\chi\right).
\]
For horizon \(T_h>0\), its average realized fraction of the steady support is
\[
L_i=1-\frac{1-e^{-\epsilon_i d_iT_h}}{\epsilon_i d_iT_h},
\qquad
\widehat G_{ij}=\frac{a_i\widehat b_{ij}}{c_i d_i},
\qquad M_{ij}=c_iL_i\widehat G_{ij}.
\]
Here \(a_i,c_i,d_i,\epsilon_i>0\), and \(\widehat b_{ij}\ge0\). These are local field parameters, distinct from the earlier single-stock example. Integrating the linear support equation and summing appropriated returns across the carrier's own decisions gives

\[
\Delta\pi(q)=\chi q p^2\bar y^\top M\bar y
-f p^{\xi_f}q-\frac{\gamma p^{\xi_v}}{1+\eta}q^{1+\eta},
\tag{17}
\]

where \(f\ge0,\gamma,\eta>0\), and \(\xi_f,\xi_v\in[0,1]\). Thus

\[
\partial_q\Delta\pi
=\chi p^2\bar y^\top M\bar y
-f p^{\xi_f}-\gamma p^{\xi_v}q^\eta.
\tag{18}
\]

When \(\chi\bar y^\top M\bar y>0\) and \(f>0\), a positive initial gradient requires
\[
p>\left[\frac{f}{\chi\bar y^\top M\bar y}\right]^{1/(2-\xi_f)}.
\tag{19}
\]
The right side may exceed one, leaving no feasible carrier share satisfying it. Concavity of (17) makes nonpositive initial gradient sufficient to exclude a beneficial positive trait in this fixed-return family. Under pure selection of fixed faithfully transmitted trait values, increasing return on a nondegenerate occupied trait support gives a strictly positive covariance and increasing mean trait. Mutation or within-carrier trait change adds separate terms.

This identifies conditions under which capacity to alter future selection is itself selected. Appropriability, persistence, delay, scale, cost, and transmission are material premises. It is not a theorem that all power corrupts, that every institutional amendment is capture, or that rivals' adaptation leaves the frozen return matrix unchanged. The latest source is the v7.3 selection-of-conversion result; its larger claims require the additional field and transition assumptions in that manuscript.

### 7.5 A field can sustain what an isolated domain would lose

For domains \(i\), the supplied nonnegative field model is
\[
\dot x_i=\zeta_i x_i(1-x_i)(a_i z_i-c_i),\qquad
\dot z_i=\epsilon_i\left(\sum_j b_{ij}x_j-d_i z_i\right),
\qquad G_{ij}=\frac{a_i b_{ij}}{c_i d_i}.
\]
All rate and cost coefficients are positive and \(b_{ij}\ge0\). The forward-invariant state domain is \(x\in[0,1]^n\), \(z\in[0,\infty)^n\). Practice share \(x_i\) is reinforced by rule support \(z_i\), including conversion from other domains. If \(\sum_jG_{ij}<1\), bounded input comparison gives eventual \(a_i z_i<c_i\); thus \(x_i\to0\) from \(x_i(0)<1\), regardless of other domains. This is an eventual-recovery certificate. It does not bound transient damage, and \(x_i=1\) is an invariant boundary.

At a binary configuration with active set \(\mathcal A\), steady support is \(z_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal A}b_{ij}/d_i\). The linear share eigenvalues are negative exactly when \(\sum_{j\in\mathcal A}G_{ij}>1\) for active domains and \(<1\) for inactive domains; the support eigenvalues are negative. Strict inequalities therefore characterize local stability of these hyperbolic configurations. Off-diagonal conversions can make full activity stable even when each diagonal gain is below one. Neither these local results nor a row-sum certificate proves a universal global basin classification.

Calling such a regime capture requires independent evidence that changed selector control produces persistent advantage while impairing an entrusted purpose or protected function. Concentration and rule change alone do not establish that. The model can represent mutually supported institutional persistence, but its nonnegative feedback form is not a universal account of cycles or every meta-attractor.

Transmission also matters independently of support production. In the separate two-practice renewal model, inherited productive capacity and copying of expenditure savings yield
\(\dot x=(\eta-\kappa)E(u_H-u_L)x(1-x)\).
Here \(x\) is the share of capacity carrying effort \(u_H>u_L>0\), \(E\ge0\) is support, \(\eta>0\) converts expenditure into inherited capacity, and \(\kappa\ge0\) measures copying in response to current expenditure savings. For \(E>0\) and \(0<x<1\), high effort spreads when \(\eta>\kappa\) and declines when the inequality reverses; zero support or a pure boundary share makes the derivative zero. These are stipulated distinct channels; accounting for production alone cannot select between them. Dividing output between private incentives and shared renewal can likewise improve adoption while weakening the common support flow. A mechanism for enforcement remains necessary even when its cost is charged.

## 8 Information and the possibility of a safe continuation

For a finite model let \(S\) be states, \(U\) actions, \(C\subseteq S\) acceptable states, \(F(s,u)\ne\varnothing\) possible successors, and \(O(s)\) observations. Represent an unavailable action by an explicit failure transition, or include observed action permissions in the state and policy restrictions. Time, reserves, authority, pending work, and memory belong in the state when consequential.

An information set \(B\) contains states compatible with the whole observed history, including the initial observation already available. Already distinguishable initial observations must not be merged into a fictitious common first decision. Its update after action \(u\) and observation \(o\) is

\[
\Phi(B,u,o)=
\left(\bigcup_{s\in B}F(s,u)\right)\cap O^{-1}(o).
\tag{20}
\]

**Proposition 4 Exact finite-horizon safe-control recursion.**

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak W_0&=\{B:\varnothing\ne B\subseteq C\},\\
\mathfrak W_{h+1}
&=\{B:\varnothing\ne B\subseteq C,\ 
\exists u\ \forall o\text{ with }\Phi(B,u,o)\ne\varnothing,\ 
\Phi(B,u,o)\in\mathfrak W_h\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

An observation-admissible policy guarantees safety for \(h\) transitions exactly when its initial information set lies in \(\mathfrak W_h\).

**Proof.** At horizon zero only current acceptability matters. At the next step, identical information requires a common first action. Every possible next observation must leave an information set admitting the remaining safe policy. Necessity and sufficiency follow by induction. ∎

This includes active sensing. Complete state knowledge is unnecessary when compatible states admit a common safe continuation. Conversely, if the intersection of full-information-safe first actions across \(B\) is empty, observation-based safety is impossible. A nonempty first-action intersection alone is insufficient because an unresolved conflict can occur later.

For finite horizons and finite actions, randomization cannot restore a probability-one guarantee if every action with positive probability exposes a compatible state to failure. It can improve other objectives and satisfy expected constraints. Failure of a guarantee is not a proof of certain failure.

Equation (21) concerns the existence of a permitted policy. Actual institutions may never select or implement it. Decentralized agents with different information require a common implementable strategy profile; a centralized information-set calculation cannot silently grant them pooled private knowledge.

## 9 Learning representations and changing commitments

### 9.1 Information acquisition has conditions

An observation channel requires opportunities, access, competence, recording, interpretation, and sometimes costly interventions. Maintaining it can have value even when taking a measurement now has none. For example, a known healthy current state makes a present test useless, while rebuilding a diagnostic channel with a one-period delay can be necessary for tomorrow's uncertain fault. The option to ask a later question is a maintained capacity.

Records can remain historically correct while losing current decision relevance. Conversely, visible readiness, a sender's silence, an announced assignment, or a retained tool can transmit information even if an explicit message is absent. A successor's information model must include every surviving channel actually visible to it. The existence of a stored proposition alone does not establish access to its interpretation or applicability.

### 9.2 A summary is sufficient for a question

Suppose a statistic \(T(h)\) of history \(h\) preserves all predictions required by a supplied model family. A later family can distinguish histories previously merged by \(T\). In the existing binary-history construction, 00010 and 01000 share endpoint zero, two switches, and four transitions. Those statistics suffice for an unknown homogeneous switching-rate family. They need not suffice when slow-then-fast and fast-then-slow regimes are admitted, because temporal order then affects inference.

**Proposition 5 No lossy summary preserves every new finite-history query.** If \(T(h_1)=T(h_2)\) for distinct histories, the query \(\mathbf1\{h=h_1\}\) cannot be answered exactly from \(T\) on both histories.

**Proof.** The summary takes the same value while the query takes different values. Any deterministic answer based on the summary is therefore wrong on at least one history; a randomized answer cannot be certainly correct on both. ∎

This does not require retaining everything for every practical task. It establishes that sufficiency is relative to the declared future questions. A preserved raw history can also lack a new, unmeasured relation. More storage alone does not guarantee adequate evidence.

In a finite admitted Bayesian model family, a model assigned prior mass zero cannot acquire positive posterior mass through ordinary updating on events of positive prior predictive probability. Expanding the model family requires an admission or construction mechanism. This is a statement about the specified finite family; it should not be generalized to every nonparametric prior or representation-learning procedure.

### 9.3 Useful inquiry need not start with an old anomaly

Let \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) be the laws of every observation available to an admission rule in two environments. For any admission event \(A\),

\[
|P_1(A)-P_0(A)|\le\operatorname{TV}(P_1,P_0).
\tag{22}
\]

The inequality is the defining event bound for total variation. If the available archives have identical laws, no archive-only anomaly gate can admit a useful new hypothesis more often in one environment than the other. In particular, high detection power and low false admission cannot coexist under identical available evidence.

The prospective-inquiry construction supplies environments with identical old archive laws but different usefulness of a fresh linkage between order and later need. A direct paid question can acquire that linkage. Thus hypothesis formation and investigation cannot universally be reduced to reacting to an already detectable residual in the old model.

The same issue recurs over time. Cumulative evidence can support a time-uniform error guarantee under a fixed correctly specified observation model. If the physical relation changes, retaining the old sufficient statistic does not retain the guarantee's premise. Drift detection, response during uncertainty, and renewal of relevant measurements require their own mechanisms.

### 9.4 A bounded formation mechanism

The existing construction distinguishes belief updating, representation expansion, commitment revision, and execution. Fresh cases have independent fair binary attributes \(X_1,X_2\). Harm obeys the stipulated response family

\[
H=(a\oplus X_{j_*}\oplus\theta)\oplus\xi,
\qquad \xi\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\nu),\quad 0\le\nu<1/2.
\tag{23}
\]

Action \(a=1\) also imposes a separate burden. The initial frame cannot condition action on current attributes, and its archive stores only \((a,H)\). A harmed participant can submit a linked complaint. A standing gate can admit it. A paid inquiry collects fresh linked records, and a supplied grammar compares the four expressions \(X_j\oplus s\). Separate gates admit the expression, enable execution, and ratify any proposed commitment change. Training and succession must transmit the interpretation and implementation package.

Under the blind frame, the full \((a,H)\) history has the same law in all four environments. Even an oracle identifying the environment does not make an attribute-blind response satisfy harm ceilings below \(1/2\) for both opposite-need contexts. Information, an executable distinction, and authority are separate requirements.

With the correct distinction installed, require conditional harm at most \(r\) in every context and total action-one burden at most \(b\), with \(\nu\le r<1/2\). The requirements are jointly feasible exactly when

\[
b\ge b_{\min}(r,\nu)=\frac{1-\nu-r}{2(1-2\nu)}.
\tag{24}
\]

**Proof.** In contexts requiring action one, its probability must be at least \((1-\nu-r)/(1-2\nu)\). Such contexts have probability one half, giving the necessary burden. Set action zero always where correct, and use precisely that probability where action one is correct. This attains the bound and proves sufficiency. ∎

For \(\nu=.1,r=.2\), the boundary is \(7/16\). A burden ceiling .45 is feasible and .40 is infeasible even with correct knowledge, full execution access, and agreement about the stated constraints.

Commitments are not entirely fixed in this construction. An admitted account of harm can change a participant's proposed ceiling from .30 to .20; an environmental warning can change the burden steward's ceiling from .50 to .45 or .40. These are explicit experience-dependent behavioral rules. Ratification requires the declared endorsements and veto conditions. An infeasible proposal remains disagreement rather than becoming acceptable by an unrecorded relaxation.

The raw attributes, finite grammar, reflection dispositions, standing, and ratification procedure remain supplied. The mechanism models bounded formation and revision; it does not explain unrestricted concept invention or derive legitimacy from signatures. Equal endpoint preferences can result from deliberation, coercion, or selective exclusion. Their formation histories and available alternatives matter.

## 10 Reproduction replacement and safe transition

### 10.1 Presence is not renewal

A capability may remain available because it is durable, because reserves are being depleted, because another system subsidizes it, or because actual work renews it. These are different mechanisms with different responses to interventions.

Where renewal is required, a minimal balance is \(\dot C=P-L\), with independently specified production and loss. A steady value \(C\) can conceal both high replacement activity and zero replacement. Delivered training, repair, inquiry, and successor acquisition should therefore be recorded as events or flows where relevant.

In one maintained-corrector model,

\[
\dot E=\frac{aC}{1+C/K}-\delta E-uEC,\qquad
\dot C=\eta uEC-\mu C.
\tag{25}
\]

Here \(a,K,\delta,\eta,\mu>0\), and \(E,C\ge0\). Corrective capacity enables replenishment from declared feedstock, and effort \(u>0\) consumes support to rebuild capacity. Put \(m=\mu/\eta\), \(A=a/m\), and \(d=\delta/(uK)\). Positive equilibria have \(E^*=m/u\); two exist exactly when

\[
A>(1+\sqrt d)^2,
\quad
C_\pm=\frac K2\left[A-1-d\pm\sqrt{(A-1-d)^2-4d}\right].
\tag{26}
\]

The equilibrium equation is obtained by substituting \(E^*=m/u\) into \(\dot E=0\). Its discriminant and positivity give (26). The Jacobian has negative trace and determinant
\(\eta uCm[1-A/(1+C/K)^2]\), making the smaller positive equilibrium a saddle and the larger locally stable. The origin remains locally stable. Thus installed maintenance can support a functioning regime without guaranteeing recovery from depletion.

A positive locally stable equilibrium above a protected support floor requires both a lower effort bound for regeneration and an upper bound preventing the equilibrium support stock from falling below the floor:

\[
\frac{\delta}{K(\sqrt A-1)^2}<u<
\frac{\mu}{\eta E_{\min}},\qquad A>1.
\tag{27}
\]

These inequalities concern existence and local stability. They do not prove that the actual trajectory safely reaches the equilibrium or that selection installs the required effort.

### 10.2 Positive average reward can coexist with loss of capacity

Consider a renewal reserve capped at two units. Each operating period uses one unit and receives either zero or three units, independently with equal probability; a positive receipt restores the reserve to its cap. Two consecutive zero receipts exhaust it, and the declared model has no restart. The uncapped mean receipt exceeds cost by .5, yet eventual outage has probability one.

Let \(T_2,T_1\) be expected remaining operating periods from reserve two and one. Then
\[
T_2=1+\tfrac12T_2+\tfrac12T_1,\qquad
T_1=1+\tfrac12T_2,
\]
so \(T_2=6\). This is a constructed finite-buffer result, not a general claim about success-based support. Unlimited saving, different timing, guaranteed replenishment, or a restart route changes the process.

More generally, if an absorbing shortfall pattern has conditional probability at least \(\epsilon>0\) in each successive block while operating, survival through \(n\) blocks is at most \((1-\epsilon)^n\). Independence is unnecessary. A bounded total quantity of one-time improvement credits likewise cannot finance an unbounded count of operations with a fixed positive recurring cost. Diminishing costs, recurring productive returns, and restart must be analyzed separately.

### 10.3 A favorable successor can still arrive too late

Let adoption of a new practice follow
\[
\dot x=sx(1-x),\qquad x(0)=\varepsilon\in(0,1),\quad s>0.
\]
The initial seed is mature; each later adopter matures after delay \(L>0\), so
\[
m(t)=\varepsilon\quad(t<L),\qquad m(t)=x(t-L)\quad(t\ge L).
\]

Under immediate retirement of the predecessor, let old full-scale service meet demand \(\kappa\), while mature successors produce \(\eta\kappa\), \(\eta>1\). Then

\[
\dot E=\kappa(\eta m-x).
\tag{28}
\]

Even granting adoption advantage and superior mature productivity, a service deficit can arise. For \(\eta\varepsilon<1\), it occurs exactly when

\[
e^{sL}>\frac{\eta(1-\varepsilon)}{1-\eta\varepsilon}.
\tag{29}
\]

To verify this, before \(L\) the flow becomes negative precisely when \(x(L)>\eta\varepsilon\). After \(L\), writing \(w=\varepsilon e^{st}/(1-\varepsilon)\) and \(k=e^{sL}\) gives
\[
\eta m-x=\frac{w[(\eta-k)+(\eta-1)w]}{(k+w)(1+w)}.
\]
Its numerator crosses zero once after the negative interval. The exact required initial reserve is
\[
R_{\rm req}=\max\left\{0,\sup_{t\ge0}\int_0^t\kappa[x(\tau)-\eta m(\tau)]\,d\tau\right\}.
\tag{30}
\]
The floor is respected exactly when \(E_0-E_{\min}\ge R_{\rm req}\) in this declared model.

A constructive alternative retains each predecessor until its successor matures. Then predecessor service is \(1-m\), and \(\dot E=\kappa(\eta-1)m\ge0\). But the overlapping capacity \(x-m\) requires actual training and operating resources. Their time integral and peak demand are additional constraints; overlap is not free.

The v7.3 transition construction extends the same logic: a temporary corrector, a delayed durable change, and recurring maintenance must be financed and authorized throughout a path into a protected region. A sustainable endpoint, an invasion advantage, and a sufficient transition certificate are different objects. Failure of a conservative certificate does not prove that every actual path fails.

## 11 Plural feasibility and continuing participation

### 11.1 Several requirements can remain separate

For one finite-horizon model with sufficient observation state, let \(\pi\) range over executable policies and \(z(\pi)\in\mathbb R^d\) be expected consequences, all oriented so lower is preferred. Coordinates may represent different parties' burdens, failure probabilities, resource expenditure, or other declared requirements. Given bounds \(b\), ask whether one policy satisfies \(z(\pi)\le b\).

Assume at least one executable policy. On a finite time-indexed graph, occupation measures \(y\ge0\) satisfy \(Ay=\rho\). Let \(G\) map occupied actions to expected consequences. Then the attainable set is
\[
\mathcal Z=\{Gy:y\ge0,\ Ay=\rho\}.
\tag{31}
\]
It is compact and convex under the specified finite horizon and allowed randomization. A feasible \(y\) induces an executable randomized rule by normalizing action mass at each reached sufficient state. This converse fails if the state representation has merged histories relevant to future consequences, information, or obligations.

**Proposition 6 Common feasibility or a separating obstruction.** For declared positive coordinate scales \(s\), let
\[
t_*=\min_{y,t}\{t:Ay=\rho,\ y\ge0,\ Gy-st\le b\}.
\tag{32}
\]
Then \(t_*\le0\) exactly when all requirements are jointly feasible. Its dual is
\[
t_*=\max_{w,v}\{\rho^\top v-b^\top w:
w\ge0,\ w^\top s=1,\ A^\top v\le G^\top w\}.
\tag{33}
\]

**Proof.** Feasibility of the original requirements gives an admissible point with \(t\le0\), and conversely. Compactness of \(\mathcal Z\) makes the optimum finite and attained. The Lagrangian using multipliers \(w\) for \(Gy-st-b\le0\) and \(v\) for \(\rho-Ay=0\) is bounded below over free \(t\) and nonnegative \(y\) exactly under the dual constraints. Finite linear-programming strong duality gives equality. ∎

A positive dual margin proves every policy violates at least one requirement. Its weights are a certificate, not a discovered common utility or permission to sacrifice a party. A scalar algorithmic objective used to find a witness does not resolve normative disagreement.

The one-step attainable vectors \((1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\) make the distinction explicit. Every pair of ceilings .3 can be met by some action, but no policy meets all three, because coordinates sum to one. At ceilings \(1/3\), a uniform lottery meets all expected bounds, while no realized action satisfies all three pathwise bounds. Expected feasibility, realized protection, and consent are distinct.

Different declared beliefs can also be represented: evaluate the same finite set of executable policies separately under each participant's model, then take allowed lotteries over that common policy set. Convex separation still applies to expected consequence vectors. A common executable policy is essential; separately optimizing for each hidden environment or participant does not produce one implemented solution.

### 11.2 Participation must be assessed when a choice is made

Let \(h\) be a public history and \(a\) a disclosed recommendation. Participant \(i\)'s expected future loss from continuation is \(V_i(h,a)\); its loss from the specified refusal option is \(O_i(h,a)\). Lower loss is preferred. Continuing participation requires

\[
V_i(h,a)\le O_i(h,a)
\quad\text{at every reached decision gate}.
\tag{34}
\]

**Proposition 7 Finite exit consistency.** For a committed continuation rule and a specified permanent-exit option, (34) at every reached gate implies that no history-dependent stopping time for exit improves the participant's expected loss while the others follow the rule.

**Proof.** At the last gate the condition is exactly the comparison. If future exit cannot improve the stipulated continuation, the current best deviation compares that continuation with current exit. The inequality again favors continuation. Finite backward induction completes the argument. ∎

This does not establish truthfulness, effort incentives, coalition stability, fairness, or credibility when the controller can revise promised behavior. Temporary refusal with reentry requires a different deviation calculation. The quality of the outside option matters ethically; acceptance relative to a coercively degraded fallback is not a proof of legitimacy.

For an unmerged finite public-history tree, let \(y_n\) be reach probability of a recommendation node \(n\), and \(D(n)\) its descendant actions including itself. With additive incremental losses \(\ell_i(j)\), the participation constraint is
\[
\sum_{j\in D(n)}y_j\ell_i(j)-y_nO_i(n)\le0.
\tag{35}
\]
Division by positive \(y_n\) recovers (34); zero reach makes the row vacuous. Full-history descendants and a correctly specified outside value are essential. A small reach-weighted numerical residual can conceal a large conditional refusal gain at a rare node.

### 11.3 Aggregate gains and dated implementation are different

In the existing one-period repair example, two participants' passive losses are \(-.65\) each. A fair initial worker lottery gives repair losses averaging \(-.8\) each, so both prefer the lottery before assignment. After assignment, the worker's uncompensated loss is \(-.3\), while the other participant's is \(-1.3\); the worker refuses.

Gross compensation \(k\) paid from their equally owned pool changes losses to
\[
V_W=-.3-\tfrac12k,\qquad V_D=-1.3+\tfrac12k.
\]
Both accept exactly for \(.7\le k\le1.3\). Physical repair costs three before production, so the minimum divisible prefunding is 3.7 under the stated no-credit rule. Initial cash three makes physical repair affordable but the voluntarily implemented compensated repair unavailable. A changed credit, commitment, or ownership mechanism changes that conclusion.

Transfers cancel when correctly summing participants' wealth changes. They can redistribute gains but cannot create missing aggregate surplus. They can nevertheless consume scarce funds accessible at the required time. Material production, personal incidence, participation, and liquidity are therefore separate conditions of implementation.

## 12 Faithful representation and constructive closure

### 12.1 Exact intervention preservation

Let \(S\) and \(U\) be finite, with stochastic kernels \(K_u\). Include declared boundary modes among the inputs, or model their generating process inside the state. Let \(L(s)\) retain protected outcomes and event monitors, and let \(O_i(s)\) record each participant's actual observations. Action permissions must be preserved as well.

A deterministic summary \(q:S\to\bar S\) is faithful under this contract if labels, observations, and permissions factor through \(q\), and every pair it merges has equal next-block distributions for every permitted input:

\[
q(s)=q(t)\Longrightarrow
\sum_{v:q(v)=b}K_u(s,v)
=\sum_{v:q(v)=b}K_u(t,v)
\quad\text{for every }u,b.
\tag{36}
\]

**Proposition 8 Exact quotient and path preservation.** Condition (36), together with preservation of labels, observations, and permissions, is necessary and sufficient for a quotient satisfying this contract and assigning the same retained transition law from every fine state in each block. Under corresponding initial distributions and identical observation-admissible history policies, it preserves all retained path-event probabilities.

**Proof.** Necessity follows because a single quotient row must represent every member of its block. For sufficiency define the quotient row using the common block sums. Conditional on any retained history and action, mixing over compatible fine histories mixes identical rows. Preserved observations yield the same policy action distribution. Induction equates finite cylinder probabilities, which determine the path measure on the countable product of finite alphabets. ∎

This is strong lumpability or controlled probabilistic bisimulation, an established mathematical notion. It is stronger than equality of selected output-trace laws and is not a minimum over every possible predictive or latent-state model. Analyst-retained variables do not become observations available to agents.

Start a partition with identical labels, every participant's observations, and permissions. Repeatedly split blocks whose members have different block-transition sums under any input. Every strict round increases block count, so refinement terminates. Any stable partition respecting the initial contract refines every intermediate partition by induction; therefore the result is the unique coarsest deterministic faithful partition for this fixed contract.

Adding protected distinctions, interventions, or additional supplied model kernels can only refine that partition when the old requirements are retained. A common partition for several models does not imply identical quotient kernels across those models, and an unknown model index is not automatically observed.

### 12.2 Deriving a sufficient boundary from local mechanisms

Suppose the finite state has coordinates \(z_i\) and, for each input \(u\),
\[
z_i^+=f_i^u(z_{\operatorname{Pa}_i^u},\eta),\qquad \eta\sim\nu_u.
\tag{37}
\]
Every next-state circuit uses the old state. Its parent list includes guards, permissions, rule selectors, unchanged-value branches, and state-dependent probability selectors. Conditional on input \(u\), the fresh joint noise has the same law \(\nu_u\) for every current state and prior history. A state-dependent noise selector must be made explicit in the circuit and parent list under a common-noise representation. Modules may share arbitrarily correlated components of the fresh draw; no product-of-marginals assumption is made.

Choose \(R_0\) determining the retained outcomes, monitors, observations, permissions, and any controller memory required by the claim. Iterate
\[
R_{n+1}=R_n\cup
\bigcup_{i\in R_n,\ u}\operatorname{Pa}_i^u
\tag{38}
\]
until its least fixed point \(R_*\).

**Proposition 9 Structural closure gives a faithful projection.** The coordinate projection \(q(z)=z_{R_*}\) satisfies (36).

**Proof.** The finite increasing sequence terminates and is contained in every parent-closed set containing \(R_0\), proving leastness. Two states agreeing on \(R_*\), given the same input and joint-noise realization, supply identical arguments to every retained circuit. Their entire retained next states agree pathwise. Taking the common noise law gives equal joint projected rows. Iterating with corresponding permitted policies preserves trajectories. ∎

This closure is syntactic and sufficient. A written dependency can cancel; unreachable branches or state constraints can make a coordinate redundant. It need not be the smallest semantic quotient. A hidden state-dependent noise law invalidates the proof unless its selector is represented. Persistent shocks need state or an explicitly modeled boundary process.

Temporal feedback cycles can close under (38). Simultaneous equations for unknown next-state values require separate existence and uniqueness conditions. Calling an interacting environment “external” does not erase dependencies if its input choices depend on omitted state.

### 12.3 Expressiveness and its boundary

Finite Boolean registers, single-bit tests and writes, fresh rational coin writes, and bounded finite sequencing can generate every finite rational controlled kernel at atomic macrostep boundaries.

For a row with masses \(p_1,\ldots,p_n\), sequentially choose target \(j\) with conditional probability \(p_j/(1-\sum_{k<j}p_k)\) until the remaining target is certain. Bit tests dispatch the state and input, and deterministic writes produce the chosen next state in a separate register bank. Finite rational paths also show the converse: every such bounded program has rational output probabilities.

With fair coins only and a bounded draw count, possible probabilities are exactly dyadic rationals: pad all paths to one common depth and count equally likely bit strings. Thus one third cannot be generated exactly in that restricted grammar. Unbounded rejection sampling changes the execution and cost contract.

The construction is expressiveness, not explanatory compression: supplied probabilities can encode an entire unexplained transition table. Atomic endpoint equivalence does not preserve intermediate deadlines, computation cost, or hazards during register transfer unless those are represented. Finite generating rules also do not imply finite sufficient state: an unbounded increment/decrement counter has two rules but infinitely many states distinguishable by future decrement sequences.

## 13 An integrated finite theorem

The preceding mechanisms can be connected rigorously in one finite architecture. This does not automatically embed every continuous model in Sections 5–10; doing so requires exact finite representability or an explicit approximation.

**Contract.** Supply finite states and input kernels \(K_u\), actual observation maps, permissions, an independently specified acceptable set \(C\), and finite monitors \(T_1,\ldots,T_m\) for required recurring events. For the safety recursion, use possible successors \(F(s,u)=\{t:K_u(s,t)>0\}\). The initial information set is conditioned on observations already available. A quotient \(q\) preserves all these objects and satisfies (36). For the actual-process clause, additionally supply an installed finite controller with memory, rule updates, execution gates, and boundary dynamics that yield a time-homogeneous joint kernel \(K^{\mathrm{inst}}\). The quotient must also preserve those installed update mechanisms.

**Theorem 1 Representation feasibility and actual renewal.**

1. Fine and quotient models have equal retained path laws under corresponding observation-admissible policies.
2. For a single decision process with the declared observation history, finite-horizon sure-safe feasibility is equivalent:
\[
B\in\mathfrak W_h\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q(B)\in\bar{\mathfrak W}_h.
\tag{39}
\]
3. The installed process satisfies
\[
\Pr\!\left\{S_t\in C\ \forall t,\quad
S_t\in T_j\ \text{infinitely often for every }j\right\}=1
\tag{40}
\]
from a declared initial support exactly when every positive-probability reachable state lies in \(C\), and every reachable bottom strongly connected component of its support graph intersects every \(T_j\).
4. Structural closure in Proposition 9 is a constructive sufficient route to the quotient assumption. It does not supply the graph conditions in clause 3.

**Proof.** Clause 1 is Proposition 8. Positivity of block sums gives
\(q(F(s,u))=\bar F(q(s),u)\); observation preservation then gives
\(q(\Phi(B,u,o))=\bar\Phi(q(B),u,o)\).
Induction in (21) proves clause 2. For clause 3, every finite homogeneous Markov chain almost surely enters a bottom recurrent class and visits each state in that class infinitely often. The two graph conditions therefore imply (40). Conversely, a reachable unacceptable state or reachable bottom class missing a target has a positive-probability path witnessing failure. Clause 4 is Proposition 9. ∎

This theorem joins three distinct questions without identifying them:

- Does the representation preserve relevant consequences?
- Could a policy using the actual information satisfy the requirements?
- Does the installed process actually maintain them and realize the required renewal?

An available successful policy does not imply success of the installed one. Retaining controller memory is not permission to optimize the installed rule from outside the system. Decentralized policy preservation can be included in clause 1; clause 2 as written does not solve arbitrary decentralized synthesis.

For actual safety alone, the greatest closed acceptable set is obtained by
\[
W_0=C,\qquad W_{n+1}=\{s\in W_n:K^{\mathrm{inst}}(s,W_n)=1\}.
\tag{41}
\]
States removed by this finite iteration have positive-probability paths to failure. Its fixed point is therefore precisely the set with almost-sure perpetual safety.

Required renewal monitors must denote substantive events: delivered training, effective response to a recorded discrepancy, or a successor acquiring a usable package. Repeatedly occupying a state labeled “correction available” is not evidence of repeated correction. Deadlines require pending flags and age counters. An ideal capability requiring no repeated replacement should not be assigned a fictitious renewal obligation.

### 13.1 Finite reliability and uncertain models

Finite-horizon safety under the installed kernel has recursion
\[
v_0(s)=\mathbf1_C(s),\qquad
v_{h+1}(s)=\mathbf1_C(s)\sum_tK^{\mathrm{inst}}(s,t)v_h(t).
\tag{42}
\]
This is a probability, not a binary guarantee. Low but persistent irreversible hazards can yield excellent finite performance and zero infinite-horizon survival. Rare positive edges must not be dropped to obtain a favorable closed class.

With matched initial distributions, if the true conditional next-state law is within total variation \(\epsilon\) of the modeled law at every relevant history through horizon \(H\), then every retained \(H\)-step event obeys
\[
|\Pr_{\rm true}(A)-\Pr_{\rm model}(A)|
\le1-(1-\epsilon)^H\le H\epsilon.
\tag{43}
\]
Couple each pair of transitions while their histories agree; conditional agreement probability is at least \(1-\epsilon\). Multiplying these conditional lower bounds bounds the probability of any divergence. Independence of errors is unnecessary.

The premise is a uniform conditional bound, not average prediction accuracy. If initial total-variation error is \(\epsilon_0\), the bound becomes \(1-(1-\epsilon_0)(1-\epsilon)^H\le\epsilon_0+H\epsilon\). Event monitors and observation/action interfaces must be covered if their events are claimed. This finite-horizon bound does not certify infinite recurrence. An adversarially switching family of kernels also requires a different recurrence analysis from the fixed-chain theorem.

## 14 Normative commitments and limits of a direction

The programme's normative commitment is to protect plural centres of experience and agency, their shared enabling conditions, meaningful standing to contest decisions, and legitimate possibilities of revision. This commitment motivates the chosen evaluation specifications. It remains open to philosophical and political criticism.

The descriptive foundation can represent a stable prison, inventive exploitation, or a lineage that renews itself while damaging its host. These cases show why persistence, novelty, complexity, positive growth, or organizational closure alone do not establish goodness. Functional replacement and legitimate retirement also show why preserving each existing identity is not the target.

Goals can change within the model through experience, learning, deliberation, selection, coercion, or institutional amendment. Their empirical formation is an object of explanation. The justification of a changed goal is a separate question. The mechanism in Section 9 explicitly represents some changes without claiming that every resulting commitment is legitimate.

For a declared compliance margin \(b(y,r)\), with physical consequences \(y\) and current standard \(r\),
\[
\dot b=\nabla_y b\cdot\dot y+\nabla_r b\cdot\dot r.
\tag{44}
\]
An improved verdict can therefore arise from changed consequences or changed standards. Both changes can be legitimate, but the equation does not decide that. Recording them separately prevents the system's self-redefinition from silently counting as physical improvement.

Under a fixed public specification, the framework can exclude infeasible interventions and sometimes partially order viable ones. It cannot generally identify one gradient of ethical improvement. A transition can improve one protected capacity while worsening another; a less demanding standard can be justified by an unavoidable conflict or imposed through loss of standing. The reasons, affected parties, process, alternatives, and actual burdens must remain visible.

“Alignment as reproduction of its conditions” accordingly means continued ability to honor specified commitments, including legitimate revision where required. It does not mean unconditional survival of a particular institution, continual surveillance, preservation of every capacity, immunity of existing authorities, or automatic obedience to a principal.

## 15 Cross-domain application and AI alignment

### 15.1 The mapping must preserve interventions

For domain \(D\), let \(S_D\) be its state, \(K^D_u\) its intervention kernels, and \(\varphi_D:S_D\to S\) a proposed map to a shared model. A quantitative transfer claim can require
\[
\sup_{s,u}\operatorname{TV}
\left((\varphi_D)_\#K^D_u(s,\cdot),
K_u(\varphi_D(s),\cdot)\right)\le\epsilon_D
\tag{45}
\]
on the declared region, with compatible observations, permissions, initial distributions, and outcome events.

For a common retained history, the projected domain transition is a mixture of fine rows in the current fiber. Convexity of total variation preserves the bound in (45), so the coupling argument for (43) supplies a finite-horizon event bound. Two domains mapped to the same model consequently have event-probability difference at most the sum of their bounds, capped at one.

These conditions are a target for validation. Passive transition agreement does not justify transferring an intervention absent from the observed regime. Merely renaming variables or displaying similar attractor shapes does not establish (45).

| Role | Human institution | AI ecosystem | Biological system |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activity or carrier | Practices, participants, organizations | Deployed systems, procedures, designs | Cells, organisms, functional processes |
| Differential continuation | Adoption, retention, appointments, replacement | Deployment, allocation, model replacement | Reproduction, mortality, ecological interactions |
| Enabling functions | Skills, evidence, infrastructure, operative rights | Compute, energy, data quality, human expertise, intervention access | Nutrient flows, habitat, tissue function, repair |
| Changes to later conditions | Rules, education, public categories, allocation | Evaluation design, data production, interfaces, human dependence | Environmental modification, developmental and ecological feedback |
| Independent reference outcome | Declared public commitments and personal incidence | Specified human and other affected interests, effective authorized correction | Specified organism, population, ecosystem, or patient function |

Rows identify candidate roles, not interchangeable quantities. Money is not biological reproduction; weight updates are not organizational succession; people are not morally ranked cell types. A cancer application requires a particular cell–environment–host mechanism and clinical reference outcome. No universal cancer attractor or medical intervention is established here.

### 15.2 What this changes in the AI question

The framework widens the unit of analysis to the process that forms, deploys, evaluates, authorizes, replaces, and learns from AI systems. A current model can perform well while its deployment erodes later expertise, independent evidence, meaningful alternatives, or implementation authority. Conversely, some deployments can strengthen those capacities. The sign must be established, not inferred from capability growth alone.

Perfect fidelity to a principal does not imply that the principal's aims or the surrounding interaction are collectively acceptable. Human goals, institutions, and selection pressures are part of the alignment problem when they affect the relevant outcome. Existing multi-agent and sociotechnical alignment research already recognizes this wider setting; this programme proposes a particular account of its continuation mechanisms.

Concrete research questions include whether:

1. Evaluator succession preserves interpretable counterevidence and authority to act on it.
2. Capability gains change the cost or appropriability of influence over later evaluation.
3. Automation changes human skill renewal, fallback options, or knowledge of system limitations.
4. New affected parties and revised questions require distinctions erased by inherited summaries.
5. A feasible cooperative intervention survives its construction delay, funding needs, participation decisions, and later institutional adaptation.

These are proposed applications. The current dossier does not report a deployment establishing a solution to AI alignment. It identifies what such a claim would need to account for.

The outcome–maintenance comparison deserves emphasis. A feedback model makes turnover probability depend on realized harm; a rival fixes turnover at its calibrated mean. The theorem remains correct because its stationarity premise was violated. Maintaining evidence and maintaining its current applicability are different conditions.

### 16.2 Empirical results include failures and unresolved links

The programme's public institutional reanalysis of [DeCaro and colleagues' 2024 study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0307832) reports a 42.59 percentage-point contrast on retained sanction permission, with an approximate session-based interval of 22.02 to 63.17 points. However, 66 of 72 groups retained the same agreement category throughout. The archive did not establish the randomization log behind session treatment scheduling, and coder differences affected five group endpoints. These data concern agreement formation and persistence; they do not demonstrate repeated institutional renewal.

An earlier one-round authority-investment estimate was inconclusive. The microbial study's declared primary predictive criterion failed. The AI selector pilot also failed its first prediction concerning selection of conversion capacity under the intended conditions. These outcomes remain part of the record. They prevent the collection from being presented as uniformly confirmatory evidence for a complete causal loop.

A prospective, unrun handoff design separately randomizes timely verified outcome information before a maintenance choice and successor access after the package is sealed. It asks whether outcomes change delivered maintenance and whether that transmission route changes successor response. Non-maintenance and nonresponse remain in the analysis. A factorial interaction is not automatically a mediation estimate. One successful handoff would still leave repeated renewal and transport to other settings unresolved.

### 16.3 Falsifiability and rival explanations

Balance identities are not falsified while their definitions are retained. Their applications can fail because the proposed dependency, loss bound, boundary, substitution set, or measurement is wrong. The generative account gains scientific content by committing to those objects before observing the outcome.

Useful rivals include a fixed-environment selection model, a stock-only model without endogenous maintenance, a fixed-competence controller, an outcome-insensitive turnover model, an institution with changing beliefs but unchanged executable categories, and a model of initial agreement without continuing participation. Model comparisons should preserve the same declared observations and interventions.

Three tests are especially discriminating:

1. At matched current performance, intervene separately on delivered maintenance, interpretation, and execution access, then test response after turnover.
2. At matched initial authority and competence, vary whether successful actors can change later appointment or evaluation rules; distinguish expertise-driven improvement from impairment of a protected function.
3. Fit a restricted mechanism in one regime and predict withheld interventions, including beneficial competition, failed cooperation, substitution, and harmful inheritance.

A model that can explain every outcome only by changing unexplained functions after the fact has not earned explanatory generality.

## Appendix A A complete finite structural example

This example permits direct review of the closure claim without consulting implementation files. It is constructed for mathematical discrimination, not fitted to an institution or proposed as a desirable governance system.

The state consists of ten bits \((c,y,r,k,a,v,m,b,p,j)\):

| Bit | Meaning in this example |
|---|---|
| \(c\) | Current circumstance |
| \(y\) | Implemented response |
| \(r\) | Recorded response proposal |
| \(k\) | Inquiry or training capacity |
| \(a\) | Operative authority |
| \(v\) | Active veto |
| \(m\) | Delivered maintenance |
| \(b\) | Recorded burden |
| \(p\) | Veto eligibility |
| \(j\) | Initially decorative badge |

All 1,024 states are admitted. Inputs \(u=(u_e,u_o,u_t,u_v)\) are four proposal bits for execution, probing, training, and veto. All sixteen inputs have defined transitions; their effective implementation is gated by the equations. At each step draw fresh independent \(E\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/4)\) and \(J\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\). The same \(E\) is reused across updates:

\[
\begin{aligned}
c^+&=c\oplus E,\\
y^+&=\begin{cases}r,&a\land\neg v\land k\land u_e,\\y,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\\
r^+&=\begin{cases}c\oplus E,&k\land u_o,\\r,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\\
k^+&=\begin{cases}1,&m\land u_t,\\k\land\neg E,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\\
a^+&=p\land(a\lor m)\land\neg v,\\
v^+&=p\land u_v,\\
m^+&=(b\lor r)\land a\land\neg v,\\
b^+&=(y\oplus c)\lor(m\land E),\\
p^+&=p\land(k\lor\neg b),\\
j^+&=j\oplus J.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A1}
\]

Every right-hand side reads the old state. Protect and expose to the participant only \(R_0=\{b,v,p\}\). Its policy may use those observations and its retained memory; other coordinates remain available only to the specified automatic circuits.

The union read sets are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
c&c\\
y&a,v,k,r,y\\
r&k,c,r\\
k&m,k\\
a&p,a,m,v\\
v&p\\
m&b,r,a,v\\
b&y,c,m\\
p&p,k,b\\
j&j
\end{array}
\]
so dependency closure gives
\[
\{b,v,p\}
\longrightarrow\{b,v,p,y,c,m,k\}
\longrightarrow I\setminus\{j\}.
\tag{A2}
\]
The nine-coordinate projection is faithful under all sixteen inputs, by Proposition 9. This is a sufficient 512-state coordinate description, not a proof that no smaller semantic quotient exists.

Two states agreeing on the seed, with \(y=c=0,p=1\), but differing in maintenance \(m=0\) versus \(m=1\), give next-burden probabilities zero and one quarter. Thus the original three-coordinate projection actually fails; the omitted dependency is not merely a syntactic warning.

Now add a mode changing only the authority update to
\[
a^+=p\land(a\lor m)\land\neg v\land j.
\tag{A3}
\]
The read graph includes the badge, so closure expands to all ten coordinates. States differing only in \(j\), with \(p=a=1,v=0\), now have different next-authority bits.

The distinction also reaches the original protected burden. Start at
\[
(c,y,r,k,a,v,m,b,p,j)=(0,0,1,1,1,0,0,0,1,j),
\]
use mode (A3) throughout, and supply inputs \((0,0,0,0)\), \((1,0,0,0)\), then \((0,0,0,0)\). For initial badge zero, final burden is \(E_1\oplus E_2\), with probability \(3/8\). For initial badge one, it is \(\neg E_2\lor E_3\), with probability \(13/16\). These follow by direct substitution into (A1); fresh badge noise has no effect on this particular witness.

An exhaustive producer check compared all 8,192 pairs of projected baseline rows. An independently written checker compared all 32,768 paired state/input/noise branches and separately enumerated the three-step witness. These computations verify the supplied finite example. They do not validate the interpretations of the bits as empirical institutional mechanisms.

## Appendix B Further limits relevant to completeness

**Statistical power and deadlines.** Suppose a decision based on the complete permitted transcript declares a condition with probability at most \(\alpha\) under \(P_0\) and at least \(1-\beta\) under \(P_1\), with \(1-\beta>\alpha\). Data processing gives
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_1\|P_0)\ge
\operatorname{kl}(1-\beta,\alpha).
\]
If at most \(n\) timely samples contribute at most \(D\) conditional KL each and other available channels contribute at most \(I_o\), then
\[
nD+I_o\ge\operatorname{kl}(1-\beta,\alpha)
\]
is necessary. This follows by the KL chain rule and the binary decision map. All visible channels and adaptive sampling histories must be included; finite information bounds require appropriate absolute continuity. Funding a late test does not overcome a pre-decision information bound.

**Lifetime burden.** Suppose burdens are nonnegative, whether to initiate each investigation is decided from prior information, and each initiated investigation has conditional expected burden at least \(h_0>0\). For their possibly random number \(N\), \(\mathbb E[\text{total burden}]\ge h_0\mathbb E[N]\), by summing conditional expectations and nonnegative terms. Unlimited recurring investigation cannot have a finite undiscounted expected lifetime burden under that premise. A discounted, finite-horizon, average-rate, or declining-burden criterion is different.

**Fresh confirmation.** Discovery and confirmation can be separated. If each adaptively proposed candidate is tested on fresh valid confirmation data with conditional false-acceptance probability at most a preassigned \(\alpha_k\), then the probability of any false acceptance is at most \(\sum_k\alpha_k\). Predictably chosen error allowances also suffice when their total is bounded by a declared budget on every history. This follows from conditional error control and the union bound; it does not validate a misspecified experiment, outcome-selected samples, or reuse of exploratory data without adjustment. Valid tests still require resources and initiation before the deadline.

**Replacement work.** Under a reliable complete monotone rollout with mature seed \(\varepsilon\) and fixed delay \(L\), total pending-capacity time is \(L(1-\varepsilon)\): every newly adopted position contributes exactly \(L\) units before maturation, and integrating over adoption events gives the identity. Its total is schedule independent; peak overlap, financing dates, and safety are not.

**Permission and resources can be complements.** A known repair requiring both a tool and permission is feasible only when both are available. Varying either alone from a state lacking both can have no effect. A null marginal effect therefore does not establish irrelevance of an enabling condition. More resources or information expand attainable outcomes only when the old policy can genuinely be emulated with timing, rights, costs, and physical consequences preserved.

**A finite observation record cannot certify unrestricted future adequacy.** Two processes can agree for any fixed observation horizon and differ afterward, or agree under every observed input and differ under a new intervention. Empirical identification needs a restricted mechanism class and assumptions about transport. This does not prevent conditional proof or useful bounded prediction.
