# Prior-art positioning and normative foundations audit

5 October 2026. AI-assisted specialist review support. This memo audits the proposed generative foundation against eight primary references and the earlier 生生 texts. It does not establish priority, provide independent human peer review, or modify the source corpus. The main `FOUNDATION.md` was not yet present when this first pass began; the assessment therefore addresses the established programme and supplies checks for its integration.

## Recommendation

The strongest defensible contribution claim is a **specified integration**: examine how activities reproduce or erode their enabling conditions, how differential persistence changes the mechanisms selecting later activities, and how protected capacities for observation, correction, succession, representation and participation can themselves remain effective within those dynamics. This is an explanatory generative principle with conditional mathematical consequences. It need not be the uniquely possible ontology to be useful.

Do not claim novelty for mutually maintained conditions, feedback from organisms to their selective environments, endogenous effects of prediction, state aggregation, or the political dimension of alignment individually. All have close prior art. A defensible new contribution must identify what the integrated account permits us to derive, distinguish, measure or intervene on that a simpler account misses. Claiming that all of these topics are absent from existing alignment research would be inaccurate.

## Eight essential primary references

The summaries below identify overlap; the proposed additions and boundaries are this review's assessment, not claims made by the cited authors. These are an initial comparison set, not an exhaustive novelty search.

1. **Montévil, M., and Mossio, M. (2015), “Biological organisation as closure of constraints,” Journal of Theoretical Biology 372, 179–191.** Their framework distinguishes processes from constraints maintained at relevant timescales and characterizes organization through mutually dependent maintenance. This is the closest antecedent to the claim that a system's activities contribute to sustaining its own conditions. Cite it prominently, rather than presenting the dependence idea as newly discovered. Our questions about selected authority, changing representations and protected participants are additional commitments and mechanisms; their reducibility to constraint closure requires demonstration. [Author's full text and publication record](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2015-mm-organisation-closure-constraints/).

2. **Dittrich, P., and Speroni di Fenizio, P. (2007), “Chemical Organisation Theory,” Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 69, 1199–1231.** Organizations are closed, mass-maintaining component sets; the theory connects reaction dynamics with movement among organizations. This already addresses maintenance and constructive change in networks where components interact and produce other components. It does not make every viable social institution a chemical organization or provide a moral criterion. A comparison should specify whether our dependencies are stoichiometric production, causal influence, authority or information, and which formal results survive those translations. [Author manuscript](https://users.fmi.uni-jena.de/~dittrich/p/DS2007.pdf); [primary preprint and journal metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0501016).

3. **Laland, K. N., Odling-Smee, J., and Feldman, M. W. (2000), “Niche construction, biological evolution, and cultural change,” Behavioral and Brain Sciences 23(1), 131–146.** This conceptual model joins biological evolution, environmental modification, development and culture; organisms help alter the selective conditions they encounter. Thus “selected forms reshape future selection” is an established mechanism, not a novel general discovery. Our candidate extension is an explicit account of the reproduction of correction, representation, permission and alternatives within such feedback. Do not assume environmental modification is always adaptive or benevolent. [Publisher's original research article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/niche-construction-biological-evolution-and-cultural-change/F724F02AC61EEF1294C676C1CC7C4F07). The publication is in the February 2000 issue; the page also reports online publication in 2001.

4. **Perdomo, J., Zrnic, T., Mendler-Dünner, C., and Hardt, M. (2020), “Performative Prediction,” ICML, PMLR 119, 7599–7609.** Predictions used in decisions can alter the distribution being predicted. The paper defines performative stability and proves convergence results under specified assumptions. This is a concrete antecedent to representations changing the world that later appears to confirm them. Performative stability is neither truth independent of the intervention nor ethical legitimacy; neither does this framework establish institutional conceptual capture. Our account must name the additional feedback through authority, future categories and reproduction of alternatives. [Official proceedings and paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/perdomo20a.html).

5. **Givan, R., Dean, T., and Greig, M. (2003), “Equivalence notions and model minimization in Markov decision processes,” Artificial Intelligence 147, 163–223.** The paper develops stochastic bisimulation and symbolic partition refinement for factored MDPs. State reduction depends on the equivalence being preserved; it is not unrestricted elimination of all causally meaningful distinctions. Our finite quotient theorem belongs in this established lineage. Retaining a vector of protected labels and an explicit intervention interface is a scope choice for applying the mathematics, not evidence that the quotient theorem itself is new. [Author-hosted manuscript](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~givan/papers/mm.pdf). This working link was verified through the author's publication page; the Brown mirror failed during this audit.

6. **Ostrom, E. (2010), “Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems,” American Economic Review 100(3), 641–672.** Her framework studies diverse institutional arrangements, motivation, trust, rules, monitoring and multilevel governance. Institutions can support cooperation; commons outcomes do not follow from a universal inevitability of tragedy, domination or centralization. The present programme should explain variation among these outcomes and examine how functioning arrangements and their correction mechanisms persist. No single optimal institutional shape follows from its generative principle. [Publisher record](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.100.3.641); [full published article hosted by Portland State University](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf).

7. **Critch, A., and Krueger, D. (2020), “AI Research Considerations for Human Existential Safety (ARCHES).”** Sections 2 and 5–9 explicitly widen the problem beyond a single principal and a single AI to multiple systems, stakeholders, interactions and oversight. The report discusses cooperation, races and diverse governance. Our central claim should therefore be a proposed development within this landscape, not that existing alignment theory treats all of society as an external fixed principal. The candidate addition is the endogenous formation and continued reproduction of principals, correction mechanisms and available alternatives. [Primary report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04948); [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04948). The historical author PDF now redirected to another site, so cite the arXiv record.

8. **Gabriel, I. (2020), “Artificial Intelligence, Values, and Alignment,” Minds and Machines 30, 411–437.** This paper distinguishes technical from normative alignment questions and discusses instructions, intentions, preferences, interests, values and fair principles under moral disagreement. It is essential prior art for the claim that alignment needs an explicit normative account and cannot be settled by observed preferences alone. Our public commitment to plural sources of value should be argued alongside this literature. A mathematical persistence condition does not establish that commitment. [Publisher's full text](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-020-09539-2); [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09768).

## Normative and constitutive claims

The earlier [证明篇 v0.6](../../../../sources/07-shengsheng-buxi/downloads-2026-07-08/2026-08-09-生生不息论-证明篇-v0.6.md), especially §§6.1–7.5, contains a useful conditional argument. The model defines a locked region and a judgement event requiring distinguishable continuations. A goal whose achievement requires indefinitely recurring judgement cannot be achieved by a policy that inevitably reaches a region permanently excluding judgement after finitely many steps. The necessary direction follows from the definitions and reachability assumptions.

That result is worth retaining: it locates a common dependence beneath otherwise different continuing projects. It is not a derivation that every agent must choose an infinite project, that every presently meaningful action requires unlimited future action, or that the speaker's present use of inquiry commits them to preserving all others' inquiry. An agent can intelligibly reject continued inquiry or prefer a terminal achievement. An oppressive regime can preserve its own capacity while destroying others'. Such choices may be objectionable under our public commitments without being logical contradictions.

The older text's §7.4 converse also needs care. Availability of indefinitely many branches is not, on its own, a mechanism realizing indefinitely many judgement events. The specified observer must be able to distinguish the branches, and a judgement mechanism must remain possible. For the foundation, use the necessary implication about loss of capacity; do not rely on an unrestricted “avoids lock-in iff continues judging” theorem unless those extra assumptions are made explicit. This audit has inspected the prose proof, not certified a mechanized theorem.

The later [生生不息论 v2 plan](../../../../sources/06-zhishan/shengsheng/生生不息论v2/PLAN-v2.md) gives the stronger methodological foundation. At §§1–2.3 it separates the descriptive account from a publicly declared commitment: morally significant centres of experience and agency, their shared conditions, affected parties' standing, and revisability under uncertainty. It explicitly rejects deduction of a unique morality from natural facts, maximization of a single generative quantity, a complete ontology, and a replacement for all technical alignment research. Section 2.3 acknowledges that a consistent dominator can reject the initial public commitment.

Recommended formulation: **Given a commitment to protecting plural centres of value and their capacity to answer, refuse, revise and create, the dependence analysis identifies conditions that policies must preserve or replace if they are to honour that commitment over the specified horizon.** This supplies practical constraints and sometimes a partial ordering among interventions. It does not automatically supply a scalar ethical gradient, resolve all conflicts, or license a single representative to decide for everyone.

## Claim language for the foundation

| Intended claim | Carefully scoped statement |
|---|---|
| We have the generative root | We have articulated a common generative principle and several explicit mathematical realizations: activities alter conditions that enable and select their successors, including conditions of correction and revision. |
| Alignment is reproduction of its conditions | Sustained alignment with declared commitments depends on maintaining, replacing or securing the effective conditions needed to continue honouring those commitments. Their existence and maintenance cannot be inferred merely from current performance. |
| Human and AI alignment share foundations | Some human and AI coordination problems admit the same causal and dynamical dependencies. The model does not by itself show that their moral status, permissible interventions or empirical parameters coincide. |
| Closure proves completeness | A closure or quotient theorem certifies sufficiency relative to supplied mechanisms, observations, interventions and outputs. Empirical causal adequacy and the completeness of those supplied interfaces require separate support. |
| We see the direction or gradient | Under explicit commitments and a specified feasible set, some interventions can be excluded or partially ordered by their consequences for protected capacities; several incompatible yet admissible directions may remain. |
| The theory explains domination | It identifies conditions under which local advantage can be converted into control over future selection and correction. It must also permit competition without capture, enduring cooperation, and repair. |
| This is a new foundation | The candidate contribution is the explicit integration and its discriminating consequences. Its ingredients have substantial established lineage, and priority for the integration has not yet been established. |

## Questions for specialist reviewers

1. Does the integrated model produce a result that is not obtainable by simply appending labels to an arbitrary state transition law? Name the restrictive assumptions and their independent motivation.
2. Which inherited results come from organizational closure, constructive reaction networks, niche construction, decision theory and bisimulation? Which additional theorem, prediction or measurement follows specifically from their integration here?
3. Are enabling dependencies independently identifiable before observing persistence or failure, with explicit substitution and supported-input alternatives?
4. Does the model predict distinct outcomes for interventions on material capacity, actionable information, authority, replacement and refusal, rather than recoding all failure as “missing support”?
5. When a representation, goal or participant changes, which earlier equivalences fail, and what mechanism makes the revised question and information available?
6. Is “reproduction” used to mean continued effective access, rather than requiring each component to recreate itself or requiring all activity to remain permanently active?
7. Are physical persistence, continuing functional capacity, and legitimate alignment stated as separate properties? Does the account allow durable oppressive systems and ethically justified endings?
8. Is the constitutive norm of continuing inquiry confined to projects that actually require continuing inquiry? Is concern for other parties supplied openly rather than inferred from the speaker's existence?
9. Are protected commitments compatible, collectively feasible and legitimately revisable? Who can contest the protected set or veto a transition, and how is that standing maintained?
10. What observed intervention outcome would favour this account over an ordinary resource-stock model, a fixed-goal controller, niche construction alone or a simpler institutional explanation?
