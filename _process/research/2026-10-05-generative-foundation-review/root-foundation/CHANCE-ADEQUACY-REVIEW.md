## 1. What could genuinely advance

[J3](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/PRACTICAL-JUDGMENT.md) already gives bounded duties of fulfillment, repair and transition after justified undertakings or accountable changes of dependence. [Crossed claims](CROSSED-CLAIMS-INTEGRATION.md) already preserves supplied floors through a new joint route; [chance revision](CHANCE-REVISION-INTEGRATION.md) already protects a warranted settlement at its scope. None should be presented as newly discovered.

The proposed new join is **specific protected support content → what a replacement undertaking must actually continue → a necessary constraint on its initial chance arrangement**. That can make a constraint substantive without assigning a universal severity multiplier. It remains an application of existing responsibility commitments with an additional correspondence burden, not an original history-free solution of CP.

Two simpler routes do not supply that content. Equal basic two-against-one rescue already has BC1's determinate priority; it does not supply an equal lottery baseline. Replacing it with a one-against-one comparison silently changes the feasible protection. A requirement that worsening one request should not reduce its chance would itself need normative defense and, even if accepted, would not identify an adequate starting share. These alternatives are not refuted in every scope; they do not complete this particular inference.

## 2. The most important objection: an old chance is not automatically a right

A support route can be real, beneficial and permissibly used without its current success rate being an indefeasible entitlement. A legitimate arrangement can also be justifiably reorganized. Thus “the old route existed,” “people expected it to continue” and “the old arrangement was legitimate” are each weaker than “this particular future protection must be preserved by the proposed replacement.”

J3 does not automatically turn every worsening of a marginal chance into prohibited breach. Its responses can include repair, transition, release and justified revision. A proposed restraint must specify who undertook what, for whom, over which period, under which capacity and permission conditions, and what constitutes fulfillment or an authorized change. Only then can the actual support law help describe the requirement's prospective content.

“No overriding reason exists” cannot manufacture the original entitlement. It can preserve the force of a ground already established, but it cannot stand in for explaining why this exact aspect of the old protection was owed. Conversely, the undertaking need not explicitly promise a numerical probability. A concrete obligation to sustain an identified service or access relation can have probabilistic consequences, if that is what faithful description of its actual implementation establishes. This is a correspondence claim to justify, not a fairness ratio inferred from observed frequencies.

The strongest rival accepts genuine losses from takeover and still allows legitimate reform to reduce a previously available chance. It can offer a more adequate common arrangement, an independently warranted redistribution, a valid release or another substantial reason. Calling every such change dispossession or status-quo betrayal would assume what needs comparing. The candidate must retain the possibility that the actual dispute remains unresolved.

## 3. Required tests of an application

**One actual alternative.** The compared fallback must be an available whole continuation, with its resources, timing and common hazards. Combining each position's best outcome from incompatible counterfactual arrangements creates no jointly available baseline. An old success rate whose supporting conditions are naturally expiring does not describe continued protection without takeover. Accountable earlier obstruction cannot be used to lower the comparison and then advertise restoration as a gift.

**Keep non-takeover in the menu.** If legitimate support can continue, the common operator cannot assume that a new exclusive lottery must replace it. Retaining, improving or adding to the old routes may be the relevant response. A failure to find a replacement meeting all protected requirements can reveal a problem with the proposed takeover rather than give permission to discard those requirements. It does not prove that every rescue response is impossible.

**Protect content, not status.** Neither wealth, earlier control nor a discriminatory allocation by itself earns permanent priority. Conversely, an unjust surrounding institution does not make every dependent person's basic living unprotected. Specify the particular valid support or transition claim without certifying the entire former distribution. Manufactured threats and dependency do not confer a new authority to choose the terms of rescue.

**Different positions, jointly organized support.** A route protecting Q and R gives each the route's actual protection prospect; it does not split that prospect between them as if a route ticket were a divisible individual benefit. But equal individual prospects do not establish preservation of common support, permissions or history. The completed joint-risk and chance-history results already require those relations to remain visible. A necessary marginal restraint is not a complete certificate of adequate treatment.

**No universal floor from absence.** Where there is no such justified undertaking, or its content does not protect the relevant prospect, this argument gives no corresponding floor. Where the prior protection provides no positive opportunity, it cannot alone establish CP's positive opportunity claim. These are limits of the application, not permission to ignore otherwise genuine basic reasons.

The supported positions, prior arrangements, new operator and authority all form within the same history. This account requires neither isolated value owners nor a larger collective owner. It also does not require every protected life to predict its own future or formulate a subjective expectation before its actual dependence can matter.

## 4. New primary-source comparison

Source-derived summaries are concentrated here. Both texts address ethical transition questions; neither establishes our particular rescue requirement or a numerical chance boundary.

**Lukas H. Meyer and Santiago Truccone, “Legitimate Expectations and Compensation in Changing Circumstances,” *The Journal of Ethics* 29 (2025):635–662.** Read the [publisher's primary text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10892-025-09514-y), §§2–4, the closing discussion of §6 and §7. The authors distinguish normative interests from rights-protected expectations and allow justified change even where expectations were legitimate. Changed circumstances can alter their standing; protection of adjustment and possible compensation does not mean preserving every earlier outcome. Their account supports distinguishing continuing duties from an immutable status quo. Its institutional and autonomy commitments are not adopted as a universal generative foundation or a formula for rescue shares.

**Fergus Green, “Legal Transitions without Legitimate Expectations,” *The Journal of Political Philosophy* 28(4) (2020):397–420.** Read the [UCL-preserved published text](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10137485/1/Legal%20Transitions%20without%20Legitimate%20Expectations_published%2C%20paginated.pdf), pp.400–410 and the conclusion on pp.419–420. Green separates shared practice from predictable conduct and challenges extending interpersonal expectation claims to general legislative change. Particular grounds and permissible identification matter; a justified institution does not automatically legitimate every expectation of stability. He leaves room for transition assistance grounded otherwise. This is a substantive rival to an indiscriminate expectation argument, not a denial of every support duty or a source for making subjective expectation necessary to all protection.

These are link-only primary readings, without new visual page verification, imported source bytes or empirical corroboration. The lottery literature inspected earlier retains its original scope. Search leads about other weighted procedures do not count as additional evidence for this support argument.
