## What changes in the comparison

The existing whole-process dominance comparison preserves relevant differentiated lives while removing a burden. The new comparison does not: one available path would form one subject, another path a different subject. Consequently, it cannot obtain its conclusion by reusing a theorem or judgment about relieving the same patient. This is a new application within the current whole-process inquiry; the repository's inherited Shiffrin discussion already treats harm without counterfactual worsening.

The argument preserves worthwhile experience, relationships and activity in both possible lives. It assigns no welfare level to nonexistence, treats neither possible subject as presently experiencing, and leaves creation versus noncreation unranked. Means permissibility and protection of existing claims do not prejudge the complete choices. Distinctness requires an independently supported identity account; generated selfhood alone supplies none.

## The substantive rival

A comparative person-affecting account can grant the burden's adverse aspect while requiring a reason to prefer one history to concern someone's being worse off than that same person could otherwise be. It can accept that subjects form within generative activity. Its restriction concerns comparisons across histories, not primitive ownership or private repayment.

The positive proposal rejects that restriction: the formative choice's foreseeable imposition can count against realizing A even when A also enables its bearer's worthwhile existence. The burden's reality supports identifying the consideration but does not deductively establish its comparative authority. Calling the relation formative responsibility must therefore retain the distinction between causal involvement, warranted attribution and its defended ethical significance.

## Conditions that could change the judgment

Comparable worthwhile possibilities do not establish equal whole-life value. An identity-sensitive relationship, valid undertaking, different risk, transition burden or affected third party can defeat an allegedly matched comparison. Those considerations must be identified independently rather than excluded by calling B less burdened. Existing commitments and authority remain relevant without becoming a demand that good repay its originator; the [commitment review](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#55-reasons-to-revise-and-powers-to-release) already establishes that distinction.

The conclusion is one consideration favoring a formation path. It establishes neither an all-things ranking, a duty to create, a population total nor permission to replace an existing person. Even accepting it leaves a further explanatory rival: prospective responsibility for imposed adversity may explain the judgment without establishing universal stranger assistance or the unique necessity of this whole-process ontology.

## Refinement review: conditional prevention and different-person selection

The stronger rival changes the interpretation of §7. A nonoriginator may have a reason to prevent adversity before a beneficiary exists when that same future life would arise under either compared option. It does not follow that the rival accepts a reason to select a different worthwhile life. Section 8's A/R/B control holds timing and responder fixed to distinguish those positions. Independent identity evidence is required; labels and present absence of experience cannot establish the claimed histories or erase protected continuities.

The global-filter control is narrower still. Adding genuinely available B can remove q from the intersection of all option populations while preserving A and R. A rule excluding all reasons concerning such contingent people would erase the q-directed ground for R over A. The positive judgment retains that ground without assuming that the complete requirement or strength of reasons is invariant when options change. A pairwise same-person account survives this objection and may still deny B over A.

The affirmative selection argument identifies why actual replacement is objectionable and asks whether those grounds obtain before either future bearer exists. Their absence can strengthen the candidate; it does not by itself establish exhaustive coverage of every identity-sensitive ground. No benefit to a nonexistent q, compensation through r or duty to create follows. Legitimate existing claims and independently protected formative relations remain potential defeaters.

The Frick comparison makes the additional ethical work of selection explicit. The project does not adopt his stronger denial of an unconditional welfare reason to create happy people, his general maximization rule or an unearned extension of formative responsibility to uninvolved actors. Warrant for a particular comparison, universal actor coverage and assumption-free grounding remain distinct achievements.
