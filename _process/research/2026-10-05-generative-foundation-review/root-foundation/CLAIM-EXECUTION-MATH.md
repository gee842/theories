## Partial evidence and a fixed admitted ledger

Let K be independently admitted claims. Claim identity includes its patient and relevant context, reason and addressee. Let H be the possibilities retained by warranted evidence, with the actual situation h in H. A selected action must have separately warranted permission and an actual enabled execution under the relevant physical law. If the partial certificate D guarantees fulfillment for its addressed claims in every retained possibility, it guarantees fulfillment in h.

The corresponding ledger has two distinct predicates:

\[
\operatorname{certified}(k)=K(k)\land D(k),\qquad
\operatorname{unresolved}(k)=K(k)\land\neg D(k).
\]

They partition K. The second is a lack of certified fulfillment, not an assertion of actual harm or a defeat of the original reason. Execution or failed execution does not amend K in this interface. A genuine defeater, change of context, discharged duty or legitimate transfer of responsibility needs a separately justified revision rule. Claim persistence is consequently a scoped premise, not a rule that every past demand remains forever actionable.

There is no cardinal aggregation, private-reward maximization or automatic priority rule here. The action selection and ethical grounds remain explicit. This ledger is mathematical verification state, not a newly built recorder. A physical implementation retaining these certificates would have to provide actual storage and access; the theorem does not grant those for free. The inherited event law accounts for actual action expenditures. Constructing or funding a deliberation algorithm is outside this result.

The concrete `paid_partial_evidence_action` theorem instantiates this contract on `ReasonProtection.Step`, using exact conversions between the generic and inherited trace types. Its possibilities share the claim's initial harm baseline and satisfy the apparatus conditions; permission is warranted throughout that class, while enabled execution is required in the actual state. It derives the addressed no-new-harm effects and exact action bill without another full audit. It does not construct the evidence class or guarantee completion from every alternative state. ## Why certificate accumulation needs a persistence premise

Suppose an earlier trace certifies D₁ and a later trace certifies D₂. The union is sound at the later assessment only if each retained earlier certificate still denotes a fulfilled claim there. For a one-time delivered service with an immutable episode identity, that may be a warranted persistence condition. It does not follow from the existence of a previous certificate, a copied record or the fact that some later action occurred.

In particular, protection over a longer interval is a different proposition from protection over its first segment. The unchanged protection model counts cumulative harm to each patient i by Hᵢ(s). Every enabled event preserves or increases this count; hence so does every actual trace. For consecutive actual traces s → u → t,

\[
H_i(s)\le H_i(u)\le H_i(t),\qquad
H_i(t)=H_i(s)\iff
\bigl(H_i(u)=H_i(s)\land H_i(t)=H_i(u)\bigr).
\]

Thus the exact set of patients receiving no new harm throughout the concatenation is the **intersection** of the sets protected in its two segments. Certified subsets can conservatively compose the same way. Taking their union could wrongly present two separate successful acts as continuous protection of both patients.

This is a consequence of the declared cumulative event ledger. It does not assert that injury cannot heal, that welfare only decreases, or that a repaired later state is morally identical to an unrepaired one. Repair does not rewrite whether an earlier harmful episode occurred. For other outcome semantics, supply and check the corresponding composition law.

## Concrete inherited tests of the distinction

The parent process first protects both patients, reaching harm counts `(0,0)`. Its next current action protects q and reaches `(1,0)`; the later successor episode again protects q and reaches `(2,0)`. The initial full-protection certificate remains true about its original interval. It cannot certify p's protection over the whole later history. Existing energy and actual-trace results are retained; no endpoint or source bank is reset.

An actual delivery cut leaves the installed reasons intact but permits a release harming both patients. This separates a warranted or installed reason, operational obstruction and physical fulfillment. Conversely, the model permits withdrawal without additional harm; an absent protection certificate need not mean that someone was harmed. Neither withdrawal nor avoided hazard is automatically a delivered positive service.

The concrete ledger can annotate both patients as addressed while the reached publication supports only q. That addressed set is a separately supplied specification. It is not a proof that the parent's withdrawn derivation for p remains warranted. Whenever a claim of continued normative warrant is made, the generic interface requires an independent ground for the admitted claim.

These p/q labels denote two patients in `ReasonProtection`, not the two command alternatives for one recipient in the accepted fresh-reason study. No raw-mask translation or physical integration between those producers is asserted.

The additional generic scarcity example must therefore concern explicitly specified positive services, with its complete local menu stated, rather than borrowing the parent's armed-action cost as a lower bound on every helpful action. In the real parent, cheaper voluntary safeguards and withdrawal remain possible.
