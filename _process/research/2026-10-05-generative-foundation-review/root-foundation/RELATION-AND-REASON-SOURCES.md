This is a primary philosophical comparison, not an empirical result or proof that every capable process necessarily has the proposed reason.

**Bounded synopsis.** In 2A6, the sudden sight of a child nearing a well occasions alarm and compassion, “怵惕惻隱之心.” The explanation excludes seeking connection with the parents, reputation and the text's third consideration concerning 聲. These responses are beginnings requiring development, situated within a strong account of human moral capacities and humane government. In 7A45–46, care remains differentiated: familial intimacy, humane concern for people and care for things are not identical responses; appropriate priorities also matter. The passages therefore combine noninstrumental responsiveness with differentiation, rather than making all concern identical or wholly elective.

## 3. What changes in the proposed alternatives

“Suffering itself or relationship?” can misleadingly oppose a detached property to a subsequently created moral bridge. The actual case already concerns a differentiated undergoing and a possible response within the same unfolding. Its relief is something that this available activity could accomplish. Those relations identify the practical question before any friendship, promise or helping project is formed.

The comparator suggests asking what becomes appropriately salient in an encounter, while keeping differentiated relationships and responses. It does not require treating all people as one's children, inventing a prior attachment to an unfamiliar sufferer, or deriving every reason from that sufferer's own capacity for action.

Three source limits matter. First, excluding a motive of **seeking** connection with the parents does not explicitly stipulate that no prior connection exists. An unfamiliar child without an antecedent undertaking is our deliberate reconstruction of the generic encounter, not an extra sentence in the text. Second, the child's danger concerns prevention; the passage does not stipulate already occurring severe pain. Its extension to the repository's suffering case requires an argument about the relevant continuity. Third, excluding these particular motives does not exclude every learned disposition or background concern.

The source's moral-capacity claim is substantive. It cannot be reduced silently to a reflex description; equally, calling a response a moral beginning does not prove its universal normative correctness. Neither the asserted human commonality nor a reader's intuitive response demonstrates a theorem covering every possible practical agent.

## 4. Benefit, fitting response and action reason

Keep three tests distinct:

| Proposed connection | What would support it | What remains insufficient |
|---|---|---|
| Actual benefit | The intervention prevents or relieves the identified adverse condition | A helper's satisfaction or disappearance of a report |
| Fitting concern | The condition makes concern appropriate, independently of its occurrence | Merely predicting alarm or compassion |
| Practical favor | What the available intervention would accomplish positively justifies this choice before an undertaking | Mere permission, a fitting emotion alone, or successful execution of an already chosen goal |

The direct candidate affirms the third connection in the restricted case. Present access and actual benefit jointly identify its ground; the claim need not concern benefit considered apart from every response position. This is the sense in which a reason can be relational without requiring an antecedent privileged relation.

A special relationship may nevertheless constitute a different reason, an owed obligation or a new good. It may alter appropriate response and legitimate authority. Nothing in the affirmative baseline requires explaining all these differences as multiplication of one fixed quantity. Conversely, demonstrating a genuinely relational obligation does not establish that every patient-grounded contribution requires that relationship.

The serious rival grants benefit and possibly fitting concern but denies any positive practical favor until a further normative bond arises. If it instead holds present eligible access and the specified undergoing–relief conditions sufficient for practical favor, it accepts the proposed scope under relational language. The dispute persists only where something additional is required. That condition and its exclusivity must be explained independently.

## 5. Adjudication and limits

The primary comparison supports a coherent alternative to exclusive antecedent-bond grounding: responsiveness can be warranted in an encounter while differentiated care retains its own grounds. The account remains a defended philosophical position. A spontaneous response is neither a substitute for identifying permissible means nor an argument that every fitting feeling requires action.
