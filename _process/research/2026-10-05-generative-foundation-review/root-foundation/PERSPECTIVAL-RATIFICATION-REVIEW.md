# Ratification under dependence: counterreview and adjudication

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored philosophical continuation of [formed goods and transition responsibility](PERSPECTIVAL-TRAJECTORY-COUNTERREVIEW.md), under [Ren's exploration authorization](../../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-07-perspectival-trajectory-research-direction.md) and the [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). **Status:** specified cases and substantive conditional judgments for internal review; no mathematical proof, empirical observation or completed universal grounding.

**Adjudication:** current competent ratification can alter an arrangement's authority, and a valid specific release can end a reparative claim. Dependence does not automatically invalidate either. Legitimately constituted common decision procedures can also bind present dissenters within their warranted scope. The correction is therefore not that endorsement never changes authority. It is that enjoyment, approval, permission, release and a common decision are different practical achievements within one history; the occurrence of one does not establish the others.

**Concrete positive result:** in the specified association below, members can authorize a demanding future practice while retaining an outstanding claim concerning the earlier unauthorized expansion. Other members may validly release such a claim under specified conditions. A common schedule may also remain binding through a warranted decision procedure despite disagreement. None of these judgments makes every acquired attachment an authority to impose further terms, or every loss of attachment something others must prevent.

## 1. Inherited results and the new question

The [canonical account](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md#formation-acquired-good-and-the-cost-of-change) already distinguishes prospective formation, acquired present goods and transformation. [Whole-practical-ground integration](WHOLE-PRACTICAL-GROUND-INTEGRATION.md#4-ethical-assessment-of-the-candidate) already compares introducing a burden, withdrawing support after invited reliance, declining new support and completing temporary support without the specified dependence. It establishes no universal assistance rule merely from additional directed responsibility. Its historical-responsibility test already rejects erasing an independently warranted claim simply by disabling its uptake. These results are inherited, not newly established here.

The question now changes: **what actual subsequent activity can legitimately change a historical responsibility or authorize a continuation?** Merely saying that a ground remains after selective forgetting does not answer this. A release, completed repair, changed undertaking or authorized common decision can change relevant relationships rather than conceal them. The account must permit this possibility if it treats agency as genuinely formative.

The universal objective concerns differentiated practical significance across intelligence. It need not impose the same act on every position. A teacher, member, coordinator, dissenter and uninvolved observer can have different reasons concerning the same continuing practice. These roles are established through actual activity and its history, not independently complete owners to whom value must later be transferred.

## 2. What ratification could do within one unfolding

Learning and shared work develop skills, attachments, expectations and practical capacities together. Some ensuing speech and conduct can then organize the continuation: an invitation can open participation, an undertaking can make reliance appropriate, and a decision can settle a common schedule. Their normative authority is under examination, not inferred merely from communication causing a predictable reaction.

Four functions need to be identified rather than bundled into one positive utterance:

| Function in the shared history | Relevant question |
|---|---|
| Appreciation or approval | What is enjoyed, endorsed or understood as worthwhile? |
| Authorization of specified conduct | Which future activity has been permitted, by whom, under which conditions? |
| Release or settlement of a specified claim | Which outstanding demand has been fulfilled, replaced or validly relinquished? |
| An authoritative common decision | Which question can this procedure settle, with what continuing protection for affected participants? |

These are not fundamental kinds or separate sovereign compartments. They distinguish what the particular relation accomplishes. One event can perform several functions, but its content, authority and circumstances must support each. A simple “I want to stay” may express appreciation and authorize continued ordinary participation while leaving a prior repair demand unchanged. A carefully specified settlement can do more.

No decision can be assessed outside the conditions under which it forms. Equally, requiring a preference untouched by social formation would eliminate every relevant exercise of agency. Dependence, attachment and limited alternatives are conditions to examine; they are not universal invalidators. A choice among difficult options can remain meaningful and normatively effective.

## 3. Strongest ratification and commitment rival

The **present-association rival** preserves the previous review's full history: genuine skill and attachment, initial permissions, the office's later expansion of control, actual dependence, mixed endorsement and dissent, and real transition losses. It can criticize the expansion when it occurred. It nevertheless makes three serious claims.

First, members need not remain normatively frozen at their earlier stage of formation. Their present competent understanding can authorize terms they could not previously appreciate. An account that treats those terms as forever contaminated by their origin would deny the participants' current activity any ability to transform the relation.

Second, commitments may remain binding after enthusiasm changes. A shared practice can require stable coordination, and some of its good consists in continuing together through ordinary difficulty. The fact that a later perspective prefers exit does not show that earlier commitment has no remaining force. Otherwise the proposed defense of present agency would privilege the latest preference without argument.

Third, an authorized common procedure can settle a question despite disagreement. Requiring a fresh individual endorsement for every common choice would undo the relation that participants established. Such a procedure need not guarantee unanimous approval, costless exit or preservation of every individual project.

This rival is stronger than the claim that every final approval legitimates everything preceding it. It allows wrongs, refusal, limits, release and reform; it disputes which current exercises validly settle them. The affirmative account should accept its genuinely warranted instances and reject its extensions only on specified grounds.

## 4. One history, adjudicated continuations

Retain a learning association that formed a craft, belonging and reasonable reliance on stated terms. Its office then expanded mandatory work beyond those terms without adequate authorization. At the present boundary, the added demands, actual past losses, current goods and available alternatives are accurately understood. The case does not assume that every period of demanding work is harmful or every initial term was legitimate. For the claims below, the expanded requirement exceeded a previously warranted scope, and a particular resulting restoration claim is independently supported. Those are normative inputs, made explicit to prevent the exercise from pretending to derive them.

### A. Continued practice with a retained repair claim

A member understands the proposed next six months, affirmatively authorizes those specified demands, and explicitly retains the earlier restoration claim. The association accepts continued participation on those terms. The authorization is within the relevant scope; no third-party claim or overriding restriction defeats it.

**Judgment:** the future practice can now be authorized while the earlier restoration remains outstanding. Treating continued enjoyment or membership as implied surrender would contradict the practical content of the act actually undertaken. This is not a deduction from private ownership. It follows from respecting the specified relation the parties have now formed, including the retained claim, and from the premise that their exercise has this normative authority.

The association's prior contribution to the member's skill supports acknowledging its actual achievement. It does not enlarge the member's statement into an unrestricted permission. Nor does recognition of a past wrong establish that the member ought to abandon the vocation.

### B. A genuine specific release

At another reached position, a member understands the particular restoration claim and its significance. Current support that is already owed is not made conditional on release. The association offers the actual restoration, and the member instead expressly relinquishes this claim as part of an otherwise permissible settlement. The claim is one this exercise can legitimately release; no dissenter's claim, concealed burden or independently nonwaivable requirement is included.

**Judgment:** the claim can cease to bind in its earlier form. A theory that insists it survives simply because the initiating event still belongs to the history would misdescribe what the history now contains. The release itself is a new practical relationship. It may be generous, prudent or part of a mutually beneficial settlement; no universal duty to release follows.

This case defeats an unrestricted preservation principle stating that formed responsibility must persist until the original material condition is reversed. Some responsibilities can be fulfilled or validly released without that reversal. The ground and scope of the specific release, rather than a generic preference change, explain the difference.

### C. Extracting a release by withholding what is already owed

Now preserve informed approval of the craft and willingness to continue, but make essential support already owed under the standing undertaking conditional on surrendering the restoration claim. The office has sufficient means to meet that undertaking and supplies no independently sufficient reason to withhold it. It deliberately exploits its control over this support to obtain the surrender.

**Judgment:** choosing the offered continuation may be reasonable for the member without validating the office's demand or discharging the claim. A person's competent adaptation to the option actually presented and the office's entitlement to structure that option are different aspects of the same relation. The first need not erase the second. The positive offer can improve the immediate situation relative to abandonment while retaining the objection to how that choice was imposed.

The substantive premise is that withholding the specified owed support cannot generate the authority to extract this release. This is an ethical judgment about the ongoing relationship, not a psychological claim that pressure makes comprehension impossible. It would need revision if the support was not owed, inability genuinely prevented delivery, or a different justified ground supported the condition. Those changes do not automatically validate every bargain; they remove this particular reason for rejecting it.

The contrast between B and C is discriminating even when both members accurately understand the terms, sincerely assent and benefit from continuation. The difference concerns the authority enacted through the whole history, rather than stronger approval in one mind.

### D. Dependence without an invalidating demand

A shared practice faces a genuine short-lived scarcity that the office neither created wrongfully nor withholds the means to remedy. The available continuation is demanding but transparently specified; alternatives are poor. Participants competently authorize a bounded arrangement within its permissible scope. No existing entitlement is withheld to purchase their assent.

**Judgment:** scarcity and dependence do not by themselves nullify this authorization. Insisting on an equally attractive outside option would exclude many legitimate decisions about finite shared activity. Whether the particular demand is proportionate and permissible still matters. The case corrects a blanket equation of dependence with capture; it does not certify every offer made under necessity.

### E. A common decision that binds dissenters within scope

The association previously established an independently warranted procedure for allocating the shared kiln's operating periods. Participants knew that their preferred schedules would sometimes lose. A current decision uses that procedure accurately, stays within its limited subject matter, respects its justified protections and leaves its conditions of validity intact. Some participants now dissent.

**Judgment:** current dissent alone does not remove the decision's authority over the common schedule. The continuation is authorized through the established relation, not through an imagined private assent from each person. This accepts the legitimate-commitment rival in its strongest specified instance.

It does not follow that the same vote can impose new bodily risks outside that scope, surrender another's restoration claim, or eliminate protections constitutive of the procedure's authority. These are proposed extensions requiring their own grounds. Prior agreement is one possible source of a legitimate procedure, not a universal derivation of political authority; neither mere benefit nor numerical majority establishes the scope here.

The corrected principle is therefore: **one participant's endorsement does not, merely as endorsement, authorize imposing terms on others. A warranted common authority can do so within its actual scope.** This is compatible with monist grounding and differentiated participation; no unanimity axiom is installed.

## 5. What subsequent events can change about a past act

An absolute claim that later events never change appraisal of an earlier act is too strong. New evidence can correct a mistaken description; actual consequences can complete a relation whose significance was unsettled; and an independently warranted ratification practice can alter a transaction's present status. In some theories, genuine forgiveness or ratification also changes current blame, demand or standing to complain. None should be excluded by definition.

The question must specify its time and content. Was the earlier decision warranted under the then-relevant circumstances? What status does the continuing transaction now have? What repair is currently due? What can an affected participant presently forgive or release? Those may differ without declaring the past unreal or ethical judgment immutable.

The defended restriction is narrow: current enjoyment or authorization does not **by itself** answer every earlier justification question or release every resulting claim. In B the specific release actually changes a current claim. In A the member expressly preserves it. A theory that classifies those histories identically loses the practical content of their difference. If a legitimate ratification practice also changes a specified retrospective status, that must be retained rather than ruled out by the slogan that the past cannot be changed.

## 6. Whole-grounded defense and exact normative inputs

The affirmative ground is the quality of the evolving relation: lived activity, practical dependence, actual communication, mutual reliance, permissible control, formed goods and the conditions of continuation constitute one assessed history. Authorization is relevant because a competent exercise can help make participation and coordination an activity conducted together; its absence or violation can make that same imposed continuation a different relationship. This supports treating the exercise as more than an indicator of pleasure. It does not derive the entire normative power from the mere fact that coordinated conduct occurs.

The judgments above use the following normative content explicitly:

| Input requiring substantive defense | Work it does here |
|---|---|
| The specified craft, attachment and lived participation have favorable significance within their actual relations. | Blocks treating acquired goods as unreal solely because the formation is criticized. |
| The initial undertaking and induced reliance justify a specified responsibility; the later expansion exceeded its warranted scope. | Supplies the historical claim tested, rather than inferring it from causation alone. |
| Competent authorization and legitimate release have limited normative powers under their actual conditions. | Grounds A and B; without this content, an utterance's effectiveness would not establish authority. |
| Deliberately withholding the specified owed support lacks authority to exact the proposed release. | Grounds C; scarcity or pressure alone is not its premise. |
| A bounded, independently warranted common procedure retains authority while its constitutive conditions hold. | Grounds E, including justified binding of present dissenters. |
| Another's particular claim or protected participation is not surrendered by a participant lacking relevant authority. | Limits an exercise's scope without demanding universal unanimity. |

These need not be independent metaphysical axioms. A fuller whole-grounded explanation could account for them together. At this stage, identifying their common place in the unfolding does not establish all of their necessity. Nor can one simply define “valid” as producing the desired judgment and claim to have derived it.

The same-history rival can accept A, B, D and E while disputing C's restriction, for example by granting much wider bargaining authority to the office. That rival must explain why the prior undertaking does not constrain the office's deployment of support. The affirmative answer is that the arrangement creating that support responsibility remains part of the present control relation; the office cannot cite the member's need for fulfillment as its authority to defeat the fulfillment's stipulated terms. This is a substantive defense through the relation being enacted. The antecedent undertaking and the authority of its terms remain the key supplied grounds.

An intelligence may recognize those grounds and still prefer control. That establishes a possible failure of motivation or selection, not the absence of the defended reason. A rival denying even the contributory moral force is a stronger grounding challenge. Nothing here proves that every intelligence will implement these judgments, nor is that required for the universal normative target.

## 7. Result and revision conditions

The adjudication rejects three unrestricted claims: every dependence invalidates consent; every later endorsement legitimates the full history; and no present dissent can be bound. It also rejects preserving every historical claim regardless of actual release. Each would erase a relevant change within the same generative process.

The positive candidate is **scope-sensitive transformation of authority and responsibility through actual participation**. An acquired perspective can make a real contribution to what should happen next. Its authority depends on the relation it actually enacts, including what it specifies, which claims it can affect, how support is controlled, and the rights or permissions the whole account has justified. This is more informative than treating preference either as infallible warrant or as an effect without normative significance.

For transition care, the practical consequence is differentiated: retain an outstanding claim in A and C; recognize its legitimate alteration in B; respect a permissible bounded commitment in D; and allow warranted common coordination in E. None promises preservation of every attachment, infinite support or immunity from finite completion. Fulfillment, release, valid reassignment or legitimate completion can end particular responsibilities.

Revise these judgments if the alleged undertaking lacked authority, the supposed release did not cover the disputed claim, competence or disclosure was absent, a procedure exceeded its scope, a third-party burden was omitted, or a countervailing restriction was genuinely justified. If “owed support” is itself the only disputed premise, this review supplies a conditional application rather than an independent derivation of that premise.

The universal advance is an identification constraint: differently formed intelligences must be appraised through the same relevant kinds of actual relationships without assuming identical participation or commands. Establishing their common ethical sufficiency remains the larger task. This round has not proved it; it has decided the stated conditional cases and narrowed the candidate so that real agency, legitimate commitment and differentiated authority survive its critique of induced dependence.
