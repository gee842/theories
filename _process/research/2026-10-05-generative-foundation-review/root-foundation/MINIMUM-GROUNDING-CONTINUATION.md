The strongest new local judgment concerns the fresh relief opportunity. P has received a warranted fair chance, Q has won, and P still suffers. An independent actor can relieve P without taking Q's resource, disturbing the result, violating permission or overlooking another cost or claim. Receiving a chance and receiving relief have different satisfaction conditions. The completed chance claim therefore does not itself explain away what the new response would do for P. This supports patient-directed favor before the actor adopts a helping project.

The argument openly grants the local procedure's legitimacy, actual adverse experience, efficacy, permission and accounted costs. Its favorable moral assessment is defended as an assessment, not silently supplied as a proved consequence of those conditions. A critic can challenge its justification or defend a further exclusion. Merely citing the completed lottery does not yet give that exclusion the required scope. The comparison does not create another right against the lottery administrator or imply that P's need warrants every response.

## 2. Fit with a nondual generative beginning

Occurrence and formable transformation remain prior in the explanatory order. “Patient” and “responder” describe differentiated relations within the developing history; they are not independently existing primitive owners. An episode can be localized and its consequences distinguished without making an enduring narrative I foundational. Removing that I, however, neither identifies experiential aversiveness nor establishes the moral address of relief.

The patient-domain question is therefore substantive. If “still-unwanted” requires an occurrent desire, a hypothetical experiencer without conation requires a defended extension. If aversive undergoing itself suffices, that identification needs explanation. Silence, absent memory and absent reflective agency do not settle it. The model must not erase experiential differences to obtain an impersonal notation.

For a response that must first be formed, observing adversity is also different from knowing that an invitation or cooperative pathway can relieve it. The [prospective application](DIRECT-GROUNDING-EPISTEMOLOGY.md#a-prospective-first-step-with-a-definite-way-to-be-mistaken) preserves uncertainty, separate participation and permission. It does not treat anticipated relief as already actual or turn voluntary participation into an obligation to join.

## 3. The next discriminating ethical test

**Does warranted desert extinguish patient-directed favor for independently permitted relief, or supply a consideration against that relief?**

Keep the adverse experience real, distinguish the patient's culpability from whether the experience occurs, and assess a specified effective response whose permission has independent support. A prohibition on interfering with punishment would change the permission condition. Permission to impose suffering concerns a different act. A reason to leave suffering unrelieved might oppose relief without eliminating every favorable aspect of it. None of these alternatives settles the extinction claim by itself.

The positive account must explain why the beneficial transformation retains moral significance in this case. A serious rival must explain why desert removes that significance, rather than merely supporting an opposing reason. These are reciprocal argumentative burdens, not a presumption that the minimum wins by default. Adding “undeserved” to the patient definition, or defining an eligible response as one for which the minimum is true, would evade the test.

A defensible extinction case with continuing adversity and independently permitted relief would require revising the present universal candidate. A justified account of surviving favor would extend its positive defense to a harder case. Neither outcome alone determines comparative weights, duty, authority to compel, a general punishment theory or an exceptionless ethical foundation. This is the next focused test of an existing completion obligation, not a new completion condition.
