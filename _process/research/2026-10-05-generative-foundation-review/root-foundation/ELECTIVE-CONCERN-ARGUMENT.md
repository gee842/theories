# Elective concern: creating a reason does not establish an exclusive gate

6 October 2026. AI-authored continuation of the constitutive-grounding candidate. Named connection: **the normative power to choose a project or commitment → the alleged necessity of that choice for every prospective moral reason to relieve a stranger**. This advances the [private-adoption comparison](CONSTITUTIVE-SIGNIFICANCE-ARGUMENT.md#private-adoption-and-renunciation-isolate-the-proposed-normative-power), rather than treating the unchanged patient as sufficient proof that every reason stays unchanged.

**Result:** a choice can genuinely create reasons, relationships and responsibilities. The grounds of that power do not, merely by establishing its existence, establish that every moral favor concerning the same act comes from its exercise. The exclusive elective account therefore needs an assistance-specific exhaustion claim. This finding defeats a proposed derivation of exclusivity from reason-creation; it does not independently prove that this patient's relief supplies an unchosen moral reason. Our affirmative judgment about that relief retains its separately defended ground.

The advance is an assessment of the power's source and scope, including a genuine voluntarist source rather than a view on which every commitment merely discovers an existing reason. It leaves open a direct debate about the disputed stranger-relief instance.

## 1. Fix the reason whose source is disputed

An independently identified patient p undergoes a severe, still-unwanted experiential burden. Actor a has a known, permissible effective response y. Neither an initial helping end nor a special undertaking is supplied. The question is whether the patient's actual relief already morally favors y for a before a adopts the project.

Keep this target distinct from four other claims:

- Adopting a project may make pursuing it coherent with the actor's intentions.
- A commitment may create a new practical reason, without that reason necessarily being a moral reason.
- A promise or relationship may create a new moral duty, claim or responsibility.
- Given all relevant reasons, the actor may be required, permitted or forbidden to perform y.

None is synonymous with the target. The latest [bounded-rescue argument](RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-ARGUMENT.md) defends a further requirement through an explicit additional comparison. That requirement is not used here to prove the moral favor from which it proceeds. Such reverse use would leave the baseline supported by its own downstream consequence.

The elective rival under assessment is narrow enough to be credible. It need not make all morality elective. It can accept unchosen reasons against interference and valid inherited responsibilities, yet contend that **every positive assistance reason toward an uncommitted stranger requires an authored connection**. Its scope is therefore assistance-specific. Refuting unlimited voluntarism would not refute this restricted account.

## 2. Four different things voluntary choice can do

First, choice can **select among antecedently worthwhile possibilities**. Several options may have reasons in their favor without one being uniquely required. Selection settles what the agent will do. This alone does not show that choice creates the underlying merits, or that selecting none makes them worthless.

Second, choice can **create a project-related reason**. A freely adopted activity can acquire significance through the actor's self-direction, settled direction or ongoing practical identity. We need not reduce every such reason to an unchanged prior reason plus a factual belief. It is possible to defend a normative power whose exercise itself grounds a new reason. The existence, type and limits of that power require an account; demanding a complete preexisting reason for the very same action would beg the question against genuine creation.

Third, action can **create a new relational good**. Building a shared practice, developing trust or forming a relationship changes what exists and what its participants can lose. Keeping the original patient's pain fixed does not hold those new stakes fixed. A reason to preserve the created relationship cannot be assessed as though only a private attitude had changed.

Fourth, an undertaking can **create a directed normative relation**. A valid promise can change what one person owes another. Its content, beneficiary, authority, release conditions and downstream reliance matter. A private intention is not automatically a promise; nor does disavowing a valid promise necessarily discharge it.

These functions can coexist in one history. Their distinction matters because the elective argument can otherwise move from an established example of the fourth or third to an unsupported claim about all reasons in the original stranger case. A new claim to performance, a new good of a relationship, and the original significance of relief are different possible grounds concerning the same intervention.

## 3. Give genuine reason-creation its strongest case

A crude objection says: if choosing could create any reason, any whim could justify anything. That does not follow. The reason created may be contributory, of a particular kind, and unable to change the overall verdict. A harmful option's possessing some practical reason in its favor does not establish permission, decisive moral justification, or an authority to impose it on another person.

The opposite objection says: any successful normative power must merely trigger antecedent rules, so choice never really grounds a reason. That too assumes what is disputed. An account may propose the exercise of will as a metaphysical ground, while relying on a philosophical defense of that proposal. **Theoretical warrant for attributing a power is different from the power's operation being only an application of a prior norm.** Our critique must not require the latter by definition.

Assessment of the activity must also be distinguished from guidance of the particular exercise. A reason to cultivate self-direction or use a normative power need not determine which specific consideration the actor wills into a reason. We cannot demand such determining antecedent guidance as the price of admitting every successful exercise. The prospective relief comparison matters only insofar as its patient-based merit has independent support; it is not a universal argument against reason-creation by choices that lack an antecedently preferred answer.

The [primary comparison](ELECTIVE-CONCERN-SOURCES.md) therefore takes Chang's hybrid view seriously. Her 2013 account allows both given and will-based reasons; a created contributory reason can favor harm without reversing the overall given verdict. It does not establish that such a reason is our target moral favor, or that stranger-relief is among the given reasons. Her later treatment distinguishes genuinely grounding a reason from merely triggering one. These are source claims at the dossier's stated scopes, not acceptance of every part of her theory.

This argument's author separately read the 2013 chapter's printed pp.104–110, including note 55, from the author-hosted PDF by text extraction. The remaining source assessment is delegated. The chapter's explicit two-source structure is sufficient to reject presenting all voluntarism as an exclusive elective gate. It does not settle which source grounds the particular moral consideration under investigation.

Our generative account can therefore admit real normative novelty. A history can form relationships, settle projects and create responsibilities that were absent before. A satisfactory theory must explain this capacity along with prior patient significance. It should not secure universal concern by denying the real significance of agency.

## 4. The source of a power must be distinguished from the scope claimed for it

Suppose a rival defends the power to form a commitment by appealing to the importance of self-direction, the ability to give a life a particular shape, or a genuine capacity of will. Grant that defense for the relevant power. What has it established?

It may establish that a certain exercise of choice creates a reason of a certain kind; that the actor may settle among multiple eligible lives; that some commitments can persist despite fluctuating motivation; or that a specified act can release a specified commitment. Those are positive claims about the power and its effects.

To infer that an unrelated patient's relief has no moral favor before the exercise, the rival needs more:

> Every positive assistance consideration capable of morally favoring this actor's action must be grounded in an authored connection of the specified kind.

Call this the **exhaustion claim**. It is not an inference rule licensed merely by an example of successful creation. It asserts that no independent patient-ground can supply a further reason concerning the same act. A demonstrated source need not be the only source; a necessary constituent of one reason need not be necessary for every reason with similar content.

This is not predicate-counting. The extra condition could be true and normatively important. Nor is it the assumption that every source adds reasons monotonically: reasons can interact, defeat or exclude one another under a justified account. The point is that each of those stronger effects needs an appropriate ground. Establishing a power to create a particular reason does not establish its exhaustive control over all assistance-related favor.

The direct candidate has the corresponding burden. Showing that the exhaustion claim is not established does not show that a patient-ground exists. The two positions remain compatible with the established power until the particular unchosen reason is assessed. Our [prospective relief argument](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-ARGUMENT.md#a-positive-justification-test-with-a-modest-cost) supplies positive, defeasible support for that instance: an actual benefit can make an available sacrifice worth choosing for the patient's sake before an undertaking creates an additional claim.

The limited inferential result is therefore exact: **reason-creation plus its stated source does not establish assistance-specific exhaustion without a further exclusivity argument.** This is a diagnosis of the argument's scope, not a new formal theorem or a proof that all adequate theories must recognize the baseline.

## 5. Does self-direction independently justify the exhaustion claim?

The strongest further argument appeals to the authority to decide what gives one's practical life its positive direction. If every other person's good already supplies reasons, the objection says, the agent ceases to author that direction. This deserves an answer at its actual strength.

First, shaping an action and determining the significance of every consideration relevant to it are different powers. An agent can choose among several worthwhile courses, form commitments, reject proposals and determine its future while recognizing facts it did not choose as reasons. The very exercise of practical judgment involves assessing considerations whose significance is not settled merely by deciding to act. The elective account may contest the scope of those considerations, but it cannot infer that all unchosen moral favor eliminates self-direction without explaining this alleged incompatibility.

Second, a baseline favor is not an automatic command to adopt the largest charitable project. Protection of an agent's worthwhile life could justify weights, discretion, limited duties, refusal rights or limits on others' authority. Whether such protections are valid must be defended. Even when they are, their content need not include erasing every moral consideration concerning a stranger. The [plurality account](../../../../theories/becoming/history/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md#3-plurality-requires-positive-grounds-not-arbitrary-exemptions) explicitly treats those protections as substantive grounds, not arbitrary exceptions or outputs of an actor's preference.

Third, the restricted rival may accept unchosen reasons to respect other people's self-direction while excluding unchosen assistance reasons. That remains a coherent difference of scope. It cannot be dismissed by saying that the rival has conceded some objective reason. But now its defense must explain **why assistance, even as contributory moral favor, falls outside the relevant practical address while other reasons not created by the choice remain**. A general celebration of autonomy does not distinguish those domains on its own.

These considerations undermine the proposed necessity argument from self-direction. They leave room for a richer argument locating a genuine difference between restraint, required aid, and merely favored aid. They also leave room for severe conflicts in which respecting the helper's life changes what ought to be done. Our judgment is that autonomy's importance supports actual participation and protected choices more directly than an authority to determine whether a stranger's relief has any moral merit for action at all.

This is an affirmative comparative conclusion. It credits self-direction as a possible genuine source while favoring a foundation that can contain both prior patient grounds and newly created project reasons. It does not derive C2 from the value of autonomy, or assume that autonomy has already been completely grounded in the causal root.

## 6. A source-sensitive comparison improves the private-choice test

The [earlier private adoption and valid withdrawal contract](CONSTITUTIVE-SIGNIFICANCE-COUNTERREVIEW.md#private-adoption-and-renunciation-provide-a-sharper-contrast) remains useful, but should now be run with distinct arms:

| Arm | What may genuinely change | What the arm can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Private adoption of a helping project | An intention, project, self-governance relation, or successful will-created reason | Whether this particular source creates a new reason; not whether all earlier moral favor was absent |
| Formation of an actual shared practice | Trust, coordination, meaning and the participants' welfare or agency | Reasons concerning newly created goods; the full patient situation was not held fixed |
| Valid undertaking to the patient | Directed claims, responsibility, legitimate reliance and release conditions | A special moral relation with its own content and history |
| Valid cessation of the private source, with no remaining undertaking or reliance | The identified project or commitment reason may cease | Withdrawal of that ground, not automatic disappearance of every reason concerning relief |

These are conceptual distinctions, not empirical findings or claims that every described act has the stated normative power. Each attribution needs warrant. A private change may fail to end a valid commitment; a communicated undertaking may be invalid; an apparent relationship may conceal coercion. Such findings revise the arm's interpretation rather than settling the baseline by stipulation.

The meaningful observation is not just whether helping rises after endorsement. That could reflect motivation, attention, a new practical reason or a changed relationship. The explanation must identify **which ground changed and which normative relation it supports**. Likewise, a created practical reason with the same patient-related content is not automatically a newly created moral reason.

The [counterreview's prospective identification argument](ELECTIVE-CONCERN-COUNTERREVIEW.md#4-a-stronger-comparison-than-observing-helpful-choices) adds a sharper limitation. If H is inferred simply from intentional helping, selecting completed helpful actions also selects the supposed constitutive condition. Successful charity and subsequent patient-sensitive correction cannot then discriminate a reason that preceded adoption from one created through adoption. The alleged power must be characterized independently, and the justification of the available option assessed before its exercise. This limits a proposed observation route without settling the normative verdict.

If a theory accounts for new project or relationship reasons while leaving the original experiential contribution open, it has explained a real development without answering the exhaustion question. If it claims the private source controls all moral favor, the pre-adoption and valid post-withdrawal cases test that additional thesis. The patient being unchanged is one control, not the whole proof; actor-side normative powers must be examined too.

## 7. Adjudication for the unified candidate

We retain the direct patient-ground candidate and strengthen its account of agency. Voluntary concern can create genuine new reasons, not merely make existing reasons salient. Relationships and undertakings can also create new stakes and responsibilities. None of these developments requires reducing the original patient to an interchangeable object or treating the actor as a passive conduit.

This joint architecture is not a guarantee that inserting a baseline leaves every proposed exercise of power unchanged. If a particular power requires antecedent reasons to leave an issue unsettled, recognizing a previously omitted patient-ground may change whether that condition holds. Conversely, a created relationship can change the grounds relevant to a later comparison. The sources must be integrated under their actual conditions, not simply added while preserving every old verdict by fiat.

The exclusive elective account has gained a fairer formulation but not the needed necessity argument. Its credible reason-creation claim explains reasons that depend on endorsement. Its assistance-specific exhaustion claim remains unsupported by that fact alone. The general value of self-direction also does not, without more, explain why all pre-endorsement moral favor must be absent. The independently defended prospective relief judgment continues to favor C2.

The advance primarily **defeats a putative argument that election is necessary**, while providing positive comparative support for allowing both prior patient grounds and created commitments. It does not independently establish the sufficiency of every experiential patient-ground, nor does it rule out every possible defense of assistance-specific exclusion. A sound exclusion argument would narrow C2. A theory that accepts the same moral baseline but gives another account of its ground could preserve the minimum while revising the stronger explanatory claim.

For the generative programme, the resulting task is more precise: explain how a common reality can contain already significant patient conditions, agents capable of making commitments, and historically generated reasons, without assigning all normative authority to whichever process currently selects an end. The present study does not resolve the metaphysics of that common source. It identifies the extra monopoly claim that one exclusion argument needs and preserves the real generative contribution that reason-creation can make.

**Remaining premises:** the identified patient burden; the legitimacy, success and scope of each claimed normative power; the prospective patient-grounded moral judgment; and any account of how sources interact. None is certified by observing endorsement or by a count of reasons. No new scientific experiment, controller, formal compilation or whole-foundation completion is claimed.
