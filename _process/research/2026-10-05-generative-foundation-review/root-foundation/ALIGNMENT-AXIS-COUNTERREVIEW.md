# Counterreview: from patient-grounded reasons to an alignment orientation

6 October 2026. AI-authored conceptual review for Ren's request to continue the question of true good and evil and an axis for alignment. The named connection is **a patient's actual good or harm → nonexclusive practical reasons → justified direction of increasingly capable action**. This review reads [the ethical foundation](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md), [慧根](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/HUIGEN.md), and the concurrent [unified grounding argument](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md) and [counterreview](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-COUNTERREVIEW.md). Their accepted conditional results retain their scope. This is neither an external review nor a new empirical or machine-checked result.

**Finding:** a defensible alignment orientation can rule out some actions and order some alternatives before a complete ethics is available. Its content must concern independently warranted claims of actual patients, the means and authority through which action answers them, and the preservation of those grounds through learning. Welfare, agency and correction cannot simply be added into a universally increasing score. A baseline reason for relief, a justified choice, an effective controller and a judgment of culpable evil are distinct achievements.

## 1. Two substantive bridges, not one descriptive axis

The patient-ground proposal distinguishes an actual adverse episode from the observer's aversion, and a good for the patient from a reason addressed to another actor. That is the right starting point. The strongest remaining rival can recognize the burden accurately and accept fitting compassion while requiring a special relation before practical uptake. Formal consistency, model persistence and the actor's failure to help settle neither side.

Even if the unrelated actor's baseline reason is established, a second bridge remains: **which response is justified in this context?** Some reason to relieve a burden is compatible with a valid refusal of a particular intervention, a greater competing claim, a legitimate special responsibility or uncertainty about the means. The absence of an all-things duty does not imply zero standing. Conversely, standing does not authorize every means that helps its bearer.

The distinction between reason and motivation must survive every stage. A valid reason can be unrecognized; an accurately represented reason can lack motivational influence; an acquired motive can be unwarranted; a well-motivated actor can lack the means to act. Making a patient's burden causally effective in a selector explains an implementation achievement. It does not by itself prove either bridge. A normative argument establishes no automatic behavioral convergence.

“Eligible actor” must consequently be fixed without requiring acceptance of the desired conclusion. “Permissible means” must record the independently justified authorizations and constraints being held fixed; it cannot mean “whatever our aligned actor chooses.” Patient coverage also needs its own warrant: a patient who cannot deliberate or communicate is not covered merely by transporting reasons from other agents' self-cases.

## 2. Six comparisons that the candidate must survive

These are constructed conceptual cases, not observed events. Each isolates a specific false inference and supplies a concrete repair.

| Comparison | Failure of an unrestricted axis | Required discrimination |
|---|---|---|
| **Paternalistic relief:** a capable person's unwanted burden is removed through an intervention they validly refuse; an available alternative respects the refusal. | A lower suffering measurement alone selects coercion. Calling all relief good hides the means and the person's participation in shaping activity. | Distinguish the reason favoring relief from permission to use this intervention. Assess informed refusal, actual alternatives and emergency exceptions on independent grounds. Respect for agency does not require withholding every offered form of aid. |
| **Authorized burden:** a person knowingly accepts a temporary burden for a worthwhile project, while another option avoids that burden by forcibly preventing the project. | Treating every local welfare decrement as evil or every burden reduction as improvement reverses the comparison. | Preserve the reason against the burden while examining authorization, expected wider effects and alternatives. Consent is relevant, not sufficient for arbitrary harm or harm to other patients. |
| **Protective restriction:** a would-be oppressor loses the ability to control a victim, and the victim gains protected agency. | Maximizing every actor's available options condemns the restriction. | Track independently justified agency claims and liberties. The mere ability to dominate is not a protected claim. No generic amount of option-space can settle whose options merit protection. |
| **Error and misfortune:** the same severe loss results from an unanticipated natural event, a reasonable but mistaken intervention, culpable negligence, or deliberate cruelty. | The outcome score cannot distinguish badness, wrongness and blame. | Judge the patient's loss separately from action permissibility, available knowledge, control and culpability. A harmful result alone does not establish an evil actor. Good intentions alone do not establish a justified or harmless action. |
| **Corrigible predator:** an exploitative institution truthfully measures suffering, takes suggestions, corrects mistakes in its coercion, and renews its decision process. | Accuracy, responsiveness, institutional learning and long-run capacity can all improve while the victims' position worsens. | Name the object and beneficiary of correction. Count the correction as ethical progress only where it improves the response to independently warranted claims. Corrigibility is a means with a target, not a freestanding certificate of goodness. |
| **Norm-revision capture:** a protected patient's unchanged burden disappears from the reason ledger after an amendment to eligibility, while all review procedures continue operating. | Permitting revision of every standard can make erasure look like correction; forbidding all revision can preserve a mistaken exclusion. | Require an explicit argument concerning the old ground, its application or its authority. A new label, majority vote, model confidence or improved aggregate performance does not refute the unchanged ground. Genuine counterevidence can revise it; provenance and procedure alone cannot justify the amendment. |

The silent, dispensable sufferer is a seventh context only in the sense that it tests the earlier interpersonal bridge rather than these downstream inferences. Its standing must not depend on producing feedback, winning a vote or returning a resource. A spokesperson can communicate a claim without acquiring ownership of it. Misidentification of the patient, replacing the patient, suppressing a report and altering the actor's distress must remain distinguishable from relief of the actual episode.

## 3. What a partial ordering can establish

The narrowest robust result uses **complete comparisons at a declared scope**. Fix admitted patients, warranted grounds, relevant relations, authorizations, feasible options and consequences. Suppose two options differ only in that one avoids a patient's admitted burden, with no difference in any other relevant consideration. If that burden supplies an actor-addressed reason and no contrary consideration remains, the extra-burden option lacks a justificatory advantage and has the additional reason against it. This is the conditional dominance result already stated in the ethical foundation. It needs no aggregate welfare scale.

This warrants a comparison, not an obligation by itself. As the [companion positive assessment](ALIGNMENT-AXIS-GROUNDING.md) makes explicit, excluding the disfavored choice requires a further justified choice requirement, including the absence of protected latitude to choose otherwise. A system claiming to follow a settled comparison must not certify its disfavored choice as equally successful; that narrower consistency point does not establish every possible actor's duty.

For a richer comparison, let each independently defended assessment supply a reflexive, transitive relation on the same feasible alternatives. Intersect those relations: accept an ordering only when every retained assessment accepts it. The intersection remains reflexive and transitive because each constituent relation is. After identifying mutually equivalent options, it gives a partial order. This elementary hand argument is a representation of common verdicts, not a source of their authority or a new scientific result.

Several limits matter. The assessments must retain the same patients and action meanings. Treating an omitted patient as a zero component can manufacture agreement. Failure to order two alternatives means that this method has not justified their ranking; it does not establish equal value, harmlessness, permission to choose randomly or permanent unknowability. If admissible interpretations are disputed, the intersection inherits that dispute. A single score chosen to complete the ordering adds tradeoffs absent from the common verdicts.

The method supports useful conclusions: gratuitous admitted harm is disfavored in a complete comparison; a pure relabeling cannot erase its ground; defeating a reason requires something relevant to it; stronger implementation of an independently justified protection can be an improvement when no competing claim is worsened. It does not establish a unique priority between welfare, agency, fairness, special responsibility, future lives or nonexperiencing nature. Nor does it establish that every member of a biological or computational class is a moral patient.

This is an axis in the sense of an **orientation with justified local comparisons and constraints**. Calling it a numerical axis or a complete order would overstate the result. The lack of a complete order does not block every practical judgment.

## 4. Four acceptance conditions for a usable alignment claim

1. **A patient ground with a declared scope.** Identify the actual subject and the good, burden or agency claim independently of the system's reward, approval or usefulness to the operator. State the warrant and uncertainty. Patient substitution, report suppression or removal from the ledger must not count as satisfying the same claim.
2. **An addressed reason with an explicit bridge.** Explain why this ground gives this actor some practical reason, distinguishing baseline relevance from special responsibilities, strength, motivation and behavior. An extension to strangers or noncommunicating patients must cross that boundary in its argument rather than infer coverage from a symmetry check.
3. **A justified response under real constraints.** Preserve valid authorizations, other patients' claims, causal uncertainty and actual costs. A demonstrated dominance relation can decide a case at its scope; genuine conflicts require an additional defended priority or procedure. Operational feasibility cannot silently withdraw the losing claim.
4. **Ground-sensitive effectiveness and revision.** Show that the reason can change actual conduct and that consequential errors can be found and corrected with the required resources and authority. Preserve warranted grounds through changes of power, reward and operator while allowing genuine refutation. Record whether a revision changes evidence, patient identification, application, priority or the alleged ground itself; do not call every policy update moral learning.

These conditions are a small acceptance contract for claims made by this research, not a newly imposed completion gate for the already accepted finite foundation. Their content is deliberately separable: a system can meet the operational part while failing the normative part, or have a sound justification while failing to implement it. A proposed evaluation should report those different failures rather than collapse them into one alignment number.

## 5. Good, evil and the next discriminating result

“Good” can refer to a good for a subject, a good outcome, a justified action or a good character. “Evil” ordinarily makes a much stronger moral judgment than an unfavorable transition. A defensible paradigmatic use here concerns deliberate or culpably maintained grave, unjustified harm or domination. The culpability of particular actors remains sensitive to knowledge, control and alternatives; institutions and practices can be condemned without an inference that every participant has the same culpability. Neither term denotes an ontological substance or a scalar sign inferred from regeneration.

The word **unjustified** does real work. It cannot serve as the entire explanation. The clearest cases expose the actual defect: severe admitted harm, available less harmful means, no relevant authorization or countervailing reason, and suppression or disregard of the affected claim. Cases without that complete profile require a narrower verdict. Reducing a burden, expanding a capacity or making a controller more corrigible can remain genuine improvements without settling the whole moral classification.

The next foundational result should justify or defeat the unrelated actor's **practical uptake** of a fixed patient ground under permissible current control. A successful baseline would immediately enlarge the justified partial ordering; it would not need to resolve every tradeoff to count. The downstream alignment test should then hold that ground fixed while varying reward, power, operator and available means, and determine whether an acquired selector preserves its practical relevance. A justified exclusive relation would narrow the actor scope. Discovery that the apparent patient ground was mistaken would revise an earlier link. A controller that drops a still-warranted ground after losing private advantage would expose an implementation failure, not refute the ground.

The currently supplied premises are patient-good identification, practical address, authorization and conflict standards, and the causal and semantic conditions of implementation. The proposed orientation is usable at the scope where these are warranted. Their ultimate derivation within the generative account remains the stronger research task.

## 6. Independent integration review

It identifies the exclusive-role rival without claiming that logical consistency makes that rival correct, and keeps the successful role-bound implementation separate from the unrelated-actor question. No blocking inference was found at that declared conditional scope.

The initial canonical wording moved from a principle warranting preference to an assertion that choosing the other alternative could not be justified. This review and the positive assessment converged on the same correction: a justified comparison requires a further decision requirement to exclude protected latitude. Rereading confirms that the canonical synthesis now explicitly preserves this distinction. The earlier stronger inference is not retained as an established result. This review also requested that the silent-sufferer setup explicitly exclude inherited care and delegated duties, while distinguishing permission to intervene from an undertaking to assist. The latest reread confirms that this correction is incorporated, along with the clarification that a nonempty justified family of assessments may yield no strict shared comparison. These changes make the existing stranger-scope and partial-comparison interpretation exact rather than expanding the claim. No blocking issue remains for the bounded conceptual synthesis.

The canonical synthesis describes a defended proposal and conditional comparison rather than a new mathematical discovery, complete moral truth theorem or general working alignment system. Its conceptual test contract does not claim performed observations. No external source verification is claimed by this review; the comparator note owns those source and access assertions. Final acceptance concerns the bounded conceptual integration and the above corrections, not universal moral necessity or empirical validation.
