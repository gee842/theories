## 1. Begin with histories of the whole

Let h be the history so far, and let a and b be two specified feasible changes or continuation arrangements. Let μ_a and μ_b describe their causally supported distributions of subsequent histories H. They include the activity through which participants, opportunities, interpretations and later choices form. They are not an unexplained benevolent scheduler, and conditioning on observing a is not automatically the same as estimating what making a happen would cause.

Let G_T(H) assess the affected continuation through horizon T, retaining relevant history before it. It concerns the whole differentiated configuration, including real benefits, burdens, relationships and the formation or loss of possibilities. It is not a sum of antecedently independent owners' preferences. An entire-history assessment can also remain nonadditive or only partially ordered.

Where a scalar assessment and expectation are justified and both finite-horizon expectations are finite, define the comparative significance:

\[
\Delta_T(a,b\mid h)
=\mathbb E_{\mu_a}[G_T(H)]-\mathbb E_{\mu_b}[G_T(H)].
\]

This answers how a specified difference changes the prospective good of the unfolding. A baseline is necessary for this attribution; an absolute continuation value answers a different question. Selecting b is not a moral permission to impose it. Substituting different future people also requires an assessment of the histories and existing claims, not assigning zero welfare to a person's nonexistence.

Where temporal addition is justified, one representation is

\[
G_T(H)=\int_{t_0}^{T}g(s,H_{\le s})\,ds+B_T(H).
\]

In this realization-rate interpretation, g is the contribution realized at s; it can depend on the history of the activity. B_T, if needed, records terminal or completion value not already included in the integral. These are representations of value to defend, not a derivation of its ethical character. A rate-only model is inadequate if it erases meaningful histories or if the relevant goods lack a warranted common scale. Section 7 develops a different, whole-first representation whose temporal terms need not have this same local meaning.

## 2. What potential means here

Mutually exclusive continuations cannot all be counted as though they occur together. If a hypothetical supported transition has equal chances of two alternatives worth 2 and 6 on an admitted scale, its expected contribution is 4, not 8. Those numbers only illustrate probability accounting; they supply no welfare scale. If probabilities are unwarranted, retain a set or range of possible consequences and state which comparisons are robust across it.

Taking the best possible continuation instead measures attainable potential under specified future choices. It does not establish likely realization, affordable access or an ability to select that continuation. More branches can introduce harmful possibilities as well as good ones. Protection of usable options can matter through later adaptation and learning, but mere branch count is insufficient.

Do not integrate a value-to-go at every step as if each were fresh realized good. With contributions 1 now and 1 at the next step, total contribution is 2. The successive remaining values are 2 and 1; summing them gives 3 because the later contribution was counted twice. An enabling condition can derive significance from what it supports without creating another independent copy of that same eventual benefit. Genuine additional relational or capacity goods require their own identification.

## 3. Present fulfillment and adverse histories remain

The integral starts with what is being realized now. Rest, enjoyment or relief near the end of a life can contribute even if they enable no later product. A completed good is not outside generative reality because it needs no further sequel. A model that assigns value only to the production of later value would omit this possibility.

The [whole-history repair comparison](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#7-continuation-the-origin-of-a-need-and-the-value-of-its-repair) constrains the integrand. Introducing avoidable adversity and then repairing it can contain a real benefit while worsening the history relative to prevention. Counting future helpful episodes and omitting the adversity that makes them necessary reproduces that failure. The ethical comparison remains defended; a resource or repair count is not silently identified with suffering.

Knowledge transmitted after an initiator withdraws supplies the positive pattern. Its significance can continue through later understanding and changed practices; private return is unnecessary. Trace actual access, adaptation, displaced burdens and competing uses of support. The [relational-goodness account](RELATIONAL-GOODNESS-ARGUMENT.md) already supplies this direction. [Self-ground audit §36](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#present-realization-matters-as-well-as-future-capacity) already preserves present realization beyond future capacity. The new construction specifies a possible temporal and modal representation of those inherited distinctions.

## 4. To the end of time

A finite end T is mathematically different from an infinite limit. For an unbounded horizon, inspect finite-horizon comparisons before taking a limit:

\[
\Delta_\infty(a,b\mid h)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\Delta_T(a,b\mid h),
\]

when that limit exists in the intended sense.

Two total integrals can diverge while this difference stays well defined. In the following two examples take t_0=0 and B_T=0. On a hypothetical admitted value scale, take g_A(t)=1+1_{[0,1]}(t) and g_B(t)=1. Their totals both grow without bound, but their finite-horizon difference equals 1 for every T≥1. This is a limit of finite differences, not the undefined expression infinity minus infinity.

Other comparisons fail to stabilize. With g_A(t)=1+cos(t) and g_B(t)=1, both rates are nonnegative but their accumulated difference is sin(T). It has no limit and changes sign indefinitely. A unique infinite-horizon ranking does not follow from writing an integral.

Discounting, average rates and eventual-dominance rules answer different questions. Discounting solely by date needs ethical justification; uncertainty or extinction risk should be represented through the actual probability model rather than silently counted a second time. A long-run average can erase a finite lived loss. No particular repair is mandated here. Where justified comparisons remain incomplete, preserve that incompleteness. A formal full-horizon criterion also does not imply that a situated participant can predict or calculate it.

The [existing joint-formation proof](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/JOINT-FORMATION-MATH.md#proof-the-discounted-infinite-game-has-such-an-equilibrium) already controls discounted tails for bounded operator receipts; those receipts are not ethical value. ## 5. Can recursion ground goodness itself?

Here is an elementary diagnostic, not a theorem about every possible ethical theory. Let P be a Markov expectation operator on bounded real functions, so its sup norm satisfies ||PV||∞≤||V||∞. Let 0≤γ<1. A purely recursive definition

\[
V=\gamma PV
\]

implies ||V||∞≤γ||V||∞, hence V=0. Nonzero bounded value cannot be determined solely by discounted continuation of itself.

With bounded realized contribution g, the equation

\[
V=g+\gamma PV
\]

instead has the unique bounded solution

\[
V=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma^nP^ng.
\]

The series converges uniformly by the geometric bound; substitution verifies the equation, and the same norm inequality proves uniqueness. At γ=1, every constant solves V=PV because P1=1. The equation then fixes neither scale nor sign; other harmonic solutions can occur. For a finite horizon of N transitions, count g at stages 0 through N−1 and a bounded terminal value b at stage N:

\[
V_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\gamma^nP^ng+\gamma^NP^Nb.
\]

If both g and b vanish, so does V_N. A nonzero boundary supplies evaluative content whose justification remains to be explained.

The standard Bellman/contraction comparison is directly supported by [Stanford CS234's week-2 solutions](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs234/cs234.1224/sessions/Problem_Sessions_CS234_Jan20_solutions.pdf), PDF pages 2 and 5. Root read those portions through browser extraction. This application is a hand argument, not a new theorem, Lean certification or empirical finding. No reward signal in that source is imported as an ethical ground.

The mathematical limitation is precise: this recursion transmits an interpretation of good; it does not choose that interpretation. It does not exclude a substantive nonadditive, relational or immanent account. In the proposed foundation, g or G could be constituted by actual fulfillment, adversity, participation and their relationships within the unfolding. Supplying that identification to a calculation does not establish that it is ontologically external or irreducible.

## 7. Whole appraisal can precede its temporal expression

The preceding result requires value-identifying content in the account. It does not require that content to consist of independently given local ethical atoms. A whole-history account can instead supply the assessment from which temporal contributions are derived. This makes the direction of explanation accountable to the nondual method while retaining the substantive obligation to justify that assessment.

### Deriving contributions from prefixes

Suppose F assigns a justified scalar appraisal to each relevant finite history h_n, including its actual relationships and effects. Define

\[
d_n=F(h_{n+1})-F(h_n).
\]

Then telescoping gives

\[
F(h_N)=F(h_0)+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}d_n.
\]

The increments need not be independent local goods. Each can depend on the whole prefix and on how the developing relation changes its appraisal. No temporal separability into context-free values has been proved. Conversely, the identity does not justify F: it represents the warranted appraisal stipulated for this construction.

For a specified F and partition, these increments are fixed. Knowing only the completed history's value does not determine F on all shorter prefixes. If F is replaced by a conditional expectation of a later total, its increments are updates in expected value; good news can change them without itself producing the improvement reported. Such updates must not silently stand for newly realized good.

### Different temporal expressions can preserve one whole value

For a finite undiscounted expression \(G_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_n+B_N\), let Φ_n depend on the history through n. Set

\[
g'_n=g_n+\Phi_{n+1}-\Phi_n,\qquad
B'_N=B_N-\Phi_N+\Phi_0.
\]

Summing the potential differences proves G'_N=G_N exactly. The initial Φ_0 must concern the common initial history, rather than secretly encode different futures when it is treated as a common offset.

For a concrete numerical representation, take two stages with g_0=0, g_1=1 and B_2=0. Choose Φ_0=Φ_2=0 and Φ_1=c. The transformed contributions are c and 1−c, with total 1 for any real c. The total alone does not identify either local term's sign. This is a fact about representation, not a claim that real suffering can be moved between times or made unreal by arithmetic. If g independently means goodness realized at that time, many transformed terms no longer preserve that interpretation.

The continuous version requires regularity. Along each history, if the prefix-dependent Φ_t is absolutely continuous, then \(g'=g+d\Phi/dt\) and \(B'_T=B_T-\Phi_T+\Phi_{t_0}\) preserve the integral plus terminal value. General stochastic paths need not be differentiable; discrete telescoping does not need that assumption.

### Preserve histories and distinguish action significance

Any finite scalar whole-history appraisal permits an undiscounted terminal-only representation: g=0 and B_T(H)=G_T(H). This does not mean that all goodness occurs at the final time. It means that a terminal expression records the history's value.

The earlier burden-and-repair comparison remains decisive. An unburdened history and a history of introduced adversity followed by genuine repair may end with the same selected health or capacity features. The defended whole appraisal can still distinguish their lived histories. A terminal term retaining those histories can preserve that distinction. Replacing it with a function only of the matched endpoint features cannot. No claim that the complete physical states are identical is required.

Nor is a realized contribution identical to an action's counterfactual significance. On a hypothetical signed scale, stopping a burden can prevent further negative accrual without producing a positive rate afterward. Relative to continued adversity, that response can still improve the continuation. The comparison Δ(a,b) concerns alternatives; the rate g or prefix increment d concerns an expression of one history. A causal contribution, a temporal allocation and a change in predicted value require their own identification.

### Retain the boundary at an infinite horizon

For the discounted finite sum \(G_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\gamma^ng_n+\gamma^Nb_N\), define g'_n=g_n+γΦ_{n+1}−Φ_n and b'_N=b_N−Φ_N. Telescoping gives G'_N=G_N−Φ_0. Adding back Φ_0 restores equality; for alternatives with the same initial history, the fixed offset already preserves their ordering.

Without a terminal adjustment, the difference of partial sums includes the remainder γ^NΦ_N−Φ_0. Under 0≤γ<1 and a uniformly bounded potential, the first term vanishes as N grows. Without that condition or another justified boundary limit, it cannot be discarded. Moving all value to an infinite boundary can require an unbounded potential and fall outside §5's bounded-function result. There is no contradiction with the earlier zero-solution proof.

The constructive conclusion is that **a whole appraisal can determine a temporal expression without the temporal terms being its independently justified foundations**. The earlier realization-rate interpretation remains available where supported. Choosing between representations does not settle whether the underlying whole appraisal is ethically correct. The remaining grounding task concerns that actual quality and its warranted comparison, rather than finding a compulsory primitive reward at every stage.

## 8. Does a worse past change the significance of present relief?

The next test concerns **retained lived history → the ethical significance of an available present transformation**. Including the past in G does not yet decide whether it changes the priority of a later benefit. The proposed comparison applies an established temporal-priority dispute to this generative account; it is not a new discovery in moral philosophy. The [self-ground audit §41](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#41-local-benefit-history-and-the-value-of-a-life) already establishes that a common past cannot simply be cancelled without justifying separability. The present extension tests its role in distributing relief between two continuing lives, rather than reclaiming that general observation as new. The [primary reading record](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md#8-past-adversity-and-present-priority) distinguishes the relevant antecedents and their scopes.

### Identify the whole relation before selecting a score

Two continuing participants now undergo comparably severe, unwanted episodes. Relief available now would have matched effects on felt intensity and duration, with matched efficacy, access, costs and foreseeable later consequences. Only one can receive that relief in the present round. Their ages, elapsed lifespans and remaining-life prospects are also matched. Both have received genuine reciprocal care; neither has an unequal prior allocation, undertaking, liability or desert claim concerning this opportunity.

One participant has endured substantially more past adversity; those episodes have ended. For the priority test, greater overall past disadvantage must also be independently warranted after considering any differing past fulfillment and relationships. A larger count of unpleasant episodes alone would not establish the required ordering. The comparison excludes residual differences that would make the present intervention more effective, remove a larger current burden or restore different future opportunities. It does not erase all causal traces: records and knowledge must identify the past accurately. The history is fixed under both available actions. These are demanding conceptual controls, not an empirical claim that all can be matched in practice.

Do not stipulate equal *complete welfare gains* or equal *moral strength*. Those are among the contested conclusions. The stipulated equality concerns independently specified relief and its causal effects. A relational account might argue that the same local relief benefits a whole life differently; another might grant equal benefit but attach different moral importance to it.

The participants are continuities formed within the unfolding, not primitive owners from which a whole is assembled. Identifying which present continuity underwent the earlier adversity is necessary for the proposed historical ground. This requires an account of continuity; it requires neither an unchanging ego nor that every meaningful relation be confined to one individual. Renaming both participants while carrying their histories and relations with them changes no relevant fact. Both competing appraisals can satisfy that invariance.

### An affirmative reason and a substantive rival

The positive lifetime-sensitive proposal is that directing the available good toward the worse lived history can partly redress its disadvantage. Its point concerns how the continuing life goes, rather than a return to whoever supplies relief. The earlier suffering is neither undone nor reclassified as good. A later benefit can improve that life's history without erasing what occurred, and without being payment earned by suffering. This is the proposed ethical relation, not something established by naming the response compensation.

Completion of earlier adverse episodes does not imply completion of the life they partly constitute. Those episodes cannot now be relieved, but the next development can still improve how that continuing life goes. This identifies an alterable continuation; it does not establish a priority for altering it. Causal inactivity of the past is therefore insufficient to settle the ethical question.

The stronger affirmative premise would be that **alleviating an independently warranted serious shortfall in the good realized through a continuing life can give an available improvement additional significance**. That requires identifying the shortfall, defending its ethical relevance and explaining how this response addresses it. Mere continuity or a larger past pain count does not establish those claims. Where defended, the ground might concern a different whole-life benefit or greater moral importance of a benefit; neither interpretation permits counting the same relief twice. It supplies no general rule of decreasing lifetime weights.

The strongest rival need not deny any of those past experiences, their badness, or the moral significance of either present relief. It may retain the past as a fixed adverse contribution common to both available whole histories, while denying that it changes the comparative force of the present claims under these matched conditions. On that account, the relevant current claims may be equal. Other accounts can combine present severity with lifetime disadvantage instead of making either exclusive.

Thus the dispute is not complete representation versus forgetting the past. It concerns whether the past changes the evaluative contribution of what happens next. Recognizing a genuine historical difference does not establish that it should influence every present choice; recognizing equal local effects does not establish that the full histories have equal significance. Each position owes an account of the relevance it asserts or denies.

### A small diagnostic makes the extra structure explicit

For this illustration only, admit a positive cardinal lifetime-welfare scale and an equal one-unit benefit. Suppose the relevant lifetime outcomes would be A = (2, 4) if relief goes to the historically worse-off participant, and B = (1, 5) if it goes to the other. These numbers are not measurements of the conceptual case or new premises of the generative foundation.

An additive whole-history appraisal gives both outcomes 6. A distinct appraisal sums the square roots of the two lifetime levels:

\[
\sqrt{2}+2 > 1+\sqrt{5}.
\]

Indeed, \(\sqrt{2}+1>\sqrt{5}\), since squaring the positive sides reduces this to \(\sqrt{2}>1\). The second appraisal therefore favors A. Applying any single function to the already equal aggregate 6 would leave the tie intact. The changed ordering comes from a different evaluative structure, not harmless relabeling of the same total. Common positive affine changes of an admitted interval scale preserve equal increments; a nonlinear transform is not merely another such unit choice. This pair alone also cannot distinguish priority to a worse life from concern about inequality.

Both formulas can include the entire relevant past. They are illustrations of independently motivated evaluative alternatives, not arbitrary switches offered as a refutation of the fuller theory. Choosing the second formula would encode a substantive judgment about histories. Its calculation cannot vindicate that judgment. If cardinal comparison is unwarranted, retain the qualitative dispute instead.

Temporal integration does not decide between them. A history-dependent rate or prefix increment can express an already justified history-sensitive appraisal. A temporally additive account can instead retain past adversity while letting its fixed contribution cancel in the present comparison. The representation must follow the defended account of good, rather than settle the question through an unnoticed separability assumption.

### What changes the account, and what remains of the minimum

If earlier adversity causes a larger present burden or different benefits from relief, an account focused on prospective effects can already recognize that difference. If responsibility or a valid undertaking supplies a reparative claim, it introduces another ground. These variants help identify why a judgment changes, but do not decide the controlled historical-priority question. If the alleged continuity or past condition is misidentified, this application fails. A defended argument that lifetime disadvantage modifies benefit or priority would substantively enrich G; a stronger account of present claims could restrict that proposal.

The original minimum retains a positive relief-ground concerning each participant under its defended conditions. Neither available allocation is the earlier no-countervailing-loss improvement: a real burden remains unmet either way. The current disagreement therefore neither refutes that minimum nor closes its grounding. Nor does unresolved priority prove a tie or authorize a lottery by default. Such a procedure needs a separate justification.

The advance is a precise test of ethical content beyond history inclusion. The next question **within this supporting extension** is how the identified continuity and its lived past bear on present benefit or priority, including limits when current burdens differ. Resolving that ranking is not a prerequisite for the contributory minimum: both rivals already grant relief-based favor and dispute its relative strength. The governing whole-process task remains the ethical quality of differentiated realization and impairment together, including the formation and correction of its appraisals. C1/C2 remain local interfaces, not a replacement starting point. The whole cannot be reduced to its initiator's return, but that correction alone does not choose a lifetime-weighting rule. No ethical axiom, Lean theorem or completed universal necessity claim is added here.

## 9. Mutual production can determine relative importance

The next connection is **actual production relations → relative contribution to their continuation → the proposed ethical interpretation**. Section 5's zero-result concerns a bounded Markov expectation operator. It does not cover every recursive account. Production can generate several successors together, and its operator need not preserve constants or contract the maximum norm. This allows a positive construction without assigning a separate reward to each type.

### A relational construction and its exact scope

Let K be a finite nonnegative matrix. K_ij is the expected number of type-j successors actually produced per type-i activity under a specified continuing regime. Use row counts x, with expected next counts xK. These entries represent a justified linear production law with jointly feasible outputs, not mere graph links or separately attainable maxima. Shared resources, joint production and competition can invalidate this linear law; they must be accounted for in its domain. Multiple successors within one history differ from mutually exclusive complete futures, whose probability accounting remains governed by §2.

Suppose K is irreducible and ρ(K)>0. The standard Perron–Frobenius result gives a unique positive eigenvector ray v satisfying Kv=ρ(K)v. With β=1/ρ(K),

\[
v=\beta Kv.
\]

The relative weights are jointly determined by production relations, up to a common scale. No local reward vector appears. For expected counts, xKv=ρ(K)xv: v measures contribution to the specified reproductive mode. This is a precise structural meaning of importance. Choosing a scale or writing β does not establish ethical standing, a moral discount rate or an implemented evaluation mechanism.

For a direct hand-check, take

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}0&4\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
v=\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \beta=\tfrac12.
\]

Kv=(4,2)ᵀ=2v, so the recursive equation has a bounded nonzero solution even with β<1. There is no contradiction with §5: K is a production operator, not a Markov expectation operator. This example alternates between types; irreducibility alone does not make normalized population trajectories converge.

The normalization is constrained. If wᵀ is a positive left Perron vector and a nonzero v≥0 satisfies v=βKv, multiplying by wᵀ gives βρ(K)=1. For a fixed β not meeting that condition, this construction supplies no nonzero nonnegative solution. Moreover, a positive diagonal unit change x′=xD gives K′=D⁻¹KD and v′=D⁻¹v, preserving ρ and x′v′=xv. Coordinate weights are not unit-free quantities of moral worth.

These relations require the specified continuing regime. They do not establish inexhaustible resources, pathwise survival of a finite stochastic lineage or a single linear description of the whole generative unfolding.

### A recursive weight is not a lifetime integral

At the balancing normalization, βⁿKⁿv=v for every n. Therefore

\[
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\beta^nK^nv=Nv.
\]

The fixed-point weight is finite, while adding that same normalized contribution at every generation diverges as N grows. This does not show that every integral of future goods diverges. It shows that a reproductive weight and an accumulated stream are different constructions. Counting value-to-go repeatedly also retains the double-counting problem in §2.

An actual finite end provides another distinction. If v_n=β_nK_nv_{n+1} and the final weight v_N is zero, backward substitution gives v_n=0 throughout, regardless of intermediate amplification. Positive stationary type weights do not remove that boundary condition. They describe a continuing production relation, rather than proving value for a finite history whose terminal significance has been excluded by assumption.

### Test the proposed ethical identification

**A final good without successors.** Now allow a reducible K so that its scope includes a final episode of relief or enjoyment with no productive descendants. Its row is zero, so the homogeneous equation assigns it v_i=0. This tests extending the measure beyond the irreducible theorem's domain. The repository's [relational-goodness account](RELATIONAL-GOODNESS-ARGUMENT.md) recognizes such realized good without requiring a further product. Given that inherited ethical judgment, a purely reproductive weight cannot exhaust ethical significance. Removing terminal types to obtain irreducibility narrows the target; adding a fictitious self-loop changes the represented process. A history appraisal or terminal contribution can retain the realized good, without making it an ontologically independent ethical atom. This test applies an existing judgment; it does not newly prove that judgment's universal authority.

**A harmful arrangement that renews itself.** Accurate sensing, learning and mutual support can contribute to maintaining imposed subordination. A faithful production operator may assign positive contribution to that maintenance. Such an arrangement can be causally important while giving reasons to transform it. Positive reproductive contribution therefore does not by itself mean favorable ethical value. If the representation omits lived effects, authority or alternative possibilities, that omission limits the representation; it does not refute a fuller account retaining them. No claim is made that an old domination example already fixes this particular K.

These tests distinguish contribution to continuation, magnitude of ethical significance, and favorable value. If the represented relationships are independently defended as good-producing, their ethical content can reside in those relationships rather than in separately assigned rewards. The matrix equation can then propagate that content. The outstanding connection is why those actual relationships and realizations are good, including where continuation should change or finish. It is an unresolved explanation within the proposed whole account, not evidence for an ethical realm outside generation.

### Internal formation does not make every signal authoritative

### A causal change of the future field

Compare two feasible arrangements a and b from the same relevant past, representing their implementation, costs and consequences. Put their distributions Φ_a and Φ_b on a common space Ω of sufficiently described continuations; an unavailable history can have probability zero. If new kinds of events arise, embed both descriptions in a common enlarged history space rather than subtracting measures on different domains.

Define the signed measure F_{a:b}=Φ_a−Φ_b. For a measurable event E, F_{a:b}(E) is the change in its probability caused by the difference between the arrangements under the model. Its total mass is zero because both probabilities normalize to one. That is probability accounting, not conservation of goodness. Conditioning on observing a does not by itself identify its causal effect.

This provides a precise interpretation of the proposed force of the future as comparative causal influence. It is not a mechanical force, a unique velocity field or a force exerted backward by an already existing future. An actual dynamical force would require further state-space, metric and law specifications. Causal strength has no ethical sign merely from its magnitude.

### A conditional probability-transfer result

Use a finite candidate-history space to state the elementary result without convergence issues. Let H_+ and H_- be sufficiently described histories whose ethical comparison H_+ ≻ H_- has been defended, including relevant means, relationships and consequences. For 0 < ε ≤ Φ_b(H_-), suppose

\[
\Phi_a=\Phi_b+\varepsilon(\delta_{H_+}-\delta_{H_-}).
\]

All other history probabilities are unchanged. For every finite real-valued representation v respecting this pair's strict comparison,

\[
\mathbb E_{\Phi_a}[v]-\mathbb E_{\Phi_b}[v]
=\varepsilon\bigl(v(H_+)-v(H_-)\bigr)>0.
\]

Proof: expand the finite expectations; the unchanged terms cancel. This establishes agreement on the direction across all such numerical representations, without assigning a unique magnitude or aggregating independently owned utilities. It does not derive H_+ ≻ H_-, establish that all ethical judgments have a scalar representation, or mandate expected-value maximization generally. Favoring arrangement a requires the separately defended dominance principle that this probability improvement, with no further adverse ethical difference, supplies prospective favor. Permission, obligation and an all-things-considered choice require their actual additional conditions. Section 4's horizon and convergence limits remain in force; omitted later effects are not zero by default.

The result gives present causal organization prospective significance through the whole histories it changes. A favorable tendency can exist before a favorable outcome is realized; a realized loss remains a loss even if a choice improved its probability. The self's care, finite fulfillment and completion remain within the appraisal without requiring another productive sequel. Do not count the eventual benefit again merely because its enabling condition is also described as significant.

The simple transfer is a restricted comparison, not the form of every causal change. Exact histories recording which arrangement occurred can have disjoint supports. Such implementation differences may not be erased merely to obtain shared H_+ and H_-. A more general finite formulation uses a coupling π(H_a,H_b) with marginals Φ_a and Φ_b. If every pair of positive coupling weight satisfies H_a ≽ H_b, with a strict improvement on positive weight, then every v strictly respecting those comparisons satisfies

\[
\mathbb E_{\Phi_a}[v]-\mathbb E_{\Phi_b}[v]
=\sum_{H_a,H_b}\pi(H_a,H_b)[v(H_a)-v(H_b)]>0.
\]

This follows by taking the marginals and summing nonnegative terms with a positive term. The coupling is a mathematical comparison device; the paired histories do not occur together, and no unique physical correspondence between unrealized histories is inferred. Its existence, the ethical pairwise ordering and the dominance principle remain explicit conditions.

One possible completion of the field idea would identify goodness with greater entropy of accessible future histories. Test that specific candidate against the same two histories H_+ ≻ H_- used above. For the binary distribution giving H_+ probability p, Shannon entropy is

\[
S(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p).
\]

The distributions p=0.1 and p=0.9 have equal entropy, while the probability-transfer argument favors the latter under its stated ethical premises. Moving from p=0.9 to p=1 further improves the prospect and lowers entropy to zero. Thus this scalar cannot represent those defended strict comparisons. This holds the histories and their ordering fixed while changing their probabilities; it does not freely toggle a moral predicate and purport to refute the rich generative proposal. It also does not show that diversity never matters. It rules out identifying ethical direction with this probability-diversity measure alone, conditional on the retained whole-history judgments.

The selected [causal-entropic-force paper](https://cfreer.org/papers/PhysRevLett_110-168702.pdf) is a relevant physical comparator, not a theory of ethics refuted here. Its use of path diversity motivates making the candidate distinction explicit. Our entropy test concerns an attempted ethical extension, not the paper's reported mechanical simulations.

### A distribution of usual outcomes can omit real response powers

The next positive step is to represent not only what normally occurs but how feasible responses can change continuation. An elementary local comparison isolates the point. An established activity has two physically supported modes: continue and pause. Let r=0 signify continuing and r=1 signify a pause signal issued through a formed signaling route. Let y=1 mean the activity actually pauses. In organization A, y=r; in organization B, y=0 because the signal is blocked. Under a baseline practice in which r=0, both yield y=0. The same coarse usual-outcome distribution therefore leaves the response relation unidentified. When r=1 is actually issued, their outputs differ.

This is a specification, not an empirical discovery or a claim that the complete histories match. The blocking arrangement, any knowledge of it, and its consequences belong to the fuller history and can distinguish Φ_A from Φ_B. The matched projection retains the usual output and omits its responsiveness. A model family Φ^u indexed by physically realized, feasible changes u can represent the difference; the availability, costs and formation of those changes must be supplied or explained. A formal intervention does not grant an outside observer power over the whole or establish permission to perform it.

Actual response organization can partly constitute an ability, rather than only cause a later feeling about that ability. This advances the descriptive side of the identity candidate: a future field must retain which continuations are jointly accessible through which relationships, not just how many there are. The inherited [support-and-exclusion comparison](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#15-indispensable-support-and-the-control-of-alternatives) and [policy discussion](#the-policy-that-maintains-future-constraints) already recognize maintained restrictions. ### What the positive connection explains, and what it still supplies

Consider a scoped ethical application of the response example. A pause would prevent an independently identified unwanted burden, a supported resumption remains available, and the comparison introduces no loss of another warranted realization, undertaking or protective condition. In that specified case, the actual responsive relation explains why A can avoid the burden that B maintains. Under the defended whole-history improvement and dominance principles, increasing the probability of that responsive continuation is favorable. The explanation is through the real relation between activity, undergoing and changed continuation; neither an external reward signal nor a return to the initiating self is needed.

That application does not yet explain the ethical qualification from generativity alone. A pause route can also interrupt protection or defeat a justified undertaking; blocking a harmful option can be warranted. Those changed conditions defeat a general identification of more control with more good. A substantive rival can accept every specified response relation while explaining its favorable role entirely through the burden avoided and worthwhile activity preserved. It need not posit privately contained experience or deny actual relational constitution. If it accepts the same ground, it is compatible with this generative explanation rather than a defeated alternative.

The resulting candidate is more specific: **ethical quality concerns how actual organization realizes and relates differentiated living, including how that realization changes the conditions of its continuation, correction and completion**. The field describes this modal organization; it does not replace its content with probability mass or entropy. No additional organizational value is required on top of the same realization. ## Fair chances as a quality of the organized future

7 October 2026. Continuation at Ren's request to pursue the connection and include the findings in the theory. Named connection: **actual organization of possible continuations → a proposed present relational good → its place in whole-history appraisal**. The initial plan to test recursive expansion against successful prevention was set aside as a new central result: [grounding §7](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#7-continuation-the-origin-of-a-need-and-the-value-of-its-repair) and the concurrently developed [§33](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#33-renewal-causal-importance-and-the-good-realized) already preserve the decisive recurrence and completion distinctions. This round instead tests a positive ethical role for the distribution of opportunities itself.

### Fix a formed allocation relationship

Consider a legitimate common undertaking with one remaining indivisible opportunity. Its two participants A and B have equally strong claims under the undertaking. Receiving it would enable comparable worthwhile activity; no relevant difference in urgency, efficacy, prior receipt or competing responsibility warrants priority. Splitting the opportunity or supplying both is unavailable in this case. The implementations being compared introduce no countervailing difference in cost, delay or harm. These are substantive case conditions, including ethical ones; they are not derived merely from two beings belonging to the whole.

Compare an effectively implemented impartial lottery L, giving each participant probability 1/2, with a direct allocation D_A to A without a relevant priority ground. In either case exactly one participant receives the opportunity. The lottery's probabilities concern a supported selection procedure before its outcome is settled. They are not a bystander's ignorance about a secretly fixed favored recipient. Manipulation, false announcements or a draw whose outcome will not be honored would change the specified relationship.

The participants' standing is investigated within the undertaking's actual history and relations, rather than installed as independent private ownership at the foundation. This local test does not make prior membership or contribution necessary for every ethical claim. The legitimacy and equal standing of this particular arrangement remain premises whose wider generative explanation is not completed here.

### The positive ethical argument

The fair-chance candidate favors L in this scope because the operative selection relation gives effect to both equal claims without assigning one a privileged route on an irrelevant basis. Merely considering B's claim before always excluding it does not instantiate that relation. The procedure addresses an unavoidable incompatibility in final receipt through a form of treatment available before the outcome. Its proposed favorable quality belongs to the actual organization of access, even when the eventual winner is A in both compared histories.

This identifies a way in which a possible future can matter as part of the present: access to it is an actual relation. The future benefit is still unreceived. Providing a fair chance and providing the opportunity have different satisfaction conditions, so losing does not become equivalent to winning. Nor does equal access erase the shortage. The [earlier post-allocation analysis](PATIENT-GROUND-AFTER-FAIR-ALLOCATION.md) already shows why a completed chance claim need not extinguish reasons for a newly available, independently permissible benefit. The present addition locates the proposed fairness in the causal field, rather than discovering the chance/receipt distinction anew.

The claim is not that every lottery is good or that all participants must always receive equal chances. A justified priority, a genuinely divisible opportunity, harmful delay, a permitted delegation or another valid undertaking can change the case. Equal zero chances also fail to provide the available opportunity. The whole appraisal retains realization as well as relative treatment; equality is not a license to withhold all benefit.

### What symmetry does and does not derive

Suppose the allocation rule is required to treat these relevantly equal participant roles neutrally, with no privileged identity determining the chance. If p is A's probability and 1−p is B's, this requirement yields p=1−p and hence p=1/2. That is a conditional derivation of equal probabilities from the specified procedural norm and exhaustive allocation of one opportunity.

Neutral treatment is a substantive normative condition here. It does not follow from a mere change of names in a representation, causal connectedness or conservation of probability. A deterministic rule can be described consistently while violating it. The argument for why this formed relation calls for neutral treatment is the ethical work above, not the algebra.

In this restricted setting, the equal distribution also maximizes binary entropy. That coincidence does not make entropy the source of its ethical character. The earlier entropy test used histories with a defended better–worse difference; this case concerns comparable claims and the relationship through which one is fulfilled. Different ethical targets can favor lower uncertainty or equal opportunity. No single preference for more randomness represents both.

### Why an endpoint-only expected value misses the candidate

Let X_A and X_B describe only who receives the opportunity and the comparable realized benefit. A numerical appraisal of those endpoints that assigns v(X_A)=v(X_B)=c necessarily gives

\[
\mathbb E_{L}[v]=\tfrac12c+\tfrac12c=c=v(X_A)=\mathbb E_{D_A}[v].
\]

It therefore cannot express a strict preference for L grounded in its fair treatment. This is a representation result conditional on that ethical judgment, not an independent proof of the judgment or a general refutation of expected-value reasoning.

Our whole-history account can retain the omitted relationship. H_{L,A} records an actual impartial allocation process followed by A's receipt; H_{D_A,A} records the direct exclusion of B followed by the same receipt. They are different histories, even though the selected endpoint agrees. The causal organization giving both participants an effective opportunity belongs within the former history. Including it is substantive description, not permission to attach a fairness bonus to any favored label. An alternative formal representation could appraise the organized prospect directly; neither representation settles the ethical question by notation.

Accordingly, the preceding probability-transfer and coupling results remain valid under their stated premises, but their history descriptions must retain relevant chance relations and implementation. Endpoints that omit those relations cannot silently serve as complete ethical outcomes. Where such an omission changes the appraisal, the deficiency is in the representation. A separate additive quantity of fairness is not required, and the eventual benefit must not be counted twice simply because its chance mattered earlier.

### A substantive rival and the remaining dependence

A rival can accept the equal claims, the actual lottery and its different causal access relation, yet deny that giving chances has additional ethical significance once a permissible selection among tied claims is made. It may hold that neither claimant is entitled to selection, that someone must lose, and that a direct tie-break need not express inferior standing. This preserves the factual target and disputes the fair-chance candidate's explanation; it is not a rival created by toggling an empty good predicate.

The candidate's reply is that equal treatment concerns the actual opportunity afforded to each claimant before selection, as well as the reasonableness of the eventual recipient. Unavoidable unequal receipt does not itself make avoidable exclusion from consideration as a possible recipient ethically indifferent. The rival disputes that procedural ground rather than the probability calculation. The present argument supports the candidate but does not establish that every account accepting equal claims must accept it. A warranted differential reason to favor A, such as a valid prior priority undertaking, changes the specified case. The rival's general permission to resolve a tie by either selection does not supply such a differential reason and is not excluded by that qualification; it remains the disagreement being tested.

The result advances the field proposal at a bounded scope: **the way futures are causally made available can partly constitute a defensible relational good, rather than merely predict later benefits**. Its ethical content is the proposed fair relationship among actual participants, not probability mass in isolation. This is compatible with the richer relation-inclusive accounts preserved in the main grounding study and does not establish an additional organizational value over the same realization.

The next unresolved connection is why the relevant forms of standing and treatment are warranted within generative unfolding, including relationships not founded on a prior undertaking. This fair-chance case is supporting evidence about ethical constitution, not a mandatory gate for all good. Self-care, finite fulfillment and concern without prior membership retain their existing grounds. The universal identity and the necessity of an extra irreducible ethical principle remain unestablished.

## A future field can generate differentiated claims

### Preserve what the earlier work already established

### The same chances can organize different continuations

Retain the preceding case's equally situated participants, comparable benefit and single opportunity. Before selection, they establish a public procedure: a supported fair signal Z takes values 0 or 1, and the operative rule q₀ assigns the opportunity to A on 0 and B on 1. Both understand which selection is to settle this allocation. The signal, their understanding, the formed undertaking and the administrator's supported response are actual features of the history. Their occurrence does not by itself certify moral authority. In the positive ethical application below, the warrant of this procedure and its settlement function are explicit premises; no fraud, urgent change, warranted release or other defeating ground is introduced.

Code the actual delivery recipient D as 0 for A and 1 for B. Compare three continuations under the same established q₀. The second and third are unannounced departures, not alternative protocols adopted beforehand. Match delivery time and resource costs in this conceptual comparison; later disappointment, reliance and trust effects, if present, would be additional differences rather than the mathematical argument's hidden explanation.

| Continuation | Joint law of signal and delivery (Z,D) | Initial probability that A receives | Probability of performing q₀ |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Honor the selection: D=Z | (0,0) and (1,1), each with probability 1/2 | 1/2 | 1 |
| Reverse the selection: D=1−Z | (0,1) and (1,0), each with probability 1/2 | 1/2 | 0 |
| Independently redraw the recipient | All four pairs, each with probability 1/4 | 1/2 | 1/2 |

Every continuation provides the opportunity to exactly one person, with the same initial recipient probabilities and the same expected endpoint benefit under the preceding equal-benefit appraisal. The first two also have the same joint Shannon entropy, one bit, and the same mutual information between signal and delivery, one bit. The redraw has joint entropy two bits and mutual information zero. These follow directly from the displayed uniform laws and I(Z;D)=H(Z)+H(D)−H(Z,D); both marginal entropies are one bit in all three cases. All three laws are unchanged by jointly exchanging the two labels. Thus neither initial equal chances, this exchange symmetry, nor the amount of statistical dependence distinguishes fulfillment from reversal.

This is a hand-derived representation result, not a proof that the latter continuations are always wrong. It limits those proposed summaries of the field. A probability measure over sufficiently described whole histories can already retain the relevant distinction; the comparison does not refute that representation or require a new substance beyond the unfolding.

### What selection changes within the relationship

On the defended settlement account, before the signal each participant has a claim to the specified fair procedure, without a claim to be its eventual recipient. After Z=0, A has a particular claim to delivery under that same undertaking. B's equal basic standing has not diminished, and B's continuing need has not disappeared. What differs is the allocation-specific relationship that has now formed. Delivery can then fulfill A's award claim without settling every other ground concerning either participant.

The selection therefore does more than reveal a permanently privileged person. An event within the shared activity differentiates a new practical position. This explanation uses the continuing warrant of the formed procedure; it does not infer authority from the mere causal success of the draw. Likewise, discovering a signal that was already settled changes an observer's information but need not form a new award at the moment of discovery.

Conditional on this settlement warrant, restoring a 1/2 chance for each person after the result is not required by their equal standing. It can instead disregard the particular award the fair procedure has just made. Good continuation may close alternatives by completing an undertaking. Equal standing, equal initial chances and equal chances after every event are different requirements. The older post-allocation result also remains: preserving the award does not forbid independently helping B through a newly available, warranted route.

This is a positive, bounded derivation of differentiated treatment from an earlier shared relationship. Its particular addressee depends on actual history, rather than on a fixed priority assigned to A or B at the foundation. Initial standing and the ethical authority of the undertaking remain to be explained at the stronger level.

### The governing relation is not the numerical sign

Suppose the participants had instead established q₁, assigning B on 0 and A on 1, with an otherwise comparably warranted settlement function. Then D=1−Z would perform that undertaking. The same displayed numerical law that departed from q₀ now fulfills q₁. The complete history has changed: actual prior understanding and the undertaking's content differ. This is not an unchanged generative situation with freely reassigned ethical labels. Nor is a post-result declaration that the rule always meant q₁ sufficient to establish the stipulated earlier history.

The comparison explains why the rule's supplied status in a small model does not make it external to generative reality. Its meaning can be formed and maintained through shared activity. It also identifies an explanatory limit: explaining how q was established is different from defending why it should govern delivery. A harmful or coercively imposed procedure does not gain moral authority merely through clear specification or faithful execution.

### Preserve the substantive rival

A rival can grant the equal initial chances and all the described protocol facts while holding that, absent other losses, any continuation with the same fair recipient distribution remains acceptable. It need not mistake reversal for performance of q₀. It can instead deny that performing this particular undertaking supplies an additional moral reason. This preserves the descriptive target while challenging the settlement premise; it is not a counterexample to an implication that explicitly assumes that premise.

The positive reply is that a warranted undertaking can make selection settle what is to be done: its participants organize a common future on that basis. Reopening or reversing it then changes the relationship they actually formed, even when the initial marginal chances remain equal. The earlier commitment argument supports taking this realization seriously, without requiring an extra sum of predicted distress to represent it. The rival still presses a real question about the undertaking's warrant. Calling q₀ the rule, or measuring successful performance, cannot answer that question by itself.

The result consequently refines the field candidate: **ethical significance can concern how actual history transforms the warranted organization of future possibilities, including which possibilities should now be fulfilled or closed**.
