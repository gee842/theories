**What a finite stretch verifies, and what none can refute** ([what-a-finite-stretch-verifies](../results/records/what-a-finite-stretch-verifies.md), [Becoming.Verification](../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/Verification.lean)). A reading is verifiable where every run inside it passes through a stretch through which only runs inside pass. A class of histories is open where it holds every history above one it holds and, holding the union of a directed family, holds a member of it. The open classes are exactly the classes meeting an upper set of stretches, fixed by their finite histories, and exactly the verifiable classes, with no premise either way (`Stretches.open_iff_upper`, `Ideals.open_iff_verifiable`): unlike refuting and closure, verifying and openness are one notion constructively. Refuting a reading is verifying its complement (`refutable_iff_compl_verifiable`). The step back, from a verifiable reading to a refutable complement, is, asked of every order, exactly double-negation elimination, and on runs it gives Markov's principle (`Ideals.verifiable_compl_refutable_iff`, `verifiable_compl_refutable_markov`): what a verification must find is a stretch it is only known not to miss. Every reading is its hull, which finite stretches can refute, met with its remainder, the reading's holding wherever it is adhered to (`split`). The remainder is the largest part that so splits the reading (`remainder_largest`), and no finite stretch refutes it (`remainder_not_refuted`); it is met through every stretch up to double negation, and outright where it is decided whether the reading can still hold at the stretch (`remainder_meets_not_not`, `remainder_dense_of_decided`). For the disjunctive remainder, holding or not adhering, that density is, asked of every process, exactly excluded middle (`orNotAdheres_dense_iff_em`). This is the decomposition of every property into a safety and a liveness property (Alpern and Schneider 1985), stated constructively: separating what a reading says that a finite record can count against from what no record can is deciding, at each stretch, whether the reading can still hold there. What a stretch finds against a reading is exactly what it finds against its hull (`refutes_iff_refutes_hull`), so the remainder, added or taken away, changes no refutation. A reading is dense where every run adheres to it; a refutable reading refuted by no stretch excludes nothing, up to double negation (`Refutable.noStretchRefutes_not_not`). A practice keeping its verdicts within a favoured reading, each verdict fixed by the record so far and met through the record's stretch, exists exactly where the favoured reading is dense (`insulates_iff_dense`), and a refutable favoured reading so kept excludes nothing (`Insulates.refutable_not_not`). In the sequences of moves, some move being `true` is verifiable and dense, and recurrence is dense and neither verifiable nor refutable: no finite record counts for or against it (`Sequences.recurs_dense`, `Sequences.recurs_not_verifiable`). These laws are the formal side of the [demarcation assessment](SCIENTIFIC-PHILOSOPHY.md#15-scientific-standing-and-warranted-correction): a practice that systematically insulates its favoured verdict keeps a verdict no finite record refutes. They do not make refutability a criterion of standing, since a hypothesis about an unending course, such as recurrence, can be honest and dense; what they show is that a verdict kept dense by revision cannot also claim that finite records bear on it. Which readings of an actual inquiry are verifiable, refutable or dense depends on the process they are read on.
