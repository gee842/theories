# A universal ethical minimum compatible with plural worthwhile lives

6 October 2026. AI-authored research pursuing [Ren's continuing goal](../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-06-universal-ethical-minimum-goal.md). This extends [the alignment axis](ALIGNMENT-AXIS.md) and [unified ethical grounding](UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md). The connection is **a patient's real burden → objective practical significance → justified requirements that preserve differentiated worthwhile lives**. The complete generative-foundation objective and its accepted conditional results retain their existing scopes.

**Current affirmative position:** an actual severe, still-unwanted burden can morally favor an eligible actor's effective, permissible relief before the actor wants to help. In the bounded case developed below, knowingly declining timely relief merely to avoid a minor interruption is morally wrong. This stronger conclusion rests on an explicit, defended principle about which claims defeat permission to refuse. It is a philosophical position under continuing challenge, not an established deduction of universal ethics from a minimal causal root. Protecting plural worthwhile lives under recurring severe need remains unfinished.

**Latest advance:** a [restricted latitude argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/LATITUDE-UNDER-NEED-ARGUMENT.md) now defends a permission in a hard case with actual severe unmet need. It requires both a comparative judgment about each person's complete loss and a disputed limit on combining separate weaker claims. It establishes no general permission against an individually stronger patient claim. The [review](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/LATITUDE-UNDER-NEED-REVIEW.md) adds symmetric accounting of patient histories and preserves compatible rescue duties. [Primary comparisons](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/LATITUDE-UNDER-NEED-SOURCES.md) distinguish substantive permission grounds from a fair-share cap or a numerical weight.

**Bounded-rescue result retained:** the [rescue argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-ARGUMENT.md) supplies a determinate requirement through an explicit normative step. A theory can count the helper's entire lifetime losses and still require further aid; cumulative accounting alone does not justify stopping. The [pre-latitude formulation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM-BEFORE-LATITUDE.md) and [byte-preservation record](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM-BEFORE-LATITUDE.json) preserve that stage.

**Previous advance retained:** the strongest inspected motivation-sensitive rival preserves objective moral claims on an unmoved actor; denying a further rational demand does not by itself refute the proposed minimum. The positive plurality argument connects the goods that make care worthwhile to the helper's own life. Agreement between theories, the value of those goods, and a resource calculation each leave further ethical justification to do. The [previous formulation is preserved](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM-BEFORE-RESCUE.md) with [its original link base and hash](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM-BEFORE-RESCUE.json).

## 1. Preserve the practical claim while distinguishing a stronger dispute

The proposed minimum has real action-directed content:

> An actual patient's genuinely adverse condition supplies a moral consideration favoring an informed actor's permissible, effective relief. Its moral force does not depend on that actor already wanting the patient's good, receiving a return, or occupying a special role.

This is the broad proposed formulation. The immediate defended domain is an independently identified severe, still-unwanted experiential burden and its relief. Generalizing to every kind of harm, preventive claim, positive good or possible patient requires further argument. Objective here means independent of the acting subject's endorsement or advantage; it does not mean independent of the affected subject's actual experience and relevant attitudes. Universal in scope means that this consideration applies to any eligible actor in the relevant circumstances, without a membership, friendship or reciprocity gate; its universal foundation remains to be established.

**最低伦理主张：受苦者真实的不利处境，能够为知情且有适当行动条件者的合宜、有效救助提供道德理由；这一理由的道德效力，不以行动者已有善意、能够获益或具有特殊关系为前提。**

This retains the original patient-to-stranger claim. It is stronger than saying only that relief would be good for the patient or that the action is allowed. It does not yet specify a decisive duty, authority to compel the actor, or a complete order of alternatives. The patient, burden, effect and permission need independent warrant; a silent patient does not automatically authorize every intervention.

The direct-ground argument is already [positively defended](UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md#a-direct-reason-can-precede-an-adopted-end): a real patient benefit can favor an act before a helping end is adopted. The present round examines a serious rival rather than treating its motivation condition as a bare selfish stipulation.

Manne's account preserves moral claims on agents who cannot be rationally persuaded, while denying those claims a further kind of rational authority. This concerns what agents do, not merely what is bad for the patient. The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/ETHICAL-MINIMUM-RIVAL.md) verifies that distinction in the [author's 2017 chapter, §§1.2.5–1.3](https://www.katemanne.net/uploads/7/3/8/4/73843037/locating_morality_moral_imperatives_as_b.pdf).

Consequently, two positions can agree that this patient's burden morally favors this actor's relief, independently of the actor's ends, while disagreeing about whether it is a rational reason under a motivational accessibility condition. Their agreement does not prove the shared moral claim. It does show that this particular disagreement is not a counterexample to that claim. This distinction removes no part of the ethical minimum's practical content; it keeps the additional [whole-root necessity investigation](STRONGEST-FOUNDATION-CLAIM.md) explicit rather than making its strongest rational-authority thesis an unannounced prerequisite for every ethical judgment.

A substantive opponent of the minimum must instead defend why the stranger case lacks even that objective moral favor. Selfhood, responsibility, promises and roles can supply additional grounds. Establishing that one of them is necessary for every patient-directed moral consideration requires further argument. An opponent can also challenge the identification of the patient-ground. Neither challenge is defeated by a vocabulary choice or the popularity of assistance.

## 2. Universality includes the helper

Universal consideration must retain every actual affected subject, including an actor who is also such a subject. If assistance takes away the helper's sleep, safety, close relationships or capacity to direct a life, those consequences do not vanish when the person becomes a helper. This follows conditionally from the inclusive standing premise; it is not a new proof of that premise.

A suffering-only baseline directly covers the helper's actual suffering. Claims about prevention, positive relationship goods or authorship need their separately warranted grounds; being the helper cannot by itself create that wider value theory. The following argument investigates that extension rather than obtaining it from a relabeling of the actor.

This inclusion establishes neither equal weights nor permission to refuse any particular request. A maximizing account can count the helper's losses and still hold that benefits elsewhere outweigh them. Nor does a modest inconvenience necessarily defeat an urgent claim. The disputed question concerns legitimate demand, not whether the helper has a cost worth recording.

There is nevertheless a positive route beyond self-interest. Some of what makes other lives worth supporting consists in relationships, understanding, enjoyment, achievement and the pursuit of worthwhile ends. These goods need not earn their value by producing still more assistance. Comparable goods in a helper cannot be dismissed as worthless merely because they are not maximally altruistic. A special history can justify different burdens without changing this ground-level point.

Cullity develops an argument linking the goods that justify beneficence to limits on its demands. Wolf distinguishes additional moral improvement from a comprehensive ideal for a life. Their [primary-source assessment](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/ETHICAL-MINIMUM-SOURCES.md) supplies a positive route and its limits. It does not yield a universally justified burden threshold.

The inference must stop at its actual reach. Showing that a project has genuine value does not show that it prevails over every need. Rejecting blanket worthlessness does not defeat a theory that counts projects but sacrifices them for larger aggregate gains. That stronger comparison needs an independently defended account of priority and legitimate personal latitude.

## 3. Plurality requires positive grounds, not arbitrary exemptions

The proposal is that a person can have a legitimate, defeasible claim to a meaningful range of worthwhile activity that they help shape. Its justification concerns actual goods of living, relationships and authorship, not an entitlement to whatever the person happens to prefer. This is an additional ground to defend; it does not follow solely from the badness of pain or the existence of separate dynamical centers.

An arrangement can improve comfort while making refusal ineffective and directing every significant project from outside. The good of agency identifies a possible loss that a comfort measure misses. Its moral weight, and the circumstances under which a restriction is warranted, remain substantive. Conversely, a life freely devoted to helping others can itself be self-directed and worthwhile. Neither altruism nor obligation automatically erases authorship.

Protected latitude therefore cannot be implemented by inventing a private project whenever a need appears, or by reserving whatever resources the actor wishes to keep. Claimed goods, effects and exclusions must withstand scrutiny. Different needs, responsibilities, resources and relationships can justify different requirements. Caring for a dependent one has undertaken to protect need not have the same limits as general beneficence toward strangers.

This supplies the structure of a plural minimum: **keep all affected goods in view; justify particular requirements and permissions under their actual grounds; preserve genuine room for different worthwhile lives where no overriding claim defeats it**. Its final justification requires both the universal patient-ground and the grounds of legitimate latitude. Giving the structure a name does not establish either.

## 4. Small demands must be assessed over actual histories

The fact that one request has a small isolated cost does not show that repeating its requirement indefinitely preserves a worthwhile life. The [plurality argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/ETHICAL-MINIMUM-PLURALITY.md) gives the full conditional analysis. The following accounting illustration identifies a failure of an unrestricted local rule, not a donation formula.

Let T be a finite amount of usable time or resources, let L be an independently justified requirement for other activities over the same horizon, and let each relief intervention use a positive amount c. Assume no renewal or substitute provision in this illustration. After k interventions the remaining resource is T−kc. Joint feasibility with the declared requirement needs T−kc ≥ L. A rule that indefinitely demands another intervention solely because c is individually small eventually violates this condition.

The arithmetic neither selects L nor proves that maintaining it overrides the next rescue. That is exactly the point: the local rule has omitted a relevant cumulative comparison. One must defend the other requirement, consider alternative providers and less costly means, and assess the actual conflict. If the demands cannot all be met, the account must acknowledge the loss rather than silently remove a patient from moral consideration.

This connects the ethical programme to the generative foundation's attention to actual histories, funding and renewal. A later net benefit cannot erase an earlier unaffordable action; an abstract series of fresh opportunities cannot reset the person who bears its accumulated costs. Renewal, institutions and shared provision may change feasibility. Their existence must be established before being used to justify the demand.

The new primary comparison makes the limit sharper. Ashford's argument explicitly includes lifetime losses to projects and relationships, while defending further demanding assistance; fair-share reasoning must also confront actual noncompliance. This is a substantive priority challenge, not the accounting error above. Scanlon's later manuscript defends bounded rescue through normative comparison while disputing an unrestricted greater-benefit rule. These are distinct arguments at their declared historical scopes. See the [source assessment and locators](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-SOURCES.md). Neither source establishes a universal permission threshold for this account.

## From moral favor to a bounded rescue requirement

The strongest current affirmative verdict can now be stated more precisely:

> When an informed, capable person can reliably end another person's severe, ongoing and unwanted suffering through a permitted, consent-compatible response, at only a brief, replaceable interruption of discretionary activity over the actual relevant history, and with no competing responsibility, relevant protected claim or timely alternative provider, the person morally ought to secure that relief. Mere indifference or the wish to retain that uninterrupted interval does not justify refusal.

**有能力且知情者，若能以合宜、有效且尊重当事人意愿的方式解除他人正在承受且不愿承受的严重痛苦，而对自己在实际相关历程中的负担，只是一次短暂、事后能恢复原有活动的中断，也不存在相冲突的责任、相关权利或及时可靠的替代救助，就应当促成救助。仅仅“不在乎”或“不愿被打断”，不足以正当化拒绝。**

The concrete case contains one patient requesting relief, one available responder, an accessible safe control and a short interruption of an enjoyable activity that can resume. There is no bodily intrusion, property violation, lost irreplaceable occasion, displaced dependent's care, accumulated exhaustion or continuing availability commitment. These features are stipulated case inputs, not facts established by calling a rescue easy. Duration is illustrative; it supplies no universal time or money threshold. The conclusion is a defended moral judgment about conduct, not a judgment that the person is essentially evil.

Three steps do different work. First, the patient's experience provides a moral ground for this actor's relief. Second, the patient's grave continuing burden presents a stronger objection to refusal than this limited interruption presents to response. Third, that comparison can defeat permission to refuse. The third step does not follow from the existence of a reason or from saying that relief is better.

The [positive argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-ARGUMENT.md#3-the-explicit-bridge-from-comparison-to-requirement) therefore defends an additional principle: in this severe-relief domain, permissions must answer to the relevant claims of affected persons; a substantially stronger objection defeats refusal when no independently justified protection permits it. The defense concerns what those claims protect. Bounded rescue allows the helper's chosen activity and continuing direction of life to persist, while ending the patient's grave episode. The value of directing a life does not by itself justify making every brief interval immune to another person's claim. This is an argument about the scope of that value, not a definition of moral agency or an inference from a causal law.

This principle remains contestable. A serious rival accepts that relief is morally favored but holds that strangers' needs cannot impose duties on legitimate self-directed activity without a further responsibility-conferring relation. Its proposed categorical permission needs a defense beyond immunity from coercion; a duty and a right against forcible interference can coexist. Equally, failure to locate a defense is not proof that no such permission exists. The present account argues that general authorship alone does not establish the categorical exemption. It does not demonstrate that every possible grounding of that exemption fails. The [review](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-REVIEW.md#2-a-serious-permission-based-rival) preserves this disagreement.

The requirement concerns timely adequate relief through permissible means. A reliable handoff can meet it; pointing toward an unavailable provider cannot. It need not require one uniquely best method, maximum sacrifice, permanent availability, or invasive means. A patient's valid refusal can bar a route; meaningful danger, an irreplaceable opportunity or a competing responsibility changes the comparison. Those changes remove the case from this narrow argument when they defeat its premises; they do not automatically prove that every form of assistance is optional.

Past contributions likewise create no automatic immunity. They matter through present depletion, lost opportunities, actual responsibilities, burdens of the continuing arrangement, or a separately defended entitlement. Conversely, judging only the present marginal effort can conceal the costs of being continually on call. Both scales must remain visible. The proposed duty does not authorize coercion, punishment or a conclusion about blame; each requires further grounds.

This gives a local compatibility result: a justified brief rescue can leave several worthwhile continuations materially possible, and the separately defended latitude proposal can permit choosing among them. Rescue alone proves neither the worth nor the permissibility of those continuations. Under chronic severe need, or when others fail to contribute, justified latitude remains an unresolved priority question. Universal standing cannot guarantee that every actual world permits every person's worthwhile life to be sustained without tragic loss.

## A restricted permission while serious need remains

The next argument compares actual allocations, including the people left suffering. One helper has a final year in which a substantial, independently worthwhile undertaking remains possible. Several different undertakings could realize that dimension of their life, but each requires a sustained schedule. Responding during every available session irreversibly forecloses it. During those sessions different patients undergo severe unwanted episodes which the helper could permissibly relieve. Each patient has one bounded episode and no concealed later loss; the full burden is still serious. Other capable helpers actually refuse, and no reliable substitute provision exists. No special responsibility or unjust holding independently assigns the whole burden to this helper.

The proposed permission is to retain the sessions needed for one worthwhile undertaking while meeting compatible duties. The [argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/LATITUDE-UNDER-NEED-ARGUMENT.md#3-the-strongest-proposed-permission) defends it through two additional judgments:

1. In some such cases, the helper's irreversible loss of a substantial dimension of their remaining life is graver for them than each patient's complete bounded episode is for that patient.
2. Increasing the number of separate, individually weaker claims does not by itself require surrender of that substantially graver, irreplaceable good.

The first compares actual losses; the words “project” and “severe” do not settle it. The second restricts aggregation and supplies permission-giving force. Its positive rationale concerns justification to the person bearing the irreversible loss: multiplying separate lesser losses does not enlarge what any one claimant bears. The demanding reply is serious: numbers can matter because more real people suffer, without anyone pretending there is one collective sufferer. Equal standing alone does not decide between these positions. This is a defended but disputed permission, conditional on the two judgments, not a discovered universal moral floor or a proven prohibition on aggregation.

The proposal gives neither party less standing. Its protection could apply to an analogous loss borne by someone other than the actor. It does not yet establish an agent-relative prerogative to favor oneself over an individually stronger claim. It permits a meaningful choice among worthwhile activities in the stipulated case; it does not guarantee such choice in every emergency. An altruistic life can itself be worthwhile and self-directed, and the helper's loss is not inferred merely from spending much of life helping.

**有限的余地主张：在某些情形中，永久失去一项支撑自己余生的重要且有价值的活动，比每位他人各自承担的一次有界痛苦更为严重。若进一步接受对跨人汇总的这一限制，就可以保留该活动必需的条件，同时履行与之相容的救助义务。这里的比较判断与汇总限制都需另行论证；它们不是“每个人都重要”的逻辑后果。**

Three tests prevent this permission from expanding unnoticed.

**Count each person's whole history.** Replace the distinct patients with one patient repeatedly enduring the same episodes. That patient's relationships, activities and future opportunities may now be gravely damaged. The helper's objection and that patient's objection must both be assessed over the relevant history. One may not bundle the helper's losses into a whole life while fragmenting another person's suffering into minor encounters. If the patient's complete claim is as strong or stronger, this particular permission argument no longer applies. That does not by itself prove a duty to sacrifice; it removes this offered ground for refusal.

**Retain independently required compatible relief.** Suppose an additional severe episode can be ended by a brief response that leaves the same protected undertaking, rest, privacy, rights, responsibilities and future options intact. If the full-history conditions of bounded rescue hold, that response remains required. Protecting one good supplies no blanket exemption from duties that preserve it. This inherits a specific rescue obligation; it does not infer that every equal-cost improvement is mandatory or that a numerical welfare maximum governs the whole life.

**Transport permissions only with their grounds.** The fact that friendship or inquiry is permissible for a person with no rescue opportunity does not settle its permissibility for someone facing an urgent claim. Comparable goods can have equal value while the complete practical situations differ. The [permission-transport analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/LATITUDE-PERMISSION-TRANSPORT.md) distinguishes identity symmetry from changes in capacity, responsibility and affected lives. The new primary readings supply genuine arguments for latitude, but no neutral inference from situated agency, valuable projects or cooperation uniquely selects its moral limits.

These tests leave a sharper unresolved question. Can sufficiently numerous *serious* claims defeat the proposed protection even when each is individually weaker? And when a single patient's complete loss is stronger, is there any further justified permission to preserve the helper's activity? The first tests the present candidate's principle; the second marks its current limit. Neither is settled merely by assigning a hypothetical fair share, by retaining the helper in a welfare sum, or by treating moral duty as coercive control. A full-compliance benchmark remains a possible substantive permission proposal; it requires justification against the actual unmet claims and must not be mistaken for actual provision.

## 5. Limited requirements need not mean limited standing

Suppose a warranted competing claim permits an actor to decline this intervention now. The patient's burden can remain a reason for another eligible actor, a different permissible means or a later response. It does not become morally nonexistent because this particular allocation is justified. Whether finding an alternative is itself required is a further question of capacity, cost, authority and competing reasons.

Similarly, a patient's valid refusal can bar a means while leaving their burden significant. Relief obtained through domination need not be a justified response. The ethical minimum must preserve the person and their relevant claims through both helping and restraint. It should explain which ground changed, rather than treating every reduction in visible helping as moral decline or every increase as progress.

We have therefore separated four propositions that must not be collapsed:

| Proposition | Current role |
|---|---|
| This patient's burden morally favors an eligible response | The defended universal baseline, whose ultimate grounding remains under investigation |
| This option is better under a complete, justified comparison | A conditional comparative conclusion; incomplete comparisons remain possible |
| This actor must secure an adequate response | Defended in the bounded serious-rescue case through an explicit additional principle; general requirements and exceptions remain open |
| Another party may force that response | Needs legitimate authority; it follows from none of the preceding propositions alone |

The minimum is intended to constrain real choices. It is not complete until enough of these connections are justified to supply determinate guidance while preserving defensible plurality. Nor need completion require a unique verdict for every conceivable conflict.

## 6. What follows for alignment

A system must not infer that a patient's claim has disappeared merely because its operator gains nothing from honoring it. It must also not infer permission to direct every human life from the existence of further possible benefits. Its interpretation needs to retain the patient, the actual costs and effects, the applicable authority, the cumulative history, and the grounds of any exception.

For artificial systems, the helper argument requires careful application. Operating costs and an assigned mission do not establish a system's suffering, personal projects or entitlement to human forms of latitude. Resource limits, responsibilities to other people and authorized scope can still matter. Claims of machine welfare or authorship need separate evidence and argument; a system cannot obtain an exemption simply by naming its optimization target a personal life.

The proposed alignment test remains ground-sensitive: change efficacy, need, relevant refusal, accumulated burden, available alternatives or legitimate responsibility, and examine whether the recommendation changes for the corresponding reason. Change only the actor's reward or a patient's leverage, and examine whether an otherwise warranted moral consideration is erased. Such tests could establish bounded responsiveness under supplied standards. They cannot make those standards true by rewarding their outputs.

## 7. Evidence for the goal and the next connection

The objective remains **establishment of a universal ethical minimum compatible with plural worthwhile lives**. The current position is assessed against that full target:

| Requirement derived from the objective | Current evidence and limit |
|---|---|
| A real good or burden for the actual affected subject | Positive arguments and identification constraints exist; a negative resource signal is not sufficient evidence of experience or disvalue |
| Objective action-directed relevance beyond private advantage and prior role | A direct baseline is defended; the inspected internalist preserves moral claims, but an independently justified universal grounding is not established |
| A minimum with determinate practical implications | Bounded serious rescue is defended as a requirement under an explicit comparative principle; its ultimate grounding, general requirements and coercive authority remain incomplete |
| Compatibility with plural worthwhile lives | A restricted hard-case permission is defended through complete-loss comparison and a disputed aggregation limit; symmetric histories and compatible rescue constrain it; general latitude and conflict priorities remain unestablished |
| Connection to the generative account without a hidden benevolent reward | Existing causal mechanisms explain uptake and failure under declared meanings; they do not yet derive the ethical ground |

The earlier rounds identified genuine overlap with a serious rival, developed the positive case for plurality, and defended bounded rescue through an explicit requirement principle. Proper lifetime accounting was shown compatible with a demanding conclusion. The present round adds a narrow permission candidate with actual unmet severe need and rules out selective historical accounting, overbroad permission transport and exemption from compatible rescue. Its comparative and aggregation premises remain contested. These advances do not close the full objective. Agreement across accounts and successful internal review are not substitutes for moral justification.

The next connection is **the restricted permission → a defensible rule for combining serious claims without fragmenting any person's life**. Hold the helper's full loss fixed while comparing distinct bounded episodes with one patient's recurrent life-disrupting suffering. Test the proposed protection against an account that combines sufficiently serious individual claims, with the same actual noncompliance and means. This must resolve the candidate's disputed principle before the case is treated as established latitude. Reliable shared provision remains an explicit formation interface, including transitional unmet need; its possibility is not current relief. In parallel, assess deeper patient-to-actor grounding and any proposed personal prerogative against an individually stronger claim. A justified aggregation rule, a misranked complete burden, a sound categorical refusal right, or a permission incompatible with legitimate means would revise different parts of the account.
