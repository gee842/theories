### 3.2 What finite stretches decide

**In words.** A history is read off its finite stretches: the histories are the ideals of the stretches, their completion. What a finite stretch can refute is a closed class, what it can verify an open one; every reading splits into a part finite records bear on and a remainder no record refutes; and above both lies what a method comes to say in the limit. Telling whether a history ends is deciding whether it changes again.

- `Stretches.adheres_iff_closed` with `Refutable.not_not`: adhering is the least closed class holding a reading's runs, and a refutable reading holds, up to double negation, at every run adhering to it; removing the double negation, for every process, is exactly double-negation elimination (`refutable_holds_iff`), and telling a settling history from a going one is exactly LPO (`Stretches.cut_decided_iff_lpo`). The finite histories are a compact basis (`Stretches.histories_representation`). Read by moves alone, a history is a course or a sequence of moves (`run_goesOn`), and a process can have every course feasible and no run (`history_without_run`). Used in §2.1 and §8.6.
- `Ideals.open_iff_verifiable`: the open classes are exactly the verifiable ones, with no premise either way; the step from a verifiable reading to a refutable complement is double-negation elimination, and on runs Markov's principle (`verifiable_compl_refutable_markov`). Every reading is its hull met with its remainder (`split`), the largest such part (`remainder_largest`), refuted by no stretch (`remainder_not_refuted`); this is the safety–liveness decomposition (Alpern and Schneider 1985) stated constructively, and its disjunctive form is excluded middle (`orNotAdheres_dense_iff_em`).
- `insulates_iff_dense`: a practice keeping its verdicts within a favoured reading, each fixed by the record so far, exists exactly where the favoured reading is dense, and a refutable reading so kept excludes nothing (`Insulates.refutable_not_not`). Recurrence is dense and neither verifiable nor refutable (`Sequences.recurs_not_verifiable`). Used in §3.5.
- `Limit.still_verifies` with `Limit.Free.settles_not_refuted`: where configurations are told apart, “the last step changed nothing” verifies settling in the limit, while under Markov's principle no method refutes it in the limit; recurrence is the other way round (`Limit.Sequences.lastMove_refutes`, `Limit.Sequences.recurs_not_verified`). Identification in the limit (Putnam, Gold, Kelly) is tracking a constant target (`Limit.identifies_iff_tracks`), and learning through a map is tracking (`LearnableRuns.learnable_iff_tracks`), identification only where the law leaves the feature unchanged (`LearnableRuns.Merge.learnable_not_identifies`). Used in §3.3.

Settling thus sits on three levels: no finite stretch verifies or refutes it, a method verifies it in the limit, and deciding it for every process of a finite world is LPO (`LastChange.lpo_iff_settles`, §3.4). Which readings of an actual inquiry are verifiable, refutable or dense depends on the process they are read on.
