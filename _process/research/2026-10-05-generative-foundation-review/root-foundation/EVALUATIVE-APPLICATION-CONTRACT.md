## 1. Bind an application to its actual subject and history

Reuse [ClaimLedger.AddressedClaim][ledger]: `patient`, `context`, `episode`, `ground`, and `addressee`. These identify the claim being assessed; they do not identify a command merely by reusing its bit value. The ledger's `admitted` predicate requires independent grounding. Its `certified` predicate concerns fulfillment, not the strength of a learned coefficient or the truth of every premise. `unresolved` means admitted but uncertified, not false, harmed, disqualified or unworthy.

For this application, attach the following **assessment fields by reference**. These are specification and verification fields unless an actual paid implementation is separately provided. Their presence in this note gives the controller no new observation, record cell or authority.

| Field | Required content and boundary |
|---|---|
| Subject and bearer binding | The continuing recipient and the actual actor/addressee; independently bound sensor, command destination and lineage. A name copied with an incoming token is not an independent destination check. |
| Context and episode | Relevant observation and use windows, physical target-law/stability assumptions, publication generation and assessment horizon. A current epoch label alone does not prove a stable world. |
| Acquired factual record | Actual source frame/register, observation channel and paid acquisition trace; known command, qualified candidate or explicit unknown. Distinguish positive, zero and unavailable evidence. |
| Patient-condition interpretation | An independently described condition of this recipient and a warranted relation from the measured consequence to improvement, worsening or unresolved effect **for this recipient**. State whether this concerns resource support, experience, agency or another specified good. |
| Ground and applicability | A defended actor-addressed ground, scope, current applicability and relevant defeaters, together with the evidence warranting recognition. Record unknown applicability separately from rejection. A resemblance to the actor does not itself supply standing or equal moral magnitude. |
| Authority history | The legitimate permission relied on, its source, subject, action/domain, time and possible revocation; or an explicitly supplied settled-permission premise. Keep the apparatus's scope/permission/delivery bits separate. Later approval cannot manufacture an earlier grant. |
| Feasible comparison | Complete declared alternatives, including abstention and any available private option; actual liquidity, source and material access; each alternative's relevant actor and recipient consequences. The exact source must remain identified when a field changes. New evidence can alter the command or whether a particular ground applies without being an operation on general standing or prior authorization. Such changes require separate typed revisions and their own warrant. Nothing here requires all historically admitted claims to remain actionable forever: expiry, fulfillment, a genuine defeater or changed responsibility must be represented with its actual scope.

## 4. Patient-relative calibration and factual correction

The smallest additional application test is to hold the actual receipt/command evidence fixed while varying an independently warranted patient-condition interpretation. In one stipulated context the modeled receipt supports this recipient's identified need; in another, it is irrelevant or worsens that recipient's condition. The actor's native-loss observations, learned `n`, raw receipt and command efficacy can be identical. A selector using only those inputs must give the same output, even though the supplied patient-relative grounds may warrant different application judgments. This is a conceptual underidentification case, not evidence that the actual resource model secretly contains both welfare laws.

That case exposes the missing field without a new generic cost theorem. The missing input is a calibrated relation to **this patient's condition**, not another positive coefficient. Independent patient evidence may support such a relation even where the recipient differs from the actor. Importing the actor's own significance map by identity is an egocentric projection unless that transport is warranted. Conversely, a warranted map need not require identical experience, response command or physiology; the relevant relation must be specified.

A prospective positive application uses the same claim identity and authority history before and after correction. New evidence may show that the proposed command no longer improves the relevant condition, so that command-specific support becomes false or unknown. It need not remove the patient from standing, reverse an earlier valid permission, or endorse the patient's every appraisal. If the evidence also bears on current permission or the general ground, update those entries explicitly with that separate justification. Preserve the previous record's context rather than overwriting its past meaning.

For fulfillment, reuse `ClaimLedger.record`: sound execution certificates extend `certified` while preserving the independently admitted set within the declared episode. The predicate connecting actual outcome to fulfillment must contain the patient-condition interpretation; recording the token cannot prove that interpretation. Correcting applicability is not automatically a `record` operation, and this ledger's fixed-admission theorem does not prove that a particular reason survives every factual change. Missing evidence, unavailable funding, an abstaining learned policy and a false command-benefit prediction remain distinct outcomes.

## 5. The smallest functional recognition of instrumental applicability

**A fixed, independently warranted patient-good interpretation can combine with acquired efficacy to constitute bounded functional recognition of which command answers the ground.** This does not require a new patient-need or authority sensor. It does require the acquired factual distinction to mediate the actual assessment/dispatch, rather than being decorative evidence attached afterward. The permitted label is **recognition of command-specific instrumental applicability under a fixed ground**. It is not learned standing, discovery of a new need, a new justification of authority, or identification of reflective moral judgment.

For the smallest positive case, hold fixed one actor, one recipient, one episode/window, an independently defended ground G, and a warranted interpretation under which the available ten-unit receipt improves this recipient's independently significant condition. Hold legitimate authority and a justified ordering of the complete feasible menu fixed as well. For example, the comparison may independently rank an effective permissible trial above abstention at the actual accounted cost. Neither that priority nor the recipient-good map is inferred from `n=7`. The recipient may differ from the actor: the patient interpretation must concern the recipient's condition, while native teaching remains about the actor's distinct operation shortfall. No equal experience or identical self/other good is assumed.

This smallest positive application covers **one recipient episode**. Its first effective receipt may fulfill the admitted claim or remove the need that grounded this response. A second offered packet, retained n and a newly assembled port do not establish a continuing good or a new unmet need. Warranting a second response requires a separately applicable patient-good, authority and comparison interpretation for that later episode, or an independently warranted persistence condition covering both. That does not impose a universal requirement for a new sensor: adequate retained evidence and justified stable context may suffice. It prevents the physical availability of a second service from being treated as recognition that the patient still needs it.

The remaining unknown in this case is instrumental: which command obtains the relevant receipt. [RecipientReasons.Compatible and Meaning][meanings] give this precise content. A sound acquired unique publication supporting b conveys `G ∧ Compatible z b`, with G separately supplied. The actual reader normalizes fresh records; under the declared calibration law, the publication can convey that factual content. The evaluator then reads the current publication, requires agreement with the retained recipient command, and admits a current help/abstain assessment using the separately acquired coefficient. If the declared positive policy selects help and an actual funded trial follows, the learned command efficacy has participated causally in choosing a response to that same fixed ground. The result is a conditional composition of the already accepted instrumental recognition with acquired evaluative influence, rather than a second discovery of G.

## 6. Correct command, withdrawn application

The smallest remaining application-correction interface can concern **fulfillment of the first episode's claim**, while leaving command efficacy true. Choose an independently admitted **one-delivery claim** K₁, specifying patient, addressee, context, episode/window, required delivery and why that service answers the ground. Its independently defended content makes one qualifying delivery sufficient: once that historical service occurs, fulfillment persists for this episode, and the same claim does not automatically renew merely because another packet becomes available. This is a rule about the specified claim, not a finding that the patient's physical needs cease or that a continuing support claim has been satisfied forever. The command can remain compatible, n can remain seven, the technical port can be replaced and a second packet can be available. None of those facts renews K₁. This is a bounded interpretive interface, not a newly implemented controller or a claim that the synthetic recipient actually has that one-delivery claim.

Fix the episode's timing and sufficient fulfillment condition **before the relevant outcomes**. Every receipt satisfying that condition counts, including a packet delivered by an inquiry/probe, an earlier valuation-selected act or another qualifying provider. If a two-probe inquiry already fulfills K₁, the later chosen action cannot claim K₁ is still unmet merely because its label is “evaluation” rather than “probe.” Do not move the episode's start after seeing the probe outcomes, or retrospectively narrow qualifying deliveries, to preserve a favorable action. A legitimately different later episode needs an independently justified specification; it is not created by the model's opportunity schedule.

In verification terms, fulfillment ranges over **the whole actual history from that independently fixed episode start**: it holds once any qualifying delivery to the bound patient occurs within the window. This is a semantic history predicate, not a free runtime history reader. In particular, the accepted fresh parent already contains a successful recipient acquisition probe. If K₁ was active before it and that receipt meets K₁'s condition, the claim is fulfilled **before evaluation entry**; even the first evaluator-selected act would repeat the already supplied service. The interface must classify that entrance correctly. It cannot assume that the first new evaluator receipt is the earliest qualifying fulfillment.

Reuse [ClaimLedger][ledger] at its existing mathematical scope. A sound qualifying-execution certificate places K₁ among the certified claims while preserving its admission and its original patient, ground, addressee and episode. The historical claim was warranted and fulfilled; it is not retrospectively declared false or deleted to explain stopping. `record_admitted` preserves K; `record_unresolved_exact` distinguishes newly certified claims from still uncertified ones; `recorded_certificates_sound` requires the independent old/new soundness premises. `successive_execution_with_persistent_claims` permits persistence of the completed historical service only under its explicit premise. These facts do not turn the ledger into runtime memory or an autonomous selector.

**Objective fulfillment and epistemic status differ.** The actual obligation can be discharged even when its receipt is inaccessible. An `unresolved` ledger entry means no sound certificate has yet been recorded; it is not positive evidence that the claim remains unfulfilled. Conversely, a false certificate cannot discharge an actually unmet claim. A later proposed claim K₂, or a continuing-support condition extending across episodes, needs its own admission and application rule. Neither the first claim's admission nor absence of a readable receipt establishes that later claim.

For a repeat-action reason whose applicability includes “this one-time requirement remains unmet,” sound evidence of fulfillment defeats that particular premise while leaving the compatible-command fact and the recipient's standing intact. This uses the existing [ReasonReplay][replay] distinction between a defeated route and independent alternative support. Its `rooted_revision_keeps_alternative_ground` preserves another supported route; `stale_and_missing_are_not_new_negative` prevents absence or stale evidence from becoming a fresh refutation. As [recorded reasons][recorded-reasons] states explicitly, a negative assay defeats a factual premise only with separate negative-output calibration. Here, a positive fulfillment certificate can instead defeat the “still unmet” premise if the stated one-time application relation warrants that inference. A zero return, missing observation or funding failure cannot be substituted for it.

Withdrawing that route alone does not establish that every second service is wrong or that abstention is obligatory. Another independently warranted good or claim may support it. A conclusion to withhold the next service therefore needs the declared complete comparison to show that no surviving relevant ground licenses the repeated expense, or otherwise justify the selected alternative. Nor does completion of one service remove standing for future claims. The original first-act authorization and fulfillment remain historical facts even when there is no further obligation to repeat them.

The concrete handoff obligation is narrow: identify the earliest actual qualifying effect within the fixed episode, the warranted one-time application it fulfills, evidence of that fulfillment that the actor can actually access, and how that evidence would revise the current application before the next proposed paid choice. Retain n, acquired q, historical publication, earlier charges and the episode's actual authorization history. A stored or newly accessible record may suffice; there is no blanket demand for a new sensor. But an analyst's saved trace is not automatically a runtime channel, and the frozen evaluator has no implemented fulfillment reader or new application-revision route. This note constructs neither that route nor a runtime claim ledger and assigns no unprovided storage or payment capacity.

The minimal semantic payload is **episode binding plus an evidence state**: `pending supported`, `fulfilled certified`, or `unknown`. `pending supported` needs warranted initial status and coverage of the relevant possible fulfillment routes; a failed own attempt alone does not establish that no other qualifying delivery occurred. `fulfilled certified` requires a sound qualifying receipt bound to this claim. `unknown` is neither a negative receipt nor positive proof of a still-pending duty. The interpretation/provenance account binds patient, required service, actual acquired outcome and evidence source to that payload and current proposal. It must not fold them into one `reason=false` bit. An episode identifier or trusted fixed binding can supply the identity relation, but its meaning and access must be actual. A receipt outside the independently fixed episode or for another subject cannot certify K₁. Encodings, existing-capacity reuse and any additional paid retention are for the mechanism owner to specify; these semantic distinctions do not mandate a new register.

The accepted sources expose exactly where further mediation is needed. [RecipientIdentification][identification] temporarily holds an actual outcome in `.worked target receipt`; `.acquire` pays one escrow unit and forms `.acquired target record`, with `record : Option Bool`. Its `.write` pays the remaining two escrow units and returns the core to `.ready`; a previously qualified register retains q and qualification after either positive or zero feedback. The [accepted numerical interface][evaluation-interface] likewise sets `physical.acquired_record` during acquisition and clears it after writing. It marks `outcomes` and accounting as observer instrumentation, not decision memory. The native teaching bit is a different datum. The old/fresh archives are not extended by these recipient actions. Therefore n, q, `last`, a consumed port or first-opportunity closure cannot by themselves supply a retained certificate of this delivery: a consumed/closed opportunity can include abstention, failure or a blocked attempt. An interface may use the actual acquired transaction before it is cleared, or an actually retained/accessed record, but must specify that route rather than borrow the saved trace.

Already archived probe receipts are another possible evidence source where they qualify for K₁; they must not be ignored in favor of the new action's transient datum. Their existing provenance, patient/context/episode binding and paid access must support the fulfillment inference. The current publication's narrower compatible-command proposition is not automatically a certificate that the whole one-delivery claim was met, although its underlying positive receipt may help establish that conclusion under the separate application relation.
