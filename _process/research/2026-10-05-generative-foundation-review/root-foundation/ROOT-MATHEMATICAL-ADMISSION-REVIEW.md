# Full admission and graph completion: independent review

7 October 2026. AI-authored counterreview of the next mathematical-root step, following the [nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). The question is whether the generative root warrants full higher-order domains, beyond reconstructing their truth once those domains are supplied. Mathematical claims below are tied to the new Lean declarations; the foundational appraisal is philosophical argument. Final verification status is recorded in §7.

## 1. What would complete this step

The intended target includes all predicates over an independently fixed admitted carrier and all functions between the relevant admitted carriers. This is the full target already used in the [higher-order reconstruction](ROOT-MATHEMATICAL-HIGHER-ORDER-INTEGRATION.md), not a selected definable or computable range. A successful root argument must explain why every required complete relation is admitted, with an independently defensible rule for the family domain over which completion operates. Replacing the target with a narrower family would revise this question.

Three achievements must remain separate:

1. **Reconstruction:** show that complete predicates and functions have exact presentations by finite-part ideals, and that the specified language retains its full truth under these presentations.
2. **Admission:** justify the availability of all the ideals required for that reconstruction from the root's independently defended formation principles.
3. **Unique constitution:** justify why every adequate account of that same full content must have the proposed dependence on this root.

These are questions about explanatory relations within one account. They do not separate mathematical reality from generative reality or impose an independent mathematical realm. A locally supplied ideal domain can be wholly immanent while its derivation remains unfinished.

## 2. What the admission characterization actually says

In [Admission.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/constitution/admission/formal/Admission.lean), `C : (A → Prop) → Prop` records which predicates are admitted. `IncludesFinite C` admits every list-witnessed finite predicate. `DirectedUnionClosed C` requires closure under **every ambient nonempty directed family** of predicates whose members satisfy `C`. The family variable has type `(A → Prop) → Prop`; it is not restricted to families produced by a language, algorithm or separately admitted family sort.

`full_iff_finite_directed` identifies fullness exactly with finite admission plus this closure. `directed_iff_full_given_finite` isolates the closure commitment when finite admission is given. The proof is informative about the dependency: for arbitrary `S`, `FiniteProfiles S` selects all finite predicates contained in `S`; that family is directed, its members are admitted, and its union is exactly `S` (`finiteProfiles_directed`, `finiteProfiles_admitted`, `union_finiteProfiles`).

This is a valid conditional derivation rather than a formal mistake or a circular Lean proof. Nevertheless, the proposed root cannot treat the family-selection step as already discharged by admitting each finite member. `S` appears in the criterion selecting the whole family. The root's warrant must cover that complete selection and the operation on it. The equivalence is therefore a precise test of a candidate admission principle: in the presence of finite admission, this unrestricted closure has exactly the force of the full predicate claim being sought.

The theorem does not establish that no independent reason could justify the closure. It makes that reason's required strength visible. The open question is whether the root's account of coherent formation supports this full range, not whether we can rename fullness as a completion property.

## 3. The finite/cofinite boundary and what symmetry does not settle

The new module proves a concrete restricted comparison, `FiniteOrCofinite`. It admits all finite predicates, empty and full predicates and singletons, and preserves complement and binary union. `finiteOrCofinite_pattern` realizes any specified membership pattern on a finite list. `finiteOrCofinite_bijection_iff` preserves and reflects admission under every supplied bijection, including arbitrary relabelings of a carrier.

On `Nat`, parity is excluded: `even_not_finite`, `even_complement_not_finite` and `even_not_admitted` give the explicit failure. `parity_directed_failure` identifies a nonempty directed family of admitted finite parity pieces whose union is excluded. The family therefore passes the stated finite and symmetry tests while failing unrestricted directed-union closure and fullness.

This bears directly on one proposed warrant: **the absence of a privileged name or finite discriminating feature does not force all complete patterns to be admitted**. The restricted criterion treats relabelings uniformly and realizes every finite membership pattern. It is not an arbitrary exception for one named subset. Conversely, these properties do not make its restriction appropriate for full second-order mathematics. The fixed full target excludes this family as an adequate interpretation before any metaphysical comparison begins.

Thus this is a genuine counterexample to sufficiency of the weaker finite/symmetry conditions. It is not a counterexample to the fuller root, a full comprehension model, or a rival account preserving every higher-order truth. Nor does it show that all proper restrictions must change a sentence in every chosen language. The actual formal counterexample here is the omitted parity predicate and its directed family.

## 4. The function-graph result is a substantive positive advance

[FunctionGraphs.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/constitution/admission/formal/FunctionGraphs.lean) removes a specific representational input retained in `TypedTruth.lean`: arrow carriers in the completed interpretation need no longer be supplied as ordinary full function spaces. They are represented by ideals of finite relation graphs satisfying functional uniqueness and total coverage.

`functional_iff_finite` connects global single-valuedness with single-valuedness of every finite member, using directedness to bring any two purported values into a common member. `total_iff_finite_coverage` says exactly that every input occurs in some finite graph member. The latter remains a global requirement over all inputs. `empty_functional` and `empty_not_total` show that coherence alone does not supply it when an input is present.

`encodeFunction` takes the finite parts of an actual graph. `decodeFunction` selects the uniquely determined output from supplied totality using `Classical.choose`. `decode_encode` and `encode_decode` prove both inverses. In particular, the result is not merely a surjection onto some functions or a coding of isolated examples.

Two further checked connections prevent shifting the same admission burden between presentations. `functions_admitted_by_completion` admits every supplied function's graph when finite admission and unrestricted directed closure hold on the product carrier. `full_boolean_functions_iff_predicates` identifies admission of the true fibers of **all Boolean-valued functions** with full predicate admission, using the classical characteristic function of each predicate. Its left side concerns the same predicate admission condition `C` applied to those fibers, not a separately defined function-admission family. Thus it closes the proposed route through full Boolean functions when their fiber interpretation is admitted; it does not infer all predicate operations from a bare unspecified function repertoire.

The recursive `GraphCompleted` replaces both predicate and arrow types at every finite simple type: predicates by ideals, arrows by total functional graph ideals over the already reconstructed carriers. Application uses decoding; lambda abstraction encodes its semantic function. `graph_term_transport`, `graph_formula_transport` and `graph_sentence_transport` check these operations and arbitrary mixed finite-type quantification. `higher_eta_graph` supplies a closed higher-type instance. This is stronger than merely remarking that functions can be identified with graphs: the complete declared term/formula semantics is connected to that representation.

The remaining resources are equally concrete. `GraphIdeal` ranges over all ideals on finite subsets of a product carrier. `FunctionIdeal` selects the total functional members of that full domain. Both the forward graph formation and the lambda clause use the ambient semantic function when constructing its ideal. The result establishes exact representation from these resources, not an independent admission procedure for every arbitrary function. No finite algorithm enumerating all functions, constructive totality proof for unspecified relations or independence from the ambient Lean function/Prop framework is claimed.

The language scope is inherited unchanged: finite simple types generated by base, arrow and predicate; terms for variables, application and lambda; formulas for equality, membership, negation, conjunction and existential quantification at any such type. It contains no object-level truth-value type, formula-to-term comprehension constructor, dependent types or internal universe. The theorem is semantic truth preservation, not a decision procedure for that language.

## 5. Strongest affirmative argument and a matched rival

The affirmative proposal deserves its full strength: a mathematical relation is exhausted by its complete extensional compatibility profile, rather than requiring a further hidden bearer. Finite discrimination identifies its parts; directed coherence makes those parts jointly consistent; completion presents the relation itself. The graph construction strengthens this proposal because arrows can now be articulated through the same finite-relation completion interface as predicates.

The reviewed theorem supports the **no surplus extensional content** part at its stated scope. There is no extra function value left unspecified once a total functional ideal is fixed, and there is no extra predicate membership left unspecified once its ideal is fixed. But moving from this conditional exhaustiveness to admission of **every** compatible complete profile requires an additional substantive claim. Neither the absence of an extra bearer nor finite discrimination by itself explains why the entire family exists or is admitted. The symmetry comparison challenges one proposed justification for that move; it does not refute maximality as a possible independently warranted principle.

A stronger matched rival therefore accepts all the very same predicate domains, graph ideals, generation operations, extensional equalities and higher-type truths. It understands their admission through a structural-modal principle: complete compatible relations are mathematically admissible because of that principle's independently specified structural role; finite formation and completion articulate those same relations within the whole. It denies that the minimal generative description has yet explained why this maximal principle holds or why explanatory priority must run from that description to the full domain.

This rival does not require mathematics and activity to inhabit different substances. Its difference is the explanatory status of full structural admission within the same whole. It incurs a real cost: it too owes a warrant for its structural-modal principle. The objection is not that the rival has completed foundations while the root has failed; it is that the present representations do not yet discriminate between these two explanations of the same full target. Declaring the maximal principle part of the root would make a stronger conditional candidate explicit, but would not by itself establish its necessity or eliminate that cost.

## 6. Genealogy and the next inference worth pursuing

The [older closure resolution](MATHEMATICS-CLOSURE-RESOLUTION.md), especially P2/P3/P3a, already separated finite Boolean closure from all external union closure and used finite/cofinite predicates as a boundary. Those results were recorded as classical hand arguments. This round's admission theorem is a checked refinement using directed families, with explicit finite-pattern and bijective-invariance tests. It must not be presented as the first discovery that finite closure is weaker than full admission. The [previous completion attempt](ROOT-MATHEMATICAL-COMPLETION-ATTEMPT.md) also already tested the no-arbitrary-exclusion rationale; repetition under the word “maximality” does not discharge it.

The graph-based recursive interpretation is a further positive completion of the representation programme. Its philosophical lesson is narrower than a universal root conclusion: a common completion interface suffices for all predicate and arrow domains of the declared finite simple-type semantics, under the stated full ideal resources and base correspondence.

The next substantive inference would give an independently defended account of which **whole families** the root admits and why. It would need to explain why this account extends to the entire `FiniteProfiles S` family for every intended `S`, and why total functional graph families at all required levels are included. A successful account may justify an infinitary principle; it need not turn every mathematical object into an effective finite construction. The test is whether its warrant is independent of simply restating full coverage and whether it defeats the matched explanation, not whether its implementation avoids the word “powerset.”

Until such a premise or comparison is supplied, another equivalent encoding will not finish root-derived universality or unique constitution. This is the present argument's limit, not a theorem that those stronger objectives are impossible. The [focused source assessment](ROOT-MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSION-SOURCES.md) helps identify the required admission accounting without supplying its conclusion.
