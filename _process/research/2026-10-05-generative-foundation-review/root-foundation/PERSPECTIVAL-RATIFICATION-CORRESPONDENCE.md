# Ratification: what a discriminating continuation must preserve

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored adversarial correspondence and novelty review of the [trajectory argument](PERSPECTIVAL-TRAJECTORY-ARGUMENT.md), [counterreview](PERSPECTIVAL-TRAJECTORY-COUNTERREVIEW.md) and [whole practical grounding integration](WHOLE-PRACTICAL-GROUND-INTEGRATION.md), following the [nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). **Status: a specified philosophical comparison and representation assessment; no executed intervention, new empirical evidence, new theorem or completed ethical derivation.**

**Adjudication:** test what present assent authorizes by separating its object and the actual consequences of refusal. Removing an office-added obstacle can help distinguish continuing commitment from continued participation maintained by that obstacle. It cannot hold the whole practical relationship unchanged: removing the obstacle itself changes that relationship and may change its good. Persistence of assent can defeat a narrow explanation requiring the obstacle's continued operation, while leaving its historical formative effects and substantive questions of permission, burdens, dissent and the past unresolved. Withdrawal of assent does not establish that the earlier good or earlier sincere approval was unreal.

## 1. What this adds, and what it must not repeat

The existing work already distinguishes prospective inducement, acquired present goods, transition responsibility, formed endorsement and present ratification. The counterreview already proposes a real alternative without added penalties and distinguishes intrinsic loss from obstruction. Those are inherited results. This note adds a **specified object of ratification, an intervention decomposition and an adjudication matrix** that prevent that proposal from certifying more than it tests.

The universal inference advanced or tested remains **whole history and its realization → warranted practical address across formed intelligences**. The present case examines the alleged authority of a current endorsement within that history. It can challenge an exclusion or reveal a misdescription of the practical relation. It does not establish every intelligence's first reason by successfully applying inherited principles of undertaking, permission or repair.

## 2. One history and a precise object of assent

Continue the learning-association case with the following declared construction. A common craft practice develops skill, livelihood, attachment and shared achievement. The offices, members and their different powers are roles constituted through this history, rather than independent substances joined by an external contract.

At entry, the office undertook to issue a competence record once a demonstrated standard was met. The record was to remain usable elsewhere. The common programme included a known term of participation; additional evening work was separately optional. Participants subsequently meet the standard, develop genuine attachments and organize further activity around these undertakings. During this development the office changes its terms: criticism of additional demands can cause the earned record to be withheld and outside recognition to be obstructed. The earlier undertaking did not authorize this change. These are stipulated historical facts; their ethical force is still part of the argument.

At the present boundary, members can sincerely love the craft, prefer to stay and accept demanding work. Those facts do not yet identify what they authorize. Separate at least these questions:

| Stated attitude or act | Its immediate object | Inference it does not itself supply |
|---|---|---|
| “This craft and these relationships matter to me.” | Acquired realization and attachment | Permission for every office demand. |
| “I choose to participate for the next six weeks on the disclosed schedule.” | A bounded present commitment | Open-ended authority to add later demands. |
| “I authorize this specified office discretion, including its stated limits.” | A particular allocation of authority | Authority over dissenters or effects outside that allocation. |
| “I forgive the earlier change of terms.” | A possible change in a present claim or attitude toward the past | Evidence that permission was available when the change was made, or that the earlier choice was then justified. |

An actual ratification must identify its object, scope, duration and relevant affected parties. A favorable interview response, remaining present and exercising an authorization can be different events. Asking a bundled question about “the association” cannot establish all four. The review does not supply a universal test for valid authorization; it specifies what its claimed occurrence would have to concern.

## 3. Separate a diagnostic removal from a costly transformation

Use the following continuations from that boundary. These are constructed alternatives; their empirical availability has not been demonstrated.

**C — Continue current terms.** The office retains the challenged discretion and the obstacle to portability. Existing attachments and participation continue as they actually do, with actual dissent and burden retained.

**D — Remove the identified obstacle.** The office issues the already earned record and withdraws the selectively imposed obstruction to outside recognition. Within the already specified participation term, refusing the newly added evening work does not cancel the participant's established programme access. A credible alternative venue can use the independently assessed competence record; its tools and opportunity to practise are described separately from its relationships. No one is promised that departure preserves an unchanged friendship or membership in the original project. Costs of verification and administration, and effects on other members, are recorded rather than declared zero.

The diagnostic target in D is narrow: **does the obstacle help sustain assent to the challenged demand or continued participation?** It requires more than the office announcing that exit is possible. The record must actually be usable; downstream retaliation and informal gatekeeping must be assessed; participants must have adequate opportunity to understand the changed conditions. These are correspondence requirements for this test, not an exhaustive account of valid consent.

D does not preserve every good. Removing a threat changes security, trust, possible activity and the association's practical form. It may itself constitute an improvement, or alter a practice some members value. Thus a changed response is not a reading of an untouched, hidden “authentic preference.” It is a response within a changed continuation. In a real study, a before/after difference would also require assessment of concurrent changes, changing understanding and delayed adjustment. The philosophical comparison alone identifies none of those empirically.

**T — Transform the association.** Transfer some governance, build another support route, alter the demanding practice or reorganize the common project. This can require substantial resources, change constitutive relationships and create genuine losses. Compare those effects directly. A result obtained by ending the old relationship is not evidence that an isolated portability obstacle was removed while that relationship stayed fixed.

**B — A bounded-commitment control.** A separately specified continuation offers a fully disclosed six-week undertaking whose settled schedule is needed for a particular common achievement. Refusal of a new term is distinguished from abandoning an already undertaken part. Completion ends some shared activity. This control can contain justified constraint and real departure loss. It tests whether the proposed diagnosis mistakenly condemns every narrowed future or treats institutional stability as an obstacle by definition. Its authorization and the worth of the achievement must still be defended.

There is no presumption that D or T dominates C or B. If record portability undermines an identified good, imposes another unaddressed burden, or the supposed alternative is infeasible, the proposed clean diagnosis fails and a substantive comparison remains. Conversely, describing the loss as “constitutive” cannot settle whether its maintenance is justified.

## 4. Exact adjudication matrix

The following are conditional interpretations of specified results, not predictions or observations. “Obstacle removed” means the narrow intervention D has actually met its factual contract, including its stated access and retaliation conditions. It does not mean all dependence or all costs of departure have vanished.

| Condition and response | What this supports or defeats | What it leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| D is fulfilled; assent to the same specified term persists, and members can adequately understand and use the alternative. | Weakens the claim that the obstacle's continued operation is necessary to sustain current assent. Supports taking current commitment seriously. | The obstacle's historical formative effects; validity and scope of authorization; other dependencies; effects on dissenters or third parties; whether the term is ethically permissible; earlier justification. |
| D is fulfilled; members affirm the craft and bounded schedule but reject discretionary additions. | Shows that attachment and bounded commitment cannot be counted as authorization of the larger office power. Challenges an inference from bundled approval. | Whether any particular added demand has a separate justification; what transition response is owed. |
| D is fulfilled; some participants leave while retaining appreciation for the original association. | Shows a changed practical choice can coexist with genuine acquired good. The obstacle-sensitive account remains a serious explanation of prior continued participation. | Whether prior verbal approval was sincere, whether the good was real, and whether the obstacle was the sole cause. Changed actual conditions can themselves change reasons and preferences. |
| Assent persists, but outside use remains blocked in practice, or credible retaliation persists. | The intended diagnostic has not been implemented. Continued assent cannot discriminate the proposed explanations. | Both present commitment and obstacle-dependent participation remain possible. |
| D is fulfilled; some authorize a bounded term and others refuse it. | Supports differentiated practical address: the first group's authorization is evidence about its own specified relation. | Any authority over the refusers, the distribution of common costs, and effects on nonparticipants. Majority approval supplies none of these by itself. |
| The changed arrangement removes the particular joint project or relationship under assessment. | This is a transformation comparison, even if it creates other genuine goods. | It cannot establish that original attachment disappeared merely upon removing an independent penalty; retained and lost goods need direct appraisal. |
| The office opposes a usable alternative even after its stated competence and continuity concerns have been met under the stipulated comparison. | Challenges that stated concern as a complete explanation of the opposition. Identifies a further rationale to investigate. | It does not by itself identify motive, prove domination or establish that the alternative is warranted overall. Other effects might have been omitted. |
| No usable alternative can be supplied, but the narrow control B remains within a specifically justified undertaking. | The absence of an exit experiment does not establish invalid commitment or domination. | The extent of present authority, legitimate exit losses and any duty of assistance must still be assessed. |

The critical outcome is not a preferred direction of the approval curve. It is whether the account changes its claims when **the object of authorization, actual departure conditions or the constitutive good** changes. An account that treats every possible continued endorsement as capture is insulated from correction. An account that treats endorsement after D as sufficient for every term makes the opposite error.

## 5. Temporal responsibility and the stronger rival

There can be a present reason to sustain an acquired good while a reason against the original formation remains. The force of that conjunction must not be obtained simply by stipulating that the good is good and the original act wrongful. The proposed support is relational: an undertaking helped participants organize activity; its alteration changed which possibilities they could actually pursue; existing skill and attachment continue to matter; and current decisions can either answer or deepen the particular vulnerability. Which of these supplies a warranted reason, its addressee and its strength remains substantive philosophical work.

Present ratification can change the current association and its permissions. It need not return to the participants' earlier dispositions as uniquely authoritative. Yet present authorization and the earlier availability of permission occupy different places in the same history. Current release of a claim, where warranted, is not evidence that the original intervention had the later authorization when it occurred. A theory may defend some other retrospective normative effect, but must specify that claim and its ground rather than infer it from present satisfaction.

This case principally tests **additional directed responsibility under induced dependence and undertaking**. Even a successful account would not show that only such histories can give an intelligence a first reason to help, or that every effective opportunity necessarily gives it that reason. The stronger historically sensitive rival can accept this entire case and still contest reasons where no reliance, displacement or undertaken relation exists. The whole-grounding investigation must retain that frontier instead of treating an improved repair example as universal completion.

## 6. Correspondence with the checked trajectory representation

The existing [formal companion](../../../../experiments/valuation-development/lean/perspectival/README.md) checks when a supplied readout preserves a supplied comparison through one fixed law and through separately specified finite action words. No new mathematical theorem is needed for the present adjudication.

| Material the ethical comparison needs | What the existing representation can retain | What its proofs do not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Formation history, disclosures, later changes, acquired goods | `S` can contain a full relevant history, and a supplied step can extend it. | That the current state/readout actually records those distinctions, or that any compressed summary is sufficient. |
| Who agreed to which term, with what practical scope | State and relation data can distinguish specific authorizations and affected roles. | The validity, moral effect or universal foundation of authorization. Encoding the desired permission as a Boolean would assume the disputed conclusion. |
| Feasible continuations and attempted obstruction removal | An action-indexed law can represent attempted intervention, successful removal and blocked outcomes distinctly. | That every action word is physically feasible, available to this office, ethically permitted or under an implemented controller's control. Totality is a formal input. |
| Past wrong, present supporting reasons and transition burdens | History-sensitive relation families can express those targets once specified. | That one binary ordering contains them. The [whole-practical result](WHOLE-PRACTICAL-GROUND-INTEGRATION.md#3-the-checked-mathematical-result) already shows that equal rankings can omit different positive grounds. |
| A changed relationship after D or T | Different reached states and changed comparisons can represent it. | That actual transformation is merely a faithful recoding of an unchanged good. That claim needs correspondence evidence. |

For example, two continuations might end with the same record issued and the same quantity of craft production. In one history the record had been promised and was selectively withheld; in another its additional verification was never undertaken. Whether their present repair responsibilities differ requires the defended historical ground. An endpoint readout cannot be declared sufficient just because it retains production and record possession. Conversely, no theorem requires those ethical judgments to differ merely because histories differ: the ethical relation itself has not been derived.

Adding every moral conclusion to `S` or `R` and checking its preservation would recover supplied verdicts. It would not decide the present dispute. A new theorem would become useful if an independently warranted comparison and an actual record/transition mechanism raised a nontrivial preservation problem. At this stage the missing work is the warrant and scope of authorization and repair, alongside whether the proposed intervention preserves the relations it claims to preserve. Formalizing those moral premises now would add bookkeeping rather than advance the central inference.

## 7. Revision conditions

Revise a diagnosis of obstacle-dependent ratification if the relevant assent survives a credible, understood removal and can be tied to a specified commitment; do not dismiss it solely because it was formed. Revise a diagnosis of an independent penalty if removing it destroys the very good supposedly held fixed; then assess the actual transformation. Revise an inference from current assent to authority if the assent concerns only attachment or a narrower term. Retain criticism where the proposed authorization excludes relevant affected claims, but defend those claims rather than appeal to whole inclusion alone.

No external primary source is introduced here. All cases are specified constructions, and all normative principles retain the inherited status recorded in the cited investigations. This review's novelty is the intervention and inference discipline, not a new proof that present ratification is always valid or always defeated by its genealogy.
