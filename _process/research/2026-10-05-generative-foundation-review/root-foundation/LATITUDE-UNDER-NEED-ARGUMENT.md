# Protecting a life under actual continuing need

6 October 2026. AI-authored philosophical construction for [the universal ethical minimum](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md). Named bridge: **bounded rescue → justified permission when further rescue would irreversibly sacrifice substantial self-direction**. The [rescue argument](RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-ARGUMENT.md), [review](RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-REVIEW.md), [integration](RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-INTEGRATION.md) and [plurality study](ETHICAL-MINIMUM-PLURALITY.md) supply the starting distinctions. This is a proposed normative advance, not a causal derivation or Ren quotation.

**Result:** a narrow permission can be defended by comparing each person's complete loss and resisting the automatic pooling of separate, weaker claims against a life-defining loss. This requires an additional substantive priority principle. Equal status alone does not establish it. The argument does not establish a general permission to preserve ordinary projects against individually stronger severe needs; that demandingness problem remains.

## 1. A hard case with actual patients left unrelieved

Consider a capable adult in a finite final year of opportunity for a longstanding, independently worthwhile undertaking. The undertaking might be learning and practicing a difficult art, a sustained inquiry, or participation in a nonexploitative community practice. Its significance includes what this person actually understands, makes and participates in; another person's equivalent output would not give them that lived activity. Different worthwhile undertakings remain available, but each requires a sustained schedule. The account does not privilege one approved vocation.

The person's circumstances have already removed other substantial opportunities. Completing an adequate sequence of uninterrupted sessions is now necessary to retain a life-integrating activity beyond care work and routine maintenance. Fewer sessions permanently destroy that opportunity. This is a strong factual stipulation about opportunities, time and capacities, not a definition of what morality must permit. An application would need evidence that alternate schedules, less demanding versions and other genuinely worthwhile activities cannot preserve the relevant good.

During every feasible session, identifiable patients suffer severe, unwanted episodes which this person can reliably and permissibly relieve by responding. The episodes cause intense distress and disrupt the patients' activities. Each patient undergoes one bounded episode; no patient dies, acquires a lasting impairment or bears concealed subsequent losses. Different patients are affected on different occasions. Their complete burdens remain serious. Cases in which the same patient repeatedly suffers must aggregate that patient's history, exactly as the helper's history is aggregated.

Responding to every request prevents the uninterrupted sessions. Taking the needed sessions leaves actual severe episodes unrelieved. Other capable adults are wrongfully refusing assistance; none supplies timely relief. There is no funded substitute, reliable handoff or institution arriving before the opportunities expire. The helper did not create the emergency, appropriate resources unjustly or undertake exclusive responsibility for these patients. Prior contributions do not themselves earn an exemption.

The proposed permission is to preserve an adequate schedule for the undertaking while meeting compatible duties. It does not reserve every desirable hour. This self-direction ground alone cannot justify an extra omission when another feasible schedule preserves the same independently warranted goods and all relevant protections. An extra response remains required when it meets the inherited bounded severe-rescue conditions; a mere improvement does not automatically become a duty. Selecting a schedule, communicating availability and correcting avoidable gaps have their own actual costs and efficacy conditions.

The hardship is real on both sides. Preserving the undertaking leaves people suffering. Abandoning it may still leave the helper with an intelligible and valuable caring life. The claimed loss is a particular substantial dimension of their life, not the assertion that altruism makes a life worthless or unchosen.

## 2. What equal status establishes, and where it stops

The patient's burden supplies a reason for this actor, independently of friendship or motivation. The helper's lived goods supply a contrary consideration. Neither person's interests become unreal because the other person's interests are urgent. These are inherited evaluative commitments, not results newly established here.

Equal standing excludes the inference that the helper's loss is negligible simply because it belongs to the person who could help. It also excludes treating patients as less important because they cannot reciprocate. But equal standing does not assign the claims equal weights, prohibit aggregation or reserve a private domain. A maximizing account can recognize both people fully and require the sacrifice. A demanding individual-claim account can judge even one patient's complete loss stronger.

Nor does non-substitution settle priority. Another person's achievement cannot replace this person's activity; likewise, an outsider's comfort cannot remove a patient's pain. Irreplaceability identifies the loss correctly. It does not establish that avoiding it defeats another claim. The positive argument must therefore supply a comparison and a rule about that comparison.

## 3. The strongest proposed permission

The first proposal is evaluative:

> In at least some cases, permanent foreclosure of a person's remaining substantial opportunity for self-directed, worthwhile activity is a graver personal loss than another person's severe but temporally bounded episode without lasting consequences.

This does not equate seriousness with duration. Intensity, risk, lost relationships and control also matter. “Life-integrating” cannot make a cherished triviality prevail by definition. The judgment requires the actual opportunities and burdens just described. Its defense is that losing a substantial dimension through which a person can live their remaining life can reach more deeply than an episode which ends with the patient's ordinary opportunities intact. A critic can coherently dispute that ranking; the pain's severity has not been explained away.

The second proposal concerns requiring force:

> Where protecting such a substantial, irreplaceable good conflicts with relieving separate patients' individually less grave complete burdens, increasing the number of those separate burdens does not, by itself, require the person to surrender the good. The person may retain the activity's necessary conditions while meeting independently justified compatible duties.

This is a **limited protection against pooling claims**, not a theorem from “each person matters.” It applies only where the comparative judgment holds and no responsibility, means restriction or further independently defended priority changes the case. It leaves severe unmet need inside the moral account. It supplies a permission, rather than merely showing that the small-cost rescue argument has stopped applying.

Why accept it? The proposed requirement would make one person's permanent loss the price of avoiding other persons' individually smaller losses. Its defense cannot be that the resulting total is itself someone's larger loss: nobody bears that total. The affected patients each have a serious complaint, but multiplying bearers does not change what any individual stands to lose. Protecting persons as distinct beneficiaries and claimants therefore gives a positive reason to resist a rule that lets any sufficiently numerous collection of lesser claims require this life-defining sacrifice.

The further ethical judgment is that, in this comparison, that reason warrants permission. Without it, the helper's substantial self-direction can be counted accurately yet remain always disposable to the sum. The protected activity need not be socially more productive than aid; its ground is the person's actual life. This supplies a reason for the protection that differs from selecting an attractive donation percentage.

However, the premise remains contestable. Pooling theorists need not invent a collective sufferer: they can say that responding to more genuine persons matters directly. That is a serious counterargument. The present construction answers that numbers alone do not defeat this particular kind of graver individual objection. It does not demonstrate that every account which disagrees misdescribes persons. A stronger foundation must defend that answer against alternative aggregation principles, including principles which aggregate only sufficiently serious claims.

Conditional on these two judgments, the helper may retain the necessary sessions even though severe need remains. The permission has determinate content in the stipulated case. It is narrower than a universal self-direction floor, and the session schedule comes from the activity's actual requirements rather than from an ethical quota invented in advance.

## 4. Why this is not yet an agent-relative prerogative

The argument does not claim that the actor's interest counts more merely because it is theirs. Its comparison could also protect another person's analogous life-defining good from being sacrificed to the same separate lesser claims. The special practical conclusion is a permission for the person whose activity would bear the loss. An agent-relative multiplier is neither supplied nor needed for this limited result.

A broader prerogative would permit preserving one's activity even when another person's complete complaint is stronger. Equal self-direction does not establish that. Nor does the fact that only this person can live this life: only the patient can undergo the patient's life, too. A principle of personal jurisdiction might furnish an additional reason, but its scope and why it defeats the patient's stronger claim would need an independent defense.

“No one may take over my life” is especially ambiguous. Moral duty need not give anyone authority to coerce or direct its performance. A person can recognize demanding duties through their own deliberation. Conversely, recognizing a duty does not erase genuine forgone goods. Neither autonomous endorsement nor opposition to coercion decides whether this sacrifice is required. This note therefore cannot claim to derive personal latitude from a right against conscription.

## 5. The demanding challenge survives in its strongest domain

The [preceding primary-source assessment](RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-SOURCES.md#2-ashford-recognizing-the-helpers-whole-life-does-not-settle-priority) reports Ashford's attention to lifetime project costs and actual noncompliance. This note relies on that assessment, not a fresh primary reading. The important challenge is substantive: patients' remaining complete losses can be worse than the helper's entire sacrifice. Counting the helper more carefully then changes no verdict.

The present permission does not answer that challenge where its comparative premise fails. If one patient faces prolonged severe suffering, deprivation of a worthwhile life or death, that individual's full claim may exceed the helper's self-direction loss. No pooling is then needed to defend a demanding requirement. Likewise, a single severe episode might be judged worse than the particular opportunity forgone. The limited protection supplies no exemption in either case.

Actual defaulting helpers do not disappear from the account. Their refusal may be wrongful and institutions should address it, but the permission proposed here rests on the burden comparison and claim principle, not on the fiction that everyone has helped. If reliable substitute provision becomes available, the conflict changes. If it remains unavailable, the patients still suffer and the permission still requires its difficult justification.

The account also allows grave sacrifice. Where another call satisfies the original bounded severe-rescue conditions, including the same protected undertaking and all relevant rights, histories and other protections, that argument still requires answering. Self-direction alone cannot justify refusing that response merely because another schedule was chosen first. A schedule is not morally insulated by calling it a project. Where all feasible histories impose life-defining losses, this study offers no guarantee that everyone retains a worthwhile range.

## 6. Derivation status and the next decisive test

The supplied factual premises are the patients' complete burdens, consent-compatible effective means, the helper's actual opportunities and schedule dependence, finite history, actual noncompliance and absence of substitutes. The inherited normative premises are patient-directed moral favor, the reality of some worthwhile self-direction goods and bounded rescue. The **new commitments** are a comparative ranking in a specified hard case and limited protection against pooling weaker individual claims. No causal law, learned reward or success measure establishes either commitment.

The positive result is therefore a defeasibly defended permission under those commitments. It advances beyond an unexplained resource reserve by identifying both the protected loss and the exact additional principle. It does not establish a general theory of moderate demandingness, justify permanent availability, or prove that every worthwhile project receives protection. The universal-minimum objective and the broader generative foundation remain unfinished.

Three results would change the account. If the patients' complete losses in the proposed case are at least as grave as the helper's, the present argument cannot establish permission. If a justified aggregation principle can defeat the helper's complaint without erasing its bearer or granting automatic priority to totals, the proposed protection needs revision. If even a stronger individual patient claim can be answered by independently grounded personal jurisdiction, robust latitude would extend beyond this construction.

The next central test should hold the helper's full self-direction loss fixed while replacing many distinct bounded episodes with one patient's equally sustained life-defining suffering. That prevents success in an aggregation-sensitive case from masquerading as a solution to the harder individual conflict. The next explanatory bridge in the generative programme is then how agents recognize, revise and act on whichever priority account survives—not how a controller can be made to implement a desired permission.
