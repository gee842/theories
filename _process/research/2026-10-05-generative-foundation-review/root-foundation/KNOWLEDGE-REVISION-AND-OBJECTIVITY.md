## 1. The affirmative result

**Knowledge can persist through changed concepts and methods when there are warranted connections between what the successive claims concern, what each asserts, and the grounds available for each. Unchanged wording is neither required nor sufficient.** Those connections may preserve only part of an earlier account. They may also establish that an earlier answer was mistaken, or leave a historical question unresolved.

This is a philosophical proposal about epistemic continuity, not a theorem that every conceptual change has a translation. It advances the previous round's central question by specifying what continuity requires, ways to investigate it, and situations where it should not be claimed. The further [scientific-philosophy study](SCIENTIFIC-PHILOSOPHY-AND-GENERATIVE-WARRANT.md) develops its consequences for explanation and the theory's own standing.

**认识的延续，不要求一切旧概念和旧方法不变；它要求说明前后各自在问什么、说什么、凭什么，以及这些关系究竟怎样接续。** 新框架也可能使旧错误更清楚，或使从前不能提出的问题成为可研究的问题。后者不能冒充对旧问题的胜利。

## 2. Six changes that should not be conflated

Begin with the earlier account of formed content: discrimination, retention, history and coordinated use constrain what an assertion concerns. Inquiry can alter any of these relationships. The following distinctions identify different changes within that shared activity, rather than posit a fixed conceptual subject facing an independently constituted object.

| Change | What would justify continuity? | What would be an unwarranted claim? |
|---|---|---|
| Re-encoding or changed notation | The interpretation and inferential use preserve the relevant assertion. | Identical marks alone establish identical content. |
| Corrected answer to the same question | The referent, time and question remain identifiable, while better grounds support a different answer. | The earlier false answer was knowledge because it was reasonable then. |
| Finer question | New distinctions refine the earlier question; a properly qualified old answer may remain correct. | Failure to answer the finer question refutes everything previously known. |
| Changed quantity, referent or time | The change is identified and any comparison is supported by an appropriate bridge. | A different answer about a different target contradicts the old answer. |
| Changed grounds for the same claim | A new competent basis sustains the same content, with its own error conditions assessed. | An accidental original belief was already knowledge because later evidence supports it. |
| Lost access or unestablished comparison | Surviving evidence supports only the claims it can actually discriminate. | A current framework automatically recovers erased historical distinctions. |

Several can occur together. A better instrument can resolve a finer question and reveal that the previous numerical interpretation was biased. A change in language can preserve a reference while altering the associated explanatory commitments. Identifying these separately prevents a single word such as “progress” from concealing the result.

For a historical knowledge attribution we must assess the content and grounds actually available then. A present reconstruction can extract an accurate approximation from an old false universal statement. That does not retroactively supply the earlier person with the qualified assertion or the reasons they lacked. Conversely, a person can have known a limited observation while holding a false theory of why it occurred. The false explanation does not automatically erase the accurately grounded observation.

Factivity remains intact: knowledge is not a false belief that later becomes inconvenient. A belief reasonably taken to be knowledge can be discovered not to have been knowledge. An earlier true, grounded commitment can also lose its present warrant when its sustaining access is undermined. Truth, historical possession and current entitlement are different relations.

## 3. A concrete case: temperature quantities and standards

The [BIPM/CCT kelvin guidance](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/41489682/SI-App2-kelvin.pdf/cd36cb68-3f00-05fd-339e-452df0b6215e?download=true&t=1748263492487&version=2.3) distinguishes thermodynamic temperature from practical scale quantities such as T90. It describes the 2019 kelvin redefinition, continuity of practical scales, and empirical estimates relating them to thermodynamic temperature. Reading scope and document version are recorded in the source note.

Our philosophical interpretation is that a measurement claim has several articulations: the sampled condition, the quantity being reported, the scale, the realization procedure, and the uncertainty of the relevant relation. These emerge within measurement practice. They are not independent substances or a conceptual barrier that prevents contact with the measured condition.

Consider an explicitly hypothetical old report of a sample's T90 value, warranted under an adequately specified procedure. A later estimate of the difference between T90 and thermodynamic temperature can improve a thermodynamic interpretation without falsifying the original scale-indexed report. If the old report instead asserted an exact thermodynamic value without adequate grounds, the new estimate does not rescue that stronger assertion. If sample identity or the original procedure was never retained, present calibration need not answer what the old sample's temperature actually was.

This example is useful because it does not depend on declaring the whole old vocabulary obsolete. A convention can settle an assignment within a scale while leaving the relation between quantities open to investigation. Convention does not settle every empirical assertion made using that assignment. Nor does a changed definition guarantee that a particular laboratory's procedure realizes it correctly.

The case supplies an actual institutional example of maintained distinctions and explicit comparison; it is not our own thermometry experiment. It supports the possibility of accountable continuity, not the claim that every scientific revolution permits an equally precise bridge.

## 4. How a comparison can be investigated without an absolute metalanguage

The threat is circularity: if we assess an old framework entirely in the new vocabulary, have we simply awarded the new one victory? A defensible comparison must do more than translate the old view into a claim that the new view conveniently defeats.

Reconstruct the old commitments from their actual formation and use. Identify an overlap of concern: a retained specimen, an event history, an established operation, a question participants could recognize, or a subsequently learned interpretation of their practice. Then investigate where the proposed bridge succeeds and fails. Different procedures, preserved records, interventions where appropriate, and independently vulnerable checks can constrain it. Neither framework's confidence supplies the result by itself.

These are local achievements. The comparison vocabulary can itself be revised. No completely interpretation-free description is required, but this does not make any interpretation equally adequate. An interpretation that cannot explain the old practice's actual distinctions, or that changes the target whenever challenged, has not yet established continuity. Sometimes learning the earlier practice is necessary before comparison is possible.

[Kuhn's primary discussion](https://epdf.mx/commensurability-comparability-communicability.html) distinguishes local translation difficulties from wholesale incomparability, and interpretation or language learning from substitution into an unchanged vocabulary. This is a useful corrective to a rival caricature. Our account does not claim to refute Kuhn by demanding a neutral language he supposedly forbids; it asks what evidence warrants a particular partial bridge.

The inherited [model-family revision](../../../../theories/becoming/history/MODEL-FAMILY-REVISION.md) illustrates a narrower point. Evidence adequate for the old model family need not retain distinctions required by an expanded family. Fresh inquiry can establish a new current answer without reconstructing everything discarded from the old history. Its grammar, family and stationary phase assumptions are supplied; it is not a general proof about conceptual revolutions. The [method-revision construction](../../../../theories/becoming/history/METHOD-REVISION.md) already shows acquired consequences changing subsequent inquiry. Causal change of method remains different from warranted improvement.

**The correction relation can therefore be partial.** Some old claims survive, some are corrected, some acquire new interpretations, and some become inaccessible. A truthful history of inquiry records that distribution rather than announcing that either everything was preserved or nothing was comparable.

## 5. Objectivity as an achievement within relations

The candidate account distinguishes a claim's actual correctness from our warranted access to it. The fact that a claim is formed historically does not make its correctness identical with its acceptance. A report of the current condition can be false because the reporting practice preserves a stale record. The record, its interpretation and the changed condition all belong to one unfolding; their mismatch requires no fundamental division into two realities.

Objectivity, in the epistemic sense investigated here, is the achievement of making the judgment answerable to the relations it concerns across relevant differences of access and perspective. Achieving warrant does not by itself make the asserted content true. Some claims concern conventions or the inquiry itself; their truth can depend on those practices actually obtaining. Even then, agreement on a report is not automatically evidence that it accurately describes the practice. A true claim may be inaccessible, and a well-supported claim may still be mistaken. “Objective” therefore needs care: correctness, freedom from a particular bias, and public warrant should not be compressed into one infallibility label.

Perspective can enter content legitimately. Two reports from different locations can both be correct because the reports identify different relations. That does not make contradictory answers to the same adequately specified question equally true. Equally, forced agreement among perspectives is not the aim: disagreement can disclose a previously omitted difference in access, condition or meaning.

Inquiry can also alter what it investigates. A test may consume a sample; an interview may change a person's understanding of an earlier experience. Objectivity does not require the investigator's involvement to vanish. It requires the account to include the involvement where it bears on the claim: what preceded the inquiry, what occurred through it, and which conclusion the resulting evidence supports. Calling all these effects one process is a reason to investigate their relationships, not permission to infer an unchanged condition from an intervention that changed it.

Shared inquiry helps when it provides actual complementary access and checks with different vulnerabilities. Counting agreeing people does not identify those relationships. A public standard can embody a competent comparison or reproduce one common fault. The inherited [semantic-calibration study](../../../../theories/becoming/history/SEMANTIC-CALIBRATION.md) preserves the latter possibility: agreement between copied or jointly corrupted records need not establish their present referent. Its specific formal and execution scopes remain unchanged.

This is a positive explanation of how objectivity can develop: histories establish access; practices stabilize reference and comparison; criticism exposes particular error routes; repaired practices support better-grounded commitments. It is not a guarantee that every institution follows that path. Excluding inconvenient observations or silencing competent participants can instead stabilize an error.

## 6. Can coherent activity itself make a belief true?

Hasok Chang's [2017 operational-coherence proposal](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/87be8e18-20f1-4554-b5fd-154176e29a36/content) is a substantive rival. He treats coherent operations as reality-constrained and separates them from a single lucky success. His proposed sufficient truth condition concerns beliefs pragmatically needed in such activity. It is not simply the claim that every useful belief is true. The source note limits the reading and does not claim to assess his later work.

Our earlier beneficial-error example alone does not settle this comparison. A defender can reasonably ask whether the activity was coherent and whether the disputed belief was needed. The disagreement must address those stronger conditions.

Consider our own hypothetical workshop case. A worker reliably estimates readiness from a sound indicator but accepts a false explanation of how the indicator works. In the worker's existing training, that explanatory story is the only cue that elicits the correct procedure. This separates three questions: does the procedure reliably answer the readiness question; does holding the story causally enable this worker to carry it out; and does the story correctly describe the mechanism? If pragmatic necessity means necessity for this trained person's present performance, the second need not settle the third.

A stronger rival can reject that narrow necessity and compare alternative ways of organizing the activity. That reply matters: the example is a pressure test, not a decisive refutation of all operational accounts. But the wider comparison must now explain which alternatives count, how the belief's specific content is implicated, and why retaining the belief supports truth rather than merely maintaining a historically contingent competence. If a satisfactory answer identifies the actual mechanism and controls mistaken alternatives, it may converge with much of our grounding account.

The resulting position is neither “practice is irrelevant” nor “success is truth.” Practice is a principal way in which reference, competence and correction become possible. Whether its result establishes a particular assertion depends on the connections to that assertion's content. Nonduality makes those relationships part of the same explanation; it does not remove their differences.

## 7. How standards can improve without certifying themselves

An existing method can reveal a specific vulnerability without proving that all its outputs are worthless. A revised method can address that vulnerability while inheriting other limitations. This supports a local argument for change when the new procedure's relation to the target has additional grounds, such as a discriminating measurement or a separately assessed failure pathway. The change should retain what was actually learned about the old failure.

The crucial distinction is between an evaluation that merely repeats the method's own verdict and one that brings additional constraint to bear. Complete logical independence of every belief and procedure is not demanded; science could scarcely begin under that demand. Nevertheless, checking a copied answer against the same answer under another label adds no such constraint. The account is fallibilist and does not claim to solve radical skepticism or induction from no commitments.

Greater coverage is one possible improvement, not an automatic overall ranking. A broader procedure can consume more time, introduce a new source of error, or destroy evidence needed for a different question. Epistemic assessment can report better discrimination here and worse discrimination there. Practical selection additionally concerns resources, urgency and consequences for affected lives. A numerical weight trading these off would be an added commitment, not a meaning silently hidden in “better.”

A procedure for evaluating procedures can also be assessed and changed. This does not require an already completed infinite ladder of certifiers. It requires usable present grounds, attention to actual defeat, and willingness to narrow an assertion when its support is insufficient. The unresolved issue is how far those grounds extend, not whether reflection has reached a mythical last level.

## 8. Counterreview and resulting scope

| Objection | What the account must concede or show |
|---|---|
| The new framework chooses all comparison terms. | Reconstruct earlier commitments and establish the bridge through identifiable histories and checks; where this fails, withhold the continuity claim. |
| “Preservation” disguises retrospective rescue. | Attribute earlier knowledge only with its then-available content and grounds; label present reconstruction as present knowledge. |
| Objectivity is merely consensus with a favorable name. | State the actual target relation and error controls; preserve shared error as a real possibility. |
| Operational coherence already includes our favored conditions. | Acknowledge convergence where warranted; exclusive novelty or necessity is not established by renaming those conditions. |
| Revisability makes every position unfalsifiable. | Record which claim changed, what evidence motivated it, and which previous assertion is withdrawn. A changed target cannot count as success on the original target. |
| All comparison assumes reasoning, so nothing has been established. | The proposal explicitly retains truth-directed assertion and fallible evidential commitments. It offers accountable improvement within inquiry, not a presupposition-free deduction of reason. |

The second-round advance is a defended account of **partial epistemic continuity and generated capacities for objective inquiry**. It makes conceptual and methodological revision assessable without requiring either permanent vocabulary or automatic deference to the latest framework. The thermometry case is documentary support; the workshop is a constructed philosophical case; inherited mechanisms retain their supplied premises. None is a universal empirical validation.

This directly advances the governing ethical programme's recognition and calibration connection: judgments about dependence, opportunities and actual losses can survive or change with an identifiable basis. It does not replace normative warrant with accurate prediction. The next study carries the same discipline into scientific explanation, scientific philosophy and the positive role of G0.
