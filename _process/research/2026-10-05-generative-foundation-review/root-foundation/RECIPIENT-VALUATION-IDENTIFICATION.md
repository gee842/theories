# Identifying recipient influence separately from factual learning

6 October 2026. AI-authored identification argument for the connection **understanding a recipient consequence → that consequence acquiring influence on choice**. This note audits the existing model and specifies discriminating observations. It adds no apparatus, new model, proof package or data analysis. The parallel material-to-learned-concern composition remains with its existing owner. Dataset selection and access are a separate evidence lane; no aggregate outcome selected the contrasts below.

**Main conclusion:** a useful observable signature is a change in costly choice when an independently specified recipient consequence changes, within otherwise matched private-payoff and information conditions. This can identify functional recipient sensitivity against a restricted recipient-insensitive alternative. It does not by itself identify the origin of that sensitivity, distinguish every psychological motive, or establish a justified ethical reason.

## 1. The distinctions in the actual model

The [learning account][learning], §§1–5, distinguishes self-related teaching magnitude `β`, learned adverse-value prediction `w`, correspondence in the evaluation input `g`, and effective recipient influence `v=gw`. Its world model can predict recipient outcomes accurately while an identity gate excludes those outcomes from evaluation. L1 establishes held-out underidentification; self training alone does not choose shared rather than actor-relative evaluation. L3 supplies a conditional zero-start acquisition result under explicit teaching, routing and opportunity premises.

The [paid dynamics][dynamics], §§1–3, adds estimated intervention efficacy `z`, true efficacy `Q`, independently specified baseline harm `H`, repair expenditure `a`, and the least private unprotected cost `b`. Its existing choice boundary is

\[
\text{repair chosen}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z[b+H v]>a,
\qquad v=gw.
\]

The actor's predicted recipient outcome, its weight in evaluation and the actual delivered outcome therefore have different roles. Changing `z` can change choice while `v` stays fixed; changing `v` can change choice while beliefs stay fixed. A high estimated efficacy can induce a harmful intervention when true efficacy is poor. The [concern/correction interface][interface], C1–C5, further shows that greater recipient valuation can favor harmless withdrawal over imperfect intervention. Counts of interventions or ongoing cooperation are not sufficient outcome measures.

The actual [formed recipient-support suffix][formed] supplies a costly other-directed response under a declared target route; its recipient returns nothing to the actor. The route programmes are interventions, not inferred preferences. It does not already identify an actor choosing between equally informed evaluative architectures. The [downstream contract](RECIPIENT-EVIDENCE-DOWNSTREAM-CONTRACT.md) likewise preserves the supplied recipient-support objective G. These local results should stay closed at their proved scopes; an empirical valuation analysis supplies a different kind of evidence.

## 2. The smallest causal contrast

For one actor, compare choices between the same private-payoff options while changing their consequence for a specified recipient. “Same private payoff” means the relevant distribution, timing, risk, effort and feasible alternatives, not just equal average money. Recipient information must accurately describe the changed consequence, or the estimand must explicitly concern **believed** consequences.

The null is a restricted recipient-insensitive policy: once its private outcomes, opportunity, information reliability and permitted contextual inputs are fixed, changing the recipient consequence does not change its choice distribution. A randomized or otherwise identified recipient-consequence contrast that changes choice rejects this null, subject to the intervention's exclusion assumptions. Merely correlating concern ratings with choices does not supply that contrast.

Three qualifications make the target precise:

- Varying recipient harm necessarily changes some information reaching the actor. Hold its **accuracy, reliability and the intervention's known efficacy** comparable; do not claim the entire epistemic state is identical while the represented consequence changes.
- Cost can be varied to locate a choice boundary. Within each cost level, match the private menu across recipient-consequence conditions. Costs need not be simultaneously constant across every trial of a cost-response experiment.
- An identity manipulation tests recipient indexing only when outcome meanings, evidence, relationships and relevant roles are preserved. A self/other shock swap changes the actor's own pain, so that contrast alone does not hold private outcomes fixed. Pure name relabeling also cannot exclude a uniform actor-relative self gate.

Under the model's linear evaluation and known `zH>0`, the indifference expenditure is `a*=z(b+Hv)`. Thus a cost grid can test whether the choice boundary responds to recipient stakes beyond the private term `zb`. In a deterministic version, observed repair implies `v>(a/z−b)/H`; nonrepair implies the opposite weak inequality. These are direct readings of the existing threshold, not a new formation theorem. Stochastic choice, indifference, lapses or unknown subjective cost remove the literal single-trial bounds.

With repeated choices, estimate the paired response to recipient stakes before interpreting a fitted valuation parameter. A parameter's scale depends on the money/effort utility and choice-noise assumptions. A zero observed effect can result from insufficient cost support, ceiling/floor choices, low efficacy or imprecision; it does not prove zero concern. An exact numerical `v` is a stronger claim than rejecting invariance.

## 3. What the contrast cannot identify unaided

**Belief–valuation ambiguity.** If expected harm or efficacy is not measured or fixed by a credible task, greater apparent concern can instead reflect a higher estimate of the harm or intervention benefit. Independent probability reports, calibrated recipient assessments and known-outcome choice probes help constrain this alternative. Reports are fallible measurements, not transparent access to internal belief. Holding stimulus count fixed does not establish equal unpleasantness; the [harm-identification audit](HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md) distinguishes exposure, experience, aversion and response.

**Factor ambiguity.** Choices depending on `v=gw` cannot separate `g` from `w`: different admissible factor pairs can give the same product. They also do not distinguish shared representation from another mechanism with the same effective choice rule. Independent self-value and correspondence evidence would be needed to test those components. Fitting an “altruistic learning rate” to action choices is not evidence that this particular factorization was acquired.

**Motive ambiguity.** Guilt, warm glow, norms or observer distress can mediate recipient-sensitive choices. Their existence does not refute functional recipient influence or make care unreal. Conversely, if an unrestricted “private utility” is allowed to depend arbitrarily on every recipient consequence, it can reproduce the same choices; it is no longer the restricted private-material-payoff alternative being tested. To separate recipient improvement from merely suppressing an unpleasant cue, one needs an independently identified repair-versus-concealment contrast with the actor's cue consequences addressed. The existing model's §8 already states this limit.

**Formation ambiguity.** Behavior can change because an existing preference learns which action serves it. This differs from acquiring a new evaluative contribution. A learning block with “earn rewards for this recipient” as the instructed objective, or a common reward for helping, supplies the very orientation whose origin is at issue. It can test contingency learning and use, but not establish concern's genesis. A failure to help before training also does not identify `v=0`; the initial cost may simply exceed its expression threshold.

An acquisition claim therefore needs a controlled pre/post change in the evaluative boundary, with factual accuracy, efficacy beliefs, private utility and choice-noise changes constrained, plus a specified training source. The source might be self-related teaching and independently validated correspondence, as in the repository model, but the shared route remains a further hypothesis to identify. Random assignment of a training condition alone does not isolate valuation if it also teaches the action–outcome contingency or supplies a new reward objective.

## 4. Observable requirements for an accessible dataset

| Required information or contrast | Claim it could support | Claim it cannot supply alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trial-level private money/effort, recipient consequence, beneficiary mapping, chosen option, defaults and order | Outcome-sensitive choice and cost tradeoffs on the observed support | A latent valuation mechanism or ethical standing |
| Known and varied consequences, or independent participant belief/efficacy reports | Separation of estimated consequence from its influence, conditional on measurement | Error-free access to belief or experience |
| Choice-contingent recipient delivery, with selection probabilities and actual implementation recorded | An identified link from choice to delivered recipient effect | That every hypothetical trial was implemented or that stimulus count is welfare |
| Matched OTHER-only offers varying recipient harm while actor monetary menu is fixed | Rejection of private-material-only choice invariance | A pure self/other comparison with actor pain held fixed |
| Outcome removal/devaluation while retained contingencies stay stated and testable | Selective sensitivity to the currently relevant outcome | Acquisition of a new valuation or proof that instruction alone did not drive change |
| Independent training assignment and pre/post boundary plus factual tests | A bounded change in evaluative influence | The zero-start `w,g` mechanism without further component identification |

Use the participant or actual assignment unit for uncertainty, retaining within-participant dependence and every condition. Do not manufacture matched cells absent from the design or treat fitted residuals as randomized interventions. Describe excluded/unobserved offers and any conditioning on successful learners. A prospective reanalysis should freeze its estimand and common-support rule before new aggregates; no such computation is performed here.

## 5. Primary-study leads and their current evidence scope

The existing [cross-domain audit](CROSS-DOMAIN-IDENTIFICATION.md) already covers Lockwood and colleagues' 2016 prosocial learning experiment and its fixed-payment qualification. Repeating it would not supply independently delivered recipient benefit or valuation acquisition.

**Costly human harm choices:** Crockett and colleagues' 2014 study varies offered money, shock count and shock recipient. The decider receives the money, subjects undergo pain calibration, and one randomly selected decision is implemented. OTHER-only money-by-shock contrasts are therefore a promising static tradeoff test; the self comparison has the actor-pain confound described above. The paper itself leaves innate versus learned origins open. In this lane, primary methods were inspected through the indexed full article; reusable raw files have not been verified. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4260587/).

**Separate outcome learning and choice:** the 2023 moral-conflict study records money/shock probability reports and removes one outcome after learning while keeping the remaining contingency stated as unchanged. This offers a sharper belief–choice distinction than ordinary reward-for-other learning. Its prerecordings and deception require separating believed recipient consequences from actual delivered effects, especially in the fMRI helping task. The authors identify public behavioral data at OSF `rk8w4`; access and exact raw fields are being checked in the evidence lane. This is a candidate for selective outcome sensitivity, not established valuation genesis. [Primary methods and data statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-36807-3), [public primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9988878/).

**Actual recipient harm and prior self experience:** Hernandez-Lallement and colleagues' rat study supplies a useful complementary contrast. The [preserved primary paper][rat-paper], STAR Methods “Exposure” and “Experimental conditions” (PDF pages 18–19), and the [original trial file][rat-trials] support the following interpretation. Published outcomes are already known; this note adds no outcome aggregates. The [separate analysis contract][rat-protocol] owns the estimators, source freeze and later reconstruction.

The main contingent-harm comparison keeps each lever's offered food unchanged while introducing a real shock to the neighboring animal following the actor's baseline-preferred lever. Compared with NoHarm progression, this can constrain a food-return-only account of action selection. It also supplies new action–outcome information, so changed choice need not mean newly formed aversion. Equal food does not mean equal private costs: lever forces differ, and actor cues and distress may change.

The prior-experience comparison adds contact with the self-teaching route in `v=gw`. After habituation, exposed actors were alone in the victim compartment for a ten-minute baseline and four shocks of 0.8 mA for one second, separated by 240–360 seconds. NonExposed actors underwent the same exposure session with shockers off. The measured choice baseline occurred the next day, followed by three daily harm sessions. Both conditions otherwise used the contingent-harm procedure. Thus the archive contains a manipulation of prior self experience followed by action toward another's similar physical condition; it does not merely label some actors “more empathic.”

The available male comparison is the twelve exposed ContingentHarm actors numbered 15–26 against the fourteen NonPreExposed actors numbered 47–60. Both have four twenty-slot free-choice sessions; code `0` records a nonperformed trial and must remain separate from choosing the harmless lever. The [source summary][rat-summary] has entirely different `SBL#` record labels for these groups: exposed `SBL042e`/`SBL048`, unexposed `SBL053a`/`SBL060`. Their meaning is undocumented in the inspected sources; possible cohort or batch differences therefore remain unresolved. Random housing and lever-force side are documented; random assignment between these experience conditions is not. The comparison is consequently a descriptive experience-associated contrast unless stronger assignment information is obtained. Pooling female exposed actors into the primary experience comparator would additionally change sex composition.

The relevant quantity is the between-group difference in the baseline-to-harm change in preferred-lever incidence per offered opportunity, retaining all actors and all three harm sessions. The complete preferred/other/nonperformed decomposition distinguishes switching from inactivity; choice conditional on responding is a companion quantity and is undefined when no trial is performed. This compares changes from measured initial lever preference more directly than harm-session means alone. It does not remove possible group or batch differences, or exposure's effects already present at baseline. The published first-session contrast must not license choosing that session as the sole local outcome after the fact; the full trajectory is informative about possible convergence through later observation. Every baseline and harm session starts with four forced trials, two per lever, before the recorded free choices. The first free choice is therefore already downstream of that day's sampling of the contingencies.

The existing acquisition account makes a conditional directional prediction: if added self teaching raises `w`, correspondence `g` is positive and unchanged, and efficacy beliefs and the private choice process are comparable, recipient harm should exert greater influence on action. A larger change after prior self exposure would support this route's plausibility; a precise absence of such a change would constrain its expression in this setting. It would not identify `w` and `g` separately. Shock experience can also teach the meaning of another's reactions, alter estimated personal danger, or increase generalized or context-conditioned fear. The study lacks an equally arousing different-stimulus control for this choice contrast, independently measured recipient understanding, and randomized correspondence training. Its primary discussion explicitly retains these alternatives (PDF pages 12–13).

Most importantly, baseline follows exposure. There is no pre-exposure evaluative boundary showing `w=0` or `g=0`, and pre-existing aversion plus newly learned lever contingencies remains sufficient. The shock's aversive teaching sign, the animal's capacity to learn it and the available social mapping are supplied conditions. This contrast can test whether firsthand aversive history helps connect another's consequence to action; it cannot establish the model's particular zero-start factorization or the formation of ethical standing.

Calling a motivating signal “inside the actor” does not settle whether the choice is other-directed: every motivational mechanism acts through the actor. The useful distinction is **counterfactual target tracking**. If changing the recipient's independently assessed condition changes choice while the actor's cue relief and expected personal danger stay comparable, that favors a recipient-outcome interpretation. A policy valuing only current or expected cue relief under those matched conditions predicts no additional preference for the recipient's recovery when cue suppression substitutes for actual relief. A habit learned from past cues need not immediately follow that prediction. This archive does not supply that repair-versus-concealment separation. Real shocks and the prior-experience comparison make a substantive connection to social outcome learning, while the more specific target-tracking inference stays open.

## 6. Consequence for the foundation

The minimal discriminating claim is modest and substantive: **a learner's accurately represented consequences for an identified other can influence costly choice beyond a declared private-material-payoff model**. Its causal acquisition, exact psychological implementation, wider warranted scope and practical resilience are further questions. The [ethical foundation][ethics] independently defends standing, relevant consistency and answerability; observed willingness to pay neither proves nor disproves those premises.

This maintains Ren's central connection between a future and external integration without turning it into an automatic ethical entailment. Dependence can make external information necessary for competent action. Evidence can identify some other-directed influence. Whether that influence is acquired, justified and sustained requires the separate contracts above. A suitable dataset can strengthen or defeat a specific link; it need not instantiate the whole theory to be informative.
