## What counts as succession

A distinct identifier is insufficient. The successor must have separately backed writable controller state. After activation, actual evidence must change that state while leaving the retired donor's controller and acquired record unchanged. The successor owns the claimed probing, assessment and action capability; the retired donor cannot continue those operations through an alternative public event route. Retained donor hardware, residual cash, archives, ports and obligations remain represented.

The successor need not copy every archive or privately relearn the predecessor's lessons. An explicit paid access path to the retained coefficient is a legitimate inheritance mechanism. Its failure when that support is inaccessible must remain observable. The claim is then transmission of an acquired causal influence through access to retained supports. It is not a claim that the successor experienced the predecessor's losses, or that the coefficient originated within the new learner.

The copied command is initially a candidate. Current qualification, a publication, an unused action token, legitimate authority and truth about the successor's private law do not follow from copying its bits. The old actor bit, if retained in a packed representation, stays historically indexed. Preserving it does not prove correct private prediction for a new actor. Recipient identity and the addressed consequence must remain constant across the actual handoff.
