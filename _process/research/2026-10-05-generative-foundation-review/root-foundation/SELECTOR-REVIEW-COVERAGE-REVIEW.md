## 2. Strongest substantive rival: justified reduction

A responsible inquiry can discard a refuted explanation, anonymize records, combine duplicate descriptions, retain a sufficient summary, or stop funding an irrelevant line. This can protect privacy, preserve finite attention and make an actual response possible. A demand to preserve every description forever would itself conflict with the repository's commitments to finite burden and justified closure.

The positive candidate must therefore distinguish **preserving the present certificate's relevant coverage** from preserving all raw records or retaining every historical objection as live. The former can survive a changed representation. The latter is neither generally necessary nor generally justified. A removed explanation that has actually been defeated supplies no obligation to treat it as an undefeated possibility.

Conversely, choosing a smaller family because it returns a favorable verdict does not establish that omitted concerns have been resolved. If the original family remains warranted for the current decision, reducing its coverage changes what the new certificate can claim. The defect is the unsupported assurance, not the mere physical act of deletion and not automatically the final action taken under uncertainty.

## 3. What a comparison certificate cannot preserve by itself

The proposed reflection condition is about whether some eligible alternative defeats a plan. It does not necessarily preserve which alternative does so, which affected life bears the loss, the grounds making it basic, the supporting testimony, or what remedy is available. Two review families can have the same rejected plans for different reasons. The inherited whole-practical result already demonstrates the broader distinction between order and supporting content.

Accordingly, **“defeat coverage / 击败结论覆盖”** is an accurate name. **“all opposing grounds preserved / 全部反对根据已保留”** would exceed the interface without an additional content correspondence. Privacy-preserving summaries can be appropriate, but an equality of frontiers alone does not establish their epistemic warrant or authorize erasing the consequences owed to a particular affected position.

A one-way soundness condition can also hold uselessly: a conservative summary could reject every plan. Soundness, exactness, a nonempty feasible frontier and executable choice remain different achievements. A certificate lacking an actual choice is not thereby false; nor does its validity settle the decision.

## 4. The scope-changing rival

An old summary may be sufficient for the old feasible menu while failing after a genuine new alternative appears. That does not retroactively make its old scoped use mistaken. What changed is the practical comparison: the new alternative can expose a difference between descriptions that did no comparative work earlier. A sound transfer must establish coverage for that richer decision or narrow its claim.

The same argument can apply to changed facts or claim content, but a menu-change witness does not by itself demonstrate those other cases. Nor does it require retaining every distinction merely because some imagined future menu could use it. A proportionate reconstruction route, fresh evidence or a newly justified summary can suffice. The study must preserve the difference between retaining an accessible route for warranted reconsideration and an impossible requirement to anticipate every future reason.

## 5. What would constitute a real boundary

Neither the need for correspondence nor an open-ended future is by itself a newly discovered wall. The current formal interface can be completed without solving general semantic recognition. Afterward, useful further work remains possible: derive a summary from explicit descriptions, test how it reacts to an actually generated alternative, or connect its acquisition to the existing finite inquiry mechanisms. Such a construction would retain its supplied grammar, support and classification obligations.

A stronger obstruction needs evidence of its own: for example, an actually specified set of accessible observations that cannot distinguish two substantively different support relations before a deadline, together with proof that no common permitted response serves both. Existing observation-class results already provide the generic mathematical criterion; a new study would need an independently grounded realization or a genuinely new interface. Simply toggling a `Good` predicate, withholding an invented oracle, or observing that G0 does not contain a classifier would not establish a substantive impossibility.

This review therefore recommends completing and checking the narrowly new certificate result, then examining the next concrete connection. It does not recommend stopping solely because the universal application problem remains open.
