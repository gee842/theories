## 1. A stronger affirmative argument

Begin with one actual living history. Within it, relatively enduring organizations form, maintain and change their conditions of continuation. Some activity becomes sensing, remembering, learning, experiencing and responding. Other relations become mutual reliance, participation and care. These are differentiated achievements within the unfolding, not independent owners to whom an outside evaluator subsequently attaches value.

This supports a substantive distinction between merely meeting a description and realizing a life. Actual recovery changes what can be undergone and done. Actual learning can change what later counts as a reason, what can be understood and which purposes can form. An account that substitutes a report of satisfaction for those relations can misdescribe what is happening. The completed [knowledge and revision account](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/PHILOSOPHICAL-ACCOUNT.md) supplies warranted examples at its declared scope.

The positive ethical candidate is that **living realization and its further formation have significance in themselves, including the formation of new ways of living and caring**. Existing ends are among the things whose formation and adequacy the account investigates. They need not be treated as an exhaustive advance specification of everything that can matter. G0 makes that orientation explicit; it does not define 生 by first filtering for independently certified good outcomes.

The argument for this orientation has three parts:

1. **Living success and loss have real content.** They concern what can actually live, function, undergo, relate and develop. Their content need not depend on usefulness to the investigator's purposes or a current report of approval.
2. **Further formation can be constitutive of what matters.** A new relationship or acquired understanding can become part of a life and change its purposes. It is not adequately described solely as an instrument delivering an unchanged earlier preference. Earlier preference is evidence about that life, not an automatically exhaustive standard for its continuation.
3. **Whole appraisal preserves these developments together with their costs.** The continuing lives, displaced possibilities, unequal burdens and capacity to respond belong to the same history. No single organization's successful reproduction stands in for that whole.

These considerations make G0 a contentful foundation and give reasons for investigating it. They do not yet establish the universal normative conclusion. The first point identifies actual flourishing; it still requires a defense of the significance claimed for it. The second shows how new purposes and claims can arise; it does not certify every acquired purpose. The third is necessary to faithful whole appraisal; inclusion of consequences alone does not decide the ethical orientation of that appraisal.

The contribution is therefore stronger than dependence on a useful instrument, but narrower than a completed proof that all scientifically competent philosophers must accept the same foundation.

The rival also need not posit an isolated self. A conscious perspective can form through bodily, social and ecological relations; it need not be metaphysically independent. Our nondual method therefore does not disqualify this position merely because it tracks whose life changes. The live disagreement is over ethical scope within the unfolding.

Our criticism of an exclusion argument is also bounded: removing one unsupported superiority claim does not by itself justify every proposed inclusion, equal weight or priority. Likewise, including an organism in moral consideration does not decide every conflict involving it.

## 5. Follow the helping example through a real change of purposes

Consider Ren's comparison: helping benefits both people overall but changes the helper's life. Develop it as a conceptual case, not a reported observation. Two people begin with a limited practical undertaking. Through repeated cooperation they acquire trust, shared competence and a relationship neither had fully anticipated. The undertaking changes what each can do and what each comes to care about.

The favorable feature can include that newly realized way of living together. It need not be reduced to the satisfaction of the two original sets of preferences. This is a concrete positive interpretation of generativity: the activity participates in forming the lives and purposes through which its significance can later be understood.

Three safeguards are constitutive of assessing the actual case, not external disclaimers. First, changed endorsement may result from learning, but may also result from manipulation, exhausted alternatives or imposed dependence. The formation history matters; a later “yes” does not by itself certify the change. Second, mutually beneficial involvement can still displace a different life the helper could have lived. That is an actual consequence to retain. Third, the relationship's good need not require indefinite continuation in its existing form; completion, handover or separation may be warranted by the whole course of living.

This case challenges a **fixed-preference assessment**, if that assessment omits the formation of the relationship and the purposes it enables. It does not defeat every account that grounds value in conscious lives. Such an account can recognize the newly formed relationship and still dispute the broader extension to life without consciousness. The case therefore strengthens one affirmative connection without masquerading as a proof of every scope.

There is also a constructive constraint on our language: “the whole benefits” must preserve what happens in each life and in their relation. It cannot mean that one person's losses become unreal after we widen the perspective. The whole is the connected history being assessed, not a new person whose gain cancels theirs.

## 7. Counterreview of the proposed extension

**“All significance emerges through generativity, so generativity must be good.”** The premise identifies a constitutive or enabling relationship. The conclusion needs an account of the relevant ethical aspect of that relationship and its scope. The common source of relief and injury does not become favorable in every realization merely through being their common source. Our candidate concerns the whole direction of living formation and its actual losses; it is not a valence attached indiscriminately to every causal process.

**“A conscious standpoint is itself generated, so it cannot limit significance.”** Its formation explains its place within the whole. It does not eliminate the possibility that its specific organization matters ethically. The proposed boundary could be justified or mistaken; being generated alone does not decide. The affirmative account should explain what makes consciousness one form of significant living and what warrants extending significance further.

**“New relationships become valued, so their generation is justified.”** Acquisition of concern supplies a phenomenon to explain and sometimes relevant evidence. It can also preserve coercion or hide loss. The resulting orientation is subject to appraisal within the wider relation; ethical validity is not established by successful preference production.

**“If every life matters, producing further life must be favored.”** This moves from how lives count to how alternatives involving their formation count. The generative foundation offers a positive commitment concerning the latter, but the former alone has not supplied that commitment's warrant. Actual prospects, costs, imposed burdens and finite fulfillment remain relevant. No duty to create more lives is deduced here.

**“The rival has not justified its exclusion, so G0 is necessary.”** An unsupported exclusion does not complete our positive justification. It can remove a claimed defeater and keep the extension reasonable to investigate. The universal necessity claim still needs its own grounds. Conversely, the availability of a coherent rival is not proof of its truth or empirical adequacy.

**“Keeping G0 explicit makes it arbitrary.”** An explicit foundation can be rationally supported by its fit with living experience, explanatory integration, coherent consequences and resistance to serious alternatives. Such support is different from showing every adequate scientific philosophy must accept it. The present investigation adds substantive comparison at that stronger boundary; it does not demote the completed axiomatic programme to a failure.

## 11. Consciousness is a differentiation of significance

**The theory's answer is that life can matter beyond its contribution to conscious experience.** Conscious undergoing is one especially consequential development within living relations. Pain, enjoyment, anticipation, understanding and participation change what can be harmed, fulfilled or respected. Recognizing those differences does not require treating conscious experience as the act that confers all significance on everything else.

The positive ground is the living realization named by G0: activities sustaining and developing the conditions through which living continues, takes differentiated form and becomes capable of further relations. Consider a living configuration recovering its organization and capacity for renewal. Under the stated life scope, that recovery can have basic significance even apart from any conscious appreciation of it. This is an application of G0 to a specified conceptual case, not a finding that a particular organism or ecosystem lacks consciousness. Actual cases require evidence about what recovers, what else is affected and what remains uncertain.

This position has a substantive rationale. It can recognize the living relations through which experience forms without assigning all their significance solely according to their contribution to experience. It can also recognize continuation and loss where there is no demonstrated experiencing beneficiary. It thereby offers one connected account of ecological recovery, care, learning and new relationships. That breadth is a reason for the proposal, not an inference that everything causally required for consciousness inherits the same moral status. Whether the broader appraisal is right is the normative issue being defended.

The unit of appraisal is the actual connected history, retaining its differentiated lives. It is neither an isolated organism stripped of its relations nor an imagined cosmic person. Nor must every constituent be assigned a separate intrinsic-value score. A relationship can be constitutive of living well, enabling for another development, or both; the same consequence must not be counted twice merely because two descriptions capture it. Widening appraisal does not erase the person undergoing pain or authorize sacrificing that person to an aggregate.

**Inclusion does not establish equality of weight or identity of treatment.** The theory can regard a nonsentient living recovery as significant while recognizing suffering as a distinct adverse condition. Conflicts still require the actual comparison and declared priorities. No ranking by complexity, consciousness level, organism count or presumed evolutionary advancement is introduced.

The strongest objection remains the conscious-life rival in §2: it can recognize all these relations yet explain their final significance through conscious lives. Our answer rejects its restriction as the theory's position, supported by the broader living ground. We have not shown that its restriction is logically inconsistent or scientifically incompetent. The completion here is a definite defended scope, not a proof that consciousness could never be defended as a boundary by a rival.

**意识使生的意义分化为感受、理解与参与等具体形式；在本理论的生命范围中，它不是一切基本意义的准入门槛。承认更广的生，不等于抹平痛苦、地位与责任的差别。**

## 12. Further formation can be good without an existing claimant

**The theory's answer is that further living formation can itself be a positive realization, even when it does not improve an already existing beneficiary.** This is G0's explicit further-generation commitment. It is not inferred solely from a duty to treat existing lives well.

A new life, relationship or capacity for understanding can introduce actual forms of participation and fulfillment. Earlier preferences may not have specified them, and no prior individual need already have had a claim to their formation. Their positive significance concerns what can come to be lived. If that formation never occurs, we need not invent a deprived person waiting for existence. A reason concerning a possible realization and a debt owed to a determinate claimant are different relations within deliberation.

The rationale is continuity between the worth of living and the formation through which new living becomes possible. The theory gives both a place in the same appraisal, rather than requiring each new realization to justify itself entirely by improving an earlier inventory of lives or preferences. This permits genuine enrichment of that inventory. It also keeps the concrete realization central: merely producing a new label, increasing computational complexity or manufacturing an endorsed preference does not establish what living has formed or how the whole has changed.

Four judgments must remain distinct:

| Judgment | Ground and consequence |
|---|---|
| Further formation has a favorable aspect | G0, in the declared scope, gives the realization basic positive significance. Other adverse aspects may coexist. |
| One continuation is better overall | A faithful comparison must retain actual losses, basic conditions and uncertainty. J1 supports genuine no-loss improvement; difficult tradeoffs need further warranted comparison. |
| A particular response is permissible | Authority, participation, basic protections and the available alternatives matter under the declared practical commitments. Favorable significance alone does not authorize the response. |
| Someone must perform it | A bounded assignment must be justified under J3/J4, with actual capacity and legitimate alternatives retained. A possible benefit alone does not assign this duty. |

Future lives must be included when their conditions are affected; “not presently existing” is not permission to impose deprivation on those who will live. Equally, a merely possible life has no standing demand that a particular person create it. This position therefore gives positive place to future formation while rejecting a universal obligation to reproduce, expand institutions, manufacture minds or maximize population.

The existing [practical account](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/PRACTICAL-JUDGMENT.md) supplies the additional discipline. J2 prevents optional enrichment from justifying imposed basic deprivation under its stated conditions. J3 makes responsibilities bounded and responsive to actual dependence. J4 requires legitimate, jointly feasible selection. These limits are additional declared commitments; neither consent nor a priority rule is smuggled into the word 生.

Present living also has positive significance. A life need not produce descendants, greater complexity or some subsequent achievement to qualify. Rest, care, play and finite fulfillment can matter in their realization. Completing a relationship or undertaking can be appropriate without thereby repudiating the generative direction. The direction concerns the whole course of living, not monotonic growth at every position or moment.

**进一步的生可以是新的正面实现，无须先假定一个尚不存在却已受亏欠的人。正面理由、整体优先、行动许可与特定人的义务分别需要说明；生生不息不要求每个人无限生产。**

## 13. A substantive population-ethics counterreview

**Reading-scope update:** the argument below concerns Frick's welfare-related reasons. The later reading in [§16](#16-a-stronger-rival-can-also-value-continuation) finds further preservation reasons in his wider account. The earlier chapter comparison must not be read as attributing to him a denial of every reason for further living formation.

**New primary comparison:** Johann Frick, *“Making People Happy, Not Making Happy People”: A Defense of the Asymmetry Intuition in Population Ethics* (Harvard dissertation, 2014). Frick argues that welfare-related reasons depend on the existence of their bearers; respecting a person's wellbeing does not automatically supply a reason to create that person. His comparison with promising distinguishes complying with a standard from creating occasions for its application. A standard can also give reasons against foreseeable harmful creation. The proposal concerns how wellbeing supplies reasons, rather than simply privileging people currently alive. Reading scope: abstract and Chapter 1 §§5–6, printed pp. 24–38, including the argument on pp. 30–31 and the acknowledgment on p. 38 that the asymmetry cannot be justified just by stipulating an asymmetrical standard. [Primary dissertation](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/29042ed1-b2ef-4475-8404-a1a836a6e730/content). Link-only extracted text; no full-dissertation review or imported original bytes.

Our response accepts the force of distinguishing standing from formation. It does not treat conditional care as a disguised endorsement of unlimited creation. It locates the disagreement in a further premise: **does living formation have favorable significance beyond satisfying claims conditional on a particular life's existence?** G0 says yes. The ground is the forming and realization of life within the whole, while the practical account preserves particular lives and their claims.

This is an affirmative answer with an explicit foundation, not a refutation obtained from the rival's vocabulary. We need not describe a never-created person as suffering a loss, and we need not infer promotion of every valued feature from the fact that it matters when present. The further-generation commitment is stated directly. A rival may reject that commitment; no new logical contradiction has been established by the present comparison.

Three additional objections sharpen the result:

- **Replacement:** if new living is positive, can one destroy existing lives and replace them with more? Our account retains the destruction, lost histories and affected basic conditions. Favorable formation does not cancel those losses. J2 restricts optional enrichment; incomparable basic conflicts remain conflicts. No interchangeability premise has been added.
- **Expansion:** if every further possibility gives a reason, is completion impossible? A favorable aspect does not assign a duty to realize every possibility. Actual feasibility, warranted relevance, competing living and bounded responsibility shape response. Pure conceivability does not create an equal claim to inquiry or resources. G0 supplies no scalar maximization rule.
- **Harmful generation:** can oppressive institutions or destructive proliferation count as good because they generate organization? Their local formation does not establish favorable whole direction. Real capacities formed can be acknowledged together with harms. The theory must assess what lives and possibilities are enabled and foreclosed; the broader organizational scope is not a blanket approval of persistence or complexity.

These objections leave a definite position and a definite contestable premise. They do not require restarting the already completed comparison and practical machinery.

## 29. What comparability can and cannot add here
