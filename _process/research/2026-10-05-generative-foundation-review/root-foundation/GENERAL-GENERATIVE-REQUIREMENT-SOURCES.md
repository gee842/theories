## 2. New focused primary reading: necessary-means transmission

**Jakob Green Werkmäster, “Normative Transmission and Necessary Means,” *Philosophia* 47 (2019), 555–568, DOI 10.1007/s11406-018-9976-7.** [Open-access publisher text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11406-018-9976-7). The version of record was published online in May 2018; the issue date is 2019.

**Reading scope.** This reviewer read the complete visible primary article, §§1–5, notes 1–22 and references, through publisher HTML. The HTML does not preserve printed page boundaries, so section and note numbers are used. No PDF collation or independent reading of Broome, Kolodny, Kiesewetter or the other works quoted within it is claimed. Exploratory search results for other papers are not counted as readings.

**Source comparison.** The article distinguishes normativity attached to an already justified end from means–end consistency for any intention (§2). Its necessary means must contribute to a possible end, rather than follow from impossibility. It separates three strengths of transmission, defends them through a stated deontic inheritance rule (§3), and considers foreknown nonperformance and uncertain success (§4). It retains the difference between a disjunction of routes and any one route (§5; note 11). Note 12 acknowledges inheritance paradoxes and distinguishes producing a situation that generates an obligation from responding to an obligation that already applies.

**Limits.** The source's account of contribution, probability, control, strong transmission and deontic inheritance is argued, not independently established here. Its strongest principles are not imported as axioms for our ethical conclusion. It supplies a precise comparator for the current argument, not universal generative grounding.

## 3. What this comparison requires of our argument

The following assessment is ours, rather than another attributed result. A useful general practical argument must retain the actual continuation that is justified, the present circumstances and the action's contribution. It cannot start with arbitrary intention and silently conclude moral obligation. It also cannot weaken continuation to any agreeable description of an outcome while omitting the means, burdens and affected relations through which it occurs.

The temporal issue is particularly important for a theory of regeneration. Warranted recovery after an injury does not make producing the injury a warranted generative act. The injury can create a reason to repair; that differs from an available contribution that discharges the reason in the actual situation. A whole history includes the origin of the need as well as the subsequent restoration. No independently existing actor/world division is required to state that difference.

The control issue likewise constrains the interpretation of formal theorems. A possible favorable completion, an available action that preserves some favorable completion, and a policy guaranteeing a condition across its allowed outcomes are different relations. Probability or empirical uncertainty is not captured by replacing those quantifiers with one another. Neither the source nor the new deterministic or relational constructions supplies an empirical guarantee of humanity's continuation.

Finally, deriving general conditional requirements is a substantive achievement without completing the first ethical ground. If a continuation is justified at a given scope, the same justification can bear on its actual necessary relations. Which continuation is justified, whose contribution is required, and how losses and alternatives affect the claim remain questions within the account. The current mathematical package should prove its operational implications while exposing those philosophical and correspondence dependencies.

## 4. Relation to the corrected horizon direction

Ren's correction removes the proposed intrinsic-worth test for a capacity stipulated never to be exercised as a required entrance to this investigation. The source's discussion of foreknown nonperformance is a comparison about means claims; it is not used to restore that withdrawn gate. Presently unused organization can still condition later formation, and uncertainty about exercise differs from a stipulation that it will never occur.

Longer horizons can reveal an actual dependence or change a warranted forecast. They do not convert possible future use into guaranteed use, automatically rank one route above its alternatives, or erase losses already incurred. General situated requirements must retain those distinctions while explaining real continuation conditions.
