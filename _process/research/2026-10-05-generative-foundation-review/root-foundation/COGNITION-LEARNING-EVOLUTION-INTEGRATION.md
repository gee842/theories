## 2. Affirmative explanatory argument

A learned representation is useful beyond an isolated episode when the relations it preserves can participate in further organized activity. Combination provides one route: acquire what primitive operations do and how a reusable operation composes them, then apply that organization to a new structure. The new structure need not have been encountered as a whole. Its interpretation remains answerable to the actual law under which the components and composition operate.

The explanatory work is done by identifiable dependencies. Primitive observations constrain effects; an ordered composite encounter discriminates two composition procedures; the records enter formation of an interpreter; the later query is processed through that interpreter; and execution depends on available support. Failure can be located in any of those relationships. The same decomposition does not make a symbol, its referent and an interpreter three independent ontological realms. They are differentiated roles in the common local history under the [nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md).

This account explains a restricted productive capacity. It does not establish that every kind of cognition involves explicit compositional symbols. The grammar may be represented symbolically, implemented by a network or distributed through an organized practice; those are further correspondence claims. A generic assertion that a neural network can approximate the interpreter would not supply such a correspondence.

Within the larger theory, the developmental question concerns how this operation becomes available and why these encounters occur. The evolutionary question concerns which inherited and reconstructed organizations sustain or alter such learning across descendants. Explaining a learner's current generalization is not already an explanation of either origin. Conversely, the existing origin mechanisms remain relevant evidence when their exact interfaces are connected.

### One changing organization, several consequence pathways

Begin with recurring activity whose effects participate in later activity. Retained changes alter what is detected, which response occurs, how error changes a connection, and how the resulting organization is maintained or reconstructed. An organism, model, role or institution names a relatively organized pattern within that history. Its subsystem is a differentiated operation within the same dependencies; it is not an independently given entity whose interests must later be reconciled with the whole.

Three kinds of consequential change need precise correspondence. Gradient-based training computes sensitivities of a specified performance criterion and uses them to modify parameters. Differential reproduction changes the representation of inherited variants through actual successor formation. Institutional evaluation changes which practices are authorized, maintained, taught or replaced. Their common explanatory structure concerns consequences changing subsequent organization, while their respective credit, construction, retention and transmission laws do real causal work. Replacing those laws with the word selection would explain less.

The proposal is therefore broader than filtering a pre-existing list. Formation can produce new operative connections or reorganize the repertoire from which future changes arise. Our existing method-formation model starts with an absent dispatch connection, constructs and tries a candidate, replaces it after retained evidence contradicts it, and requires later invocation to traverse the replacement. Its assembly machinery and generator recipes are supplied. The calibrated-updater construction likewise forms local contacts and acquires channel references; its output controls actual updating. These are concrete origins of particular operative structures, without a claim that every formative condition is self-created.

### How consequences for a whole reach its parts

In a network, an output error can affect an internal connection through a derivative pathway. In a reconstructed organization, successful joint activity can supply the opportunities and material through which the contributing organization recurs. In an institution, actual results can enter review and revision of participation, procedures or evaluation. These pathways need not grant each part an independently measured reward. A part may persist because the jointly constituted activity is maintained, including when that part rests, consumes support or ends in enabling a successor.

Consequently, three outcome questions remain distinct: whether an organized activity continues at all; whether a particular variant increases its relative representation; and whether the resulting history renews or enriches shared conditions. Ren's generative direction is not reduced to the second statistic. The earlier recursive-selection theorem shows both favorable and adverse transmission of collective advantage under its stated law. Its adverse case refutes guaranteed contributor-frequency increase, not every generative contribution or whole-level renewal.

Whole-to-part pressure also has a route and a delay. Assembly, mixing, turnover, copying, lost association, changed evaluation and conversion during transmission can change what is reconstructed. These mechanisms are part of the positive account: they explain why similar current success can produce different later organizations. They also prevent a subsystem label alone from proving that the selector of a whole automatically favors every contributing internal relation.

### What this adds to the explanation of cognition

A useful structure need not explicitly represent its conditions. For cognition, the account must identify how discrimination, retained information, anticipation and response become organized around what is encountered. Our acquired-binding and historical-source results supply restricted examples: actual encounters constrain interpretation; later source learning changes which retained reports warrant which answers; changed grounds can revise an answer while preserving what it concerns. The distinction between acquiring content and wanting an outcome remains intact.

Selection can explain the formation and maintenance of those arrangements when their operation changes subsequent updating or reconstruction. Their cognitive role is established through the actual content-bearing and inferential relationships, rather than inferred from winning, surviving or receiving a score. Error remains possible because the modeled relationship has a referent and an application range that success on one criterion does not define. Modeling can support prospective use: acquired dependencies can change which encounter is selected before its consequence occurs. Richer counterfactual use requires an implemented comparison of represented alternatives; encounter choice alone does not establish it. This permits learned selection of experience and revision of the learning procedure, without identifying all cognition with one optimization algorithm.

The connection therefore advances the full account in two directions. Within a lifetime, formed information changes future learning and actual use. Across reconstruction, the consequences of such use can change which learning organizations recur. In social activity these histories can overlap through training, participation and institutional change. Neither timescale nor a fixed organizational boundary alone determines the order of a mechanism.

### Generative alignment and the long-run claim

The affirmative route is consequence-sensitive reconstruction. Activities that renew their relevant shared conditions can become more likely to recur when those consequences affect actual maintenance and successor formation, and when reconstruction preserves the capacities responsible. Evaluation and transmission arrangements can themselves be reconstructed through such histories. This is a substantive mechanism for selecting selectors, including routes through which recognition of a dependence changes how continuation is organized.

There are several stronger claims that this route does not imply. A successful feedback signal can track a proxy while omitting the support it consumes. An arrangement can transmit before a delayed destructive consequence arrives. A copied procedure can preserve the wrong mapping after a channel changes. Renewal by other activity can sustain a locally consuming practice, and a whole can lose the conditions for any successor. These are relationships within the same unfolding, not outside evaluators added to a neutral system. Longer duration does not itself repair their missing connections.

Accordingly, unconditional eventual convergence to generative alignment is not licensed by the existence of learning, nested selection or dependence. This assessment rejects that unconditional inference while preserving a constructive conditional direction; it does not prove a universal asymptotic impossibility. No counterexample here establishes that indefinite wholly unsupported continuation is possible, or that every local consumption is a defect. The relevant issue is which organization, condition, duration and relation of reconstruction are being claimed.

G0/J separately articulate the normative significance of living and further generation. Evolutionary prevalence, recognition, motivation and practical warrant remain distinct relations. Their distinction does not split reality into causal and evaluative substances. The current explanatory task concerns how capacities and selection arrangements actually form and continue; its success cannot silently supply an ethical ranking or universal behavioral convergence.
