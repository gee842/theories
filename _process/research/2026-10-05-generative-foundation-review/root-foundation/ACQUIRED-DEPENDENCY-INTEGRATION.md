## What the review changes before another learner is built

The [review](ACQUIRED-DEPENDENCY-REVIEW.md) derives a useful corollary of the
existing ledgers. For an initially intact active site, enough precursor places,
an A increase of n and r subsequent specified damage/repair episodes, exact
feasibility is

`F ≥ r + max(n − L − E, 0)`.

A fixed native rule meets this bound: use existing local support, then source
through capture/copy, then bulk-copy any remainder while retaining the repair
units. This establishes a genuine adequate rival to an acquired controller.
It does not install a free observer, but it rules out claiming that this task
requires every capable system to learn its action effects anew. The generic
argument is a hand consequence of the existing growth and repair ledgers, not
a new Lean proof build.

The worthwhile acquisition question is therefore more precise. A command's
actual connection to the native reactions may be initially unknown. With two
stable connections that exchange the local and bulk routes, the same current
count and total energy do not tell the controller which port preserves common
support. At the one-common-unit decision boundary, both ports replace the lost
A; only one preserves the unit needed for the stipulated later repair. The
adequate first port differs between connections. A fixed informed native rule
remains adequate when direct access is available, while a controller restricted
to initially unknown ports needs an appropriate discriminating relation to
guarantee success across both connections.
Dependency alone therefore does not establish a need for fresh inquiry: the
relevance of a retained distinction depends on the actual access and adequate
alternatives within the history.

This is an information and control distinction within the actual organization.
It is not a claim that the observer stands outside the world, or that the
native event's mathematical name is already an available command. The fixed
consumer rule must remain the same across both actual connections. Uniformly
renaming an already informed controller would not establish acquired use.

## What this can close, and what remains

The acquired content is the port-effect correspondence. Its use applies an
already specified dependency of repair on common support. Observing successful
repair or two copying operations does not establish the general necessity of
that prerequisite, the stability of the entire event law, or the worth of the
undertaking. Two direct records are a symmetric protocol with the same training
consumption across connections; one truthful observation would identify the
other connection under the supplied two-bijection family. More inquiry is not
automatically more justified.

This distinction matters to the common explanation. Native activity supplies
what is retained; retained organization changes which native activity follows;
both change the conditions of continuation. The plant/record/reader separation
tracks these roles and resource relations without making them independent
foundations. The branch also constrains the core's representation: a model of
this claimed acquisition must preserve the actual encounter, its retained
consequence, the reader's use and the resulting continuation. Equal final
success alone cannot identify that history.

The stronger next connection concerns how these access and inquiry arrangements,
and the scope of the continuation they serve, form or are revised through their
consequences. Existing method and inquiry-limit revision remain credited; this
is not a demand to add an approving controller above every controller. It is a
specific remaining explanatory relation within the same unfolding.

continues to govern concern, authority and competing goods. Common support and
copying counts are neither welfare measures nor a derivation of an ethical
