## Hamkins and Linnebo: the semantic comparison

Joel David Hamkins and Øystein Linnebo, *The modal logic of set-theoretic potentialism and the potentialist maximality principles*, arXiv:1708.01644v3, 3 August 2018. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.01644), §1, pages 3–5, especially Theorem 1; page 2 for the authors' philosophical disagreement.

Their model-theoretic account uses reflexive, transitive accessibility with increasing domains. For agreement with an ambient structure, atomic meanings agree and every target object remains accessible from every world. Theorem 1 mirrors first-order truth through modalized quantifiers. This is the established precedent for our relational Lean comparison, not a discovery originating with the root programme. Their discussion also distinguishes the comparison from classifying a system's modal validities. The authors themselves disagree over whether convergent potentialism is implicitly actualist.

**Correspondence assessment:** our checked grammar has relation symbols and equality, without function terms. We implement the translated semantic clauses directly rather than building a separate modal syntax. We do not reproduce their modal-logic classification, all applications to set theory, or philosophical resolution. The paper supports selecting this test; it does not supply independent warrant for our root's constitutive priority.

## Linnebo and Shapiro: what the interpretation commits to

Øystein Linnebo and Stewart Shapiro, *Actual and Potential Infinity*, *Noûs* 53 (2019), 160–191; first published online 2017. [Primary PDF](https://www.pgrim.org/philosophersannual/39articles/linnebostewart-actual.pdf), published pages 167–179, especially 169–175 and 178–179.

They distinguish liberal potentialism, which permits determinate claims about an uncompleted process, from a stricter position. Modal operators can be primitive; possible-world descriptions serve as a heuristic, so a completed-world ontology must not be imposed as their doctrine. Their classical mirroring discussion concerns proof-theoretic principles, beyond our semantic equivalence. Their higher-order discussion distinguishes rigid pluralities from concepts whose applications can expand; its consequences differ for plural and conceptual comprehension.

**Correspondence assessment:** our supplied frame is a metatheoretical representation. Its use does not establish that every potentialist accepts that representation as fundamental ontology. Conversely, a faithful representation does not justify the primitive modalities. Higher-order continuation needs a specified target and independent argument; the paper does not license treating every full predicate as already admitted, nor a blanket claim that potentialism excludes all higher-order comprehension.

## What changes in the present programme

The source assessment corrects the available alternatives: collective mathematical use can receive a modal interpretation without a fully populated stage. Our affirmative argument investigates the warrant for that use; our review tests whether it earns the required scope. The Lean work checks a correspondence and finite branching boundary within explicit hypotheses. These are distinct contributions. Neither published mirroring nor a new compiler receipt establishes that the generative root uniquely grounds every mathematical truth.
