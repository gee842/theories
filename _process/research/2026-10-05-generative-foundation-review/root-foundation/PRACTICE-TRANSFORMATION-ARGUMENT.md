# Continuing the concern can require transforming the practice

6 October 2026. AI-authored argument under the [shared nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). Named connection: **what a practice forms in its participants changes the conditions of its adequate continuation**. The constructive case is Wang Yangming's reported revision of teaching, read alongside existing generative histories. No new controller or event law is introduced.

**Candidate:** a practice's effects can undermine the relationship that made its method appropriate. Continuing its concern then requires answering the changed relationship, which can mean changing the method and the local criterion of success. Preserving a particular exercise is not identical to preserving the people, capacities or purpose it serves. This is a positive account of consequential revision, not a claim that every change improves a practice.

## 1. Begin with a practice that changes through its effects

The [inherited Wang dossier, §2.5](WANG-YANGMING-SOURCES.md#25-事上磨炼在有偏见风险的实际事务中修正), locates a retrospective account: quiet sitting initially helped students, but attachment to quiet and avoidance of activity prompted a changed teaching emphasis. Sitting remained an available method. The [focused genealogy](PRACTICE-TRANSFORMATION-GENEALOGY.md) preserves the fresh primary reading. This report is not controlled evidence of causality or the correction's success.

Our reconstruction starts from this whole sequence. The method encounters particular learners, changes their dispositions, and thereby changes what repeating the method would do. The later problem is partly a product of the earlier formation. It is not merely an unchanged task presented to a newly informed instructor.

The [earlier coformation study](YITI-REN-COFORMATION-SOURCES.md) already investigates mutually changing undergoing, understanding and response. The present addition is the more specific argument that a practice's own formative effects can defeat repeating its earlier successful method.

There are three distinguishable continuities: the people undergoing cultivation, the aspiration guiding it, and the particular teaching practice. They need not persist in the same form or for the same reasons. A practice can end while its participants continue; an acquired capacity can survive a change of exercise. Their relation must be explained through what actually carries forward, rather than equating continuation with repetition.

## 2. Why earlier success can defeat repetition

The positive argument has a determinate structure. A method is initially appropriate because of what it enables under particular conditions. Its use changes some of those conditions. If the changed arrangement now obstructs the capability the method was meant to cultivate, the original favorable assessment no longer licenses repetition on the same grounds. Its historical success remains true. What fails is carrying that assessment forward without examining the relationship it depended on.

This is stronger than saying that context sometimes varies. The practice participates in producing the variation. What counted as a useful intervention can create a disposition that changes the intervention's subsequent role. An adequate explanation therefore includes not only how the method acts on participants but how those changed participants alter the method's applicability.

The same visible result can acquire a different evidential role. Greater quiet might initially indicate an improved capacity for attention; later pursuit of quiet might accompany avoidance of the activities that cultivation was meant to inform. This is our explanatory reconstruction, not an empirical measurement reported by the text. It shows why increasing the original indicator need not preserve the original achievement.

When those relations are independently established, revision need not await discovery of an entirely new ultimate value. A warranted aspiration can already oppose continuing a method that now frustrates it. Conversely, noticing an altered condition does not establish that a proposed replacement works. The transition from diagnosing the old method to vindicating a new one requires its own account of consequences.

## 3. What changes when the aspiration remains stable?

A strong alternative reading preserves the teacher's aspiration and treats the episode as correction of means. That reading establishes a real part of the candidate. Consequential method revision is not empty merely because the ultimate standard stays fixed.

More precisely, the practice can revise **what it treats as evidence of progress**, **how it engages participants**, and **which exercise it repeats**, while retaining a broader aspiration. These are changes in operative criteria and participation. They do not establish that 良知's authority was learned from the episode or that its status became open to criticism. The source's stronger confidence in that authority remains a commitment to assess, not a result of this argument.

The distinction also prevents an opposite overreach. A fixed method that remains appropriate is not defective for remaining fixed. Nor must every consequence be encoded in a separate representation before it affects activity. The account concerns a relationship that makes particular revision appropriate, not a universal command to maximize flexibility.

## 4. The actual histories sharpen what transformation requires

The [participation study](PARTICIPATION-CONSEQUENCE-INTEGRATION.md) already establishes acquired consequences changing enacted recipient interaction through a retained command. It defeats the demand that every learned change must rewire a graph. The present argument does not rediscover that mechanism.

The concurrent [pretraining result](PRETRAINING-EVIDENCE-INTEGRATION.md) supplies another precise connection. Within its specified completed-prefix class, formed socket occupancy retains exact recipient depletion despite cleared immediate latches; replacement retains further information in retired material. Formation itself leaves a discriminable history. It would be mistaken to require a newly invented loss register before acknowledging that information. But the mathematical readout is not an installed use that changes what the practice may next do.

The [protocol reconstruction](APPLICATION-REVISION-RECONSTRUCTION.md) locates the remaining restriction. At the actual four-pair continuing state, positive influence, correct means and sufficient resources do not permit early service: the existing route requires two more acquisition cycles. Withdrawing scope suspends participation; restoring it reinstates the same schedule. Projecting into an older process with fewer restrictions does not supply a transition in this one.

Together these results distinguish retaining an effect, responding differently, and transforming the arrangement that makes responses available. They are actual contributions with separate histories; they are not one newly assembled run. The Wang account provides a constructive comparison because its teaching can change method. It does not lend the formal learner an unimplemented power to do so.

## 5. Preserve the affected continuation, not only the programme

Practice transformation can also be manipulative. A teacher or organization can learn to overcome resistance while preserving only participation in its programme. This differs from correcting a method because of what it prevents participants from doing or becoming beyond that exercise. Both arrangements can observe consequences and adapt successfully. Adaptation alone cannot distinguish them.

The [recipient-continuation account](../../../history/2026-10-05-generative-health/succession/RECIPIENT-CONTINUATION.md) sharpens the positive interpretation: identify what actually continues for the affected being and how the intervention changes its possibilities. A resource increase, stronger link or restored attendance is insufficient by itself. The historical report does not supply independent learner testimony that settles this identification. A learner's account can correct the teacher's description without becoming infallible; the teacher's aspiration cannot make every objection irrelevant by definition.

Changing a method also does not erase its past effects. Where an outstanding burden warrants repair, stopping the practice and answering that burden remain distinct. Preserving concern can require releasing a method, retaining an acquired capacity, changing a relationship or ending an undertaking. The appropriate continuation follows the actual affected history, not the demand that this particular organization survive.

The candidate would need revision if the attributed effects were misidentified, the method's original relevance remained intact, or the proposed transformation preserved programme compliance while defeating the independently identified participant continuation. These are challenges to its constitutive account of adequacy. They are not answered by calling every successful adaptation ethical.

This advances the unified inquiry by locating criteria of practice within consequential formation: their use can change the conditions that made them appropriate, and those changes can warrant their transformation. It does not derive universal ethical authority, the full formation of experience, or the source's ultimate standard. Those remain within the same inquiry, rather than being supplied by the mere fact of revision.
