## 1. Correct the scope of the outstanding claim

The [canonical three commitments](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md#minimal-grounding-and-universal-scope) contain different strengths of assertion. Treating them as three wholly ungrounded universal laws obscures work already done.

| Content | Current ground | Remaining distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Reality permits consequential differentiation. | Independently documented retained-use and biological formation supply actual instances. | Neither every selected difference nor every proposed attribution is thereby consequential or correct. |
| Actual organization involves jointly realized constitutive relations; activity can change their realization. | The argument below supplies general constitutive content; actual operation and transformation have supported instances. | Constitutive compatibility alone does not establish an operative causal attribution or a physical range of unperformed changes. |
| Interactions can compose and recur. | Documented successive operations and joint preparations warrant the permissive claim at their actual conditions. | Separate possibilities do not guarantee joint feasibility, present repeatability or endless continuation. |

This does not quietly replace the stronger middle commitment—“what occurs depends on the participating conditions and relations”—with an existential claim. It separates the parts that have grounds from that sentence's stronger proposed reach. A successful example warrants that such organization occurs; it does not prove that the proposed account is explanatorily sufficient wherever systems form.

Likewise, a general framework need not make every application of it automatically correct. A specific attribution can be warranted and corrigible. The earlier remaining-limit sentence should not be read as requiring an infallibility certificate for all applications, all future observations or every possible act of recognition. Those are not new completion gates.

## 2. The strongest direct constitutive argument

Identify an organization independently of the desired theory: the relations through which a documented operation is performed, a formed structure holds, or a coordinated activity is sustained. Evidence and the stated explanatory question determine which relations matter; merely calling a collection a system does not establish integration.

Where an organization involves shared conditions, its actual realization involves those conditions together. Realizing one part under one condition and another under an incompatible condition does not yet realize the claimed organization. The same material support cannot silently be counted as simultaneously available in two incompatible ways; the same boundary cannot be assigned mutually incompatible states at the same relevant occurrence. Alternation, replacement and time-dependent organization are allowed when their actual relationships supply the joining. This is a constraint of the independently identified organization, not a requirement that everything operate simultaneously or retain the same components.

The argument has substantive explanatory force. It rejects accounts that replace an actual coordination with separately successful fragments, erase a shared dependency, or count a lost option as still available. Its generality concerns joint realization, regardless of implementation or vocabulary. An atemporal whole-constraint account can preserve the same content. No independently constituted actor, environment or outside lawgiver is needed.

It also distinguishes two claims often hidden in “organization constrains change.” Changing the constitutive relations changes the organization at the declared explanatory resolution. Determining which changes are physically possible requires more: an established operative relationship or constraint and a warranted extension to the proposed alternatives. Logical consistency of a description does not establish its physical realization, and failure to realize a trial does not alone establish physical impossibility.

The existing [joining study](EXPLANATORY-JOINING-INTEGRATION.md), [branching study](EXPLANATORY-BRANCHING-INTEGRATION.md) and [derived preparation-domain study](VARIATION-WARRANT-INTEGRATION.md) already give checked mathematical articulations at their respective hypotheses. They distinguish local compatibility from complete joining, coexistence at one reached condition from separate feasible histories, and an exact formed domain from an arbitrary chosen domain. This paragraph credits those results; it introduces no new theorem about all actual systems.

## 4. The attempted stronger inference and its limit

The direct route tested in this round was:

> Actual organization involves jointly realized relationships; an adequate explanation must preserve the relevant relationships; therefore this common relational account suffices to explain the organization and possible changes of every system in its intended scope.

The first two claims do not yet establish the sufficiency in the conclusion. They constrain what an explanation may omit. They do not, by themselves, show that the proposed principles explain all the kinds of organization and change the root undertakes to explain. In a particular case, they also do not identify a causal route from a merely correlated record or extend a witnessed operation to unperformed conditions.

This is not a demand that the foundation derive every physical constant, predict every event, enumerate every possibility or establish a unique ontology. Particular laws and empirical inputs are legitimate at the already completed conditional scope. The stronger request concerns the warrant of the common principles themselves and their explanatory sufficiency, including the recognitional and inferential relations actually used to claim it. A general argument could earn that scope without inspecting every system; this investigation has not supplied such an argument at the requested strength.

Three routes were tested directly:

- **Constitution:** it earns fidelity and joint-realization constraints; it does not alone supply universal causal or modal explanatory sufficiency.
- **Enactment of inquiry:** it supplies an actual instance of consequential participation; it does not extend that instance to all system-forming reality simply because a critic also participates.
- **Accumulated mechanisms and evidence:** they earn particular laws, correspondence and class-relative consequences; their accumulation alone does not discharge the proposed universal inference.

The independent review retains genuine alternatives. A whole-history description is admissible when it preserves the relevant content. Common-driver, unused-record and changed-causal-location accounts test particular attributions where the evidence does not distinguish them. A mere replay of a weaker output list does not refute an account of richer established relationships. No full-target actual counterexample to the minimal core was found; this is not a proof that none can exist.
