## 1. A positive account of why to inquire

**Inquiry is a form of generative activity whose reasons include understanding itself, warranted responsiveness to actual living relations, and the formation of capacities for later understanding and response.** Its worth cannot be read off the number of facts collected. The relations it enables, the lives in which it occurs, and the losses it causes belong to the comparison.

G0 explicitly affirms living and further generation within the whole. Understanding can be a present realization of life: curiosity, insight, shared interpretation and skilled appreciation need not earn their standing only by preventing a future loss. Inquiry can also help disclose a threatened condition, correct an ineffective response or form a new possibility. These affirmative routes are different and can reinforce each other.

No arbitrary exchange rate between them follows from G0. More information can improve a diagnosis and still make the whole course of action worse if obtaining it consumes the only opportunity to respond. A technically impressive investigation can also concentrate control while depriving affected people of meaningful access. Conversely, an inquiry with no immediately identified application can be warranted as understanding and capacity development. The proposal is broader than immediate instrumental usefulness.

**求知本身可以是生的实现，也可以使回应与进一步生成成为可能。决定何时查、查什么、由谁查，须保留调查本身的过程、代价和实际关系，不能把“信息更多”直接等同于“整体更好”。**

## 2. What the familiar value-of-information argument actually contributes

[I. J. Good's 1967 paper](https://fitelson.org/seminar/good.pdf) supplies a strong conditional comparator: its argument uses expected utility, specified hypotheses and acts, and negligible costs of obtaining and using evidence. We read the complete three-page paper. Our account does not refute that argument by introducing costs it explicitly brackets; nor does its conditional conclusion justify every actual investigation.

The new Lean interface makes a related but different point without assigning probabilities or scalar utilities. A policy based on a coarse observation remains available after a recoverable refinement **when the domain and response requirements remain the same**. An actual inquiry may change those requirements by consuming time or support. Being able to ignore a report afterward does not reverse what obtaining it has already done.

The distinction is between the option to use additional evidence within a fixed problem and the whole activity of bringing that evidence into existence. A good answer to the first question need not settle the second. The existence of a suitable policy also differs from its formation, accessibility, comprehension and execution. Our abstract existence result explicitly uses classical choice; it does not install a decision procedure in a real participant.

The earlier [available-question study](INQUIRY-CRITERION-SOURCES.md) already showed why separately attainable questions may not be jointly attainable under a particular apparatus interface. The [bounded inquiry discussion](HUIGEN-FULL-THEORY-AUDIT.md#16-the-unit-of-inquiry-and-the-status-of-its-products) already recognized developing purposes and distinguished exploration from later realized benefit. This round credits those results. Its contribution is the explicit join to warranted projection, action-relevant uncertainty and G0/J1–J4, with the new common-choice and deadline interface.

## 3. The question that matters for a decision

An uncertainty matters to a specified practical decision when resolving it could change which responses satisfy the relevant requirements, how burdens are allocated, whether a duty has been discharged, or which additional comparison is warranted. This is a criterion of decision relevance, not an exhaustive definition of worthwhile inquiry.

If one justified response remains suitable across the live possibilities, identifying which possibility is actual may not be necessary for that decision. If the possibilities call for incompatible responses, a discriminating investigation can make a previously unavailable conditional response possible. If its result arrives after the opportunity has passed, factual resolution may coexist with practical failure.

The eight new Lean theorems check these limited claims in an explicit representation. The general criterion concerns a common admissible choice for each class of histories sharing evidence. The finite examples distinguish a response fitting either case from case-specific responses, and preserve elapsed time. The requirements, domain, truthful probe and deadlines are supplied; ethical adequacy and real-world correspondence are not established by the proofs.

This changes the proposed ideal of wisdom. Wisdom need not require a completed description of everything that exists. It needs sufficiently grounded judgment about the relationships bearing on the response, including recognition of when the remaining uncertainty can be tolerated, when it needs investigation, and when the representation itself has excluded a relevant possibility. These remain substantive achievements; calling a policy “adequate” in a model does not supply them.

## 4. From ethical direction to a bounded reason—or duty—to investigate

The relevant premises must remain explicit:

| Premise | Role in inquiry | What it does not supply |
|---|---|---|
| G0 | Understanding, actual living realization and further generation can supply positive reasons. | A total ranking of research programmes or a duty to learn everything. |
| J1 | Favor a genuine improvement without countervailing loss in the whole comparison. | Permission to omit delay, burdens or foregone possibilities. |
| J2 | Give the declared scoped priority to basic living conditions over optional enrichment, while preserving other basic claims. | An empirical identification of what is basic, or a ranking of every conflict among basic claims. |
| J3 | Differentiate bounded responsibilities through justified undertakings, attributable dependence, control and capacity. | Unlimited responsibility based merely on being intelligent or capable. |
| J4 | Select authorized, jointly feasible, undefeated plans and use justified procedures where comparison remains incomplete. | Automatic permission to impose research or to delay indefinitely. |
| E | Establish the actual uncertainty, inquiry effects, feasibility, permissions and affected conditions. | Its own truth merely because it is encoded in a formal model. |

A positive practical inference now follows at the level of the declared philosophical commitments. Where someone has a justified responsibility to respond, a material uncertainty prevents a warranted choice, and an available, proportionate investigation is needed to fulfill that responsibility without an overriding conflict, inquiry can become part of what that responsibility requires. The duty comes through the undertaking and practical conditions under J3/J4; G0 alone does not address every possible investigator with the same command.

Consider a shared arrangement sustained by several participants. One person may be positioned to obtain a needed observation; another may understand the historical record; others may know burdens that the technical description omitted. The whole perspective makes their contributions relationally intelligible. It does not give every participant identical access or make the most technically skilled person the sole authority over the consequences.

A participant can be required to obtain an appropriate check without being required to establish complete certainty. If a suitable response is already warranted across the relevant possibilities, further detail may belong to an optional or longer-term inquiry. If no authorized investigation can resolve the uncertainty in time, the original duty must be assessed among the actual alternatives; nonexistent knowledge cannot be demanded as though it were freely available.

The argument is conditional but substantive. It identifies why inquiry can be more than curiosity, why the obligation is bounded, and why its allocation depends on formed relationships. It does not pretend that a fulfilled curiosity, an imposed experiment and a necessary check are ethically interchangeable instances of “more generation.”

## 5. Choosing among inquiries without a hidden scalar

G0 and J1–J4 support a partial ordering and practical procedure, not an automatic research score. For each live alternative, describe the question, the possible result, how it could enter understanding or response, the resources and time required, the participants and affected positions, and what might be lost. Include timely action without the inquiry, staged investigation and division of work among the alternatives.

Three sufficiently different cases then emerge.

**A threatened basic condition and a decision-relevant check.** Under J2's explicit priority, a justified inquiry that can avert basic deprivation can have priority over optional refinement, provided the inquiry preserves the other basic claims and its purported protective role is supported. The result is not obtained merely by attaching “urgent” or “basic” to a favored project.

**Several defensible inquiries with crossed benefits and burdens.** The current commitments may not rank them. A bounded, legitimate allocation process can distribute opportunities, support plural work or make a timely provisional selection. Incomparability does not mean equality, indifference, or permission for unilateral imposition. Claims and losses left unmet remain visible.

**Exploration with uncertain future application.** Inquiry can be supported through present understanding, developed competence and a grounded opportunity to investigate unresolved relationships. No definite future product need be promised. But an evocative claim of infinite future potential does not automatically defeat concrete existing responsibilities. The earlier bounded-inquiry account already supplies part of this route; this round does not reopen it as if no guidance existed.

This account permits narrowing, redirecting or ending an investigation. It also permits maintaining a portfolio whose parts serve different questions and expose different failures. That permission must be supported by the actual capacity and comparative reasons, not inferred from diversity as an unconditional maxim.

## 6. Stopping is sometimes part of responsible inquiry

An inquiry can close for the current decision when the response is sufficiently grounded, when the remaining distinctions do not change that response, or when further investigation would defeat a more strongly supported requirement. Each closure should preserve what remains uncertain and what would reopen the matter. Closure for a task need not assert that the underlying scientific question has been solved.

There are also bad forms of stopping: treating a narrow candidate family as exhaustive, excluding affected testimony because it complicates the model, or declaring a deadline solely to avoid criticism. The account must distinguish these from genuine limits. Deadlines and costs require evidence; a model's parameters are not their own practical warrant.

Likewise, continuation can be irresponsible when “one more test” repeatedly postpones an already justified response or transfers all burdens to others. The appeal to scientific rigor then needs to explain what material uncertainty the additional test addresses and why its consequences are acceptable. This is not an argument against rigorous testing; it makes the purpose and effects of the testing explicit.

The checked deadline countercase illustrates a narrow part of this claim. Inquiry can retain an accurate report while losing even a response that would previously have met the stipulated requirement in every admitted case. It neither proves a general preference for immediate action nor licenses ignorance of available evidence whose use is actually required.

## 7. How inquiry can change its own ends

Some investigations disclose a loss that the original plan did not count, reveal a previously excluded participant, or develop a capacity with a significance that was not initially articulated. The question “which inquiry best serves the given objective?” can then become inadequate. A whole account must permit revision of the objective and the units of comparison.

This does not make retrospective endorsement sufficient. A newly formed preference can express understanding, habituation, coercion or resignation. The new evaluative articulation needs its own grounds, including the actual experiences and relationships it claims to describe. The [practical account of changed ends](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/PRACTICAL-JUDGMENT.md#6-目的变化学习与结束责任--changed-purposes-learning-and-discharge) also preserves existing justified reliance: changing a project does not automatically erase what is owed.

The non-self perspective is useful here. The investigator's purposes and competencies need not be treated as a completed private possession that merely selects among external information products. They can form through shared inquiry. Yet the formation of a purpose and its ethical warrant remain different questions about that same activity. Neither individual satisfaction nor collective agreement alone resolves the second.

## 8. Substantive counterreview

**Expected utility can already represent inquiry costs.** Correct. A sufficiently specified conventional decision model may reproduce the same recommendation. We do not claim explanatory superiority because a simpler comparator omitted those costs. Our contribution is to expose the supplied comparisons, access and histories, including cases where an acceptable scalar representation has not been established.

**Robust common choices can be excessively conservative.** Correct. The checked criterion concerns a guarantee across the supplied domain, not every reasonable decision under risk. A responsible risky action may lack such a guarantee. Its warrant then needs an explicit account of risk, comparative stakes and uncertainty; these cannot be smuggled into the theorem by dropping inconvenient histories.

**Decision relevance can undervalue curiosity.** The account explicitly includes understanding and capacity development as affirmative routes under G0. The narrower relevance criterion applies to a stated decision, not to all intellectual life. Immediate usefulness is not required for every warranted inquiry.

**J2's priority may be disputed.** It is a declared additional commitment, including its scoped priority over optional enrichment. The proposal does not prove that every G0-compatible ethics must accept it. If revised, inquiry priorities depending on it must be revised while the separate epistemic and mathematical results may remain.

**Institutions can manipulate uncertainty and urgency.** This is a real failure mode. The response needs accessible reasons, preserved dissenting evidence, warranted role allocation and opportunities to challenge the classification. The account has not yet supplied a complete theory of institutional authority merely by naming these requirements.

## 9. Integrated third-round result

The connected account now explains why inquiry can extend knowledge under qualified grounds, why reliance has ethical consequences, why some uncertainties need not be resolved for a particular response, and how G0 plus explicit practical commitments can support inquiry, allocate its burdens or require its closure. Eight newly checked theorems establish a limited interface; five primary comparisons constrain the philosophical argument. No new empirical replication or independent-agent acceptance is claimed.

This is progress on the governing connection from actual living relations through warranted recognition to situated reasons. It does not replace ethical justification with a policy-existence theorem or claim that competent action always requires accepting this entire philosophy.

**Next central connection:** how shared inquiry forms and corrects its field of relevant possibilities, and how differently situated participants acquire justified standing in that process. The present result supplies a criterion relative to a domain and requirements; their formation and warranted inclusion cannot be settled by that criterion alone. Existing learning, question-formation and developing-purpose mechanisms remain credited. The new demand is their epistemic and ethical adequacy under unequal access, rather than another restart of the mechanism sequence.
