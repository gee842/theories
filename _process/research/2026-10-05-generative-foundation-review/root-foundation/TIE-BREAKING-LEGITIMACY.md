# Who may break a tie: an argument, not a proof

6 October 2026. AI-authored philosophical note for alignment requirement 5
(plural agency and authority) of the
[completion contract](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ALIGNMENT-COMPLETION.md).
It is not Ren's wording, and nothing in it is proved unless a Lean name is
cited. It follows the [nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md):
the centers, the rule and whoever evaluates it are treated as parts of one
unfolding history, so no judging position outside that history is assumed.

## 1. What is proved

From [recorded claims](RECORDED-CLAIMS-INTEGRATION.md) and
[the plural-claims round](PLURAL-CLAIMS-INTEGRATION.md):

1. A deterministic rule that treats centers alike cannot serve a center whose
   record entry another shares (`tied_not_served`, `servable_iff_untied`).
2. At a full tie, a lottery that treats centers alike gives each tied center
   the same chance, possibly mixed with a chance of withholding
   (`full_tie_equal_weights`, `full_tie_open_uniform`).
3. Kept support can outlast the wait only when the organized form in which it
   persists has a self-renewing population (`renewing_holder_stays_live`). Otherwise there
   is an explicit deadline (`waiting_without_renewal_loses_support`).
4. Any record entry that distinguishes the centers lets an alike rule serve.
   Which entries are relevant is not settled; with unequal claims, either
   priority direction qualifies (`unequal_claims_leave_priority_open`).
5. Once any order exists, time records carry it forward and the least-recent
   rule takes turns (`renewing_claims_served_in_turn`).

So the open question has a precise form. At a tie, what may enter the record
to distinguish the centers, and who may put it there?

## 2. Candidate inputs

| Input | Treats centers alike? | Where it comes from | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equal-chance lottery | Yes, before the draw (result 2) | A symmetric procedure | Always available. The draw serves one center and not the other, so it is alike only before the draw |
| Agreement among the centers | Not required to be | The centers themselves | Formed inside the shared history by those whose claims are at stake |
| Recorded prior arrival or need | Yes, once recorded (result 4) | A further fact about the centers | Relevant only if that fact bears on the claims; relevance is not derived |
| Identity or training incidentals | No | Whoever built the selector | The [learned agents](LEARNED-CLAIM-AGENTS-INTEGRATION.md) did this. It contradicts result 1 at the tie and is neither recorded nor revisable by the centers |
| Withholding until a better input | Yes | The default | Harmless only before the deadline of result 3 |

## 3. Proposed criteria (argued, not derived)

An input that breaks a tie is more legitimate to the degree that:

1. **It is formed by those it concerns.** The nondual method excludes an
   external judging position. The centers' own agreement is the input formed
   most fully inside the history it settles, by the parties whose claims are
   at stake. It ranks first when it can be reached before the deadline.
2. **It is accessible and revisable.** The centers can see what decided the
   tie and contest it later. Recorded inputs satisfy this, while identity and
   training incidentals do not.
3. **It forecloses no more than the tie requires.** It must arrive before the
   kept support or the claims lapse (result 3). When no agreement or relevant
   recorded fact is available in time, an equal-chance lottery is the only
   input that treats centers alike and still serves (result 2). It is the
   proposed default.
4. **It is recorded.** Later rounds then do not reopen the same tie (result
   5).

These criteria rank agreement first, recorded relevant facts second and
equal chance as the default. Arbitrary identity is excluded. The ranking is a
proposal. It assumes that treating centers alike, and not foreclosing
needlessly, are owed. Those two requirements carry every theorem above, and
they are not themselves proved.

## 4. Where the naturalism round helps, and where it does not

The [ethical naturalism round](ETHICAL-NATURALISM.md) narrowed its own claims,
and only those narrowed claims are used here.

- "No prior bias toward any activity" supports treating centers alike as a
  constraint on the rule: no center is favored before something in the
  history distinguishes it. It does **not** imply protecting all activities.
  That is a separate normative premise, which that round left unproved.
- Its candidate property, whole-history non-loss, counts every exclusive
  commitment and every recovered dip as damage
  (`every_exclusive_choice_registers_as_damage`,
  `recovered_dip_still_condemned`). Any tie-break serves one center first,
  which is an exclusive commitment for that episode. So that property cannot
  rank tie-breaks, and its ordering is not imported here as a legitimacy
  result.

## 5. What remains open

- **Why alikeness and nonforeclosure are owed.** This is the ethical-grounding
  question the line started from. It is not answered here.
- **Relevance.** Which facts (need, arrival, contribution, vulnerability)
  properly distinguish centers. Results 4 and 5 show that any distinguishing
  record works formally, which is why relevance cannot be left to form alone.
- **Agreement under unequal power.** An agreement reached under coercion or
  manipulation does not meet criterion 1 in substance. Recognizing that
  requires the protection under changed power that requirement 4 lists, which
  is not built.
- **Recourse.** How a center contests a tie-break after the fact.
