# Authorship, protected decisions and a benefit within the chosen project

6 October 2026. AI-authored continuation of the [elective-concern argument](ELECTIVE-CONCERN-ARGUMENT.md). Named bridge: **the grounds of autonomy and project authority → a claimed exclusion of every prospective moral consideration supplied by a stranger's relief**. The proposed patient-to-action ground remains C2 of the [constitutive candidate](CONSTITUTIVE-SIGNIFICANCE-ARGUMENT.md). This note advances its defense without using the downstream rescue requirement as proof of its premise.

**Result:** preserving a person's chosen project does not always require screening out every beneficial effect available within it. A new comparison holds the project and its complete relevant costs fixed while varying whether its implementation also relieves an existing severe burden. The relief is a defensible prospective moral merit of one implementation, without a prior helping project. This narrows an authorship-based objection: the rival must defend jurisdiction over the moral significance of implementation choices, not merely protection against having one's purposes displaced. The conclusion is philosophical and defeasible, not a derivation from the causal root.

## 1. Five claims about authority must be kept distinct

The previous round accepted that choice can genuinely create project reasons and commitments. The next issue concerns what authority over a practical life protects:

| Claim | Its content |
|---|---|
| Control over conduct | The person has capacities or rights to decide and act; factual control and justified rights remain different |
| Permission to decline | Declining may be allowed even if assistance has something morally in its favor |
| Protected deliberation | A justified rule, decision or role may limit which considerations one should use, revisit or investigate |
| Extinguishing a reason | An independently valid change can terminate a particular ground, as authorized release can end a promissory claim |
| Constitutive absence | Without elective concern, the patient's benefit allegedly never supplied this actor any moral favor in the first place |

None of the first four automatically establishes the fifth. Nor are they unreal merely because the fifth is contested. The [source comparison](ASSISTANCE-JURISDICTION-SOURCES.md) assesses accounts of exclusion and practical authority; their actual grounds and scope must be preserved.

In particular, protecting a decision from renewed weighing need not mean that every excluded consideration ceased to exist. Exclusion could instead disqualify a consideration as a justification for this kind of choice while preserving its relevance elsewhere. That differs from both mere nonuse and universal absence. We must investigate the account offered rather than define all protections as simple weighting.

A valid release also prevents the opposite error. The patient's burden can remain while a particular promise-based reason really ends. “The patient is unchanged” therefore does not establish that every reason persists. The constitutive rival is nevertheless making an absence claim, not necessarily claiming a power to extinguish an already valid baseline. Our reply must reach that claim itself.

## 2. The comparison preserves the authored purpose

An actor has independently chosen a legitimate project and is already selecting its implementation. Two available routes achieve the same project outcome. They have the same relevant effects on the actor's time, resources, safety, continuing plans, expressive authorship and other responsibilities over the complete pertinent history. Both use otherwise permissible means, without an independent rights violation. Whether omitting relief is wrong remains open. The actor already knows these facts; no new surveillance, inquiry or interruption is required.

One route also brings about a permitted intervention that ends a stranger's severe, ongoing and still-unwanted experiential burden. The other leaves that independently caused burden unchanged. The actor did not cause it, undertake its care or induce dependence. Relevant patient willingness and intervention authority are independently established. No special procedure or promise governs the selection. These are transparent conditions of a conceptual case, not facts made true by calling the intervention easy.

The question is prospective: **before selecting a route or adopting relief as an end, does the beneficial route have a moral consideration in its favor because it would relieve that occurrence?** Our answer is yes. It realizes the already chosen project while answering an actual burden. The difference is an improvement for its subject, not a reward assigned by an observer or merely the actor's changed emotional state.

Equal project performance alone does not generate this reason. Two equally suitable implementations with no relevant patient difference could lack the proposed moral contrast. Here the positive ground is the independently identified relief. The comparison isolates that ground while preserving the purpose whose authorship supposedly requires its exclusion.

The actor's choice of means remains an exercise of authorship. We do not stipulate that its normative significance is zero. Nor does the test assume that all unchosen effects must enter every deliberation. It asks whether this already known, permitted and effective relief has any prospective moral favor in this one available comparison.

## 3. Awareness, intention and moral favor are different relations

The opportunity is known rather than a favorable accident discovered afterward. That establishes its place in the prospective comparison; it does not mean the actor has chosen to care. If the actor later selects the route because of relief, the helping intention is a possible response to the alleged reason. It cannot serve as evidence that an elective condition had already created that reason.

The actor could instead choose the same route for project-related reasons or by a permitted selection procedure. Whether that act deserves moral credit for compassion is a different question from whether the option had a moral merit. Our argument does not infer virtuous motivation from the good effect.

This retains the [earlier identification limitation](ELECTIVE-CONCERN-COUNTERREVIEW.md#4-a-stronger-comparison-than-observing-helpful-choices): if every intentional beneficial choice is used to diagnose prior elective concern, post-selection observation cannot discriminate the rival accounts. The normative assessment must precede selection, and the alleged elective power must have independent content.

The burden's source is also retained. We do not describe choosing the other route as creating the suffering in order to obtain an already admitted nonmaleficence reason. Relief of an independently occurring burden is the target. A theory claiming that this choice has become culpable imposition must justify that further classification separately.

## 4. What the authorship argument now has to establish

An account protecting the freedom to choose projects might oppose compulsory adoption of concern, burdensome monitoring, or repeated reopening of settled plans. But the stronger objection concerns the scope of moral answerability itself, even where continuing the project is allowed. Equal material and attentional costs do not answer that objection. They isolate it.

The stronger rival can nevertheless say that authorship includes jurisdiction over **all positive purposes expressed even in implementation**. On that account, an unchosen benefit can constrain neither the ranking of the means nor their moral assessment until the actor adopts it. That position is intelligible. It is also stronger than a protection of the project against displacement.

The candidate's direct reply distinguishes positive justification from demanding authority. Actual relief can make selecting this route morally justified for the patient's sake before concern is adopted. That supplies a practical role: the consideration can support this choice. It need not thereby authorize another to demand explanations, require monitoring of every future opportunity, or establish that refusal is wrong. Grounds for those further relations remain to be supplied.

This is a substantive ethical separation, not a definition that resolves the rival. Its support is that the benefit explains a worthy available response while authority to demand that response concerns additional relations and stakes. The rival must defend the further entailment: why would every prospective moral justification for benefitting someone already place the actor under the disputed accountability? Our position permits a response to have real moral merit without unlimited liability to demands. It does not establish how much requiring force relief ever acquires.

The patient-ground is also not the patient's directive. Whether a route provides relief does not depend on granting its beneficiary power to command the actor's project. Consent governs intervention into the patient's life; it is not a transferred authority over the actor's purposes. This applies the care/authority distinction inherited through [practical uptake](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-ARGUMENT.md), rather than discovering it anew. Responsiveness to stakes differs from subordination to another will; broader answerability remains contested.

Nor must external considerations remain valid justifications in every domain. A legitimate project or role might disqualify a consideration for this kind of choice, as distinct from prohibiting deliberation about an otherwise active reason. Its actual scope needs assessment. We have not stipulated that every justification of protection is absent; whether this project warrants excluding incidental relief is disputed.

The limited conclusion is that **protecting an authored purpose from displacement does not itself establish jurisdiction over all comparative moral merit within its implementation**. A principled protection against answerability survives the cost controls, but now faces the distinction between positively justified response and demanding authority. The [counterreview](ASSISTANCE-JURISDICTION-COUNTERREVIEW.md) preserves this stronger alternative.

## 5. Positive grounding without an ownership shortcut

The candidate's support is not the absence of an objection alone. The beneficial implementation answers an adverse occurrence while leaving the other identified grounds of choice intact. That is a positive explanation of why this route is morally worth considering before concern is elected. The same explanation identifies its correction condition: if it would not actually relieve the burden, its claimed patient-directed merit disappears. Its source is the effective relation to the occurrence, not adoption of a new identity as a helper.

The reasons protecting authorship can be taken equally seriously without inferring the candidate from simple symmetry. Actual direction of activity, relationships, attention and continuing possibilities can matter to their participants. That supports investigating protections of those goods. It does not automatically settle how another person's suffering addresses the actor, or prove that all goods have identical scope. Our argument assesses the two proposed grounds in the same concrete option comparison.

This also fits [Ren's reopened nondual question](NONDUAL-GROUNDING-QUESTION.md). “Actor,” “patient” and “project” here identify differentiated continuities, effects and capacities; they need not name independently existing owners confronting an external world. Their practical differences remain real in the comparison. Neither their relational formation nor the absence of a primitive owner proves C2. Conversely, claiming jurisdiction requires an account of what its formed relations protect; metaphysical ownership cannot serve as an unexplained reason to make every outside benefit morally absent.

## 6. Scope of the advance and possible revision

The new case supports C2 within a narrower class: known relief through an eligible implementation of a project the actor already chose. A reason need not wait for adoption of a distinct helping purpose. The case strengthens the positive prospective judgment and defeats an inference from protection against project displacement to total absence of assistance-related moral favor.

It does not refute every account of means-selection authority. It does not establish a duty to choose the beneficial route, a right to compel it, universal attention requirements, or a general verdict about costly assistance. The separate rescue and latitude arguments retain their additional premises and are not used as evidence for this ground.

An independently justified exclusion could disqualify the consideration for this choice, requiring revision of C2's application. If it merely prohibited reliance while preserving first-order favor, that existence claim could survive. But a warranted blanket exclusion barring every uncommitted actor from ever choosing for this consideration would leave the intended practical address unestablished; an inert label is insufficient. A justified account denying all prospective moral merit challenges C2 directly. Evidence of different authority, accumulated burden or expressive agency would instead revise the matched conditions. These are distinct outcomes.

The result is a new discriminating comparison and a defended local judgment. No simulation, human-subject experiment or formal theorem is claimed. The stronger task remains to explain why the actual experiential stake and eligible response constitute this moral relation in every qualifying case, alongside the real grounds of authorship and legitimate deliberative protection.
