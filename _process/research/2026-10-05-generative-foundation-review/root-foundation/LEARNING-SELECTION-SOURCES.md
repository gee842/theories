# Consequences reshape learning and selection: evidence and correspondence

9 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored source assessment for Ren's proposal connecting learning, organizations and simultaneous selection through systems and subsystems. This is a bounded evidence contribution to the existing cognition and recursive-selection studies, not a new independent theorem or an empirical rerun. It follows the [nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md).

**Begin with the continuing activity.** Consequences alter the relationships through which further activity occurs. Some changes alter current response; others persist in organization, alter how later experience changes that organization, or affect which organizations are reconstructed. Learners, evaluators and reproducing collectives differentiate within these histories. They need not be independent objects joined by an external selector.

**Results reused:** `historical-source-binding`, `retrospective-source-binding`, `encounter-selection-and-revision`, `organizational-rule-inheritance`, and `joint-practice-forms-continuation`; the unmigrated [recursive-selection analysis](RECURSIVE-GENERATIVE-SELECTION.md), [reconstruction account](EVOLUTIONARY-ORGANIZATION-INTEGRATION.md), and [matched lineage reanalysis](LINEAGE-CONTINUATION-INTEGRATION.md) retain their own proof and evidence scope. Search covered canonical aliases and existing study/theory sources before adding this note.

**Exact delta:** distinguish three actual consequence-to-change mechanisms and assess primary evidence for their higher-order operation. This closes an explanatory ambiguity in the analogy: a common causal organization does not require identical update algorithms. It also identifies what evidence connects success at one scale to changes in its constituents.

## 1. Artificial learning: changing the response structure and the update rule

### Backpropagation

Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams (1986), *Learning representations by back-propagating errors*, Nature 323:533–536. [Primary publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1038/323533a0); [author-hosted article](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~hinton/absps/naturebp.pdf).

**Reading scope:** publisher abstract, plus the author's publication listing identifying the original article. The author-hosted PDF was retrieved through the browser, but its text extraction yielded copyright notices; no full-paper reading or figure verification is claimed.

The authors report adjusting network connections against output error, with useful hidden representations developing during training. For this correspondence, performance feedback can alter internal organization even when no population of complete networks differentially reproduces. A trained connection can later participate in a new forward computation. The abstract supports acquired representation and subsequent task operation; it does not establish biological backpropagation, autonomous objective formation, universal generalization or indefinite retention.

### Learning the update procedure

Andrychowicz et al. (2016), *Learning to learn by gradient descent by gradient descent*, NeurIPS 2016. [Primary versioned paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04474v2); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.04474v2).

**Reading scope:** abstract, §§1.1–2.1, reported setup and comparisons in §§3.1–3.4, and conclusion, through searchable PDF text. Appendices, code and numerical replication were not audited.

An LSTM receives gradients and produces parameter updates; its own parameters are trained using loss along optimization trajectories. Retaining the trained optimizer allows reuse on new problems. Reported transfer includes changed network sizes; replacing sigmoid activations with ReLU defeats one transfer. The outer training distribution, loss, horizon and architecture remain supplied. The implemented meta-gradient omits some paths that would require second derivatives, and intermediate losses replace an exclusively terminal training objective. These choices matter for delayed consequences. This is direct computational evidence for a learned way of learning, with a concrete transfer boundary; it is not evidence that its highest-level criterion was learned or that every selector improves under recursion.

**Our correspondence:** backpropagation transports information about a specified computational dependence. The optimizer converts that information into a change. Retained parameters then affect later computation. Learning the optimizer adds a further actual dependence of later updating on earlier outcomes. Calling this “selection” is acceptable in the broad sense of differential stabilization or modification, but Darwinian replication has not thereby been demonstrated. A model can be learned without the updater itself containing a model of the entire world.

## 2. Organizations: translating evaluation into consequences

### An observed compensation change

Lazear (2000), *Performance Pay and Productivity*, American Economic Review 90(5):1346–1361. [Primary publisher page](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.90.5.1346).

**Reading scope:** publisher abstract only; the linked publisher PDF did not supply accessible article text. An earlier NBER version has different reported estimates and is not substituted for the published paper.

The published abstract reports a 44% increase in output per worker following Safelite's shift from hourly pay to piece rates, with changes in workforce composition also investigated. This provides a concrete organizational correspondence: an operative evaluation-to-compensation rule can accompany changed work and recruitment. At this reading scope, it does not identify a randomized causal effect, isolate effort from learning or sorting, establish quality-adjusted social benefit, or demonstrate evolutionary selection of the compensation rule itself. The figure is the author's reported result, not our reanalysis.

### Meritocratic description does not establish faithful consequence assignment

Castilla and Benard (2010), *The Paradox of Meritocracy in Organizations*, Administrative Science Quarterly 55:543–576. [Institution-hosted published article](https://ideas.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Castilla-Benard-2010.pdf).

**Reading scope:** abstract; study 1 procedure, profile construction and results; study 2 design/results excerpts; study 3 procedure/results; discussion of mechanisms and scope conditions. No raw data or independent replication was inspected.

Across three exercises, 445 participants considered fictional employee profiles. The target profiles had equal numeric performance scores and counterbalanced qualitative descriptions. Organizational-value framing affected the translation into recommended bonuses. The first two studies support the reported framing interaction; study 3 retained a male advantage within the meritocratic condition, but its gender-by-framing interaction was not significant (`p = .161`). The third study cannot be counted as another independently significant treatment contrast. These are recommendation exercises, not observed promotions, employee learning or organizational survival. The authors did not identify the underlying psychological mechanism experimentally. The evidence therefore constrains the assumption that declared merit criteria accurately connect contribution to consequences; it does not show that every merit-based practice fails.

**Our correspondence:** in shared work, detecting a contribution, interpreting it, assigning recognition or authority, supplying opportunities, retaining an effective practice and enabling successors to enact it are distinguishable relationships. A recorded performance judgment does not itself perform these later operations. Conversely, those operations can be organized together, and the existing [organizational inheritance study](EVOLUTIONARY-ORGANIZATION-INTEGRATION.md#organizational-rules-become-inheritable) already supplies a checked procedural connection. The [joint-practice continuation](EVOLUTIONARY-ORGANIZATION-INTEGRATION.md#joint-practice-forms-further-participation) adds actual participation formation; it should not be replaced with a story of ranking preformed independent persons.

The relevant candidates include revisable practices, cooperative arrangements and capacities to evaluate, not only selection among people. A meritocratic arrangement is one possible institutional implementation. It needs evidence that its operative judgments track the relevant contribution and that their consequences support subsequent activity. Inherited labels, credentials or formal rules alone do not establish this. The existing [systems reading](../../../../sources/evolutionary-selection/organizational-inheritance/SYSTEMS-READING.md) also credits delayed work/capability feedback and organizational memory literature. Those earlier readings retain their stated limits; combining different studies does not create one observed complete history.

## 3. Biological selection: collective continuation changes constituent transmission

Doulcier, Remigi, Rexin and Rainey (2025), *Evolutionary dynamics of nascent multicellular lineages*, Proceedings of the Royal Society B 292:20241195. [Primary DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2024.1195); [preserved primary XML](../../../../sources/evolutionary-selection/doulcier-2025/article.xml); [original-byte manifest](../../../../sources/evolutionary-selection/doulcier-2025/manifest.json).

**Reading scope in this assessment:** XML sections “Experimental evolution of collectives,” “Propagating mutational data to the full genealogy,” and “Experimental confirmation of adaptive changes”; existing repository provenance and lineage-analysis scope. Earlier reads and computations remain credited separately.

Cultures underwent a two-phase collective life cycle; failed lineages were replaced from surviving cultures under a supplied lottery. The authors identified an evolved `mutL` variant and performed genetic reversion to test its role in switching and cycle completion. The existing matched reanalysis records both phases completed by 42/72 evolved-strain cultures versus 7/72 revertant cultures. This connects inherited change, its expression and realized continuation. Shared ancestry alone is insufficient for the causal claim; the intervention adds evidence. Laboratory transfer, medium, timing and replacement remain actual enabling practices. The study does not show that the laboratory selector evolved or that collective success necessarily favors every constituent variant.

**Our correspondence:** the collective's realized continuation changes which developmental organization is transmitted. A constituent need not independently sense or receive a reward. Its future prevalence can change through the organization it helps realize. Existing [recursive-selection evidence](RECURSIVE-GENERATIVE-SELECTION-SOURCES.md) additionally covers the opposing within-group and between-group changes in Chuang et al.'s microbial experiment. These are positive mechanisms for Ren's cross-scale claim. They concern actual association, reconstruction and sampling; they cannot be inferred from a diagram of nested boxes.

## 4. The common explanation and its discriminating requirements

The synthesis below is our interpretation of the proposal and existing results, not a quotation from Ren or a claim that one paper proves it.

| Relation to explain | Required connection | A failure that distinguishes explanations |
|---|---|---|
| Formation | Earlier activity changes organization capable of later differentiated response | Outcome scores change while the proposed operative structure does not |
| Retention | The relevant organization persists or is reconstructed across the interval | The label or archive survives but the capacity cannot be enacted |
| Use | Retained organization makes a difference in the later activity | Retention is intact but task access, coordination or execution fails |
| Assignment of consequences | Outcomes affect changes to the relationships that produced them | A proxy changes while important delayed consequences are omitted |
| Higher-order adaptation | Outcomes alter the actual sampling, updating, evaluation or reconstruction procedure | A rule is renamed or endorsed without changing later operation |
| Cross-scale selection | Collective continuation changes constituent transmission or opportunities under the actual association | Mixing, bypass or timing removes the effect on the relevant constituents |
| Selection of selectors | Alternative consequence-assignment procedures are formed, differentially continued and reconstructed | A designer changes a fixed protocol without any differential continuation of protocols |

Formation is not exhausted by filtering pre-existing alternatives: developmental construction and feedback-directed modification can produce alternatives. Nor does every persistent arrangement perform cognition. To explain cognitive modeling, the retained distinctions must be connected to what is represented, their conditions of error, and their actual role in prediction, inference or action. The [cognition account](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/COGNITION-LEARNING-AND-EVOLUTION.md) and the reused binding results address restricted versions of that content-to-use connection. Selection explains how a capacity becomes common only after its relevant formation, expression and transmission are identified.

“Reward” can name several relationships that should be made explicit in each realization: a recorded signal, a criterion used for evaluation, an actual change in further opportunities, or differential reproduction. These can be connected without being identical or reducible to one scalar. A numerical loss need not be pleasure; biological continuation need not involve a representation of fitness; an institutional judgment need not accurately assess shared generativity. The connection concerns what feedback actually does.

The evidence supports the following affirmative statement: **consequences can form and stabilize capacities, and can also reshape the relationships that generate variation, assign consequences and reconstruct capacities. Selection across interdependent scales can favor constituent organization through what it enables collectively.** This gives a substantive causal architecture for formation, retention and use. It does not require an additional chooser outside the unfolding or an endless hierarchy of conscious evaluators.

Long-run generative alignment requires the further relationship identified in the [existing counterreview](RECURSIVE-GENERATIVE-SELECTION-REVIEW.md): consequences for renewed conditions must continue to affect the organization responsible and its successors. Delay, migration, support from elsewhere, lost association, missing alternatives and irreversible losses can interrupt that relationship. These are candidate mechanisms to investigate, not assumptions that every local success is destructive. Favorable cases and adverse cases must use independently stated measures of renewal; defining whatever persists as generative would erase the proposed explanation's test.

No new mathematical proof, empirical reanalysis, full literature survey, universal evolutionary convergence, complete cognitive explanation or unique superiority over conventional learning/evolutionary/institutional accounts is asserted.
