# Affective realization: target, constitution and authority

6 October 2026. AI-authored independent review of the [affective realization candidate](AFFECTIVE-REALIZATION-ARGUMENT.md), under the [shared nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). Target: explain an episode's felt valence through its generated organization before a narrative “I.” This review tests that explanatory transition; it neither assumes a separate fundamental subject nor establishes ethical necessity.

## Primary rival: source synopsis

[Martínez, “Imperative Transparency”](https://academic.oup.com/mind/article/131/522/585/6387153), *Mind* 131(522), 585–601 (2022), defends body-directed pain imperativism against reflexive imperativism. He argues that introspection can disclose an imperative concerning bodily conditions, while separate coping processes can explain immediate experience-directed avoidance. Thus analgesic seeking need not reveal pain's constitutive target. He challenges the proposed biological grounding of reflexive content, while accepting its abstract possibility. His conclusion is comparative, explicitly not decisive. The paper concerns pain, leaving other affective cases aside. Publisher HTML §§1–5 and substantive notes were read; cited empirical originals were not independently reviewed.

## Our analysis: preserve the proposed relationship

In Barlassina and Hayward's [candidate](AFFECTIVE-REALIZATION-ARGUMENT.md), “me” identifies the experience containing the command, not a narrative owner. The constructive question is whether generated activity binds a directive into an episode and makes that episode its target. Organismic responsiveness, bodily representation and an eventual autobiographical self can differentiate within the same history. Neither body-directed nor experience-directed content, by itself, requires the latter self.

We should distinguish two questions: **what is targeted**, and **whether the directive constitutes the affective episode**. A separate urge to end an experience does not establish that the experience is unpleasant; conversely, a command integrated into an episode could concern a bodily condition. Neither conceptual independence licenses freely changing the actual relationship while claiming to preserve the whole phenomenon.

The rival is substantive because it preserves felt unpleasantness, body representation and relief behavior while proposing another organization of their connection. A successful reflexive account must explain why the target belongs to the episode's own constitution. A successful rival must identify the separate process rather than redescribe every troublesome response as extrinsic. Neither side wins by assigning names to unmeasured states.

## Our proposed discrimination

Consider a continuing painful episode with separately available ways to alter its bodily source and its felt occurrence. Choosing analgesia is compatible with several organizations. The decisive inquiry concerns the proximal directive and its binding, not just the eventual choice. A useful investigation would independently identify bodily representations, affective changes, coping activity, learned expectations and available actions, then test how their relations change. This is a proposed study requirement, not a report of an available selective intervention.

Three findings would change the account:

1. **Separate dependence:** independently identified disruption of coping removes manifest experience-directed motivation while the relevant unpleasantness and bodily directive remain. This would support investigating a separate source, but would challenge intrinsic reflexivity only with independent evidence against a retained intrinsic disposition. Appropriate conditions for its expression must be warranted: disrupted uptake, unavailable action or overriding motivation can conceal a continuing disposition. Neither absent action nor absent manifest motive alone supplies the finding.
2. **Constitutive dependence:** the episode's distinctive aversiveness and its experience-directed motive change together through an identified binding mechanism, while bodily content, separate coping and general response capacity remain. This would support the proposed connection. Covariation alone would not identify content or exclude a common cause.
3. **Different target:** intervention identifies a constitutive directive tracking bodily correction despite available experience attenuation. This alone permits an additional reflexive constituent. To narrow the claim that all unpleasant pain is reflexive, evidence must establish that the identified bodily direction exhausts the relevant affective constitution, or independently exclude the proposed coexisting reflexive component. Such a finding would concern these episodes; it need not rule out reflexive valence elsewhere.

These tests must preserve the relation between undergoing, appraisal and report identified in [the existing measurement review](RELIEF-APPRAISAL-EVIDENCE.md). A changed rating may reflect changed experience, changed reporting or both. Pure-affect and asymbolia cases can constrain decomposition, but cannot determine a command's target merely by subtraction. Barlassina and Hayward's [2021 corrigendum](https://academic.oup.com/mind/article/130/518/731/6154421) corrects their quoted clinical passage; the uncorrected quotation is not evidence here.

Phenomenological description can guide the investigation without deciding in advance that introspection must present either an external body or a self-targeting experience. Likewise, an evolutionary benefit to the body does not by itself decide between a selected proximal control function and its further consequence. Both proposed functions need independently discriminating histories and present organization. This criticism does not supply the missing reflexive realization.

## Our assessment of ethical and formal reach

A command's semantic satisfaction conditions, its causal uptake and its justified authority are different relations within the history. Establishing that an episode directs its own reduction would explain a candidate form of aversiveness. It would not yet establish that every such directive is prudent to satisfy, that its satisfaction benefits the undergoing life, or that it supplies an unrelated responder a moral reason. Conversely, failure of reflexive imperativism would not show that suffering lacks adverse significance. The account's proposed identity and normative consequences require their own substantive defense.

The current [PatientConcern model](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/noninstrumental-concern/PatientConcern.lean) contains a numerical burden and episode identifier, with supplied faithful reads and action effects. Its episode field identifies the target of a service operation; it does not represent phenomenal binding or imperative content. [GroundingContract](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/noninstrumental-concern/GroundingContract.lean) explicitly supplies realization and normative bridges. No new theorem from those definitions can adjudicate this rival. Adding “reflexive,” “bound” and “unpleasant” predicates plus their desired implication would restate the proposed ontology conditionally.

The next useful formal interface must preserve an independently defended episode boundary, constituent relationship, directive target and intervention history. A later theorem could verify that a specified mechanism maintains or changes those relations. Their interpretation as felt valence would still require the realization argument. This is an omitted relationship in the present representation and an unresolved constitutive explanation—not a counterexample obtained by deleting an ethical predicate. No Lean code or axiom is added by this review.


## Comparative direction, pending aims and present adversity

6 October 2026 continuation. **Our conditional test**, prompted by the telic-perfectionist candidate, grants the proposed constitutive experience-directed relation for examination. It does not change an episode's phenomenal condition to manufacture a counterexample. [Barlassina's 2020 clarification](https://www.lucabarlassina.org/uploads/7/7/1/0/77107295/beyond_gb.pdf), §3.2, printed p.276, targets an experience type and specifies comparative more/less. A complete reading of Kauppinen's chapter and Chow's reply was unavailable to this reviewer; the following is not attributed to either author's argument.

A direction, an endpoint and a time of fulfillment are different features of a goal. An intrinsic direction toward less experience might concern immediate cessation, a reduction across an interval, or an ordering of possible continuations. These are candidate semantic accounts requiring evidence about the actual organization, not alternative labels an analyst may freely assign. A comparative direction alone does not identify an absolute zero endpoint or establish that it is already due.

Consider genuine, independently permitted relief that reduces an ongoing severe, still-unwanted episode before eventually ending it. The same present painfulness can be examined under the disputed explanation without denying its adversity. Four relations must remain distinguishable: responding as directed, actually reducing the experience, reaching an endpoint, and missing a warranted condition of fulfillment. An effective response can begin before achieving its aim. Equally, a current goal can remain pending without having failed. Compliance is not satisfaction; continued occurrence is not, without its temporal contract, demonstrated frustration.

This yields a discriminating requirement. If independent identification establishes immediate cessation as the constitutive aim, persistence does fail that condition, even during effective partial relief. If it instead establishes reduction across the relevant interval, actual reduction may satisfy that condition while unpleasantness remains. In the latter case, an explanation identifying all current painfulness with frustration of that very aim needs revision. This is conditional on identifying the real target and interval; neither the availability of relief nor our preferred welfare verdict establishes them.

The positive comparison is also constrained. Partial relief can improve conformity with a direction without eliminating every remaining burden. Ending the episode can differ from disabling the capacity for experience or destroying the undergoing continuity. These actual transformations need separate assessment; goal cessation alone cannot certify a benefit. Likewise, continuing pleasure might satisfy a continuation-directed aim without increasing intensity. A plateau is therefore not automatically a counterexample, and an unavailable additional pleasure need not make the current pleasure intrinsically bad. The proposed common explanation must defend the relevant asymmetry and timescale.

This extends the earlier review's safeguards: an unmanifested disposition may remain, and bodily and reflexive constituents may coexist. The new issue is whether the identified experiential aim actually has the success conditions used to explain welfare. Establishing those conditions would still leave the proposed identification of their fulfillment or frustration with local flourishing to defend. No motive becomes authority merely by being intrinsic, and no independent responder's reason follows automatically. The result is a sharper test of the constitutive bridge, not a complete refutation, proof of valence-free suffering, or new Lean axiom.
