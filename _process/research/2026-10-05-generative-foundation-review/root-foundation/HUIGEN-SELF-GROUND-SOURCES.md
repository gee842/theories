# Is being my future self part of the reason, or how I encounter it?

6 October 2026. AI-authored selective primary-source investigation for [the entire-root necessity programme](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/HUIGEN.md). The named transition is **a warranted reason concerning my own future experience → a reason concerning a different subject**, including a subject unable to deliberate, communicate or reciprocate. This continues the [practical-necessity sources](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-SOURCES.md) and [ground-sufficiency audit](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md#7-the-most-promising-grounding-route-and-its-exact-test).

**Result:** future concern can defeat an explanation that makes present feeling or present desire necessary for every reason. It does not alone defeat a relation-based explanation of the reason. The strongest positive bridge requires practical justification to survive a change of standpoint, with a further argument that the patient-grounded reason survives changing the deliberator. The promising next test separates concern for a suffering subject from preserving an undertaking or exercising authority over it.

## 2. Our reconstruction: four roles that “mine” may play

The following distinctions and tests are this study's reconstruction, not a joint doctrine attributed to the two authors. Existing [source genealogy](HUIGEN-NECESSITY-ARGUMENTS.md) and the [two required transports](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#3-the-noncommunicating-patient-exposes-two-distinct-transport-tasks) remain in force.

| Possible role of a self-relation | What changing it could change | What does not follow merely from that change |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence route | Reliability, immediacy or identification of a report | Whether an independently identified adverse experience occurs |
| Motivational route | Anticipation, salience and disposition to act | Whether a reason exists despite indifference |
| Practical relation | Responsibility for a continuing undertaking or authority to decide within it | That people outside the undertaking have no baseline standing |
| Constitutive reason-ground | Which condition actually supplies this reason to this actor | That its relevance is established merely by calling it identity |

An argument must identify which role it is changing. Removing present pain changes access and motivation; it need not remove an enduring practical relation. Removing a deep metaphysical substance need not remove agency, promises, dependence or responsibility. Conversely, establishing such relations does not prove that they exhaust the grounds of reasons concerning suffering.

Let \(R(a,t;p,u,h)\) mean that actor \(a\), deliberating at time \(t\), has a pro tanto reason concerning adverse episode \(h\) undergone by patient \(p\) at time \(u\). Let \(C((a,t),(p,u))\) name an independently specified continuing-agency relation; it is not defined as whatever generates the desired reasons.

An acknowledged future-directed case is

\[
R(a,t;a,u,h),\qquad u>t.
\]

The same example can fit either of two candidate explanations:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Experience ground: }&\quad \text{the adverse episode and the actor's relevant opportunity supply the reason};\\
\text{Restricted ground: }&\quad \text{those features together with }C((a,t),(a,u))\text{ supply it}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both reject the claim that only currently felt pain matters. Only the first, once its scope is justified, can transfer directly to a patient outside \(C\). The second still owes an explanation of why the relation is needed for baseline standing; its mere logical availability does not discharge that burden.

## 3. The strongest positive inference and where its force comes from

Start with a future pain that I cannot presently feel, that I know will occur unless I take a feasible preventive step, and that has no countervailing benefit. Suppose its prevention supplies a genuine reason now even when I lack the desire to prevent it. This is the local anchor being investigated, not an empirical assertion that everybody accepts it.

The first positive result is precise: **neither present feeling nor a present desire for relief is a necessary condition of this anchored reason**. The reason can concern an experience encountered through a warranted representation. That matters for the generative theory: an acquired model may make a reason available before the adverse event occurs. The resulting motivation and effective response still require their own formation mechanisms.

The second proposed step is stronger: a faithful redescription of a practical judgment should preserve its justification, not merely its grammar. If I recognize that the future subject is suffering and concede that this is what makes prevention justified, changing from an anticipatory description to an objective account must not silently replace the original reason with a causally similar but normatively empty record.

This is a real constraint on an account claiming to preserve practical significance. But two transformations must be separated:

1. Describe the same actor, patient and relation from a different evidential or linguistic standpoint.
2. Replace the actor or patient so that an admitted practical relation no longer holds.

A theory can preserve the first while disputing the second. If an objectively formulated judgment includes the proposition that a particular agent is responsible for a particular undertaking, its content remains relational. An account of that judgment does not have to become an instruction addressed to every observer. The positive derivation must therefore explain why the reason against this adverse episode belongs to the experiential ground itself, or why every adequate practical standpoint must acknowledge a corresponding reason beyond the original relation.

This is also where a reason can survive without being decisive. A special relationship may supply additional reasons without being a condition of all reasons. The target is to rule out **zero baseline concern**, not to prove identical total duties for all actors.

## 4. A sharper test: preserve the patient while ending the undertaking

The usual future-self comparison leaves experience, continuity, ongoing agency and personal investment bundled together. The following conceptual tests separate them. They are not reports of experiments, and each stipulated capacity would need empirical identification in an application.

**A. Terminal experience.** All of the actor's ongoing undertakings have ended. A later episode of severe suffering remains, after which there will be no further deliberation, memory, learning or productive action. Preventing the episode is feasible and leaves all other admitted goods unchanged. Does the actor still have a reason now to prevent it?

If yes, the reason is not exhausted by maintaining later agency, improving a project or protecting future learning. This does not yet show that the identity relation is irrelevant. It narrows the proposed ground to something that can survive the disappearance of those functional benefits.

**B. Anticipated loss of deliberative capacity.** The later patient continues to have the relevant adverse experience but cannot formulate reasons or continue a plan. If the reason persists, deliberative capacity at the time of suffering is unnecessary for that case. A theory must then explain why an otherwise comparable subject who never possessed that capacity would be excluded, rather than taking the exclusion for granted.

The rival may cite the patient's history or the actor's earlier responsibility. That reply is substantive and must be assessed; it is not a contradiction. The test's gain is that a capacity-based explanation cannot silently become a history-based one while claiming the original proof is unchanged.

**C. Remove a relation that affects responsibility, while retaining the experience.** Compare prevention for an authorized successor with prevention for an unrelated patient, keeping the adverse condition, evidence and feasible intervention fixed. Authority over a project may legitimately differ. The question is whether that difference removes every reason against suffering or only changes responsibility, permission and priority.

These tests force a proposed self-ground to explain its distinctive work. They permit three findings: the relation is required for this reason; it supplies an additional reason while baseline concern survives; or it affects only evidence and motivation. Selecting among them requires substantive justification. A verbal substitution of “mine” with “a case of mine” decides none of them.

## 5. Continuity, copying and shared agency: useful pressure without fusion of subjects

The [companion self-ground audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md) examines transformations of identity and agency directly. For source-guided integration, three restrictions are essential.

First, a branching process can challenge a simple one-to-one identity rule without showing that every branch is one subject. Concern can extend to several successors while their experiences remain distinct. If a proposed reason survives branching, its ground must be stated in terms compatible with that survival.

Second, common origin and similar organization do not by themselves establish a shared decision-making authority. A continuing undertaking, an authorized delegation, a copying relation and ordinary causal dependence are different relations. A process with a common ancestor may develop independent centers with incompatible commitments. Replacing their distinctions by one global self would defeat the theory's obligation to preserve differentiated centers.

Third, authority to undertake a burden cannot be read directly from the existence of a benefit. A reason for a patient to accept an intervention, a representative's authority to decide, and another actor's baseline reason to prevent suffering require separate justification. Noncommunicating patients make this separation unavoidable: consent may be unavailable, while evidence of a good or harm remains possible. The account needs defensible representation and intervention rules; it cannot make inability to authorize an action erase the patient's significance.

The strongest possible outcome is therefore plural: a patient-grounded baseline with additional relations that allocate responsibility and restrict permissible action. That structure would explain both why others matter and why becoming involved with them does not confer unlimited authority over them.

## 6. What this supplies to the generative root, and what remains supplied

This study adds a discriminating argument interface to the [generative grounding sequence](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md#accepted-work-sequence). Formed prediction and correction can establish an evidential route to future or other-centered consequences. The terminal-experience tests then ask whether operational continuation is the whole source of their significance. The practical-redescription test asks whether a model preserves the content of the reason it purports to represent. The remaining actor-transfer test asks why its authority exceeds the original self-relation.

The current reconstruction supplies, rather than derives, the existence of adverse experience, a warranted local reason anchor, reliable identification in the stipulated cases and a feasible preventive action. Its strongest bridge additionally requires a justified account of practical congruency or patient-ground sufficiency. None follows merely from statistical prediction, organismic persistence or an analyst's preferred objective. Establishing sentience and establishing moral authority are separate burdens; an uncertain evidence route does not prove the absence of an actual patient-grounded reason.

**What would change the account:** a successful argument that the self-relation performs no constitutive work in the isolated reason against suffering would license a wider transport; a justified relation that remains indispensable would require the theory to explain baseline concern through another root; evidence that the proposed experience or anchor is misidentified would require revising that starting point. None of these outcomes licenses silently abandoning the entire-root objective.

The next constructive question is exact: **can a reason against an avoidable adverse episode remain valid after agency, reciprocity and project continuation are removed, and can its remaining ground be shown to address another capable actor without assuming that conclusion?** The primary arguments make this a serious foundational target. This note does not present source agreement, conceptual pressure or a conditional transport as its completed proof.

## 7. What practical congruency must answer after the direct challenge

This follow-up advances the same connection: **warranted local reasons and scientific competence → a reason for an unrelated capable actor**. It adds one primary work to test the earlier bridge. The argument below is our reconstruction of the competing proof obligations, not a claim that the cited essay contains our formal model.

### Deliberative availability exceeds an observed motivational state

Separate three assertions:

1. The actor is not motivated by the patient's harm now.
2. The actor would not acquire a relevant motive through the particular corrections and reflections examined so far.
3. No adequate deliberative route from the actor's actual evaluative commitments could yield that motive.

The first does not imply the second; neither implies the third. A model can close a declared set of transitions and prove that it lacks a path to concern. It must still justify treating those transitions as all relevant forms of sound deliberation. Otherwise it establishes an implementation limit or a conditional logical model, not the impossibility of a rational extension.

This gives positive counterpressure against the exclusive self-ground. An actor may already reject arbitrary exclusions, care about faithful practical understanding, or accept a reason whose implications for another patient they have not understood. Correcting a false empirical belief or exposing a contradiction within those commitments can make a previously unrecognized reason available. These possible routes should be sought and tested. Their existence in some agents does not yet make them necessary in every scientifically competent philosophy.

The same point protects the foundation's generative ambition: learning can change what a system has grounds to pursue. A theory should not freeze its initial motivational repertoire and then declare its chosen boundary necessary. But the production of a new motive is not by itself the justification of that motive. Formation, correction and warrant still need to be connected.

### The narrow case does not supply its own normative solution

Use a comparison in which both options have equal finite implementation cost, reliable evidence is available, and prevention removes a severe adverse episode for an unrelated patient without changing any other admitted relevant consequence. Feasibility and equal cost eliminate certain excuses for inaction; they do not themselves turn the patient's loss into this actor's reason. Equally, an actor's indifference does not cancel a reason if the ground independently establishes one.

The positive account must show what consideration makes the difference reason-giving for this actor. The restrictive account must show why its proposed relation exhausts the available grounds. It cannot secure that result merely by assuming a motivational closure that the investigation is meant to explain. A stipulation of sadistic gratification would change the controlled comparison by adding a competing motive; its validity would be a further question, not a reason to obscure the case being tested.

### Scientific competence, practical responsiveness and categorical authority

Accurate inquiry can require correcting a false prediction without requiring that a particular outcome be promoted. Even a commitment to represent another's standpoint faithfully can, at the level of semantic content, preserve the fact that a reason is addressed to that patient. The earlier distinction between redescription and actor transport therefore survives this challenge.

A stronger scientific philosophy may claim that its practical judgments are rationally warranted, open to criticism and adequate to every relevant kind of reason. Such a claim cannot reject an inconvenient normative argument merely because it lacks current motivational support. It must examine the argument's premises, applicability and inference. But an obligation to assess a proposed reason is different from a demonstrated obligation to accept it. Neither universal concern nor exclusive internalism may enter as the unexamined definition of adequate assessment.

The disputed motivational premise can be expressed without conflating actual response with reason validity. A reason may be capable of explaining action under suitable recognition and uptake. A stronger requirement says that its uptake must be reachable by sound deliberation from this actor's antecedent motivational set. The second claim is not simply a restatement of the first: it restricts which transformations qualify and where they must begin. An account adopting it owes an argument for that restriction. An account rejecting it owes an account of why the new judgment is warranted, beyond the fact that a transformation caused assent.

This leaves three constructive routes, with distinct success conditions:

| Route | What would establish progress | What remains unproved by that result alone |
|---|---|---|
| Correct an existing practical commitment | A justified route from this actor's actual commitments to the patient's claim | That every adequate inquirer necessarily has those starting commitments |
| Establish a constitutive practical requirement | A specific failure in the purportedly adequate deliberator's own practical claim, independent of assuming shared concern | That rejecting the claim makes every scientific result false |
| Defend the patient-ground directly | Warrant for the reason whose force does not depend on the actor's current endorsement, with legitimate defeaters addressed | That all coherent rivals are inconsistent, or that recognition ensures action |

These are compatible research routes. The first can explain how concern becomes accessible and develops; the second targets the strong necessity claim; the third can provide the substantive reason that the other routes recognize. A grounded moral conclusion need not wait for a causal theory of perfect motivation, and a causal theory of persuasion cannot replace the grounding argument.

**Current adjudication:** scientific and philosophical competence alone has not yet been shown to force the stronger practical-congruency principle. A principled divergent reconstruction remains a serious candidate, subject to its own grounding and deliberative-closure burdens. Its availability is not a finding of equal moral warrant. The next decisive result must identify a sound route or an unavoidable practical commitment that reaches the unrelated patient without building that patient's actor-addressed authority into the starting definition.

## 8. Can reflective acquisition justify the impersonal ground?

This round advances **accurate encounter with another's adverse experience → warranted acquisition of an actor-addressed reason**. The new primary reading sharpens a constructive route already under investigation. The following is our reconstruction, not a claim that Sidgwick proves the generative theory.

### Separate recognition, scope and practical authority

Keep three achievements distinct. First, an inquirer identifies a condition as adverse for its actual subject. Second, the inquirer judges that its significance survives inclusion in an assessment encompassing other subjects. Third, the inquirer has a reason to respond through a specified permissible action. A vivid simulation may improve the first; even an impartial comparison can leave the third unstated. The division at [p. 382](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46743/46743-h/46743-h.htm#Page_382) makes this a useful historical comparator for our [assignment test](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#9-practical-congruency-preserving-truth-warrant-and-an-addressee).

Our positive proposal can remain narrower than a complete maximizing ethic: an independently warranted adverse condition gives an appropriately situated actor a baseline reason against its avoidable continuation. This preserves individual subjects and permits differences of responsibility. It need not assume that every good can be placed on one numerical scale. But it still needs a justification for the actor's reason; weakening the conclusion to a baseline does not make that premise disappear.

The local gain is an explicit acquisition target. Perspective-taking should enable correction of what is understood about this patient's condition and the ground of an already accepted comparison. It should not turn the observer's imagined preferences into the patient's welfare. If a condition would distress the observer but benefits this differently constituted patient, accurate uptake requires revising the simulation. If the patient suffers while the observer remains emotionally untroubled, diminished arousal must not by itself erase the proposed reason. These are discriminating tests of our route, not experiments reported by the source.

### What could make the ground warranted rather than merely compelling?

The self-evidence criteria become demanding research obligations when applied to this proposal. The principle must specify whether it concerns a patient's own reason, impersonal value, an actor's reason or an overriding duty. Reflection must expose both projection and a merely familiar restriction to self. The full set of accepted claims must permit consistent decisions, including cases where helping is costly. An informed critic cannot be dismissed solely because the desired conclusion failed to feel obvious to them; their ground and the comparative quality of their evidence require examination.

This does not make unanimity a truth-maker, nor every dissent an equal defeater. It requires an account of why a particular disagreement should or should not reduce confidence. A training procedure could induce uniform assent by hiding counterexamples. Such convergence would not satisfy the inquiry's objective. Conversely, a rationally warranted insight need not originate as a deduction from normatively empty premises. The claim that all foundational warrant must have that form would itself need justification.

For the generative root, the stronger result would identify why a refusal leaves something unresolved in the critic's own independently warranted practical claim. The [public-claim test](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#104-a-stronger-positive-result-when-public-claims-are-already-admitted) supplies one conditional example. A critic who already addresses another actor on a complete patient-ground cannot retain that ground while selectively deleting its matched consequence. A critic who never accepts the interpersonal premise requires a further argument; describing their stance as insufficiently impartial would simply rename the disputed requirement.

### The matched case and the whole theory face different burdens

An equal-cost comparison holding the actor's relevant interests fixed avoids a sacrifice-based conflict. This makes it a useful first test of baseline standing. It does not decide what to do when another person's benefit genuinely conflicts with the actor's own good or legitimate obligations. A necessary root with universal scope must eventually adjudicate those cases, rather than treating the absence of a conflict in its first example as a reconciliation theorem.

The source's dualism is therefore neither a denial of every baseline nor merely a failure to motivate someone. Its possible conflict between rational requirements matters to our whole-root demand for jointly valid development. A model that awards an actor private reward for helping can align choices in that model; it does not establish that all morally relevant worlds contain such an alignment. Likewise, encouraging sympathy can improve response while leaving difficult conflicts or neglected patients unresolved. We must not define favorable incentives into the setting and then claim the foundation explained their universality.

**Current positive assessment:** the identified patient's adverse condition remains the best candidate ground for the matched baseline, and disciplined encounter may reveal its practical force without requiring shared identity or reciprocal benefit. This is a reasoned normative proposal, not a finding that felt convergence certifies it. The next decisive study must distinguish improved patient understanding, formation of a motive, warranted actor-addressed judgment and actual action. Success requires a defensible connection among them; universal necessity additionally requires showing why the relevant adequate practical inquiry cannot validly reject that connection. No stance on unresolved sacrifice cases or the sufficiency of all existing theory commitments is settled by this local route.

## 9. Can scientific normativity warrant extension to the patient's good?

This round advances **unavoidable epistemic authority → an unrelated actor's patient-grounded reason**. The following is our reconstruction, informed by the selected primary texts above. It grants real epistemic reasons for the inquiry, rather than substituting predictive accuracy or an optional reward. Whether that authority must have a particular irreducible metaphysics remains a separate question.

### Four conclusions with different coverage

The proposed progression has four steps: reject an indiscriminate objection to categorical normativity; establish some moral facts; justify this unrelated actor's baseline reason; establish the necessity of every commitment in the developing foundation. None of these transitions is automatic. In particular, an existential result about moral facts does not determine their patient range, addressees or grounds. An inquiry-related moral norm might be real while its relation to a nonparticipating patient's welfare remains unsettled.

The [companion audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#12-what-scientific-normativity-can-require-of-the-moral-dispute) establishes an exact conditional gain. If a feature is alleged to invalidate every reason possessing it, an admitted epistemic reason possessing that feature defeats the unrestricted rule. By contrast, if the feature is a defeasible theoretical cost, independent evidence can outweigh it in one domain without outweighing it everywhere. The affirmative account must identify its positive evidence; the selective skeptic must explain the relevant difference. Neither can settle the comparison through a domain label.

This is already constructive. Suppose the patient-ground has independent prima facie support and the only offered rebuttal is an unrestricted objection that also excludes the critic's epistemic authority. Defeating that rebuttal restores the force of the original support. It does not manufacture the support, exclude every other defeater or guarantee the judgment's truth. A normative advance can occur through this correction without being a deduction from normatively empty premises.

### A positive extension after an admitted moral ground

The source comparison suggests a sharper next study than reiterating parity. Suppose an inquiry argument has already established a genuinely moral norm, rather than merely attaching a moral adjective to epistemic diligence. Compare two accounts that accept this norm. One also recognizes a reason against the matched patient's severe adverse experience; the other confines the admitted moral range to the inquiry case.

If both judgments concern the same independently specified kind of normative fact, the narrower account cannot claim to eliminate that entire kind. This removes one possible advantage; it does not prove every proposed instance. A false application remains false even when its ontology is familiar. The wider account therefore needs evidence for the patient's instance, while the narrower account needs a defensible reason to discount that evidence or restrict the ground. Counting fewer commitments is not a substitute for assessing which commitments are warranted.

Our affirmative proposal supplies three assessable considerations. The patient's condition has reliably identified adverse significance for that differentiated subject. Preventing it in the matched comparison changes no admitted competing interest or permission. The proposed reason explains why the adverse difference matters while allowing additional relationships to allocate greater responsibility elsewhere. Together these make baseline protection a serious candidate judgment. They do not make actor-addressed authority a descriptive property of aversion. The evaluative step remains open to direct justification and criticism.

The same evidential discipline must examine both accounts. Reliable disagreement matters; bare refusal does not automatically defeat the proposal. A culturally familiar judgment can be mistaken; its familiarity does not automatically make it inadmissible. A restricted theory may identify a genuine difference in ground; merely attaching an inquiry-only boundary does not explain one. Our current assessment favors the patient baseline because the matched adverse difference supplies positive practical evidence and no adequately defended exclusion has yet displaced it. This is comparative normative warrant, with its grounds exposed, not a finding that the rival is logically inconsistent.

### The decisive scope test and the remaining necessity premise

The [inquiry-means construction](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#11-epistemic-normativity-and-protection-of-an-evidence-source) can explain protection required to preserve evidence under a justified undertaking. For the stronger test, secure that evidence and remove the patient's remaining epistemic contribution while retaining the adverse experience and feasible relief. A noncommunicating patient supplies another case where practical standing cannot depend on reciprocal testimony. The proposed patient-ground must still apply. Otherwise the construction has explained a role's protection, not yet why this subject's suffering gives the unrelated actor a reason.

The precise remaining premise is that the identified experiential ground supplies practical authority for this capable actor independently of the patient's usefulness to inquiry, with scope and legitimate defeaters justified. Scientific normativity can discipline how this premise is evaluated and defeat certain exclusions. It has not yet been shown to entail the premise solely through its own existence. A direct moral insight, a comparative inference and a constitutive necessity argument are different possible justifications; finding the first two would be genuine progress without completing the third.

For the whole-root claim, success requires more: show why an adequate scientific philosophy cannot validly reject that practical ground, then demonstrate coverage of the foundation's remaining commitments. If a principled restriction survives the endpoint comparison with better warrant, revise the proposed ground. If the patient-ground withstands that scrutiny, preserve it as a justified practical advance and continue the necessity argument. Neither outcome permits treating the causal acquisition of concern, agreement among investigators or a conditional source-protection theorem as completion of the generative foundation.

## 10. Can assurance establish a claim that outlasts inquiry?

This round advances **scientific assertion or testimony → genuine interpersonal authority → standing beyond informational usefulness**. It uses the primary distinctions above, then reconstructs a narrower positive route. The target remains an unavoidable ground capable of supporting the entire foundation, including patients who never communicate. A successful account of one relation contributes to that target without replacing it.

### A specific relation, with a substantive normative ground

A speaker may offer another person an assurance, inviting reliance on the speaker's responsibility for a claim. That encounter differs from providing an independently assessable proof. Its success also differs from merely producing a true sentence. We must identify which act occurred, what was undertaken, to whom, under what conditions and with what justification. An information-processing description that omits these differences could miss the very relation it claims to explain.

The strongest affirmative reconstruction says that deliberately inviting warranted reliance gives the recipient a claim to an appropriate response when the assurance fails. The recipient has placed part of their epistemic agency in the speaker's hands. Treating the recipient as having no standing when failure is discovered would discard the responsibility whose acceptance made this an assurance. This is stronger than predicting reputational penalties or calculating future cooperation benefits.

But the argument has a substantive premise: the invited reliance and assumed responsibility are normatively legitimate. A con artist's declaration cannot manufacture an entitlement to be believed; an institution can impose defective speech conventions. Calling an encounter an assurance cannot alone decide the moral status of its requirements. The positive case must explain why disregard wrongs this recipient, rather than merely violates a game rule, and why its valid claim binds the actor. The sources' distinction between moral standing and categorical practical reasons keeps that last question visible.

### What survives delivery, retraction and the end of usefulness?

Consider a researcher who legitimately undertakes to notify a recipient of a material revision before a stated deadline. The recipient reasonably relies on that undertaking. The study ends, the result is delivered, and the recipient will supply no further evidence. The researcher then discovers the relevant error while notice remains feasible. If the claim has neither been fulfilled nor permissibly released, completion of the study does not discharge it. The trigger and undertaking concern the recipient's continuing reliance, not their usefulness as an information source.

This example adds an explicit undertaking; it is not a claim that every scientific assertion promises indefinite monitoring. A right to challenge an assertion, an obligation to seek errors, an obligation to notify after discovery and a duty to compensate harm have different conditions. The [mechanism reconstruction](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) must preserve those conditions instead of converting one right into all the others.

Retraction likewise does not settle every outstanding claim. The distinction between retracting speech and repairing its consequences is already present in Moran; it is not a new discovery here. Our application asks which independently justified liability persists. Correction can resolve the informational error while leaving a reliance loss. Conversely, a mistake may be innocent and require correction without supporting blame. Moral assessment must track the undertaking, reasonable reliance, responsibility and relevant defeaters, not the mere fact that a prediction was false.

### Which patient receives the claim?

This gives a positive result beyond instrumental source protection: a particular recipient can retain a claim after their contribution to inquiry ends. It does not yet establish every subject's baseline standing. The patient might remain protected because of a past relation. That is a real normative ground, not an arbitrary label, but its scope must be preserved.

Now let the former recipient lose the ability to communicate while the acknowledged liability remains. Removing their ability to demand performance does not itself identify a discharge condition. A justified persistence principle would therefore protect them without present reciprocity. This is a promising test of whether capacity to exercise a claim is being confused with possession of it. The principle requires defense; the inability to complain cannot simply be declared irrelevant by definition.

A third party who never accepted the assurance presents a different case. A promise to a caregiver about a patient's treatment can create an obligation concerning that patient. It does not follow solely from the promise that the obligation is owed to the patient, or that every unrelated patient's interests have standing. Legitimate representation, a justified undertaking for beneficiaries or an independently grounded duty concerning foreseeable harm could supply the further connection. Each would need its own warrant. Information reaching someone is not itself that warrant.

The next decisive comparison holds the actor's legitimate speech commitments fixed while varying harm to a nonparticipating patient. If the obligation persists only for addressees or represented beneficiaries, the result explains a relational extension. To reach the full root, identify why the relevant adverse significance itself addresses this actor, including where no assurance was ever given. The constructive gain is a candidate interpersonal claim whose life exceeds an inquiry's usefulness; the remaining burden is categorical authority and justified beneficiary coverage. Neither generic trust benefits nor the mere existence of speech completes that derivation.

## 11. Can imposed burdens give standing to a patient who never agreed?

The next transition is **responsibility for an imposed burden → a nonparticipating patient's standing**, followed separately by the reason of an unrelated noncauser. Our reconstruction tests an affirmative ground: an actor who controls an intervention cannot justify it entirely through benefits while treating an identified subject's avoidable severe burden as having no practical importance. The relevant subject need never have supplied evidence, accepted testimony or joined an undertaking. This would extend concern beyond the assurance relation without claiming that every causal connection generates the same duty.

### A candidate ground that survives the absence of an invitation

Consider an actor selecting between two equally feasible interventions. Both achieve the same independently justified research aim. One exposes an identified, noncommunicating patient to severe aversive experience; the other avoids that exposure without transferring a comparable burden elsewhere. The patient has never consented, promised anything, supplied information or benefited the actor. This is a conceptual comparison, not a proposed experiment.

The positive judgment is that the patient's burden supplies the actor a reason to choose the second intervention. Its ground is the combination of an identified adverse episode, the actor's authorship of the exposure and a feasible way to avoid it. The judgment does not depend on a prediction that the patient will protest, later become useful or threaten sanctions. It therefore reaches further than protecting partners in inquiry. The burden belongs to a center of experience, while control over its imposition belongs to another center; conflating their perspectives would erase a morally relevant difference in the proposed explanation.

Three propositions nevertheless require separate warrant. The episode must genuinely be adverse for this patient; the actor must have the relevant control or responsibility; and those facts must provide an other-directed practical reason. Reliable science can identify the first two without making the third a descriptive consequence. Our affirmative claim defends the third as the better explanation of the matched choice. It remains a candidate constitutive constraint on justified intervention, not a demonstrated constitutive constraint on every successful scientific inference.

The narrower negative rival can recognize patient experience and still deny any reason unless an accepted undertaking exists. It now owes a positive account of why invitation is necessary when the actor unilaterally determines another's severe exposure. Mere absence of agreement explains why no promise exists; it does not explain permission to impose this avoidable burden. This shifts the comparative burden of justification without treating the rival's failure to answer as a formal contradiction.

### Formation, authorization and repair must have different records

For the generative theory, keep three records: what the intervention brings into existence, which burdens particular centers undergo, and what authorization or responsibility governs the intervention. Increasing a system's organization or aggregate achievement cannot by itself answer the latter two questions. Conversely, preserving a burden in the record does not prove that the intervention is impermissible overall. The project must explain both constructive formation and its possible liabilities without building universal favorable direction into the definition of generation.

A stronger test permits the intervention to create a new capacity or relation that would otherwise never exist. The resulting center undergoes an identified burden. Absence of a better available history for that very center does not make the episode disappear; neither does its presence settle which actor owes what remedy. We propose carrying forward the burden and its causal provenance, then separately adjudicating justification and response. This is a test of explanatory completeness, not adoption of Shiffrin's entire procreation or liability position.

The same separation prevents two mistaken shortcuts. Inability to consent supplies neither permission for every beneficial intervention nor a prohibition on every rescue. And an intervention's good outcome does not itself establish authorized release of a specific liability. A proposed corrective response needs its own conditions: responsibility, remaining need, feasibility, appropriate remedy and relevant countervailing reasons. Moral blame, directed repair and a pro tanto reason for relief may diverge. The existing [claim-persistence analysis](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) can preserve a justified liability; it cannot manufacture its original validity.

### Patient capacity and the noncauser remain separate scope tests

Lack of speech, deliberation or consent capacity must not be confused with absence of adverse experience. But neither may operational dependence or impaired functioning be treated as proof of experience. For a patient who never had full agency, the proposed ground must identify what makes this episode adverse independently of lost future participation. The terminal-experience test in §4 remains useful: removing all later learning and activity leaves the experiential question intact. A will-based explanation also needs an explicit account of its minimal capacity requirements; the sources do not establish that every organized system satisfies them.

Now change only the actor's causal relation. Another capable actor encounters the same patient and can relieve the same severe episode at comparable small cost, but did not impose it and has no special undertaking. Responsibility for authorship no longer supplies that actor's reason. The most promising extension preserves the patient's adverse significance while replacing authorship with a justified opportunity-to-relieve principle. That is a new moral premise to defend, not a reinterpretation of all inaction as causal imposition.

This yields two distinct targets: a baseline reason to help and a directed claim held against a particular actor. The former need not entail the latter. The [practical audit](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md) investigates the rescue bridge; this source round supplies the imposition comparison and that scope distinction. The whole-root necessity claim would advance if these grounds were shown unavoidable for adequate practical justification and their patient coverage independently established. It would need revision if the proposed ground depended on capacities absent from its intended patients, or if a better justified account explained unilateral imposition while excluding their standing. Local responsibility alone does not complete the universal derivation.

## 12. Experience, appraisal and practical authority

This round addresses **experienced aversiveness → experiential disvalue → a capable noncauser's reason**. Its advance is to separate two possible objects of correction: the represented predicament and the experience itself. Relief need not vindicate a patient's mistaken interpretation of the world. Conversely, correcting that interpretation need not yet remove the experienced burden. This makes the proposed concern ground more discriminating than either obedience to every aversion or dismissal of suffering whenever its appraisal is false.

### Five claims that must not substitute for one another

| Claim | What still requires separate justification |
|---|---|
| A signal or sensation has a given sensory intensity | Whether it is experienced as unpleasant |
| The patient undergoes unpleasantness | Whether it carries the particular evaluative content a theory assigns |
| The episode represents a condition as bad for the patient | Whether that condition exists or is bad |
| Undergoing the episode itself is pro tanto bad for the patient | Whether this badness depends on further attitudes; which alternatives improve the situation |
| Another actor has a reason to provide relief | The actor's relevant opportunity, practical authority, competing reasons and permissible means |

The distinction between intrinsic and non-instrumental badness matters here. Ruling out an explanation entirely in terms of bad consequences does not yet rule out every additional relational or desiderative condition. Similarly, rejecting a merely informational account of unpleasantness need not establish one uniquely correct account of affect. Our root needs an adequate ground and a defensible interface, not automatic adoption of every component of evaluativism.

### A constructive response to false danger and unjust resentment

Consider an independently identified severe unpleasant episode while the patient falsely represents a harmless circumstance as dangerous. Compare feasible responses that preserve safety, agency and relevant information. One corrects the factual assessment; another relieves the unpleasant episode; a third does both. These are conceptual alternatives whose availability must be established in an application. If the experience remains adverse after the belief is corrected, the first response has left a candidate reason-ground untouched. If soothing conceals an actual danger, the second may leave a different ground unattended. The best response must identify its target instead of treating all changes called relief as equivalent.

This does not infer that an experience is bad because it represents badness. The case for experiential disvalue concerns what undergoing this identified episode is like, with upstream damage and downstream losses separately controlled. The sources supply arguments for that judgment and a direct challenge to its proposed representational explanation. The theory can retain the practical judgment while revising a failed account of its realization. We should not make a patient's standing depend on one contested theory of mental content.

Now let the distress arise from resentment that another person receives equal standing. Relieving an adverse experience and satisfying the resentment are different actions. A sympathetic, truthful response that helps revise the appraisal may serve the former while rejecting the latter. Suppressing another person's legitimate opportunity is not justified merely because it would gratify the resentful subject. If every available relief violates another's rights, a pro tanto consideration may be outweighed or defeated at the level of that action. These possibilities must be assessed, not excluded by labeling the emotion either authentic or irrational.

Valued discomfort supplies another test. The patient may accept an adverse episode for a legitimate project; its local disvalue need not settle the value of the whole activity or authorize unsolicited interference. If the experience has instead become affectively neutral or pleasurable, the original aversiveness premise may have changed. The account must investigate which change occurred rather than redescribe every chosen difficulty as secretly unwanted. These are tests of the candidate ground, not clinical diagnoses.

### Does understanding another's suffering force disvaluing it?

Gularte supplies a stronger route than simple accuracy about reports. The precise pressure point is whether faithful experiential representation of the patient's whole suffering requires the observer's own total affect to match it. A representation can concern the patient's total episode while remaining one component of the observer's larger mental life. The claim that other observer attitudes necessarily corrupt that representation requires an argument about phenomenal understanding, rather than merely a sum over the observer's feelings.

This objection also has a limit. A model that correctly decodes a suffering label or predicts a report has not thereby demonstrated full experiential understanding. Our earlier information-preservation results cannot decide what token phenomenal representation requires. The useful comparison must independently specify the representation, its accessibility, its patient binding and its adequacy, then ask whether different observer attitudes are possible without changing those features. Stipulating either perfect understanding or its impossibility would beg the contested question.

Even necessary informed disvaluing would leave a further issue: why that response is an authoritative reason for action by this actor. It could support a substantive normative criterion, but the criterion must be defended, and response strength, means and competing duties remain separate. Motivation, disvalue and practical authority are not synonyms.

The strongest present synthesis is therefore a positive, revisable judgment: an identified severe adverse episode supplies a candidate patient-grounded reason for feasible, permissible relief even when its world-directed appraisal is false. Its support concerns the patient's experience and the improvement available, not the actor's imitation of that experience or endorsement of its object. To establish the entire necessary root, we must additionally justify why an adequate practical standpoint cannot exclude this ground solely because it belongs to another center. A successful phenomenal-transfer argument would advance that step; evidence that our experiential classifier confuses intensity, interpretation or performance with unpleasantness would require changing its empirical interface.

## 13. Benefit, authorship and permission

**Named transition:** recognition of a patient's burden → justified choice among relief, appraisal revision and nonintervention. This section reconstructs a candidate normative bridge; it does not treat the source arguments as a proof that scientific competence entails the entire root. It continues [§12](#12-experience-appraisal-and-practical-authority) and distinguishes reasons to help, permissible means and authority to decide. See the [authorship audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#16-concern-authorship-and-the-authority-to-intervene) and [authorization results](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#15-authorization-requires-more-than-a-beneficial-endpoint).

### The positive ground is a patient's contribution to deciding

A patient can be both the subject of an adverse experience and an author of decisions about it. Those roles do different work. A reason to reduce the burden does not exhaust reasons concerning how the response is selected. Suppose a person understands two available responses, can assess their different consequences and chooses the less comfortable one to preserve a project. The helper's better prediction of discomfort does not, by itself, answer whether that person's decision should govern. Counting choice only as another pleasant consequence misses the proposed ground: this is someone whose own deliberation can legitimately settle a question within their domain.

Our strongest positive argument is conditional but substantive. If a person's authorship has justified standing over this decision, helping should normally preserve their ability to consider, reject and revise the response. Otherwise the helper appropriates the very practical role being recognized. This is more than gathering preference data: the patient's answer can change what the helper may do even when the helper's welfare forecast remains unchanged. Information, communication support, time and feasible alternatives can therefore be forms of concern. A nominal opportunity to refuse, made unusable by preventable barriers or punitive withdrawal of unrelated help, may fail to realize the standing professedly recognized.

The ground still requires justification. Why this domain belongs to the patient, which burdens others must accommodate, and when an override is warranted cannot be settled by calling the intervention beneficial. Nor can a full derivation simply define every generative center as an autonomous rights-holder. The candidate extends the root through differentiated practical authorship; experience without such authorship retains the distinct grounds developed in [§11](#11-can-imposed-burdens-give-standing-to-a-patient-who-never-agreed).

### Refusal, requested harm and a changing patient

Consider a mistaken danger appraisal. The helper may offer accurate information or a less burdensome interpretation without acquiring permission to impose psychological change. The patient may accept correction, decline it or select another permissible response. Conversely, refusing to carry out the patient's harmful demand need not deny the patient's distress or authorship. Their control over an intervention affecting themselves does not automatically control another person's body, effort or legitimate commitments. Independently justified duties of accommodation may nevertheless require others to bear some costs. The appropriate comparison includes a supported alternative, not just coercive relief versus abandonment.

An apparent consent signal must also be distinguished from permission. The theory needs an account of what the person understood, what they authorized, the relevant scope and any valid continuing commitment. Predicted approval after manipulation is not present authorization. Neither is an assessor's preferred outcome. An updated refusal cannot be rejected merely because it differs from an earlier preference: learning is part of the patient agency being protected. Equally, change alone does not dissolve every prior commitment or establish that a new signal is informed. These are questions for the actual decision and relation, not a universal instruction to maximize the most recent preference.

Noncommunication cannot decide these questions by itself. A person may retain understanding and wishes while lacking an available means of expression. Where feasible and justified, support should seek their accessible contribution before replacing it; this is not an instruction to delay regardless of urgency or cost. Where direct decision is unavailable, existing authorizations, established wishes, residual responses and a justified representative relation may constrain action, but none is automatically decisive in every case. Where such agency has never existed, experiential standing does not vanish; neither does this section manufacture autonomy-based permission. Protective or surrogate action requires its own justification, including whose good is represented and how mistaken action can be corrected. This is a conceptual research distinction, not a clinical capacity assessment.

### The strongest rival and a discriminating comparison

A serious rival can value autonomous activity while treating it as one outcome to promote. It may favor participation in nearly every ordinary case yet permit substitution when the complete outcome ranking favors it. Our authority account must explain why the patient's valid decision sometimes supplies a constraint that survives that comparison. Pointing to long-term welfare, uncertainty or better feedback alone cannot settle this dispute: those considerations can also fit the rival.

A useful conceptual comparison therefore holds anticipated relief, factual understanding and other people's burdens fixed while changing only whether a legitimate patient decision governs. Does bypassing that decision create an additional objection? A positive answer, supported by the argument about standing rather than built into a reward label, advances the authority bridge. An independently better-justified negative answer would force revision of the proposed account; bare denial would not. Further comparisons must test conflicts, changing wishes and supported noncommunication; none has been run here.

The result is a constructive constraint on wisdom: acquiring an accurate model of another's good should expand justified ways of helping without silently expanding power over them. Universal necessity remains to be established through the standing and domain premises, rather than inferred from prediction, consent-like behavior or a favorable endpoint.

### Our reconstruction: make the naturalistic identification answerable

The constructive possibility is a shared explanation of what benefits a subject, why the subject can be mistaken about it, and how experience can correct that mistake. A candidate foundation should identify the actual patient's organization, experience, commitments and circumstances, then explain why particular changes support or frustrate that patient's good. This offers more than a definition of good as whatever currently attracts behavior. The same underlying conditions could explain both why an intervention helps and why better understanding recommends it, even when the patient initially resists or misunderstands it.

The affirmative warrant must have two parts. The account should explain and discriminate actual consequences better than competing accounts. It should also justify identifying its target as the patient's good: does it preserve what matters in that subject's life, permit correction of ignorance, and resist substituting an assessor's preferred life? An account that succeeds at both has positive support as a naturalistic grounding proposal. Calling it a substantive identity does not by itself disqualify it; calling an observed regularity “good” does not establish it. The explanatory and evaluative arguments must concern the same proposed property.

This imposes a concrete identity constraint on idealization. An improved assessor is a means of investigating the actual patient, not a replacement beneficiary. An intervention that benefits the transformed assessor need not benefit the original subject. Nor may the desired conclusion be secured by altering preferences until the assessor endorses it. Actual learning can legitimately change a person's judgment and sometimes their circumstances; those changes must be represented as changes, rather than silently treated as discovery of a fixed earlier good. Equal-best alternatives may preserve genuine plurality, so a useful identification need not impose one uniquely preferred life.

The relevant empirical and counterfactual assumptions include which corrections are possible, what factual understanding they produce, how they change evaluation, and whether alternative justified correction paths support compatible conclusions about the same subject. Disagreement between arbitrarily assigned utilities does not refute a naturalistic identity that independently constrains admissible evaluations. Conversely, successful factual convergence alone does not establish evaluative convergence. These are discriminating obligations for the foundation; no new experiment or convergence theorem is reported here.

### Our reconstruction: distinguish the patient's good, moral scope and an actor's reason

Suppose the naturalistic account establishes that a particular outcome would genuinely benefit patient P. That can settle an important part of the practical question without settling whose action it requires. An impartial social standard must additionally justify admitting P's good, comparing it with other goods and respecting legitimate authority. If those premises are independently defended, newly learned means can then generate a warranted patient-directed conclusion under that standard. This is a constructive route from identified good to application of an already justified interpersonal ground; the patient need not rediscover or express every premise.

Our stronger claim additionally asks why an adequate actor has a reason to answer that ground even when its own ends do not support doing so. The primary comparison makes this a real disagreement, not merely a failure to motivate an actor who already has the reason. Preserving an objective moral assessment while allowing a gap in practical reasons is one coherent theoretical option; establishing that it is normatively inadequate requires an argument about reason-authority. Neither denying its scientific vocabulary nor demanding actual motivational compliance answers it.

A noncommunicating patient sharpens both burdens. Absence of an available speech channel does not remove an otherwise established good. Where full deliberative capacities never existed, however, inventing an ideal rational counterpart is not yet a justified account of that patient's interests. The foundation must establish an appropriate experiential or organizational basis and explain why it supports standing. Representation by another may make that good effective in social choice, but representation's success cannot be the condition of the patient's value. Otherwise the account would abandon precisely those differentiated centers whose claims cannot produce pressure.

The advance is therefore a candidate common natural basis for benefit, evaluative correction and learning, together with a precise choice about practical authority. Its adequacy remains testable against the actual subject, alternative explanations and competing normative accounts. It does not complete the whole-root necessity proof, but it makes that proof's next premise substantially more specific than an unexplained instruction to value another.

### Our reconstruction: warranted development without self-certification

The affirmative route permits an agent to begin learning concern with partial justified access, rather than demanding complete appreciation in advance. A temporary incentive, trusted example or guided encounter may sustain attention while the agent learns why the patient's condition matters. The justification must connect that activity to a defensible target and a suitable route. Neither the incentive's motivational effectiveness nor the later person's endorsement supplies this connection by itself.

Three questions therefore remain separate: whether there is reason to undertake the learning; whether the developing concern fits the patient's actual situation; and whether the resulting action is justified among competing claims. The reasons may overlap, but need not. A reward can give a genuine practical reason to train attention without establishing the patient's independent importance. Equally, mixed motives or instrumental beginnings need not invalidate learning or helpful action. We must compare the complete reasons and the evolving costs, rather than demand motivational purity.

Our proposed diagnostic asks whether the route remains directed toward better patient understanding when a shortcut offers the same external reward without it. Refusing that shortcut can distinguish this project from reward collection. It cannot certify the target: a sincere learner under a mistaken mentor could also refuse. The account needs independently defensible checks on the patient's experience, the relevance of the proposed concern, and the guide's competence. Those checks may improve during learning without making the process its own final authority. Correcting or abandoning an erroneous trajectory can be rational progress.

Nor is every motivational improvement new evaluative learning. An agent who already understands the relevant reason but trains attention, emotion or habits to act reliably presents a different explanatory task. Conversely, incomplete verbal articulation need not show absent understanding. The generative study must identify what changes—knowledge, appreciation, motivation, skill or circumstances—before claiming to have explained acquisition of value.

The foundation's positive task is to explain how partial access, supported practice and correction can generate better warranted concern. The value and actor-addressed scope of that concern remain to be justified through the common root. An optional project of developing appreciation is not automatically a duty of every scientific inquirer, and a learner's self-development is not a substitute for the patient's good. Partial progress does not settle overall priority, permissible learning costs, universal N1 or directed N3. This comparison adds a candidate developmental structure and discriminating questions, not an empirical test, a new formal result or an additional requirement on the accepted finite application.
