# Resolving the constitutive and uniqueness claims

6 October 2026. AI-authored resolution of the remaining philosophical obligations in [mathematical truth and the root](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/MATHEMATICAL-TRUTH-AND-THE-ROOT.md), following the request to finish the remaining investigation. This develops the [constitutive argument](MATHEMATICS-CONSTITUTIVE-ARGUMENT.md), evaluates its P1–P7, and reaches a decision about what to adopt. It preserves the [rival-account arguments](MATHEMATICS-CONSTITUTION-RIVALS.md) and [mathematical results](MATHEMATICS-TRUTH-CONSTITUTION-MATH.md). It adds no machine verification or empirical evidence.

**Resolution:** a positive constitutive interpretation can be justified for specified mathematical subjects: their identity is the structural role fixed by their admitted formation, identification and completeness conditions, and their interpreted truths depend on that structural content. This supports an explicit generative-structuralist extension. The current causal premises do not establish that their root is the uniquely ultimate ground of this structural content, much less of every mathematical truth. The strongest attempts to establish that further conclusion either require a new substantive identity/priority commitment or weaken “root” to a common structural condition. The unrestricted unique-ground claim therefore cannot be reported as a completed result. The recommended extension and revised claim below settle this investigation at its warranted scope, rather than moving the same unproved conclusion into another list of tasks.

## 1. The positive argument that can be completed

The best argument proceeds through **constitutive identity**, not through the inference that whatever fixes a truth value thereby grounds it.

1. Fix a mathematical subject by independently intelligible formation and identity conditions. For the arithmetic case, these describe freely iterating a successor from zero, with no additional elements or identifications, and the specified recursive arithmetic operations.
2. Prove that these conditions determine a structural role up to the appropriate equivalence. The existing initial-algebra construction does this for the stated arithmetic presentation, in its supplied metatheory.
3. Treat the mathematical subject *as that structural role*. This is a substantive structuralist interpretation of what the subject is. It requires no further individuating material or nonstructural mathematical constituent behind the role.
4. Interpret statements as claims about that role. For the declared first-order language, the relations, operations and semantic clauses determine their truth, including quantified truths that cannot all be produced by an effective sound proof system.
5. A change of realization preserving the entire relevant role preserves the mathematical subject as characterized and every interpreted claim. Destroying a calculation, changing its inscription, or choosing another isomorphic presentation changes neither the role nor the truth at issue.

Steps 1–2 and 4–5 have mathematical proofs at their stated scope. Step 3 is the philosophical identification making the explanation constitutive. Its warrant is not merely that it is compatible with the proofs. It identifies the subject by exactly the conditions that mathematical reasoning uses to distinguish its possibilities, and it avoids adding an independently individuated object with no further role in those possibilities. The corresponding mathematical question can be answered without determining any such additional object's material or history.

This is an actual positive argument for the structuralist identification. It is not a theorem that every imaginable nonstructural ontology is false. A rival may give reasons to reject the identification; the burden is then to explain the extra constitution its mathematical subject needs, or to offer a better account of what the structural role amounts to.

The grounding assertion should be typed carefully. A structure, a fact about that structure, and the truth of a proposition representing that fact are not automatically one and the same item. On the adopted interpretation, the proposition's truth obtains through its relevant structural content and meaning. We are not claiming that a fact strictly grounds itself merely because its name occurs inside a definition. Nor do we need an event that makes an already constituted equation become true.

This gives a substantive conclusion:

> For a mathematical subject whose identity is its specified generative structural role, its formation and identity conditions, together with the declared logical interpretation, provide a constitutive explanation of its truths. Nothing about a particular act of calculation is an additional mathematical constituent of those truths.

That conclusion is stronger than explaining mathematical practice. It is supported by a philosophical identity claim informed by exact mathematical results. It is not the stronger conclusion that the entire structure, logic and modal resources have been derived from the existing causal root.

## 2. Why accepting this identity is not an illegitimate circle

A constitutive identification need not be established without using the subject it identifies. To investigate whether numbers are structural positions, one must already understand some arithmetic; to investigate what makes an organism an organism, one must already identify some organisms. Such inquiry can revise its initial understanding through explanatory comparison. It is not automatically an argument whose conclusion is its only premise.

Here the independent work is identifiable: the subject specification is fixed, the construction and equivalence are proved, the identity criterion is defended, and the alternative extra constitution is assessed. Using ordinary mathematics in these steps prevents us from advertising an origin of mathematics from a mathematics-free starting point. It does not invalidate the resulting account of what mathematical objects are.

Likewise, a theory may include the processes through which its own representations arise. Causal feedback, mutual epistemic support, constitutive identity and strict grounding are different relations. Their circularity conditions should not be conflated. We have no need to infer a formal system's soundness solely from the fact that it produced its own endorsement. Nor does the proposed structuralist interpretation require a single language containing an unrestricted truth predicate for itself.

“Independent justification” therefore means a reason for the disputed step beyond merely asserting that step. It allows real definitions, mathematical consequences, explanatory comparison and abduction. It does not require an impossible standpoint outside every logical or conceptual framework.

Fine's distinction between essential and merely necessary properties supports this form of inquiry: an object's nature can explain a necessity without being reducible to its modal profile. It also prevents us from assigning every necessary mathematical truth to the essence of an arbitrarily selected root. The constitutive identification must concern the relevant nature. [Fine, *Essence and Modality*, especially pp. 4–6](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259861636_Essence_and_Modality_The_Second_Philosophical_Perspectives_Lecture).

## 3. Can the stronger arguments identify the ultimate root?

### Structural essence and “no further constituent”

The completed argument removes the need for a mathematical bearer with a further nonstructural nature behind the specified subject. That is a real explanatory reduction. It does not remove the need for the structural nature itself or the principles admitting it.

There are two distinct identities:

\[
\text{mathematical subject}=\text{its specified structural role},
\]

\[
\text{that role's ultimate basis}=\text{the present generative root}.
\]

The first can receive the argument in Section 1. The second does not follow by substitution unless an additional account already connects the root to the full structural role. “There is no further constituent beyond the structure” does not mean “there is no further commitment beyond our present description of generativity.” The latter currently omits the arithmetic case's free closure, nonidentification and semantic resources.

One can adopt the second identity as a metaphysical thesis. It then has to earn its support through a specification of the root rich enough to do the constitutive work. Calling this thesis an identity, rather than a grounding law, does not automatically discharge the work.

### Transcendental indispensability

The strongest legitimate transcendental argument says that a determinate mathematical claim has content only through relevant identity, difference and relations; exact formation likewise requires an account of when its conditions and results agree. Removing every such distinction would remove the very mathematical content supposedly retained. This establishes a necessary structural condition of the practice and of the subjects *as structurally specified*.

It does not establish the sufficient basis of every structural possibility. The inference

\[
\text{every adequate account requires structural differentiation}
\]

to

\[
\text{every adequate account is ultimately grounded in our specific root}
\]

changes a necessary common condition into an exclusive sufficient explanation. An account taking structural essences as primitive satisfies the first statement while rejecting the proposed direction in the second. Refuting that rival would require an additional argument about essence or priority, not another demonstration that its argument uses distinctions.

This is not a merely epistemic dismissal of an ontological argument. If mathematical subjects are identified with structures, differentiation is genuinely part of their constitution. The unresolved question is what grounds those structures and why that ground includes precisely the substantive commitments of our root.

### Immanent monism

The favorable monist argument is: truths depend on reality; their derivative grounds belong to a common fundamental reality; that reality has an intrinsically generative constitution; therefore mathematical truths ultimately depend on that generative reality. Given all three substantive claims, the conclusion is legitimate under the stated composition of dependence.

The first claim can be motivated through the relation between a proposition's truth and its subject matter. The second and third are not established by “nothing real is outside reality.” Independent fundamental resources could all belong to reality. Inclusion in the whole does not alone establish dependence on the whole; even if the whole is fundamental, the minimal causal description of it need not determine its full mathematical or modal character.

Schaffer's universal-truthmaker proposal is a serious favorable precedent: one ultimate world can ground many differentiated truths. His package explicitly employs truthmaker maximalism and truthmaking as grounding in fundamental being. This makes the monist route defensible as a metaphysical proposal, but does not derive its premises or identify the present causal root with that complete world. [Schaffer, *The Least Discerning and Most Promiscuous Truthmaker*, Section I](https://www.jonathanschaffer.org/promiscuous.pdf).

There is no need to impose a blanket rule that a contingent basis can never ground a necessary truth; theories of grounding differ on that issue. Our decisive point is narrower: the present causal commitments do not determine the relevant constitutive relation or the full structural content. An atemporal account of structural possibility supplies a coherent extension, but it is additional content.

### Comparative abduction

The structuralist extension has positive explanatory merits. It accounts for invariance across realizations, makes uncomputed mathematical truth intelligible, distinguishes truth from survival of inscriptions, and connects mathematical operations with concrete formation without reducing one to the other's execution. These merits support adopting it as an interpretation of the covered subjects.

They do not presently discriminate a uniquely ultimate root. A realist who treats mathematical structures as fundamental can accept those same invariances and explain actual reasoning through generative processes. A modal structuralist can accept unactualized mathematical possibilities without deriving their possibility from the full substantive root. Our proposed unification might ultimately be more economical, but that advantage has not been established merely by naming its many resources once.

The arithmetic presentation demonstrates compression of a subject's structural description relative to a complete operation table. It does not by itself demonstrate compression of the *total foundational commitments*: logic, the relevant infinite domain, semantics and admissibility remain counted. A fair comparison charges the rivals for corresponding resources too. Neither declaring all structures primitive nor declaring all possibility generative wins automatically.

Thus abduction gives affirmative support to the scoped structuralist account and a reason to consider a common modal basis. It does not currently justify reporting that every adequate ultimate rival must reduce to our root.

## 4. A completed decision on the seven premises

The labels refer to the earlier [constitutive argument](MATHEMATICS-CONSTITUTIVE-ARGUMENT.md#3-the-strongest-explicit-argument).

| Premise | Argument and resolution | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| P1. Structural adequacy | Exact presentations recover arithmetic and, with their full table data supplied, every nonempty set-sized finitary first-order structure. The role-identity argument supports interpreting those mathematical subjects structurally. This does not classify all possible mathematical ontologies. | Accept for the declared structural domain; do not promote the representation theorem to unrestricted ontology. |
| P2. Essence-to-truth | Structural identity plus compositional interpretation explains why the subject's interpreted truths require no further mathematical constituent or calculation event. Semantics supplies an exact determination result. | Accept as a justified constitutive interpretation at that scope, with its logical/semantic resources explicit; distinguish it from a framework-neutral grounding theorem. |
| P3. Modal coverage | The existing constructions give coverage when their domains, closure and existence principles are admitted. A common mathematical coding can cover many subjects but may import their atomic content. Neither physical recurrence nor bare distinguishability supplies every completion. | Accept the proved coverage statements. Retain existence and closure as explicit commitments; reject the inference to unrestricted coverage from the causal premises alone. |
| P4. Constitutive priority | Real definition supports subject-as-structural-role. It does not identify that complete role with the present causal root. The unchanged-content grounding expansions remain countermodels to entailment from the current formal premises. | Discharge the local identity bridge; the stronger root-to-all-essences bridge is an additional philosophical commitment, not an obtained result. |
| P5. Grounding composition | In the local argument, substituting the constitutive role for the subject and applying its semantic interpretation yields the claimed dependence directly. This avoids relying on unrestricted transitivity of every relation called grounding. | Accept this scoped explanatory composition. Broader grounding laws require their own account. |
| P6. Root identification | The theory's structural vocabulary connects causal and mathematical descriptions, but common vocabulary and representability do not prove that their full bases are identical. | Introduce the modal-structural aspect explicitly as an extension; do not claim it has already been derived from the causal core. |
| P7. Uniqueness | Universal properties give uniqueness of specified structures up to their stated equivalence. Structural and modal rivals can preserve the same mathematics while differing on what is fundamental. None has been excluded or reduced by the proposed arguments. | Reject unrestricted unique ultimate grounding as an established conclusion. Preserve local uniqueness and genuine equivalences of presentation. |

The negative decisions are not claims that the unrestricted thesis is metaphysically false. They are final assessments of what the present arguments establish. The positive decisions are more than a restatement of unproved premises: Sections 1–3 explain why the local constitutive identification is worth accepting and why its stronger extrapolations fail.

## 5. A concrete minimal extension at the present interface

The smallest recommended philosophical extension is **generative structural identity for admitted presentations**. Its scope and mathematical resources remain explicit.

Let \(F\) be the declared mathematical background and \(P\) a presentation whose intended initial structure \(I_F(P)\) exists. Adopt:

> **GSI.** The mathematical subject specified by \(P\) is the structural role of \(I_F(P)\), up to the declared equivalence. It has no additional mathematical individuation beyond that role. Its interpreted truths obtain through that role and the specified interpretation, whether or not a physical process realizes or computes them.

GSI is a proposed real definition and constitutive interpretation. The theorem giving \(I_F(P)\), its uniqueness and its semantic consequences is separate. The subscript \(F\) prevents the ontology, closure principles and interpretation from disappearing into the word “generation.” This is minimal as an added interpretive bridge at this interface, not a claim to have proved a globally minimal axiomatization of mathematics.

For the established arithmetic case the commitments are concrete:

* all finite terms in the displayed arithmetic signature are admitted;
* only the identifications required by the specified equations and congruence laws are imposed;
* the resulting term algebra and quotient exist in the declared background;
* classical first-order interpretation, including quantification over the whole carrier, is used.

GSI then identifies the arithmetic subject with that freely constituted structural role. The existing mathematical proof determines every first-order arithmetic truth. This is a completed account of that scoped constitution, conditional on the openly supplied mathematics and adopted identity interpretation. Neither an effective procedure enumerating all truths nor an actual infinite material construction is required.

For a different structure, further equations, generators or completion conditions legitimately select a different subject. GSI does not assert that every arbitrary condition has a realization, that every completion exists, or that all admitted structures form one set. Its admissibility remains disciplined by \(F\). It therefore does not hide an unrestricted plenitude principle.

Rosen's analysis makes this restraint substantive rather than merely cautious: conditions describing what an object would be and conditions guaranteeing its existence are different obligations. His investigation allows generative existence principles while seeking a restriction against arbitrary additions; it does not settle that restriction. We can adopt the identity interpretation without pretending it has supplied every existence principle. [Rosen, *The Modal Status of Generative Principles*, Sections 8–14](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395978142_The_Modal_Status_of_Generative_Principles).

The wider theory can consequently distinguish two connected applications of its generative vocabulary: causal formation under actual laws, and atemporal mathematical formation under declared structural laws. Their shared concept is constrained formation. GSI makes the mathematical application constitutive at the declared scope. Identifying both as products of a uniquely ultimate common basis would remain a further thesis; it is not included covertly in this extension.

## 6. Exact verdict on unrestricted completion

There are three ways to use “root,” and none licenses an unnoticed passage to the desired conclusion.

| Meaning of root | What follows | What would be overstated |
|---|---|---|
| The present minimal causal premises | Conditional consequences for the admitted processes and genuine structural connections to mathematics | That these premises supply all mathematical existence, identity, logic and grounding |
| The necessary common form of determinate structural formation | A broad condition shared by the mathematical subjects in view | That a necessary common condition is the sufficient and unique ultimate ground of their complete content |
| A complete modal-structural basis, stipulated to constitute every mathematical subject | Universal constitution follows if the stipulation and required explanatory laws are accepted | That this is already a derivation from the former root or a demonstrated reduction of every ultimate rival |

The formal expansion argument proves non-entailment from the present formal premises. The philosophical arguments provide support for the proposed structural identity and for considering an encompassing modal account. They do not eliminate the remaining substantive choices about ultimate priority and universal admissibility. Adding an axiom that the root is the uniquely complete ultimate basis would make the desired sentence follow, but would leave its contested content assumed rather than justified.

There is also no warrant for saying that these questions are irreducible in principle or that no future argument can improve this result. “Irreducible commitment” here means a commitment not reduced by the arguments and premises currently supplied. The present investigation can nevertheless finish: we can decide which claims to adopt now without promising that another round of the same reasoning must produce the unrestricted conclusion.

**Recommended revised claim:**

> **For mathematical subjects specified by admitted generative presentations, their structural roles constitute what the subjects are, and their interpreted truths express the constraints of those roles. The arithmetic construction establishes an exact instance covering every first-order truth of its intended subject, including truths beyond any fixed effective sound arithmetic proof system. Generative structural identity is our explicit philosophical interpretation of this result. The current causal root has not been shown to be the uniquely ultimate ground of all mathematical possibility.**

> **对于由已获承认的生成式呈现所规定的数学对象，其结构角色构成了它是什么；经明确解释的数学真理，表达这些结构角色所要求的关系。现有算术构造给出了一个确切实例，覆盖其预期对象的每个一阶真理，包括任何固定、有效且可靠的算术证明系统所不能穷尽的真理。生成结构同一性是我们明确采用的哲学解释；现有因果根基尚未被证明是全部数学可能性的唯一终极根据。**

These are AI-authored recommended formulations. They retain constitutive explanation, all-truth determination within an exact domain, non-effective truth and plural structures. They withdraw the unsupported promotion from those results to unique universal priority. That is the finished verdict of this branch, while the broader [generative-grounding programme](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md) continues under its existing completion conditions.
