## 2. Repair has a condition that need not be worth producing

The concurrent [origin-of-need continuation](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#7-continuation-the-origin-of-a-need-and-the-value-of-its-repair) already supplies the distinct initial and reached-state baselines, rejects repair-count sufficiency and credits earlier imposition-and-rescue work. The following three-history organization inherits that result. The distinct advance reviewed here concerns what happens when a valuable response is constituted partly by a burden that successful completion removes. Distinguish three histories:

- `H₀`: the specified imposed burden is absent and ordinary activity continues.
- `H₁`: the imposed burden occurs and an eligible response relieves it.
- `H₂`: the same burden occurs and remains without that response.

The comparison between `H₁` and `H₂` can favor actual repair at the inherited scope. It does not settle the comparison between `H₁` and `H₀`. The latter differs in the burden's occurrence, the response, the activity displaced by both, and potentially the relations constituted through care. A valid comparison must retain those differences. It cannot establish that creating the burden is warranted merely by citing the value of relief once that burden exists.

The inference being rejected is precise: **a condition through which valuable repair becomes available need not therefore be a condition worth producing or preserving.** Historical origin, a current enabling condition, constitution of the response and the condition that the response repairs have different explanatory roles within one connected unfolding. A dependency does not carry ethical favor backward to every contributor in its causal ancestry.

This also reveals the scope of the old no-loss criterion, which its original authors already stated as sufficient. It is a sufficient favorable comparison under its premises, not a definition of all warranted repair. Compassionate response to this presently existing burden can be a relational good whose repeated application depends on that burden continuing. Successful relief can end that same application. The past response, achieved relief and formed relationship remain real, while gratitude, ordinary participation or prevention can develop. Requiring every responsive good to remain available in precisely its earlier form would make successful completion disqualifying. That stronger preservation demand is therefore unsuitable as a universal repair criterion; it is an extrapolation being rejected, not the old criterion's original claim.

The ending or change of an instantiated response is not itself a demonstrated ethical loss. Completion may fulfill the activity rather than sacrifice something good, and compassion concerning past adversity can persist. The strict identity of the presently exercised response cannot be preserved while its present object disappears; whether this change constitutes any pro tanto loss is a further appraisal. Consequently, relational change alone does not show that the old no-warranted-loss antecedent fails or that the comparison lies outside every dominance-based assessment. It shows why that antecedent must be assessed rather than established by assuming literal preservation.

This judgment does not claim that every ended relationship is harmless or that every transformation is better. It distinguishes the value of an actual response from an unrestricted claim on perpetuating the occasion that made it appropriate. A richer rival may defend independently valuable cultivation, fitting response or deserved adversity. Those proposed grounds must be assessed rather than ruled out merely by naming the process repair.

This does not prove a categorical ordering between `H₀` and `H₁`. A defender of transformative meaning might claim a particular repaired history contains otherwise unavailable goods. That claim needs appraisal of the actual means, prior authority, losses, displaced alternatives and surviving burdens. Nor does the test prove that painful activity is always a mistake: competent repair may itself require a justified burden. Its conditional value and necessity cannot be extended automatically to its deliberate origin.

The test therefore constrains the generative ethical proposal at a useful interface. “Produces conditions for further good” is insufficient unless the account distinguishes **supporting the good** from **creating the impairment that makes a valuable response applicable**. The differentiation comes from the actual response and history; it does not posit an external evaluator who donates all significance afterward.

## 3. The crossed test retains felt manner and relational role

A richer candidate must distinguish the manner of undergoing from the relations constituted by the activity. Consider the following conceptual configurations. These are stipulated tests, not empirical reports or declarations that a numerical signal establishes experience.

| Configuration | Felt manner granted in the case | Further relations that the assessment must retain |
|---|---|---|
| Enjoying freely shared play | Actual enjoyment | Participation, consent and effects on others |
| Enjoying the successful humiliation of another | Actual enjoyment | The enjoyment's dependence on another's imposed humiliation |
| Enduring a needed, authorized repair | Actual aversiveness | What is repaired, the necessity of the burden, permission and alternatives |
| Enduring an imposed futile burden | Actual aversiveness | The absence of the proposed benefit, coercion and avoidable exposure |

The two enjoyment cases need not share every phenomenal feature; their occurrence as enjoyment does not settle the value of what is enjoyed. Similarly, recognizing actual aversiveness in both painful cases does not make their whole assessment identical. This retains the relevant experiential target without pretending that the relationships forming each activity are independent decorations.

If the defended ethical judgments distinguish these cases, neither successful pursuit of an installed target nor valence alone is an adequate ethical description. This is a conditional inadequacy result: the experiential and relational judgments are premises to defend. It does not prove that enjoyment has zero value in cruelty, that authorized pain loses every adverse significance, or that any particular aggregation is correct.

The positive requirement is substantive: a description claiming whole ethical adequacy must preserve both **how activity is undergone** and **what the activity does and constitutes in its relations** whenever either changes the judgment. These are connected articulations of one history, not fundamentally separate realms or additive score components.

## 4. The strongest surviving rival concerns the contribution of the part

A whole-life or organic-value rival can acknowledge every painful occurrence, every report and the whole history, while rejecting the claim that a painful occurrence contributes a fixed amount or even an invariant negative ethical contribution in every configuration. It may hold that the significance of enduring a challenge partly consists in its place within courage, warranted responsibility or a meaningful achievement. Whether that account is right must be examined; its contention is not that the painfulness did not occur.

Thus “the adverse aspect remains” has two readings. That aversive undergoing remains is a descriptive claim. That it retains a fixed pro tanto ethical polarity is the disputed constitutive claim. Descriptive non-erasure cannot establish the second by switching between these readings.

The rival also owes an account. Naming a meaningful whole cannot make every imposed burden valuable or license unnecessary injury. It must identify the actual relation through which the alleged meaning, merit or authority arises, distinguish justified cultivation from rationalized domination, and explain why the particular burden is required. The candidate in turn cannot dismiss every relational difference as a countervailing benefit after first claiming the episode's value is context-independent. Both accounts must state the scope they are defending before the test.

A clean comparison preserving every independently relevant relation while genuinely reducing still-unwanted aversiveness supports the inherited relief judgment. If a rival defends the burden itself as constitutive of a worthwhile relation, the claim that this comparison preserves every good is exactly what is disputed. Writing “no warranted loss” does not settle that dispute. An actual countercomparison must identify which relation changes and defend its significance without presuming the desired verdict.

## 5. Boundary cases that the revised account must keep

**Adaptive endorsement.** Approval, report and undergone quality can change together or separately. If conditioning only changes a report while aversiveness continues, the episode remains in the relevant descriptive history. If adaptation genuinely changes the experience, that is a real transformation. Its experiential improvement does not certify the organization that produced it; restrictions of agency and alternative possibilities may remain. Conversely, calling every adapted preference false would beg the question against genuine accommodation. The formation and consequence history must decide what changed.

**Successful domination.** Stable reproduction, capable learning, accurate knowledge and favorable controller feedback may coexist with imposed suffering. Their success therefore cannot by itself establish whole goodness. This case does not automatically settle which restrictions are legitimate or why another's burden has the proposed ethical significance. Those remain substantive parts of the account.

**Worthwhile painful activity.** A participant can accept a burden for an achievement without enjoying the painfulness itself. The burden, the participant's understanding, the achievement and the available alternatives require separate identification within the one activity. Genuine intrinsic appreciation may change the candidate's restricted experiential domain; it cannot be assumed merely from participation. Neither participation nor painfulness alone supplies the whole verdict.

**Benign completion.** A satisfying episode or an effective course of repair can properly end. The end of a task is not automatically damage to a life, and a life is not interchangeable with one of its projects. Ending an experiencing life cannot be entered as the same operation as ending its painful episode. A general preference for more continuation would erase these different transformations.

**Phenomenal parity with different authority.** Two responses can bring about similar immediate experiences while differing in consent, deception, prior commitment or use of another's body. The whole-process account must preserve these relations if they affect justification. It cannot infer permissible intervention from the proposed goodness of an experiential endpoint. Nor does a permission dispute show that the experience was unreal or its relief illusory.

## 6. What this review can accept

The revised investigation advances the foundation by making realized manner and relational role jointly answerable to concrete cases. Building on the concurrent repair-origin result, it identifies why removing a burden can change an actually valuable response, requiring appraisal of completion rather than an assertion of identical preservation. Repair's success need not conserve its earlier occasion or present form. This restricts a demand for literal preservation of every currently instantiated good; it does not establish an unavoidable ethical loss in every successful repair. Persistence, target success and future capacity retain their actual explanatory scope. The inherited general representation consequence is valid conditionally; its experiential and ethical interpretations remain separately warranted.

The retained obstruction is precise: neither the occurrence of an experience, accurate inclusion in a whole nor joint feasibility proves how its value contributes under every relational transformation. A universal criterion must defend that contribution and its conditions of preservation. The next discriminating study should therefore identify a concrete transformation and test whether an alleged change in meaning or authority genuinely changes the ground, compensates for a retained burden, or merely rationalizes it. Repeating another clean comparison with all contested relations stipulated away would not resolve this objection.

This is an unresolved explanation within the proposed common generative account. It is not evidence for an external realm of value, a demand for another controller, or a rejection of the accepted conditional foundation. No new scientific result, formal theorem or primary-source reading is claimed by this review.

### Three inadequate tests and the stronger identification

The hypothesis fixes a present activity: these participants actually exercise specified interpretive capacities in mutually responsive music. This target is more precise than a favorable sound or the label participation. It does not acquire ethical authority merely because the investigator selects it.

**Some reaction to input is insufficient.** A device can track a performer's gesture with a decorative change while independently supplying every timing and phrasing choice relevant to the declared target. Such feedback may be real without realizing the claimed exercise. Conversely, a musically significant ornament could constitute a genuine part of the activity; the target cannot be narrowed after the observation simply to dismiss it. Identify the relevant range of choices in advance and inspect their operative paths.

**But-for indispensability is too strong.** Another contribution can duplicate, mask or compensate for a real performance. An unchanged total output when one contribution is removed does not establish that the original contribution was absent. Actual execution, its role in the joint activity and admissible variations must be examined together. The proposed counterfactual tests are therefore evidence for this identified relation, not a complete causation theory or a requirement of unilateral control.

**Automation is not a classification.** Live selection, directing a device, mixing, playing an adapted instrument and listening together can all be real forms of participation. Prior recording retains its actual historical authorship. What a preset may bypass is the specified present interpretation through current gestures. Whether assistance preserves that interpretation, relocates it, partially preserves it or replaces it depends on the route and role. A change of medium cannot establish the answer by itself.

The stronger identification is consequently bounded: establish what participants actually attend to, select, execute and answer, through which existing routes, over which relevant possibilities. Preserve the differences between unused ability and present exercise, capacity to respond and actual mutual response, and an output and its formation. Reports, observed synchrony and a causal diagram each require interpretation; none alone establishes the ethical value of the relation they identify.

### Difficulty must not substitute for the activity

Technical demand, available capacity, felt effort and aversive burden can vary differently. Practice can make a technically unchanged passage easier while retaining or improving present interpretive exercise. Assistance can reduce unwanted strain while carrying the same relevant choices. Another method of reducing effort can bypass the selected exercise. Therefore lower effort alone neither certifies improved realization nor demonstrates lost achievement.

Where the independently identified project is unassisted execution of a particular technique, a device doing that technique changes the project. Where the project concerns joint phrasing, the same device can preserve its relevant exercise. This classification requires evidence about the actual practice, selected roles and legitimate interpretation. It does not justify rewriting the target after intervention, preserving difficulty merely for its own sake, or prohibiting participants from legitimately choosing another activity.

The source continuation retains a real further rival: exertion itself might contribute value even when aid preserves skill and relieves pain. The headache comparison contests that claim; it does not refute it by definition. An account valuing challenge must specify whether it concerns objective demands, experienced effort, competent achievement or an aversive obstacle. Those are different propositions, and apparent disagreement may disappear when the target is clarified.

### The positive appraisal and its retained limitation

The argument offers a candidate present good in making an interpretation together: contributions receive uptake and the shared performance exists through the participants' differentiated activity. It does not wait for greater future productivity to acquire that proposed significance. Support that preserves this relation while reducing an independently warranted burden can then realize the inherited favorable comparison when its other premises hold.

This remains a substantive value claim. A rival may grant every identified control relation yet hold that the participants' relevant welfare depends only on experience or the delivered result. Condemning deception or breached commitment does not by itself refute that account of welfare. Conversely, identifying such a rival does not show that actual shared expression lacks value. The point of disagreement is whether that realized relation contributes good, not whether a waveform proves who performed it. Participants can intentionally execute phrases instrumentally for music or enjoyment while being indifferent to mutual authorship as an intrinsic end. Under a coherent, admitted realization of all these conditions, the strong attitude-independent welfare thesis predicts a participant benefit from the actual live relation. ### Five conclusions cannot substitute for one another

An actual relation may occur without being good; a relation may be an impersonal excellence without making its indifferent participant better off; a participant's benefit may give someone a contributory reason without settling their overall choice; and a reason need not authorize coercive preservation or unrestricted intervention. These distinctions concern one practical history, not separate metaphysical realms.

An observer's apt admiration for a skilled joint performance can support a proposed impersonal appraisal. It does not by itself prove the performers' wellbeing increased. Likewise, an informed preference for listening or rest cannot simply be treated as a welfare error because active participation is available. A stronger theory must explain the claimed benefit and its relation to those alternatives. The account need not deny a possible aspectual good merely because it is outweighed, but it also cannot preserve an unsupported increment by making it arbitrarily small.
