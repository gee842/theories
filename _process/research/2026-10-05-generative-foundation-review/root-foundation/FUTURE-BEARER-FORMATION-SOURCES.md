## Harman: option-dependent bearers and reasons

Elizabeth Harman, “Can We Harm and Benefit in Creating?”, *Philosophical Perspectives* 18(1) (2004), 89–113, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1520-8583.2004.00022.x). [Preserved author-linked PDF](../../../../sources/37-ethical-minimum-primary-2026-10-06/future-formation/harman-2004-author-linked.pdf).

**Source synopsis:** Harman rejects making counterfactual worsening necessary for harm. Parallel benefits can arise for different future people, while serious harm supplies a reason against one formation route (§2, 91–94). Her §8 makes alternatives and competing reasons part of permissibility, so harm alone is not its complete explanation. Section 6 endorses reasons to create happy children while denying an obligation. Section 10 distinguishes worthwhile further benefit from required super-conservation. Section 11 evaluates prospective harm and status within each option, rather than filtering options through an actual-population list. Her species-health comparison and classification of deafness as harm (§§3, 5) remain substantive commitments; we do not adopt them. These are ethical arguments, not premise-free derivations.

## Parfit: comparative formation and the available alternative

Derek Parfit, “Future People, the Non-Identity Problem, and Person-Affecting Principles”, *Philosophy & Public Affairs* 45(2) (2017), 118–157, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/papa.12088). [Preserved journal-layout PDF](../../../../sources/37-ethical-minimum-primary-2026-10-06/future-formation/parfit-2017-journal-layout.pdf).

**Source synopsis:** Parfit separates same-person, same-number/different-person and different-number comparisons. His Sarah–Clare contrast tests whether the same resulting life is chosen when a better formation alternative is available (§§I–II). The strong narrow principles exclude worsening or wrongness when nobody is worse off; his wide principle instead compares how good outcomes are for their respective people. He defends intrinsic existential benefits without assigning welfare to nonexistence (129–132). We do not adopt that broader creation-benefit thesis or infer duties from outcome ranking. His discussion of Boonin is not an independent reading of Boonin.

## What this tests in our account

Our new interface is the comparison of feasible formation histories whose later bearers differ. An affected-bearer domain must therefore be reconstructed within each history. A fixed actual-person filter would omit part of what the generative account is trying to explain. This identification requirement does not itself supply ethical standing, interpersonal comparability, the weight of a reason, or a rule for permissible action.

Our comparison does not treat Parfit's alternative-sensitive challenge as a decisive refutation of Harman's reason-level claim: her §8 already includes alternatives in the complete judgment.

The bounded application keeps existing claims, costs and permissions in view, and compares worthwhile qualities that would actually be realized under each option. It neither treats nonexistence as a patient's welfare state nor assumes a duty to produce another bearer. Whether the wider formation is ethically better remains a substantive comparison of those histories. The difference between a reason to prefer it and a requirement to choose it remains open wherever the additional premises have not been defended.

## Frick: conditional concern does not supply selection by itself

Johann Frick, “Conditional Reasons and the Procreation Asymmetry”, *Philosophical Perspectives* 34(1) (2020), 53–87, [DOI and primary publisher page](https://doi.org/10.1111/phpe.12139). The [link-only provenance and reading record](../../../../sources/37-ethical-minimum-primary-2026-10-06/conditional-future-concern/README.md) distinguishes successful selected publisher extraction from unsuccessful original-byte retrieval.

Frick separates conditional standards for future lives from an additional selection principle (§§6–9). Threshold protection and same-person improvement alone do not yield different-person preference. His stronger asymmetry denies an unconditional welfare reason to create happy people; our claim that no creation duty follows does not adopt that denial. The source's inference rules, maximization and selection proposals retain their substantive ethical grounds.

**Our use:** the A/R/B control in [§8](FUTURE-BEARER-FORMATION-ARGUMENT.md#8-conditional-concern-and-selection-across-future-lives) separates a reason concerning one future bearer from a reason to select a different future bearer. Frick supplies a substantive comparator for that distinction; we do not attribute this uninvolved-responder case or our global-filter control to him. The case remains to justify without importing a parent's or promisor's prior role. Beneficiary-relative concern need not imply an ontologically primitive beneficiary, and this source does not characterize a whole philosophical culture.
