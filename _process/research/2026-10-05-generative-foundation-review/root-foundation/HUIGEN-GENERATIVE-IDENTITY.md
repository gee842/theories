# Generative identity, future concern and inherited reasons

6 October 2026. AI-authored continuation of the [entire 慧根 objective](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/HUIGEN.md). The named transition is **a formed center's reason to avoid its future harm → concern for plural continuations → reasons that successors and other deliberators inherit**. This is a hand-proved conceptual study of declared histories and normative premises. It supplies no empirical demonstration that persons can undergo the stipulated fission, no new Lean verification and no claim to have derived all ethical standing.

**The positive result:** if adding an equally situated continuation preserves an existing future patient's sufficient reason-ground, and judgments respect the resulting branch symmetry, the original actor has reasons concerning every branch. With at least two numerically distinct future persons, concern cannot require numerical identity with that actor. A further valid continuation of the *same responsibility for the same patients* transfers those reasons to successors, including reasons concerning their siblings. The outstanding question is therefore sharper than whether the self is generatively constituted: **which grounds survive changing the patient, and which authority survives changing the deliberator?**

The [reason-transport study](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) already characterizes exact closure under given implications. This study investigates a candidate justification for specific implications. Its new diagnostic is the difference between preserving a patient-indexed reason and rebinding the word “my” in the same program. The two operations diverge at fission even when every record is accurate.

## 1. Four relations that generation does not identify

Let a history contain stages of deliberators, independently admitted patients and the circumstances in which they can fare well or badly. Use:

- \(C(x,y)\): \(x\) causally contributes to forming \(y\);
- \(K(x,y)\): \(y\) is an admitted organizational or psychological continuation of \(x\), under an explicitly stated criterion;
- \(x\equiv y\): the proposed relation of numerical *person* identity across stages;
- \(R(a,p,c)\): a patient-grounded fact gives deliberator \(a\) a pro tanto reason in context \(c\).

These have different types and different justifications. A causal contribution can be made by a tool or an inert resource. A continuation criterion can hold between one source and several distinct centers. Numerical identity, if used in its ordinary equivalence sense, cannot identify one person with two persons that are not identical with one another. A practical reason can concern someone who is neither its deliberator nor that deliberator's successor.

Stage labels alone cannot settle personal identity: different time-stamped records are different tokens even in an uncontroversial case of one continuing person. The identity test below uses \(\equiv\), not that trivial token difference. It assumes that the simultaneous future patients in the test are distinct persons in the relevant individuation. If a theory rejects that individuation, it must explain how it represents their separately experienced consequences and independently exercisable agency; merely giving them a common label does not answer the question.

An actual history containing two future patients also differs from uncertainty about which one patient will exist. The following construction concerns plural actual continuations. It does not assign a probability that one branch will be the “real me,” or turn mutually exclusive possibilities into additional actual sufferers.

The [relational-self genealogy](../../../../theories/relational-self-and-datong/README.md) makes constitution through relations central to Ren's account. The [ethical foundation](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md) separately identifies N1 standing, N2 relevant consistency and N3 answerability. Those commitments motivate this test; relational constitution by itself is not substituted for their justification.

## 2. A positive future-to-plural-concern theorem

Consider a baseline history \(h_1\). A present deliberator \(a\), who need not now be in pain, has a pro tanto reason concerning a future patient's admitted aversive condition. Call the patient \(q\). The reason's offered ground includes whatever continuity, experience, context or responsibility is actually needed; none may be silently dropped.

Now consider a stipulated plural-continuation history \(h_n\), with \(n\ge2\), the same present deliberator and patients \(q_1,\ldots,q_n\). The construction carries the original ground to \(q_1\), and the future patients are symmetric in the respects admitted as relevant. This is a conditional comparison of histories, not an assertion that adding a branch leaves every real physical cost unchanged.

State the premises separately:

**F — Future anchor.** \(R_{h_1}(a,q)\) holds. A presently unexperienced future harm can supply a present reason under its admitted ground.

**N — Ground-preserving addition.** If the move to \(h_n\) preserves that sufficient ground, its applicability and the absence of a defeating condition for \(q_1\), then \(R_{h_n}(a,q_1)\) remains true. Merely adding the other continuations is not itself a defeater.

**S — Relevant branch symmetry.** There is a group of symmetries of the full admitted reason-relevant context that fixes \(a\), acts transitively on \(\{q_1,\ldots,q_n\}\), and preserves reason validity. Actions and consequences are carried with the patients when they are part of the reason. Thus \(R_{h_n}(a,q_i)\) implies \(R_{h_n}(a,\sigma q_i)\).

**G1 — Nonexclusive future concern.** F, an applicable N and S imply

\[
\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad R_{h_n}(a,q_i).
\]

**Proof.** F and N give the reason concerning \(q_1\) in the plural history. For each \(q_i\), transitivity supplies a context symmetry taking \(q_1\) to \(q_i\). S carries the positive reason to that patient. ∎

**G1a — Numerical identity cannot be a necessary gate.** Suppose two such patients satisfy \(q_1\not\equiv q_2\). Then the reasons established by G1 cannot all satisfy the proposed restriction \(R(a,p)\Rightarrow a\equiv p\).

**Proof.** The restriction would give \(a\equiv q_1\) and \(a\equiv q_2\). Symmetry and transitivity of numerical identity would imply \(q_1\equiv q_2\), a contradiction. ∎

This establishes more than uncertainty about private exposure. A reason survives when both future patients really exist and their separateness is known. The earlier [identity-revelation diagnostic](CONCERN-IDENTITY-DIAGNOSTIC-NOTE.md) shows why apparent other-protection based only on uncertainty does not establish this result.

G1 does not impose equal numerical welfare weights, conservation of a fixed total quantity of concern, or a command to satisfy incompatible claims simultaneously. It establishes positive standing. An inability to protect both does not refute either reason. Duplicating a record of one patient is also not creating a second patient; representational duplication must not inflate the number of claimants.

### The premises do real work

F is not a theorem of causal succession. An interpretation assigning no reasons at all respects every symmetry. N excludes a possible view under which unique succession is essential to prudential standing and all such reasons disappear at a split. S excludes preserving only a specially named branch when the name has no admitted relevance. These are independent ways for the displayed inference to fail: no anchor; branch-caused annihilation; or branch favoritism. Their logical availability does not establish their normative justification.

The positive defense of N is strongest when the complete ground has actually been identified: the same patient still undergoes the same admitted loss; the same continuity and relevant responsibility remain; no conflict or defeating fact has been introduced; only another center has acquired an equivalent claim. Deleting the first reason would then require a further grounding difference. If *uniqueness* was part of the original sufficient ground, that defense has not yet succeeded. Calling it arbitrary cannot replace examining why uniqueness was thought to matter.

Similarly, S is stronger than exchanging names while leaving the physical and relational situation asymmetric. A retained special responsibility, different anticipated harm, or different causal power can break the symmetry. The theorem gives a controlled challenge to numerical exclusivity, not a license to ignore relevant relationships.

## 3. Why concern for both branches does not yet bind each branch

Changing the patient while holding the actor fixed differs from changing the actor while holding the patient fixed. G1 concerns the first operation. To expose the second, suppress a fixed currently valid context and write a continuation-indexed interpretation:

\[
R_K(a,p)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H(p)\ \land\ p\in S(a),
\qquad
S(a)=\{p:K(a,p)\}.
\tag{G2}
\]

Here \(H\) is a true admitted adverse ground, and \(K\) is a reflexive, transitive authorized-continuation relation. Its authority as a restriction on reasons is a *supplied normative interpretation*; the causal history alone does not justify it. The same formula applies to every actor, and each actor can accurately represent every patient's condition and the entire formula. This is neither factual ignorance nor an inability to reflect on its own reasons.

For a simple fission, let \(a\) have continuers \(b,c\), with later patients \(p_b,p_c\) in their respective lines. An unrelated patient \(u\) can have the same admitted harm and no power to affect the actors. The interpretation gives:

| Deliberator | Later patient \(p_b\) | Later patient \(p_c\) | Patient \(u\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ancestor \(a\) | reason | reason | no reason under G2 |
| Successor \(b\) | reason | no reason under G2 | no reason under G2 |
| Successor \(c\) | no reason under G2 | reason | no reason under G2 |

The ancestor's concern is nonexclusive and branch symmetric. The restriction is no longer numerical identity: an actor can have reasons about several distinct future patients. Nevertheless, this rule does not grant a successor a reason about its sibling. G1a therefore defeats one specific gate while leaving a stricter-than-universal relational gate available.

The precise structural fact is that descendant scopes ordinarily **contract forward**. If \(K(a,b)\), transitivity gives \(S(b)\subseteq S(a)\). That inclusion carries a descendant's in-scope patient back into the ancestor's scope. It does not preserve all of the ancestor's patients in every descendant's scope.

**G2a — Exact inheritance condition for a scoped reason rule.** Let \(D\) be the patient domain whose grounds and context remain valid across the actor transition \(a\leadsto b\). Under G2, fixed-patient reason inheritance

\[
\forall p\in D,\quad R_K(a,p)\Rightarrow R_K(b,p)
\]

holds exactly when

\[
S(a)\cap D\cap\{p:H(p)\}
\ \subseteq\ S(b).
\tag{G3}
\]

**Proof.** Expanding the two occurrences of G2 in the implication leaves precisely the displayed inclusion, since the same \(H(p)\) holds before and after. ∎

Where \(S(b)\subseteq S(a)\), this requires equality of the two scopes on the continuing harmed patients. At a genuine fission with a surviving sibling claim, the containment normally goes the wrong way. This is the exact distinction that the phrase “successor consistency” can otherwise obscure.

The countermodel is consistent as a single actor-indexed reason rule. It can maintain unchanged grounds and evaluations through a continuation whose patient scope remains unchanged. It **does not** satisfy a stronger rule that every successor inherits every still-valid reason addressed to the predecessor. Nor does it prove that a predecessor's global plan will remain the freely chosen plan of every branch. Claiming either would silently discard the countermodel's defining change of scope. A theory requiring such stronger continuity can reject G2 for that reason, provided it justifies the requirement.

This is a model of the listed premises, not a proof that lineage-restricted concern is morally justified. The [practical-necessity audit](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md) gives the same distinction for the richer scientific and reflective capacities: a logically coherent rival locates a missing justification; it does not receive equal moral warrant merely by being coherent.

## 4. A constructive successor theorem with a preserved constituency

The strongest positive route is to specify what is inherited before asking whether inheritance occurred. Let a reason certificate have content

\[
\tau=(p,\text{ground},\text{reason kind},\text{validity conditions},r),
\]

where \(r\) is a justified continuing deliberative role. The patient is named independently of whichever actor currently occupies the role. An office entrusted with protecting named patients is one possible application; whether personal continuation supplies a comparable authority requires its own argument.

Use a **role-validity premise**: when the ground is valid and undefeated, every legitimate current occupant of \(r\) has the reason concerning this patient. A **succession premise** says that the particular successor actually continues that role and its responsibility for the relevant patient domain. Neither premise follows from copying the certificate's bytes or acquiring the predecessor's preferences.

**G4 — Cross-branch inherited reasons.** Suppose G1 grants the ancestor reasons concerning all \(q_i\), those reasons are valid claims within one such continuing role, and every successor \(b_j\) legitimately continues that role for these patients. If their grounds remain applicable and undefeated, then

\[
\forall j\ \forall i,\qquad R(b_j,q_i).
\]

**Proof.** G1 supplies each patient claim. For any successor \(b_j\), the role and succession premises apply to that same claim and patient, yielding its addressed reason. The argument applies to every pair. ∎

For \(i\ne j\), the result includes another branch's patient. A patient need not communicate or deliberate: the claim is inherited because its actual ground and the successor's responsibility remain, not because the patient signs a new agreement. A noncommunicating successor patient can therefore be covered by this route without constructing a self-anchor from that patient's own deliberation.

G4 is stronger than preserving the formula “care about my future.” Evaluating that formula anew with actor parameter \(b_j\) yields G2's smaller scope. A claim about \(q_i\) has been replaced by a different claim if its patient index is rebound. The same code, sincerely endorsed at both stages, can perform that replacement. Conversely, different code can faithfully preserve the same warranted patient claim.

The role premises have a substantive positive defense where succession includes the authority to continue making decisions for those very patients. It is difficult to claim continuity of that authority while treating an unchanged admitted claim as discharged merely by replacing its holder. The [no-unilateral-discharge argument](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md) applies: a procedure cannot truthfully declare an admitted objection settled while that objection remains undefeated. But the scope and legitimacy of the claimed authority still need justification. Being a physical descendant does not automatically constitute assuming an office or undertaking an obligation.

This also limits inherited commitments. A predecessor's harmful, mistaken or expired endorsement does not become valid because it is ancient. A successor can challenge the ground, relevance, authority, circumstances or a warranted overriding reason. Preserving a reason is compatible with warranted revision. Nor does inheriting two reasons prove that both can be satisfied; N4 and the existing support results still govern material possibilities.

## 5. Merging and common origins test the remaining scope

**Merging does not create reverse inheritance.** Add a later continuer \(m\) with \(K(b,m)\) and \(K(c,m)\). The transitive interpretation G2 grants both branches reasons concerning \(m\)'s relevant future patients. It does not thereby grant \(b\) a reason concerning \(c\)'s separate pre-merge patient. A common future is not a reverse continuation edge. If \(m\) legitimately inherits two roles, G4's role argument can preserve both sets of still-valid claims. That conclusion comes from warranted role continuity, not from the physical presence of two memory archives in one machine.

**Common causal ancestry is insufficient.** Consider a single causally earlier resource \(g\) contributing to the formation of the ancestor's family and of patient \(u\). Thus both have paths from \(g\) in \(C\), while \(u\notin S(a)\). All factual statements remain true under G2, which still excludes \(u\). The resource need not itself be a patient or deliberator and therefore need not supply any positive prudential anchor. Even if the common ancestor is a deliberator, the interpretation's scope contraction shows why its broad reasons need not be inherited by every descendant. A common root does not solve that actor transition.

**Any causal path is too broad a substitute for a reason-ground.** Causal outputs can include an organism, waste heat, an instrument and a harmful dependency. The proposed patient domain and admitted harms still have to be identified. This does not deny value to nonexperiencing entities; it says that their value requires a relevant ground rather than an untyped causal arrow. Replacing \(K\) with all of \(C\) in G2 would be a changed normative rule, not a theorem about the previous rule.

**A common role can have broader scope than a lineage.** An arrangement exercising consequential power over a previously unrelated patient can acquire reasons under independently justified N3 answerability. This route can reach \(u\) without pretending that \(u\) is the actor's future self. It is one reason the full theory should retain plural centers rather than solve concern by identifying all patients with one individual. Such inclusion advances the ethical argument only when N3's scope and authority are defended, including those unable to resist the arrangement.

## 6. What the actual reconstruction projects would have to preserve

The accepted [recorded-reasons construction](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/RECORDED-REASONS.md) already separates retained bytes, calibrated factual observations, supplied reason relevance and a charged successor audit. Its generic context/node predicates can encode all the indices used here. No defect in that formal interface is alleged.

A proposed implementation of G4 would have to preserve or re-establish:

1. the same patient and reason kind, including whether a pronoun is a fixed referent or a parameter;
2. the independently justified ground and its present validity conditions;
3. the actor's current role, actual authority and the justified patient constituency;
4. any relevant new defeater, conflict or correction;
5. the physical access, reconstruction cost, action means and timing needed for effective use.

These conditions distinguish semantic fidelity from successful byte copying. A cold successor can reconstruct an earlier claim but still lack the relevant role, control or reason to adopt it. An authorized successor can have a valid reason despite losing access to its record. A usable implementation therefore needs both the semantic warrant and the material route; neither can be certified solely by the other.

The [revisable-concern settlement](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/REVISABLE-CONCERN-SETTLEMENT.md) supplies explicit evaluative and normative inputs. The present analysis identifies a stronger succession question those inputs can be used to test. It does not retroactively claim that the settlement derived all successor authority or universal patient standing.

## 7. The exact advance toward the full necessary root

| Step | Positive consequence | Premise still to justify |
|---|---|---|
| Present concern about a future adverse experience | A reason need not coincide with present sensation | The future anchor and its complete ground |
| Preserved ground under adding symmetric continuers | Reasons concerning each distinct branch | Non-erasure and actual relevant symmetry |
| Two branches with distinct personal identities | Strict numerical identity cannot be necessary for every such reason | The patient individuation and G1's premises |
| Same warranted claims and legitimate role through succession | Cross-sibling reasons for each successor | Fixed constituency and valid actor inheritance |
| An unrelated, dispensable or noncommunicating patient | Inclusion when the actual ground and actor responsibility extend to that patient | Patient-ground sufficiency or justified answerability with that scope |

The most constructive next argument asks what grounds the original future reason. If its complete sufficient ground is the patient's adverse experience plus the actor's relevant capacity, and continued numerical or genealogical identity contributes neither that ground nor a valid defeater, the unrelated matched patient should retain the reason. G1 removes numerical identity as a universal gate under its premises, making that further examination more focused. It does not already remove every relation between actor and patient. The defender of G2 must explain why authorized continuity is reason-grounding rather than merely the source of a powerful motive; the defender of universal concern must explain why that restriction fails.

A result that would change the full-necessity account is an independently warranted argument that any claimant to continuing practical authority must preserve still-valid patient claims under fission, merge, role succession and interaction with a separately constituted patient. If that argument covers the theory's target patient and actor domains without silently assuming shared standing, it supplies a new necessary premise whose consequences can enter the [whole-root closure theorem](HUIGEN-GENERATIVE-CLOSURE.md). A finite family of branches can illustrate that result, but cannot by itself establish its universal domain.

Conversely, a rival that preserves the offered complete grounds and the same independently justified authority while denying the inherited reason would refute the proposed bridge. G2 is already a countermodel to a weaker inference from causal constitution, future concern and branch symmetry alone. It is not a countermodel once fixed-constituency role inheritance is added; it violates exactly that premise. Establishing why the stronger premise is necessary, rather than merely desirable or sometimes applicable, remains the research obligation.

The arguments G1–G4 are elementary deductions displayed in full, not claims of mathematical priority. The history is stipulated and the validity premises are explicit. No existing scientific checks were rerun for this note, and no code or prior result was altered. This study advances the root by separating a real implication beyond exclusive self-concern from the still-unclosed transition to universally shared reasons.
