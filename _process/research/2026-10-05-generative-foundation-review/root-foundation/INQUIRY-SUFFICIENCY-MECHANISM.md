Named connection: **acquired distinctions → sufficiency for an actual further activity**, beyond the present routine's requirement to identify one candidate. This continues [inquiry-interface reconstruction](INQUIRY-INTERFACE-MECHANISM.md). It analyzes existing records and their stored transition tables without importing the models, rerunning native dynamics, adding a controller or claiming a new general information theorem.

The existing history yields a positive, exact distinction. Two surviving candidates already determine the next reading, but disagree one step later. The actual agreed action/read then advances both candidates, so the *new* next reading becomes uncertain. A separate reached endpoint retains an exact model while lacking the stock required for any further native command. These are three different questions: whether a claim is settled, what its horizon is, and whether the relevant activity can occur.

The latter's §5 already warns that inquiry changes physical conditions and feasible menus, so narrowing interpretations alone need not preserve practical possibilities.

The present contribution is an exact application and dynamic witness from the acquired method history, not rediscovery of those general results. For a nonempty current candidate set \(B\), and a specified next-reading question \(F\), an exact common answer exists precisely when \(F\) is constant on \(B\). A singleton is sufficient, but not necessary. This conclusion concerns the actual process only if its relevant state/law remains represented in \(B\), its observation interpretation is appropriate, and the planned interaction has the stated meaning. Agreement in an inadequate family need not be true agreement with the world.

For an action choice, common prediction is not yet common feasibility or common warrant. The applicable condition depends on the independently specified continuation and its consequences. Prior license-intersection results are not a new utility function, nor do they produce their own action menu, grounds or executing mechanism.

Under the existing [Reviser step](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/acquisition/access/revision/revision.py), update has both parts:

\[
B' = \{T_a(b):b\in B,\ O(T_a(b))=o\}.
\]

Here a candidate includes its cursor, \(T_a\) propagates that cursor, and the observation restricts the propagated set. Describing the change only as filtering an unchanged set omits the first part. Candidate labels can remain unchanged while their relevant future implications change.

A bounded corollary locates what does carry forward. If every candidate agrees on the first \(k+\ell\) readings of the specified action sequence, then after its first \(k\) readings occur and the candidates are propagated and soundly filtered, every survivor agrees on the remaining \(\ell\) readings. This is obtained by taking the suffix of their common predicted word. Agreement only on the initial \(k\) readings gives no such guarantee for another \(\ell\); the reached pair above supplies the concrete failure. The horizon and action sequence in this statement are explicit inputs, not a newly acquired practical priority.

Accurate next use, exact future representation and continued feasibility must not be collapsed into one success label.

These cases do **not** establish that the inquiry was irrational or should have stopped earlier. Such a verdict needs the actual activity at issue, available alternatives and relevant consequences identified independently. The current source has no passive deadline or ongoing support demand that selects an optimal inquiry horizon for it. An analyst minimizing this local fuel quantity would supply an additional aim. Depletion is one demonstrated consequence of activity here; money, payment and universally scarce additive resources are not generative primitives.

## 6. What this closes and what remains to be realized

The source supports a precise positive relation: the acquired record can already settle a particular continuation without isolating one whole model, and later activity can transform the condition relative to which another continuation must be assessed. The question, current state and actual feasibility participate together; none can be omitted merely because the record becomes more discriminating. This clarifies what a criterion of sufficient inquiry would have to track.

It does not yet realize a revised operative sufficiency criterion. The current learner still uses its supplied singleton condition. The continuation or horizon whose agreement should end inquiry is also not acquired through the effects of inquiry. A mechanism that simply installs “stop when all candidates agree on the next bit” would choose one new supplied rule and could fail at the following step shown here. A fixed multi-step horizon would move the same explanatory question rather than answer it.

The next central relation remains **effects of inquiry on the relevant continuing activity → formation or revision of the operative sufficiency criterion**. A substantive result must identify that activity and what inquiry maintains, changes or interrupts; show the consequence reaches the actual criterion; and show the revised condition subsequently governs further activity, including adverse or misleading revisions. This need not take the form of a higher controller, a new scalar score or a separately encoded philosophical rule. Source omission of this relation is not evidence that it is external to generative reality, nor a general impossibility theorem.
