## 1. The claim must remain the one Ren selected

Ren's original clarification was that a philosophy claiming scientific standing must be coherent with this theory. The present selected question is why scientific philosophy must satisfy it. Coherence is weaker than endorsement: a view can leave a claim open without contradicting it. A different notation can express the same relationship. Conversely, two views can agree and both be wrong.

There are consequently four different demands:

| Demand | Its actual burden |
|---|---|
| Preserve a particular established relationship | Identify the same target and show that denying the relationship changes a warranted answer. |
| Be compatible with the generative starting point | State the starting point precisely enough to distinguish a genuine denial from another representation of it. |
| Be compatible with the entire theory | Establish why no scientifically adequate rival can negate any of its substantive commitments. |
| Endorse the entire theory | Establish positive warrant for accepting all those commitments; noncontradiction alone is insufficient. |

The first two are the most promising route toward the third. They must not be treated as already proving it.

“Scientific” also cannot mean “already correct in every assertion.” A corrigible scientific proposal can contain a mistake. The charitable target is therefore a **fully adequate philosophy of the relevant inquiry**, or a requirement that a mistaken proposal correct its established conflict. This study does not classify every false hypothesis as pseudoscience or define science by allegiance to this repository.

## 2. The strongest affirmative argument

Start with an actual episode of inquiry, not with an isolated knower facing an independent world. A preparation changes what can be encountered; contact leaves an available difference; retention and comparison make some conclusions supportable; a further result can alter the assessment while preserving what was previously asserted. Instruments, participants, records and the investigated changes are differentiated roles in that episode. The decomposition preserves the dependencies relevant to the question; it does not claim to exhaust the whole process.

The affirmative argument has five steps.

1. **The target includes real achievements and failures.** An account undertaking to explain scientific correction must distinguish a new observation from a repeated record, a withdrawn ground from evidence of the opposite claim, and a changed conclusion from a changed question. These distinctions have consequences independently of the word “generative.” The completed knowledge studies supply concrete realizations and failure cases.
2. **Those differences require corresponding relationships.** If precisely the same complete available record is read by precisely the same rule, it cannot yield two different specified conclusions. If an update is the identity on the complete state relevant to a conclusion, that conclusion cannot change through that update. The new Lean results check these elementary implications. They do not assume that a particular observed record is complete, deterministic or infallible.
3. **An adequate explanation must retain the relations that make its target differ.** A description that merges materially different access histories may falsely turn copied error into corroboration. A description that omits the conditions of an intervention may mistake changed measurement for changed production. A description that erases branch dependence may present incompatible opportunities as available together. These are substantive errors about the claimed target.
4. **This is positive content of the generative account.** Activity changes what later activity can encounter, distinguish, do and correct. Our account unites that dependency with the formation of the knowing positions themselves. It earns explanatory content where it specifies and supports these connections, including destructive and failed continuations.
5. **The same requirement applies reflexively.** An argument against these dependencies must have an account of its own evidential and inferential bearing. A global denial of every difference capable of affecting evidence cannot coherently claim that a newly acquired result corrected its conclusion. But an alternative account of those differences, or an account of a narrower target, is not such a denial.

The resulting necessity is **target-relative and invariant under adequate redescription**. It concerns the preserved content, not a requirement to repeat our terminology. The first premise and the correspondence of the selected state/record description require substantive warrant. The fifth step is a scoped philosophical argument about a practice, not a theorem that every possible mind or reality has been exhaustively characterized.

### Why this is more than calling everything generative

The content can exclude actual descriptions. Counting two copied reports as two independently vulnerable routes is wrong when their production history has only one vulnerable route. Claiming that one post-intervention state retains two options is wrong when the options belong to incompatible successor states. Claiming that a model's old validation covers a new setting is wrong when the relevant production relation changes there.

These exclusions do not depend on deciding the rival's philosophical label. They also constrain us. A generative account committing the same errors fails the same test.

## 4. Can the whole theory follow from the conditions of inquiry?

Four proposed escalations need separate assessment.

**From happening within relations to full ontological monism.** The inquiry itself cannot be explained by pretending that its evidence and uptake have no relationship. It does not follow from this that every feature of reality has been derived from a unique minimal ontology. A whole account must explain how the distinctions it uses arise and remain connected; the particular global identification remains an explanatory commitment to defend. This limitation does not posit two independent realities. It identifies how far the present argument reaches within the one process under investigation.

**From ordered dependence to metaphysical becoming.** An atemporal constraint can preserve which record corresponds to which preparation and what would differ under another preparation. The Lean graph construction checks this elementary preservation. It is not a complete block-universe theory, but it blocks the inference that using a static mathematical presentation already denies the represented change. The physical occurrence and the representation of its ordered relations are different explanatory questions. Common relational content may be required without one presentation having established metaphysical priority.

**From using inquiry to valuing all generation.** An inquirer can have a reason to maintain the conditions of this inquiry because the answer matters for a particular concern. That use alone does not establish that all living realization and further generation have basic positive normative significance. To extend the reason, explain why its ground applies beyond the selected purpose and position. The established G0 route does that work through an explicit starting commitment and further comparison principles; the mere occurrence of competent inference is not its proof.

**From G0 to every practical commitment.** G0 is authorized and retained. J1–J4 and correspondence E remain separately stated. Accepting a basic positive significance does not by itself fix every priority, responsibility or feasible response. A demand that all scientific philosophy satisfy the complete package must defend these additional commitments as well. This is not a new requirement to prove G0 without axioms before using it; it is the burden of the stronger, universal necessity claim now being tested.

There is a particularly important distinction here: **a principle can have universal normative scope under an accepted foundation without every competent reasoner necessarily endorsing it or every scientific method logically entailing it.** The existing alignment argument retains the former scope. The present audit concerns the latter entailment and compatibility demands.

## 5. Two substantive primary-source comparisons

### A rival that preserves generation while withholding ontology

In *Constructive Empiricism Now* (2001), van Fraassen describes instruments as arrangements that produce new observable phenomena. He also distinguishes accepting a scientific theory from believing its whole account of unobservables, and treats correction as central to fallible inquiry. Thus a serious rival can explain evidential production while withholding a wider ontology. Reading scope: introduction, the instrument-production discussion on printed pp. 154–155, and acceptance/belief discussion on pp. 164–167. His particular observable/unobservable boundary is contested and is not adopted here. [Author-hosted primary paper](https://www.princeton.edu/~fraassen/articles/pdfs/CE_Now.pdf).

**Our assessment:** this is a substantive alternative to the inference from effective inquiry to mandatory endorsement of our ontology. It is **not** automatically a counterexample to compatibility with the relational core: withholding belief is not denying it. If our minimal claim concerns the production and dependence he preserves, there is convergence. If our claim requires his assent to every ontological thesis, that inference needs more argument. It would be circular to disqualify his view as unscientific solely because it declines that assent.

### A substantive disagreement over what has basic value

In *Utilitarianism*, chapter II, Mill makes pleasure and freedom from pain the ends by which other goods are assessed; he also distinguishes qualities of pleasure and gives higher faculties an important place. Chapter IV discusses acquired ends and habitual willing. This is a serious evaluative account, not the caricature that only crude sensations matter. Reading scope: the specified chapter II passages and chapter IV discussion of ends and will, not the full book or a vindication of its argument. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/11224/11224-h/11224-h.htm).

**Our assessment:** a strict experiential-value rival can preserve the same biological, social and evidential formation while denying independent basic value to generation apart from its place in valuable experience. Its point of disagreement with a broader G0 reading is substantive. Whether a particular living realization has significance beyond that experiential basis cannot be settled by noting that both theories involve generative activity. Nor does the paper prove the rival correct. Mill's richer account may overlap with G0 in many concrete judgments; disagreement must be identified at an actual claim, not manufactured from a label.

The decisive stress test is therefore conceptual rather than a newly observed experiment: hold fixed the full relevant experiential and practical consequences as far as the comparison warrants, then ask whether a further living/generative realization contributes a basic reason. If those conditions are impossible, explain their inseparability; if they are possible, defend the positive reason. Merely adding an unconstrained `Good` bit to a smaller model would not test that constitutive claim. No such bit is introduced in this round.

These comparisons assess routes of inference. They do not establish that all their authors' positions are scientifically adequate, that empirical evidence can never bear on ethics, or that the complete theory is false.

## 6. The exact logical burden of universal compatibility

The formal audit uses a deliberately general semantic interface. A `View W` selects the possibilities satisfying a position's claims. `Compatible p q` means that there is at least one common satisfying possibility. `Entails p q` means every possibility satisfying `p` also satisfies `q`. These definitions concern joint satisfiability, not merely the absence of an obvious verbal contradiction.

Let `A` be an independently specified admissible class and `T` the theory. The central checked equivalence is:

> Every view having a realization in A is compatible with T **if and only if** every realization in A satisfies T.

The forward direction tests a view identifying one admissible realization. The reverse direction uses an admissible common witness. This is an elementary logical audit, not a claimed new discovery in logic.

Its philosophical force is to expose the burden: compatibility with every admissible alternative cannot serve as a shortcut around establishing the relevant commitments of T over that class. The class must not be defined by accepting T. The proof does **not** identify A with science. Its converse quantifies over all predicate views, including singleton views; whether an actual class of scientific philosophies is rich enough for the same argument is an additional requirement. For a restricted class, a separate result explicitly shows that compatibility with the one admitted rival is insufficient for universal compatibility.

Other checked distinctions are useful here. Compatibility need not entail accepting the theory. Two positions may be compatible while sharing an error about the actual case. Entailment of a package requires entailment of each conjunct, so the ethical commitment cannot disappear inside a proof of its empirical component. A changed readout requires a difference at the specified complete record interface, and a genuinely changed conclusion excludes an identity update there. An equivalent relational graph preserves that update without deciding which ontology is primary.

The formal possibilities are not a census of real worlds; the functions are not a derivation of actual scientific cognition. There is no new `Scientific`, `Understands` or normative-verdict predicate. Applicability, measurement, philosophical warrant and the real generative configuration remain substantive obligations.

**“The necessary core is too thin to distinguish our theory.”** It is thin at the highest level. Its scientific value comes from identifying the particular relationships and their consequences. A rival preserving them can be compatible without being our theory. Distinctive explanatory success requires additional support, which the local studies pursue. Necessity and uniqueness should not be conflated to conceal this.

**“If everything is generative, no rival can contradict the theory.”** Then the broad word alone has little discriminatory force. Require a definite contradiction: which dependency, formation account or ethical claim does the rival negate? The previous source-failure and branching cases provide real exclusions. Expanding “generative” after every objection would invalidate the test.

**“Using science already commits one to generative value.”** It can commit one to the means required by an acknowledged epistemic undertaking. Extending that commitment to basic value for life and generation throughout the whole requires the scope argument the claim asserts. The fact that the argument itself has living conditions establishes dependence; it does not silently settle their entire normative significance. G0 remains the authorized substantive route.

**“The rival is only withholding judgment.”** Exactly: it challenges mandatory endorsement, not necessarily compatibility. The study keeps the two apart. A genuine challenge to whole-theory compatibility needs a negated commitment that remains defensible under the proposed scientific standards. **“A mathematical graph is not an alternative explanation.”** Correct. The small graph theorem only blocks an inference from static notation to absence of represented change. **“The all-views theorem proves the whole philosophical conclusion.”** No. It proves a conditional equivalence at a semantic interface. ## 12. The affirmative route and its two extensions

Begin with one unfolding in which people, questions, instruments, acquired skills and consequences form together. A judgment's evidential warrant concerns how the judgment answers to what it claims. Deciding to spend an afternoon investigating concerns the continuation brought about through that activity. These are different questions about the same history. They need not be independently constituted realms or unrelated standards.

The strongest affirmative route is richer than preserving useful machinery. An inquiry can realize understanding, develop a capacity and form a worthwhile shared undertaking. Its value need not be exhausted by a later payoff or by satisfying a preference that existed before the inquiry. Under G0, this can be living formation with basic positive significance, including its present fulfillment. Once the inquiry is warranted, its actual dependencies can ground support for the activity, subject to competing grounds and legitimate limits. The completed projection, participation and practical-judgment accounts already explain much of this application.

For necessity, however, two extensions must be defended:

| Transition | What would support it | What the starting judgment leaves unsettled |
|---|---|---|
| This answer is accurate or well supported → this inquiry deserves pursuit. | A reason concerning the inquiry, what it enables, an undertaking, or the understanding it realizes. | Correctness alone does not identify which unanswered question merits attention, or make its acquisition permissible at any cost. |
| This inquiry or understanding has positive significance → living realization more broadly has basic positive significance. | A defense that the relevant ethical ground is living realization and applies across the proposed extension. | The first case might instead matter through its particular understanding, experience, participation or commitment. |

The second transition is not settled by expanding the description until it includes the whole. A larger description can reveal real dependencies and omitted consequences. It does not independently settle why the favorable ground applies to every living relation within it. Conversely, the presence of an additional feature such as understanding does not prove that this feature exhausts the ground. The competing identifications require assessment.

This also repairs an overly narrow possible reading of §4: reasons for inquiry need not all be instrumental. A rival can regard understanding as worth realizing for its own sake. That concession strengthens the case for valuable formation in one actual kind of activity; it still leaves the biological and ecological extension to justify. The scientific inquiry route must not succeed only by treating this stronger rival as someone who values nothing beyond private usefulness.

## 13. A test preserving both inquiry and the rival's ethical ground

Consider a hypothetical inquiry into the changing conditions of a living culture. Its participants obtain warranted observations, correct a mistaken model and retain the resulting understanding. Their description includes the culture's actual living organization, its dependencies, its destruction or continuation, and consequences for the surrounding lives. The question is about the ground of appraisal, so no assumption of absolute physical isolation is needed.

A reconstructed restrictive account can affirm the value of this understanding, the participants' conscious lives, and legitimate commitments formed through the inquiry. It can also recognize the culture's actual living and include every supported consequence. It nevertheless denies that nonconscious living supplies an additional basic ethical ground beyond those recognized considerations. This restriction is motivated by the conscious-life account assessed in the [living-scope comparison](LIVING-GOOD-GROUNDING.md#7-the-strongest-affective-restriction), rather than an unexplained Boolean switch in a formal model. Not every author there endorses this exact reconstruction.

Where does this position fail? It would misdescribe the target if it erased an actual dependency, denied a supported consequence, confused recorded success with living continuation, or claimed comprehensive appraisal while silently omitting a ground it itself accepts. Those failures are assessable through the retained history and commitments. But its successful correction is not already an assertion that all living has independent basic significance. Its rejection of G0 is therefore not refuted merely by pointing to the inquiry in which it argues. This is a result about the proposed self-contradiction argument; it does not certify the rival's entire philosophy as adequate or its ethics as true.

Our positive reply concerns the culture's living realization itself. Reducing its significance to a useful evidential role overlooks the candidate good identified in the dependence assessment: continuation, impairment and recovery concern an actual life even when no new answer is obtained. G0 recognizes that good. The stronger rival answers that actual living loss need not be basic ethical loss. The issue now lies precisely in that ethical identification, where our affirmative reasons and the rival's restriction must meet. Another demonstration that both arguments require records and correction would leave this disagreement untouched.

Two controls constrain our position. **Successful completion:** ending the inquiry after its purpose is fulfilled need not deny the value of the understanding realized. A reason concerning remaining living cannot be inferred simply from a duty to continue that finished inquiry. **Changed means:** replacing one evidential arrangement can preserve an inquiry's achievement while changing what support particular lives need. Epistemic substitution therefore does not settle ethical interchangeability. Earlier work already established legitimate closure and grounds outlasting inquiry; these controls apply those results to the necessity test.

## 14. Counterreview and the remaining route

**Does this assume an artificially narrow account of science?** No. The account may include demanding norms for inquiry, worthwhile understanding, responsible action and real social relations. Granting that richness still requires an argument for the particular G0 extension. “Scientific philosophy” cannot become a synonym for our preferred ethical conclusion.

**Does locating two transitions prove they cannot be constitutive?** No. A successful account could explain their unity. The present test identifies what such an account needs to show and tests a concrete attempted inference. The distinction records two changes of justificatory scope within one process; it does not posit a fundamental fact/value separation.

**Could a reason to understand be self-validating through inquiry?** Even granting a strong epistemic authority for accurate understanding, that grant does not yet identify the ground of significance for living unable to inquire. Epistemic authority should not be dismissed as mere desire, but neither should its scope be silently enlarged. The inherited parity and discourse assessments remain relevant here.

**Is the wider living ground arbitrary once it is explicit?** No. The dependence, loss and formation arguments give affirmative support. Calling their conclusion a premise neither refutes them nor proves them. The current outcome is a defended G0 account with an unresolved universal extension argument, not equivalence between every proposed ethical standard.

The new contribution is a test of inquiry's purposes that accommodates both epistemic norms and noninstrumental cognitive good. It closes the shortcut from the existence of a well-conducted inquiry to endorsement of all generative value. It preserves the positive route: **understanding can itself be a valuable realization, and its warranted pursuit can support its actual living conditions**. Full-theory necessity still requires defending why the ground extends beyond the features that the strongest restrictive account treats as decisive, and separately defending the additional practical commitments.

The next substantive connection is therefore the proposed common ethical ground across knowing and nonknowing living. An adequate advance must explain why living realization, rather than only the special cognitive or experiential qualities of the starting cases, warrants the wider appraisal. ## 32. A responsibility comparison within one history

Consider a constructed history in which a participant voluntarily undertakes a bounded support task through a justified arrangement. Another life develops reliance, an earlier alternative is displaced, and support remains needed. The task is still feasible within the agreed limits, no equivalent replacement is ready, and no competing basic claim has arisen that would justify unilateral immediate withdrawal. The participant later loses the inclination to continue.

All these positions arise within the same history of coordinated activity. The undertaking, changed reliance, present capacities and possible transitions are its differentiated relations. We do not begin with independent owners or infer unlimited liability from interconnection. The case is conceptual; no empirical claim is made that every stipulated combination is realized, or that the participant's whole motivational structure has been measured.

## 33. The affirmative case and its strongest counterassessment

The positive argument has content beyond insisting that moral reasons exist. The undertaking changed what others could reasonably arrange; the resulting dependence is not undone when its initiator becomes less interested. A requirement directed toward fulfillment or an adequate transition answers to those continuing relations. Its limit is equally relational: the participant's own living, capacity, other responsibilities and legitimate release constrain what can be required. The ground can therefore persist through a change of motivation while changing when the actual practical relationship changes.

This account explains why a response can be called for without predicting that it will occur. Recognizing a reason, being moved by it and complying are different developments within the same unfolding. No additional force commanding the participant from outside reality is postulated. Likewise, a reason for care does not itself authorize another person or institution to compel it. Normative consideration, allocation, permission to demand and permission to enforce require their respective grounds.

**Does shared normative structure close the necessity argument?** The strongest affirmative reply is that belief and action are both answerable to reasons, so an opponent cannot dismiss practical reasons merely because normative authority is inherently impossible. This reply can defeat that indiscriminate dismissal. But even if the same kind of relation occurs in knowing and acting, identifying its occurrence in one case does not specify all of its other instances. Common generative reality does not make every relation interchangeable. We still need the actual content that connects this living good, this practical position and this appropriate response.

**Does an explicit responsibility example supply that content?** It does under G0/J/E. The source of warrant is disclosed; it is not being generated by labeling a task “due.” Against an opponent contesting J3 itself, the example articulates the practical cost of rejection: loss of concern can leave previously induced vulnerability without the claimed requirement of transition. That cost is an affirmative reason to defend J3, not an agreed refutation unless the opponent accepts the relevant ethical appraisal. The opponent may preserve every consequence while denying its proposed practical force. We must answer that denial rather than accuse it of overlooking the history.

**Does nondual explanation remove the dispute?** It removes a misleading picture in which a detached value substance must push an independent actor. It does not by itself establish which formed relations warrant which responses. Conversely, distinguishing the grounds of belief, action and accountability does not establish separate realities. Each account owes an explanation of how those distinctions arise and remain related.

**Does denying one conception of morality necessarily deny G0/J?** No. An opponent might reject irreducible authority, retributive desert or a particular account of moral language while retaining the practical requirements we assert on other grounds. Such a position is not a counterexample merely because it uses the label “error theory.” The relevant contradiction concerns a definite requirement or ground at the same scope. Our account does not acquire a commitment to every feature ascribed to ordinary morality by an opponent's conceptual analysis.

## 34. Assess the cumulative case without claiming universal necessity

The completed comparisons must also be assessed together. Showing that self-maintenance alone, flourishing alone or epistemic self-application alone fails to establish G0 does not show that the combined ethical case has no warrant. Our affirmative account joins actual living realization, dependence without self-sufficiency, the reality of loss, the inclusion of contributors, changing responsibilities and justified endings. Its advantage is a connected explanation of why these matters can retain significance across changes of attention, usefulness and motivation.

This is a cumulative philosophical defense, not a numerical accumulation of independent confirmations. Several arguments reuse the same proposed identification of living significance; repeated formulations cannot be counted as fresh evidence. Source agreement is also not independent empirical validation. The whole package must withstand its strongest rivals while preserving one coherent interpretation of its commitments.

The present test reaches a specific endpoint: **appeal to the authority of inquiry alone has not established the theory's practical authority.** The assessed alternative can grant epistemic reasons while contesting the additional practical connection. Neither that alternative's adequacy nor the impossibility of a future unifying argument is proved. The direct denial has now been located at a responsibility commitment as well as at the earlier living-value boundary.

The next universal-compatibility argument must supply substantive warrant at those identified points, or revise the corresponding commitments in response to a real failure. Repeating that both positions involve generative activity, or showing that a reason can be implemented once accepted, would not address the disagreement.
