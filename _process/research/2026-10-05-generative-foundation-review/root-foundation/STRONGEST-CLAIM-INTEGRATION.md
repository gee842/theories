## What the round establishes

The [positive argument](STRONGEST-CLAIM-ARGUMENT.md) defends a categorical baseline through two separately contestable steps: the patient's actual good warrants an actor favoring its realization, and the same object-ground can favor a permissible effective realization. This is practical effectuation without assuming a prior desire to help or an already adopted end. Its affirmative judgment is philosophical and defeasible. It does not establish metaphysical necessity.

The argument gains content from its contrasts. An independent actor cost can compete with a patient reason without erasing it; actual changes to authority or the nature of the remedy can defeat a particular response. Motivational self-insulation can remove operative concern while leaving the patient burden unanswered. Actual loss of capacity is treated separately. Selfhood can explain access, self-management and special priority without yet justifying a zero baseline for others. Each comparison keeps its normative preservation premise explicit.

The [logic note](STRONGEST-CLAIM-LOGIC.md) identifies a hidden-domain problem: a sufficient-ground argument restricted to self-cases still contains that restriction even if its prose mentions only the patient burden. A stronger argument must justify removal of the restriction. Its audit separately checks retaining the difficult case, constraining the admitted interpretations and keeping patient/actor scope. This clarifies an entailment and scope obligation; the elementary logic is not claimed as new mathematics.

The [counterreview](STRONGEST-CLAIM-COUNTERREVIEW.md) tests naturalistic actor-relative reasons, fitting concern without practical uptake, and constitutive agency without universal concern. A consistent restrictive formula is not a justified moral theory; a favored universal explanation is not thereby necessary. The reviewer found no blocking inference at the declared status of a positive argument. The exact remaining obligation is to justify the sufficiency and scope of the proposed patient ground, including both nonexclusive favorable significance and practical effectuation.

## Review corrections

The final argument permits suspension of judgment rather than treating a critic as obliged either to accept or refute it. Permission is not made dependent on every patient being able to communicate consent. The same subject remains the beneficiary throughout changes of operator. Objective reason, warranted recognition, practical priority and successful action have separate conditions.

The canonical claim now expressly recognizes that a rival denying actor-addressed reasons has offered an answer to the practical question, not merely ignored it. Competence at physical prediction alone is not defined as endorsement of the whole moral thesis.
