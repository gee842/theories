# Review: when a patient reason requires bounded rescue

6 October 2026. AI-authored challenge to the transition **objective patient-grounded moral favor → a duty of serious rescue under a minor full-history burden**. This supports [the universal ethical minimum](../../../../theories/becoming/history/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md) and extends [the plurality analysis](ETHICAL-MINIMUM-PLURALITY.md). It tests requiring force; it does not independently establish the patient-to-stranger ground or complete the generative foundation.

**Verdict:** a bounded rescue duty is defensible as a narrow substantive ethical proposal. It is not entailed by the existence of some patient reason, even alongside the helper's real goods. Its strongest challenge is a permission to withhold assistance, not denial that suffering matters. Neither that permission nor the proposed rescue priority should enter through a definition.

## 1. A precise case and the missing bridge

An adult can operate an ordinary accessible control for thirty seconds to end another person's independently identified, severe, ongoing and unwanted pain. The patient requests this response. The mechanism is reliably understood; no relevant side effect, bodily intrusion, property violation, competing emergency or broken responsibility occurs. The actor's harmless musical practice is briefly interrupted and can resume. The actual history makes this interruption minor, including its effect on available rest, commitments and future opportunities. There is no concealed commitment to permanent availability. Stipulate one effective responder; address several responders separately below.

The patient reason favors operating the control. The actor's practice also has value. Those facts alone leave the deontic verdict open: one account requires the interruption; another regards helping as better supported but permits continuing the practice. This is logical underdetermination, not equal ethical credibility.

The affirmative proposal adds:

> In this specified comparison, effective relief of severe unwanted suffering has enough requiring force to defeat permission to retain this minor discretionary use of time.

Its defense is comparative: refusal preserves a brief, replaceable fragment of a worthwhile activity while knowingly leaving the patient in grave distress that the actor can permissibly end. Recognizing the actor as someone with a life to lead does not yet explain why control over that fragment defeats this claim. Conversely, saying that the claim is grave does not analytically establish its priority. The defended asymmetric judgment is the new ethical work.

The formulation must not say merely “rescue is required whenever no sufficient reason permits refusal.” Unless “sufficient” has independent content, that restates the conclusion. Nor can “reasonable cost,” “eligible actor,” or “unjustified indifference” serve as unexamined substitutes for the disputed requirement.

## 2. A serious permission-based rival

Consider this reconstructed **non-conscription account**:

1. Each person's suffering supplies objective moral favor for effective aid.
2. Each person has a defeasible moral permission to direct their own legitimate time and activity.
3. A stranger's need, without a further responsibility-conferring relation, can justify voluntary sacrifice without defeating that permission.
4. Causing a threat, undertaking care, or accepting a justified role can generate duties absent in the stranger case.

The rival values beneficence and can criticize a relentlessly ungenerous character. Its case is that persons need a stable jurisdiction over their lives, and that converting unchosen opportunities into claims on their activity risks assigning the conscientious person an indefinitely expanding service role. It accepts reasons to help without accepting that every favorable comparison licenses a demand. This is a philosophical reconstruction, not an attribution to every libertarian or defender of prerogatives.

The strongest reply separates three things. Jurisdiction over an entire life need not imply permission concerning every otherwise legitimate token act. A moral requirement does not itself give anyone coercive authority. And an expanding-history objection does not defeat a case whose complete burden really remains minor; it challenges the extension of that verdict to an unrestricted series. These distinctions remove three arguments for automatic exemption. They do not prove that the rival's residual permission is false.

An ownership claim requires particular care. A right against another person's forcibly using one's body or property can coexist with one's own duty to give aid. Inferring “no duty” from “no one may compel” reverses a distinction both accounts need. A genuine rival to the rescue duty must defend permission to refuse, not only immunity from enforcement. Conversely, describing an omission as a forcible use of the patient also begs the question: failure to relieve is not thereby identical to causing or imposing the pain.

The present comparison consequently locates a substantive disagreement: **does the good of directing a life support permission to retain even this minor use of time against this severe patient claim?** A narrow affirmative rescue argument can answer no without claiming that the person owes every available beneficial act.

## 3. Six discriminating comparisons

These are conceptual cases, not measured outcomes or a complete ethical test suite.

| Comparison | What changes, and what the result must distinguish |
|---|---|
| **Relief, prevention, improvement** | Contrast the ongoing severe pain above with preventing a highly credible imminent episode, then supplying an optional pleasure to a comfortable person. The first fits the developed patient-ground. Prevention needs a temporal extension; improvement needs a positive-good ground. Calling all three “benefit” must not silently give them identical requiring force. |
| **Specified means** | Keep the grave need fixed; replace the public control with theft, an unwanted bodily intervention, or a harmless request to an authorized responder. Benefit does not establish permission over the means. If two permissible means work, the requirement may be to secure relief through either, rather than to perform one selected act. |
| **History and renewal** | Compare the rested musician with a caregiver whose same thirty seconds causes the loss of an indispensable sleep window, then with someone who has previously helped extensively but now bears no relevant additional loss. The first contrast changes the burden; the second prevents past contributions becoming automatic immunity. |
| **Several rescuers and fair shares** | With two capable adults, simultaneous intervention may be redundant or harmful. Reliable designation can discharge one person's immediate task; merely pointing at the other cannot ensure relief. If the designated person refuses, another person's duty needs reassessment of actual urgency and burden. Neither “everyone must do it” nor “my share is finished” follows mechanically. |
| **One act and standing restriction** | A single brief interruption leaves several sustained activities available. A requirement to remain interruptible every minute can destroy concentration, travel or intimacy even when each intervention is brief. Count the imposed availability condition, not only button-press time. A token duty does not establish that standing regime. |
| **Kinds of burden** | Compare a negligible money payment, a small medical risk, a brief compelled intimate exposure, and interruption of an irreplaceable farewell. Equal duration or a common numerical score does not establish equivalence. Seriousness can concern type, probability, irreversibility, relationship or authority as well as quantity. |

The multiple-rescuer case also distinguishes an outcome condition from an individual action command. Someone may have to initiate minimal coordination; whether that is required depends on its actual costs and chances of success. A fair institution can allocate recurring provision, but its justice and reliability are further premises. Institutional absence or others' wrongful default cannot by themselves settle how much extra burden transfers to one person.

## 4. What “minor over the full history” must mean

Use a typed burden record, not an unexplained scalar: physical danger and discomfort; lost time and attention; forgone relational or project goods; infringed control or bodily boundaries; competing responsibilities; and depletion of future capacity. This is a provisional checklist for identifying effects, not a complete theory equating them. Record the relevant horizon, uncertainty, available substitutes and who bears each burden.

Past sacrifice matters when it changes this record: accumulated exhaustion, an endangered relationship, depleted reserves or an established expectation of availability can make a short new act costly. An irrecoverable past payment is not automatically an extra marginal cost of acting now. Yet evaluating only marginal expenditure can miss the continuing arrangement that generates repeated demands. Both the present intervention and the defended policy must retain their actual histories.

A local rule's failure to apply once burdens become serious does not prove permission to refuse. Other duties may apply. Likewise, repeated application while costs remain genuinely minor does not prove that indefinite application remains minor. A duty must be assessed at the same temporal scale as the reason offered for its modesty.

A genuinely demanding theory can acknowledge lifetime losses of projects and relationships and still hold that actual unmet dire need requires those sacrifices. Correcting a fresh-start accounting error does not refute that view. Nor may a fair-share argument count assistance that other people would provide under fictional compliance. The demanding theory's priority judgment needs assessment, but it must be met as a priority judgment rather than diagnosed as necessarily forgetting the helper.

There is no numerical cap here. Money does not transparently compensate loss of bodily control, and spare minutes cannot always replace a particular farewell. Declaring every such loss decisive would be equally premature. Their significance and priority need independent argument rather than automatic conversion or automatic veto.

## 5. Real plurality and the limits of a local demonstration

Suppose the case permits the rescue followed by several materially distinct continuations: sustained musical practice, mathematical study, time with a friend, or rest. These realize different goods through actual activities and relationships; they are not cosmetically renamed versions of compulsory service. The thirty-second interruption can leave these options feasible. Feasibility establishes that rescue need not physically eliminate a plural menu.

Ethical compatibility needs more. One must also defend permission to choose among these worthwhile continuations when no further justified requirement settles that choice. The [prior plurality study](ETHICAL-MINIMUM-PLURALITY.md#5-the-further-case-for-defeasible-latitude) proposes such latitude grounded in self-directed life. Combining that defended but disputed ground with the narrow rescue priority gives a coherent normative candidate: this relief is required, while multiple ensuing lives remain permissible. The rescue premise alone supplies neither the value nor the permission of the continuations.

This is stronger than merely displaying several buttons in an action space, but weaker than establishing universal preservation of plural lives. Recurring emergencies, background deprivation, coercive institutions or a newly defended maximizing principle could narrow the options. Catastrophe can destroy feasibility without making the lost goods unreal. Further work must test the whole proposed allocation under such conditions.

Do not protect this menu by an unargued nonaggregation shortcut. A duty to relieve one severe burden at minor cost does not imply that any number of minor benefits can never defeat a project, nor that all burdens below a threshold have zero importance. It leaves those comparisons open. Similarly, refusing a complete scalar ranking does not prove that numbers never matter. The local rescue argument requires only its specified comparison, not a comprehensive theory of population ethics.

## 6. Review disposition and revision triggers

Accept a canonical continuation if it labels the rescue priority as a defended substantive bridge, specifies the patient, means, history and competing claims independently, and distinguishes requirement from enforcement and blame. Reject a purported deduction from “some objective favor,” or any proof that installs the desired verdict inside “minor,” “permissible,” or “adequate agency.”

Three results would change the account. A justified permission covering the complete control case would defeat the proposed local duty while preserving patient significance. Evidence of hidden cumulative loss would remove the case from its stated minor-burden domain. A demonstration that the proposed recurrent allocation erases every defensibly worthwhile alternative would defeat the claimed plural compatibility even if the isolated rescue verdict survived.

Remaining supplied premises are patient and burden identification; warranted causal effectiveness; permission over the means; the full factual burden profile; the patient-to-actor moral contribution; and the normative force assigned to serious rescue and legitimate latitude. No benevolent reward, learned preference or selection success proves these premises. The next bridge is an independently defended priority account that explains this local duty and its exceptions while supporting recurring provision and real plural permissions. The wider generative account must still explain how agents acquire and revise responsiveness to those grounds.
