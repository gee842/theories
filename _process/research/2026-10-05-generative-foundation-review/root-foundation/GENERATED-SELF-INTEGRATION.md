## Result and scope

The named connections are organization → regulation → acquired self/world representation, and experiential adversity → an eligible response's moral significance. The study supplies a formation hypothesis, selected primary comparisons, a positive ethical reconstruction and counterexamples. It does not supply a new self-model implementation, a consciousness derivation, a comprehensive literature review or a universal moral theorem.

The affirmative result is a clearer proposed ground: a generated I can organize access, attribution and responsibility without conferring all ethical value. Actual adversity and permissible relief can be investigated before primitive ownership. The required occurrence-to-response bridge is defended through the difference that genuine relief makes; it is not inferred solely from causal connectedness, no-self or common generative origin.

Organization, self-model, phenomenal perspective and narrative identity are separately identified. Physical process, explanatory theory and embodied generative model are likewise separated. Their collapse would make a finding about ownership appear to establish a universal ontology. The chapter instead preserves the distinct causal, experiential and normative tasks.
