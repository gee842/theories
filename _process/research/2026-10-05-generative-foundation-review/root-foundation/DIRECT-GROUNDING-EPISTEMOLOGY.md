# Direct ethical justification and the universal grounding question

6 October 2026. AI-authored philosophical study within the existing root-foundation investigation. The named connection is **actual patient adversity and eligible independent action → prospective patient-directed moral favor (C2)**. The [universal ethical minimum](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md) and Ren's no-assumptions requirement remain the targets. No premise is added to the generative model, and no formal or empirical result is claimed.

**Result:** a newly inspected primary argument supports an epistemic route that does not require a separately believed ethical bridge before every particular judgment can be warranted. This corrects a possible proof-first restriction on investigation. It does not establish C2, a constitutive identity, universal actor coverage or a foundation without assumptions. Its application to contributory favor requires further work because the source expressly brackets that category.

## 1. What this adds to the existing investigation

The [direct practical-uptake argument](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-ARGUMENT.md) already allows relief to favor action before adopting a helping end. The [closure audit](ETHICAL-CONSTITUTION-CLOSURE.md) already allows a richer constitutive identity while rejecting an inference from accurate experience attribution alone. Neither needs to be repudiated. The new question concerns **how one could be justified in a token moral judgment without first possessing a proof of the general principle that describes its ground**.

These are different questions: whether a patient-directed reason exists; whether a person justifiably recognizes it; whether its constitutive explanation has been established; and whether the explanation covers every eligible actor. A proof-first restriction would collapse the second into the third or fourth. A declaration that a judgment is direct would collapse the first into the second. Both shortcuts should be rejected.

## 2. New primary comparison

Shlomit Wygoda Cohen, “Making Moral Judgments on Adequate Grounds: From Transparent Evidence to Justified Moral Belief,” *Ratio* 38(3), 147–156 (2025), first published 23 May 2025. [Publisher's complete open-access HTML](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/rati.12450). Accessed 6 October 2026. Read the full main argument, §§1–5, and relevant endnotes 6–7, 15–25, 35–54. No source bytes imported or other cited work independently inspected.

**Bounded source synopsis:** Wygoda Cohen defends particular moral justification through nonmoral grounds without a separately believed moral principle. Responsibly formed belief requires reliable tracking of the grounding relation, including sensitivity to disablers (§§3–4). Prospective judgments are possible; general principles need further justification (§4). Transparent evidence is factive, so its alleged grounding relation must actually obtain. Explaining a moral fact's grounds differs from explaining why those features bear normativity (§2.2). Endnote 7 brackets a developed account of contributory reasons. This is a proposed epistemology, not an independently established theory or a derivation of our C2.

The following application is this study's reconstruction, not an argument attributed to Wygoda Cohen.

## 3. The positive correction

Begin with actual undergoing and a formable relieving continuation, as in the current [generative reconstruction](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md#a-reason-to-begin-before-a-joint-response-exists). “Patient,” “observer” and “actor” identify different relations within that history; they do not posit primitive owners or closed substances. In the simplest local case, an observer accurately encounters a silent patient's severe, still-unwanted distress and understands a presently permitted, effective means of relief. The observer has no prior role and the prospective actor has no helping project. Our existing affirmative judgment is that what this act would do for the patient can count in its favor before the actor adopts that project.

One response is to demand an independently proved general conditional before taking this judgment seriously as warranted. The problem with making that demand universal is that knowing a reason and possessing a theory of why it is a reason are different achievements. A person can use evidence competently without representing every relation that makes its use competent. Requiring another justified bridge belief at every level would make the proposed ground inadequate again until a further bridge had been certified. Merely appending more conditionals cannot resolve that demand.

This is a positive reason to permit a different research route: examine whether the actual response comparison is a well-grounded moral recognition, with evidence about the patient, the available transformation and the relevant circumstances doing the justificatory work. The judgment need not be read as a deduction from the isolated descriptive sentence “there is suffering.” Nor must it be treated as an assumption solely because no universal derivation accompanies it. The candidate can be defended and corrected at the level of an actual encounter.

That defense requires more than sincere conviction. The proposed recognition concerns **the independent actor's option**, not merely the patient's aversion or an observer's compassion. The relief must figure in why the option is assessed favorably. The observer's benefit, a communicated undertaking and deserved compensation would introduce other grounds. Distinguishing those grounds is part of the justification, not a demand that the patient first articulate a request.

The positive upshot is permission to pursue and assess direct token justification. It does not license inferring a universal ethical minimum from its immediate appearance. A silent infant or non-agent patient can be the subject of such recognition without becoming a deliberator; whether the relevant experience is actually present remains an identification question.

### A prospective first step, with a definite way to be mistaken

Apply the same inquiry to the [existing complementary-controls case](RESPONSIVE-FORMATION-ARGUMENT.md). A can open a truthful invitation to B; separately authorized cooperation could relieve the identified burden. No joint response exists yet, no promise has been made, and B's participation remains voluntary. The proposed token judgment is **this invitation has patient-directed favor because it credibly opens this relieving continuation**. Its object is A's presently available first step. It neither certifies future relief nor assigns A a duty to secure B's participation.

The evidential positions differ. Present undergoing may be observed or otherwise independently identified. The available continuation depends on evidence about access, timing, costs and B's possible participation. The source's allowance for prospective judgment makes this a possible application to examine; it does not validate the prediction, the grounding relation or any probability-to-reason rule. A phenomenally vivid encounter cannot replace evidence about the future path.

Now supply a concrete undercutter: A learns that B's control has irreversibly failed and that this invitation cannot open another timely route. The originally proposed relief-based support for **this invitation** loses its causal basis, although the patient's adversity remains. If instead B can participate but has not yet agreed, that fact alone does not make the supported invitation futile or authorize its conversion into a command. Finally, if the invitation would disclose protected patient information without authorization, feasibility remains while the permissible means must change. These distinctions allow correction of the forecast, the available step and the practical scope separately. An independent restriction on considering patient benefit at all would raise the distinct jurisdiction question below.

## 4. Concrete undercutters and the exact claim they affect

These cases test the application above. They are original hypothetical comparisons, not observations or established counterexamples to C2.

| Encounter and later evidence | What must change in the assessment |
|---|---|
| A helper sees the patient's distress display cease after pressing a button. The button disables the display while the experience continues. | Confidence in effective relief was misplaced. An apparently direct moral judgment cannot certify the causal premise that would support it. This challenges the application, not the patient-ground. |
| A patient remains distressed during a chosen, meaningful performance. The observer learns that interruption is specifically refused. | A familiar “distress, therefore intervene” response fails to track permission. The patient can retain a local burden while this intervention is unwarranted. No general inference from suffering to wrongness or duty survives merely by being direct. |
| A caregiver accurately identifies every burden but calls another rescue obligatory each time, overlooking accumulated exhaustion and a competing dependent's care. | Accuracy about distress does not establish competence concerning duties. The patient-directed consideration may remain; the overreaching requirement needs revision. This is precisely why favor, duty and authority to compel cannot share one success label. |
| A judge who correctly recognizes an applicant's hardship proposes deciding a tied public allocation for that consideration alone. The governing procedure requires an independently justified fair draw. | This creates a live issue about which considerations may justify this particular choice. The draw's authority must be assessed rather than inferred from its existence. Warranted exclusion, if established, is different from a small reason being outweighed; without that warrant, the case does not refute C2. |

The last case connects to the existing [choice-jurisdiction review](ASSISTANCE-JURISDICTION-COUNTERREVIEW.md). A ban on bypassing the draw would establish a permission constraint; a claim that hardship has no current justificatory force for this choice requires its own further argument. Institutional inadmissibility also does not automatically eliminate overall moral favor. This is not a newly discovered generic reason to disregard patients. It shows why an epistemic capacity must distinguish genuine applicable protection from a convenient claim of private jurisdiction. The other cases show why good causal understanding, sincere concern or successful helping cannot establish that capacity by themselves.

Even after these corrections, a rival can accept the patient benefit and all the operational facts while denying its moral address to an uncommitted actor. That disagreement cannot be dismissed as blindness just because our judgment feels direct. The task is to assess its offered reason for restriction and our reason for nonexclusive address. A recognition test scored only by agreement with C2 would make the target its own standard of correctness.

## 5. Four achievements that must not be exchanged

| Achievement | What this study contributes |
|---|---|
| Reasonable judgment about one actual response | A positive epistemic route for defending it without a separately believed general bridge, together with concrete undercutters. No certification of this observer's reliability is claimed. |
| Independently established constitutive identity | The direct route keeps this possibility open. Naming the evidence does not establish what constitutes moral favor. |
| Universal scope across eligible actors and silent patients | Particular judgments supply possible starting cases. Scope still needs justification, including legitimate choice-specific restrictions and patients with no self-help history. |
| Generative foundation without assumptions | Unchanged and unmet. A foundational theory must explain occurrence, realization, differentiated continuities, significance and reliable recognition within the same account. |

A generated self is compatible with this route: perceptual access, memory, learned discrimination and action selection can be formations in a history rather than powers of a primitive owner. This says how epistemic capacities might belong within the programme. It does not turn their formation into the creation of ethical truth. A learner can acquire a patient-directed response and still misidentify the ground, its scope or an applicable restriction.

The next substantive test is therefore narrower than training another helpful controller: defend a **prospective contributory judgment about an independent actor's option** on its actual grounds, while showing why its warrant is neither a disguised duty judgment nor a supplied agreement criterion. A successful local defense would improve the case for C2 without completing its universal derivation. A warranted restriction would change that case's scope. Both outcomes are compatible with keeping Ren's stronger completion requirement fixed.
