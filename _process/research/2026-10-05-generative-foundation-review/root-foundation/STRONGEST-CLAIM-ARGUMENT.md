# The strongest patient-grounded argument: practical effectuation without prior care

6 October 2026. AI-authored philosophical continuation of Ren's request to aim for the strongest possible claim. The named connection is **constitutive patient significance → a categorical pro tanto practical reason for an unrelated eligible actor**. This file develops a positive argument and records its deepest surviving premise. It does not alter the accepted conditional foundation, report a new scientific experiment, or present an ethical premise as a Lean theorem.

**Adjudication:** the strongest defensible positive position is that an actual, independently warranted patient-good can give an unrelated capable actor a reason for its permissible realization, whether or not that actor antecedently wants to help. We defend the position through the practical effectuation of an object-grounded favorable response. This is stronger than merely reporting that the position has not been disproved. It is a defeasible philosophical argument, not an established proof that every coherent or scientifically competent actor must acknowledge the reason. Its important remaining commitment is that warranted favoring of an outcome for its actual subject has practical force when an eligible means becomes available.

## 1. What the previous work already established

The [unified-grounding argument](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md) separates what makes relief good for its patient from which actor has a permissible effective opportunity. The [counterreview](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-COUNTERREVIEW.md) distinguishes a restriction to prior relationships from a restriction by practical receptivity. The [self-ground audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md), particularly §§10–11, 26–29 and 32–33, already contains a positive matched-case verdict, complete-ground preservation, the internal-reasons challenge, experiential identity, and the distinction between exclusive egoism and defensible partiality. None is rediscovered here.

The earlier argument deliberately declined to infer practical uptake from fitting sadness. Its minimal means principle required an independently justified reason to pursue the end. This continuation examines a stronger bridge: **a right-kind reason to favor this outcome as an end can supply a pro tanto reason for an eligible realization of that very outcome**. The claim does not begin with a desire the actor happens to possess. It concerns why a favorable response would be warranted, and whether the source of that warrant bears on action.

The advance is an argument for this bridge, a distinction between response-specific defeat and mere absence of motivation, and a two-stage test separating a no-conflict baseline from the persistence of that reason under independently introduced costs. The self-insulation comparison then gives the claim an exact relation to the accepted causal constructions. These are philosophical developments, not new measurements or uniquely original principles.

## 2. State the strongest target without hiding the disputed scope

Fix an actual patient p, an episode e, and two prospective continuations, relief r and continuing burden h. The difference must be an independently warranted benefit **for p**: for example, relief of a severe unwanted experience, with relevant consent, refusal, representation, other interests and circumstances retained. A negative training signal, observed avoidance or an arbitrary increase in resource stock does not establish that interpretation. A patient's inability to consent does not itself erase patienthood; a permissible route of representation or action needs its own warrant.

Actor a can choose a known permissible intervention y realizing r rather than h. Eligibility specifies practical capacity, warranted access to the relevant facts, effective means and the applicable permission conditions. It does not mean already caring, accepting this argument, being morally perfect, or having received a caretaker assignment. The actor may be unrelated by origin, promise, responsibility, reciprocity and private interest. Its present opportunity is still a real actor–patient relation.

Warranted access is included in this sufficient case to isolate the practical bridge. Its absence elsewhere would not by itself prove absence of an objective reason; it can instead limit the reason's availability and the action justified by the actor's evidence.

The intended conclusion is:

> The fact that y answers this actual burden supplies a with some reason in favor of y, independently of an antecedent desire to help p.

This is a **categorical pro tanto reason** in the restricted sense just stated. It is not an absolute duty, guaranteed motivation, equal weight across actors, aggregation rule, enforceable claim, permission to use an otherwise forbidden means, or a verdict about blame. A reason can be outweighed; lacking adequate access can change what action is epistemically warranted without making the patient's burden unreal.

Fix the intended practical content independently of terminology. The patient's ground is proposed as an objective favorable consideration **in the justification of a's response**, capable of showing that an otherwise accurate response omitted something practically relevant. An account may call this an actor-addressed moral claim while reserving *reason* for motivationally accessible considerations. If it grants precisely this corrective practical force regardless of antecedent desire, that terminological difference does not refute the present baseline. If it grants only a spectator verdict that the patient's condition is unfortunate, while denying all such force in a's practical justification, it rejects the substantive target. A rival denying this same practical content genuinely disagrees; it is not disqualified by our terminology. Universal rational recognition and a sound route accessible from every actual motivational starting point are further claims; neither is hidden in the word *categorical*.

The benefit identification remains substantive. In particular, satisfying a destructive desire is not automatically a patient-good of the required kind, and alleviating one burden can conflict with agency, another person's good or the same patient's longer-term interests. The proposition concerns independently warranted ground instances, not every aversion or demand.

## 3. Patient-directed favoring has an external success condition

The positive case begins with the patient's condition rather than the actor's current reward. The unwanted burden is undergone somewhere, by this subject; its relief answers that condition even if the observer's pleasure, reputation and projects do not improve. Substituting relief of the observer's distress can leave the relevant badness untouched. This gives concern a determinate object and a corrigible success condition.

Our substantive evaluative judgment is that the actual patient's independently warranted benefit makes its realization appropriately favorable to an informed evaluator. The evaluator need not imagine itself becoming the patient. Nor must p first be a deliberating agent capable of providing a self-relief anchor. The favorable response concerns what happens to p.

Why accept that judgment? The proposed ground explains both the importance of the change and its direction: less of the independently warranted burden is better for the bearer whose episode is at stake. An evaluator's absence of appetite does not identify an error in that benefit. A special relationship can explain additional concern or priority without explaining why this benefit has no favorable significance at all outside the relationship. The affirmative explanation therefore preserves what makes the difference worth favoring while permitting additional sources of reasons.

This is positive normative support, not a deduction from the word *benefit*. An exclusive account can grant patient-relative goodness while denying universal fitting favoring. That account disputes this evaluative step. Its consistency is not its justification; conversely, our explanation's attractiveness is not yet metaphysical necessity. The two claims must remain separately contestable.

## 4. A stronger effectuation principle

Consider the following candidate.

**Object-grounded effectuation.** If a patient's actual good supplies an actor with a right-kind reason to favor an outcome as an end, and that actor has a warranted, permissible effective means to realize that very outcome, then the same good supplies a pro tanto reason for that means, unless some response-specific feature defeats that transmission.

“Right-kind” identifies the source: the outcome is worth favoring because of what it does for its actual subject. A reward for displaying compassion, a threat for failing to cultivate sympathy, or the private usefulness of becoming a nicer person does not by itself supply this premise. Such incentives may provide other valid practical reasons; they are not the patient-ground under examination. The distinction inherits the [acquisition-direction audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#27-warranted-acquisition-and-the-direction-of-justification).

The defense concerns the relation between response and object. Favoring relief as an end is directed toward that relief occurring. Once an eligible means is available, realizing the outcome answers the very feature that made its realization worth favoring. The patient's good does not cease to be answered when the response becomes causally effective. If the same object-ground warrants favorable orientation yet contributes nothing whatever to choosing its eligible realization, a further explanation is needed for this division between warranted favoring and practical pursuit.

This is not ordinary instrumental rationality from an adopted end. No prior adopted end is assumed. It is a substantive bridge between the reason that would warrant an orientation and the reason for an available action. The proposal says that the patient-ground can justify a new practical orientation; successful motivational installation does not justify the ground in reverse.

Nor does calling an attitude a desire prove the bridge. A desire can be absent, inappropriate, in conflict, or causally disconnected from action. The argument is about its independently warranted object and an action's relation to that object, not a definition that makes every fitting attitude an obligation to act.

Several apparent counterexamples delimit rather than refute this formulation:

| Case | Why it does not establish unrestricted transmission |
|---|---|
| Fitting grief for an irreversible loss | Sadness is not yet favorable pursuit, and a means to reverse that loss is unavailable. |
| A fitting wish that an impossible past had been different | No present action realizes the specified outcome. |
| Appreciation of a spontaneous or autonomous good | An intervention may destroy the very feature that made that outcome worth favoring. Its effect is then a different outcome. |
| A beneficial effect obtainable only through an impermissible intervention | Permission for that means has not been established. The theory does not derive permission from benefit. |
| Relief carrying serious competing costs | A patient-directed reason can survive while being outweighed; a full action verdict needs the other grounds. |
| A payoff for acquiring a favorable attitude | That payoff can warrant cultivation without making the attitude appropriate in respect of its object. |

The crucial surviving rival is more exact: it grants the warranted end-directed attitude, the same permissible effective outcome and absence of a response-specific defeater, but denies any practical reason absent a prior role or motive. Our claim is that this disconnect is less well explained than effectuation by the common patient-ground. That is the strongest positive philosophical judgment offered here. The rival has not been shown formally inconsistent.

## 5. Derivation and the exact remaining commitments

Let B identify the independently warranted patient benefit, F the actor-addressed reason for favoring its realization as an end, K the eligible means relation, and R the patient-grounded reason favoring the action. The proposed structure is

\[
B \longrightarrow F,
\qquad
F\land K\land\neg D \longrightarrow R,
\]

where D denotes a response-specific defeater of this transmission, not simply any countervailing reason. A large independent cost can outweigh R without being a D that erases its source. Whether a feature defeats transmission or merely competes needs justification in the actual case.

Defeater assessment must identify a concrete feature and its practical relevance independently of the desired verdict. A response that destroys the autonomy constitutive of the favored outcome, for example, fails to realize that outcome as specified; a legal permission to produce the same measured relief would not repair that lost identity. Newly discovered lack of permission or means efficacy defeats K. An actor-relative H proposed as a further exclusion remains eligible for scrutiny; absence of a prior role is neither automatically a D nor ruled out as a D by definition. Its alleged excluding force is the disputed issue. Merely renaming any disliked conclusion a defeater would make the principle empty.

The inference from these two principles is straightforward; their philosophical warrant bears the substantive burden. B alone does not entail F by notation. F alone does not entail R through an unnoticed definition of *favor*. Both principles are defended above; a grounded objection or true counterexample can defeat them, and insufficient warrant can justify withholding endorsement. Merely reporting no current motivation supplies neither by itself. This yields an affirmative categorical-baseline argument with two inspectable commitments, not a derivation from a norm-free language.

The factorization has a useful diagnostic consequence. A contrary example can defeat patient-good identification, actor scope of fitting favoring, or practical transmission without defeating all three. It is therefore more informative than a single untyped declaration that concern either follows from experience or does not.

### Why prefer this grounding, rather than merely demand an answer from its rival?

The positive explanatory comparison has three parts. First, the same actual burden explains why a favorable response is appropriate and what would make a practical response succeed; these are answers to the same patient, not two unrelated sources of authority. Second, the account explains a justified change in response when a spectator acquires an eligible means: the good remains fixed, while a newly available way of answering it makes action an appropriate form of response. Third, it explains selective correction. Discovering ineffective means changes what to do; discovering fulfillment changes the outstanding application; removing private discomfort or sympathy alone changes neither. The ground thus directs and constrains inquiry and action without deriving truth from whichever disposition survives.

These are reasons to prefer a patient-grounded explanation because they account jointly for significance, practical opportunity and the direction of correction. They are not merely a demand that the rival prove a negative, nor a count of predicates. A restricted account can reproduce the factual transitions and some warranted verdicts. Its distinctive remaining claim is that the actor's absence of a role or suitable antecedent motive removes every practical contribution of this otherwise fitting, realizable patient-good. That claim needs a positive rationale for the *zero*, beyond the well-supported relevance of roles to additional responsibility and motives to uptake. The affirmative account already has a positive source of favor: the independently warranted good that the action realizes. It need not generate that source from the failure to justify exclusion.

Selfhood has several established explanatory jobs: it identifies the bearer of an experience, often gives unusually direct access, locates capacities for self-management, and supports distinctive responsibilities. These jobs explain why self-concern is often stronger, better informed or more immediately actionable. None is, by itself, an explanation of why an equally identified and eligible stranger opportunity has zero patient-directed practical significance. Our abductive judgment is that a baseline plus independently justified special reasons gives the better account of both self-concern and the hard stranger case. This does not prove that those jobs exhaust every possible normative role of selfhood. A defensible exclusive role would revise the judgment.

Ground completeness is consequently a **defended sufficiency judgment**, not a fact read off phenomenological locality. The argument does not begin with a self-restricted reason theorem and delete its selfhood premise. It offers actor-addressed fitting favoring directly, then defends its practical transmission. That is why nonacting and noncommunicating patients remain eligible subjects, and why a critic must assess the positive normative steps rather than only the reach of a self-to-self proof. The explanation would be circular if *patient-good* meant *whatever every actor already has reason to promote*. Its independent identification here concerns the patient's actual condition; universal practical scope is precisely the further conclusion argued for.

## 6. The no-conflict baseline and the cost-insertion test

First consider an idealized choice where relief changes only the patient's admitted burden. No other person is worsened, no right is violated, and the actor gives up no independently relevant good, project or opportunity. Practical discretion is not defined away: permission to decline and absence of any reason to help remain different claims. In this comparison the patient-ground supplies the sole identified favorable difference between the options. The effectuation argument supports some reason for relief without requiring interpersonal measurement or a total value ordering.

This baseline is not proved by a Pareto formula. Calling patient-improvement a practical dominance is already a normative step. The argument for that step is the patient-ground plus the effectuation principle, not the arithmetic of unchanged coordinates.

Now introduce an actor cost while retaining the actual patient, burden, relief relation and legitimate means. We should not infer that every costly intervention is required. The narrower question is whether the added cost **outweighs** the patient-ground or **annuls** it. Under the proposed ground, an independent sacrifice supplies a competing consideration; it does not make the patient's relief cease to answer the burden. The patient-directed reason therefore survives unless the new facts defeat its application, change the outcome's identity, or supply an independently justified exclusion condition.

This is a restricted reason-preservation judgment, not global additive atomism. A cost can change the permission, nature or patient meaning of an intervention. Such cases do not preserve this comparison. Even within the preserved case, no numerical weight, continuity of weights or mandatory maximal sacrifice follows.

The two-stage argument is useful because a rival can accept a costless baseline yet deny its extension; that dispute then concerns ground persistence under a specified alteration. It cannot be settled by conflating a justified permission to retain one's resources with the claim that the patient's condition contributes no reason whatsoever. Conversely, the costless result alone does not prove persistence: the restricted preservation judgment is exposed as an additional commitment.

## 7. Self-insulation tests access rather than repairing the patient

The accepted [evaluative-grounding construction](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/EVALUATIVE-GROUNDING.md) already separates an acquired operative connection from the independently justified repair ground. A matched later update can suppress help while preserving relevant facts and sufficient resources. Those are causal and role-bound results; they do not prove the stranger baseline.

They motivate a new philosophical comparison. Hold p, e, the effective means and other independently agreed grounds fixed. Let a lose or deliberately disable the association through which p's condition influences its selector. This may change the feasibility or cost of uptake and may itself require another action. It does not, merely by changing a's response, relieve p or defeat the patient-good identification.

On the defended effectuation account, purely motivational self-insulation cannot discharge the patient-grounded reason. It can make the reason ineffective. If the intervention actually removes capacity, current action-specific reasons and obligations can change, while the history and reasons concerning the capacity-destroying choice require separate assessment. We must not assume unchanged opportunity after disabling machinery that was physically necessary to act.

An internal-reasons account can instead claim that deleting all suitable motivational routes removes R, even though B, factual understanding and physical opportunity persist. This identifies a real disagreement about grounding, not just a disagreement about whether the actor will help. The externalist advantage is that warranted criticism can continue to track the unrepaired patient rather than depend on the success of self-insulation. The internalist may defend history-sensitive obligations, indirect motives or a distinction between moral claims and agent reasons. Those responses must be assessed in their stated theories; no finite inventory of absent motives proves that every sound deliberative route is impossible.

This argument does not derive the baseline from the undesirability of self-insulation. It gives the defended baseline a discriminating normative consequence and asks each rival to account for it. A teaching process that induces concern, or broad social approval of that concern, is not a truth test.

## 8. The rational indifferent actor and the strongest permissible verdict

An actor can be logically consistent, factually accurate and instrumentally competent while remaining indifferent to p. The causal theory explains such a process; it should not exclude it from reality by definition. The present argument says that this actor nevertheless has a patient-grounded reason where the defended principles apply. Whether its indifference counts as a rational deficiency depends on the further account of practical rationality; empirical competence alone cannot decide that account.

An actor-relative restriction is not automatically arbitrary. Selfhood, undertakings and responsibility are real relations and can justify special reasons. The question is whether the relation justifies **exclusive practical address** of this patient-ground or only additional weight, priority or directed obligations. A bare name change cannot do that work. A substantive role restriction might, but needs an argument for its excluding function. The same burden applies to our proposed universal scope; we cannot announce that the complete ground excludes selfhood before defending it.

The strongest claim justified by this analysis is therefore affirmative but bounded:

> Actual independently warranted patient-good is a defensible source of categorical pro tanto practical reasons for any actor with a relevant eligible opportunity. The reason's source is the patient-good; the actor's opportunity supplies practical address; motivation supplies one possible route to effective uptake. Defended special relations and competing claims shape further reasons and their resolution without being necessary for every patient-directed reason.

We take this to be the better explanation of the isolated relief case and its restricted cost-insertion and self-insulation continuations. That judgment would be revised by a successful account of a response-specific restriction, a defeated patient-good identification, or a better explanation of why warranted favoring is normatively confined to spectatorship in the eligible case.

**What remains unproved:** that this grounding is metaphysically necessary across every admissible realization; that denying it defeats an independently specified capacity every critic must retain; that every actual learner can acquire it through a sound accessible route; or that it completes conflict resolution, directed answerability and the necessity of the whole generative root. The strongest still-live premise is object-grounded practical effectuation together with nonexclusive fitting favoring. They have been argued for, not eliminated by notation. The accepted conditional foundation stays complete at its declared scope while this stronger claim receives continued criticism.
