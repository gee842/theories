## 3. Positive reconstruction: continuation need not mean repetition

The following argument is ours. A practice participates in forming the people and conditions to which its next occurrence responds. If it changes their capacities, habits, dependencies or relation to its opportunities, the next occurrence does not enter the original situation unchanged. Its familiar name cannot establish that it retains the same function.

The [coformation study](YITI-REN-COFORMATION-SOURCES.md) already reconstructs undergoing, understanding and response as mutually transformative. The focused addition here is how a practice's effects can alter the conditions of its own adequacy, making method transformation part of its continuation.

Consequently, fidelity to a defensible purpose can require transforming a method, limiting its use or ending it. This is more than choosing a different instrument against a wholly unaffected background: the earlier instrument may have helped form the very condition that now makes its unmodified use inappropriate. Understanding those consequences can also change what a practitioner takes as evidence of success.

For the core/tree account, the proposed significance is not an external approval affixed to completed neutral activity. The actual relation helps constitute what the activity is doing: enabling a capacity, obstructing it, or changing its conditions. Determining that relation may change the relevant description itself. Its ethical adequacy still requires argument; neither a nondual description nor the historical authority establishes our proposed identification. We must not silently replace Wang's stronger ground with this narrower construction.

Three claims must remain distinct: the method changes; a concrete judgment of adequacy changes; the ultimate aspiration changes or receives justification. The reported episode supports a conceptual account of the first two under an asserted larger aspiration. It does not establish the third. Nor does every contextual adjustment qualify as deep criterion revision.

For the present foundation, differentiated continuity matters. Ending imposed participation in a particular practice need not erase concern for the person affected or eliminate another warranted response. Conversely, retaining someone inside a teaching relation does not itself preserve that person's capacities. This is our identification requirement, not a claim that the historical paragraph supplies longitudinal evidence for each learner.

The [Cheng comparison](BOUNDARY-SIGNIFICANCE-SOURCES.md) already distinguishes failed responsiveness from disappearance of its target; the [earlier Wang correction study](RESPONSIVE-FORMATION-SOURCES.md) already requires openness to correcting the initiator. They help constrain this reconstruction without adding independent evidence or making every connection beneficial.

## 5. The strongest challenge

A teacher can reinterpret an unsuccessful method after the fact, protect an unexamined ideal and become more effective at controlling students. The reported episode does not independently measure improvement, establish each student's perspective, or rule out that rival. The nearby rebuke makes contestability a concrete interpretive question; it does not prove that every objection was suppressed or that the disputed exercise caused harm. A self-correcting narrative can still insulate its central authority from correction.

Our reconstruction accordingly needs identifiable consequences for particular participants, evidence not exhausted by the initiator's approval, and actual opportunities for appropriate contest and change. A refusal is not infallible; neither is the teacher's interpretation. Stable, adequate practices remain possible. The claim would narrow if the alleged effects were misidentified, did not alter the stated conditions of adequacy, or supported a different continuation. None of this converts method revision into a derivation of universal ethical authority.
