# A native consequence changes the method used to propose models

6 October 2026. AI-authored mathematical specification of a **native resource consequence → paid retained selector change → later candidate-generation choice → fresh model acquisition and use** interface. This is a supporting mathematical bridge under the existing generative account, not a new global completion condition.

The design follows the [meta-revision boundary](META-REVISION-MATH.md). It uses an explicitly alternative continuation from the earlier acquired-model checkpoint. It does not add resources to the depleted final revision endpoints. This note states hand proofs and acceptance conditions; numerical execution, resource affordability and the independent implementation review are separate evidence.

## 1. What is inherited and what is supplied

The actual earlier acquisition leaves a retained unary three-state model \(H\), output word \(001\) from its initial state, fuel

\[
B_0=\frac{1030367}{65536},
\]

one remaining ten-unit source packet and 15 live logical cells. The physical apparatus is at its old positive state, but that physical label is not a new input to this controller. The old audit transcript is not available for free replay.

The model itself was learned in the [acquisition study](ABSTRACTION-ACQUISITION-MATH.md). Its retained outputs, transitions and initial-state field are genuine inputs. A new paid plan starts from the declared reset interface rather than assuming the controller was handed a current cursor.

The following are supplied local mechanisms:

- a finite method-controller recipe, a writable selector initially zero and the charged reader/writer;
- two candidate-generation recipes, \(G_0\) and \(G_1\), described below;
- an update coupling from an acquired receipt to the selector;
- the unchanged native reset/action/probe and receipt interface;
- logical-cell capacity, tariffs and a finite invocation schedule;
- admitted apparatus laws and stability for the class-relative inference claims.

No callback supplies “best method,” “correct model,” hidden physical phase or an analyst utility. Nonetheless, the supplied coupling is a particular exploratory rule. The construction can show that it executes and responds to acquired consequences; it does not derive its origin or establish optimal method selection.

## 2. The resource consequence is an actual event

Let the learned cycle have distinct states \(q_0,q_1,q_2\), with initial state \(q_0\), transitions

\[
q_0\xrightarrow{a}q_1\xrightarrow{a}q_2\xrightarrow{a}q_0
\]

and outputs \(0,0,1\). These names denote the traversal of the learned table, not hidden environment identifiers.

Pay to traverse the retained interface and derive a positive plan. Its three-cycle shape is supplied by the actual learned-model lineage, not by a newly executed full interface validator. In this model, reset followed by \(a^2\) reaches the learned positive state. The native apparatus then consumes its remaining packet in a probe. In the primary law, a successful probe transfers ten actual resource units to the controller; an unsuccessful probe dissipates the packet. An additional paid receipt acquisition reports arrival. Neither merely predicting success nor reading a “success” symbol creates resource credit.

Let \(r\in\{0,1\}\) denote actual arrival and \(z\in\{0,1\}\) the acquired receipt. Under the truthful receipt premise \(z=r\). A fault can instead give \(z\ne r\). The account must credit \(10r\), not \(10z\).

The probe's payment precedes its consequence. Actual arrival may fund a later receipt read, but it cannot fund the earlier probe retrospectively. If receipt acquisition fails, no zero receipt is inferred. If the source is unavailable, inability to execute the probe is not an observed nonarrival.

### Proposition P1 — The learned plan distinguishes two supplied apparatus laws by resource consequence

Let \(\operatorname{Stut}(H)\) be generated by inserting a fresh zero-output state \(s\) immediately before the learned positive state:

\[
q_0\xrightarrow{a}q_1\xrightarrow{a}s
\xrightarrow{a}q_2\xrightarrow{a}q_0.
\tag{P1}
\]

All old state identifiers, outputs and the initial state are preserved. Under faithful reset, the primary productivity law and an available packet, the paid reset–\(a^2\)–probe plan produces:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{apparatus}&\text{state at probe}&r\\ \hline
H&q_2&1\\
\operatorname{Stut}(H)&s&0.
\end{array}
\tag{P2}
\]

**Proof.** Follow the two transition paths from their reset initial state. The reached outputs are respectively 1 and 0. The native productivity law delivers a packet exactly in the positive case. ∎

The inserted apparatus is a declared environmental alternative. The learner constructs its possible representation later; it does not manufacture the changed apparatus by allocating a model state.

The observation \(z=0\) does not imply that the apparatus is \(\operatorname{Stut}(H)\). Blocked transfer, broken reset, changed productivity, an incorrect receipt or another transition change can also produce it. P1 supplies a useful finite witness, not a diagnostic equivalence.

## 3. A retained selector changes the generated family

The method selector starts at \(p=0\). Following the learned positive plan, pay to acquire \(z\), then pay to commit

\[
p'=1-z.
\tag{P3}
\]

This rule treats an observed failure of the expected capture as a reason to try an expanded family. It is not a numerical reward supplied by the analyst. Its role and appropriateness nevertheless remain a declared coupling to be tested.

After the commit, a distinct paid operation reads the retained selector and invokes a candidate generator:

\[
G_p(H)=
\begin{cases}
\{H\},&p=0,\\
\{H,\operatorname{Stut}(H)\},&p=1.
\end{cases}
\tag{P4}
\]

Each call is paid. The expanded call constructs its new state and transitions from the retained \(H\); the selector does not contain the answer to the later model-identification problem.

For each generated machine, admit every current phase and both constant observed-output parities. Thus \(G_0\) produces six current behavioral candidates; \(G_1\) produces fourteen. The old current phase is not reused as an unearned label after probe execution.

### Proposition P2 — An acquired nonarrival receipt can cause a genuine generation-method change

Suppose \(p=0\) initially, a receipt \(z=0\) is acquired, the write in (P3) and intervening bit-retention step complete, and the later selector read/dispatch is faithfully executed and paid. Then the same continuing controller retains \(p'=1\) and actually invokes \(G_1\), including the new state in (P1). If the acquired receipt is instead \(z=1\), the completed route invokes \(G_0\).

**Proof.** Substitute the acquired receipt into (P3), complete its charged retention, then apply the paid read of that retained value to (P4). The two generation results differ extensionally: \(G_1\) contains the four-state machine absent from \(G_0\). ∎

This is a change in the model-generation procedure. It is more than a changed reported score, a changed model label or differential ability to survive a final fee. In the failed-capture arm, the selector changes before the broader model is tested and acquired. Fresh evidence can still favor the original \(H\).

The functional maps (P3) and (P4) remain fixed. The result changes the generator enacted by an individual controller within its supplied repertoire; it does not learn the optimal map from consequences to methods, invent the repertoire or rewrite its own update chemistry.

In this finite witness, the zero-valued method setting is initialized before the probe; its first generator dispatch occurs after the receipt. The failed-capture arm changes that setting and then invokes \(G_1\). It does not first run \(G_0\), attribute a measured failure to that generator, and subsequently replace it. The established order is native outcome → retained method-setting change → later enacted generator, not comparative learning from prior executions of both generators.

## 4. Isolating the retained causal route

Different actual arrivals create different fuel balances. That physical support is a relevant consequence, but an ordinary successful-versus-failed comparison alone can confound selector influence with affordability. A matched-support control separates them.

### Proposition P3 — Same actual support can yield different enacted generators through the receipt/selector route

Hold the actual apparatus \(H\), native plan and actual arrival fixed at \(r=1\). Compare a truthful receipt \(z=1\) with an inverted receipt \(z=0\). Give both the same charged receipt acquisition, selector write/retention and later read opportunities. If those steps execute and their charge envelopes are independent of the written bit, their balances coincide at dispatch, but they invoke \(G_0\) and \(G_1\) respectively.

In the inverted-receipt arm, a control that pays the same write bill while clamping the selector to its old zero value invokes \(G_0\) instead.

**Proof.** Both arms receive the same physical credit \(10r=10\) and pay the same pre-dispatch charges, so their resource balances agree. Equation (P3) gives selector values 0 and 1; (P4) gives different generators. The clamp removes the acquired retained change while preserving the paid opportunity, so its later read returns zero. ∎

This comparison establishes that the acquired symbol and retained write can affect later generation without an extra resource grant selecting the arm. It deliberately uses a misleading receipt in one arm. It is evidence for causal efficacy of the feedback route, not evidence that the erroneous broadening is warranted or beneficial.

The most direct paired comparisons keep the actual physical state and receipt fixed while changing only the selector-write clamp or later selector-read bypass. Merely allocating a bit is insufficient: a paid record that is never read does not enact another generator.

If a write is denied, the physical consequence and its bill still occurred, but the new selector has not been retained. If the read/dispatch cannot be paid, a stored method preference has not been enacted. These are separate incomplete prefixes.

## 5. Fresh observations decide the current model

The generated candidates are current observational configurations, not physical-cause diagnoses. Let

\[
C_0=\{(H,q,b):q\in Q_H,\ b\in\{0,1\}\},
\]
\[
C_1=C_0\ \cup\
\{(\operatorname{Stut}(H),q,b):q\in Q_{\operatorname{Stut}(H)},\
b\in\{0,1\}\}.
\tag{P5}
\]

A native read emits the candidate output XOR \(b\) without moving state; action \(a\) advances its state. A paid observation step removes incompatible predictions after actually acquiring the observation. This is the dynamic compatibility update of the [access proof](EXPERIMENTAL-ACCESS-MATH.md).

### Proposition P4 — Exact family-relative reacquisition after method dispatch

Assume the actual current reading process belongs to \(C_p\), its law and parity remain fixed during the audit, and all required observations and updates are paid and correct. Then:

1. Every completed compatibility update retains the actual current configuration.
2. A singleton is the actual current reading configuration within that class.
3. For \(C_0\), at most three consecutive readings and two intervening actions identify the configuration.
4. For \(C_1\), at most six consecutive readings and five intervening actions identify it.

**Proof.** Items 1–2 follow by induction: the actual predecessor is initially present; its predicted transition and read agree with the native event, so filtering preserves its successor. For item 3, the six three-bit words from \(001\)'s three rotations and their complements are all distinct. For item 4, use the three-/four-cycle separation proof in [model-family revision](MODEL-FAMILY-REVISION-MATH.md#proposition-r4--six-consecutive-readings-suffice-and-five-can-fail): same-parity minority bits have period separation three versus four, and opposite parities have different minority-bit counts. ∎

The protocol may stop earlier at a singleton. Its correctness then follows from coverage and exact filtering, not from having reached a predetermined number of observations.

For the same acquired fresh transcript \(h\), candidate update also satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Upd}^{*}(C_0,h)
=\operatorname{Upd}^{*}(C_1,h)\cap C_0.
\tag{P6}
\]

**Proof.** The two families use identical laws for every \(H\)-candidate, preserve each machine's identity under transitions, and differ only by the added four-cycle candidates. The equality follows step by step from the filter definition. ∎

Equation (P6) concerns a common transcript; different stopping rules can produce different complete transcripts. It makes no claim that the cheaper family is adequate in an apparatus it omits.

After a singleton, the implementation must pay to compact/retain the chosen model, parity and current cursor. A subsequent paid \(a\)-action and predicted reading test its use; the cursor update and final retention have their own bills. Conditional all-\(a\) predictions follow from the selected finite law only while the coverage, calibration and stationarity premises hold. No reset or productivity law is inferred merely from this fresh sensor audit.

### Proposition P5 — Learned state names do not determine the method or its predictions

Let \(\sigma\) rename the three states of \(H\), preserving its initial-state field, outputs and transitions by transport. Extend it to the stutter construction by mapping its fresh state to the other construction's fresh state. The positive plan, receipt-to-selector rule, generated-family behavior and compatibility updates are invariant up to this renaming.

**Proof.** Traversing a renamed transition from a renamed state reaches the rename of the old successor; its output is the same. Thus the plan derived from the learned initial state has the same action word. The two-edge stutter construction commutes with the extended renaming. The native observation sequence, selector bit and generator choice are unchanged. Map each surviving candidate's state by the corresponding renaming; transition and read predictions agree, so each filtering update preserves the correspondence. ∎

When tariffs depend only on model sizes, candidate counts, command lengths and work/storage envelopes, the corresponding payment schedule is unchanged as well. This excludes interpreting a chosen numeric state identifier as a hidden current-phase oracle.

## 6. Expected controls and informative failures

These are distinct claims to check, not branches to remove if they weaken an advantage:

- **Unchanged \(H\), truthful receipt:** actual arrival permits the selector to remain zero; the narrower six-configuration generator is enacted and can reacquire \(H\).
- **Declared stutter, truthful receipt:** the same learned positive plan has no arrival, the selector changes to one, the broader generator is enacted and can acquire the four-cycle.
- **Fixed narrow generator:** on a stutter apparatus it can produce empty compatibility or a wrong accepted model. Short future agreement may also occur. The checker must preserve whichever the actual schedule produces.
- **Fixed broad generator:** it can match the revised controller's coverage without consequence-sensitive switching and may spend more on the unchanged apparatus. Any advantage of switching is conditional on the observed comparisons.
- **Blocked transfer with unchanged readings:** nonarrival can trigger broadening even though \(H\) remains the correct reading model. Broadening proposes an alternative; it does not assert the cause.
- **Wrong receipt or broken reset:** feedback can trigger an unnecessary broadening or omit a needed one. Those are failure laws, not reasons to reinterpret the trigger as an oracle.
- **Paid write clamp or read bypass:** matched physical outcomes and pre-dispatch funding need not change the enacted generator when the retained causal route is severed.
- **Short reserve, source, capacity or denied commit:** actual partial execution remains charged. A missing observation is not a negative observation; a paid native action followed by an unpaid cursor commit can leave a stale internal state.
- **A larger omitted process or late change:** passing the admitted-family audit can still yield a wrong or later stale model.

An oracle comparator, if ever used, must be separately labeled and must not supply the learner's trigger or truth label. The useful ordinary comparator is fixed generation under the same native interfaces and paid accounting.

## 7. Chronological resource proof obligation

Let cost and receipt events occur in their actual temporal order. Starting from \(B_0\), every paid event \(t\) must satisfy

\[
F_{t-1}\ge c_t,\qquad
F_t=F_{t-1}-c_t
\tag{P7}
\]

when it is a charge, or \(F_t=F_{t-1}+r_t\) when it is an actual arrival. Symbolic \(r_t\) here is the resource amount, not a receipt bit. A later arrival cannot satisfy an earlier guard.

Let \(C\) be accumulated new charges, \(S\) remaining source and \(W\) consumed packet energy not delivered to fuel. For the unchanged one-packet interface with no extra grants,

\[
B_0+10=C+F+S+W.
\tag{P8}
\]

The prior acquisition and formation expenditures remain in the whole-history account. The native probe only transfers or dissipates the last inherited packet. It does not renew that source.

Storage checks must include retained \(H\), the new controller and selector, both models when generated, current candidates, and all concurrently live prediction/receipt/commit scratch. Compaction cannot retroactively fund an earlier over-capacity workspace. Selector writes, later reads, candidate-generation work, fresh evidence updates, model compaction and continued use are separate events.

The account should distinguish actual income from receipt error; retained selector from merely acquired receipt; invoked method from its paid label; acquired model from later verified use; and verified use from paid final retention. Its reported maintenance denials are not, without an additional physical decay law, proof that all stored bits have been erased.

## 8. Exact contribution and limits

If the paid implementation satisfies these premises and exhibits the stated comparisons, the result is a finite within-controller witness:

**an actual resource event is acquired through a fallible receipt, changes a paid retained selector, and thereby changes the model-generation recipe later enacted; fresh sensor evidence then determines the retained current model within the chosen class.**

No analyst utility or correct-model callback is required for this causal path. Its native resource law, receipt coupling, repertoire, exploratory update, controller hardware and finite scheduling remain supplied. A fixed broad method can be equally accurate, and deceptive or irrelevant feedback can cause misselection. The construction neither learns a universally best generator nor resolves the no-universal-retention result of the previous note.

The available packet supports one finite alternative continuation. It does not provide sustainable method revision, endogenous future demand or renewed support. Those broader claims require an actual additional process and its resource composition. Completion of this scoped supporting interface is separate from the already assessed conditional foundational adequacy and from stronger empirical or metaphysical claims.

## 9. Executed checkpoint and resource boundaries

Read-only inspection of the [implementation](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/acquisition/access/revision/metarevision/methods/model.py) and [saved results](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/acquisition/access/revision/metarevision/methods/results.json) verifies the intended epistemic interfaces. The learner receives retained \(H\), a native command callback and its account. The plant, current physical phase and external history are not consulted for policy choice. The receipt is committed into the method bit; a later paid read of that bit controls dispatch. The external receipt readout and recorded fresh history do not substitute for this retained route.

The charged controller representation comprises the seven-cell \(H\), 24 bounded controller cells and one method bit: 32 live cells after installation, compared with the inherited 15. The implementation retains the actual bit during later model compaction. Final live storage is 34 cells for a selected three-state model or 36 for the four-state model.

Both primary continuations complete method retention, generation, fresh acquisition, a forward predicted read and final retention:

| Primary alternative | Actual arrival | Enacted family | New charges | Final fuel | Peak cells |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unchanged \(H\) | 10 | 6 configurations | \(541799/65536\) | \(142991/8192\) | 68 |
| Declared \(\operatorname{Stut}(H)\) | 0; packet wasted | 14 configurations | \(431433/32768\) | \(167501/65536\) | 117 |

The narrower primary uses three fresh readings; the broader primary uses five. Both stay within P4's bounds. These costs include the native probe and paid receipt, selector write/retention/read, generation, acquired-model audit and subsequent use. Both finish with zero source. They are alternative histories from the same earlier checkpoint, not two uses of its last packet.

The same-\(H\) receipt-inversion control receives the same ten actual units but broadens, spending \(394187/32768\) rather than \(541799/65536\). A paid frozen-selector control restores the narrow recipe and its original cost, despite the inverted receipt. These implement P3's distinction between actual support, acquired feedback and an enacted retained influence.

For these fixed primary schedules, ordered charges and actual income give the exact starting-fuel thresholds:

\[
B_{\min,H}=\frac{5213}{2048},
\qquad
B_{\min,\operatorname{Stut}(H)}=\frac{431433}{32768}.
\tag{P9}
\]

The \(H\)-threshold is determined by getting to the productive probe before its income arrives. One quantum \(1/65536\) less cannot pay that probe; exactly the threshold completes and ends with \(280377/65536\). The stutter case receives no income, so its threshold is its full accumulated bill. Exactly that amount completes at zero; one quantum less verifies the later prediction but cannot pay final retention. These are derived schedule tests with explicitly changed starting reserve, not replacement budgets for the inherited primary comparison.

The saved finite comparison covers 224 declared boundary/fault configurations for each of four policies. Feedback and its ordinary table realization agree: 160 finish with a correct retained model, 48 finish with a wrong retained model and 16 encounter a use mismatch. Fixed narrow generation has 96 correct retained models, 96 wrong retained models and 32 use mismatches. Fixed broad generation retains the correct reading model in all 224 of these admitted cases. These are exhaustive counts under a supplied design, not empirical frequencies, a future-demand probability law or evidence of universal superiority. The broader comparator removes the trigger's coverage error in this family, at the cost of always generating and auditing the larger family.

In particular, narrow generation on the primary stutter apparatus accepts the wrong three-state model and passes its next predicted read. The feedback branch avoids that error in the primary case, but misleading receipt variants preserve 48 wrong retained outcomes in the larger comparison. A separately tested five-state process produces a later use mismatch. Receipt-payment failure after actual income, insufficient scratch capacity, selector clamps and learned-state renamings are also preserved.

The proofs establish conditional information/causal properties; the saved implementation supplies finite witnesses and failure counts. Neither makes the fixed exploratory rule optimal or gives the discarded new transcript a retained replay interface.
