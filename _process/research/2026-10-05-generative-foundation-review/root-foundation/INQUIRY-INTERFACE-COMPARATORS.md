# Information acquisition that can change its own sequence

6 October 2026. AI-authored focused comparison under the current [NONDUAL-METHOD](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). Named connection: **what has been acquired changes which further inquiry or action is appropriate**. The selected empirical result concerns flexible use of an available action menu. It does not establish formation of that menu, autonomous revision of an access rule, or ethical authority arising from successful performance.

## The precise connection to existing work

The [practice-transformation argument](PRACTICE-TRANSFORMATION-ARGUMENT.md) distinguishes continuing a concern from repeating its method. The [acquisition-protocol reconstruction](APPLICATION-REVISION-RECONSTRUCTION.md) identifies a stricter local obstruction: information, operative influence and sufficient resources do not themselves open the existing service route before its fixed sequence completes. That is a result about an actual declared transition law, not a claim that organisms must always follow such sequences.

An empirical comparator is useful if it distinguishes carrying out a learned chain from selecting further acquisition in response to what is already available. The [earlier physiological comparison](CONSEQUENCE-ORGANIZATION-COMPARATORS.md) addresses changes in subsequent plasticity. This study instead concerns the organization of information-seeking actions. They address related interfaces without becoming one measured history.

## Primary evidence, including unsuccessful cases

**Beran and Smith (2011), [Information Seeking by Rhesus Monkeys and Capuchin Monkeys](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2011.02.016).** [Preserved primary manuscript and reading scope](../../../../sources/40-inquiry-interface-2026-10-06/README.md).

Beran and Smith tested eight experienced rhesus macaques and seven capuchins using a joystick matching task. Sessions began with sixty trials enforcing sample-reveal → comparison-reveal → match. Testing offered both reveal actions together. Later phases randomly mixed displays containing neither item, the sample alone, the alternatives alone, or both. Appropriate sequences differed: reveal missing information, then match; bypass unnecessary reveals. Correct matches earned pellets; errors produced timeouts. Six macaques reached the final phase, and four of the original eight met the criteria for every trial type. No capuchin met all final criteria. Some animals matched well despite redundant inquiry; others matched poorly after omitting needed information. Advancement depended on previous success, with at most five sessions per phase. The early-trial analysis selected the four ultimately successful macaques, so it does not describe all animals. The action menu and informational contingencies were supplied; acquired uncertainty was not isolated independently of visible display conditions. We read the complete primary author manuscript and inspected Figure 3; no raw data were analyzed.

## What the experiment discriminates

The following is our assessment of the explanatory alternatives.

A fixed sequence and an information-sensitive sequence can yield the same final answer on an ordinary trial. They differ when an earlier step is unnecessary or when acting immediately would omit information still needed. A test of both situations therefore probes the arrangement of inquiry, not simply answer accuracy. It also makes two opposing errors visible: repeating acquisition after its purpose is fulfilled, and treating action availability as evidence that inquiry is complete.

That distinction is directly relevant to the local protocol. A positive response score cannot implement a shortcut that the admitted events exclude. Conversely, a system with optional inquiry events need not demonstrate a newly formed architecture merely by choosing among them intelligently. These are different objects of explanation: available operations, their current use, and changes to the conditions making operations available. The selected evidence bears most directly on current use. It cannot retrospectively add an omitted transition to the formal learner.

A successful alternative need not be a reflective observer inspecting an independent internal register. Learned relations among displays, movement, newly exposed material and subsequent selection could constitute the information-sensitive activity itself. A conditional associative policy is therefore a substantive rival to a unique metacognitive explanation. It can also realize the bounded functional achievement. Replacing the word “association” with “understanding,” or the reverse, does not settle which relations it actually preserves.

Visible conditions and an acquired informational state must nevertheless remain distinguishable in the analysis. If the same visible configuration always calls for the same response, behavior need not reveal whether the subject tracks what it has previously learned. Nor does swift transfer prove that the relevant organization was newly formed during this experiment; earlier learning may support it. The issue is causal history and generalization, not a demand that every adequate learner explicitly describe its uncertainty.

## A discriminator for the remaining bridge

A stronger comparison would independently vary **the usable information retained from an earlier action** while keeping the later choice display and available operations matched. Earlier access could deliver relevant information, an equally salient but uninformative result, or information whose applicability subsequently changes. The later episode would allow both further inquiry and effective action. A complete account would retain what was actually acquired, which further request was chosen, what that request exposed, and the resulting action—not merely the final answer.

This would challenge three restricted explanations: repeat the trained sequence regardless of what it accomplished; act whenever the final action is visible; or respond only to a present “missing” cue. Selective continuation after the information-bearing encounter, with renewed inquiry when the retained content becomes inapplicable, would advance acquired-information-dependent scheduling. It would not uniquely eliminate an ordinary stateful policy. Such a policy remains a serious comparator when given the same history and available operations.

The stronger claim that **consequences reorganize the acquisition interface** needs a further discriminating feature: prior outcomes change which operation or order the organism can subsequently initiate, not merely which one a fixed menu presents. This need not require new anatomy or graph edges; retained changes in timing, inhibition or effective access may suffice. Changing the menu or access rules solely in the apparatus cannot be counted as retained reorganization within the organism. An experimentally delivered encounter can still induce such reorganization; the account must trace where the change occurs. Identifying the carrier and its use matters more than calling a response second-order.

No such experiment is run here. These are proposed tests of the named claim, not new universal prerequisites for cognition. All animals and unsuccessful stages should remain visible in an eventual test; apparent rapid flexibility in a selected successful subset cannot alone characterize the original population.

## What remains locally supplied

Task correctness can operationalize whether particular information enables a particular action. It cannot alone explain why that continuation matters for the organism, still less why it is warranted for other affected activity. The experimental target, possible queries and consequences are local starting conditions of this study. They exist within the same material process; identifying them as supplied to the explanation does not make them metaphysically external.

The legitimate addition is a concrete empirical challenge to mandatory inquiry sequences and a sharper separation between selecting an available inquiry and forming a revisable inquiry interface. It preserves both successful flexibility and failure. It does not derive ethical norms from pellets, equate all continuation with increased reward, or establish completed alignment.
