## The transport problem

Suppose someone's friendship is a real good and, in a specified situation, another person ought to provide brief assistance that makes a meeting possible. If the friendship interest itself grounds the requirement, it is natural to examine whether the first person's participation must be permissible. This inference already uses a substantive claim about requirement-grounding interests; a good's mere existence is insufficient.

Even granting that step, a second inference requires care. Permission for person P to pursue this friendship does not immediately establish permission for actor A to pursue an otherwise similar friendship. P may lack any opportunity to relieve another patient's suffering while A faces such an opportunity. Their goods can be of the same kind while their complete practical situations differ. Capacity, authority, available alternatives, actual needs and valid undertakings may matter without making one person's friendship intrinsically less valuable.

The proper invariance test changes the person's identity while retaining every morally relevant feature. If the permission then changes solely because the person has been renamed, the account owes a ground for that asymmetry. Changing the identity together with effective rescue capacity or a valid responsibility is a different test. The universal minimum rejects a gate based merely on friendship or reciprocity; it has never rejected the relevance of actual capacity and competing claims.

This is a limit on an inference, not a complete answer in favor of demandingness. A demanding account that makes all comparable personal pursuits impermissible must confront the alleged ordinary aid judgments and explain what remains of their grounds. It can consistently reject some of those judgments, or distinguish their factual circumstances. Whether that revision is ethically convincing is further work. Logical consistency is not vindication.

## Two opposed tests

**Match the full situation.** Compare two equally capable persons, with the same kinds of worthwhile projects, responsibilities, means and full-history patient consequences. If the defended permission differs only with their names, one account is applying an unexplained asymmetric rule. This does not decide between a uniformly demanding rule and a uniformly permissive one.

**Match the good but change the opportunity.** One person has no permitted effective way to relieve the identified suffering; the other has one. The first person's permission to continue a project cannot by itself settle the second person's duty. Conversely, having an opportunity does not by itself settle its requiring force: the cost and any independently justified permission still matter.

Together these tests prevent two shortcuts. Equality of persons cannot erase factual differences in their situations. Differences in situations cannot be used to erase the equal moral relevance of comparable goods. A complete latitude argument must say which changed feature alters permission and why.

## A useful consequence even before the hard priority is settled

Suppose a proposed protected activity can be preserved by two available routes. One route also permits a brief, reliable, consent-compatible response ending someone's present severe unwanted suffering. It imposes only a genuinely minor additional burden over the actual relevant history and violates no other claim. The other route leaves that person suffering. The inherited [bounded rescue argument](RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-ARGUMENT.md) can require the first response without deciding whether the protected activity should itself be surrendered for further aid.

This conclusion needs all of those premises. Equal money or clock time is not proof of equal effects on concentration, rest, privacy, authority or other people. Nor does pairwise rescue imply global maximization or authorize unrelated harmful means. The result instead constrains the scope of a claimed permission: a ground that protects a project does not automatically excuse every omission occurring while that project is pursued.

The unresolved hard comparison remains the one in which preserving the good really excludes further serious relief. The current tests identify what a positive permission must justify and prohibit an easy but misleading transfer of permissions between different situations. They supplement the [latitude argument](LATITUDE-UNDER-NEED-ARGUMENT.md) and [review](LATITUDE-UNDER-NEED-REVIEW.md), with no empirical or formal verification claim.
