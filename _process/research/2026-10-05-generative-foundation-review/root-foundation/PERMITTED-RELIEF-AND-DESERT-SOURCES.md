# Permitted relief and desert: primary comparisons

6 October 2026. AI-authored research within the existing root-foundation study. The transition is **an adverse experiential occurrence and an available response → a patient-ground consideration for that response, even when desert is invoked**. Local actor/patient vocabulary does not reinstall a fundamental subject–object division. The [universal minimum](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md) remains the target; innocence is not added to its eligibility conditions.

**Finding:** a primary desert argument now concerns the provision of relief itself, rather than only permission to inflict suffering. Its explicit scope nevertheless prevents treating an adverse desert ranking as the disappearance of every patient-ground consideration. The next argument can therefore address a concrete case without assuming that all retributivists either erase or preserve that consideration.

## 2. Kagan's actual painkiller case

Shelly Kagan, *The Geometry of Desert*, Lindley Lecture, University of Kansas, **2005**; [institutional original](https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/f1117412-56a8-4560-ac33-e52864e5c773/content). Read complete printed **1–11**, including the two-dose comparison, multipliers and their revision; these are PDF 5–15. The 2012 book is not the inspected work.

**Source synopsis:** Kagan first gives scarce analgesia to the blameless worker before the culpable one, then distinguishes a retributivist who may leave a sufficiently vicious worker unaided even when both can receive relief (2–3). Zero and negative culpability multipliers express different desert rankings (4–5). Crucially, the graph excludes other intrinsic values: it represents desert's contribution, not overall value (6). Unlimited deserved suffering is then rejected in favor of bounded desert peaks (9–11). The intrinsic significance of desert is assumed for investigation, not established (1–2). Thus the aid example neither independently establishes permission to give relief nor demonstrates that its benefit has no favorable moral contribution.

## 3. Hurka's explicit retention of the component bad

Thomas Hurka, “Desert: Individualistic and Holistic,” in *Desert and Justice* (2003), 45–68, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199259762.003.0003). Relied-on reading: [author manuscript](https://thomashurka.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/desert-holistic-and-individualistic.pdf), complete **1–2, 8–9**. Locators are manuscript/PDF pages, not published pages.

**Source synopsis:** Hurka characterizes desert value as additional value in combinations: a vicious person's pain and vice are two evils, while their conjunction in one life adds a desert-good (2). This is a substantive account of the components and their relation, not a neutral consequence of recognizing desert. He also distinguishes whole-life moral desert from legal retributive desert attached to specific criminal acts and specified punishments (8–9). It therefore matters whether an argument concerns suffering, a life, or a sentence. The selected passage supports the possibility of retaining suffering's badness while valuing deserved punishment; it supplies no complete moral justification of an independent actor's relief.

## 4. What the comparison changes

The strongest challenge can now use an intervention that genuinely ends an adverse experience, has no scarcity cost for another patient, and is available to an independent responder. That is a better test than moving from justified punishment to a general claim about a wrongdoer's welfare. The case still requires an independently warranted permission judgment; enough medicine does not establish that judgment.

Keep three propositions separate:

| Proposition | Consequence for the current minimum |
|---|---|
| A particular relief is impermissible because it defeats an independently justified claim. | The proposed eligible-relief conditions are not all present. This does not erase the patient's adversity. |
| Relief has a patient-ground advantage, but a contrary ground makes withholding it better or required. | The minimum consideration survives; the verdict about what to do is a further question. |
| The experience remains locally adverse and relief remains permitted, but its patient benefit contributes no favorable moral consideration. | This directly challenges the proposed universal patient-ground contribution. It needs a defense of the missing contribution, not just a ranking against relief. |

The third position cannot be attributed to a source merely because its desert-specific ranking reaches zero or becomes negative. Nor can it be rejected merely because a component-retaining theory gives a different account. The competing explanation must say whether moral assessment preserves local adversity as a ground, treats its moral significance as conditional, or includes it only within an indivisible evaluation of the whole response. These are substantive alternatives.

This also disciplines an affirmative argument. Writing a negative suffering term and a positive desert term would build in the preservation being defended. The positive account instead needs to explain why ending this actual adverse episode continues to count for this response when culpability and the alleged deserved amount remain fixed, even if relief changes whether that desert is fulfilled. It may then acknowledge further grounds governing the verdict. The mere intelligibility of a whole-state ranking establishes neither a patient-ground reason nor its absence.

Likewise, a reduction in a legal sentence, forgiveness by a victim and analgesia for an offender are different acts. An account of permission for one cannot silently authorize the others. A finding about the imposition of deserved hardship does not settle who may relieve which experience afterward.

## 5. Decisive next test

Use the same concrete relief comparison for both arguments. Identify the actual adverse episode, the response and its effect, the precise alleged desert object, and the independent permission ground. Then ask what changes in moral justification when the episode is relieved while culpability, the alleged deserved amount and the permission basis remain fixed. Do not pretend that fulfilling suffering-based desert also remains unchanged when the suffering is removed.

An affirmative explanation must justify the persistence of the patient-ground consideration without equating it with the final verdict. A successful contrary explanation must justify its extinction while preserving both real benefit and permission. If permission is instead denied, or a competing claim prevails, that is a different result and must be recorded as such. No “undeserved suffering” restriction is silently inserted into eligibility.

The new contribution is the directly relevant primary aid case and the explicit component-retaining alternative, with their limits. It narrows the next defense to a real philosophical disagreement. It does not establish an assumption-free universal minimum, decide a general theory of punishment, or add a new formal result.
