## Three primary sources

### 1. Organizational renewal

Matteo Mossio, Cristian Saborido and Alvaro Moreno, “An Organizational Account of Biological Functions,” *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 60 (2009), 813–841; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axp036), [research-group PDF](https://www.ias-research.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Mossio_25592036.pdf).

**Fresh access:** web PDF opening failed; ordinary HTTPS retrieval and local text extraction succeeded. Read printed 820–827 and 834–837, including the displayed notes. No full-article or facsimile reading is claimed.

**Source claim:** Organization contributes to sustaining the conditions enabling its own activity. Closure is context-dependent, not independence from surrounding conditions (824, note 5). Biological functions additionally require differentiated contributions; enzymes and other maintained structures participate in the organization renewing them (825–827). Reproduction raises a further explanatory problem because its contribution can extend beyond the producing individual. The proposed historical/ecological higher-order account is expressly unfinished in this paper (834–835).

**Limit:** This is a theoretical account of functional norms, not an experimental demonstration that every system must generate novelty, or that continued organization is universally ethically warranted. Its renewal relation strongly matches the proposed explanatory bridge without establishing the stronger ethical identification.

### 2. Diversity and climatic disturbance

Forest Isbell and colleagues, “Biodiversity increases the resistance of ecosystem productivity to climate extremes,” *Nature* 526 (2015), 574–577; [publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature15374), [US Forest Service PDF](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/53554.pdf).

**Fresh access:** read the abstract and complete main argumentative text, 574–576, including table/figure-caption text, through the 16-page PDF's searchable text. A later Methods navigation failed. Methods, extended-data pages, raw data and figure pixels were not inspected.

**Reported evidence:** The analysis combines 46 experiments manipulating grassland plant diversity in Europe and North America. Across climate events, productivity changed approximately 50% in communities with 1–2 species and 25% with 16–32 species. Diversity strengthened resistance; recovery showed no consistent diversity advantage. After prolonged wet events, higher diversity could reduce the study's recovery measure. Both richness levels often recovered or overshot within a year.

**Limit:** This supports a particular diversity–stability relationship, with productivity and disturbance precisely specified. It measures neither production of new kinds nor the entire quality of an ecosystem's continuation. Neither universal recovery benefits nor moral superiority follows. These are the authors' results, not our replication.

### 3. Renewal, adaptive regulation and prospective significance

Ezequiel A. Di Paolo, “Autopoiesis, adaptivity, teleology, agency,” *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 4 (2005), 429–452; [author-hosted published PDF](https://ezequieldipaolo.net/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/autopoiesis_teleology_2005.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-005-9002-y).

**Fresh access:** searchable 24-page PDF; read complete 436–440, selected 434–435 and 441–445 passages, the displayed concluding passages at 447–448, and note 8 at 450. The prior complete main-text/notes reading remains separately credited above. No facsimile or empirical replication.

**Source argument:** Continued self-production does not by itself explain active discrimination of deteriorating tendencies. Adaptivity adds sensitivity and compensating regulation, permitting several viable conditions rather than requiring one optimum. The author explicitly allows autopoietic survival without adaptivity; internal adaptive regulation alone also does not establish agency (note 8). The proposed extensions to nonmetabolic values and temporally extended reproduction are speculative.

**Limit:** The paper offers a route from renewal to operational significance, while preventing survival, intelligence, novelty and moral warrant from being collapsed into one term. It does not prove that every continuing process must learn or acquire richer possibilities.

## Synthesis for the universal inference

The following is our philosophical reconstruction, not an additional reported finding. Ren's suggestion becomes strongest when its dependency is identified in a particular history: which activities maintain or transform which conditions, and how those conditions permit later life. Biological renewal supplies a substantive candidate connection. The ecological result makes diversity a credible contributor in a tested regime. Neither licenses replacing all relevant relationships with a count of individuals, species, outputs or available options.

The chosen analytical scales preserve different relationships. A metabolic account follows relatively enduring organization and its differentiated contributions; an ecological account follows interactions and productivity across a community. These are selections within connected activity, not fundamentally separate organism/world domains. The selected evidence does not yet reconstruct their complete joint history or humanity's institutional, technological and cultural renewal. That omission identifies a correspondence task, not a refutation of the proposed whole account.

The affirmative ethical route is that continuing life can depend on conditions constituted through shared activity, so practical reflection formed within that activity can recognize consequential support and destruction beyond its initiating position. The live warrant question is why those real dependencies constitute ethical quality and reasons with universal scope. Calling them constitutive does not settle that question; neither does a hostile stipulation that the evaluator simply prefers something else defeat the strongest proposal. A substantive rival must retain the actual shared formation, differentiated consequences and proposed ethical significance, then explain those relationships better.

The next central comparison should therefore hold the relevant whole history in view while contrasting renewal that sustains differentiated lives and future formation with renewal that maintains an oppressive or destructive organization. It must show why their difference warrants a practical response, including where ending or transforming a particular organization supports the wider continuation. Source support for renewal can inform that test; source citation cannot serve as its missing ethical argument. This round leaves universal necessity and the stronger no-assumptions objective open while identifying a concrete, evidence-sensitive route forward.
