## A positive connection within the existing law

The [previous round](ORGANIZATIONAL-REQUIREMENT-INTEGRATION.md) identified
condition-sensitive copying, repair and capture already operating in the native
process. Its completed acquired reader cannot rewrite its filled cells through
the declared events. The organization contains another consequential relation:
which template occupies the native site. That relation can change.

An A resident can use captured local support to produce another A template.
When the original resident disappears, free A and H templates compete to occupy
the site. Their actual abundance enters the association rates. The earlier
material product therefore changes the distribution from which a later
response carrier forms. Its effect continues after the earlier carrier is gone.
The new resident then governs which local copying reaction can occur and
whether immediate repair is available.

This relation was present in the [formation source](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/process.py);
it is reconstructed and tested here, not added as a new recipe. No success
score, desired-type flag, maintenance quota or source-to-common conversion
enters the native reactions. Formation, retained consequence and later response
are differentiated phases of one material history.

This also fixes the scope change precisely. The native resident is a different
physical carrier from the acquired reader. Showing that it can be reconstructed
does not discharge the reader's missing rewriting pathway. It supplies a
positive same-law instance of the broader generative connection rather than
declaring the installed reader revisable by renaming another component.

## What the formation bias actually says

Let a,h be free counts at an empty native site and let F>0 be common support.
Under the source's unit association coefficient, the rates are aF and hF.
Conditional on the next event being an association, the probability of A is
`a/(a+h)`. For a+h>0, retaining one additional A changes it by

\[
\frac{a+1}{a+h+1}-\frac{a}{a+h}
=\frac{h}{(a+h)(a+h+1)}.
\]

It is positive when H is present. The reached contrast is35/71 versus34/70,
with difference18/2485. This is an elementary hand identity specialized to the
actual formation law, not a novel general selection theorem.

Other native events can occur first. Including all their competing rates, the
probability that the **very next event** is A association is
`614687500/1260313769` in the copy branch and
`2388500000/4971009923` in the loss branch. These differ from the conditional
shares above. Neither answers which type will eventually associate after
arbitrarily many intervening changes, or how often the whole earlier formation
and acquisition sequence reaches this comparison.

### Post-replay hand generalization with all competing channels

The positive immediate-race sign has a more general source-based proof. This
deduction was added after the first replay; it is not another prospectively
specified experiment. Independent algebraic review checks it separately.

For empty residence, an intact site and local support0, write n=a+h,
P=M−n, with native material capacity M, common support F>0 and source E≥0.
Take the unit fast coefficients and common positive slow coefficient ε of the
active source law. The total exit rate is

\[
T(n)=Fn+E+\varepsilon\{Fn(M-n)+n+1+F\}.
\]

The terms are association, capture, bulk copying, free-template wear, damage
and common leakage. Formation is closed by intactness, local copying by empty
residence, and resident wear by the absence of a resident.

Compare valid states with a and a+1 free A templates, keeping h,M,F,E fixed.
For n+1≤M, the difference in next-event A-association probability is

\[
\frac{(a+1)F}{T(n+1)}-\frac{aF}{T(n)}
=\frac{F D}{T(n)T(n+1)},
\]

where direct subtraction gives

\[
D=Fh+E+\varepsilon\{F[hP+a(n+1)]+h+1+F\}>0.
\]

Both exit rates are positive. Thus the immediate association bias persists
when every competing channel is included under these specific coefficients
and site conditions. The compared states alone require n+1≤M. Realizing them
through the preceding active-resident copy-versus-loss and resident-wear words
additionally needs space for that resident and the copy; in the empty baseline
this is P≥2, as the actual history supplies. No unperformed copy from an empty
site is credited. Different kinetic coefficients or site conditions require
their own derivation; longer-run adaptation and ethical direction do not follow.

## Why a material total is not the whole organization

Within either branch, compare the reached states after A versus H association
and the next capture. They agree in total A/H counts, intactness, local/common/
source support, expenditure and the complete acquired apparatus. They differ
in which template occupies the site. From A residence, local A copying reaches
one particular projected successor; from H residence, the summed rate to that
same projected successor is zero. The rate pair is1 versus0 after retained
copying, and2 versus0 after local loss. Bulk A copying has a different support
change, so it does not supply the missing rate into this same successor class.

Consequently the projection retaining those totals and accounts while omitting
resident placement is not an exact Markov quotient valid at both reached
states. Such a quotient would require equal aggregate rates into every
projected successor class. Retaining placement, relevant history or an adequate
conditional distribution can resolve the representational omission. This is
not a failure of every scalar description or an independent metaphysical
domain called organization.

The result makes core/tree reciprocity concrete: activity changes the material
relations from which later activity forms, and those relations cannot always
be replaced by totals. A core articulation that retained only expenditure and
abundance would omit the very constitution through which the response changes.

## The next unresolved explanation

The bounded native connection is now **consequence retention changes the
formation and use of a response carrier**. The particular acquired-reader
connection remains separate: its declared lifecycle supplies no operation that
reconstructs its stored correspondence. An observation-preserving composition
cannot map an invariant reader output to a selector trace whose output changes.
Showing a different physical carrier of that function, with actual observation
and later use, could change this assessment; silently importing a renewable
apparatus cannot.

For the foundation, the sharper task is to explain when retained consequences
produce **context-sensitive revision of the relation being maintained**, beyond
changing its reconstruction abundance. Existing learned-coupling and inquiry
results provide conditional examples; their physical functions and application

generate that revision, so no endless chain of meta-controllers is required.
