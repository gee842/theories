This follows the [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). An encounter can produce a distinction while changing the conditions through which other distinctions could be acquired. Questions, retained answers and subsequent activity are differentiated relations within that continuing configuration.

This note does **not** install a new criterion-revision mechanism. The [existing acquisition learner](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/acquisition/model.py) already forms distinguishing suffixes through inconsistency; its final gate remains bounded conformance testing. The concurrent [concern inquiry revision](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/INQUIRY-REVISION.md) changes the actual six-encounter requirement to four under its supplied occupancy-to-rewrite law. Those are substantive results with different scopes. This study neither duplicates them nor redescribes them as wholly absent.

The present contribution is an exact constraint on the further formation question: even in a finite deterministic configuration, the questions individually answerable through an encounter need not have one greatest jointly attainable refinement. Consequently, a proposal to acquire a sufficient inquiry criterion cannot always replace relevance by “obtain the most informative available answer.” The partial order itself can have several incompatible maximal possibilities. The result is a conditional structural implication, not a proof of what continuation deserves priority, a new formalism of all mathematical truth, or a claim of mathematical priority.

## 1. Fix the target through the changing inquiry

Use the finite labeled-belief setup of [the inquiry-continuation proof](INQUIRY-CONTINUATION-MATH.md#1-what-is-to-be-identified-must-persist-through-the-investigation). Let \(L\) be the finite nonempty set of initial target labels, and let \(B_0\subseteq L\times X\) be the initial belief, with every \(\ell\in L\) represented. Current configuration \(x\) changes during inquiry; initial label \(\ell\) remains attached to its history. All currently accessible information belongs to the information state, and every consequential change relevant to the claim belongs to \(x\).

Actions have their specified observation/successor relation. An admissible finite inquiry uses a common feasible action at every encountered belief, branches only on accessible retained observations, and satisfies whatever prefix conditions the fixed application contract requires. Actual observable failures and their effects are successors, rather than vanished attempts. For this note a completed inquiry must return a correct answer on every admitted execution; probabilistic or partial-success alternatives are different contracts. Its finite decision tree is an informational and transition-level object. Claiming a physically formed controller also requires its construction, storage, execution and reporting means.

A question \(f:L\to V\) induces the partition of \(L\) into equal-answer classes. Thus questions with the same answer partition ask for the same target distinction at this scope, even if their answer names differ. Write a partition as \(P\), and define

\[
P\preceq Q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every block of }Q\text{ is contained in a block of }P.
\tag{K1}
\]

Under this convention \(Q\) is at least as informative as \(P\). The trivial partition \(\mathbf 0=\{L\}\) asks for no distinction. The discrete partition \(\mathbf 1=\{\{\ell\}:\ell\in L\}\) identifies the initial target completely. The join is the common refinement

\[
P\vee Q=\{C\cap D:C\in P,\ D\in Q,\ C\cap D\ne\varnothing\}.
\tag{K2}
\]

Call \(P\) **attainable from \(B_0\)** when one admissible finite inquiry has, at every terminal leaf, initial labels contained within a single block of \(P\). Let \(\mathscr A(B_0)\) be the family of attainable partitions. Ending an informational episode without further action is allowed here, so \(\mathbf 0\in\mathscr A(B_0)\). If an application requires an executed terminal response, include its feasibility and effects in the terminal contract; this note does not make physically unavailable reporting or response operations free.

This definition keeps the initial target fixed. If inquiry causes two current configurations to merge, their initial labels can still require different answers. Conversely, a question about current remaining possibilities has a different target and can have become trivial. [The earlier C4 result](INQUIRY-CONTINUATION-MATH.md#proposition-c4--current-behavioral-collapse-is-not-initial-identification) explains why these questions must not be substituted for one another.

## 2. Attainability has a partial order, but need not have a greatest element

### Proposition K1 — Downward closure

If \(Q\in\mathscr A(B_0)\) and \(P\preceq Q\), then \(P\in\mathscr A(B_0)\).

**Proof.** Use the same inquiry. Each terminal label set lies in one block of \(Q\), and therefore in one block of \(P\). Relabeling its logical answer supplies the coarser answer. If that relabeling is claimed as a physical operation, its implementation belongs to the application contract. ∎

There are only finitely many partitions of finite \(L\). Hence every attainable partition lies below a maximal attainable partition. A **greatest** attainable partition, however, would have to refine every attainable partition at once. Existence of maximal members does not give such a greatest member.

### Proposition K2 — A missing join prevents a greatest attainable partition

If \(P,Q\in\mathscr A(B_0)\) but \(P\vee Q\notin\mathscr A(B_0)\), then \(\mathscr A(B_0)\) has no greatest element.

**Proof.** If \(G\) were greatest, both \(P\preceq G\) and \(Q\preceq G\) would hold. The join is their least common refinement, so \(P\vee Q\preceq G\). Downward closure would make the join attainable, a contradiction. ∎

The inference concerns a common experimental starting condition. It does not combine answers from separate executions as if they had been acquired in one history. Doing that would assume the continuation or reproducibility relation at issue.

### Corollary K2a — Exact order criterion for a greatest attainable question

For finite \(L\), a nonempty downward-closed family of partitions has a greatest member if and only if it is closed under binary joins.

**Proof.** If \(G\) is greatest, then for any members \(P,Q\), both are below \(G\), so \(P\vee Q\preceq G\). Downward closure puts their join in the family. Conversely, there are finitely many partitions of finite \(L\). Binary join closure therefore makes the join of all members a member; it is greatest by the definition of join. ∎

This is an exact criterion for the order structure. An actual restoration mechanism is one sufficient way to establish join closure, as K4a shows below; it is not necessary merely because the order criterion holds.

## 3. The existing one-packet apparatus has two incomparable maximal questions

Use the native apparatus and fixed comparison conditions of [C2–C5a](INQUIRY-CONTINUATION-MATH.md#3-the-apparatus-after-its-existing-sensor-investigation). Sensor inquiry has established common current phase, output reversal parity and reset behavior. Four residual parameter configurations give three initial behavioral targets:

| Initial target | Probe at learned output 1 | Probe at learned output 0 |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(A\): open, no readiness reversal | 1 | 0 |
| \(B\): open, readiness reversed | 0 | 1 |
| \(C\): blocked, either readiness parameterization | 0 | 0 |

The target labels are intervention-response classes fixed before probing. Exactly one source packet remains. Either probe consumes it; sensor movement and reset do not restore it. On each negative branch the unresolved pair has identical subsequent accessible receipts, sensor outcomes, source denials and fuel consequences. The statement includes accessible fuel information rather than concealing it. Arbitrarily ample ordinary fuel and computation cannot replace the missing source packet under this law.

Define

\[
P_A=\{\{A\},\{B,C\}\},\qquad
P_B=\{\{B\},\{A,C\}\},\qquad
P_C=\{\{C\},\{A,B\}\}.
\tag{K3}
\]

### Theorem K3 — Exact attainable family for the one-packet comparison

At the represented interface, allowing the ordinary support required by the individual tests,

\[
\mathscr A(B_0)=\{\mathbf 0,P_A,P_B\}.
\tag{K4}
\]

In particular, \(P_A\) and \(P_B\) are incomparable maximal attainable partitions, while

\[
P_A\vee P_B=\mathbf 1
\notin\mathscr A(B_0).
\tag{K5}
\]

**Proof.** Immediate termination attains \(\mathbf 0\). Probing at learned output 1 attains \(P_A\); probing at output 0 attains \(P_B\). All three pairs of initial targets are distinguishable by one of these alternative tests.

There are five partitions of a three-element set: the trivial and discrete partitions and the three displayed two-block partitions. C3 rules out the discrete partition. To exclude \(P_C\), observe that all preprobe histories have equal accessible observations across \(A,B,C\). Thus any deterministic policy reaches the same first probe phase in all three cases. A probe at output 1 has a negative branch containing \(B,C\); a probe at output 0 has a negative branch containing \(A,C\). In either case that negative pair is forever indistinguishable after source consumption, but its members lie in different blocks of \(P_C\). Stopping without probing also fails to answer \(P_C\). No admissible identifying tree for \(P_C\) therefore exists. This exhausts the partitions. ∎

The same exclusions hold for a randomized controller required to have zero error and almost-sure termination for each initial target. Couple its random tape across the finite set of initial targets: a tape in the intersection of their probability-one success sets would give a deterministic successful controller, contradicting the respective impossibility. This is the argument of C3a, applied to a partition rather than only complete identification.

The two maximal questions have the same number of blocks, but that numerical equality is not the reason no greatest question exists. Their answer distinctions cross, and the experiment that answers one removes the route needed to settle the unresolved branch of the other. A scalar uncertainty measure would add a comparison convention; it would not restore a physically absent jointly executable inquiry.

The blocked readiness parameterizations remain behaviorally equivalent even with extra packets under the declared interface. They are not counted as distinct labels here. Conversely, after consumption two initial labels can have equal current future behavior. Neither fact turns the three initial profiles into mere freely chosen names.

## 4. When sequentially combining attainable questions is warranted

The missing join is not inevitable in every configuration. It depends on how encounters change the conditions of further inquiry.

### Proposition K4 — A sufficient continuation condition for a join

Suppose an admissible inquiry attains \(P\). At each terminal history \(h\), let \(B_h\) be its actual successor belief, with initial target labels still attached. If there is an admissible finite continuation from every such \(B_h\) that identifies its surviving labels up to \(Q\), while preserving the first answer and all required prefix conditions, then \(P\vee Q\) is attainable from \(B_0\).

**Proof.** Attach the corresponding continuation to each terminal leaf of the first finite tree. Each combined terminal history preserves its \(P\)-block and identifies a \(Q\)-block. Its possible initial labels lie in their intersection, which is a block of (K2). There are finitely many first-stage leaves and each appended tree is finite, so the combined tree is finite. Common feasibility, prefix admissibility and exact retained evidence hold by the hypotheses. ∎

This is sufficient, not necessary: another inquiry could attain the join without having the chosen first experiment as its prefix. The proposition does not require literal return to an earlier physical state. It requires an appropriate continuation from the state the first inquiry actually produced.

A stronger reusable-experiment premise can supply that condition: after each inquiry, a finite admissible restoration procedure returns the relevant testing configuration to its initial comparison condition for the **same target**, preserves the acquired answer, and leaves the next inquiry executable on every remaining candidate. The exact retained transcript must update candidate labels correctly. The procedure must work and terminate for all those candidates, including ones outside any selected pair. Its effects and required conditions must be included. Calling a command `reset` does not establish these properties; the existing apparatus's reset changes phase and does not restore a source packet.

### Corollary K4a — Reuse gives join closure and a greatest attainable partition

If the reusable-experiment premise applies to every pair of attainable inquiries, then \(\mathscr A(B_0)\) is closed under finite joins. For finite \(L\), it therefore has a greatest element,

\[
G=\bigvee_{P\in\mathscr A(B_0)}P.
\tag{K6}
\]

**Proof.** Restore after an inquiry attaining \(P\), then execute one attaining \(Q\). A policy valid for all initial candidates remains valid when restricted to the surviving candidates, and the retained first answer can be combined with the second. K4 gives the binary join. Induction gives finite joins. There are finitely many partitions of finite \(L\), so (K6) is a finite attainable join and refines every member. ∎

This premise includes sufficient conditions for the successive restorations; it is not a free renewal claim. In the particular apparatus, two **initially supplied** packets suffice for the join of \(P_A,P_B\): test output 1, and on a negative result test output 0 using the remaining packet. That is the existing C5 counterfactual. It does not add a packet to the actual one-packet history, implement replenishment, or prove arbitrary repeatability.

## 5. Knowing the demand before acting differs from learning it after access changes

Let \(\mathcal F\) be a finite family of target questions, with partitions \(P_f\). There are two different contracts:

1. **Demand available first:** after being told which \(f\) is required, choose an inquiry for that question. This requires
   \[
   \forall f\in\mathcal F\ \exists\pi_f\quad\pi_f\text{ answers }f.
   \tag{K7}
   \]
2. **One inquiry before disclosure:** perform a single inquiry independently of which question will be required, preserve its accessible result, and later answer whichever \(f\) is disclosed without further target-dependent encounters. This requires one common preparation policy and a decoder for each later demand.

The second contract can describe a consumed opportunity or a fixed-record replay task. Its restriction must be established in an application. It must not be silently imposed when the system could wait for the demand and then investigate, or could retain an unused opportunity for subsequent demand-dependent interaction.

### Theorem K5 — Universal later decoding requires the joint question

Under the second contract, exact answers for all later demands are possible if and only if the one preparatory inquiry attains

\[
P_{\mathcal F}=\bigvee_{f\in\mathcal F}P_f,
\tag{K8}
\]

with sufficient accessible retention of that terminal distinction.

**Proof.** If the same retained result could arise from two target labels with different answers to some \(f\), no decoder receiving only that result and \(f\) could always answer correctly. Thus each retained-result fiber lies within one block of every \(P_f\), hence within one block of their join. This proves necessity. Conversely, a retained join block determines its containing block in every \(P_f\), so each requested answer can be decoded. ∎

This factorization principle is inherited from [meta-revision M2](META-REVISION-MATH.md#proposition-m2--universal-exact-replay-for-a-question-family-is-profile-sufficiency), which already explains exact replay through all accessible retained information. The new implication here is about **which joint profiles can be acquired through one effectful history**, before any subsequent compression. Retaining the complete accessible transcript does not remove the one-packet obstruction: the missing distinction was never acquired on its negative branch.

For \(\mathcal F=\{\text{“is }A\text{?”},\text{“is }B\text{?”}\}\), (K7) holds: select the corresponding probe after the demand is known. But a single probe before demand disclosure cannot guarantee the answer to either possible later question. Their join is discrete and unattainable by K3. After a test for \(A\) returns negative, a later demand concerning \(B\) remains unresolved; the symmetric failure holds in the other direction.

**Waiting is a genuine escape when available.** If the demand can be obtained before spending the packet, choose the matching test. If it is disclosed after an actual source-consuming probe, the changed native law supplies no further distinguishing access on the negative branch. A proposal to force earlier commitment needs its own independently identified timing or continuation relation; this theorem supplies none. Likewise, when additional encounters remain possible after disclosure, analyze those actual successor configurations using K4 or the earlier labeled-belief recurrence instead of applying fixed-record K5 indiscriminately.

## 6. Consequences for a formed criterion of sufficient inquiry

An operative criterion can treat different unresolved questions as requiring further encounter. K3 shows why its acquisition cannot always be reduced to finding one universally most informative executable inquiry: such an inquiry may not exist. K4 identifies the additional continuation relation that can make separate questions jointly settleable. K5 identifies why an actual later demand can expose a previously unacquired distinction even when the earlier inquiry succeeded exactly at its own question and retained every accessible consequence.

This is a substantive constraint on criterion formation, not a new mechanism selecting a criterion. To explain why one maximal question governs activity, a construction must connect that question to independently identified continuing consequences, encounters, constraints or requests in the same history. An analyst's numerical preference cannot silently perform that causal or justificatory work. Equally, the absence of a greatest attainable question does not establish that the actual situation contains no grounds for prioritizing one: its particular dependencies may supply them. Their effects must be shown to form or revise the operative criterion, with causal controls separating that revision from ordinary belief filtering or a changed current phase.

The concurrent concern construction already changes an operative prerequisite through retained consequences. Its criterion's wider selection and scope remain questions for its own continuing history. The present note supplies a complementary mathematical constraint and does not replace that mechanism with another supplied controller. The weaker retention theorem, the destructive-probe obstruction and the conditional reusable case remain distinct contributions within one line of investigation.

Local premises remain visible: a finite initial target and its independently specified interpretation; an adequate configuration representation; the available encounters and observation channels; a fixed correctness and prefix contract; the one-packet source law for K3; and actual restoration and retention conditions where K4a is invoked. These laws are local model inputs, not declarations that their referents lie outside generative reality. Ordinary partition and decision-tree reasoning is used without claiming that the model derives its own whole mathematical background.

The conclusions would change if a new accessible consequence distinguished a surviving negative pair, if an actual nonconsuming interaction or source-restoration path made the missing joint inquiry feasible, or if the intended target were shown to concern only current behavior rather than the initial response profile. Those are substantive revisions to the represented relation or target. Finding a correct uniform decoder for both late questions using only an actual retained one-probe transcript under the stated law would refute the obstruction. Reassigning labels without changing their independently specified intervention profiles would not.
