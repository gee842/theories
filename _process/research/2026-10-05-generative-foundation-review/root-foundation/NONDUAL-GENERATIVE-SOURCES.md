## 2. 《易傳》: generation, goodness and formed nature

**Directly verified passages, retained in Chinese:**

> 一陰一陽之謂道：繼之者善也，成之者性也。
>
> 生生之謂易。

These occur within [《繫辭上》第五章](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=易傳/繫辭上&oldid=2611140). The received [《繫辭下》第一章](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=易傳/繫辭下&oldid=2611136) includes:

> 天地之大德曰生。

**Bounded synopsis:** The first passage connects alternating yin and yang, continuation as 善, and completion as 性; its paragraph also names renewed generation as 易. It distinguishes the activity stirring things from the sage's concern. The second links heaven and earth's great 德 with 生, then turns immediately to the sage's office, 仁, resources and 義. Generation, evaluation and differentiated completion thus appear together, within a cosmological, divinatory and political text.

**Scope:** Chinese wording is primary here. The English synopsis is our explanatory gloss, not a quotation from a published translation. Neither 善 nor 德 is silently equated with a contemporary impartial moral-reason predicate. The websites reproduce a received text; their traditional attribution to Confucius is not independent authorship evidence. No manuscript collation or reconstruction of a unique original meaning was performed.

## 3. 《中論》: dependency also constrains the proposed root

**Primary edition:** *Zhonglun*, attributed to Nāgārjuna, with commentary attributed to Qingmu, translated into Chinese by Kumārajīva; *Taishō* volume 30, no. 1564. Read the [CBETA electronic edition hosted by National Taiwan University](https://dlbs.liberal.ntu.edu.tw/BDLM/sutra/html/T30/T30n1564.htm), version 1.14, completed 5 November 2001. Root verses and explanatory prose are distinguishable in the transcription.

The verse corresponding to the familiar XXIV.18 passage, at 33b12–13, reads:

> 眾因緣生法，我說即是無；亦為是假名，亦是中道義。

This edition actually prints **無** in the verse. Its following prose at 33b15–20 uses **空**, including **「空亦復空」**. We retain that difference rather than silently substituting the familiar wording. The prose is commentary, not another root verse or an independent translation of the Sanskrit.

**Bounded synopsis:** Chapter 24 answers the objection that emptiness destroys causation, the four truths and practice. It retains conventional discourse, connects conditional arising with emptiness and designation, and argues that fixed intrinsic natures would obstruct arising, cessation and cultivation. The commentary also denies making emptiness self-standing. Chapter 13's closing passage warns against attachment to an emptiness view. This is a soteriological argument, not an endorsement of maximizing production or a proof of our ethical theory.

**Actual scope:** chapter 24, Taishō 32b12–33c16, including verses and commentary; chapter 13's closing exchange, 18c06–28; title, attribution and edition metadata. These are selected passages, not the complete work. No Sanskrit–Chinese critical comparison or modern English translation was independently read in this round.

## 4. What these alternatives change in our inquiry

The following is our reconstruction. It is not an additional doctrine attributed to either text.

The first change concerns explanatory order. Instead of giving an independent subject a neutral world and asking how it adds value, investigate how situated undergoing, object distinction, response and evaluation become differentiated within an ongoing reality. The earlier actor and patient indices may remain useful results of that explanation. Their convenience in a local model does not entitle them to become unexplained foundational divisions.

The second concerns the meaning of a common root. A unified explanation need not posit a single independently existing thing that produces everything else. Replacing a primitive ego with a cosmic owner, universal subject or self-sufficient generative substance would preserve much of the structure under challenge. We can investigate mutually dependent formation and transformation without claiming that one total entity literally experiences everyone's lives.

The third concerns evaluative articulation. The constructive hypothesis is stronger than “everything is connected”: certain modes of continuation, formation and response may constitute flourishing or injury within the very processes that realize them. If defensible, the ethical account could articulate those relations rather than import a sovereign evaluator's preferences. This is a research possibility, not yet a derivation. Calling successful persistence 善 would only conceal a selection criterion unless the account explains why exploitative persistence fails it.

A fourth change is methodological. Nonduality cannot be assessed only by asking whether it accepts a preselected Western classification. Nor should a Chinese or Buddhist sentence be treated as exempt from counterexamples. The comparison can revise which distinctions are starting assumptions, which are formed achievements and what counts as an explanation. It can also expose a false demand for an ultimate substance without showing that causal specification, experience identification or ethical justification are unnecessary.

## 5. Keep the productive differences

The two texts should place pressure on one another within our reconstruction. Giving generative continuation a positive interpretation does not establish that every continuation is good. Ending a destructive process can be favorable even when it reduces that process's growth. Conversely, rejecting self-standing natures does not tell us which dependencies to preserve, which burdens to relieve or which capacities to cultivate.

This prevents an easy synthesis in which 生生 and emptiness become synonyms for a benevolent universal process. Their actual arguments and purposes differ. A common research question can survive that difference: how can differentiated, consequential lives and warranted responses be explained without independent proprietors or an evaluator outside reality?

A useful next comparison holds ordinary causal success fixed while contrasting mutual support with successful extraction, truthful correction with enforced harmony, and genuine relief with elimination of the sufferer's continuing life. These are our proposed cases, not textual examples or completed experiments. A candidate that labels every successful regime generative-good fails the first contrast. A candidate that dissolves all differentiated stakes into one whole fails the latter contrasts. A stronger account must explain the relevant difference without assuming benevolent preferences at its root.
