## 1. The connection under test

The transition is organizational dependence → retained bodily effects → later discrimination and motion → altered microbial conditions → subsequent living organization. Molecular processing, neuronal expression, locomotion, bacterial growth and measurement interventions are differentiated relations within this unfolding. There is no independent cognitive chooser outside the body. “Inside” and “outside” identify transport locations relative to a measured body boundary; neither is a separately constituted realm.

This study tests factual premises needed for practical judgments about consequences. A more persistent memory, greater microbial abundance or larger worm population is not automatically an ethical improvement. Pathogenicity, competition, loss of beneficial food and finite nutrient support remain distinct consequences; normative warrant is not supplied by a fitted curve.

The [reproducible package](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/biological-scale-correspondence/renewal/README.md) contains plans, original-source bindings, code, per-observation values, predictions and figure. These are secondary analyses of published experiments, not new biological experiments conducted here. All new numerical results below are empirical computations, not new mathematical theorems.

## 2. A molecular route to retained response

Sengupta et al. (2024), [Pv1 and inherited avoidance](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011178), connects a bacterial small RNA, its uptake/processing, `maco-1` sequence correspondence, ASI neuronal reporter expression and altered choice. The original workbook and article are [preserved](../../../../sources/biological-scale-correspondence/pgen-1011178/README.md). All **6,503 numeric cells** agree between openpyxl and direct XML decoding.

The study reports that exposure to *P. vranovensis* GRb0427 or Pv1-expressing *E. coli* induces avoidance and its inheritance. Removing the matching 16-nucleotide region, or changing the Pv1 sequence while retaining its predicted secondary structure, disrupts the response. The mismatch construct has seven substitutions, four within the match. Pathway mutants test uptake, processing and transport. These interventions give the proposed connection more substance than merely adding a free “memory” coordinate to a model; the detailed kinetics and full pathway are not thereby identified.

Our extraction of Figure 3E preserves each choice-assay plate, including the last F4-trained value in J299:

Different weighting need not equal the pooled contrast. Missing plate-to-day assignments prevent reconstructing that weighting or pairing arbitrary rows.

Within each imaging panel, trained/control mean ASI fluorescence ratios are **1.438884, 1.591266, 1.139662 and 1.008408** for F1, F2, F4 and F5 respectively. The methods permit different exposure settings across generations. Each dot is a neuron, with no retained two-neuron-to-worm identity. Consequently, these ratios are descriptive within-panel contrasts; absolute GFP values cannot be fitted as a common cross-generation state, and neurons cannot be treated as independent animals.

We report no new p-values from unidentified batches. These are complementary assay-level observations, not a per-animal fitted neuron-to-action function.

Failures and costs remain: only a subset of tested pathogenic species induced learning, and inheritance was narrower still. Trained animals also avoid a beneficial *Pseudomonas* species. Disappearance by F5 therefore cannot be called either simple degeneration or proven optimal forgetting. Forced-exposure survival assays do not isolate the survival effect of freely enacted avoidance.

## 3. Replication changes the required description

Primary-source comparison is essential here. [Gainey, Shubin and Hunter (2025)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.100254) obtained parental/F1 effects but not reliable F2 persistence despite testing alternative strains and conditions. Their reporter and behavior results can dissociate: loss of particular RNA-transport functions prevents inherited behavior while F1 reporter elevation remains. A neuronal reporter is therefore not a sufficient interchangeable measure of acquired avoidance. Their multi-copy reporter instability and neuron-pair dependence are further relevant measurement limitations.

[Kaletsky et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.105673) show that trapping first choices with azide differs from allowing worms to continue moving during the assay. Their account emphasizes growth conditions that induce the bacterial small-RNA signal. [Akinosho et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.107034) later report independent F1/F2 persistence using a specified trapping assay. This earns credit as a published independent positive report, but it does not validate Pv1, F4 persistence, or ecological consequences; neither report alone resolves all laboratory differences.

We preserved all three articles and the later replication workbook, then recomputed **all 145 Table B assay indices** from raw counts. Every supplied rounded index agrees to its displayed precision. Unfiltered means are −0.350311 (naïve, 40 plates), 0.550170 (trained, 40), 0.372260 (F1, 20) and 0.253770 (F2, 45). F2-minus-naïve contrasts are positive in each of four trial labels, ranging 0.505984–0.683418. These calculations preserve the table's positive descriptive pattern, but uncover two source issues:

1. The article states 143 observations before five exclusions and 138 afterward; the raw table contains 145. Its published figure uses 38/38/19/43. No exclusion mask identifies the intended analysis subset.
2. Table B rows 32–41 (naïve trial 4) exactly repeat the ten-assay count sequence of rows 64–73 (trained trial 3), with the bacterial columns exchanged. The source does not explain this relationship.

The [audit](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/biological-scale-correspondence/renewal/replication-results.json) flags both issues without deleting observations, inventing corrections, inferring misconduct or declaring that the positive effect is disproved. Clarification is needed before these sequences count as established independent observations or the exclusion-based inference is reproduced. We did not contact the authors.

This produces a substantive revision: descriptions must distinguish **first encounter choice, continued movement, reporter expression, acquired response and inherited response**. Immobilizing an animal can record one relationship while interrupting the later feedback of interest. Its assay cannot simply stand in for an uninterrupted ecological trajectory.

## 4. Actual fluorescence-to-viable-count correspondence

Boddu et al. (2025), [gut-load dynamics](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013110), supplies **169 matched individual-worm fluorescence and CFU observations** in a [preserved archive](../../../../sources/biological-scale-correspondence/boddu-2025/README.md). This directly addresses the earlier lack of a viable-count calibration example. It is a different bacterium, instrument and preparation from the avoidance studies, so coefficients are not transferred to them.

The exploratory split was fixed before computing fits: 70 positive-count observations at T48 for training; 96 at T72 for testing. Three T48 zero counts remain explicitly excluded from the conditional log10-count regression. The T72 data include 72 observations in the three training inhibition conditions and 24 in an added CM25 condition. The original notebook fitted T72; its frozen parameters would not be held-out evidence here.

We refit the authors' inverse-tanh form, `b + c × atanh(log10(GFP)/a)`, using training loss alone. Five deterministic starts converge to essentially the same training sum of squares. Parameters are approximately **(a,b,c) = (3.822655, 1.214542, 2.247934)**. Numerical bounds on the domain and positive scale are explicit analyst choices. All 96 test predictions are defined; no clipping or silent test exclusion occurs.

| Frozen T48 fit | T72 RMSE, log10 CFU | T72 mean absolute error | T72 bias, prediction − observation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Constant training mean | 1.450968 | 1.282866 | −0.736517 |
| Linear in log10 fluorescence | 0.784138 | 0.665657 | −0.322757 |
| Inverse-tanh | **0.662542** | **0.524470** | **−0.197029** |

For inverse-tanh, RMSE is 0.645723 in shared conditions and 0.710618 in the added condition. This supports a useful conditional observation map and retains systematic underprediction. It is not a bounded-error certificate, detection-limit model for zeros, or validation under another reporter/pathogen. CFU counts viable colony-forming units under the assay; it is not an exact count of all microbial cells. The distinction agrees with the [earlier failed hard-bound calibration route](BOUND-CALIBRATION-INTEGRATION.md): finite residuals do not become universal bounds merely because this fit is useful.

## 5. Later colonization and finite ecological return

The same archive supplies two sorted-cohort experiments, **1,625 and 1,969 rows**, with high/low initial fluorescence and migration-permitted/restricted treatments. Both show higher later median fluorescence in the low-initial-load migration-permitted group: at T48, medians are 1,106 versus 82.4 in run 1, and 645 versus 112 in run 2. Inhibition, feeding and migration conditions are actual experimental interventions; they are not a fitted cognitive command.

Sample counts decline substantially, and there are no longitudinal individual identities. Furthermore, run 2 repeats the entire T0 high sequence (346 rows) and low sequence (348 rows) under both migration labels. We retain all source rows and identify the **694 repeated baseline entries**. They cannot count as independent observations. Cohort changes do not independently identify births, retention, expulsion, selection or a unique switching mechanism; the paper's conventional dynamical alternatives receive credit.

For the ecological return itself, [Thutupalli et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1608961114) provides a more direct experiment. Worm movement redistributes viable bacteria, predominantly through surface adhesion/entrainment and also excretion; colonies grow and worms revisit them. `srf-3` animals have comparable exploration and brood size but reduced dispersal. Wild-type-associated bacterial growth and the later **144-hour** worm population are higher; artificial dispersal benefits both strains. Thus finite movement → bacterial growth → subsequent population feedback has experimental support, including consequences for other worms. This is primary-paper assessment, not a reproduction of unavailable raw population data. Supplied agar nutrients, decline at larger exploration distances and roughly fourfold model/data magnitude differences remain. Incidental dispersal does not demonstrate learned farming or indefinite renewal.

## 6. What is completed and what full validation still requires

| Obligation | Current evidence and status |
|---|---|
| Mechanistic retained-response candidate beyond a supplied memory bit | Sequence/pathway interventions and measured choice support one candidate; replication/assay dependence and incomplete kinetics remain |
| Measured fluorescence → viable-count map with a held-out test | Completed for the T48→T72 conditional calibration above, with error and bias retained |
| Activity → microbial growth → later population consequence | Finite empirical example established in the cited dispersal preparation; raw-model reanalysis not performed |
| Calibrated cell/tissue → acquired response → motion/contact → subsequent microbial state in one transportable preparation | **Not completed**; no retained dataset joins these observations or establishes transport between the preparations |
| Repeated renewal, nutrient accounting and consequences for multiple participants | **Not completed**; finite growth on supplied nutrients does not establish these stronger continuations |
| Full general cell–organ–body–ecosystem account | **Not certified**, by these experiments or by Lean |

The [joint measurement specification](BIOLOGICAL-JOINT-VALIDATION.md) makes the remaining empirical work concrete: shared plate/cohort lineage, non-destructive behavior paired with sacrificial measurement cohorts, calibration in that preparation, explicit assay disturbance, viable redistribution/regrowth and later population measures, plus whole-day/intervention holdouts. It is an unexecuted experiment specification; no samples, instruments or laboratory work are available in this workspace. A search of related foraging/replication studies did not yield this joined dataset, which is not a claim that none exists anywhere.

Full validation remains Ren's chosen target. It cannot be reached here by concatenating unrelated datasets, imposing another favorable toy mechanism, or labeling a protocol as an experiment. The next empirical event that could change this status is acquisition of the specified joint records, including resolution of their observation maps and relevant replication conditions.
