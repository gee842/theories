## 1. The precise advance and the correction already required

BC1 concerns equivalent basic rescue under its stated permissions, burdens and prior-claim conditions. It explicitly pays a cost concerning fair chances. A concurrent study has now added BC2 for clearly graver one-to-one loss under declared conditions, distinguishing baseline, preventable harm and effective timing. After its own source checking and reception of this counterreview, that study revised its initial decisive priority to a pro tanto reason. I reread current §§9–14 to establish its present scope; this does not claim independent full acceptance of the separate study. J2 does not settle crossed basic claims by comparing how many labels each plan protects. The current question is the remaining joint connection: **when severity and numbers both change, can loss descriptions support a restricted comparison without first adopting a universal exchange rate?**

An initial strong draft in this thread would have made any strict matching improvement decisive. This review objected. A difference in loss can supply a reason without settling whether someone should receive no chance. Pairwise matching also changes which position suffers; it is not automatically the same person's improvement. The root adopted the correction: **LC, the present joint loss-comparison candidate, supplies a scoped, pro tanto reason concerning the losses. It does not, merely through strict profile improvement, mandate a deterministic choice or defeat every fairness objection.** Our draft's revision and the separate author's later BC2 revision have their own histories. LC does not replace or rename BC2. BC1 retains its stronger equivalent-rescue commitment; present BC2 supplies the narrower one-to-one reason. Neither gives an automatic many-to-many or minute-difference allocation rule.

This is a substantive restriction, not a verbal distinction. A strict cost inequality, if proved, cannot be exported as a total judgment, permission, unique choice or justification for suppressing a competing claim. A practical conclusion must explain how this reason interacts with other reasons and the inherited decision procedure.

## 2. Strongest counterarguments

**Matching may erase who has the claim.** Suppose a correspondence matches a loss at one position to a lesser loss at another. That establishes no compensation for the first position and discharges no undertaking to it. Prior claims, imposed injury, legitimate authority and continuing responsibilities must remain in the shared history before any loss-only comparison is used. An anonymous profile can be informative after those relations have been assessed; it cannot perform their assessment by omitting them.

**The loss order may contain the answer.** Calling one outcome more severe is already an ethically interpreted judgment, not raw observation. A declared comparison can be warranted by depth, duration, possibilities foreclosed and support relations. Those dimensions can also conflict. The candidate should defend particular comparisons and retain uncertainty elsewhere. Theorems about a supplied order do not show that a reversible loss is always less serious, or that the order covers every form of living.

**Recovery may conceal an earlier deprivation.** A return to function does not erase what occurred before it, the burdens of recovery, an irretrievable occasion or an imposed interruption. Conversely, permanence alone does not establish overriding priority over every severe reversible injury. Both continuation and realized loss belong to the history; neither final-state equality nor an irreversibility label is a sufficient assessment.

**A tier boundary can manufacture a large ethical difference.** Two positive numeric weights can describe a useful diagnostic family. Their positivity does not explain where ethically meaningful classes begin or what significance a tiny difference has. Classification uncertainty must stay distinct from substantive parity and from absence of any possible comparison. A classification chosen after seeing its favored result would not justify that result.

**Extra descriptions may masquerade as extra living.** Distinct record identifiers do not prove distinct grounds. Conversely, shared infrastructure or common origin does not make two actually differentiated losses one. A de-duplication theorem can preserve an already supplied equivalence relation; it cannot discover the correct equivalence or decide whether an actual division has created additional living relations. The root's unit analysis must supply that interpretive connection without treating isolated value owners as primitives.

**A matching procedure may require a fictitious decomposition.** Interdependent capacities can fail jointly. Splitting them into individually countable claims may count one loss twice, while merging their descriptions may hide another loss. A certificate should concern a warranted inventory of losses and its domain, not every imaginable partition. Componentwise improvement remains only as informative as the relations preserved by the components.

**The profile may miss operative dependence or opportunity.** Equal counts at each severity need not determine what a shared organization can still do. Nor do identical anonymous outcomes determine what is at stake for each position between the available alternatives. The planned complementary-role counterexample properly challenges profile sufficiency; it must not be weakened into a freely assigned ethical predicate. Its difference should follow from the same explicit support relation and different losses within it.

**A partial answer can be mistaken for an impossibility result.** If two admissible diagnostic assignments reverse a comparison, that shows this family does not select a unique answer from its stated premises. It does not show that no further ethical reason could settle the case. A subsequent justified comparison principle could do so. Conversely, presenting a familiar weighted sum as that principle does not itself justify its weights.

The [BC1 source record](BASIC-CONFLICT-INTEGRATION.md#3-primary-source-assessment-and-the-revision-it-required) already credits Otsuka and the fair-chance challenge; that work is not a new discovery here.

He challenges anonymous Pareto priority, including a tiny difference between two substantial rescue benefits. This motivates the present distinction between a strict loss-comparison reason and a decisive allocation verdict. It does not establish that every matching comparison is invalid. Link-only, page-labelled text reading; no new visual or exhaustive article audit is claimed.

**Further substantive rival:** Alex Voorhoeve, “How Should We Aggregate Competing Claims?” (2014), *Ethics* 125(1):64–87, DOI `10.1086/677022`. Read the [author-uploaded manuscript text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280809074_How_Should_We_Aggregate_Competing_Claims), §§I–III, V and the opening examples of VIII. The uploaded document is a manuscript; its page numbering is not the published pagination. The LSE PDF request timed out. The concurrent [severity study](BASIC-LOSS-SEVERITY-INTEGRATION.md#3-primary-source-assessment--原典评估) already credits ARC's relevance and aggregation proposal, so that comparison is reused. The additional targeted §VIII reading challenges treating an anonymous outcome distribution as a complete description of what is at stake for each position. We neither import its welfare scale nor claim it establishes the present G0/J comparison. Its relevance rationale is a normative proposal, not an empirically measured threshold.

These are primary theoretical comparisons, not clinical prescriptions or empirical validation. Differentiated stakes can be reconstructed within one shared history. The fact that the sources use individual-oriented language is not, by itself, an objection to their substantive arguments. Our assessment concerns which relationships their reasons preserve and what additional commitments would be needed here.

## 5. Current disposition

**Accepted at the declared scope.** I read the final [affirmative argument](UNEQUAL-BASIC-LOSSES-ARGUMENT.md), [integration](UNEQUAL-BASIC-LOSSES-INTEGRATION.md), proof explanation and checked artifacts. The argument publicly distinguishes the cross-position and combination commitments from G0, rather than making successful matching its own ethical justification. Its shared-care example jointly changes number and gravity: A leaves two lower-tier basic losses, `(2, 0)`; B leaves one lower and two higher, `(1, 2)`. The surviving claims of the positions left unprotected remain explicit. This is a substantive positive application of a loss-reduction reason, not an unargued allocation verdict.

The integration's account of actual units meets this round's requirement: records, shared causes and differentiated living relations are distinguished through their history and dependence. It does not require causal independence, count every description as another loss, or merge every shared injury into one larger subject. It also does not claim to have derived universal individuation or a cross-domain severity scale. The direct-interruption counterexample and deduplication theorem retain their narrower interpretive roles.

The final text preserves the strongest unresolved objection: jointly reducing the described losses does not by itself determine fair chances or justify a definite choice in every crossed case. The new primary-source review actually narrowed the initial claim, and the final formal audit found no outstanding blocker. The result advances a limited comparison while leaving the substantive allocation dispute open. The next missing connection is a warranted principle for the remaining crossed comparisons and its response to changed feasible options; it is not a reason to reopen G0's axiom status or declare the whole programme blocked.
