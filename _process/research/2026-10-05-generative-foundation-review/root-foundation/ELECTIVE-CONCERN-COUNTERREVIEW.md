# Elective concern: real powers do not yet settle the scope of patient reasons

6 October 2026. AI-authored counterreview for **patient significance → prospective moral favor for an unrelated eligible actor**, continuing the [constitutive candidate](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md) and [previous exclusion challenge](CONSTITUTIVE-SIGNIFICANCE-COUNTERREVIEW.md). This round grants genuine normative powers and tests their proposed exclusivity. It supplies conceptual adjudication, not new empirical evidence or a deduction from the causal root.

**Result:** a defensible power to create a reason can explain a real difference made by elective concern. It does not by itself explain why every prospective moral consideration supplied by an existing patient's relief must await that concern. The strongest exclusion account can restrict only assistance reasons while retaining other given reasons; it need not make all normativity elective. Its unresolved premise is therefore specific: **the actor's authority to author optional purposes is also exclusive authority over whether this antecedent patient benefit supplies any positive moral favor.** The direct candidate remains defensible, but the existence of given reasons elsewhere does not establish that patient relief belongs among them.

## 1. Fix the target before assessing the power

The clean case retains a real patient undergoing severe, still-unwanted adverse experience, a permissible act that would relieve it while preserving the patient, reliable knowledge and an actor able to perform it. The patient-good identification is the bounded candidate already stated, not an identity between every pain signal and badness. No endorsement by the helper has yet occurred.

The disputed target is some prospective moral consideration favoring this actor's available act for the patient's sake. It is distinct from permission, a duty, rational requirement, motivation, praise and another person's authority to demand. A reason can precede its recognition or the adoption of its end; granting that possibility does not establish this particular reason. Equally, a theory allowing special practical reasons need not classify every created reason as patient-directed moral favor.

The concurrent [rescue review](RESCUE-REQUIREMENT-REVIEW.md) begins further downstream. Its strongest non-conscription rival already accepts moral favor and denies that it defeats permission to retain a minor discretionary activity. Its defended rescue judgment adds a comparative requiring principle. Neither that rival nor that new principle settles the present dispute about whether any patient favor exists before concern. Treating the rescue argument as another proof of our root transition would recycle its starting premise.

## 2. A grounded exclusion account

The strongest reconstructed opponent is an **authored-purpose account**. This is our comparison model, not a doctrine attributed to Chang or a reported consensus.

It grants that agents live amid objectively better and worse patient conditions. It also grants some given reasons: self-regarding goods, constraints against injury, responsibilities for threats one caused and legitimate antecedent roles. It denies neither ordinary moral correction nor actual patient badness. Within the remaining field of optional positive projects, it proposes that an agent has a genuine normative power to make a valuable possibility its own purpose. The exercise partly grounds a special reason; it need not merely trigger an antecedent conditional rule.

This is more than adding an unexplained Boolean H. The account appeals to agents as authors of sustained activities, relationships and practical identities. Starting a shared practice can create a good that previously did not exist. Undertaking a project can create a stake in its continuation. A legitimate commitment can make a consideration count specially for its maker, even where no antecedent reason selected that particular commitment. Patient benefit can remain the true success condition of a charitable project rather than a means to the helper's pleasure. The new reason can require correction when chosen means do not help.

Its assistance-specific exclusion thesis adds that an unrelated patient's unmet need is only eligible content for such a purpose. Before adoption it can warrant fitting compassion, make a possible life admirable, and render aid permissible. It allegedly contributes no positive moral favor to this particular actor's choice. After a valid exercise, the same patient's actual relief helps justify the actor's act. The proposed rationale is jurisdiction over positive purposes: constraints limit how a person pursues a life, but the person constitutes which optional beneficial purposes become theirs.

That rationale is intelligible and more substantial than arbitrary desire. It still contains the central disputed move. Jurisdiction over what one undertakes can ground liberty, control over demanding roles, or new special reasons without granting jurisdiction over whether another person's benefit counts at all. Positive contributory favor neither executes an act nor commits the actor to a project. An exclusion theorist may nevertheless defend that stronger jurisdiction. The present reply identifies what requires defense; it does not infer its falsity merely because it is an additional premise.

No blanket anti-bootstrapping objection is warranted. Choice can create reasons without an antecedent reason to choose that very option. Nor must every acceptable power be reduced to activating an independently complete norm: that would exclude the constitutive alternative by stipulation. What is required is an account of the power's subject, exercise, normative domain, persistence and scope, including why that power excludes rather than supplements the proposed patient ground.

## 3. Distinguish the ground created from the act it concerns

The [earlier private-adoption comparison](CONSTITUTIVE-SIGNIFICANCE-COUNTERREVIEW.md#private-adoption-and-renunciation-provide-a-sharper-contrast) tracked onset and withdrawal. The new discrimination identifies what becomes true through adoption.

| Case | What adoption may constitute | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing a private project | A legitimate agent-specific commitment or self-direction interest, if the proposed power exists | That no consideration concerning a beneficiary could favor the choice beforehand |
| Creating a shared practice or relationship | New relational goods, expectations and possibly responsibilities | That reasons based on an already existing adverse episode have exactly the same creation condition |
| Giving a valid promise or accepting entrusted care | A new claim, responsibility or authority relation | That the promise created every reason for the promised helpful act |
| Privately adopting relief of an existing stranger's burden | A special commitment concerning independently existing patient stakes | That its special ground exhausts all moral grounds concerning the relief |

The first two cases are important counterexamples to an overbroad direct argument. A reason to sustain *this existing relationship* need not predate the relationship. Before a particular shared practice exists, the same relational good is not simply waiting unchanged to be discovered. If the direct account identifies every created reason with antecedent patient value, it loses a real explanatory distinction.

But an existing person's adverse episode is not created by the helper's undertaking. Its relief may have both a candidate patient ground and a newly constituted project ground. The absence of the latter before choice does not establish the absence of the former. Conversely, the persistence of the patient episode does not establish that every relation involving the actor is unchanged: a genuine new ground may arise. Thus neither “the patient is unchanged, so no reason can change” nor “a reason was created, so there was no reason before” is sound.

This does not require a numerical sum of separable reason quantities. Different grounds may support the same consideration, alter its importance or affect conditions under which a normative power operates. In particular, adding an alleged given reason can change a comparison of options. Logical coexistence of given and created reasons does not prove that every precise exercise condition remains unchanged in a new mixed case.

Withdrawal likewise needs a ground-specific account. A private attitude change may not legitimately terminate a commitment. A terminated commitment may leave reliance, losses or other consequences. A relationship can end while the harms of its ending generate new reasons. None of these outcomes is captured by setting every reason to zero when a current preference changes. The clean private arm must independently specify a legitimate onset and ending, and then ask which ground each changes.

## 4. A stronger comparison than observing helpful choices

A successful charitable act ordinarily follows some intention to help. If the elective condition is identified merely by that intention, observing success and patient-sensitive correction will not distinguish the rivals: the allegedly constitutive condition is selected along with the observed act. This is a limitation of the proposed identification, not evidence that the elective account is true. The comparison must independently characterize the normative power and inspect prospective justification before its exercise.

Use three questions about the same clean patient and available act:

1. Before adoption, can the patient's actual relief positively justify selecting the act for that patient's sake?
2. If a commitment is then validly formed, what additional or changed ground explains its special force?
3. When that commitment legitimately ends, does the explanation remove only that ground or also every prospective patient-based consideration, and why?

An affirmative answer to the first already accepts the disputed minimal moral favor, even if charity remains optional. “The option is permissible,” “a compassionate attitude fits,” and “an already committed helper would have a reason” are different answers. There is no contradiction in an opponent explicitly restricting its concessions to those answers. The point of the comparison is to prevent its account from obtaining the appeal of prospective patient justification while officially excluding that very relation.

The [matched-cost comparison](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-ARGUMENT.md) remains useful but does not force a verdict. Hold independently identified agency, exploration and project goods fixed; compare an available intervention that would truly relieve this patient with an otherwise comparable empty intervention. The direct judgment is that real relief supplies a positive moral justification before selection. The exclusion account can instead allow the actor to choose a project whose later success depends on relief, while denying that the patient prospect favors its initial selection. Its explanation must maintain that temporal restriction openly. Calling the option “worthwhile” cannot silently import the prospective favor it denies.

This sharpens the issue beyond the previous temporal table: **which ground and normative relation does the prospective comparison actually warrant?** Post-adoption correction alone cannot decide. Neither can a tally of helpful behavior or the helper's reported motivational ease. A substantive defense of pre-adoption justification, or a substantive account of assistance-specific exclusion, would bear on the unresolved bridge.

## 5. What the primary comparison changes

The [source investigator's direct Chang readings](ELECTIVE-CONCERN-SOURCES.md) make the opponent stronger. Her proposed powers can be genuine grounds rather than mere triggers. Her view retains given as well as will-based reasons. The 2020 exercise condition concerns incommensurability and must not be narrowed to ties or equal strength. The 2013 account can even allow a harmful will-based contributory reason while preventing it from reversing the overall direction of given reasons. Describing her account as “only good chosen projects create reasons” would erase a consequential feature.

That uncomfortable concession also tests our own inference. A created practical reason is not automatically a moral consideration favoring a patient's welfare, nor does its existence certify the chosen act's permissibility. Moral undertakings and internal commitments must remain distinct. The source establishes neither that every reason is elective nor that this patient's benefit is among the given reasons. A hybrid structure permits both an inclusive and an assistance-restricted reconstruction. The source therefore blocks an inference from genuine creation to universal exclusivity; it does not by itself refute the specific H gate.

These are uses of the source note, which this reviewer read in full. Its primary inspection belongs to that investigator; no independent primary reading, complete-work review or empirical confirmation is claimed here.

## 6. Adjudication and the remaining premise

The direct candidate retains affirmative warrant in the clean case. The actual relief is a good explanation of what makes the prospective act morally worth considering, before one has chosen to care. Legitimate self-direction explains why an actor may acquire special commitments and why its life cannot be treated as an unlimited service resource. It does not yet explain why that patient-ground contributes exactly nothing until selected. The case for adding a commitment reason is stronger than the current case for excluding every baseline reason.

This is a defended comparative judgment, not a proof that the authored-purpose account is impossible. A stronger exclusion account would justify a normative jurisdiction over positive *moral address*, distinguish it from permission over conduct, and explain why that jurisdiction covers the antecedent patient's relief while retaining its own given constraints. A stronger direct account would explain why the patient-ground belongs among given reasons under this eligible action relation; it cannot establish this merely by finding given reasons elsewhere or by calling the alleged relation constitutive.

The round therefore yields an actual narrowing. Genuine reason creation is accepted for comparison; the general bootstrapping objection is set aside; the remaining dispute concerns the ground and domain of one proposed exclusion, with a prospective identification contract that cannot be met by post-choice success alone. The acquisition of worthwhile projects and relational goods is a real part of generative organization and concern. It need not exhaust the significance of existing patients. Which part of that account follows necessarily from the minimal root remains unresolved. No advance here supplies a consciousness criterion, aggregation rule, complete wisdom account or unrestricted rescue duty.

## 7. Review scope

This counterreview read the current constitutive formulation, the prior constitutive counterreview, the new source note and the concurrent rescue review. The complete [positive argument](ELECTIVE-CONCERN-ARGUMENT.md) was then reviewed. No blocking inference was found at its declared scope: it distinguishes genuine creation from triggering, retains harmful contributory practical reasons without treating them as moral favor, restricts the challenged gate to assistance, and acknowledges that adding a patient ground can change a power's exercise conditions. Its diagnosis of the inference to exclusivity is not a proof of C2.

This review preserves the earlier temporal test while adding ground classification, the distinction between creating a good and addressing an antecedent good, and the limitation of selecting evidence only after helpful intention. These are conceptual refinements by collaborating AI reviewers, not independent observations confirming the candidate.

The argument contributor read this complete counterreview and reported no blocker. Its subsequent paragraph adopting the prospective identification limitation was rechecked; it does not present the limitation as settling the moral verdict.
