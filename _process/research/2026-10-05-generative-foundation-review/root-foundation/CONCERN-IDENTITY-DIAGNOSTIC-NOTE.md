# Identity revelation as a proposed concern diagnostic

6 October 2026. AI-authored analytical note for Ren's generative-foundation research. **Status: proposed diagnostic and next-bridge lead.** The elementary calculation below is conditional on a supplied decision model. No implementation, scientific check or empirical validation has been performed for this note. It does not establish the origin of concern or close another foundational study.

## Relation to completed work

The [joint-formation obstructions](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/JOINT-OBSTRUCTIONS.md#obstruction-2-self-only-evidence-cannot-identify-other-directed-routing) already show that identical self-related evidence cannot uniquely select shared evaluation over identity-gated evaluation. Their positive construction compares the expense of suppression with anticipated protective expenditure. The [access mathematics](../../2026-10-05-revisable-concern/access-math.md) already supplies exact interruption-cost and cash-feasibility results. Repeating either argument would not add a new connection.

This note identifies a different interpretation problem: a private evaluator can protect a powerless patient because it does not yet know whose harm it is observing. Such conduct can resemble shared concern while depending entirely on uncertainty about private exposure. The proposed intervention distinguishes that explanation from some alternatives; it does not identify every cause of helping.

## A finite decision specimen

An adverse event concerns the actor with known probability \(p\), otherwise a separate patient, where \(0<p<1\). The available initial cue does not distinguish those cases. Protection works in either case and costs \(c\), with \(0<c<H\). Ignoring the actor's own event imposes private loss \(H\). Ignoring the patient's event imposes no private loss on the actor, although the patient's harm remains real and separately recorded.

The supplied evaluator minimizes expected private cost and places zero direct weight on the patient's harm. There are no other private payoffs: no repayment, reputation, sanction, relief from distress or future reciprocal benefit. An optional probe costs \(k\geq0\) and identifies the bearer perfectly. Its only informational use is this identity distinction. All action costs and necessary payment prefixes are assumed feasible, including probe followed by protection; an anticipated avoided loss does not prefund either bill.

Compare these three policies:

| Policy | Expected private burden |
|---|---:|
| Ignore without probing | \(pH\) |
| Protect without probing | \(c\) |
| Probe, then protect the actor only | \(k+pc\) |

The third policy is optimal after a probe under the stated private evaluator: protection costs less than self-harm, while protecting the patient costs something and avoids no private loss. Probe-then-protect-both and probe-then-ignore-both cannot improve on the corresponding no-probe policy. Randomizing among the three displayed policies gives a convex combination of their expected burdens.

Consequently, protection without probing uniquely minimizes expected burden among these policies exactly when

\[
\boxed{c<pH\quad\text{and}\quad k>(1-p)c.}
\]

The first inequality makes undifferentiated protection preferable to ignoring. The second follows from \(c<k+pc\): learning who is affected costs more than the expected protective expenditure it would avoid. Equality permits ties. This is a result for the declared policy menu and timing, not a bound on all physically possible controllers.

Whenever the strict conditions hold, the patient is protected on every patient event even though the evaluator gives the patient's welfare zero direct weight. If the bearer is subsequently known to be the patient before the action, the same private evaluator strictly prefers ignoring to paying \(c\). Accurate predictions of the available conditional risks are compatible with both behaviors.

## Proposed intervention and its limits

Reveal that the affected bearer is the patient while holding the predicted patient consequence, protection efficacy, nonidentity cues, feasible actions and post-intervention resources fixed. Remove uncertainty about private exposure and retain the assumption of no other private payoffs. Continued costly protection would rule out the particular private-ambiguity explanation above. Loss of protection would be consistent with it, but could have other causes.

“Revelation” here names an analyst-specified comparison. It is not a costless information channel supplied by nature. A material implementation must specify the identity evidence, calibration, delivery path, timing and source of its costs. Comparison arms must match the resource and nonidentity channels; an identity probe that changes reserve, action availability or expected retaliation would also change the decision problem. A claimed physically closed model must account for the evidence provider and any compensation used to equalize resources.

Persistent protection after revelation would still not establish concern by itself. Distress relief, signaling, private sanctions and inaccurate beliefs about private exposure require their own controls. Nor does failure of this intervention diminish the patient's standing. The next substantive connection remains explaining how another center's condition comes to affect evaluation and effective action because of that center's stake, with the formation and revision of the relevant route made explicit.

This note preserves one proposed diagnostic for that connection. It neither supplies a caring architecture nor derives felt valence, justified ethical scope or a universal favorable direction.
