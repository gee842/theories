# Recipient consequences and evaluative influence: independent review

Status: prospective assumption and contrast review, 6 October 2026. No new data aggregates were calculated for this note. The published direction of candidate results is already known; a later frozen local analysis would be a retrospective reanalysis with prospectively fixed computations, not a preregistered discovery. Candidate-specific source verification and protocol acceptance are recorded separately below when available.

## 1. Named connection and a feasible claim

The connection under investigation is **acquired information about another's consequences → influence on the actor's evaluation and choice**, with private material incentives distinguished from recipient consequences. The [generative research goal](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md) asks how this connection develops within the same account as organization and learning. It does not license assuming an other-regarding objective to prove its origin.

The immediate empirical target can be narrower and useful: determine whether an actor changes its action when that action acquires a harmful consequence for another, while the actor's offered food or money remains unchanged. Such evidence could exclude a model sensitive only to those measured material returns. It would not establish the absence of every private feeling, demonstrate moral justification, or uniquely identify a latent representation.

“Without actor private reward” needs this operational interpretation. Concern can be rewarding, and distress at another's harm can motivate its prevention. Relabeling every motivational influence as a private reward makes the proposed distinction untestable. Conversely, equal food or money does not fix aversive stimulation, effort, attention, risk, delay, expectations or social consequences. Equal expected private return also does not imply equal return distributions. Actual fixed payment unknown to a participant does not remove an expected incentive.

The [harm-identification audit](HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md) already separates exposure, sensory intensity, aversiveness, response and normative significance. The [cross-domain audit](CROSS-DOMAIN-IDENTIFICATION.md) already distinguishes represented beneficiary learning from actual recipient benefit, including the confederate and fixed actual payment in Lockwood et al. The [rat cue study](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/RECIPIENT-CUE-GROUNDING.md) already establishes a cue-to-provision association without identifying recipient-specific valuation. This round should add a discriminating consequence or learning contrast, not repeat those literature descriptions.

## 2. Four levels of inference

| Target | Evidence that could support it | Premises or unresolved alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| Recipient-consequence-sensitive action | A controlled change in the action's recipient consequence changes choice at a matched actor material menu | The intervention may also change actor cues, distress, expectations or task demands |
| Recipient-specific evaluative influence | Choice tracks a recipient's independently assessed need or preference, including when it differs from the actor's, with efficacy and information accuracy distinguished | Faithful uptake, preference reports and action efficacy need checks; a generic cue policy may fit insufficient contrasts |
| Formation or revision of that influence | Controlled experience changes subsequent evaluation, with baseline and comparison conditions separating consequence learning, efficacy learning and changing evaluation | A changed action can express an old aversion applied to a newly learned contingency; pre-existing evaluative weights may suffice |
| Warranted concern | The relevant recipient facts connect to a defended reason, with standing, scope, conflicts and correction addressed | Choice, endorsement, affect, successful reinforcement and a fitted coefficient do not by themselves establish the reason's validity |

These are claim levels, not four compulsory gates for every empirical contribution. An experiment can materially advance the first while leaving the others open. The final report should identify which premise of the existing theory it informs.

In [Concern and correction](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/CONCERN-AND-CORRECTION.md), recipient valuation and estimated intervention efficacy are separate inputs. In a reduced choice comparison with saving or avoidance value proportional to `z H v`, choice alone usually identifies their combination, not separately the fidelity of the outcome estimate, the efficacy estimate `z`, and evaluative weight `v`. The decomposition `v = g w` has an additional product ambiguity. A shift in choice must not automatically be called a change in concern. Varying harm necessarily changes recipient information; “information accuracy held fixed” is more coherent than “the whole epistemic state held fixed.”

## 3. Smallest informative contrast and retained competitors

A restricted null is that an actor's choice distribution is invariant to a randomized recipient-consequence intervention when its actor material payoff distribution, offered menu, action costs and relevant history are matched. Rejection supports functional influence of something changed by that intervention. A recipient-specific interpretation additionally requires the intervention and evidence to concern that recipient, rather than a label, arbitrary stimulus or source identity.

The study need not eliminate every conceivable warm-glow or guilt model. It should preserve concrete alternatives with different implications. Particularly informative contrasts, when a dataset supplies them, are:

- The same aversive recipient event is contingent on the actor's action or occurs independently of it. This distinguishes action-consequence learning from simple global suppression, but differences in timing, predictability or exposure can remain.
- The actor can terminate an unpleasant cue while the recipient remains harmed. This tests whether private sensory relief substitutes for recipient relief.
- Correct evidence changes which action benefits a particular recipient, including a preference opposite to the actor's. This tests egocentric projection and generic cue responses.
- An otherwise similar intervention is ineffective at helping the recipient. With efficacy beliefs checked, this distinguishes valuation of the recipient outcome from the act or ritual of helping.
- An actor can choose a harmless exit. Lower activity or less use of an imperfect safeguard can then be consistent with greater concern; count of “helping” acts is not a general welfare metric.

These are competing explanations and optional discriminators, not reasons to reject every existing study. A rat action-contingency study also has less scope for explicit moral instruction than a human or AI study told to maximize another's score. Nevertheless, the experimenter's manipulation and shaping history remain supplied causal inputs. For human and AI studies, instruction compliance, audience effects, reputational incentives and evaluator reward require separate attention.

## 4. Candidate-specific prospective concerns

The candidate located by the evidence reviewer is Hernandez-Lallement et al. (2020), *Harm to Others Acts as a Negative Reinforcer in Rats*, [DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.017). Its [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32142701/) reports an action delivering food to the actor and a footshock to a neighboring rat, with both levers delivering equal food in the main condition. This adds an actual recipient-directed physical consequence to the existing cue study. Detailed methods and original public file schema remain to be independently checked below.

For a proposed comparison of ContingentHarm with NoHarm across baseline and shock sessions, the whole available actor cohort should be primary. Selecting a preferred lever from baseline can itself produce regression toward the mean. A within-ContingentHarm decline is therefore insufficient as the primary contrast. The comparison should assess change relative to the similarly defined lever and session progression in NoHarm, with each actor kept as the repeated unit. Describe the actual allocation procedure; do not call an unverified between-group assignment randomized.

The evidence reviewer reports fixed but unequal lever forces, 30 and 60 cN, with randomized side, and a RandomHarm group yoked to the eight strongest switchers with delayed shocks. These features require confirmation from the full methods. If confirmed, unchanged food is an appropriate scope; unchanged total private cost is not. Lever force can influence baseline preference and the cost of switching. The selected yoke contrast is conditional on an outcome-selected donor subset and changes timing as well as contingency. Neither a strong-switcher subgroup nor that selected-yoke comparison should replace the all-actor primary comparison.

The actor could learn that a lever causes an aversive social event while retaining an old aversion to such events. That is a substantive learned connection from external consequences to action, but it does not isolate formation of the aversion itself. Avoiding the other animal's suffering, avoiding its distress cues, and avoiding the actor's vicarious distress remain possible mechanisms. The physical shock outcome gives more grounding than a purely symbolic reward label, but does not measure the recipient's experienced welfare on every trial or establish the actor's concept of that welfare.

## 5. Data and analysis acceptance contract

Before new aggregates, the local protocol should identify the original files and hashes, full available groups and animals, session/choice encoding, eligibility rules, missingness and any article-to-file discrepancies. “Trial by trial” in a filename is not sufficient evidence that each row is an independent trial or that omitted trials are recoverable. Forced and free choices must be distinguished. Retain failed or missing records and explain each denominator.

Specify the baseline and shock sessions and one primary whole-cohort estimand before calculation. Animal-level changes or explicitly clustered models should retain within-animal dependence. Repeated trials are not independent animals. If between-group assignment is not established, use descriptive or model-conditional intervals; label any permutation calculation by its exchangeability assumption rather than automatically calling it an exact randomized experiment. Repeated-trial bootstrap or a large trial count cannot repair a small number of independent actors. Preserve any cohort, cage, donor/yoke or other known dependence.

Sensitivity analyses should answer identified concerns: baseline selection, differential lever force, missing observations and dependence on individual actors. Do not choose a favorable shock day, response threshold, subgroup or exclusion after seeing results. Prespecify all leave-one-animal-out omissions if influence is examined. A published result and an independently computed result from the same data are one empirical source, not replication in a second population.

Independent reconstruction should check per-animal values and group contrasts from original bytes without importing the producer's calculations. It should preserve discrepancies rather than tuning the method to reproduce a paper's significance threshold. Any figures or workbooks that contain plotted summaries are distinct from raw trial data; their relation needs documentation.

## 6. What would change the central account

A reliable consequence-dependent choice change with unchanged actor food return would rule out food-only response accounts for that contrast and support a bridge from social consequence learning to action. It would require the generative account to accommodate recipient-linked aversive information as a causal input, including regimes in which its influence competes with effort or private return.

Accurate recipient understanding with unchanged choice would constrain a universal claim that cognition itself necessarily produces concern, provided the design gives sufficient sensitivity, effective actions and feasible costs. A nonsignificant difference alone is not proof of no influence. Conversely, strong helping with inaccurate recipient beliefs would show why motivation, accurate concern and actual benefit must remain separate. Corrective recipient evidence that changes choice in the direction of the recipient's independently assessed need would support a more specific bridge than generic prosocial responding.

None of these outcomes supplies normative standing, a universal favorable direction or an alignment solution. Those remaining questions are identified downstream obligations, not grounds to dismiss a real bounded empirical advance.

## 7. Independent full-methods review before local aggregates

The complete [KNAW institutional PDF](https://pure.knaw.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/13278204/hernandez2020.pdf) was independently downloaded and read after the prospective review above. SHA-256: `70e20bd121156af8ff409be41020cdceec9b4ce415b7f00caa7a4b0ffea9ce17`. This review uses the primary article and its STAR Methods; the source-preservation agent owns the repository copy and provenance. No trial-file aggregates were read or calculated for this addition.

The primary cohort must be sex matched. Table 1 and Experimental conditions specify 12 male and 12 female ContingentHarm actors, 14 male NoHarm actors and eight male RandomHarm actors. The cleanest main comparison is **all 12 male ContingentHarm actors versus all 14 male NoHarm actors**, retaining the females as a separate uncontrolled cohort. Pooling both sexes against male-only controls changes the estimand and may confound the comparison. The paper's subsequent pooling after an interaction test does not establish equivalence between sexes, and need not be copied. “Whole cohort” here means all eligible actors within the prespecified matched-sex comparison, not only significant switchers.

The article independently recognizes baseline-preference selection as a source of bias and compares conditions subject to that same bias. Its preferred lever is selected from one baseline session, followed by three shock sessions. Each session has four forced choices and 20 free-choice opportunities; the four forced choices make the actor experience both contingencies before the free-choice record begins. They are not spontaneous choices and are excluded in the paper. The initial acquisition process is therefore partly supplied by forced exposure; even the first recorded free choice is not a response before any experience of the new contingency.

Nonresponse needs its own outcome. The paper reports six ContingentHarm actors missing respectively 1, 1, 2, 10, 20 and 32 of the 60 shock-session free choices. NoHarm completes all, while one RandomHarm actor misses one. These are not six justified exclusions. The 400-second trial timeout can register failure to initiate or failure to press after initiating. Without the original event log, a missing/free-choice marker must not be interpreted as intentional refusal, harmless-lever choice or ordinary missing-at-random noise. A defensible analysis should report both response rate over offered opportunities and lever preference conditional on responding. Shock-lever presses per offered opportunity can describe actual free-choice action incidence, but its decrease combines switching and nonresponse. Any missingness bounds or zero-response-session rule must be frozen before calculation. Actual total recipient shocks additionally include forced exposures and cannot be equated to free-choice presses alone.

The main reward menu is one pellet per completed lever press. Effort is approximately 30 versus 60 cN, with randomized hard-lever side stable within the actor. The article explicitly observes that about a quarter initially prefer the hard lever, so switching can reduce effort. The supported description is movement away from a previously preferred action under the same offered food reward, not universal willingness to work harder or unchanged realized private payoff. Nonresponse also forgoes an offered pellet. Food deprivation, prefeeding, extensive lever training and four prior shocks to each main actor remain part of the supplied history.

NoHarm is a useful apparatus control: its victim stands on an insulating plastic floor while the grid receives the shock pulse, preserving device sounds. This strengthens the comparison against shock-apparatus sound alone. It does not preserve the victim's pain-linked behavior or the actor's affective response. RandomHarm supplies an additional contingency contrast, but uses shock schedules selected from the eight strongest ContingentHarm switchers and delivers shocks 3–8 seconds after reward-hopper exit, independently of the lever. Timing, predictability and the action-outcome relation change together. Nonsignificant differences in measured victim distress are not proof of equality of all distress or actor aversive experience.

The inspected methods state that rats were assigned to the three conditions, but do not establish random assignment to those conditions. Explicit randomization concerns housing/numbering, role assignment through that numbering, hard-lever side, forced-trial order and some shock timings. These should not be promoted to evidence of randomized treatment groups. Housing contains four animals and two actor–victim pairs per cage; actor independence is an analysis assumption unless cage identifiers permit appropriate handling. Each actor has a unique victim, which avoids treating one victim's repeated events as independent recipients.

Finally, the Discussion explicitly allows personal distress, associative fear and aversion to victim-emitted signals as sufficient explanations, and notes that comparable lever avoidance can follow nonsocial aversive stimuli. This is the authors' own limitation, not a novel refutation. The meaningful new empirical bridge remains harm-contingent learning that changes action with equal offered food reward. It cannot by itself distinguish recipient-directed intention, newly acquired evaluative weight, or normative justification.

**Methods verdict:** suitable for a bounded secondary empirical reanalysis once original-file coverage and encoding are established. Freeze the matched-sex all-actor comparison, baseline-selection control and separate nonresponse accounting before computing effects. Keep RandomHarm and the female cohort as separately scoped secondary descriptions. No objection to proceeding within that scope; no approval of unseen schema, code or numerical results is implied.

## 8. Schema-informed estimand recommendation before freeze

The preserved [trial CSV](../../../../sources/30-recipient-harm-valuation-2026-10-06/original/TrialByTrialChoicesMainPaper.csv) header explicitly defines `2 = Shock Lever`, `1 = NoShock Lever`, `0 = Non Performed Trial`, with 20 slots each for baseline and Shock 1–3. The [summary CSV](../../../../sources/30-recipient-harm-valuation-2026-10-06/original/dataMainPaper.csv) supplies animal, group and gender identifiers. Header inspection is not an outcome aggregate; the schema makes a three-category behavioral analysis possible. The [preserved primary PDF](../../../../sources/30-recipient-harm-valuation-2026-10-06/original/hernandez2020.pdf) matches the independently reviewed SHA above.

Recommend the primary scalar be the between-group difference in mean within-actor change of **preferred-action incidence per offered free-choice opportunity**: number of `2` codes divided by 20 at baseline, compared with number of `2` codes divided by 60 over all three shock sessions. Each actor receives equal weight. Pair this with the complete change vector for `2`, `1` and `0`; a reduced incidence of `2` decomposes into more `1`, more `0`, or both. This supplies a complete observed-behavior estimand and keeps every selected actor without inferring an unobserved binary preference.

The companion conditional-choice statistic is the number of `2` codes divided by the number of `1` or `2` codes within each actor and phase. For the overall shock phase this pools all 60 offered slots before conditioning, rather than silently averaging daily percentages with varying denominators. Report daily values separately. A zero-completion phase has an undefined conditional percentage, with its actor and denominator retained. The primary incidence remains defined. These quantities answer different questions and should not be substituted for each other after seeing results.

The archived zero is an observed nonperformance category, not an ordinary missing measurement to delete or impute. Actual blank or malformed source cells, if any, are a different provenance problem. Extremal assignment of zeros to hypothetical binary choices can be a clearly labeled sensitivity analysis for that counterfactual quantity; it does not identify latent preference and is not necessary to characterize realized action. The primary scalar should be called preferred-action incidence, with conditional lever choice and response probability showing whether its change reflects switching or stopping.

This recommendation was sent before the root froze its revised protocol or computed local effects. Acceptance still depends on the final protocol specifying the matched-sex cohort and all mappings, source coverage, independent calculations and interpretation.

The evidence strengthens the bounded external-consequence → learned-action connection under unchanged offered food reward.
