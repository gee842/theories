# Prospective control over acquired capacities: substantive counterreview

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored continuation of the [ratification investigation](PERSPECTIVAL-RATIFICATION-INTEGRATION.md#7-what-is-gained-and-what-is-still-supplied), under the [nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). **Scope:** philosophical reconstruction, counterexamples and conditional adjudication. No new mathematical proof, empirical result or completed universal grounding is claimed.

**Main correction:** a restriction's technical relation to a good does not settle its authority. A separable acquired skill can be subject to justified restrictions; a dependence constitutive of a shared activity can be part of an objectionable arrangement. Nor does recovery of formation costs automatically end a commitment. The strongest comparison concerns the scope of continuing control in the actual whole history, including the new grounds that commitment itself may have created.

**Adjudication:** accurately disclosed, prospective agreement can support substantial bounded commitments, and can help make their goods possible. It does not establish, merely by occurring, the authority of every indefinite veto over later ordinary use of an acquired capacity. In the specified indefinite-control case below, the review favors rejecting that broad power while preserving earned return, actual reliance, meaningful affiliation and any narrower warranted commitment. The disputed premises and surviving rival are explicit; this is a defended ethical judgment, not an inference from connectedness alone.

## 1. Exact inference under test

The universal connection is **whole qualitative realization → differentiated practical reasons and limits**, including limits on the creation of later authority through agreement. The previous [undertaking case](PERSPECTIVAL-RATIFICATION-INTEGRATION.md#2-the-specified-history) included an already warranted delivery obligation. Its violation supplied the central ground. This case removes that breach. Accurate disclosure, competence and prospective agreement are retained, while the agreement's normative reach remains to be assessed.

The question is whether learning and commitment can create real goods and obligations without conferring unrestricted authority on the arrangement that helped form them. It is not whether every participant must receive the same response, whether every capacity must be portable, or whether a new teaching arrangement must be provided. Nor may a universal freedom to revise erase warranted finite commitments.

Begin with actual activity: shared instruction changes competence; practice develops appreciation and attachment; support maintains the work; agreements coordinate expectation and responsibility; and later decisions alter the availability of further activity. Teacher, learner, supporter and office name differentiated positions in that history. They do not begin as independent proprietors of causal contributions whose rights can be read directly from their relative amounts.

## 2. Strongest rival: formation through credible commitment

The **formation-and-commitment rival**, constructed here rather than attributed to an external author, accepts the whole history and gives a positive defense of restrictions.

**Formation may need dependable contribution.** Teaching consumes time, material means and opportunities; other participants may organize their lives around the expected contribution of those being trained. An arrangement that allows every acquired capacity to leave immediately can make the shared formation infeasible. A bound on future activity may enable this actual good rather than merely obstruct a better one available for free.

**An agreement can add a reason.** Accurate, competent agreement need not merely register independently existing reasons. It can help constitute a commitment, reciprocal reliance or a shared project. A later shift of preference does not automatically undo those relations. An agreement that allocates risk can remain relevant even when one participant's particular investment turns out to be recovered early.

**Some goods are particular shared achievements.** The value of performing with these collaborators, completing this work, or participating in this tradition cannot always be preserved by substituting a different organization. A request for portability may change what is being offered. Gratitude and durable affiliation can also be genuine responses to what the relationship has made possible; they need not be reduced to repayment.

**Optional assistance may be offered on terms.** If a provider may permissibly decline to teach, the rival argues that it may often offer a mutually improving arrangement instead. An informed learner might reasonably choose substantial self-binding, including a continuing allegiance, over never acquiring the good. The possibility of such a choice is part of the generative account of agency, not evidence that agency has been absent.

The fourth claim is strongest when the offered restriction actually makes formation possible and the learner has an adequately understood choice. It becomes a further disputed inference when permission to decline all involvement is treated as permission to impose any disclosed term. A relation undertaken can have responsibilities absent before undertaking; optional entry does not by itself specify their limits. The prior formation-duty work already makes that temporal point. This review must therefore examine the particular term, not announce that any benefit-bearing offer is legitimate or that all dependence is invalidating.

## 3. Cases that defeat simple dividing lines

| Proposed shortcut | Countercase retaining the relevant good | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| A skill can be used elsewhere, so restricting its use is unwarranted. | Musicians acquire a separable technique while preparing a shared premiere. A disclosed, limited restraint on revealing the prepared work protects actual collaborators' contribution and the specific first performance. The scope and warrant are defended independently. | Technical separability does not eliminate legitimate claims concerning its exercise. The restraint need not amount to control over every later use of the underlying skill. |
| A restriction constitutes the shared practice, so it is justified. | An association's distinctive cohesion and pleasure partly consist in requiring a subordinate group to remain deferential. Removing the requirement changes that exact activity. | Constitutive dependence identifies what would change; it does not certify the relation's ethical character. The subordinate group's actual living remains within the whole appraisal. |
| Formation costs have been recovered, so every commitment ends. | A fixed-term shared undertaking allocates uncertain burdens; early recovery of one teaching cost leaves agreed work, others' reliance and a still worthwhile completion outstanding. | Investment recovery is one changed fact, not release from every independently warranted obligation. |
| Staying is now voluntary, so earlier formation and control are irrelevant. | Earlier restriction formed a vocation, relations and dispositions that continue after its removal. | Current choice and historical constitution are both relevant; a formative effect can outlast the condition that produced it. |
| Continued dependence makes assent invalid. | A transparent finite undertaking has no equally attractive outside alternative but remains competently and permissibly undertaken. | Meaningful agency does not require costless exit or absence of dependence. |
| No broken promise means no possible objection. | Before learning begins, participants knowingly agree to a rule allowing a permanent office to stop ordinary use of the resulting capacity for its changing purposes. | Accurate agreement removes a deception or breach objection; the rule's authority and scope still need defense. |

The premiere case does not assert that every embargo, exclusivity term or noncompetition clause is permissible. It specifies a legitimate limited instance. The deference case does not infer wrongdoing merely from hierarchy; its authority and affected relations must be substantively judged. Both prevent the representation's categories from carrying the verdict by definition.

## 4. A prospective comparison with no breached undertaking

Consider a guild that teaches a useful craft. Applicants accurately understand the proposed arrangements and competently choose among the options actually available. Learning produces genuine competence, enjoyment and relationships. The provider's contribution is substantial. Portability has actual technical and social requirements; their costs, third-party effects and feasibility are part of the comparison.

Compare two candidate arrangements as complete formative histories, not identical worlds differentiated by a label.

**Bounded formation arrangement.** A stated period of contribution supports instruction, the shared project and a fair allocation of its risks. Some temporary use restrictions protect identified safety, confidentiality or collaborative claims. The arrangement can impose real losses on an early departure, subject to the defended scope of those commitments. Completing its terms does not require the guild to fund future independent activity or recreate every lost relationship.

**Continuing-veto arrangement.** The guild provides the craft on an accurately disclosed condition that an office retains indefinite discretion over the participant's later ordinary use of the acquired competence. This reaches uses that require no continuing guild support and do not appropriate others' confidential materials or purport to speak for the guild. A refusal of the office's later demands can activate the restriction. Its actual enforcement may use control over a necessary support or recognition route; the mechanism must be specified rather than imagining that a skill can literally be removed at will.

In the second history, the formative contribution has been fairly settled, no relevant collaborator's outstanding reliance is being defeated, and no current safety or confidentiality ground supports the particular veto under examination. These are constructed conditions, not assumptions to make in a real case. They remove several familiar justifications while leaving the hardest ones in view: the original agreement, durable allegiance, gratitude, the provider's latitude in offering aid, and the alleged value of binding oneself in this way.

Technical and social separation must actually work before the comparison can be used. If the ordinary use still depends on the guild's labor, claims to portable use cannot silently demand continued provision. If public recognition would falsely certify competence, it is not the promised independent use. If members' departure would unravel an outstanding worthwhile joint completion, that consequence cannot be dropped by calling the skill separable.

## 5. Adjudication and positive ground

The bounded arrangement can be justified. Its significance concerns what the restrictions make possible, whom they bind, what they protect and how their actual terms organize shared living. Accurate agreement can help constitute that relationship. A later preference for unrestricted use does not by itself defeat it.

The continuing-veto arrangement remains more difficult even after transparency is granted. The affirmative reason against its specified broad power is **the avoidable subordination of a developed capacity's ordinary worthwhile exercise to changing demands that are not justified by the relevant ongoing relation**. This is a proposed ethical characterization requiring defense, not a consequence of the word “capacity.”

The positive defense has three parts. First, the capacity's development is itself a realization in the whole, and its ordinary exercise can continue that realization without consuming new provider support or defeating the specified others' claims. Preventing that exercise therefore affects something real that the formative history has achieved; it is not merely refusing a new gift. Second, the continued veto changes the form of participation: the office can make unrelated later compliance a condition of exercising that capacity, even after the original contribution and associated claims have been answered. The account must appraise this exercise of control together with the learning, rather than letting the learning certify every subsequent use of power. Third, preserving gratitude, a chosen affiliation and specific commitments need not always require that entire discretionary power. If those goods can actually continue under narrower authority, their existence alone does not justify the larger scope.

This is not a premise that every restriction must be indispensable or that every less restrictive alternative is better. A narrower arrangement can lose a genuine good, increase justified burdens or defeat a still-binding agreement. Those consequences must be assessed. Nor is “guaranteed allegiance” automatically worthless: keeping a worthwhile promise can matter even when it is difficult. The point of dispute is whether this indefinite, open-ended power over ordinary acquired activity is an appropriate form of that allegiance, rather than a legitimate finite commitment merely given an unfavorable name.

**Review judgment:** the described ordinary-use veto lacks an adequate justification from formative contribution, accurate disclosure, gratitude or beneficial comparison with no training alone. The acquired realization and absence of the listed current protective grounds support a reason to limit that power. That reason addresses an informed office even if it prefers greater control. It favors release from the excessive scope while preserving whatever narrower warranted commitment remains; it does not automatically favor abandoning the guild or transferring all its resources to departing members.

The strongest rival grants these facts but treats competent prospective self-binding as sufficient to create precisely the continuing authority. It rejects the inference that an additional ground beyond that normative power is required. The affirmative must therefore defend a limit on the power's scope; it cannot win by saying agreement creates no reasons. This review favors such a limit for the stated configuration, but has not derived its universal necessity. Calling the agreement valid would assume the disputed result; calling it domination without the foregoing account would do the same in the other direction.

## 6. When an old purpose ends but a commitment remains

“Authority ends when its ground ends” is useful only if all the relevant grounds have actually been identified. A restriction might originally be proposed to finance training, yet the undertaking it establishes can create further coordination, reliance or a definite promise. One instrumental explanation's disappearance need not end the complete practical ground.

An unexpected surplus, for example, can settle teaching expenditure without settling the agreed risk distribution or the common work itself. Conversely, invoking “commitment” cannot determine its scope: a promise to complete a particular project is not thereby a promise to obey unrelated demands indefinitely. A release or renegotiation may be warranted even when not yet achieved; alternatively, the original finite duty can remain binding. Those judgments require the actual history.

Gratitude supplies a further counterpressure. A person may have reason to honor a formative relationship after any calculable contribution is settled. This defeats a claim that moral relations expire when accounts balance. It does not identify gratitude with a provider's power to demand any desired expression, or show that gratitude excludes further development elsewhere. Gratitude, continuing affection, a valid promise and enforceable authority are related possibilities, not interchangeable conclusions.

The proposed restriction is therefore scope-sensitive: a ground sufficient to warrant some contribution or loyalty does not automatically warrant every method of securing it. The office's ability to veto may make compliance effective; that effectiveness does not independently establish its entitlement. A complete grounding must explain the relation between the worthwhile commitment and the particular control claimed to realize it.

## 7. Exact premises, revisions and concrete improvements

The affirmative judgment uses substantive premises that remain to defend:

1. The specified developed activity and its ordinary exercise have favorable significance within the whole history, rather than merely being desired or productive.
2. Competent agreement can genuinely create commitments, but its normative power has limits that must be assessed through the relation it establishes and its effects.
3. Meaningful self-binding, gratitude and optional assistance do not alone justify the specified indefinite discretionary control after the relevant formation and protective claims have been addressed.
4. Practical authority should be proportionate to the independently defended relationship it governs; a claimed wider scope cannot be established merely by effective enforcement or by repeating assent to that very scope.
5. Actual competing goods, reliance, costs and appropriate protection can justify restrictions, including restrictions on separable activity and temporary limits on revision.

These are possible mutually supporting articulations of one whole ground. They are not presented as independently complete value atoms, and naming them does not prove their necessity. In particular, premise 3 is the main substantive disagreement with the self-binding rival. The affirmative has supplied reasons for it in §5; the rival's intelligibility neither refutes those reasons nor establishes the affirmative conclusion automatically.

Concrete improvements to the governing comparison are:

- **Replace a portability verdict with an actual correspondence account.** Identify what use can continue, which support it needs, what changes in the original relation, and what it costs. A credential, capacity, shared performance and continuing service have different requirements.
- **Retain newly created grounds.** Record the original contribution, resulting commitment, actual reliance, risk allocation and gratitude separately where their histories differ. Removing one does not delete the others.
- **Specify the claimed power.** Distinguish a finite service obligation, control of shared equipment, representation of the guild, protection of confidential work and indefinite control of ordinary independent activity. The same favorable attitude can bear differently on each.
- **Test narrower authority without assuming it is costless.** Compare what genuine goods and justified assurances survive, what new burdens appear, and which participant has reason to bear them. If nothing material or ethically relevant is preserved, a merely verbal promise of portability is no answer.
- **Let the rival win genuine countercases.** Accept a defended finite completion, collaborative restraint or bounded formation commitment. Revise the indefinite-veto judgment if the supposed independent use actually defeats those grounds or if the claimed self-binding power receives an adequate substantive defense at that scope.

The universal inference advanced is a candidate limit on how formation and agreement generate authority: **the same whole must explain both their real constructive force and the bounds of their application to later activity**. It does not install an authority-free individual, make all relationships revisable at will, or infer ethics from which arrangement reproduces best.

No primary literature was newly read for this review. Its sources are the cited repository studies and the constructed cases above. The result is a sharpened substantive contest and the stated conditional adjudication. It neither supplies an empirical diagnosis of actual guilds nor proves a universal anti-dependence or maximum-portability ethic.
