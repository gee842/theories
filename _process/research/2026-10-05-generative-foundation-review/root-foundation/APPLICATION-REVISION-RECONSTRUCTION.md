## 3. Existing controls do not supply selective restraint

The permission control is specifically a delivery control. `CanStart` requires it, but `Admitted`, used by `ownPay`, does not. `recipientPay` checks its phase and recipient funds. Setting permission false therefore does not prevent the next acquisition payment. Reading this bit as consent to further teaching would change its operational meaning.

Cutting scope prevents the next `ownPay`, but also removes command applicability. It does not open early closure or preserve a usable repair route. Restoring scope restores the old schedule; it does not shorten it. Additional reason reading cannot change count, acquisition phase or the physical recipient's condition.

These controls distinguish blocking activity from revising it. Stopping all activity can avoid further imposition while leaving the old shortfall outstanding. Successful selective correction would require an actual way to suspend or change acquisition while retaining a warranted response. The existing law supplies no such early-service path at this starting state.

## 4. What this changes in the generative account

The practical boundary is partly the organization of access itself. An activity can retain accurate evidence, formed positive influence, effective means and adequate resources while tying their use to continuation of its acquisition practice. This relation occurs within the same history that produced the outstanding stock loss. It is not adequately described as an isolated indifferent learner or a missing ethical label.

Under the separately defended deprivation, responsibility and remedy interpretation, this becomes a candidate target of correction: preserve the affected continuity and outstanding response while reconsidering the protocol that conditions access. Responsibility for that organization may belong to an operator or institution; the current learner has no demonstrated power to revise it. The obstruction establishes neither a duty to complete the schedule nor the illegitimacy of every fixed protocol. Its ethical assessment still requires actual burdens, authorization, alternatives and competing claims.

The remaining connection is consequently sharper than “make the learner care more”: explain how acquired consequences can revise which practices and responses are available, with an actual carrier, permitted authority and funded continuation. The current model's recipient still lacks its own maintained organization. Removing the protocol obstruction alone would not derive that organization, identify lived adversity or complete universal ethics.

This result would fail if an admitted event could shorten the limit, bypass the closed-phase gate or begin new service through the settlement exception. The inspected definitions and world-preservation result exclude those routes. A changed law could supply them, but would require its own explanation and validation. No such change is made here.
