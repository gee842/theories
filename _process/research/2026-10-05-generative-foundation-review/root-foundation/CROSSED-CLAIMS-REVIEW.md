## 1. What the new question does and does not reopen

BC1 retains its disclosed stronger priority in equivalent basic rescue. Current BC2 gives a one-to-one gravity reason. LC adds a joint, defeasible loss-reduction reason when warranted comparisons all support the same direction. The two-tier diagnostic does not decide every case in which fewer graver losses oppose more lesser basic losses. Those results are inputs here, not unfinished general grounding exercises.

The remaining question concerns what to do with genuinely crossed claims and what happens when a new feasible activity changes whom a shared response can protect together. The proposed new affirmative bridge is **LR: a limited local persistence of BC1's priority when the smaller group's basic loss becomes slightly more severe in otherwise preserved relations**. It is not enough to assign a severity multiplier and call the resulting verdict or lottery fair. Conversely, inability to justify a unique exchange rate does not prevent improving a particular response when a new route preserves its existing protection and adds real protection elsewhere.

The analytical positions and rescue routes must remain relations within one care or support history. A person can participate in several jointly feasible rescue groups without becoming several claimants. Different routes to the same living relation do not automatically add distinct moral tickets. This is precisely where the relationship between group selection and actual receipt of protection must be reconstructed.

## 2. The strongest challenge to local persistence

A substantive opponent can accept BC1 at exactly equivalent losses yet reject LR. She may hold that any genuine severity advantage, however slight within the same basic kind, restores enough claim to block a definite greater-number choice or require the more endangered position to retain a chance. That is a controversial boundary at equality, but it is not refuted merely by noting that the physical difference is small. BC2 and LC, as pro tanto reasons, do not exclude it either.

LR must defend its different commitment: the reason to preserve an additional whole basic living relation need not have practical force only at the single point of exact equivalence. A small comparative aggravation can be morally real without immediately extinguishing that stronger rescue reason. This is a possible affirmative rationale for LR; it neither makes the extra aggravation unreal nor proves that the opponent contradicts G0. The candidate must also retain BC1's already acknowledged cost to the excluded position's chance, which persists when that position faces a slightly graver loss.

Two formulations would make the proposal vacuous. First, “small enough” cannot mean merely “insufficient to reverse the verdict.” That repeats the desired conclusion. Second, “no relevant threshold is crossed” cannot mean “no morally decisive fact changes.” The application needs independently described relations: whether a recovery window closes, an irreplaceable participation is lost, an imposed injury or entitlement changes, or a common support collapses. These descriptions can exclude particular structural discontinuities; they do not establish that every possible ethical boundary has been enumerated.

Nor does boundedness alone establish smallness: a finite increase can be very serious. Local persistence should name its comparative direction and kept conditions, distinguish a qualitative normative commitment from an observed numerical neighborhood, and admit that a concrete application still needs grounds for regarding the change as within that range. No theorem about physical continuity or a supplied additive score would by itself establish the needed ethical continuity.

The claimed range also cannot renew itself for free. A later judgment is not automatically another BC1 equivalence point; successive added losses do not disappear when the description is reset. Enlarging the population, composing distinct rescue histories and changing the menu need their own grounds. The completed aggregation-context work already identifies composition obligations. This is an application of that limitation, not a newly proved impossibility of all local aggregation.

## 3. A new source objection to global exclusion

The opponent accepts that some requests are too weak to displace a particular much graver request. She challenges an additional inference: **a request that should yield to A need not thereby yield to B when A is not actually protected.** A rule can use the availability of A to exclude C, then choose B instead of A. The original explanation for C's yielding has not yet explained this outcome.

This is stronger than announcing a numerical cycle or saying every menu effect is irrational. An actually available rescue may change what is at stake, responsibilities, achievable opportunities or the meaning of a choice. The objection is to treating one binary exclusion as a universal permission to silence that request throughout a larger menu. The theory must identify what further normative reason gives it that scope.

Nor does merely adding a label such as “irrelevant in this menu” settle the objection. Such a label may describe a coherent rule. Its ethical justification still depends on the actual alternatives and differentiated claims. Our candidate should not evade the problem by defining a new option whenever its preferred verdict changes; neither should the review insist that only physical endpoints can ever matter.

The targeted test applies the existing reason-retention requirement to a further case: after the actual menu changes, state whose protection supplied the earlier exclusion reason, whether that protection is now provided, and what warrants the exclusion if it is not. This does not assert a universal ban on menu-sensitive reasons or introduce reason retention for the first time. It identifies the further explanation required when the strongest unsatisfied request supplies the filter.

## 4. Requirements and counterexamples the candidate must retain

**Chance is a distinct normative step.** Taking a request into account is not identical to giving it a prospect of realization; giving it a prospect is not realizing it. A theory can defend procedural opportunity, but cannot count a lottery ticket as already repaired basic living. Severity ordering alone does not determine an exact chance ratio. The rule must disclose any added claim-strength scale, floor, relevance cutoff or priority of fairness over other reasons.

**A universal positive-chance requirement would conflict with an inherited result.** BC1 sometimes supports definite equivalent rescue of the greater number. A new rule giving every basic claimant an undefeated positive chance would change that commitment. If opportunity is defended only in presently unresolved crossed cases, that scope must be explicit. If it can be overridden, its defeating grounds must be stated. LC's pro tanto status does not by itself decide the matter.

**Probability constraints can obstruct newly available protection.** Suppose two routes protect P alone or Q together with R, and a new, permitted route can protect P together with Q. A response that insists on retaining some abstract equality may exclude the new route even though it could add Q's protection while retaining the original outcomes for P and R. This supplies a test of the equality demand, not a universal proof that fairness never justifies an opportunity constraint. Costs, common dependence and prior rights must be genuinely preserved before the proposed improvement is used.

**Marginal chances are not a complete common history.** Equal individual success rates can conceal different joint failure or shared-support consequences. The [joint-risk study](JOINT-RISK-REVISION-INTEGRATION.md) already establishes that limitation. Reuse it. The intended new positive construction should identify what changes on the same outcome or ticket, preserving relevant common consequences; it must not rebrand mere equality of marginal rates as complete protection equivalence.

**A lottery over route names need not be a lottery over claims.** Duplicate descriptions, overlapping groups and changes in route enumeration can alter group probabilities without creating new living relations. Normalizing arbitrary route weights after adding a duplicate is not a moral response to a new claim. Conversely, a genuinely new joint repair can improve protection even if it serves no newly formed person. The existence and content of that change require independent explanation.

**A cutoff is not acquired ethical authority.** A rule that counts a loss only when it reaches half the strongest loss can be formally tested. The fraction does not emerge from G0 or from the fact that all losses are basic. In a changing menu the cutoff can shift even when the two formerly competing routes retain their actual consequences. Whether that shift is justified is the issue, not a fact established by the score calculation.

**Do not turn a counterexample to one rule into universal impossibility.** A pairwise cutoff rule can cycle; a menu-wide version can avoid an empty choice while changing which claims count. Neither result excludes every limited-aggregation theory, every lottery or every further justified principle. The significant conclusion must identify the premise tested and the normative cost of the proposed repair.

## 5. Primary sources and what is new in this reading

The earlier LC round already read Otsuka's minute-difference objection and Voorhoeve's discussion of distinct stakes hidden by the same anonymous distribution. Those results are reused; this round does not retell them as new discoveries. Source-derived summaries are concentrated here to avoid repeating the same material across the round's notes.

**John Broome, “Fairness,” *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 91 (1990–91):87–101.** Read the [author-hosted PDF](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~sfop0060/pdf/Fairness.pdf), especially §§III–V, printed pp.91–99. Broome distinguishes claims from other reasons and treats chances as a possible surrogate satisfaction when indivisible goods cannot meet all claims. His fairness argument does not equate a fair lottery with the overall required act; he explicitly preserves other reasons and the difference between procedural prospect and actual satisfaction. This is a substantive rival and resource for our chance proposal, not a derivation of its numeric weights or of universal positive chances.

**Alex Voorhoeve, “How Should We Aggregate Competing Claims?” *Ethics* 125(1) (2014):64–87.** This round reads [the author's December 2013 accepted version](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/voorhoev/How%20Should%20We%20Aggregate%20Competing%20Claims%20--%20Ethics%20accepted%20version%20Dec%202013docx.pdf), concentrating on §§V–VII, manuscript pp.15–22. He assesses relevance against the strongest competing claim in the feasible set and defends the resulting menu sensitivity through morally pertinent option individuation. The review therefore does not assume unconditional contraction consistency is a neutral ethical premise. The concurrent aggregation-context study already credits that response; it is reused here. The manuscript pagination is not the published pagination; the previously read §VIII retains its prior scope.

**Johanna Privitera, “Aggregate Relevant Claims in Rescue Cases?” *Utilitas* 30(2) (2018):228–236, DOI `10.1017/S0953820817000279` (online 2017).** Read the complete [published article preserved by the German National Library](https://d-nb.info/1216104093/34), with the new targeted objection in pp.234–236. Privitera grants that finer option individuation can address formal rationality while challenging the substantive argument for globally excluding a claim: yielding to the strongest does not justify yielding to the group actually selected instead. This motivates §3's opponent. It is a criticism of that rationale, not a demonstrated impossibility of every bounded aggregation rule.

Gerard Vong's [“Weighing Up Weighted Lotteries” (2020)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/707212) supplies the overlap comparison assessed by the root researcher; this reviewer also read its introductory and overlap sections but leaves its substantive source summary to the round's source ledger. These are philosophical sources, not clinical guidance or empirical validation. All readings above used primary text; no fresh visual page audit, local source preservation or empirical replication is claimed.

## 7. Final disposition

**Accepted as a defended, explicitly additional normative candidate and a checked conditional opportunity construction.** The final [affirmative argument](CROSSED-CLAIMS-ARGUMENT.md) and [integration](CROSSED-CLAIMS-INTEGRATION.md) preserve the objections above. LR does not infer decisive force merely from retention of a pro tanto reason: it publicly adds local persistence to BC1's already stronger priority. It recognizes that the equality-boundary opponent can remain consistent with the inherited principles, preserves the loss and chance cost, and does not hide ethical stability in a definition of smallness. The claim is philosophical and contestable; the Lean module does not prove it.

The actual-chance construction advances a different connection. It preserves existing protection on the same lottery realization while adding jointly feasible protection. Its application requires legitimate replacement, including relevant procedural, participation or identity commitments, rather than preservation of numerical floors alone. The initial allocation is not certified merely by being the status quo; positive old solo weight is required for strict improvement. Shared routes do not create duplicated claimants.

Two revisions mattered to acceptance: the initial wording about unchanged opportunity grounds was narrowed to existing commitments, leaving the equality-boundary opponent live; and the unsatisfied-anchor criticism was restricted to an unsupported inference from binary yielding to global exclusion. Neither all menu dependence nor every unsatisfied request's influence is prohibited.

The precise remaining boundary is substantive: LR's qualitative existence claim does not calibrate which particular real increase lies within its scope, and more distant crossed losses still require a justified aggregation or opportunity principle. Real costs, risks and procedural conflicts can also defeat the represented transfer. These limitations do not establish universal incomparability or block all further progress. The completed candidate, its philosophical counterposition and the eight checked consequences should be reused rather than reopening G0 or the earlier LC/context diagnostics.
