# Why agreement has normative power: two primary-source challenges

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored assessment continuing [prospective control](PERSPECTIVAL-PROSPECTIVE-CONTROL-INTEGRATION.md) and its [source assessment](PERSPECTIVAL-PROSPECTIVE-CONTROL-SOURCES.md), with the [current affirmative argument and review](PERSPECTIVAL-AGREEMENT-GROUND-REVIEW.md). This round adds exactly two philosophical works. The [nondual method and language correction](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md#formation-participation-and-responsibility) governs the reconstruction. No empirical finding, new mathematical result or legal determination is claimed.

The universal inference under examination is **whole qualitative realization → the power of a formed undertaking to change practical reasons and authority**. An agreement can change what is owed, rather than merely reveal an attitude. Explaining that change still requires explaining why the relevant relation has this ethical significance. Formation, effective influence and justified authority remain different questions within one unfolding.

## 1. Shiffrin: the power to commit can be constitutive of decent relationships

Seana Valentine Shiffrin, “Promising, Intimate Relationships, and Conventionalism,” *The Philosophical Review* 117(4), 481–524 (2008), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1215/00318108-2008-014); [author-hosted scan](https://webshare.law.ucla.edu/Faculty/bibs/shiffrin/Shiffrin-PromisingIntimateRelationships.pdf), linked by her [UCLA bibliography](https://www.law.ucla.edu/faculty/faculty-profiles/seana-shiffrin). Printed pages, not PDF-image numbers, are used below.

On pp. 492–496, Shiffrin rejects expected performance, reliance and a shared valued end as necessary for binding promises. On pp. 498–503, her positive argument assumes autonomy rights and the possibility of minimally decent relationships, then argues that commitment powers are necessary for their adequate conduct. Mere declarations of current intention leave particular vulnerabilities unresolved. On pp. 514–519, she describes transferred decision rights, varied commitments and solicited trust. Promise is not simply revocable permission; it can bind into the future.

**Normative dependence and limit:** the possibility and moral character of decent relationships are premises, including the substantive role of equality. Page 517 acknowledges that the argument does not directly explain how the power is possible; pp. 518–519 supply a trust-based account of bindingness. The paper offers a philosophical grounding candidate, not evidence that every intelligence or every accepted restriction has that authority.

## 2. Raz: reasons justify authority and determine its scope

Joseph Raz, “The Problem of Authority: Revisiting the Service Conception,” *Minnesota Law Review* 90, 1003–1044 (2006), [publisher record](https://journals.law.umn.edu/mlr/vol90/iss4/the-problem-of-authority-revisiting-the-service-conception), [author-institution record](https://scholarship.law.columbia.edu/faculty_scholarship/752/). The assessment uses indexed primary text from the journal's [2011 PDF location](https://www.minnesotalawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Raz_3fmt.pdf) and [2019 location](https://www.minnesotalawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Raz_3fmt.pdf); direct full-text retrieval failed.

Pages 1014–1016 justify authority through better conformity with applicable reasons, qualified by the importance of deciding oneself. Page 1027 prevents justification in one domain from automatically covering extensions that fail its conditions. Pages 1037–1039 distinguish consenting to a specified undertaking from becoming subject to another's future decisions. Consent does not resolve the latter problem simply by occurring: independent considerations must justify its force and reach.

**Normative dependence and limit:** this presupposes substantive reasons and a judgment about when another's direction better serves them. “Independent” here means not justified solely by the authority's directive or the act of consent; it need not mean external to the generative unfolding. These passages do not prove that all authority needs consent, that all delegation is illegitimate, or that Raz has supplied our universal ground.

## 3. The direct correction to a shared-future explanation

The following is our assessment of the repository candidate, not a claim that either source endorses it. The [affirmative review](PERSPECTIVAL-AGREEMENT-GROUND-REVIEW.md#2-a-discriminator-that-does-not-reduce-agreement-to-induced-loss) rightly tests whether faithful continuation matters when a fallback prevents identified material losses. That is a genuine discriminator against a merely loss-based account. It also preserves the limit: a favorable relational feature does not by itself establish a duty owed to a particular person, their power to release it, or permitted enforcement.

The next correction is to avoid making **a jointly valued end** necessary for all agreement-generated reasons. Take a deliberately undertaken, permissible contribution whose content the promisor does not independently value. The recipient may reasonably retain the claim even after the promisor's enthusiasm disappears. Alternatively, both can already favor an activity before any promise is made. A further undertaking might change their practical positions without adding another shared goal. These possibilities require separate adjudication; they cannot be excluded by describing every promise as creating a new common aim.

“Shared continuation” can still describe the resulting relationship if it means that their conduct becomes connected through the undertaking. But that broad description must not do the work of a normative premise. The account should identify what actually changes: who undertakes what, to whom, what consideration is thereafter insufficient to excuse nonperformance, and who can modify or release the undertaking. The reason why those changes are warranted remains substantive. Naming them as a settled relation does not derive them.

This is also a novelty control. A relational explanation of promissory power is already a developed philosophical proposal. The current study's contribution would be a better account of its place and scope within whole generative realization, including other intelligences, rather than the discovery that promises can form relationships or survive changing preferences.

## 4. A positive route and the point at which it remains conditional

The sources suggest a productive reconstruction rather than another verdict about the guild. Explain the power at two connected levels:

1. **Why such a power can be worthwhile.** Where activities and differentiated lives depend on one another, a capacity to undertake a specified future contribution can let participants act through something more than forecasts of each other's moods. It can change the practical organization of dependence and make actual contributions to shared decisions effective.
2. **What its particular exercise establishes.** The content and context of this undertaking can give an identified participant standing concerning an identified performance. Its force need not disappear whenever unilateral revision would now be attractive. The existence of that force is a proposed ethical feature of the relation, not another empirical prediction.

The strongest consent rival should receive both claims. A person can exercise agency by making some later choices unavailable, and faithful completion can remain meaningful after immediate coordination gains or current approval end. The source challenge therefore rules out the shortcut “the agreement no longer maximizes present options, so it no longer binds.” It also warns against treating each promise as justified only if the particular performance produces a new cooperative benefit. The general significance of being able to commit and the grounds of a particular obligation need not coincide in that way.

The unresolved bridge is exact: why does this worthwhile capacity establish **this participant's claim**, rather than only a general reason to sustain reliable relationships? A proposed answer must connect the deliberate undertaking to the recipient's changed standing and explain why later unilateral withdrawal defeats that relation. That answer may invoke a substantive principle about trust, respect, decisions or warranted commitments. It should defend that principle through the relevant whole realization rather than represent it as already proved by the formation mechanism.

## 5. Testing limits without erasing commitment

A useful constructed comparison holds comprehension, sincerity and later regret constant while varying the actual power conferred:

| Undertaking | What the theory must explain |
|---|---|
| Perform a specified contribution; the recipient can release it. | Why a directed claim exists despite no material reliance loss, and why release has this effect. |
| Follow an expedition leader's navigation decisions for the trip, including disliked decisions. | Why settling who decides can be justified, and why later disagreement alone need not reopen the question. |
| Accept the same leader's indefinite direction over unrelated ordinary activity after the trip. | Whether the expanded domain has a further warrant; clear disclosure prevents evading this question through a misunderstanding diagnosis. |

Do not assume that only the first two can be justified. The third may express a deliberately sought form of enduring allegiance. The affirmative must explain the relevant ethical difference, including what such allegiance realizes and obstructs. Nor does the rejection of a particular interference automatically erase a performance claim or establish a participant's permission to disregard every undertaking. Permission, obligation, delegated discretion and enforcement remain distinct possible effects of the same formed relationship.

For the universal programme, the constructive result is a sharpened candidate: warranted commitment can change practical reasons because of the form of participation and dependence it establishes, while its grounds may also explain its limits. The substantive burden is to justify that form and its differentiated powers across the relevant kinds of intelligence. These human philosophical accounts neither settle that scope nor require identical powers wherever intelligence occurs. The earlier Ferguson, Mill and Wenner assessments remain inherited challenges, not additional new confirmations.
