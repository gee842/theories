## 1. Result and inherited work

Adaptive self-maintenance provides a serious candidate explanation of how something can go well or badly for a nonsentient life. It is a stronger starting point than a count of persistence, reproduction or successful outputs. Our ethical proposal nevertheless concerns **the living continuation in its actual relations**, including dependence, impairment, recovery and endings. It does not make successful self-care the measure of standing or treat the continuation of every regulating process as good overall.

The [earlier adaptive-significance assessment](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#35-adaptive-significance-and-the-good-of-the-bearer) already distinguishes self-production, adaptivity and ethical warrant through Di Paolo's work. The [completed living-dependencies account](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/LIVING-DEPENDENCIES.md) already explains retained provision, supported recovery and changing requirements. Neither is a new result of this round. The contribution here is a specific comparison of two proposed grounds for nonsentient good, followed by a test of what they can support in the wider G0 argument.

## 3. Affirmative argument: what is being sustained?

Begin with the actual formation and continuation of a life. Its organization, material exchanges, capacities and relations arise together. A boundary identifies a relatively continuing organization; it does not make its conditions independent. In a suitable case, a deprivation damages the activity through which that living organization continues, whether or not anyone notices, owns it or finds it useful. Describing this relationship can explain why a change counts as functional impairment rather than merely a deviation from an observer's preference.

The proposed ethical identification is stronger: **that living continuation itself has basic positive significance within the whole**. This is G0 applied to an adequately described case. Its favorable ground is not the bare fact that a regulatory loop meets its target. It concerns the living whose conditions the regulation helps realize. Consequently, failed regulation can disclose a threat to something significant; success is not required before there is anything to protect.

There are substantive reasons for this identification. It recognizes the actual life rather than only its usefulness to an incumbent evaluator. It allows losses in nonsentient living to count without inventing hidden pain. It explains why support can remain directed toward the same living continuation when the supported activity cannot restore its own conditions unaided. It also joins self-care and help within one account: where support is provided does not by itself decide whose living it enables or whether that living matters.

These reasons favor the proposed foundation; they do not make it true by definition. “Living” must have descriptive criteria that remain applicable in an ethically adverse history. One cannot first select only the processes already judged worth continuing and then claim all living has positive significance. Conversely, an unfavorable whole verdict need not erase the real living that occurs within that history.

The premises and their roles are explicit:

| Commitment | Role in this argument |
|---|---|
| There is a specified living continuation, with adequately described relations and consequences. | Correspondence requirement; biological classification or organizational interpretation needs evidence appropriate to the case. |
| Certain changes support or impair that continuation. | Causal and functional assessment within the same history; not a verdict that every successful process deserves promotion. |
| G0: living and its further generation within the whole have basic positive normative significance. | Substantive ethical identification defended here, and already accepted as the alignment task's axiom. It is not hidden in “adaptive” or “functional.” |
| J1–J4 and faithful correspondence E. | Additional commitments governing whole comparison, basic protection, bounded responsibility and legitimate feasible choice. They are reused from the completed account. |

This separation records the argument's dependencies. It does not posit a second reality of values or prohibit a future successful constitutive explanation. Such an explanation would need to defend the identification, not merely rename the functional description.

## 5. Strongest counterreview

**First objection: an objective functional loss need not be an ethically significant loss.** The serious rival accepts the whole relational description, including the organism's regulation and dependence. It holds that these features explain success for that organization while conscious stakes, separately warranted relationships or other specified grounds explain moral significance. It need not deny biology or claim that only currently felt episodes matter. The [earlier conscious-relations comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#41-conscious-stakes-and-actual-relations) already permits a stronger account of conscious lives whose goods extend beyond presently experienced benefits.

Our reply is that nonsentient living has a positively articulated candidate ground, not merely an absence of the rival's favored property. Supporting and destroying an actual living continuation differ in what is realized and lost. G0 takes that difference to have basic significance. The rival owes an account of why this significance is exhausted by other stakes or absent when those stakes are absent. Yet the demand is reciprocal: our claim that the loss matters ethically must be defended, rather than established by calling it a loss. The present comparison favors a wider account without demonstrating that every restriction is incoherent.

**Second objection: dependence makes the boundary of the alleged good arbitrary.** If many activities sustain a life, why privilege this life rather than its components or the entire ecological configuration? Our answer begins with differentiated organization and actual undergoing, not an independent owner. The kind of good at issue must track the continuation and relationships it concerns. Whole appraisal can include several kinds of living significance without assuming equal weights or duplicating one benefit each time a description changes. This is a requirement on correspondence and appraisal, not a completed universal rule for counting all possible forms of life.

**Third objection: the theory recognizes a positive aspect too cheaply.** A basic consideration that never changes a justified response would do little explanatory work. Our account therefore retains practical consequences where G0/J/E warrant them: a supported living continuation can provide a reason for care, recovery or noninterference, and real conflict can constrain that response. No numerical size is assigned merely to secure this result. Establishing those consequences for a particular unfamiliar form still requires its actual conditions, alternatives and responsibility relations. Broad organizational resemblance alone is insufficient.

**Fourth objection: the argument should establish scientific necessity.** Its success would strengthen G0's ethical case, but the claim that every scientifically adequate philosophy must be compatible with it requires a further argument. A rival that retains the specified biological and epistemic relations does not become scientifically inadequate solely because it offers a different normative explanation. To establish that conclusion, we would have to show a substantive explanatory failure, contradiction or independently warranted ethical error at the disputed scope. This round has not done so. That limit is about this argument's achievement; it is not a proof that such a necessity argument is impossible.

## 8. What a change in feeling does and does not settle

Our own assessment must apply equal discipline in both directions. Removing feeling from an imagined relationship cannot simply leave every familiar good intact. Felt companionship, enjoyment and suffering are actual constituents of the lives in question. If they are absent, the account has to describe the changed relationship and reassess its grounds. A verbal report or identical output does not substitute for undergoing an experience.

Equally, the absence of an experiential constituent does not by itself establish the disappearance of every ethical ground. That further judgment concerns what remains: an actual living continuation, its organization, participation, dependence and effects. Under G0, such living already has basic positive significance. The argument must identify that living independently of the desired verdict and explain why its realization matters; successful regulation or the word “care” cannot do this work alone. This is the affirmative ground defended in §§3–5, not an inference that whatever remains after subtraction must retain the original value.

Consider Ren's helping comparison. Help can benefit both people and change the helper's life through new participation, acquired capacities and altered projects. Looking at both within the whole reveals these transformations; it does not erase which person bears a burden, participates voluntarily or loses another possibility. Shared satisfaction can be one constituent. A changed feeling can alter the relationship without settling every question about continued support, an acquired commitment or a life now able to continue. Those reasons require their particular G0/J/E grounds. No claim that all aspects have equal value, or that helper and helped are interchangeable, follows.

This comparison is not a clinical test. Temporary loss of felt engagement in an actual human history is different from a stipulated being forever incapable of affect. Existing capacities, commitments, recovery and participation must remain in view. Neither a report of emotional flatness nor reduced expression warrants removing someone's protection or treating their life as empty.

“Consciousness differentiates significance” also does not mean that consciousness multiplies every good by a positive constant. Particular experiences can enrich, injure or change a continuation. The contribution must be explained in the case; a general numerical bonus or automatic priority has not been justified. Likewise, an argument for nonconscious mentality would still leave the significance of nonmental living to defend.

**Counterreview.** A restrictive opponent can accept this description while denying that the remaining living grounds independent ethical value. Our reply gives an affirmative reason: the continuation realizes a life whose actual loss is not exhausted by loss of a useful output or a felt episode. G0 recognizes that realization positively. This is a substantive ethical identification, supported by the dependence and loss comparisons, rather than an independently established scientific entailment. The opponent's narrower welfare thesis alone does not refute it; our distinction between welfare and wider significance alone does not prove it. Both sides must address the same living history.

## 11. The affirmative identification and its limits

The common ground proposed here is **living being realized through its actual relations**. Knowing is a differentiated activity within some such histories. Its significance can include understanding, participation and felt fulfillment. Living without knowing can still have organized continuation, dependence, development and actual loss. Those descriptions must be established through their particular relations; ethical approval is not a condition of calling the process living.

G0 recognizes this living realization positively. Its strongest rationale here is that continuation can be something worth sustaining in the living history itself, rather than acquiring all its significance from recognition, usefulness or a future contribution to cognition. This allows care to follow what support actually realizes, including dependence and impaired regulation. It also allows a finite life to matter without needing to justify itself through indefinite production. The account connects care, self-care and formation through a common ground while retaining their distinct consequences.

That rationale is an ethical identification, not a deduction from the shared occurrence of metabolism or regulation. A common property does not automatically explain the value of every case possessing it. Nor does the formation of consciousness within living make consciousness ethically dispensable: a differentiated capacity can introduce a new kind of good or harm. The proposal is that such differences enrich or alter the grounds of appraisal without exhausting all of them. That last claim is precisely what the restrictive opponent disputes.

Three shortcuts therefore fail to complete the necessity argument. The biological role of experience does not settle its ethical role. Gradual formation does not make every proposed ethical threshold arbitrary. And the dependence of knowing on living does not confer identical standing on every supporting component. These are limitations of the attempted inferences, not evidence of two independent realities. The whole remains differentiated, with actual dependencies, experiences and losses.

## 12. A comparison that makes the commitment consequential

Consider a **conceptual maintenance decision**, not an experimental result. A legitimate undertaking can proceed through either of two available arrangements. One retains a specified nonconscious living culture; the other interrupts its provision and ends that living continuation. The culture's ongoing organization, dependence and cessation are part of the description, independently of any ethical verdict.

For the narrow comparison, the following conditions are explicit. The alternatives achieve the same warranted task, with equivalent burdens, risks and effects on other protected living. Retention requires no unauthorized use of another's resources, imposed care, foregone legitimate purpose or concealed destructive consequence. Effects on experience, inquiry, commitments and other living relations have been considered. These are demanding correspondence conditions, not a claim that actual laboratory or ecological choices routinely satisfy them. Nor is the culture imagined outside all relations: the difference is precisely which continuation its provision enables.

Under G0, the retained living supplies a positive consideration even when its usefulness to conscious life does not distinguish the alternatives. With the genuine absence of countervailing loss, **J1 adds comparative favor for retention**. If this comparison is warranted, the menu is complete for the decision and both plans are authorized and jointly feasible, J4 gives that favor practical force by excluding the defeated option. This is an application of the already declared philosophical commitments, not a new mathematical theorem or an unconditional duty to maintain cultures. J3 does not assign an indefinite caretaker merely because that person could help.

Thus the theory can change a decision, rather than adding an ethical label that always disappears during comparison. No numerical value for the culture is introduced. A restrictive account can still reject the independent reason, or favor retention for another identified ground. Either response must address the same continuation. Identical choices for different reasons would not by themselves decide between the accounts.

If retention introduces a genuine burden or threat, the clean comparison ends. Actual investigation must restore that difference to the description and use the applicable practical principles; one cannot declare the burden negligible to preserve the favored verdict. For severe uncertainty, the case remains conditional. A real discriminating application would need credible evidence about living status, experience, consequences, alternatives and permissions. This conceptual comparison establishes neither that correspondence nor a universal empirical prediction.

Each account must defend its normative identification at the claimed scope.

## 15. Stronger importance does not establish exclusive significance

The first control concerns what a comparison actually establishes. Suppose, in a stipulated tragic choice, preserving a conscious life is warranted over preserving some nonconscious living. This control cuts both ways. G0 does not require selecting every new living continuation, assigning equal weight to all lives, or making any finite quantity of one kind of gain outweigh another kind of loss. Conversely, preferring conscious continuation does not by itself vindicate a zero-significance boundary. A comparative verdict must identify the ground that survives in the foregone alternative and the reason the other ground prevails. Calling the choice obvious cannot replace that explanation.

The second control concerns capacities and their exercise. An account restricted to the capacity for consciousness must not be criticized as though it counted only presently felt episodes. Actual continuity, retained capacity and the formation or loss of a capacity need their own descriptions. Temporary absence of an episode is not a philosophical demonstration of permanent absence of capacity. This is a scope requirement for the conceptual comparison, not a clinical classification or advice about any actual patient.

The third control concerns gradual change. Continuity of biological formation does not itself eliminate every possible ethical threshold. Equally, a proposed threshold must identify the ethical difference it tracks. The question is not whether a line can be drawn, but why crossing it should remove every other proposed basic ground. An account of richer conscious realization can support a difference in significance while leaving that stronger exclusion unsupported.

## 20. Actual good is not identical to marginal improvement

Consider a supported life continuing through a particular arrangement. Another feasible arrangement could have sustained that same life equally well. The availability of the second arrangement does not erase what the first actually realizes. It does, however, matter when deciding which arrangement is needed and who has a reason or responsibility to provide it. This is our application of the distinction tested by the source comparison, not a claim that every substitute is genuinely equivalent.

In the earlier helping case, both people may benefit while the helper's life changes shape. A welfare-based account can already recognize particular relationships, distinctive benefits and forgone possibilities; we cannot refute it by replacing that richer view with a single net total. The whole perspective asks which living and relationships actually continue, through whose changing activity, with which losses. It does not make every beneficial contribution indispensable.

This corrects a possible misuse of G0. **Realized living good, additional good relative to an alternative, and an obligation to realize it are different judgments.** None can silently serve as the measure of the others. A replacement arrangement can discharge a particular service need without making the earlier support worthless. Conversely, a genuine positive aspect does not show that adding another arrangement improves the whole, especially when it duplicates provision, displaces other living or imposes burdens. The completed J/E account already governs those comparisons and responsibilities; no new allocation rule is introduced.

The same caution concerns levels of description.
