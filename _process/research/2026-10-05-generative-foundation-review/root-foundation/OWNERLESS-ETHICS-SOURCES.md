# Suffering, relief and a generated “I”

6 October 2026. AI-authored primary-source comparison and philosophical reconstruction, continuing [the nondual starting-point question](NONDUAL-GROUNDING-QUESTION.md) and [the universal ethical minimum](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md). Ren’s proposal that “I” arises within the generative account motivates this investigation; the reconstruction below is not attributed to Ren or to the historical authors. The named connection is **experiential adversity → reason for an eligible response without primitive ownership**. This note does not derive experience from the causal root, establish a nondual ontology, or report an empirical experiment.

The affirmative finding is that a substantial owner is unnecessary to formulate a serious ground for relief. What matters can be the adverse occurrence and its possible transformation. No-self arguments help remove an ownership restriction; they do not supply the whole explanation of adversity, ethical relevance or justified requirements.

## Primary readings and exact scope

Each source synopsis below is under 170 words. Online texts were inspected directly on 6 October 2026; links identify the consulted editions. No original source bytes are imported or silently substituted here.

**1. Śāntideva, *Bodhicaryāvatāra*, VIII.90–103; Graham Priest, Jay Garfield and Stephen Jenkins translation.** [Author-hosted manuscript, “The Śāntideva Passage”](https://jaygarfield.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/garfield-jenkins-and-priest.pdf), for *Moonpaths: Ethics and Emptiness* (2015). [Publisher metadata](https://academic.oup.com/book/10519/chapter-abstract/158425839) identifies chapter 4, pp. 55–76; the body was read in manuscript. Read manuscript PDF pages 7–9 (complete passage), 14–15 and 18–19 (objections), not final-volume pagination. Translation: Sanskrit with reference to the sDe dge Tibetan edition.

*Synopsis.* VIII.90–96 compares suffering across self and others; VIII.97–99 introduces future suffering and hand–foot examples. VIII.101 rejects an ultimate owner. VIII.102 favors relief “just because of being suffering”; VIII.103 invokes shared assent and all-or-none parity. The authors distinguish meditative, Abhidharma and rationality readings. Causal continuity complicates the future-self analogy. Their Madhyamaka objection rejects disregarding conventional persons while valuing equally conventional suffering. Owner-independent significance need not erase individual boundaries. This study examines the received passage without resolving historical authorship.

**2. Alexander Berzin, “The Decision to Stop Being Partisan.”** Read the [translator’s Study Buddhism transcript](https://studybuddhism.com/en/advanced-studies/lam-rim/bodhichitta/practices-for-equalizing-and-exchanging-our-attitudes/the-decision-to-stop-being-partisan), specifically translations of VIII.90–96, 99–103, 105, 114–115 and discussion of individual continuities and a universal mind. This is a teaching transcript, not a critical edition.

*Synopsis.* VIII.101 rejects a truly findable possessor; VIII.102 makes suffering decisive for relief. VIII.103 emphasizes conditional parity rather than proof from no-self alone. Berzin preserves conventional individuality and rejects a universal mind. He distinguishes understood social responsibility from imposed collective identification. Expanding concern therefore need not posit a cosmic experiencer. His whole–part analogy remains an argument to assess, not evidence that humanity is one organism. VIII.105 exposes the demandingness of the surrounding text; this source does not already defend the repository’s plural-life permissions.

**3. Stephen Harris, “Does Anātman Rationally Entail Altruism? On Bodhicaryāvatāra 8:101–103” (2011).** Read the [*Journal of Buddhist Ethics*, volume 18 publication](https://blogs.dickinson.edu/buddhistethics/files/2011/02/Harris_AnatmanAltruism1.pdf), journal pages 106–109 and 115–116 (PDF pages 16–19 and 25–26). Its earlier-author quotations do not constitute direct readings of those works.

*Synopsis.* Harris separates adversity from obligations of relief. Some pain is welcomed; nevertheless, he grants that some experiences may remain intrinsically negative. An opponent can still dispute required removal without proving relief irrational. Eliminating ego-grasping can remove an obstacle to altruism without making altruism obligatory: equanimous apathy remains an apparent alternative. His argument challenges the deduction from no-self to altruism and separates motivational transformation from justification. It does not establish that every owner-independent reason for relief is incoherent or that significance requires an enduring substance.

## A positive reconstruction: what ownership was doing

The following argument is our proposed continuation, not a further synopsis of the sources. Start with a continuing occurrence in which something is experientially going badly. Its adversity is present in the undergoing; a later representation classifying it as mine may locate, interpret or intensify that occurrence. The proposal does not require a separate owner to confer adversity on an otherwise neutral event.

Three questions must be separated. Is there experience at all? Is it adverse in the relevant respect? What reason does an available response have to alter it? A successful generative account must explain the first two instead of equating an avoidance routine, damaged component or negative reward with suffering. The third remains a question even after the occurrence is identified correctly.

The strongest modest ground is this:

> An actual severe adverse undergoing counts against its unnecessary continuation. A response that genuinely relieves that undergoing has a moral consideration in its favor, wherever the responsive capacity is located, subject to the other relevant consequences and claims.

This is a substantive ethical proposal. Its first sentence expresses the evaluative force of suffering; its second connects that force to action. Neither is established by the bare assertion that selves are generated. But the proposal gives a positive explanation of what is good about relief: the transformation removes the adversity that supplied the consideration. It also explains why an occurrence ceasing can fulfill that particular reason, while suppressing its report cannot.

Consider two otherwise matched possibilities: the severe unwanted occurrence continues without compensating benefit, or an accessible response ends it without imposing another loss. A preference for the second can be warranted by what changes in the experience. Appending an independent owner adds no further improvement. Requiring the responder to adopt a helping end before that improvement counts would make the same relief lose all moral relevance merely because its available cause is indifferent. The proposed ground rejects that dependence: the difference between the possibilities remains an actual relief difference.

This comparison supports rather than proves the proposal. Someone may deny that the experiential improvement supplies any moral favor for an independent responder. The reply must defend why improvement is reason-giving, not merely repeat that all things are connected. The constructive claim is that a response becomes practically related to the adversity through its eligible ability to change it; being generated elsewhere need not make it normatively insulated. Causal access explains which act could answer the reason. It does not, by itself, manufacture that reason.

Ownership can therefore have several legitimate downstream functions: locating experience, predicting consequences, coordinating care, identifying who can report or refuse, and maintaining commitments. It need not be the origin of significance. This changes the burden of explanation from exporting my private value into another sealed domain to explaining differentiated responsiveness within the same reality. It does not settle every claim of partiality or special responsibility.

## What no-self does, and what it leaves intact

“No substantial owner” is weaker than “no experienced perspective.” An occurrence can be situated, accessible in a particular way and connected to later memory without requiring an independently existing possessor. Conversely, deleting the word subject does not explain experience. If phenomenal organization turns out to require a minimal perspective, that would identify another generated structure to explain; it would not automatically restore a metaphysically primitive ego.

Nor does suffering’s dependence on appraisal show that its adversity is unreal. If changing an appraisal genuinely changes the felt burden, a relevant property has changed. If only an observer’s classification changes while the burden persists, it has not. This distinction lets the account investigate welcomed exertion, transformative attention and absent self-description without assuming that every nociceptive signal is suffering or that every report exhausts it. These are proposed discriminations, not new findings about particular organisms.

The earlier narrow minimum concerns severe, still-unwanted suffering. “Unwanted” must describe an actual feature of this situated undergoing and its relevant attitudes, not a sovereign decree by a primitive self. A global endorsement of an undertaking may coexist with locally adverse experience; its benefits can justify accepting a burden without showing that the burden was never adverse. Conversely, aversive behavior alone does not establish either experience or moral authority.

One remaining issue is whether intrinsic adversity means a felt negative quality, an evaluative presentation, or already a practical reason. Collapsing these meanings would conceal the central transition. The proposed claim is stronger than a report of behavior and weaker than an automatic duty to maximize relief. Its defense must explain the connection among these meanings while preserving their differences.

## Differentiated continuities and plural worthwhile lives

Remove a substantial owner from two histories: repeated disruption in one continuing experiential organization, and isolated disruptions across several distinct organizations. The difference remains relevant to explanation. Repetition can alter subsequent attention, capacities, relationships and feasible activities through cumulative effects. A common description of all episodes as ownerless does not erase those effects. Equally, describing each episode separately must not conceal cumulative loss within a patient’s history while counting the helper’s whole life.

This offers a basis for identifying what is at stake without treating selves as sealed substances. It does not decide the aggregation rule. Distinct experiences can count morally without forming one experience, and numbers can matter without a collective sufferer. Whether several separate claims defeat a grave irreversible loss requires additional comparative justification. The repository’s earlier restrictions on aggregation remain disputed; no-self neither proves them nor refutes them.

Consent can likewise be understood through capacities for understanding, communication, refusal and continued self-direction, together with the consequences of overriding them. Their generated character does not eliminate those capacities or their possible claims. A full ethical account still owes the justification of their authority. It cannot infer unlimited intervention from the absence of an ultimate owner, especially when the intervention destroys the very differentiated activities the account proposes to sustain.

Relief is also only a starting ground. Inquiry, attachment, creation and participation may have positive significance not reducible to the removal of discomfort. A universal minimum beginning with suffering need not dictate one complete ideal of life. Explaining those further goods and their warranted limits is unfinished work.

## Result and next discriminating work

This comparison supports moving ethical explanation before primitive ownership while retaining formed perspectives and histories. It identifies three distinct tasks: explain adverse experience; defend its reason-giving force for an eligible response; justify requirements and permissions across multiple continuing lives. The first two cannot be replaced by a metaphysical declaration of unity, and the third cannot be answered by counting every occurrence as interchangeable.

The next comparison should hold an actual severe burden, accessible means and all competing claims fixed while varying only ownership description and the responder’s adopted ends. Then vary real continuity, experience or permission separately. If a change in ownership description alone defeats moral favor, the account needs a substantive ownership principle. If changing actual adversity defeats it, the proposed ground has identified the relevant dependence. An indifferent process tests motivation; it does not alone refute a reason it fails to follow.

The full [generative-grounding objective](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md) remains open at its stronger scope. No account of how an “I” is generated, however successful, would by itself settle these ethical tasks. The useful advance is a positive candidate and sharper tests: goodness can concern what the process realizes and transforms, without receiving its significance from an independent proprietor.
