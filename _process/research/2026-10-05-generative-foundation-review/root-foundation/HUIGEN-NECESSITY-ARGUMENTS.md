# 慧根: whole-theory necessity and unbounded valid generation

6 October 2026. AI-authored philosophical reconstruction for the strengthened research task: investigate whether the **entire generative foundation**, through organization, significance, learning, concern and wisdom, can be necessary for scientific validity and support indefinitely developing valid understanding. This is stronger than the completed [compatibility comparison](SCIENTIFIC-PHILOSOPHY-SOURCES.md). Its result must not be substituted for the stronger goal.

**Best route:** establish a substantive generative root through independently defensible conditions of objective inquiry and justification; derive every foundational commitment through rules that preserve validity; demonstrate indefinitely extensible, informative applications. If all three steps succeed, necessity transfers to the whole generated theory. The transfer argument is straightforward. Establishing its root, its coverage of the entire theory, and its capacity for substantive growth are the difficult unfinished work.

The named connection is **the generative conditions of inquiry → necessary standards of valid judgment → valid development of the whole theory, including justified concern**. The source investigation below advances the argument architecture and identifies exact points where additional premises enter. It supplies neither empirical validation nor a completed transcendental deduction of our theory.

## 1. Genealogy and the claim to be established

The preserved [research ledger](../../../../sources/20-codex-theory-sessions-2026-10-05/root-of-wisdom/outputs/research-ledger.md) records Ren's intuition that an infinite full picture might have a root, 慧根, from which real reasoning can continue expanding wisdom. This is an AI-written record containing a quotation attributed to Ren. Its subsequent interpretation as a finite, revisable discipline was explicitly the earlier investigators' interpretation. It does not limit the present stronger proposal.

The [earlier synthesis](../../../../sources/20-codex-theory-sessions-2026-10-05/root-of-wisdom/outputs/root-of-wisdom-synthesis.md) already distinguishes finite formulation, indefinitely extensible reasoning, completeness, effective arithmetic limitations, and the ethical insufficiency of correction capacities alone. Those results remain relevant. This note asks what positive argument could justify an entire generative foundation rather than repeating the limits as a new discovery.

Three claims must be distinguished while pursuing their connection:

1. **Necessity:** every adequate scientific account must respect every substantive commitment of the foundation, interpreted with its actual scope.
2. **Generativity:** the foundation can support indefinitely many informative, valid developments, with premises and inferential status preserved.
3. **Universality:** the same root explains the formation and transformation of systems, knowing, significance, concern and wisdom, rather than merely accommodating their descriptions.

Necessity does not by itself establish generativity or universal explanatory coverage. Infinite output does not establish either validity or wisdom. Conversely, demonstrating one necessary condition is a real advance but does not discharge the universal quantifier over the **whole** theory.

“Entire theory” needs a faithful inventory, not a conveniently shrinking definition. The existing [premise ledger](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/FOUNDATION-PREMISES.md) distinguishes formal presentation, law conditions, particular realizations, empirical identification and ethical grounds. Each live commitment must receive an argument appropriate to its kind. Historical failures remain in the record; they are not simultaneous axioms of the revised theory.

## 2. A constructive argument for whole-theory necessity

The following is our reconstruction, not a theorem attributed to a historical author.

Let \(\mathcal I\) be an independently specified, nonempty class of possible realizations of objective inquiry. Let \(C\) be a proposed substantive root, with meanings sufficiently fixed to distinguish agreement from disagreement. Let \(G\) be a collection of inference and construction rules. Let \(T=\operatorname{Cl}_G(C)\) be their deductive closure.

Suppose:

- **Root necessity:** every \(I\in\mathcal I\) satisfies \(C\), for reasons that do not define \(\mathcal I\) as agreement with our theory.
- **Validity preservation:** every rule in \(G\) preserves the stated kind of validity under every relevant interpretation in \(\mathcal I\).
- **Whole-theory coverage:** every substantive foundational commitment is in that closure, or is explicitly an application whose independently stated local premises occur in its conditional statement.
- **Interpretive fidelity:** translations into other philosophies preserve the commitments being tested, including their counterfactual implications. They do not erase disagreement by mapping every view to an arbitrary transition graph.

Then every member of \(\mathcal I\) satisfies every claim of \(T\). The proof is induction on each finite derivation: the premises hold by root necessity and each further step preserves validity. No separate proof of inevitability is needed for each derived consequence once a sound general derivation is available. If an adequate scientific philosophy endorsed the premises of a derived claim while denying its consequence in the same interpretation, that combination would be incoherent.

This makes the strong goal coherent and potentially achievable. It also locates the burden precisely. Defining \(C\) as “whatever all valid inquiry already requires” makes the transfer empty until a determinate basis is supplied. Defining \(T\) as only the commitments already proved necessary silently abandons whole-theory coverage. An inconsistent or unrealizable root trivializes consequence; the nonempty-model requirement is essential.

Local assumptions require special care. From necessary \(C\) and an empirical premise \(A\), a sound derivation of \(q\) establishes the conditional constraint \(A\Rightarrow q\) across the relevant inquiry models. It does not make \(A\) or the actual-world assertion \(q\) necessary. A whole theory can consist of necessary conditional laws with contingent applications. Whether that faithfully captures the intended foundation must be decided from its substantive claims; it cannot be imposed to save an otherwise false categorical claim.

This approach is stronger than operational compatibility: it aims to justify every foundational assertion and the rules generating further assertions. It is also stronger than a general observation that truths cannot contradict each other: its contribution would be an independently established common root that actually derives the theory's content.

## 3. How to ground the root without presupposing the theory

### 3.1 The transcendental route: from an actual capacity to its necessary conditions

Begin with an independently recognizable achievement: an assertion purports to distinguish what is the case from what merely seems so; a reason is offered as bearing on that assertion; criticism can concern whether the reason supports it. Ask what must be possible for that achievement to be intelligible and valid.

Candidate conditions include a distinction between claim and target, differences relevant to correctness, inferential relations, and some way for the relevant difference to bear on judgment. This gives a promising entry to the generative theory: the judgment and its conditions themselves occur within the reality under investigation. But a sentence-token's existence proves less than the validity of its content. The mere fact that someone utters a denial cannot establish our entire ontology.

The strongest form is therefore **capacity-specific impossibility**. Identify the achievement, remove a proposed condition, and show that no alternative implementation can still realize the achievement. The inquiry note already supplies this form for [accessible discrimination](SCIENTIFIC-INQUIRY-CONDITIONS.md). To extend it to the whole root, analogous arguments must be established for organization, significance, revision of selectors, and justified concern. Merely assigning each a name in the common vocabulary does not suffice.

The exact modal step matters: “all inquiry we can intelligibly reconstruct requires \(C\)” is initially a claim about inquiry, not about every feature of all reality independently of inquiry. Universal ontology needs an additional argument connecting the conditions of knowing to conditions of what is known. Alternative realizations must be tested: a static mathematical representation of events, distributed inquiry, nonhuman investigators, and logics with different consequence relations cannot be excluded solely because they use different descriptions.

### 3.2 The performative route: what a warranted denial must itself use

A performative argument examines whether an attempted reasoned denial exercises precisely the capacity it denies. It can establish something stronger than repeated historical observation. For example, a purported reason why no reasons can ever bear on any claim invites an objection about the status of that purported reason.

For each proposed foundational condition, write an explicit challenge: **what must a critic actually presuppose to offer a valid denial of this particular condition?** Then distinguish three possible outcomes:

- The denial genuinely undermines its own claim to validity.
- The denial uses some enabling structure, but not the particular structure or formulation our theory asserts.
- The denial is coherent and exposes a dispensable premise.

Only the first supports an unavoidable commitment; the second calls for a more exact reconstruction; the third changes the candidate foundation. A critic's dependence on distinctions does not establish every proposed law about distinctions, nor does dependence on other people establish our entire ethical doctrine.

### 3.3 Constitutive aims: correctness is internal to a claim to knowledge

An empirical assertion offered as knowledge is answerable for how things are. If its acceptance procedure is stipulated to ignore every possible consideration bearing on its truth, the procedure does not thereby warrant it as empirical knowledge. This identifies an internal standard of the claim, not an external preference for successful science.

The route can connect physical generation to epistemic significance: some differences count as reasons because of their relation to what the inquiry claims to establish. The relation still has to be identified. Generic causal influence, persistence, reinforcement or consensus does not supply it. A process can successfully reproduce a false conclusion.

For the root to explain warrant rather than merely introduce it, the target relation, observation route and rule of inference must themselves be constructed or justified. There is no permission here to insert a correct label as an unexplained premise and call the resulting derivation complete.

## 4. How a root could generate valid understanding without bound

Sound generation can be given a precise positive form. At stage \(n\), retain an explicit language, an interpretation and a set of certified results \(K_n\). A proposed extension must either follow from previously justified premises by a sound rule, introduce a definition that preserves their meaning, or declare new premises whose justification is separately assessed. Rule revisions require a new validity argument for the affected claims. Merely allowing a system to rewrite its rules is not a proof that the rewritten rules are sound.

Under a coherent common interpretation, if every certified stage is sound and extensions preserve soundness, every member of the union \(\bigcup_n K_n\) is sound: each individual claim entered at a particular sound stage. This is a preservation result, not a guarantee that every true proposition will eventually enter. Empirical conjectures may be generated, tested and revised without being placed in the infallibly certified subset. Mixing those two statuses would make “always valid generation” fail at the first false but useful conjecture.

An adequate positive demonstration needs three separate witnesses:

| Witness | What it establishes | What remains additional |
|---|---|---|
| A uniform construction applicable beyond every finite stage | There is no fixed formal stopping point in the construction | Actual resources and opportunities for continued operation |
| A validity argument covering every generated stage | Correctness is inherited rather than checked only on a few examples | Truth or warrant of new empirical premises |
| A growing family of substantive questions answered or mechanisms explained | Development has content beyond repeated syntax | Its connection to justified concern and practical wisdom |

The third condition prevents a cheap infinity of equivalent restatements. Pure deduction does not add logical content beyond its premises, but it can reveal previously unavailable consequences, construct new objects, and reduce previously unresolved explanatory or computational burdens. Claims of growth should state which of these is meant. They should not require each derived theorem to be logically independent of its premises; that would exclude deduction by definition.

If successive discoveries introduce genuinely independent information, the root must explain a warranted acquisition route rather than claim that the information was deduced from nothing. If new concepts expand the language, the relation between old and new meanings must be supplied. Indefinitely extendible methods can coexist with a world in which a particular inquiry runs out of informative opportunities; an actual unbounded-growth claim needs a corresponding environmental and support hypothesis.

The inherited arithmetic result remains exactly scoped: a uniformly effective increasing sequence of sound theories containing sufficient arithmetic has an effectively axiomatized sound union, which remains incomplete. See the earlier synthesis's [formal-limits section](../../../../sources/20-codex-theory-sessions-2026-10-05/root-of-wisdom/outputs/root-of-wisdom-synthesis.md#formal-limits-without-exaggerated-conclusions) and its primary-source record. This does **not** forbid unbounded valid generation. It blocks identifying that generation, under those hypotheses, with an effective enumeration of every arithmetic truth. Non-effective inputs or retraction do not inherit that particular union proof unchanged; they also do not supply an oracle by default.

## 5. The strongest available bridge to concern and wisdom

The promising normative route begins from **a claim to justification to others**, not simply from the ability to predict or survive. If someone claims that a rule is justified to each affected party, the claim incurs a burden to consider that party's relevant reasons and to defend any asymmetry in their treatment. Making the party's objection inaccessible does not answer it. This creates a possible connection between epistemic answerability, nonarbitrary standing and the preservation of differentiated centers.

Its strongest version would have to show that the relevant reciprocal standing is already implicit in the act of claiming universally valid justification. It would then derive obligations for real conditions of inquiry and action, including whose perspectives can be represented, how dependence and coercion alter participation, and when protecting one possibility restricts another. This is a candidate argument for the theory's ethical content, not a decision to reduce it to a preference.

Four steps still require separate arguments:

1. From genuine reason-giving to equal argumentative standing, despite differences in competence, evidence and practical role.
2. From standing within argument to warranted treatment outside the argument.
3. From actual or possible speakers to affected nonparticipants, including those unable to speak and future centers.
4. From justified treatment to a determinate account of wise judgment where reasons conflict, knowledge is limited and action is costly.

These are distinct from deriving a motivation or material capacity to act on a reason. An agent may acknowledge a justified demand and still fail to act. The generative account must explain that failure without making the justification disappear. Likewise, an epistemically excellent institution may engage in exploitation; that is a counterexample to an automatic truth-to-goodness inference, not to the possibility of a further normative derivation.

The Apel–Habermas disagreement below is especially useful because it directly contests whether argument's presuppositions carry the required moral force. Neither author supplies a completed derivation of all our ethical content. Their disagreement identifies a productive proof obligation rather than grounds for abandoning it.

## 6. Five primary texts and their precise contribution

### Kant: the architecture of necessary conditions, with an empirical remainder

**Source:** *Critique of Pure Reason*, second-edition Transcendental Deduction, §§16 and 26–27; Norman Kemp Smith translation, [CUHK HTML transcription](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/humftp/E-text/Kant/cpr.kemp-smith/08td-b.htm). Inspected §16 on unity of apperception and §§26–27 on categories and possible experience, especially the final part of §26, translation pp. 172–173. English transcription; no German edition comparison in this round.

Kant argues that conditions necessary for possible experience have objective validity for its objects. This is a model of the strong argumentative architecture sought here: necessity follows from an account of what makes the capacity possible. Yet the specific categories and forms of intuition belong to his system; resemblance does not transfer their justification to ours. He expressly distinguishes general conditions of experience from particular natural laws, whose specific content requires experience. The passage therefore supports attempting a root-level deduction while marking the burden of deriving contingent content. It does not authorize inferring a complete independent ontology from whatever a particular investigator happens to find unavoidable.

### Peirce: generative inference without a privately certain starting intuition

**Source:** “Some Consequences of Four Incapacities,” *Journal of Speculative Philosophy* 2 (1868), 140–157; [Arisbe transcription](https://cspeirce.com/menu/library/bycsp/conseq/cn-main.htm). Inspected CP 5.264–5.270 and 5.310–5.316, with the intervening example of incomplete inference at 5.308. Ransdell/Kariger public-domain transcription, carrying CP and *Writings* locations; no original journal facsimile comparison.

Peirce treats cognition as mediated, inferential development and makes a community capable of continued increase central to his conception of reality. He rejects private certainty as the final philosophical tribunal. This offers a strong generative alternative to a root understood as one self-evident intuition. But his four starting denials are explicitly presented as uncertain hypotheses whose consequences are to be tested. He also notes that an apparently abbreviated valid inference may depend on a true implicit premise. The useful connection is disciplined continuation with explicit dependencies and communal criticism; his text does not certify a finite premise set that generates every future truth, and his ideal-community claims require their own defense.

### Tarski: semantic validity does not supply empirical warrant

**Source:** “The Semantic Conception of Truth and the Foundations of Semantics,” *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 4 (1944), 341–376; [HTML transcription](https://jfsowa.com/logic/tarski.htm), with [CMU-hosted PDF](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/kk3n/epistclass/tarski.pdf) as an alternative access link. Inspected HTML §§4, 8–12, 14 and 18–19. The HTML was accessible earlier in this research round; later web-tool opens failed. The alternative PDF is a locator, not an additional inspected edition.

Tarski's semantic construction separates an object language from a suitable metalanguage and defines truth through satisfaction under stated formal conditions. His paradox diagnosis concerns semantic closure together with the relevant ordinary logical principles; it is not a prohibition of every form of self-reference. More decisively for this task, he distinguishes defining truth from supplying criteria for warranted assertion and declines to make the semantic conception decide substantive metaphysical disputes. Thus semantic soundness is a real part of valid generativity, but it cannot silently certify empirical starting premises. The whole-root programme needs a justified evidence interface in addition to rules preserving truth.

### Apel: the strongest reflective-foundation proposal in this source set

**Source:** “Regarding the Relationship of Morality, Law and Democracy: On Habermas's ‘Philosophy of Law’ (1992) from a Transcendental-Pragmatic Point of View,” *Invenio* 8(15), 2005, 11–24; [journal PDF via Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/877/87781502.pdf). Inspected §§II–III, printed pp. 13–17, and the part-A/part-B discussion, pp. 18–19. Direct PDF text extraction; web-reader retrieval failed but direct PDF retrieval succeeded. This is Apel's own argument and his interpretation of Habermas, not a neutral record of their agreement.

Apel argues that unavoidable presuppositions cannot be refuted without being exercised in the refutation. He grounds their necessity reflectively, rather than deducing a foundation from an earlier foundation. He includes equal argumentative rights and co-responsibility among these commitments. This is a serious precedent for pursuing strong necessity. Nevertheless, he distinguishes procedural grounding from substantive moral norms reached through practical discourse, and introduces a further treatment of responsibility under nonideal institutional conditions. Even this ambitious foundational approach does not directly derive every concrete ethical conclusion or social arrangement from one undifferentiated principle.

### Habermas: unavoidable practice versus corrigible reconstruction

**Source:** *Justification and Application: Remarks on Discourse Ethics*, trans. Ciaran Cronin, MIT Press, 1993; [accessible scan](https://teddykw2.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/07/jc3bcrgen-habermas-justification-and-application-remarks-on-discourse-ethics.pdf). Inspected author's text pp. 31–33 and 54–56, plus his interview answers pp. 162–164. The scan is the 2001 third printing of the 1993 edition. Direct extraction; printed pp. 31–32 also visually checked against page images. The translator's introduction is not used as Habermas's assertion.

Habermas distinguishes constitutive presuppositions of argument from practical obligations. His moral derivation also requires specifying what justification of a moral norm means; rationality presuppositions alone do not carry the whole result. He further distinguishes unavoidable practical know-how from philosophical descriptions of it, which may be wrong or inadequate. Performative contradiction can expose particular failures without directly deciding which complete ethical theory is best. This is the central challenge to identifying our entire formulation with inquiry's unavoidable root: necessity of the underlying practice does not automatically certify one reconstruction. It calls for testing rival reconstructions and independently defending every added commitment.

## 7. When would a conflicting claim fail as science?

The strong conjecture is most precise when the object assessed is specified. These are our distinctions for applying it, not a historical consensus definition of science.

| Case | What follows |
|---|---|
| A provisional empirical conjecture proves false | Its asserted content fails; proposing and testing it can remain valid scientific inquiry |
| A conclusion denies a proved necessary condition while retaining that proof's meanings and premises | The combined position is invalid at the demonstrated scope |
| A critic rejects one of our premises, interpretation or inference rules and supplies an alternative | There is an unresolved argument to adjudicate; incompatibility alone does not disqualify the critic |
| A procedure claims empirical warrant while insulating its conclusion from every relevant possible correction | Its claimed warrant fails; a fuller demarcation of a programme requires examining what it actually does |
| An institution studies the world accurately while acting unjustly | Its ethical justification may fail even where particular scientific results hold |
| Our proposed necessary root has a coherent counterexample realization of the claimed capacity | The alleged necessity must be revised; the counterexample cannot be excluded solely for disagreeing with us |

If the whole-theory derivation succeeds, a substantive contradiction of its necessary commitments would indeed invalidate a claimed adequate scientific philosophy. That is the strong destination. Present disagreement with an unproved part of our theory is not yet evidence that another philosophy lacks scientific standing. Nor would one invalid claim automatically erase all scientific work of a person or institution.

## 10. Necessity across representations without trivial containment

6 October 2026. The named connection returns to the whole root: **independently necessary conditions of inquiry → faithful interpretation of the finite foundation → its entire sound development**. This supplements, rather than replaces, the [bounded evaluative integration](HUIGEN-FULL-THEORY-AUDIT.md#applicability-transfer-and-costly-choice). The immediate formation study need not wait for every philosophical issue below, but a claim that its general principles are necessary for scientific philosophy must address them.

### A stronger positive result, with the right quantifiers

The [new hand argument](HUIGEN-GENERATIVE-CLOSURE.md#9-one-coherent-interpretation-can-cover-the-unbounded-closure) shows why a finite root is a substantive advantage. If its conjunction has one faithful interpretation in an independently admitted inquiry realization, and the rules preserve truth throughout that interpretation, every finite descendant inherits validity there. Different realizations can require different encodings. There is no requirement that all scientific descriptions literally contain the same state variables, notation or proof syntax.

One interpretation must nevertheless cover the joint commitment within each realization. Separate favorable translations of the root's individual claims need not fit together. The converse qualification is equally important: if *every claim in the complete conjunction-closed theory* has some interpretation, a finite root and globally sound candidate interpretations already supply a common witness through the conjunction of its premises. It would be wrong to use a two-row mismatch as a refutation of that stronger premise. An infinite independently generated root can fail the analogous inference without an appropriate compactness condition.

These results reduce an unbounded downstream task to a joint root-and-rules obligation. They do not prove that our chosen root passes it. They also distinguish necessary representability from the truth of already intended claims: a mathematical dictionary may fit a structure without identifying the actual patient, consequence, reason or causal relation under discussion.

### What makes an interpretation admissible

The restrictions should follow from the independently specified questions, not from a requirement that the translated theory turn out true. They are proposed assessment conditions for this programme, not a completed universal theory of scientific meaning.

| Requirement | Its independent purpose and boundary |
|---|---|
| Preserve designated reference | Keep the same target, actor, patient and question throughout the relevant comparison. Changing “this patient” to whichever subject benefits changes the claim. This fixes what is at issue without stipulating its answer. |
| Preserve the claimed inferential content | Interpret connectives, quantifier domains and the actual inference fragment coherently. A translation must retain a rule's required premises; preserving a few favorable conclusions is insufficient. Different target logics remain possible when the claimed fragment is sound under the stated semantics. |
| Preserve dependencies and their status | Keep empirical calibration, supplied recipes, contingent laws and ethical grounds visible. Translating a conditional into an unconditional assertion supplies an extra commitment rather than simplifying notation. |
| Preserve relevant distinctions and comparisons | A mapping answering intervention, history, cost, agency or permission questions must preserve the distinctions those questions require. Complete microstate isomorphism is unnecessary when the advertised task does not require it. Equal passive observations alone do not guarantee equal intervention answers. |
| Use a coherent interpretation across the joint claim | A changed representation may use a changed map, but both must recover the same designated content. Revising an interpretation after evidence is legitimate; treating incompatible interpretations as one simultaneously justified account is not. |
| Expose imported information and realization | An externally supplied truth table, calibration or dictionary remains an input. A claim about actual acquired understanding additionally needs a realized access and use history. Pure mathematical representation does not itself require an executable or affordable decoder. |

For example, if an interpretation maps every source statement to a true sentence, bare truth preservation becomes vacuous. Preserving the intended distinctions and the possibility of false answers blocks that maneuver. A weaker, task-specific description can still be legitimate: it should declare what it preserves and avoid claiming conclusions requiring discarded distinctions. Likewise, preserving the questions asked about “reason” and “preference” does not prejudge whether a particular reason exists. This does not prohibit a defended reduction or an independently established identity; it prohibits obtaining that reduction merely by replacing the term in the translation. The same applies to moral authority and operational permission. The substantive disagreement remains available for adjudication.

These constraints also prevent a claim of universal containment from obtaining its own success by changing meanings. A substantive root must constrain which interpreted situations are possible, which implications follow, or which capacities can be realized. Encoding arbitrary histories in a transition graph does not yet explain those histories. The [current premise ledger](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/FOUNDATION-PREMISES.md) and [model-admissibility assessment](MATHEMATICS-MODEL-ADMISSIBILITY.md) already separate representation, law restrictions and intended realization; this round makes their role in whole-theory necessity explicit.

### A decisive test instead of endless translations

For a proposed root commitment, first specify the independently recognized inquiry capacity and the content a faithful representation must preserve. Then seek an actual realization, or a model independently admitted by the laws or modal constraints of the claimed necessity class, for which every independently admissible interpretation violates the commitment. Such a case would defeat its alleged necessity at that scope. A merely consistent model can refute entailment from stated premises without thereby establishing a physical or metaphysical counterpossibility. A missing translation is not automatically evidence that no translation exists; a mere coding failure is not a counterexample to the capacity.

If several admissible interpretations agree on observations but disagree on the substantive commitment, the identification is unresolved. One favorable map is not enough to establish the intended claim. It can establish a weaker representation result if that is what was actually proved. Conversely, rejection of our notation is not a counterexample when another account preserves the same necessary relation. Neither literal vocabulary agreement nor bare empirical fit settles the dispute.

The polarity example in the companion gives an operational test: a suitable decoder exists for each sensor calibration, while an agent lacking calibration cannot recover the target across both possibilities. Supplying that decoder externally does not explain its acquisition. This is directly relevant to the recipient-evaluation construction: acquired causal sensitivity, a justified interpretation of the affected subject, and a warranted ground for action must fit one continuing account. The root's universal claim succeeds only if their substantive dependencies can be retained together, rather than made separately plausible by changing the interpretation between steps.

Their many-sorted first-order criterion allows explicitly defined products, coproducts, subsorts and quotient sorts. Morita extensions preserve old consequences, permit expansion uniquely up to isomorphism, and support appropriately coded formula translation; sentences have old-language equivalents. Morita equivalence therefore permits more changes of presentation than ordinary definitional equivalence. It implies equivalence of their model categories, whose arrows are elementary embeddings, but the converse fails in their counterexample. This does not condemn every categorical criterion. Our use is to allow substantive preservation despite changes of vocabulary and sorts, while demanding an explicit comparison. We do not require all science to be Morita-equivalent to the proposed root: interpreting a common necessary core is weaker. The empirical-equivalence distinctions above are ours, not a result attributed to this paper.
