## 2. Exact application to our completion argument

The following is our explicit application of the standard machinery, not an additional attribution to those authors. Fix the ambient history carrier before using the criterion.

For infinite words \(K\subseteq\Sigma^{\mathbb N}\), define the cylinder \([u]\) of a finite prefix \(u\). Then

\[
\alpha\notin K\ \Longrightarrow\
\exists u\preceq\alpha\;([u]\cap K=\varnothing)
\]

is exactly closedness in the prefix topology: each excluded point has a basic open neighborhood disjoint from \(K\). Equivalently,

\[
K=\{\alpha:\text{every finite prefix of }\alpha
\text{ prefixes some member of }K\}.
\]

For arbitrary \(K\), that right side is its closure. No finite-alphabet or compactness hypothesis is needed for this equivalence. If only some executions are possible, use cylinders intersected with that carrier and the resulting subspace topology. One must not silently replace physically constrained continuations by all words.

There are two distinct questions here. A finite history can be admissible as a partial history without already satisfying a requirement for completed success. Also, a prefix-closed language can have finite dead ends and no infinite boundary. Ideal completion retains these finite objects; an infinite-word safety property contains only infinite points. Neither construction licenses dropping this distinction.

Most importantly, the equivalence does not establish its own application. Independently specified local transition prohibitions can warrant a subject consisting of all coherent histories that respect those prohibitions. If that is the complete exclusion rule, a limit cannot introduce a new violation absent from every finite approximation. If the target also requires a global outcome, closing its finite restrictions may erase part of the target. Calling the broader class admissible does not settle that dispute.

## 3. Domain-theoretic form and its exact source

**Samson Abramsky and Achim Jung, “Domain Theory,”** [author-hosted corrected handbook chapter](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/samson.abramsky/handbook.pdf). Directly inspected §2.3.1, Definition 2.3.1, printed/PDF p. 29: Scott-closed subsets of a dcpo are lower and closed under directed suprema. The principal compact basis and ideal-completion results used here were already inspected at §2.2.6, Definition 2.2.21 and Propositions 2.2.22–2.2.25, pp. 26–28; see the preceding [source review](MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSIBILITY-SOURCES.md). The chapter supplies these standard ingredients. The correspondence below is our explicit corollary, not a separately numbered theorem attributed to the chapter.

Let \(D=\operatorname{Idl}(P)\) use nonempty directed lower ideals, and let \(C\subseteq D\). With

\[
Q=\{p\in P:\downarrow p\in C\},
\]

Scott-closedness of \(C\) gives

\[
C=\{I\in D:I\subseteq Q\}=\operatorname{Idl}(Q).
\]

The reason is precise. Lower closure makes \(Q\) lower and sends every principal approximation of an admitted ideal into \(C\). Conversely, all principal ideals of \(I\subseteq Q\) lie in \(C\); they form a directed family whose union is \(I\), so directed closure admits \(I\). For any lower \(Q\), the displayed class is Scott-closed. Empty \(Q\) gives empty \(C\) under the stated nonempty-ideal convention.

Thus every excluded ideal contains a particular \(p\notin Q\) that no admitted ideal can contain. Conversely, requiring such an incompatible finite witness for every excluded ideal forces both lower closure and closure under directed unions. Merely saying that every admitted ideal has permitted finite approximations establishes only one direction. The prohibition of an additional limit-only exclusion is substantive.

This characterizes a class **inside a supplied full ideal space**. It does not derive the existence of that background space from finite performances alone. The ambient logic, set formation, approximation order and the warrant that these are the complete exclusion conditions remain accountable premises. The result nevertheless has positive force: once that role is justified, its completed admission class is determined rather than separately selected.

## 4. What the criterion cannot silently absorb

These counterexamples are mathematical applications, not new empirical evidence.

- **Termination.** In the unary finite/infinite word domain, admitting every finite word while excluding the infinite word is lower but not directed-closed. Every finite approximation of the excluded word is itself admitted. Completing this class removes the termination restriction.
- **Recurrence.** Among infinite binary words, infinitely many occurrences of 1 is dense: every finite prefix has a satisfying continuation. Yet the all-zero word fails it. Its closure admits every infinite word, so safety closure alone loses the recurrence requirement.
- **Effective realization.** Every finite binary word has a computable extension. Restricting infinite words to the computable ones therefore cannot be expressed solely by forbidden finite prefixes. The full completion contains more objects than an effective generator can enumerate.
- **Effective recognition.** A finite witness exists semantically without necessarily being decidable, computably enumerable, observable, or findable within a bounded time. Even effective detection of violations gives no automatic finite certificate that an infinite run will never violate the condition.

The previous hidden-counter example remains relevant: feasible projected prefixes may rely on different initial finite budgets, leaving no single infinite concrete run. Restoring the actual counter choice to the representation can restore the finite transition constraint. This is a representation diagnosis; it does not establish indefinite physical realization. The [prior lifting discussion](MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSIBILITY-SOURCES.md#compatible-action-prefixes-and-a-coherent-history) retains its explicit finite-initial-state and finite-branching conditions.

“Safety” here classifies an execution property. It supplies no ethical standard and does not mean a completed system is harmless, fair, wise or successful.

## 5. A substantive structuralist rival

**Geoffrey Hellman, *Mathematics and Its Logics: Philosophical Essays* (Cambridge University Press, 2021), Introduction, printed p. 3.** Directly inspected in the [publisher's book preview distributed by Pageplace](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781316999608_A45557950/preview-9781316999608_A45557950.pdf), PDF page 13. The preview includes title/copyright pages and the Introduction. The [Cambridge-hosted excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811087/14006/excerpt/9781108714006_excerpt.pdf) was located but its direct retrieval timed out; it is not the basis of the inspection claim.

Hellman treats mathematical axioms as conditions on structural types and mathematical statements as necessary conditionals about suitable logically possible structures. He separately requires the possibility of a structure satisfying the conditions, preventing vacuity. He stresses that such existence postulates do not follow from word meanings alone. These are his own formulations of modal structuralism, not a secondary reconstruction. This audit does not claim to have inspected his whole book or established the adequacy of his complete program.

Our proposed comparator applies that distinction to the present result. It accepts the same approximation order, permitted finite contents, ideal-completion proof, and universal property in an adequate structural setting. It interprets the theorem as necessary structural relations, conditional on the appropriate domain's possibility, while treating the warrant for that possibility as a separate modal/foundational question. The theorem requires no change under this interpretation. This application is ours; Hellman does not discuss this repository's root or assert this particular comparison.

Such an account can also accept that actual mathematical inquiry, concept formation and proof are activities with generative conditions. Its disagreement need not be that activity is unreal or that relational laws come from an external meaning-giver. It can instead deny that explaining those activities establishes the unique ultimate ground of every mathematical necessity. Calling it a static ontology that cannot accommodate activity would therefore miss the rival.

The outstanding burden is substantive: show that the rival's modal or structural warrant depends on the proposed root, or demonstrate that it fails to explain an independently specified feature of the same target. Neither an unconstrained grounding predicate nor the existence of an isomorphic completion establishes that result. Conversely, the availability of this articulated rival is not itself a proof that the generative interpretation is false. The finite-refutation theorem can advance the latter's positive explanation while leaving unique ultimate constitution open.
