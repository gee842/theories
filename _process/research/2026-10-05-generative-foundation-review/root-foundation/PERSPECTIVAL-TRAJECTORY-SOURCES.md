# Coformed evaluation and trajectory direction: discriminating sources

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored primary-source assessment supporting [the situated-good investigation](PERSPECTIVAL-GOOD-AND-TRAJECTORIES.md). **Status: source assessment and a proposed discriminating test; no executed experiment, new proof, or established universal ethical derivation.** This is a supporting contribution to **whole realization → warranted ethical quality → practical reasons**, governed by the [nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md).

The three sources below have different evidential roles: an original mathematical model, an original controlled experiment, and an original conceptual proposal. They are neither three replications nor independent confirmations of the generative foundation. They constrain what a faithful account of formed perspectives and their effects must explain.

## 1. Reciprocal feedback supplies mechanisms, not a fixed direction

**Andrew R. Tilman, Joshua B. Plotkin and Erol Akçay (2020), “Evolutionary games with environmental feedbacks,” Nature Communications 11, 915.** [Version of record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-14531-6); [institution-hosted article PDF](https://www.zoology.ubc.ca/let/pdfs/Tilman%20et%20al%202020.pdf).

**Locators:** Results, “Eco-evolutionary games,” equations (1)–(4); “Dynamical regimes in eco-evolutionary games,” Figures 2–4; “Case study 3: common-pool resource harvesting,” equation (18).

The model couples strategy frequencies to conditions that those strategies themselves change. Payoffs, impact functions and update rules are supplied. Different parameters support coexistence, single-strategy dominance, bistability or cycles. In the basic common-pool example, under the stated opposite endpoint-profit conditions, the authors obtain stable coexistence rather than cycles. Frequency-dependent harvesting efficiency can change the dynamics.

**Use here:** reciprocal constitution of the modeled dynamics does not determine an ethical direction or guarantee an attraction basin of the imagined kind. Resource renewal, strategic success and stability need separate correspondence assessments. An observed cycle must be explained by an identified law; feedback rhetoric is insufficient.

**Limit:** this is mathematical modeling, not experimental evidence of preference formation. A strategy's assigned payoff is not the origin of its experienced or endorsed good. We report the authors' published results; no Lean migration or independent checking of their proofs was performed here.

## 2. Institution-dependent conduct need not demonstrate lasting value change

**Armin Falk and Nora Szech (2013), “Morals and Markets,” Science 340, 707–711.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1231566); [author-institution article PDF](https://polit.econ.kit.edu/downloads/papers/MoralsMarkets_Science.pdf).

**Locators:** pp. 707–708, “The Mouse Paradigm” and Figure 1; p. 708, discussion of responsibility and pivotality; p. 709, Figure 2 discussion.

Random assignment to individual decision, bilateral bargaining or multilateral trading produced different willingness to accept a mouse's death for money. At €10 or less, reported proportions were 45.9%, 72.2% and 75.9%, respectively; the market fractions use sellers' lowest accepted transaction prices. Participants received information about the consequences. The two market rates were not significantly different. Declining multilateral prices are interpreted as suggestive of social learning and norm formation.

**Use here:** information about an affected life does not alone explain practical weighting. Interaction structure can change conduct.

**Limit:** the authors explicitly discuss diminished pivotality allowing conduct to change while values remain unchanged. Their result therefore does not, by itself, establish enduring preference revision, ignorance-free endorsement of harm, or broad concern being eliminated by evolutionary selection. Different opportunity structures and elicitation methods matter. The experiment does not justify its own ethical appraisal merely by measuring exchange prices.

## 3. A relational account of sense-making retains differentiated participants

**Hanne De Jaegher and Ezequiel Di Paolo (2007), “Participatory sense-making: An enactive approach to social cognition,” Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences 6, 485–507.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-007-9076-9); [author-hosted article PDF](https://hannedejaegher.net/wp-content/uploads/2009/11/dejaegherdipaolo07participatorysensemaking.pdf).

**Locators:** pp. 492–495, account and definition of social interaction; pp. 496–497, “Sense-makers interacting” and Figure 2; pp. 499–501, joint meaning formation.

The authors propose that coordination changes participants' sense-making and can generate meanings unavailable to either alone. Their interaction-level organization does not erase the participants' autonomy. They also distinguish the continuation of an interaction from fulfillment of its participants' intentions, and note that highly coordinated conflict need not have positive affect.

**Use here:** a field of significance can be investigated through enacted relations and their history, rather than represented exclusively as fixed weights inside a preconstituted individual. Differentiated participation remains explanatorily important.

**Limit:** this is a conceptual proposal drawing on other work, not a fresh experimental demonstration that every value is socially formed. Its operative definition of social interaction cannot itself justify protecting autonomy in every ethical conflict. It supplies neither a Gaussian field law nor universal moral warrant.

## What these sources change in the proposed investigation

The working decomposition must preserve one actual history while distinguishing at least four possible changes:

1. **Changed conditions:** the same disposition encounters a different set of consequences or feasible actions.
2. **Changed understanding or significance:** the disposition registers different consequences, possibilities or meanings.
3. **Changed endorsement:** the same understood alternatives are evaluated differently.
4. **Changed influence:** dispositions remain, but the activity gives some of them greater control over future conditions and judgments.

These are research distinctions within one unfolding, not independent ontological ingredients. For example, arranging who gets heard can simultaneously change understanding, opportunities and later endorsement. A representation that measures only expressed preference cannot determine which relation changed.

**Candidate explanatory hypothesis, not a source-established result:** broad initial concern can lose practical influence if its exercise reduces the relative success through which a practice allocates subsequent authority, replication or attention. The required connection is specific: identify the practice that translates relative success into future influence, identify whether concern actually affects that success, and trace where the resulting burden goes. Broad concern may instead persist, alter that practice, or be protected by other relations. It is also possible for concern to stay unchanged while its practical effect declines. None of the three papers demonstrates the full hypothesis.

## One concrete distinguishable test

Use a repeated, low-stakes common-resource task with informed participants and an additional beneficiary whose bonus depends on the resulting shared resource. The task is a prospective design, not conducted research. The participation roles and resource relation are produced by the task's arrangements; they are not proposed as the theory's fundamental kinds.

Before interaction, elicit forecasts, attention to consequences, and choices in a common set of alternatives. Balance initial dispositions across conditions. Independently vary **how later decision influence is allocated**: one condition makes it depend on prior relative extraction success; a comparison fixes influence independently of that success. Keep the resource law, consequence information and available actions matched. The influence protocol itself may change experienced legitimacy and responsibility; measure these rather than pretending that the treatment changes only a scalar coefficient.

During the interaction, record realized resource and beneficiary outcomes, forecasts, expressed evaluations, allocations of influence and actual choices. Afterwards, repeat the original common decision task with equal authority and the same alternatives. A follow-up outside the interaction distinguishes transient conduct from retained change.

| Observation | What it discriminates |
|---|---|
| Lower beneficiary provision while later common-task evaluation is retained | Changed opportunities or allocation of influence may explain the conduct; enduring endorsement change has not been identified. |
| Retained evaluation change after common conditions are restored | Supports disposition change; distinguish belief revision, responsibility judgments and endorsement before attributing a new value. |
| Outcome tracks the influence protocol despite matched initial concern | Tests whether the selection of whose evaluation matters adds explanatory power beyond initial field width. |
| Accurate consequence forecasts coexist with adverse outcomes | Challenges an ignorance-only account; does not establish that participants deny all moral reasons. |

The design would weaken the proposed influence mechanism if practical weighting and trajectories remained insensitive to the protocol over its prespecified range, or if the apparent effect disappeared after correcting a forecast or opportunity mismatch. A null result would constrain that mechanism and scope, not refute every form of coformation. A successful synthetic implementation would check model consequences only; claims about actual human formation require actual observations.

## Contribution to universal normative grounding

The precise supporting premise is that **the formation, breadth and social success of an evaluation cannot serve interchangeably as evidence of its warrant**. Establishing this distinction prevents the proposed universal inference from quietly treating selected endorsement as justified whole appraisal. It also directs the philosophical comparison toward a fully informed, effectively organized rival rather than assuming that destructive conduct is always a cognitive error.

The affirmative ethical argument still needs to identify what in the actual continuation grounds its judgment and why this provides practical reasons across differently formed intelligences. If a practice discounts burdens because those undergoing them cannot affect selection, that explains the exclusion; it does not yet vindicate it or refute it. Conversely, a universal reason need not predict universal compliance. These are unresolved warrant questions about the same continuation studied by the mechanisms above, not evidence for a separate causal/value realm.

No source licenses a general claim that wider concern is better, greater coordination is better, favorable feedback is inevitable, or generative dynamics guarantee ethical convergence. The study should preserve constructive bounded care, harmful broad control, persistent justified dissent and successful domination as differentiated possibilities requiring actual assessment.
