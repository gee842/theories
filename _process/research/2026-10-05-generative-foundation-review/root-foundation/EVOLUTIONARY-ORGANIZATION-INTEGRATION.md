## The explanatory connection completed here

The starting problem is how a new recurring organization arises within activity that already has some reproduction and transmitted variation, and how the resulting organization changes later evolution. The explanation must not require collective selection to produce the first collective, copy a completed group into every generation by stipulation, or infer inheritance from persistence of a structure alone.

The account has five connected steps. An inherited change first alters an operative relation such as daughter separation. Actual development forms new associations and consequences. Reproduction then carries a capacity for rebuilding those relations into successors. Subsequent development reconstructs organization with actual material and activity support. That organization changes the production, association and retention of further variants, which can in turn revise its developmental or reproductive relations. Collective evolutionary properties can therefore be outcomes of earlier processes. They are not all unexplained initial labels.

The new formal step is **actual sampling → carried developmental state → reconstructed bonds across transfers**, with explicit provision and removal accounting. The [previous developmental study](DEVELOPMENTAL-ASSOCIATION-INTEGRATION.md) already checked association formation and reconstruction by a detached descendant within a culture. The [generated recurrence study](GENERATED-SELECTION-RECURRENCE-INTEGRATION.md) already computed reconstruction from carrier pools and recovered conventional recurrences. Neither is reopened or claimed as new. The extension now follows the selected cell and its redevelopment in the same admitted history instead of supplying the next culture's retention independently.

This is a mechanism-based synthesis with a new checked connection, not a uniquely new principle that development can evolve. Existing biology supplies important parts of the account. The first emergence of any chemically reproducing organization remains a distinct origin problem; the chapter identifies that boundary explicitly. There is no claim here to explain every origin from unspecified initial conditions.

Those dependencies can then change when the next item begins and which activities occur together. The explanatory work is to trace those passages through the actual history, including alternative routes of correction and learning. Fewer scheduled training sessions do not by themselves imply less learning; more repeated work does not by itself imply an inability to learn.

The relevant structure is which activity actually informs or enables which later activity, with which participants and records; its dynamics includes changes in those connections, their ordering and the interval before consequences return. A named subsystem is a relatively recurring organization of such relations at the selected scale. The whole denotes their connected unfolding, not an additional cause outside it. This description does not assume that every useful relationship is a simple pairwise edge, that all effects occur in sequence rather than concurrently, or that every episode must complete the same cycle.

### Joining, retention and separation change the same dynamics

| Relational change | What changes in the ongoing organization | What a continuation would have to retain or reconstruct | Failure that must remain possible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joining review, incident knowledge and learning | Errors and interpretations can inform later work; responsibilities may differentiate through repeated collaboration | Access to records, competent interpretation, repeated joint work and recognition of contributions | More connections create delay or overload without useful learning |
| Retaining a practice through turnover | A pattern can outlast particular participants when others acquire and enact its relations | Overlap, training, usable infrastructure, authorization and actual opportunities to practice | Documents persist but the skill or supporting time disappears |
| Separating a release path or forming a branch | Some approval dependencies end while expertise, infrastructure or communication may remain shared | A viable arrangement for the functions previously supported across the cut | Independence in one decision destroys access to maintenance or further revision |
| Rejoining previously separated work | Practices and dependencies are recombined, potentially changing both continuations | Translation, negotiation, shared work and compatible access; not simple file addition | Reconnection restores some capability while disrupting another |

These are candidate mechanisms with declared conditions, not an exhaustive classification or proof that any particular link is necessary. A split need not cut every relation, and continuation need not follow a single parent tree. Differentiation also happens within an organization that retains its name and participants.

### What makes the difference inheritable

Here “inheritable” concerns a causal continuation of an organizing difference through turnover, delegation, branching or other succession. Persistence with unchanged participants is relevant but insufficient to establish that stronger claim. Similarity between two organizations is also insufficient: both might independently respond to the same pressures.

The proposed causal chain is: earlier activity changes a relation; later activity has access to its carriers and supports; new participants or a differentiated continuation enact or reconstruct it; the retained difference affects what they can subsequently do. The carriers can include skilled participation, examples, records, tools, expectations and recognized authority. They are not independent little substances containing the whole organization; they do their carrying only through the relations that make them usable.

For example, a branch may retain both a revised review rule and a mentoring practice. The inherited organization is then more than the text: newcomers learn how to judge, gain access, receive recognized responsibilities and eventually help others participate. Alternatively, the branch may retain the rule while losing the people or time supporting this practice. Its apparent procedural similarity can conceal a shrinking capacity to recreate the organization. These are alternative explanatory histories to investigate, not measured outcomes of the source projects.

An operational inheritance claim should identify the changed relation, the actual passage through succession, its enactment after succession, and its consequences for later activity. Where feasible, variation in access, overlap or support can help distinguish transmission from independent reconstruction. Such contrasts must account for selection into succession and shared conditions; they are not automatically causal interventions. No universal threshold or new necessity theorem is asserted here.

The account therefore permits inheritance of destructive dynamics as well as enabling ones. Repeatedly bypassing maintenance can be learned and reenacted; new personnel can acquire the same dependence on emergency work. Being transmissible does not mean sustaining a rich capacity for further change, and retaining one function can coincide with losing another.

### Comparative direction and completion boundary

The strong comparison has two components: existing system dynamics explains feedback, accumulation and delays; organizational routine theory explains enacted patterns, variation and the limits of copying artifacts. Both must be credited on the same evidence. Ren's correction is not a reason to characterize those theories as necessarily dualist or to compare only against an isolated-parts caricature.

The candidate contribution is to connect **formation of differentiation → reciprocal maintenance and transformation → actual transmission/reconstruction → changed possibilities of further reorganization** in one history. A useful comparison must give rival accounts the same work records, turnover, support, procedures and timing. Ask which account explains the joint history with fewer independently supplied mechanisms, or makes a discriminating prediction about the consequences of losing or retaining a particular relation. A more expansive vocabulary does not establish an advantage.

At this correction's scope, the immediate unresolved inference was whether the activity that an organizational change enables also reproduces, transforms or destroys the supports through which that change can continue. The prior proof hands off a procedure with participation supplied. The continuation below now connects one concrete production of further participation. Wider formation and loss of capabilities, availability and subsystem boundaries remain open; neither this correction nor the new local composition declares the full evolutionary theory superior.

### Primary evidence, strong comparison and counterreview

The [two additional primary readings](../../../../sources/evolutionary-selection/organizational-inheritance/SYSTEMS-READING.md#shared-activity-and-later-participation) motivate the distinction between accumulated individual experience, joint activity and performance after organizational change. Their publisher abstracts support that bounded comparison; they do not document our configuration/admission history. The earlier Valkey/TDF records concern actual organizational reconstitution and rule change, while the 2001 system-dynamics source concerns delayed work/capability feedback. Combining these readings does not turn separate studies into observations of one complete mechanism.

Transactive-memory research is a strong existing explanation of how a history of joint work changes later coordination. Routine theory already addresses enacted variation and reproduction of practice. Systems dynamics already addresses consequences changing later conditions. Our local contribution is a formally connected producer/consumer mechanism inside the repository, not a claim to have first discovered relational learning or organizational inheritance. A conventional transition model with the same task, response, configuration, mandate and event history yields the same predictions. No matched predictive or parsimony advantage has been shown.

**Is competence hidden in a label?** No unrestricted competence predicate is used: the procedure is inferred from a computed response and then executed. But the inference algorithm and the narrow task family are supplied. Calling this a general explanation of human learning would exceed the proof.

**Is recognition reduced to correct execution?** No. A successful retry forms one review relation; the separate inherited governance requirement controls admission. These are distinct relations within one history. The example neither endorses that particular procedure nor requires it in every organization.

**Has the full work/participation feedback now closed?** One previously missing connection is closed: tested activity forms a relation consumed by later activity and further formation. Physical and social availability, task selection, the current testing relation and ongoing support remain supplied. The initial reviewer can leave in the checked history, but this is not a proof of indefinite self-maintenance.

**What would change the correspondence?** If the actual task admits multiple procedures fitting the observed pair, the generalization step needs more evidence or a narrower conclusion. If a recorded mandate does not provide the practical access it represents, the admission-to-response correspondence fails. If another route supplies the response after a departure, the modeled failure is not applicable. Each objection identifies a relation to check rather than freely toggling an unexplained capability flag.

The remaining central connection is how jointly encountered differences revise the task, testing relation or means of participation themselves, including loss and alternative reconstruction. The present result provides an enacted history on which to investigate that question. The full explanatory and comparative goal remains open at its original scope.
