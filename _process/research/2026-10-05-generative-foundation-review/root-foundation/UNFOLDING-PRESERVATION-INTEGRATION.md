# What an adequate explanation must preserve about unfolding

8 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored research responding to Ren's request, “establish what an adequate explanation must preserve about an actual unfolding.” This completes a scoped preservation argument and its Lean checks; it does not declare the universal assumption-free foundation complete.

## 1. The criterion and why it is warranted

**An account of an unfolding must preserve the relevant occurrences, distinctions and relationships through which that unfolding became what it was, together with the supported differences in what could follow. It may change vocabulary, scale or presentation; it must not make the target's successful performance indistinguishable from its relevant failure.**

The obligation is fixed by what the account undertakes to explain. An account of a final answer alone has a narrower task than an account of how that answer was acquired, used, mistaken and corrected. When the latter is the declared target, erasing the acquisition, mistake or correction changes the subject. This is the substantive argument for the question family; calling a formal predicate `ExactAccount` does not supply that argument.

For this investigation, the independently identified target is the inherited episode of formed interpretation. Before choosing any new code or fidelity relation, we ask: which contrasting comparisons were actually acquired; which retained grounds shaped interpretation; what judgment occurred; how error arose with that interpretation unchanged; whether a fresh comparison affected the judgment; and which access, support and prior changes made the next correction possible or unsuccessful. These questions are already present in the original construction and its performances. They are not predicates freely toggled in a weaker substitute model.

Six preservation requirements follow at the scope of those questions:

| Requirement | What must remain recoverable | What may be omitted |
|---|---|---|
| Occurrence and provenance | What actually happened and how the relevant record or organization arose | Unrelated history; no demand to reproduce every detail |
| Operative relationship | How a relevant difference was acquired, retained, used or transformed in producing another difference | The original names, notation and coordinate layout |
| Boundary and compatibility | The actual conditions at which a continuation begins, including support consumed or lost | Coordinates shown irrelevant to the selected continuation questions |
| Consequential distinctions | Differences that change a warranted answer about a relevant continuation | Differences indistinguishable for those questions |
| Failure and alternative routes | Errors, blocked effects, exhausted support, regression and supported substitutes | Imagined interventions with no warranted applicability |
| Scope of the claim | Whether the account establishes occurrence, possibility, all completed outcomes, or reliable continuation | No promotion of one of these into another without an argument |

The list is a defended necessary fidelity criterion, not a theorem that these six headings exhaust explanation. A complete catalogue could preserve answers without explaining their organization. An explanatory account must additionally make its claimed dependencies intelligible and justify their correspondence to the phenomenon. Selection of relevant questions remains open to criticism: a missing question may expose an omitted relationship, while an unmotivated question may demand needless detail.

## 2. One unfolding, differentiated analytical roles

The acquisition, record, interpretation, judged relation, support and correction are positions and transformations within the same represented occurrence. An acquired reading is a partial articulation of it; its limits do not place a mind outside a separate world. A change not retained in that reading can become available through later contact. The mathematical observer used to ask about the whole constructed history is not silently installed as an omniscient participant in the history.

The inherited construction supplies the event law, comparison, access, finite support, candidate interpretation repertoire and review architecture. We retain that construction without weakening its relations or replacing its inputs. This study identifies what a faithful account of it cannot erase. It does not infer the actual laws of every physical unfolding from logic, or claim that supplying a local law places law outside generative reality. The [shared nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md) governs this distinction.

## 3. Native joining, not independently successful fragments

The earlier contextual-composition study fixed list concatenation and a target predicate, then recovered its canonical contextual structure. Here joining is connected to the original [GenerativeCore trace relation](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/GenerativeCore.lean): a first trace and a second trace compose only through the same intermediate state. The core already proves this relation; the new work does not claim to discover it again.

The generic [UnfoldingPreservation module](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/deeper/UnfoldingPreservation.lean) proves how exact accounts inherit it. `SegmentAccount` contains an encoding and answer relation, with no supplied composition operation or homomorphism law. Exact answers about native starting and ending boundaries force:

> The account admits the joined history from a starting boundary to an ending boundary exactly when it admits the two pieces through one shared intermediate boundary.

`exact_segment_join` and `exact_segment_admitted_join` establish this implication. Equal codes in such an account allow substitution inside admitted left and right contexts (`equal_segment_codes_substitute`). A successful first piece in one history and a successful second piece from a different, incompatible boundary do not authorize their composition.

This interface is deliberately stronger than selected-outcome fidelity: it asks about every raw native boundary. Its theorem does not show that every explanation must retain every physical coordinate. `Account`, the separate weaker interface, permits an observation function omitting coordinates and asks only which selected outcomes native continuations can produce. A differing answer to even one of these questions forbids collapsing the corresponding starts (`answer_difference_separates`). The concrete separation witnesses need only their named continuations; the all-finite-word interface is a convenient generalization, not a demand that every actual explanation acquire all conceivable answers.

Formal words can always be concatenated; admitted traces need not exist. The general trace interface permits genuinely absent continuations. In the inherited formed-ground model, however, failed acquisition is an actual no-op event under a total step function. We preserve that different failure convention. The concrete cases establish absence of an effect, not nonexistence of the attempted event.

## 4. Possible outcomes, completed outcomes and real continuation

The new generic module makes three meanings explicit:

- `May`: some completed trace ends with the selected property.
- `Must`: every completed trace ends with it; this is vacuously true when no trace completes.
- `Completes`: at least one trace completes and all completed endpoints have it. This still does not guarantee that every nondeterministic branch can complete.

`may_append`, `must_append` and `completes_append_iff` preserve their different intermediate quantifiers. A stronger supplied requirement that every reached intermediate boundary can complete is sufficient (`all_reached_continue_suffices`). It is not silently inferred from one successful trace.

The checked finite counterexample has a first step reaching either boundary 1 or boundary 2. The second step runs only from 1 to 3. The joined history has a successful completion and every completed endpoint is 3, but the actual first-stage branch to 2 has no continuation. `existential_completion_does_not_complete_every_branch` retains this counterexample. It prevents interpreting existential feasibility as reliable support through every branch. These are standard relational distinctions now connected to the explanatory interface; they are not a new claim of guaranteed generative improvement.

## 5. Same-law witnesses: what compression loses

The [FormedUnfoldingPreservation module](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/deeper/FormedUnfoldingPreservation.lean) uses the unchanged [FormedGrounds law](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/universal-ethics/generative-capacity/FormedGrounds.lean) and one fixed signal convention throughout. Every named concrete boundary is reached from the original initial condition by an explicit finite event word. `native_trace_iff_run` proves that the new relational wrapper has exactly the original executions. These are actual executions of the specified mathematical model, not newly collected observations of a learner.

| Original episode contrast | Established loss if omitted |
|---|---|
| Calibration acquires two contrasting contacts; the formed interpretation issues a judgment; signal disturbance produces error; review corrects it | A final Boolean verdict alone does not describe formation, error and correction |
| The initially issued and later corrected judgments agree, but reapplying `.judge` after correction restores error | Equal present judgments need not license the same future answer; correction did not repair the disturbed stored signal |
| From the mistaken boundary, `.inspectOnly` and `.review` acquire the same native comparison, but only review changes the judgment | Acquiring a record and using a fresh comparison in revision have different consequences |
| `.inspectOnly` consumes the last unit; a subsequent `.review` leaves the mistaken state unchanged | The support used in one continuation cannot be reused by splicing in the success of another |
| Closing access leaves one support unit but prevents the review effect | Available stock alone does not establish effective acquisition |
| Two reachable boundaries have the same complete selected operative reading, but different current compared reference; the same `.review` yields different judgments | The acquired reading alone cannot answer every subsequent acquisition question |
| Two equal-length native histories end at exactly the same full state, but acquire different intermediate records | Even a full endpoint cannot reconstruct every relevant past acquisition |

The review chronology matters. `.review` acquires a **fresh** sample, compares it with the current judgment and corrects that judgment in the same event. It does not consume an earlier saved `.inspectOnly` receipt. The recorded-only and successful-review cases are alternative continuations from the same mistaken boundary. Taking the former first exhausts support and prevents the latter's effect. The study does not invent a successful “record now, use that record later” mechanism.

Four exact-account separation results apply the generic theorem to future judgment, future reading, closure and exhaustion. Two impossibility results rule out an exact future account whose code consists only of the current judgment or current operative reading. Separately, `no_endpoint_decoder_recovers_all_acquired_paths` proves that no function of the full final state alone recovers all the acquired paths: the two equally long histories converge to the same endpoint while their readings differ.

The last result does not imply that every account must store a complete past, or that the native process needs its erased history for future operation. It applies when the explanatory task includes that past acquisition. The same endpoint may suffice for future behavior under the native law while failing to answer a historical question. This distinction is itself something an adequate account should preserve.

## 6. Constitutive participation: what this advances

The positive result is more specific than “everything changes.” In the fixed episode, prior contacts form retained calibration grounds; prior acquisition consumes finite support; prior signal disturbance survives a corrected judgment; and these reached conditions differentiate later native performances. They are not independent background facts merely written next to activity. Their formation and subsequent consequences belong to the same execution, and erasing them yields demonstrably wrong answers to the selected explanation questions.

This earns a conditional necessity: **an account purporting to explain this formation and continuation must preserve the relevant formed relationships, or an equivalent recoverable structure.** The consequence follows from independently specified native differences plus fidelity, without adding a structure-preservation axiom to the account.

It does not derive the comparison or revision law from nothing. The native law establishes participation within this mathematical construction; warranted empirical identification must establish the corresponding participation in an actual investigated case. It also does not show that all prior activity contributes to every subsequent condition. Forgotten details, idle steps, alternative routes and destructive endings remain possible.

## 7. Strong rivals, evidence and primary-source comparison

An atemporal constraint on the whole history may preserve occurrence order, acquired grounds, actual use, consumed support, error, regression and warranted alternative continuations. It passes this criterion. A whole-history table may also pass factual fidelity; a further explanatory argument must distinguish organization from cataloguing. Neither chronological presentation nor the word “generative” is uniquely mandated by the result.

A common cause or prearranged sequence may match an observed chronology without the earlier acquired record contributing to the later correction. A chronology alone therefore does not establish mediation. Supported process evidence, matched variations or other discriminating evidence must decide between those explanations. Nor is simple but-for dependence necessary for participation: redundant routes can preserve an outcome despite removal of one contributing route. Evidence must retain those routes and avoid fabricating unique necessity.

The independent reviewer read two primary comparators. Rubenstein et al., [Causal Consistency of Structural Equation Models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.00819), §4.3 and §5.3, motivate comparing corresponding effects under specified interventions and permit a scoped equilibrium description. Beckers and Halpern, [Abstracting Causal Models](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/4117/3995), Example 3.4 and §§3.2–3.3, expose weaknesses of sparse tests and unconstrained intervention correspondence, while recognizing restricted intervention domains. These support scrutiny of target and correspondence; this Lean package does not implement or independently prove their causal-abstraction theorems.

The [independent review](UNFOLDING-PRESERVATION-REVIEW.md) develops these rivals and audits the exact chronology. No new empirical dataset, experiment, universal causal identification or ethical conclusion is claimed.

## 9. What is established, and the next discriminating test

Established: a defended, abstraction-permitting preservation criterion; derived native joining and substitution for the stated exact interfaces; concrete omissions that fail under the same unchanged law; actual correction failure and regression; and the distinction between mathematical, empirical and explanatory adequacy. An equivalent global account survives. This advances the bridge from independently identified relationships to unavoidable explanatory content.

The next substantive test is a case with warranted observed acquisition, use and altered continuation, evaluated against a matched common-cause or predetermined-sequence rival. It should identify which relationships can be recovered from the evidence and which remain underdetermined, allowing redundancy, loss and legitimate abstraction. For a more general formal interface, concurrent and differently typed boundaries remain outside these finite linear traces. Neither perpetual continuation nor unique vocabulary is added as a foundation-completion condition.
