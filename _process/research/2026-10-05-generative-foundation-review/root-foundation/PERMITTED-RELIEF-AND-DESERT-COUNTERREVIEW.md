# Permitted relief and desert: the strongest extinction rival

6 October 2026. AI-authored counterreview of **actual patient adversity → moral favor for an independent actor's independently permissible relief**, including a patient alleged to deserve the suffering. This continues the [ethical minimum](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md#7-evidence-for-the-goal-and-the-next-connection) and the [preceding primary comparison](PATIENT-GROUND-AND-AGENT-SCOPE-SOURCES.md). No new ethical axiom, empirical finding or formal result is introduced.

**Assessment:** desert supplies a substantive challenge to the inference from a patient's local badness to a moral reason for relief. The strongest rival makes the patient benefit's moral contribution depend on desert, while leaving the burden bad for the patient. This is stronger than saying that deserved suffering has another valuable feature or that refraining from aid is better overall. The inspected primary case establishes neither extinction of the patient consideration nor an actual counterexample satisfying the minimum's permission condition. The extinction rival remains to be justified; it is not defeated merely because relief is permitted or compassion seems intelligible.

**Accountable decomposition:** following the [project-wide nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md), the target is the whole relevant history of wrongdoing, culpability, adverse undergoing and possible remission. Patient-relative badness, moral contribution and practical permission distinguish proposed relations within that unfolding; they are not independently existing realms or stipulated primitive properties. “Independent actor” here means absence of the specified prior role or tie, not metaphysical independence. The decomposition preserves the same bearer, episode, deserved burden, response and permission question so that the rival's actual explanatory difference can be assessed. It leaves the formation and ultimate grounding of those relations unresolved at this local scope.

Freedom to vary C1 or C2 in a weaker formal representation would not alone establish that the reconstructed history permits their separation. The present challenge instead asks whether a substantive desert account explains a different moral contribution while preserving §4's fixed conditions. The remaining gap is an unresolved ethical explanation within the reconstructed account, not a demonstrated irreducibility of experience and significance or a proved failure of the foundation. This clarification changes neither the test conditions nor the verdict.

## 1. The source gives a real relief case, with a decisive scope limit

Shelly Kagan's 2005 Lindley Lecture, [*The Geometry of Desert*](https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/f1117412-56a8-4560-ac33-e52864e5c773/content), printed 1–6, directly addresses assistance. Amos and Boris suffer after a factory explosion. With one dose of painkiller, Boris's fault can affect priority. With enough for both, Kagan distinguishes a moderate who favors helping both from a retributivist who may favor leaving sufficiently vicious Boris unaided. Zero or negative desert contributions are then considered. Crucially, printed 6 specifies that the vertical axis measures only desert's contribution, excluding other intrinsic values. The lecture assumes desert's intrinsic significance to explore its structure; it does not establish that significance or that it exhausts all reasons for relief.

This is a stronger comparator than an arbitrary alternative relation: it concerns the same kind of aid, explicitly removes resource competition, and changes a proposed morally relevant history. But a negative contribution from desert does not establish that the patient benefit supplies no other moral consideration. Nor does the best outcome on a desert measure, by itself, specify what an independent actor is permitted or required to do.

The [round's source comparison](PERMITTED-RELIEF-AND-DESERT-SOURCES.md) preserves the original Kagan and Hurka files and their distinct reading scopes. Its component-retaining Hurka account is a substantive alternative, not an axiom shared by every desert theory.

The preceding McMahan passage has a different limit. It discusses possible nullification of reasons **not to cause** suffering. A reason to allow an existing burden to continue, a permission to impose it, and absence of any reason to relieve it are separate claims. The Kagan case reaches the relief question directly, but still leaves the contribution and permission questions open.

## 2. Reconstruct the strongest rival

The following is this review's reconstruction, not a doctrine attributed in full to Kagan, McMahan or every retributivist.

1. An actual episode is seriously adverse for its bearer. Relief would improve that person's experience. The rival grants this patient-relative judgment.
2. Culpable wrongdoing can make a specified adverse condition deserved. The proposed normative connection concerns responsibility, the wrong and its fitting consequence; it is not simply a dislike of this patient.
3. Within the justified scope of that desert, the patient's welfare improvement contributes no moral favor to relief. The moral contribution of this welfare change depends on its relation to desert. It is not a favorable moral consideration defeated by a second, stronger one.
4. The act of relief can nevertheless remain permissible if there is an independently justified permission not to realize every desert-based good. Such permission does not itself make the act morally better or supply a patient-grounded justification.

Step 3 is the decisive additional thesis. Merely granting steps 1 and 2 leaves open an account on which patient adversity still favors relief while fitting desert opposes it. The rival must defend why desert transforms or disqualifies the benefit's moral contribution instead. Step 4 also requires grounds: a view demanding exact punishment in every case does not supply the independently permitted act sought here.

This rival therefore cannot be dismissed as incoherent merely because it allows relief. Permissible acts can lack a particular proposed reason. Conversely, its intelligible structure is not an independently established ethical counterexample. The burden of explaining the moral effect of desert remains as substantive as our burden of explaining patient-to-actor favor.

The rival need not claim that the wrongdoer becomes a nonpatient, loses consciousness, or has no interests. It can preserve accurate concern about what the person undergoes while denying that improvement of that condition morally favors this response. This is why repeating C1, or diagnosing the opponent as phenomenally blind, does not answer it.

## 3. Four shifts that would evade the dispute

**From a part to a whole.** A culpable act and its fitting consequence might form a morally preferable whole while the suffering retains a bad-making contribution. Alternatively, context might change a component's moral value. These are different explanations even when they rank outcomes alike. Saying that the whole is better does not decide between them. Do not use “holism” indiscriminately for both, or assume that a surviving prudential bad automatically has a surviving moral contribution.

There is also a third possibility: the relieving or forgiving response can have contextual moral significance even if the episode's moral valence changes with desert. Preserving an invariant negative moral atom and eliminating every reason for relief are therefore not exhaustive alternatives. Leo Zaibert's [“Responses to Critics”](https://www.rutgerslawreview.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/06_Zaibert.pdf), printed 1041–42 and 1048, makes this possibility concrete: he accepts desert-dependent moral valence and maintains value in merciful remission. On 1044–45 he also distinguishes the motive that makes an act forgiveness from the correctness of that evaluation. Classification can survive mistake. These passages block a false dichotomy; they do not establish pre-adoption patient favor for every independent actor.

**From bad for the patient to morally bad without qualification.** C1 concerns the patient's undergoing. A rival can grant that adversity while denying its moral force in a deserved case. Our positive account can reject that denial, but must explain why the patient-relative improvement remains a moral consideration. It cannot silently change the meaning of bad midway through the argument.

**From permission to praise.** A legal allowance is not yet moral permission, and moral permission is not patient-directed favor. If relief is independently established as admirable precisely because it benefits this patient, the affirmative account has gained substantive support. Calling the act mercy, generous or supererogatory cannot establish that assessment. A rival may regard it as merely allowed, or praise another feature without conceding this patient-ground. The same caution applies to compassion: fitting sorrow or regret need not by itself justify intervention.

**From liability to complete loss of aid reasons.** A person may lose a particular claim against necessary defensive harm without losing every claim against harm or every moral consideration favoring relief. Likewise, authorization of punishment does not automatically authorize any person, amount, method or later burden. The claimant's responsibility and the actor's authority must be kept distinct.

## 4. A discriminating case must preserve the contested interval

Consider a conceptual case in which the burden is severe, unwanted and genuinely adverse; relief would reliably change that same patient's experience. Grant, for the purpose of assessing the rival, that the particular remaining burden falls within a warranted desert judgment. The actor neither caused the original wrong nor occupies a helping role. Hold return, relationship and helping intention absent.

Now independently establish that the proposed relief is morally permitted. This may require identifying who holds a punitive claim, whether another person's relief usurps that authority, and what any release actually changes. Absence of a judge's role does not establish a third party's permission. If a waiver extinguishes the desert relation itself, the relieved case no longer tests whether **still-deserved** suffering retains favorable moral significance. If waiver only removes a directed prohibition while leaving desert-based value intact, that different account must be defended. “Optional relief of still-deserved suffering” is therefore a joint condition to justify, not a contradiction already shown or a permission obtained from C2.

The following controls locate the dispute:

| Case | What it can establish |
|---|---|
| The patient is blameless and relief is independently permitted. | An affirmative anchor, if warranted. It does not settle the change introduced by deserved suffering. |
| The patient is culpable but this burden exceeds, is outside, or follows completion of the justified desert. | A reason for relief may be supported by both welfare and desert. This does not answer the strongest extinction rival within its own claimed scope. |
| The particular burden remains deserved and relief is independently permitted. | The central test: does actual improvement for this patient still morally favor the act, even if another consideration opposes it? |
| Relief would defeat a justified requirement or violate another person's unwaived claim. | A permission failure; it does not preserve the preceding test. |

No real person's deserved quantity of suffering is certified here. A serious desert account must state its basis, magnitude and satisfaction conditions. It must distinguish a penal burden from overall lifetime welfare, and explain which prior suffering or other responses count toward fulfillment. Kagan's principal horizontal axis concerns overall well-being; it must not be silently treated as a sentencing scale. A bare fact of past culpability cannot do the work of an indefinitely renewed desert balance.

The affirmative account should meet the central case rather than move to excess punishment, unrelated illness, uncertainty about guilt, repentance, deterrence failure or someone else's greater need. Those can justify relief on other grounds. They do not yet establish that the very burden stipulated deserved retains its patient-grounded moral favor.

## 5. What could discriminate the two explanations

An outcome ranking alone is insufficient. A two-contribution account, a contextual account of remission, and an extinction account may sometimes prefer continued suffering, or permit relief. Observed helping, disapproval or a preference questionnaire therefore cannot by itself identify the grounds of the judgment.

The affirmative explanation must defend an actual patient-directed contribution: the response answers this patient's adversity, and this can speak for choosing it even while desert speaks against. This need not be represented as an invariant negative moral atom inside the experience. Compassionate mercy is a promising case only if its patient-directed justification is established, not presupposed by naming the action. An empty act must lack that justification; relief to the actual bearer must explain it. A source that characterizes mercy partly through an existing compassionate motive has not, on that basis alone, shown that this favor preceded the motive.

Nor would just any valuable feature of remission establish the target. A benefit consisting solely in the actor's admirable character would differ from moral favor grounded in what the response does for the patient. A contextual account could reach the latter, but must show that connection. Substituting unspecified virtue value for the intended patient-directed interpretation of `Merit` in the existing formal contract would change the claim rather than prove C2.

The extinction account must explain why the bearer’s improvement no longer has this moral significance. A finite desert-specific loss is not yet the needed answer. It must show either that desert exhausts the moral contribution of welfare here, or that a justified exclusion defeats its otherwise independent contribution. These are alternative substantive theories, not implications of the sign of a desert score.

A successful exclusion could require revising C2 even when permission is retained. A successful patient-ground defense would support C2 in the deserved case without showing that relief is required, desert never matters, or every punishment is wrong. Neither result can be obtained by defining eligible patients as innocent or by assigning the desired verdict to a formal predicate.

## 6. Verdict and next obligation

The rival survives as a substantive account of moral contribution, while two shortcuts fail: negative desert value does not by itself erase independent patient favor, and permissible mercy does not by itself establish that favor. This is the present scope result. It is neither a verified counterexample to the minimum nor a derivation eliminating C2.

The next argument should justify the joint central case and then defend or reject the alleged change in moral contribution. The generative programme still owes realization, patient badness and practical significance; a history of wrongdoing adds another relation to explain, not a license to treat the bearer as outside that programme. No-desert and retributive theories can both remain under consideration without turning either into an assumed foundation.
