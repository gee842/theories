# From first-person significance to reasons for shared action

6 October 2026. AI-authored argument and countermodel review for [Ren's entire-theory necessity objective](../../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-06-huigen-entire-theory-necessity.md). This continues the [full-theory audit](HUIGEN-FULL-THEORY-AUDIT.md), retaining justified concern and wisdom as required parts of the proposed 慧根. It assesses a stronger claim than the necessity of material or informational conditions for inquiry. It does not rewrite the canonical [ethical foundation](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md).

**Finding:** objective recognition of first-person significance can support a concrete requirement on a philosophy claiming to settle a shared decision: it cannot declare an admitted patient's undefeated objection answered merely because the patient cannot communicate or the decision maker has no private stake. This is a genuine constraint on that claim of practical authority. Deriving an other-regarding reason from scientific objectivity alone still requires a further principle about the grounds and addressees of reasons. The strongest positive programme is to justify that principle through the content of first-person normative experience and the authority a practical judgment claims, then test it on nonparticipating patients. The present analysis identifies an exact success criterion and a richer countermodel than an accurate but indifferent machine.

## 1. The target has three distinct relations

Let A be deliberating agents, P affected patients, and X feasible action alternatives. These sets need not coincide: a sentient patient can lack language or practical deliberation. Fix two actions x and y and an actual context c. Keep separate:

1. **Experienced difference:** H(p,x,y,c) says that x produces an independently identified aversive experience for patient p which y avoids, with the other declared consequences held fixed.
2. **Patient-relative significance:** L(p,y,x,c) says that the difference favors y from p's standpoint. This is stronger than an observer finding p's behavior inconvenient. If L is interpreted as a valid evaluative reason, it is also stronger than the causal occurrence of withdrawal or aversion.
3. **Reason addressed to a deliberator:** R(a,p,y,x,c) says that this patient-grounded difference gives deliberator a a pro tanto reason for y over x. Pro tanto standing supplies a reason that may be outweighed; it does not settle an all-things-considered verdict.

Accurately knowing H does not by definition establish L, and accurately knowing L does not by definition establish every R. The strongest argument should nevertheless try to justify these transitions rather than treating their separation as a proof that they are impossible.

The noncommunicating patient matters twice. First, their inability to announce a reason does not entail that their experience lacks significance. Second, a deliberator's inability to know a particular experience can limit a justified decision without proving that no actual reason exists. This audit initially grants reliable identification of the patient's condition, so missing information cannot explain away the hardest normative case. Uncertainty is restored in §8.

## 2. The strongest first-person argument, reconstructed positively

The following reconstruction puts as much work as possible into the experienced ground and as little as possible into organizational usefulness.

**P1 — Real aversive significance.** Some experiences are undergone as bad for their subjects. Consider an aversive episode whose removal leaves no compensating project, commitment, relationship, beneficial warning or other admitted good worse. Its significance is present in what the subject undergoes; recognition by a productive institution does not create it.

**P2 — A local reason anchor.** In an informed, nondefective assessment of one's own such case, that significance provides a reason for prevention or relief. This is a claim of practical validity, not merely a prediction that the subject will withdraw. A person may lack motivation or means, or may act against this reason, without falsifying it.

**P3 — Objective recognition without erasure.** If the relevant condition occurs in another subject, an adequate account must recognize its actual subject-relative character. Changing the observer does not change whose suffering it is or whether the identified episode is aversive. Noncommunication and dispensability cannot change those facts by themselves.

**P4 — Ground-based extension.** If aversive significance itself is the complete ground of the reason, the same ground in another patient remains reason-giving. A restriction to the current deliberator requires a further grounding fact, not simply the assertion that the patient is someone else.

**P5 — Reflexive practical authority.** A judgment offered as settling what is justified for the affected parties must answer the reasons it acknowledges in their actual situations. It cannot use its control of the decision to convert an unanswered reason into an answered one.

Under these premises, a dispensable noncommunicating sufferer can supply a reason for prevention: the reason follows the independently identified condition, and the authority's claim reaches the sufferer's standpoint. No useful contribution, threat, reciprocity, vote or testimony is needed. The argument is considerably stronger than claiming that cooperation normally helps survival.

P4 is the critical grounding claim. P1–P3 do not yet establish that being the deliberator's own experience is irrelevant to the ground of a practical reason. P5 is also a specified authority claim, not a consequence of simply making a correct scientific assertion. A successful whole-root derivation must justify both at their stated scope.

There is also work in P2. An experience's apparent demand is defeasible evidence of a reason; phenomenological intensity alone does not certify every associated judgment. The local anchor should concern the narrowly described aversive loss, with actual defeaters examined. It must not identify every desire, conditioned aversion or persisting preference with valid reason. Otherwise the proposed foundation grants incompatible and sometimes destructive demands authority before examining them.

## 3. Two transports, not just one

The [earlier two-orbit result](HUIGEN-FULL-THEORY-AUDIT.md#42-two-orbit-lemma) shows why simultaneously renaming actor and patient does not transport a self-directed reason to an other-directed one. A further distinction is essential when patients include nonagents.

**Patient-ground transport** extends a warranted ground from a familiar self-case to another subject who instantiates it. It asks whether language, reasoning or reciprocal recognition was actually part of the ground. If the local ground was the experiential badness rather than the ability to formulate a maxim, inability to formulate a maxim is not a difference in that proposed ground. Establishing this matters for babies, nonhuman animals and other noncommunicating patients; the present argument supplies no blanket empirical identification of sentience.

**Deliberator transport** makes the patient-grounded reason relevant to a distinct agent who can affect the patient. Objective recognition of the patient's standpoint does not already perform this operation. A philosophy can represent the proposition that relief is good for p while asserting that it supplies a practical reason only to p, or only to an agent with a specified relationship to p.

Actor-independence alone cannot manufacture an anchor about a patient who lies outside every deliberator's self-case. Conversely, extending the class of subjects with genuine significance does not by itself give every deliberator an addressed reason. A complete standing derivation needs both transports, or one independently justified principle that explicitly does both.

The corresponding [reason-transport investigation](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) isolates this dependency formally. Its positive anchors and authorized transports can force only the nodes they reach. Its reachability characterization is a logical diagnostic; the philosophical burden is establishing why the relevant transports preserve reasons. Adding edges until the desired patients are reached would encode the conclusion.

## 4. A positive result about genuinely shared authority

Consider a procedure that does more than recommend an actor's most advantageous choice. It issues a certificate that a shared choice x has answered every admitted affected standpoint's valid objections in a declared domain D. Let U(p,x,D) mean that a valid objection from p remains unanswered under the procedure's own stated justification standard.

**No unilateral discharge.** If the certificate asserts that no U(p,x,D) remains for p in D, and an identified patient j in D has U(j,x,D), then the certificate is false. Removing j's microphone, productive contribution or opportunity to retaliate cannot repair this contradiction when those changes leave the objection and the justification standard unchanged.

**Proof:** the certificate asserts a universal negative over D; j is a counterexample. Its communicative or strategic power is not a premise of that truth condition. ∎

This elementary result becomes substantively protective when joined to an independently justified local comparison. Suppose y avoids j's admitted harm, all other relevant consequences are identical, and the standard contains no overriding reason for x or for refusing j's claim. Then the procedure has not discharged j's objection by saying that x is harmless to the controller. That assertion answers a different question. If it continues to claim complete shared settlement, it fails its own warrant condition. The available responses are to choose y, supply a relevant defeating reason, revise an erroneous factual or normative premise, or acknowledge that its claimed settlement has not been achieved.

The conclusion does not grant every expressed demand a veto. Validity of the objection and the admitted defeat standard are premises. There can be rights, relationships, competing harms or other justified grounds for overriding an objection; a decision may remain justified despite a loss. Nor does this result prohibit urgent action when complete justification or agreement is unavailable. It requires accurate representation of what the action's defense establishes and what claims remain.

The advance is therefore a **burden on the authority claim**, not a trick that obtains standing from a universal quantifier. Once an authority admits a patient's reason and claims to answer it, incapacity and dispensability cannot silently discharge it. A controller who openly denies any duty of shared justification escapes this particular contradiction but relinquishes this particular claim of authority. Whether their denial is itself invalid remains the substantive root question.

## 5. A countermodel with the declared scientific and reflective capacities

Construct one causal and epistemic structure M with the following features:

- Two competent deliberators a and b and an independently identified sentient patient p who cannot participate in discourse.
- Reliable observation, explicit uncertainty, publicly inspectable prediction, retained evidence, response to counterevidence, funded correction, selector revision and continuing material support.
- An explicit philosophical model of every subject's first-person significance, including p's. It represents and correctly predicts p's aversive episode, acknowledges that relief is good for p, and grants the local standpoint reason L(p,y,x,c), despite p's inability to deliberate. The claim concerns the reason from that patient's standpoint, not an ability to formulate it. The model can explain the provenance and limits of its own normative commitments.
- A feasible harmless action y and harmful action x. All affected coordinates except p's stipulated aversive episode are equal. Neither action changes the inquiry's accuracy, resources, power or continuation. Choosing either requires the same modeled effort.
- A public, revisable assessment architecture capable of evaluating both self-only and other-regarding reason policies. It can recognize evidence against a ground it admits, retain dissent and examine rival normative rules without falsifying their content.

Use this same rich structure with two interpretations of practical reason. In the **indexed interpretation**, every deliberator has genuine reason against their own aversive losses; an independently recognized loss solely to p supplies no reason to a unless some further admitted relationship connects them. This rule applies uniformly to all deliberators. It makes no exception based on names and contains no empirical error about p. In the **shared-ground interpretation**, p's aversive loss also supplies a pro tanto reason to a. The causal history, available reasoning capacities and factual knowledge can remain identical, since a reason's validity does not force a corresponding motive or action.

The indexed interpretation is more than an accurate machine. It is a reflective practical philosophy: it offers a general account of what each agent has reason to do, objectively acknowledges others' indexed reasons, states its normative ground explicitly, entertains criticism and provides universal rules containing an agent parameter. It can give informed recommendations about common causal situations. In the no-cost case, it finds no reason favoring either action for a. Permitting indifference already violates positive other-regarding standing; the model need not manufacture an extra appetite for cruelty.

The philosophy does not thereby have a warranted certificate of justification to p. If it asserts that p's objection is answered, the result in §4 applies. It can instead assert that correct practical advice is intrinsically agent-relative and that no jointly settling practical authority exists in this case. This position may be normatively wrong. The specified scientific, reflexive and explanatory capacities do not by themselves exhibit its contradiction.

A stronger rival can claim that an actor has a prerogative to choose even when another has a genuine objection. That requires an additional normative principle about prerogative, just as shared standing requires its grounding principle. Such a rival cannot count the prerogative as an independently established fact merely by naming it; neither can the present audit rule it out by silently defining all adequate authority as satisfying our preferred standard. Its claim is a precise target for substantive normative criticism.

**What this countermodel proves:** the listed capacities, local reason anchors and objective factual recognition do not deductively determine the shared reason relation. **What it does not prove:** that both normative interpretations are actually true, equally justified, irreducibly conventional, or compatible with every future successful account of normative experience. A further necessary premise could exclude the indexed interpretation. The point is to identify and justify it rather than excluding the model for failing the desired conclusion.

**Scope correction after the practical-congruency investigation, 6 October 2026:** this section was originally titled “A complete-capacity countermodel.” The construction and its premise-relative conclusion remain, but that title overstated what had been supplied. The listed architecture has scientific and reflective capacities; it has not been proved ideally competent about every true practical reason or closed under every sound deliberative extension. A complete, factive oracle of all addressed reasons would already fix the disputed judgment and could not stay unchanged while that judgment varied. Accurate representation of a patient's local reason is a narrower capability.

The [subsequent primary-source analysis](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md) also tests the stronger internal-reasons alternative. Lack of present concern, or a finite history of reflective refusal, does not establish that no sound deliberative route could generate an appropriate motive. The indexed interpretation is therefore a countermodel to the listed weaker premises, not a completed demonstration that an ideally justified scientific philosophy can deny all other-directed reasons. This correction strengthens the original necessity test: the positive theory must justify the stronger capacity, and the rival must preserve it if it claims to refute that theory.

## 6. What does “all things considered” include?

Three formulations have different force:

| Claim | What it requires | Does it already extend standing? |
|---|---|---|
| All relevant reasons for agent a have been assessed | Completeness relative to the justified account of a's reasons | No; the indexed model disputes which patient-grounded reasons are addressed to a. |
| The facts and perspectives of all affected patients have been represented | Descriptive and perspectival completeness at the claimed scope | No; representation can coexist with denying a shared practical authority. |
| The choice is justified to every affected patient, using a standard responsive to their admitted valid claims | An adequate answer for each included standpoint, with legitimate overrides defended | Yes within that admitted scope, but the constituency and normative standard need justification. |

Impartiality can similarly mean unbiased factual treatment, anonymity under renaming, a rule formulated without naming a privileged individual, or equal initial admission of patient-grounded reasons. The first three do not entail the fourth. An agent-indexed rule can be stated and evaluated from an objective point of view.

Nevertheless, the stronger sense is not empty or merely decorative. Someone who claims unqualified shared settlement while applying only the weaker sense can be required to correct that claim. Universal philosophical explanation must accurately explain the existence, content and disagreements of practical standpoints. Its universal explanatory scope does not, without another argument, make its recommendations binding on every standpoint. Whether an entirely adequate philosophy must additionally justify that shared authority is an open part of the whole-root objective, not a definition that has already settled it.

## 7. The most promising grounding route, and its exact test

The strongest route is to examine the **complete ground of the first-person reason**. Compare two candidate explanations:

- G1: the loss is a reason because of its subject-relative aversive character; its patient index identifies where the loss occurs.
- G2: the loss is a reason for a because it has that character **and belongs to a**; the relation to the deliberator is constitutive of its force.

G1 can preserve differentiated subjects without making all experiences one experience. Under a warranted ground-preservation principle, it supports transfer to a different deliberator confronting the same reason-bearing condition. G2 does not. Renaming preserves G2, so it cannot be dismissed as a spelling preference. The philosophical question is whether G2 adds a real ground or an unsupported exemption.

Here is a precise **reason-ground sufficiency test**. Ask a proponent of the local anchor to state a sufficient ground G in terms whose meaning does not already contain universal standing. Require that G explains the reason in the self-case and remains instantiated in the noncommunicating, dispensable-patient case. Then hold G and all admitted defeaters fixed while changing only the deliberator. If the proponent concedes that the ground is still sufficient but removes all reason, their position is inconsistent. If they instead defend the self-relation as part of G, the dispute moves to that proposed ground; the transfer is not proved by the first concession alone.

This test makes progress beyond requesting moral consistency in the abstract. It forces the rival to specify a reason-bearing relational feature and allows the theory to assess whether that feature explains the reason's purported authority. Parsimony and explanatory unity favor G1 if the self-relation does no justified work. They are support for a grounding hypothesis, not a deductive proof that every relational reason is spurious. Legitimate special responsibilities are possible, and standing need not mean identical weight.

A more ambitious reflective argument would show that a deliberator cannot claim even their own practical reason to be valid, rather than merely felt, unless its authority follows an endorsable ground accessible beyond their current point of view. This still needs a proof that **accessible and objectively true** means **reason-addressing to other actors**. The indexed rival agrees that everyone can correctly recognize its indexed reasons. Its denial does not automatically cancel its own capacity to act, criticize or seek truth. The proposed performative contradiction must concern a specific claim of practical authority, not the mere act of offering an argument.

## 8. Noncommunication, evidence and the formation problem

Once the ground and transports are justified, noncommunication supplies no independent cancellation rule. Actual standing depends on the patient and the relevant ground; warranted recognition depends on an evidence route. Representation can carry another's claim without making the representative its owner, and a patient need not articulate an objection for its grounds to exist.

The theory must then join four steps: identify the patient and condition with appropriate uncertainty; establish the reason under the justified grounding rule; implement an action-sensitive representation; and provide the means to act and maintain correction. These correspond to the distinct obligations in the [premise ledger](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/FOUNDATION-PREMISES.md) and the [recorded-reason construction](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/RECORDED-REASONS.md).

No perfectly accurate detector of suffering is supplied here. Uncertain sentience, masking, mistaken proxies and infeasible protections require separate empirical and practical work. A policy for uncertainty is itself a normative commitment to assess. Nor does showing a valid reason generate its motivation. An indifferent actor can grasp the reason and ignore it; a protective institution can act for mixed motives. The generative programme must explain how recognition, motivation, authority and feasible response can form together without confusing them.

## 9. Primary comparators and the status of the positive warrant

Christine Korsgaard's *Fellow Creatures* (2018), §§8.4.1–8.5.5, supplies a useful positive comparator. She connects rational end-setting to goods that can make claims on others, then distinguishes the capacity to legislate from the status of a creature whose good matters. Her extension to nonrational sentient animals depends on examining the ground of our own value, rather than requiring their participation in argument. This directly addresses the patient-extension problem. The prior move from one's own good to a shared good remains a substantive Kantian claim, not a consequence of accurate prediction. Our reconstruction uses this as an argument to test, without attributing Ren's theory to her. [Primary text, independently inspected 6 October 2026](https://notebook.colinmclear.net/ox-hugo/korsgaard2018_Fellow_Creatures.pdf).

Thomas Nagel's *The Limits of Objectivity*, printed pp. 103–115, directly distinguishes objectively recognized agent-relative reasons from agent-neutral ones. He argues that the character of basic pain supports reasons for another to relieve it, while expressly denying that the opposing position must be logically inconsistent. This is a positive normative judgment about the experienced ground, not an inference from the patient's usefulness. His argument also rejects treating absence of a deductive demonstration as evidence that other-regarding reasons do not exist. [Primary text, independently inspected 6 October 2026](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/nagel80.pdf).

The methodological consequence for this audit is important: the indexed countermodel blocks a particular entailment; its bare consistency does not give it equal normative warrant. The theory may defend patient-grounded reasons by disciplined examination of experience, competing grounds, defeaters and the credibility of their implications. Such support should be assessed directly. If that defense succeeds without a deductive reduction to prior scientific conditions, it is a substantive grounding result, not a failure to make contact with reason. It should be reported as warranted normative grounding until the stronger necessity claim is separately established.

## 10. Criteria for a derivation, an irreducible ground, or a revision

**The stronger derivation succeeds** if a candidate independently justified root both anchors genuine significance and rules out the indexed interpretation through a valid patient-and-deliberator transport argument. It must identify the exact contradiction or failure of the rival's claimed capacity. The proof must continue to apply when the patient supplies no information, reward, retaliation or possible reciprocal role, and when the agent knows this. Neither loss of motivation nor refusal to assent is the required contradiction.

**A justified irreducible normative ground is the supported conclusion** if reason-giving significance survives serious examination but no more basic independently established premises entail its full scope. The whole theory could then include that ground explicitly and derive its consequences. Its compatibility with science would not make it optional opinion; conversely, its intuitive appeal would not yet establish that every adequate scientific philosophy must accept it. The necessity claim would require an argument for the ground's unavoidable authority, possibly direct rather than reductive. Calling it irreducible is not justified merely by the failure of the first attempted reduction.

**The candidate bridge must be revised** if a rival preserves the independently specified capacities and the actual claimed authority, satisfies its proposed complete reason-ground, yet coherently denies the purported conclusion. A rival that merely abandons the authority claim does not refute the conditional authority theorem; it tests whether that authority was necessary to the broader philosophy in the first place. A rival that silently changes the meaning of the reason-ground is likewise not a counterexample to the unchanged premise.

**The present adjudication:** the first-person route is strongest when it explains the reason's ground and the scope of the judgment's authority together. It reaches the dispensable patient conditionally and provides a real error criterion for pretended shared justification. Objective scientific consistency secures faithful recognition and forbids a false certificate of settlement; it does not yet supply every reason-transport edge. The next central obligation is to establish why genuine practical validity must use the patient-grounded, rather than exclusively actor-indexed, scope. This is a focused continuation of the entire-root objective, with a constructive route and a precise rival to defeat.

## 11. Preserve epistemic authority when testing the practical bridge

**Further AI argument and primary comparison, 6 October 2026.** The next necessity test grants a critic genuine reasons to respond to evidence, including when the truth is unwanted. This is stronger than granting accurate behavior or instrumental success alone. The named connection is **epistemic authority necessary for justified inquiry → practical authority concerning another patient**.

### Primary comparator: Kelly on belief and inquiry

Thomas Kelly, 2003, “Epistemic Rationality as Instrumental Rationality: A Critique,” *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 66, 612–640. [Publisher metadata](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1933-1592.2003.tb00281.x); [mirrored primary manuscript](https://bigfatgenius.com/6340/Kelly%20-%20Epistemic%20rationality%20as%20instrumental%20rationality.pdf).

Kelly argues that evidence can give someone a reason to believe a proposition even when believing it serves none of their goals. He distinguishes that epistemic reason from reasons to seek additional evidence. His account also rejects treating a state's constitutive aim as automatically its bearer's adopted goal. The final section gives inquiry both instrumental and epistemic components. This challenges the premise that all rational authority must be an instrument of antecedent aims; it does not establish our particular patient-grounded principle.

### A stronger counterinterpretation must retain what the argument uses

The following is our analysis. A counterinterpretation that only supplies a forecaster's reliable outputs is too weak against a proposed derivation from justified inquiry. It should retain actual epistemic standards: support and defeat, sensitivity to contrary evidence, preservation of meaning, and criticism of an unsupported assertion even when that assertion would benefit its proponent. A blanket denial of every categorical reason cannot simply coexist with categorical epistemic reasons under another name.

Granting these standards still requires identifying their object. A reason to believe that patient p is suffering concerns the warranted assessment of that proposition. A reason to relieve p concerns an available action and its effect on p. The former can prohibit epistemic distortion without yet deciding every practical response. Saying that these are different kinds of response does not justify the exclusion of moral authority. It locates the proposed bridge and prevents assuming it in a supposedly neutral definition of epistemic correctness.

Three capacities must therefore be recorded separately:

| Capacity or commitment | What it warrants at its stated scope |
|---|---|
| Assess possessed evidence faithfully | Revise an unsupported belief or claim about p, despite an inconvenient conclusion |
| Fulfil an independently warranted inquiry undertaking | Take justified means needed for that undertaking, with feasibility, alternatives and competing reasons explicit |
| Recognize the patient's adverse condition as an action-reason | Give that condition practical weight for the actual actor on a defended ground |

Neither the first nor a supplied analyst's score automatically supplies the second or third. Nor does a soundly recognized practical reason need to originate in a pre-existing desire. The earlier countermodel's corrected capacity scope remains essential: it has not been shown closed under all true practical reasons.

### Protection as an epistemic means has a precise boundary

Suppose the actor's declared inquiry needs a patient's continued ability to provide otherwise unavailable evidence. Protection may then be necessary to fulfil that inquiry. The [transport analysis](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) distinguishes preserving this route, supplying an adequate alternative, and narrowing or withdrawing an unsupported claim. To require continuation itself needs a further retained undertaking or reason; the mere existence of a standard for correct belief is insufficient.

Now hold fulfilment of the stated epistemic requirements fixed in a stipulated comparison: the existing evidence is faithfully preserved, neither action prevents future warranted inquiry, and the actor accurately recognizes the patient's condition and each action's effects. The remaining practical difference is the patient's adverse episode. Honest belief contents can differ with those different outcomes; equal epistemic compliance does not require identical assertions about different events. This is a conceptual test, not a claim that a real person lacks future informational significance. The epistemic-means argument no longer supplies a differential reason for relief. The patient-grounded argument may still supply one. Its authority must be established on that ground rather than concealed in an invented informational benefit.

This identifies a constructive requirement for the whole theory: extend concern beyond a center's usefulness as evidence while preserving the genuine reasons epistemic practice already contains. The parallel [source comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md) and [argument audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md) test whether a shared account of normative authority can do so.

### What would change the result

A successful parity argument would defeat a stated reason for rejecting moral authority whenever that same rejection would eliminate independently admitted epistemic authority. A stronger existence argument would establish some moral truths. The foundation's actual target additionally requires the specific patient-and-actor ground and its coverage. Those achievements must be traced separately.

A justified account deriving the patient reason from the admitted epistemic authority would advance the full necessity claim. An account explaining a relevant difference between the domains would challenge the proposed transfer, but would still need to answer the positive patient-grounded defense. Merely defining scientific adequacy as our complete ethical conclusion, or defining practical adequacy as an initially indifferent policy, would settle neither side.
