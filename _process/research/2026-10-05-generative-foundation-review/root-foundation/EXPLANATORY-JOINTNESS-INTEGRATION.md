## 1. The affirmative explanatory argument

An explanation claiming to cover the joint realization of activities must distinguish success in separate episodes from support available during overlapping participation. One support position can be acquired, released and reused by either activity. That fact does not establish that both can hold it during the same interval. The independently specified target changes: it asks about overlapping realization, not merely separate completed episodes.

The relevant distinctions are relationships within the same configuration: which activity role has access to which support position; whether that position is occupied; what acquisition changes; what release restores. The activities and support are differentiated roles in one local law. They are not fundamentally independent kinds or actors facing an external environment. The model does not claim to explain the universal origin of the supplied access pattern, exclusive holding law or Boolean repertoire.

This gives a target-relative reason for including a joint question. If the account claims to explain whether both holding requirements can be met together, omitting the overlap relation yields an incorrect answer in an explicit mechanism. A rival can use global constraints, an allocation table or a process law, provided it retains the relevant content. Every adequate local explanation need not have this broader target, and no universal necessity of a particular vocabulary follows.

The supporting contribution to generative alignment is accurate treatment of shared realization: preserving separate capabilities or separately successful plans can misdescribe what can be supported together. This is not a derivation of G0, ethical ranking, practical responsibility or a universal duty to preserve every joint option. Completed ethical candidates retain their own explicit premises and review scopes.

## 2. Which questions are now distinguished

The new `Test` retains truth, atoms, negation, conjunction and event continuation, and adds `together f g`:

> The condition admits a pair of jointly realizable parts, the first satisfying `f` and the second `g`.

The three-place relation is oriented as `joint whole left right`. It has no assumed associativity, commutativity, unit, completeness or free duplication. Different applications must justify what their parts and joint relation preserve. Its generic form can represent a decomposition or joint participation; it is not automatically a complete operational semantics for concurrency.

| Question | Retained content |
|---|---|
| `f AND g` at one condition | Both assertions are true there. Their witnessing uses may still conflict. |
| `together f g` | One compatible pair jointly witnesses the two requirements. |
| Can these supplied parts join? | The actual relational compatibility of those particular parts, including shared identity. |
| Can all leaves of a nested plan be realized concurrently? | Potentially additional cross-branch compatibility, duration, footprint and scheduling content. |

The last two rows are not automatically reducible to the first two. Questions here are semantic specifications. Access to their answers, supported negative evidence and preservation of an actual partner during testing remain application obligations; no unrestricted physical test access is inferred.

## 3. Recover correlated structure from richer answers

`FiberCompact domain truth` states joint realization of an entire requirement family whenever every finite part is jointly realizable within that same domain. The new `StepCompact` uses the successors of a given event. `JointCompact` uses the **actual correlated pairs** admitted by a whole, tested by requirements on both coordinates. Compactness of separately projected left and right sets would not supply a compatible pair and is not used.

`finite_fiber_compact` proves that a finite domain supplies this property. Separate corollaries cover finite successors and finite pair fibers. The general theorem does not require a finite whole state space.

For a pair admitted by the first model, collect all finite question pairs true of its two parts. For each finite list, conjoin all left questions and all right questions and ask the single `together` question at the whole. Agreement of whole answers transfers that pair requirement. Correlated-pair compactness yields one admitted pair satisfying the entire family. Negation in the language turns complete-theory preservation into agreement in both directions. This derives `compact_joint_match`; it does not assume a relation-matching field in the initial encoding.

Together with the analogous step argument, `compact_same_iff_bisimilar` characterizes richer finite-test agreement by a bisimulation preserving atoms, steps and joint pairs. `Reach` follows either an event or a part of an admitted joint relation from a starting whole. `bisimulation_lifts_reach` and `compact_generated_correspondence` derive uniquely matched positions when all relevant positions are thus generated and question-separated. `separated_step_correspondence` and `separated_joint_correspondence` preserve and reflect the two native relations under those matches.

The generic relational interface is supplied; the theorem does not discover which relation describes reality. Its substantive advance is a derived preservation condition for a concretely motivated richer target, alongside explicit limitations on that target's reach.

## 4. Exact code plans and the limit on substitution

The code joint relation existentially projects a native joint triple. Under accurate readings, reduction by all richer answers and joint compactness, `code_joint_lifts_from_whole` shows that an admitted coded pair can be realized from **any native whole carrying the source code**. The witnessing parts may be chosen to fit that whole.

`joint_plan_lifts` extends this to every finite binary plan with correctly read leaf requirements. Each node has an actual compatible pair, and the chosen witnesses recursively support its descendants. This is preservation of the specified nodewise realization relation. It does not fix arbitrary leaf identities, discharge cross-branch resource conditions omitted by the native relation, or prove that any bracketing of a plan has the same meaning. No associativity or operational scheduling theorem is added.

The exact scope matters: this is existence of suitable parts, not permission to substitute any part with the same isolated profile. The next example shows the stronger substitution claim can fail even when the whole model is finite and compact.

## 5. Shared support is an actual temporal conflict

`ExplanatorySharedSupport` compares two access patterns. In one, both activity roles reach the same support slot; in the other, they reach distinct slots. Each activity alone has an accessible slot in either arrangement. Holding two slots jointly requires the positions to differ. This exclusivity rule is a declared local condition, not a universal law of generativity.

At the completed-episode interface, performing an activity returns the whole to the same released condition. Both arrangements agree on every ordinary finite modal question, including negation and branching conjunction. Yet `together` distinguishes them. Their steps and joint fibers are both finite, so the general recovery theorem's compactness conditions are verified for these concrete models.

`ExplanatoryLeaseDynamics` supplies the temporal correspondence. `Acquire` requires access and an unoccupied slot, then marks it occupied. `Release` requires an occupied slot and clears it. `ordinary_step_has_native_round` connects a completed ordinary step to an acquisition followed by release. `joint_answer_iff_overlapping_acquisitions` connects the joint answer exactly to acquisition of both activities' supports before either is released.

All finite sequences of complete episodes return to vacancy in either arrangement. In the shared arrangement the second overlapping acquisition is impossible; in the distinct arrangement a concrete overlapping pair is realized. The full native acquisition/release histories therefore differ. Their agreement is at the declared completed-activity interface, not at every possible internal observation. This is a genuine loss under that projection, not a refutation of a richer account already retaining occupancy and access.

## 6. Even joint profiles can lose a given part's relational identity

A second model contains a whole with two distinct exclusive slots. Either ordered pair of different slots can jointly participate; one slot cannot be counted twice. At each isolated slot there are no further transitions or decompositions, and the atom vocabulary is empty. The two slots therefore agree on **all** richer finite tests, including every nested `together` question.

The explicit `slotAccount` has two codes: one for the whole and one shared by both slots. `slot_account_exact` and `slot_account_reduced` verify it, so the counterexample is not conditional on an impossible presentation. `code_joint_does_not_validate_duplicate_piece` demonstrates a valid code-level joint pair with two identical slot codes while the triple formed by repeating one particular native slot is invalid.

`no_reduced_exact_native_join` applies the earlier [ternary recoverability criterion](EXPLANATORY-UNIVERSALITY-INTEGRATION.md) to rule out **any** exact native-relation reader on reduced isolated-answer codes. The obstruction is relational identity, not a need to give every slot an intrinsic name. With a fixed partner, one slot conflicts while the other fits; `partner_answers_recover_slot` proves that these partner-relative answers distinguish the two actual positions.

This supplies a more specific preservation requirement for explanations undertaking to predict interactions among supplied parts. Their sufficient descriptions must preserve the relevant contextual relationships, even when the parts are interchangeable in isolation. It does not demand arbitrary carrier identity in every explanation. Earlier interface-probe work remains credited; the new witness shows why an existential whole query does not automatically provide a fixed-partner query.

## 9. Integrated conclusion and next test

The necessary content can now be stated more precisely: **an explanation of common realization must preserve which witnesses can coexist, and an explanation of interactions among supplied parts must preserve their relevant contextual identities.** The two requirements differ, and both have substantive checked examples.

This extends the common-foundation programme from separate possibilities to correlated realization without declaring a particular representation uniquely necessary. The remaining universal connection concerns warrant for the actual joint relation and the scope of contextual questions. A next discriminating test is global cross-branch sharing: identify the footprint or persistent-part relations that make local joint certificates compose into one simultaneous realization, or construct a genuinely conflicting case where they do not. Do not infer such global composition from the finite binary-plan theorem alone.
