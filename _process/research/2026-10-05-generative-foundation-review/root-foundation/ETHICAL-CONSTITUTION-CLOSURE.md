# Constitutive ethical grounding: what this attempt actually closes

6 October 2026. AI-authored research and argument audit within the existing root-foundation study. The named connection is **actual experiential adversity → local patient badness (C1) → a patient-directed reason for an eligible independent actor (C2)**. This note responds to the continuing requirement to complete the foundation without added assumptions. It does not amend that requirement or substitute conditional completeness for it.

**Result: this attempt does not discharge C1 or C2.** It identifies the strongest available positive route, tests the proposed inference from phenomenal accuracy to practical authority, and records the exact semantic obligation at the formal interface. No new opposite ranking, controller, Lean declaration, empirical result or source survey is presented as progress on necessity.

## 1. What is inherited, and what is new here

The [constitutive candidate](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md) already defends patient significance and prospective relief before an actor adopts care. The [recent root argument](NONINSTRUMENTAL-ROOT-ARGUMENT.md) already tests occurrence, phenomenal identification and fixed-patient operator substitution. Its distinction between a felt direction and a valid demand must not be rediscovered as an advance. The [existing acquaintance comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#29-experiential-grounds-and-warranted-concern) also already separates contact with a quality, its evaluative identity and its interpersonal force.

Likewise, [RootGrounding.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/noninstrumental-concern/RootGrounding.lean) establishes that event anchors together with operator locality are equivalent to the stated universal reason contract. Renaming C2 with those two conditions does not eliminate its content. The history-formed restriction and silent-patient cases remain useful tests at their declared abstract scope; they do not refute a richer, independently justified constitutive identity.

The present contribution is an integrated **closure test for the attempted constitutive inference**. It grants the candidate more than the minimal root presently supplies, locates the surviving authority question without denying phenomenal access, and states which alleged counterexamples preserve the question. It also distinguishes the formal contract's patient-directed merit from the philosophical text's still-disputed scope of current justification. These are research clarifications, not newly proved ethical grounds.

## 2. The strongest positive attempt

The patient-ground route has a substantive advantage over beginning with the actor's private ends. It starts with the condition that relief changes for the actual bearer. Its proposed order is:

1. An actual episode has a particular aversive experiential character.
2. That character constitutes a local burden for its bearer, without needing an external score or a reflective ownership judgment to make it so.
3. An eligible response transforms that same bearer's condition by removing the burden.
4. This patient-directed transformation supplies a prospective justification for the eligible actor's option, before care has been chosen as a project.

The positive explanation is that the patient's changed undergoing, rather than approval by a spectator, answers what makes the response beneficial. It distinguishes actual relief from quieting a report, reducing the observer's discomfort or repairing an unrelated resource counter. It preserves the target when an initially selected means proves ineffective. These are reasons to continue defending the candidate; they are inherited explanatory support rather than a fresh derivation.

The important possible gain at step 2 is real. A warranted identity between an experiential property and local badness could explain C1 rather than leave a second arbitrarily assigned evaluation on top of experience. Nothing in logic requires every constitutive explanation to have that redundant architecture. Conversely, a formal interpretation that changes badness while claiming to preserve the identified property would cease to be a counterexample once that identity had independently been established. The question is whether the identity is justified, not whether it can be written down.

## 3. Grant phenomenal accuracy, then examine exactly what follows

For this test, grant that the patient actually has the specified experience. Grant a qualifying direct acquaintance with its character, and grant C1 itself. These are concessions made to test the strongest route; they are not newly established foundational premises. The patient need not possess a theory of introspection for the burden to exist. Another actor's warranted access can be mediated and does not require recreating the patient's episode in the observer.

What is then established is a truth about the patient: this condition is locally bad for this bearer. Accurate acquaintance, if available, can make the denial of that very phenomenal condition a mistake. It does not yet make every proposition uttered while acquainted with it true. In particular, a judgment about which independent actors have reasons has additional relational content.

This qualification must not be overstated. **C2 does not need to obtain its opportunity facts from introspection.** Independent evidence can supply the actor, means, efficacy and permission. A comparison that changes the opportunity while preserving the experience therefore tests the scope of an experiential observation; it does not refute C1, C2 or a properly composed direct-ground argument. Once those opportunity facts are supplied, the missing inference is still exactly:

\[
\text{this is locally bad for the patient}
\;\land\;
\text{this actor can permissibly relieve it}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the relief counts for this actor's choice}.
\]

Calling the left-hand judgment accurate does not establish the arrow. The proposed constitution could nevertheless establish it if the actual property were shown to be intrinsically a ground for such responses. That richer identity would have to explain its practical range. Evidence establishing only local patient badness does not establish the richer identity merely because both conclusions use the word bad.

There is a positive response to one weak objection here: an error about represented bodily damage need not erase the adverse experience of representing it. The ground sought is the patient's undergoing, not the truth of every represented threat. However, that response does not settle whether the whole experience has the claimed welfare status, and it does not establish the independent actor's practical address.

## 4. A focused primary comparison of the target of relief

Matthew Kinakin, “Why intentionalists can't take painkillers,” *Philosophical Studies* 183 (2026), 1905–1929; published 3 April 2026. [Publisher's original](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-026-02523-z). Accessed 6 October 2026. Read the abstract and §§1–4 through publisher HTML, not the full later argument. No underlying empirical source was checked or source bytes imported.

**Bounded synopsis:** Kinakin argues that explaining motivation to change a represented bodily condition differs from explaining motivation to end the painful experience itself. His target is strong intentionalism that exhausts phenomenal character in world-directed content and retains transparency. He examines reflexive contents as a possible response, then argues that they conflict with that package. This is a philosophical challenge to a specified theory of painfulness, not a proof of patient badness, universal concern, or the present model's experiential realization.

For this study, keep the response's target explicit: a represented condition, an experience, a further attitude, or a report. The distinction is inherited; this comparator is not a closure of C1/C2. The note makes no medical recommendation.

## 5. The strongest surviving rival and the counterexample it must supply

The serious rival can accept actual suffering, its local badness, epistemically warranted identification, and the real benefit of relief. It can also accept self-directed, entrusted, reparative and voluntarily undertaken reasons. Its disputed claim is that **benefiting a patient is not sufficient to make that benefit a reason within every independent actor's choice**. It appeals to a substantive account of practical address or legitimate choice jurisdiction, rather than a private dislike of the patient or a numerical sign reversal.

The rival's causal description and recognition of C1 can therefore be entirely accurate. Our reply cannot simply charge it with not understanding the suffering. The positive pressure on it is that the same real improvement explains the prospective patient-directed advantage of an option before adoption, including an option within an independently chosen project. The rival must explain why its jurisdictional restriction excludes that justification instead of merely leaving assistance optional, reducing its weight or limiting deliberative demands. The [assistance-jurisdiction review](ASSISTANCE-JURISDICTION-COUNTERREVIEW.md) retains this distinction.

Conversely, the rival is not defeated merely because it has not justified the restriction to our satisfaction. Nor does its formal expressibility establish that its restriction is justified. Necessity of C2 requires an argument that excludes the rival's claimed restriction on grounds independent of C2. The present attempt has not supplied that argument.

A relevant challenge must preserve the target rather than replace it:

| Challenge | What must be preserved or independently established |
|---|---|
| Challenge C1 by changing an attitude | Preserve the full declared experiential whole, including any constitutive aversion; changing only sensation intensity is insufficient. |
| Challenge C1 through wider-life benefit | Distinguish the claimed absence of a local burden from the claim that undergoing it is worthwhile overall. Either conclusion needs its own argument. |
| Challenge C2 through actor cost | Show absence of the patient-directed consideration, rather than merely a reason that outweighs it. |
| Challenge C2 through jurisdiction | Establish a legitimate exclusion that actually applies to this choice; calling the actor independent does not establish such a power. |
| Challenge a constitutive identity through a formal interpretation | Preserve the independently warranted content of the proposed identity. Varying an unconstrained predicate alone cannot refute an identity already established on other grounds. |

This is not a requirement that a naturalistic grounding persuade every rival. It specifies the disputed proposition and the conditions under which an argument would establish or defeat it.

## 6. The formal interface needs a declared meaning of current justification

The current [GroundingContract.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/noninstrumental-concern/GroundingContract.lean) states:

```lean
def C2 (Bad : PatientCondition → Prop) (Merit : State → Patient → Prop) : Prop :=
  ∀ s i, Bad (condition s i) → Eligible s i → Merit s i
```

In [PatientConcern.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/noninstrumental-concern/PatientConcern.lean), `Eligible` is positive burden, `permitted = true`, and sufficient fuel for the specified remaining plan. The process provides its route efficacy. `Merit` is independently interpreted and is not a runtime variable. The proof correctly derives consequences **if that interpretation satisfies C2**.

The current [philosophical candidate](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md#protection-can-affect-justification-itself) explicitly distinguishes “Exclusion from justifying a particular kind of choice” from merely limiting attention, and states that an otherwise significant consideration “may not currently count for that choice”. It also says that “an inert moral label would not suffice” for the intended C2. This qualification concerns a possible legitimate exclusion under assessment; it does not establish that any actual case in the formal domain is excluded.

**No inconsistency of the proof or actual counterexample to C2 is established here.** Baseline moral favor, an all-things-considered duty and authority to compel remain different, as the [ethical minimum](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md) already explains. A choice-specific exclusion, if justified, would raise a further question about current use of a ground; it cannot be silently treated as ordinary outweighed favor or as already established.

The narrow semantic clarification is therefore: the formal result establishes current prospective justification only for an interpretation and domain in which **Bad plus the independently specified operational conditions is sufficient for such justification**. Calling `Merit` a standing patient-related good can preserve a weaker claim, but would not by itself establish its warranted use in the actor's current choice. Calling `Eligible` morally adequate by definition would relocate the target. Neither change is made here. Whether the richer sufficient condition is justified remains part of C2's grounding burden; no new disqualification gate is introduced as an axiom.

## 7. Exact completion judgment and the result that would change it

This attempt has not found a new independent warrant that removes C1 or C2. It does not follow that every constitutive account is impossible, that only arbitrary preferences exist, or that the previously defended relief judgment should be abandoned. The affirmative candidate continues to explain what the response accomplishes for its patient and why a private reward need not supply that benefit's significance. Its philosophical support remains defeasible and distinct from the no-assumptions target.

The next substantive success would be one of two things: a defended identification of the actual experiential property with local patient badness that survives the fixed-domain challenges, or a defended account showing why that very patient-ground has nonexclusive practical address in the specified eligible cases. The latter must address the jurisdiction rival rather than define adequate agency as accepting C2. A justified exclusion would instead require revising the candidate's scope. A realization theory, accurate report, helpful behavior or another equivalent contract would not by itself decide either question.

Actual phenomenal realization, patient and episode identification, permissions and efficacy, the truth and scope of C1/C2, and the formal background remain undischarged where supplied. The generated self need not be a primitive owner of value; removing it as a primitive does not complete those other explanations. This is a closed audit of this particular inference attempt and an open foundation.
