## 3. Strongest rival: the test removed a condition of supported performance

A capacity can be relationally supported. Removing a familiar helper, disrupting timing, creating anxiety, withholding an indispensable interpretive resource or changing the task can impair performance without showing that the original capacity never existed. An assay that demands isolated, unaided achievement can therefore misdescribe a jointly realized capacity. The theory must preserve this serious rival.

The selected comparison is stronger than merely removing help: it varies the helper's access to specific content. If the reported, interpreted response follows content made available to the helper rather than the different content to be conveyed, that result bears on the route generating the report. A generic account of anxiety or unfamiliarity explains possible failure but does not by itself explain why a particular alternative content appears. Its defenders would need to account for that positive dependence.

This does not make every experimental detail irrelevant. Information leakage, task comprehension, remembered content, scoring, order, expectations and effects on the helping activity remain proper evidential questions. The inference must be assessed against what was actually controlled. The response variable includes interpretation and oral reporting, so locating an informational dependence does not isolate motor control, establish intentional deception or identify the exclusive source of every character in every setting. The body retains an exception to the abstract's absolute wording; the review does not repair that discrepancy by treating the authors' suggested explanation as an observation.

The narrower warranted revision is to the demonstrated performance and its use as an assay. **“This output has not established the participant's attributed message through this route”** differs from **“this person has no understanding, no experience or no communicative possibility.”** The latter assertions require other evidence and are not the conclusion being investigated.

The evidence audit adds a useful exact distinction: exclusion from one estimate does not erase every other evidential role of the occurrence. An expression can defeat the information separation needed to assess one route while providing evidence of expression through another. Keeping both roles does not require reinserting the event into the first statistic. Nor does it certify a fully adequate alternative: the second use needs its own correspondence and scope. This is a sound philosophical application to the case, with no new mathematical theorem asserted.

## 4. Strongest monist rival: communication is always co-formed

All actual communication is shaped through relations: language, attention, material support, earlier interaction and the present interlocutor matter. A demand for an entirely uncaused, self-originating message would contradict the repository's method and would not be a sensible attribution standard.

That observation does not erase differentiated informational influence. Within one interaction, it can matter whether the relevant person can introduce or correct the content attributed to them, whether a helper is translating that content, or whether a helper's different information determines the attributed response. These are distinguishable relations within the same process. The test should identify them without reinstalling independent minds or private value ownership as primitives.

Co-formation therefore defeats an isolation requirement, not every accountability requirement. If an undertaking presents a message as conveying one participant's answer or choice, its evidential route must support that narrower attribution. A jointly composed text can instead be presented as jointly composed. What is unjustified is shifting between those claims without recognizing the change.

Conversely, assistance which contributes content is not automatically wrongful. Suggestions, interpretation and joint deliberation can be warranted and valuable. The ethical issue depends on the represented claim, meaningful participation, authority and actual effects. A source-dependence result is not a complete assessment of the relationship or a rule to remove all help.

## 5. The ethical inference must preserve its premises

G0/J already provide grounds concerning actual living, effective participation, bounded responsibilities and warranted practical choice. The source study does not derive those commitments. It supplies evidence relevant to an application: whether a particular output can bear the attributed communicative role.

Several distinctions should remain explicit. Failure of this communication assay does not by itself erase grounds for recognition or support. An erroneous attribution can itself obscure someone's available expression or refusal. Correcting that attribution need not eliminate support; the practical question is which supported avenues genuinely enable participation, with uncertainty and concrete burdens included. The rejection of one asserted route does not establish a replacement's availability, nor discharge every earlier undertaking to help.

The same reasoning prevents an opposite overreach. A hope of inclusion, a helper's sincere conviction or the desirability of the output does not make the attribution true. Treating a relationship as generative cannot authenticate each claim made within it. Preserving the person's significance and revising a mistaken account of the channel are compatible responses under the existing ethical commitments. The paper itself already separates rejecting this procedure from withdrawing dignity or communication support (printed p.199); our application must credit that precedent rather than claim the ethical distinction as its own discovery.

This is an application of the declared normative framework, not a diagnosis of any contemporary person or a universal prescription inferred from one historical sample. The case concerns a specific kind of physically facilitated communication; findings about that procedure must not be indiscriminately extended to other forms of supported or augmentative communication.

The advance is an **evidence-constrained revision of a joint description**: the evaluated activity yields a report; different accounts attribute that performance differently; a content-specific intervention tests those accounts; the result changes which claim the measurement can support. The descriptions, measurement and correction arise within one interaction and inquiry history. No outside infallible observer or unexplained `Faithful` label settles the answer.

This is a useful step beyond a wholly stipulated toy. Its value does not depend on being historically novel: the original researchers did the discriminating experimental work, and conventional causal and measurement approaches already explain it. The present theory's contribution is to integrate the acquired scope correction with its ethical target, retaining both the demonstrated failure and the person's unexhausted possibilities.

The NPL case supplies the positive counterpart. A partially assessed observation route can make an existing reference questionable and help initiate a correction; it need not first receive that reference's complete certification. The evidence still develops in stages. A discrepancy may defeat confidence or motivate investigation before its cause is identified. The subsequent diagnosis needs more than agreement after adjustment. The argument preserves these stages and gives the actual investigators credit.

The strongest remaining rival is that this is ordinary fallible measurement, already covered by the repository's knowledge account. That objection defeats any claim to a new general epistemology or historical priority, but it does not defeat this round's narrower integration: the application now has reported discriminating evidence, a positive correction history and a precise boundary on transferring its conclusions into practical classification. The methods used are compatible with other philosophical accounts; these cases do not uniquely establish generative monism.

The limits are equally substantial. The communication researchers were given a disputed attribution and designed a test; the paper does not establish autonomous discovery of every new capacity or ethical category. A supported rejection of an old measurement does not by itself produce a successful new measurement of the broader capacity. The NPL correction establishes a different reported success, not the missing communication replacement. Likewise, learning why an earlier claim was unwarranted does not prove that every participant subsequently recognizes or acts on the correction.

The source does, however, report that escape or avoidance led to trials being stopped; I independently reread that passage at printed pp.197–198.
