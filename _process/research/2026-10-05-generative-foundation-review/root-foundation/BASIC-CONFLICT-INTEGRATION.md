The question advanced is **warranted but crossed basic claims → substantive comparison or acknowledged procedural latitude**. Original J2 compares protection of the same indexed requirements by inclusion. A plan protecting different lives may fail that inclusion even if it protects more. We must neither mislabel this as an old theorem nor remove the unprotected claims to manufacture dominance.

The [formation-sequence study](FORMATION-SEQUENCE-ARGUMENT.md) concerns a different connection, complete-history comparison across new formation. The present study concerns an existing affected population and a specified urgent allocation; it derives no population-creation priority. Whole histories, not only endpoint counts, remain the objects of appraisal.

## 2. The affirmative commitment and its rationale

BC1 applies only when relevant basic rescue stakes and efficacy are equivalent, the plans are independently authorized, and differential imposed injury, burdens, valid undertakings or established allocation rights do not supply a contrary reason. The operative limitation is not that all biographies must be indistinguishable; it is that the differences relevant to this rescue have been assessed without smuggling its verdict into the assessment. Duplicating records or descriptions supplies no additional living realization.

G0 grounds positive concern for the actual living to be protected. BC1 further holds that common responsive capacity should realize more of that equivalent protection rather than deliberately leave its more extensive realization contingent on a new equal-chance demand. This comparative commitment is additional. It answers a restricted conflict through limited aggregation; it does not claim that joint formation alone entails the judgment.

The position pays a real normative cost: in a one-route allocation some affected lives may receive no chance. The account does not make them less significant, identify them as expendable, or pretend that the resulting loss is compensated for them. Instead it judges a shared response under which more equally grave and equally avoidable losses are prevented. Equal-chance and weighted-chance rivals can reject this priority. The argument needs to address their account of fairness, not accuse them of indifference.

This distinguishes two prohibitions that could otherwise be conflated. J2's protection against sacrificing basic conditions for optional enrichment does not itself prohibit combining equivalent basic rescue reasons. Conversely, permitting that combination does not license accumulating minor gains against a basic loss. The contrast preserves the existing scoped commitment without claiming to have proved the global consistency of every possible extension.

## 3. Primary-source assessment and the revision it required

His discussion also warns against disguising a contested moral judgment as its own explanation. We therefore disclose BC1 rather than claim a nonaggregative deduction. These are targeted readings, not acceptance of his complete ethical position or a comprehensive priority review.

## 4. Worked application and differentiation of grounds

The theory constructs a maintained refuge network with a deadline and two reliable rescue routes, protecting one or two equally threatened people. Support history, responder burden and permissions are stipulated; coordination and other timely alternatives have been considered within the stated scope. This setup isolates a normative question. It does not establish how often those conditions hold in actual disasters or recommend a medical triage policy.

BC1 favors the route rescuing two. Changing the danger to optional disruption, adding an imposed injury, introducing unequal efficacy or a valid prior allocation right changes the argument. Where joint rescue becomes feasible, the claimed forced allocation no longer describes the case. Where both options protect one equivalent claim, BC1 supplies no directional comparison and the inherited procedure remains available.

The distinction between priority and assignment is preserved. An independently justified allocation principle does not create rescue equipment, competence, permission or unlimited personal service. Immediate action and later scrutiny can differ in their evidential opportunities. Historical responsibility for avoidable scarcity remains relevant after emergency allocation, rather than being erased by the mere fact that someone had to choose.

## 5. Substantive author counterreview

**“You have turned whole generativity into maximizing headcount.”** BC1 is narrower: equivalent, established basic rescue under independently assessed burdens and permissions. It provides no conversion between pain, expression, ecological continuity or future production. It does not value new copies merely by their labels or authorize producing lives to outvote existing claims. If wider consequences differentiate the rescue routes, the narrow comparison alone is insufficient.

**“The word equivalent contains the desired winner.”** The classification must describe comparable losses and protections before evaluating the plans. Equivalent stakes do not say whether priority or a lottery is correct; the rival grants them. BC1 carries that further normative content openly. A procedure that calls every favored case equivalent and every adverse case different would defeat the claimed application.

**“Equal importance should require equal chances.”** This is a real disagreement. We defend a narrower conception of equal respect in which equivalent actual protection has priority in the stated allocation. The smaller group can object to receiving no chance. Their objection is not answered by denying their need or saying that they should identify with a larger whole. BC1 remains a contestable commitment, revisable if its account of fairness fails.

**“A weighted lottery respects both concerns.”** It is a genuine alternative. Acknowledging numerical relevance alone does not establish BC1's stronger judgment. The candidate deliberately gives more extensive actual rescue priority in this scope; it does not pretend to derive a uniquely fair lottery weight or treat chance equality as irrelevant in all allocations.

**“This licenses inflicting harm on a smaller group.”** Permission is independently required; newly imposed injury changes the full history. The constructed case does not pay for rescue by destroying someone else's basic support. This study settles no general doctrine about doing versus allowing harm, overriding property, emergency consent or rights exceptions. Those cannot be imported by referring to the rescue count.

**“An excluded minority always loses under this rule.”** Repeated institutional allocation changes the relevant history. Systematically denied access, accumulated loss and obligations formed by earlier allocation cannot be deleted to recreate an otherwise-equal one-off case indefinitely. When those relations differ, this application of BC1 is unsupported. We have not supplied a complete distributive theory or a calibrated cumulative-burden threshold.

**“Any new axiom can encode its favored answer.”** Disclosure alone is insufficient. The defended rationale concerns how a formed common capacity should respond to equally serious actual living claims; the case tests the cost of that rationale against fair-chance rivals and actual loss. This does not prove universal necessity, and it does not pretend to. The authorized axiomatic route permits explicit substantive development; its arguments remain answerable to consequences and criticism.

**“The existing Lean comparator proves the new choice.”** False. It remains unchanged and does not decide the crossed-index example by mere inclusion. This round makes no mathematical proof claim. A generalized executable comparator would need exact new assumptions, a check of interactions and actual Lean verification before receiving that status. Reusing old audit counts would misrepresent what has been checked.

## 6. Nondual and scope audit

The shared provision, differentiated need, available action and evaluative practice form within one history. The whole is not an additional bearer whose gain absorbs another's loss. Nor must each life be isolated from all relations before its protection can count. Equivalent rescue is a scoped comparison of those differentiated relationships.

Actual loss, grounds for considering it, permission, priority, procedural authority and motivation are distinguished by their roles in that history. They are not separate ontological domains. Their connection does not imply inevitable agreement, identical duties or the capacity to enact every warranted response.

Completed here: the BC1 philosophical candidate, its precise relation to J2 and J4, a definite conditional judgment, the cost concerning chances, and substantive revision conditions. Uncompleted: all-basic-loss ranking, unequal severity, uncertain efficacy, complete cross-context consistency, empirical correspondence, and reliable implemented concern. The next theoretical question is a warranted comparison of unequal basic losses, not another generic lottery mechanism or a reopening of the basic-standing account.
