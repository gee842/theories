## 1. What warrants going beyond the examined cases?

The affirmative proposal is **supported continuity of the relationships relevant to the question**. We can have reasons to expect a result to extend when the sampling procedure, causal organization, calibration or other dependence that supported it is also supported in the new setting. This is a fallible explanatory standard. It is neither a proof that the future must resemble the past nor a requirement to possess a complete causal theory before making any empirical inference.

The previous round distinguished a new answer from a changed question. This round initially holds the question sufficiently stable to investigate extension: another occasion, location, population, scale or intervention. Those changes can affect the relation being projected even when its name remains unchanged. Conversely, a conspicuous difference can be irrelevant to the particular inference. The task is to establish which differences matter and why.

The relevant relationship can be statistical. A justified sampling design can support a population estimate without identifying every individual's causal history. A mechanistic explanation can support extension to a new case without treating every feature of its history as identical. A verified proof can establish what follows wherever its hypotheses hold, while evidence is still needed that a physical application satisfies them. These are different routes, not interchangeable certificates.

**推展的根据，不是“它们都属于同一整体”，而是已经有根据地辨明：支撑这个判断的关系，在新的情境中怎样保持、改变或失效。** 共同生成使这些关系能够被相连研究；它不自动使每个局部规律处处不变。

## 2. Hume's challenge survives the word “generative”

[Hume's first Enquiry, §IV](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/4), asks what supports the move beyond observed conjunctions; [§V, Part I](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/5) offers an account involving custom. The argument prevents us from treating repeated success, or a formed habit of expectation, as an assumption-free demonstration of future conformity. We use that challenge without adopting every claim about causation or reasoning in his account.

“Reality is generative” does not yet identify which regularity will continue. Generative activity can reorganize its own conditions, destroy a previously stable relationship or form a new one. Monism also does not entail stationarity. A theory allowing these changes cannot use its overarching vocabulary as a guarantee against them.

Our response is consequently local and comparative. Suppose a measurement route has succeeded in one operating range. We investigate a suspected source of failure through a calibration that can reveal it, assess a competing explanation, or obtain evidence in another relevant range. Such work can improve the grounds of a qualified extension. It does not establish every background assumption from no premises.

This relies on the preceding knowledge account's explicit commitment that competent, relevant evidence can provide fallible entitlement. That commitment is not derived from G0. Calling it explicit does not justify every application: we must still show why a proposed test bears on the disputed extension. A test that cannot distinguish the favored explanation from its live rival supplies less support than claimed. A test selected only after inspecting its favorable result needs an account of that selection.

The positive achievement is the ability to discriminate better and worse grounds for a particular projection. Global skeptical certainty is not the completion criterion for this authorized line. Nor is the absence of such certainty a reason to treat every forecast as equally unsupported.

## 3. What a responsible extension must identify

The account proposes five connected requirements for explicit scientific projection. They are standards for assessing an articulated inference, not psychological steps that every ordinary knower must perform consciously.

| Question | What supplies support | What remains a possible failure |
|---|---|---|
| What is being extended? | A claim with an identifiable quantity, relation, time and range. | A new meaning is presented as confirmation of an old claim. |
| Why should the supporting relation continue? | Evidence for an appropriate sampling relationship, stable dependency, calibration or warranted theoretical application. | Similar appearance conceals a changed dependency. |
| What has changed between settings? | Investigation of differences that could affect this inference, including measurement and selection. | A proxy changes its relation to what matters. |
| What would count against extension? | An accessible discriminating test, failure pattern or independently supported undercutter. | Tests repeat a shared blind spot or exclude contrary cases. |
| How far does the support reach? | A qualified claim, explicit uncertainty and conditions for reassessment. | Local success becomes unrestricted confidence. |

The table should not be read as “support an assumption by assuming its truth.” A substantive bridge names a relation with consequences beyond the desired conclusion and provides grounds that bear on those consequences. For example, an independently assessed calibration can test a proposed channel defect; evidence about selection can distinguish a sampling difference from a change in the underlying response. Complete independence from all previous inquiry is not required, but merely restating the favored outcome adds no constraint.

Several kinds of uncertainty can coexist: uncertainty about the answer within a model, about the model's applicable range, about the measurement relation, and about whether the candidate family includes the actual case. More precision in the first does not necessarily reduce the others. This is especially significant for exact formal guarantees: they remain exact about the specified domain even if that domain was mistakenly chosen.

## 4. A causal comparison: transfer depends on what differs

[Pearl and Bareinboim's transportability paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01603) makes cross-population inference conditional on specified causal similarities and differences. Its examples distinguish a relevant covariate from a proxy whose relationship can change. The reading and proof-access limits are recorded separately; we do not claim to have reproduced its general algorithm.

The philosophical point we draw is that “same observed category” and “same relationship supporting the inference” can diverge. A bridge that works when a measured feature participates in the relevant dependency can fail when the same measurement merely tracks another feature differently across settings. A change can require adjustment, a different question, or suspension of the transfer claim.

The model's background variables do not stand outside generative reality. They represent relations omitted from the chosen local description. Likewise, the investigator's choice of a diagram is part of inquiry, but choosing it does not establish that its exclusions are correct. The account therefore distinguishes formal identifiability under assumptions from empirical warrant for those assumptions and from successful measurement of the terms involved.

This is a concrete route from the generative method to scientific discipline: examine the formation and operation of the relationships through which a result is expected to extend. It is compatible with conventional causal inference; it does not establish that a new generative vocabulary is uniquely necessary.

## 5. An empirical check on easy projection

Recht, Roelofs, Schmidt and Shankar's [2019 primary study](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/recht19a.html) reconstructed image-classification test sets. Accuracy fell while model rankings were largely preserved; selection procedures substantially affected performance. Their discussion does not exclude every form of adaptive overfitting. We read the main article, not its separate supplementary experiments, and did not rerun the models.

This supports a limited but useful comparison. The proposition “this model ranks above another under these evaluations” may transfer more successfully than a particular absolute performance estimate. Both can be related to the same trained capacity. Failure of one projection does not erase every supported comparison; success of the other does not certify the failed numerical extrapolation.

The case also prevents us from treating data collection as a transparent window onto a completed distribution. Sampling and annotation are actual activities that participate in forming the evaluated relation. An account of generalization must include them where relevant. That does not make performance arbitrary or whatever annotators wish it to be. It makes the reported claim accountable to the procedure and target it actually measures.

We do not generalize this historical result to every current model, dataset or kind of cognition. Its contribution is evidence that apparently close reconstruction can preserve some comparisons while altering others, and that the differences require investigation.

## 6. The truth of a claim, support for it, and permission to rely on it

Three questions now need to be held together:

1. **Does the specified content obtain?** This is the truth question.
2. **How well do the available grounds support it?** This concerns evidence, competence and uncertainty.
3. **What reliance is justified here?** This concerns a particular use or assertion, its consequences, alternatives and responsibilities.

These are differentiated relations within one activity. Their distinction does not restore separate factual and ethical worlds. Collecting evidence takes time, affects people and can change what will be possible; accepting a claim can guide action. Nevertheless, the fact that a conclusion would be welcome does not make the evidence support it more strongly.

Heather Douglas's [inductive-risk argument](https://thehangedman.com/teaching-files/stv/douglas-inductiverisk.pdf) gives a substantive comparison. Foreseeable consequences of error can bear on methodological and acceptance decisions within science. Her argument does not license treating a desired conclusion as true. We assess this distinction and its implications, not the current toxicological questions used in her historical examples.

Our proposed refinement is to state the role of acceptance. A claim accepted provisionally for a low-cost exploration is not automatically established well enough for an irreversible decision. Conversely, insufficient grounds to assert a danger as known can coexist with sufficient grounds for a proportionate protective measure, when its burdens and alternatives are appropriately assessed. Acting under uncertainty need not be disguised as possessing certainty.

Ethical concerns may change which errors are sought, how a sample is collected, how categories are investigated, and how much additional evidence is demanded before a particular reliance. They therefore can affect the evidence that eventually exists. They should not be counted twice: once as a reason to seek discriminating evidence and again as if moral importance were itself an observation confirming the result.

This round does not settle the separate epistemological dispute over whether practical stakes change the truth of “knows.” Its argument needs the less controversial distinction between a factual claim and authorization to rely on it in a specific way. The canonical candidate still requires truth and competent grounding; operational acceptance should not be silently substituted for knowledge.

## 7. Why greater stakes do not always mean “wait for more evidence”

High stakes can favor a more demanding check when an incorrect intervention would cause an avoidable loss. They can also favor timely action when waiting would eliminate the remaining opportunity to respond. Which applies depends on the whole comparison. Neither “be cautious” nor “be decisive” fixes it in advance.

Consider a hypothetical shared facility with an uncertain failure mode. A common protective action may work under both remaining explanations. If its costs and authority are justified, uncertainty about the exact fault need not prevent that action. A detailed diagnosis may still be worthwhile afterward. In another case, the alternatives require incompatible responses and a timely test can identify which response fits. Then the same amount of factual uncertainty has a different practical role.

The [new checked inquiry interface](INQUIRY-ADEQUACY-CORRESPONDENCE.md) formalizes this difference for supplied requirements. It also retains elapsed investigation time, showing a truthful test that misses the response deadline. These are conditional mathematical results, not empirical facts about facilities or proofs that a particular intervention is morally required.

The example exposes two errors in an undifferentiated demand for knowledge. One is paralysis until every relevant fact is identified, even where the admissible response does not depend on the unresolved distinction. The other is confident action from a more detailed diagnosis after the conditions making the response effective have already disappeared. Epistemic resolution and practical adequacy must be composed in time.

## 8. Counterreview and result

**The Humean objection remains:** invoking stable dependencies uses empirical commitments whose universal legitimacy has not been demonstrated from nothing. The response is an explicit fallibilist account of local warrant; no global solution is claimed. A generative explanation must do actual comparative work within that account.

**A statistical rival need not provide a complete mechanism:** justified sampling and predictive validation can support a limited projection. Our proposal must accept that success at its scope. A failure to explain deeper formation is not automatically a failure to estimate the stated quantity.

**A values-in-science rival may reject our separation of support and reliance:** some scientific categories already embed purposes and risk choices. We therefore require the claim's content and category formation to be examined, not treated as neutral by default. Once a content and evidential relationship are identified, a desired consequence still cannot replace evidence that the content obtains.

**A projection can be supported and fail:** fallible warrant is not a promise of token success. Failure nevertheless demands assessment of the relevant support; “fallible” cannot immunize a method against patterns that undermine it. Retaining original predictions and conditions is essential to that assessment.

The round establishes a defended philosophical route from local knowing to qualified extension and situated reliance. It combines primary philosophical comparison, an empirical report and a new checked decision interface with separate scopes. It advances recognition and calibration for the governing normative programme. The next study supplies the positive G0 argument for selecting, limiting and sometimes requiring inquiry, rather than treating accurate projection as the whole ethical answer.
