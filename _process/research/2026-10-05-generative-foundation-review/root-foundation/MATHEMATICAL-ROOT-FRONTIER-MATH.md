# Reconstructing mathematical structure from a root: naturality, definability and truth

6 October 2026. AI-authored mathematical development of the stronger requested thesis: **our root uniquely constitutes every mathematical truth**. This is a frontier beyond the accepted conditional foundation, not an additional completion requirement. It starts from the [existing truth-constitution theorem](MATHEMATICS-TRUTH-CONSTITUTION-MATH.md), [closure resolution](MATHEMATICS-CLOSURE-RESOLUTION.md), [structural results](MATHEMATICS-STRUCTURAL-RESULTS.md) and [deeper causal root](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/DEEPER-GENERATIVE-ROOT.md).

The earlier work already established unique satisfaction for fixed interpreted structures, least-generation and identity results, an explicit arithmetic construction, and an exact truth-factorization criterion. Repeating those results would not establish ultimate constitution. This note adds two stronger bridges and their limits:

1. **Reverse reconstruction:** sufficiently uniform operations across an entire algebraic family are necessarily generated by its terms. Applied to deterministic instances of the process root, every unary operation commuting with all system maps is one uniquely determined finite action word.
2. **Implicit-to-explicit determination:** in classical first-order logic, unique interpretation of an added relation over fixed root reducts entails a definition in the root language. Combined with diagonalization, this rules out one precise same-level all-truth proposal even for a noneffective theory.

The second result does not refute a semantic or metaphysical ground of mathematics. The current root's own least reachability construction provides a positive example outside a uniform first-order definability restriction. Its success depends on a real added closure resource. The issue is therefore which structures and closure principles the proposed root justifies, not whether the word “generation” can be attached to them.

All proofs below are hand arguments in ordinary classical mathematics. Standard antecedents are identified; no mathematical priority, empirical result, finite test of every model, or new proof-assistant verification is claimed.

## 1. The target of uniqueness must be fixed

Three questions can now be distinguished more sharply:

- **Reconstruction:** do the proposed root data and an independently identified class of structure-preserving maps force the mathematical operation or interpretation?
- **Truth sufficiency:** after its subject and interpretation are fixed, does that structure determine the designated truth?
- **Ultimate constitution:** is the root what the mathematical subject and truth hold in virtue of, and are rival ultimate grounds either equivalent presentations of it or genuinely inadequate?

The first two can have exact mathematical answers. Their affirmative answers do not entail the third without a constitutive argument. Conversely, a metaphysical constitution claim need not imply an effective truth procedure or a truth predicate definable inside the very language whose entire truth it covers.

The actual deeper root has supplied types, event laws and admitted histories. It does not say that every formal presentation realizes the same physical dynamics. Its deterministic specialization below is deliberately narrower than all relational processes. An enriched universal interpretation can concern a plural family of structural possibilities without choosing one universe as the sole mathematical subject. The indices identifying those subjects and the rules for transporting content must remain explicit.

## 2. A new positive converse: uniform algebraic operations must be generated

Fix a finitary algebraic signature and equations. Let \(\mathcal V\) be their category of algebras and **all** homomorphisms preserving the specified operations. Write \(U(A)\) for an algebra's underlying set. Assume a free algebra \(F(n)\) on designated generators \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), for \(n\ge1\), with its usual universal property.

Suppose every algebra \(A\) is assigned an operation

\[
\eta_A:U(A)^n\longrightarrow U(A)
\]

and every homomorphism \(h:A\to B\) satisfies

\[
h(\eta_A(a_1,\ldots,a_n))
=\eta_B(h(a_1),\ldots,h(a_n)).
\tag{N1}
\]

This is naturality for the chosen underlying-set functor. It requires compatibility with every admitted homomorphism, not merely with renaming elements inside one structure.

### Theorem N1 — Natural operations are exactly term operations

There is one term operation, unique modulo the equations valid in \(\mathcal V\), whose interpretation is \(\eta_A\) for every \(A\). Conversely, every term operation satisfies (N1).

**Proof.** Let

\[
t=\eta_{F(n)}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in U(F(n)).
\]

Every element of the free algebra is represented by a term in its generators. Given \(\mathbf a\in U(A)^n\), let \(e_{\mathbf a}:F(n)\to A\) be the unique homomorphism taking \(x_i\) to \(a_i\). Equation (N1) gives

\[
\eta_A(\mathbf a)=e_{\mathbf a}(t)=t^A(\mathbf a).
\tag{N2}
\]

If two terms give the same family, evaluate them at the free generators; their elements of \(F(n)\) coincide. This is exactly equality modulo the variety's equations. Conversely, induction on a term shows that every homomorphism preserves its value. ∎

The result reverses the earlier direction “generated expressions admit coherent interpretation.” Here a strong independently stated coherence condition forces an operation to be generated. It is an elementary instance of Lawvere's reconstruction: the operations of an algebraic theory correspond to natural operations on its underlying-set functor. [Lawvere, *Functorial Semantics of Algebraic Theories* (1963), printed p. 870](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/lawvere.pdf).

Composition and projections are also visible among these natural operations. Consequently the concrete family \((\mathcal V,U)\) reconstructs the term-operation clone, including substitution and equational identities. Here a concrete equivalence to \((\mathcal W,V)\) means an equivalence \(F:\mathcal V\to\mathcal W\) together with a specified natural isomorphism \(U\cong V\circ F\). It transports those operations, projections and compositions and gives the same reconstructed algebraic theory at this level. It need not preserve a particular list of primitive symbols or a particular axiom presentation.

The class of algebras, its maps, its underlying-set functor and its equations remain substantive inputs. An abstract equivalence of categories that forgets the concrete functor is not automatically this reconstruction theorem. The case \(n=0\) needs a free zero-generated algebra under the chosen convention about empty algebras; no such convention is silently assumed here.

### Corollary N1a — For the deterministic process root, uniform unary operations are finite action words

Fix a set \(A\) of action labels. Consider every system consisting of a set \(S\) and one total transition \(\delta_a:S\to S\) per \(a\in A\). A system map commutes with every \(\delta_a\). This is the algebraic signature of unary action operations with no equations imposed.

For a finite word \(w=a_1\cdots a_k\), let \(\delta_w\) mean execute \(a_1\) first, then \(a_2\), and so on; \(\delta_\varepsilon\) is the identity. Thus \(\delta_{uv}=\delta_v\circ\delta_u\): word concatenation follows chronological execution. The free one-generated system has carrier \(A^*\), generator \(\varepsilon\), and transition \(w\mapsto wa\).

Every assignment \(\eta_S:S\to S\) satisfying (N1) for all these systems and maps is therefore

\[
\eta_S(s)=\delta_w(s)
\tag{N3}
\]

for **one unique finite word** \(w\in A^*\).

**Proof.** Apply N1 to the free system. Its chosen element is a finite word \(w\). The homomorphism determined by \(s\) sends each word \(v\) to \(\delta_v(s)\), giving (N3). Two different words have different values at \(\varepsilon\) in that free system, so they cannot give the same family. ∎

Thus within this precise class, uniform state transformation is not merely compatible with finite generativity: it is exhausted by it. This is a genuine positive necessity-and-sufficiency result connected to the process root. It does not cover arbitrary observations, probabilities, nondeterministic relations, infinite-limit operations or all mathematical truth. In particular, negation and arbitrary universal statements are not generally preserved by a homomorphism.

For groups, the analogous unary statement says that every operation natural under all group homomorphisms has the form \(g\mapsto g^k\) for one integer \(k\): the free group on one generator is infinite cyclic. This illustrates why the whole semantic family, including its free objects, matters. Checking finitely many examples is not a substitute for the universal hypothesis.

## 3. Renaming invariance is necessary but substantially weaker

Any root-defined structure that introduces no distinguished external names should respect automorphisms of that root. This gives a useful obstruction, but not a complete constitutive theory.

### Proposition N2 — A homogeneous carrier cannot canonically choose an internal asymmetry

Let \(D\) have at least three elements and contain only equality, or add one holding action whose transition is the identity. Every permutation of \(D\) is an automorphism. Then:

1. A permutation-invariant subset of \(D\) is either empty or all of \(D\).
2. No point of \(D\) is fixed by all automorphisms.
3. A function \(f:D\to D\) commuting with all permutations must be the identity.

**Proof.** For item 1, a permutation can take any element to any other. Item 2 follows by exchanging any proposed point with another. For item 3, if \(f(x)=y\ne x\), choose a third element \(z\) and a permutation fixing \(x\) while exchanging \(y,z\). Then \(f(\pi x)=y\), but \(\pi f(x)=z\), contradicting commutation. ∎

An unlabelled carrier of this kind therefore does not canonically yield an internal distinguished zero, order or nontrivial successor. A later operation may add a distinguished object on a **new** carrier, take a quotient, or introduce additional structure. N2 concerns unearned asymmetry inside the fixed original carrier, not the impossibility of every structural construction. Its holding process is a legitimate instance of the generic root, so distinctions plus bare holding dynamics cannot alone entail such an internal choice.

Two further examples separate invariance from generation and determination:

- **Invariant but not homomorphism-natural.** On each group, send \(g\) to itself if it has finite order and to the identity otherwise. Isomorphisms preserve this rule. Under the quotient \(\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), however, the generator has infinite order before the quotient and finite order afterward. Applying the rule then mapping gives zero; mapping then applying gives one. N1 fails.
- **Invariant but not definable.** Standard \((\mathbb N,0,S,+,\times)\) is rigid: an automorphism fixes every numeral by induction. Every subset is therefore invariant. Only countably many subsets are first-order definable, even with finite tuples of natural parameters, while there are uncountably many subsets. Many invariant subsets have no such definition. This applies the already established [definability/fullness distinction](MATHEMATICS-CLOSURE-RESOLUTION.md#6-three-strict-closure-levels-over-exactly-the-same-arithmetic-base) to an invariance criterion.

Even invariance and definability together do not choose one unspecified relation: both the empty and universal predicates are definable and invariant. An admissibility law must specify which role the relation is meant to realize. Naturalness without a designated role cannot substitute for that content.

## 4. Uniform unique interpretation entails explicit definability in first-order logic

Fix a classical first-order root language \(L\) and a new finite-arity predicate \(P\). Let \(T\) be a theory in \(L\cup\{P\}\). A root reduct forgets only the interpretation of \(P\); it retains the same domain and the interpretation of every symbol of \(L\).

Say that \(T\) **implicitly determines** \(P\) if each \(L\)-structure has at most one expansion satisfying \(T\). This is a uniqueness assertion, not an existence assertion. Introducing a fresh predicate \(P'\) and replacing \(P\) throughout \(T\), it is equivalently

\[
T(P)\cup T(P')\models
\forall\mathbf x\,[P(\mathbf x)\leftrightarrow P'(\mathbf x)].
\tag{B1}
\]

### Theorem B1 — Beth's definability criterion

The condition above holds if and only if some \(L\)-formula \(\theta(\mathbf x)\) satisfies

\[
T\models\forall\mathbf x\,[P(\mathbf x)\leftrightarrow\theta(\mathbf x)].
\tag{B2}
\]

This is the standard Beth theorem. Its proof obtains a definition from compactness and Craig interpolation; the converse follows because \(\theta\)'s interpretation is already fixed in the reduct. A primary research exposition gives the interpolation proof in [Parikh, *Beth definability, interpolation and language splitting*, §1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220608027_Beth_definability_interpolation_and_language_splitting). The exact at-most-one-expansion formulation and proof are also in [Benno van den Berg's model-theory notes, Definition 3.5 and Theorem 3.6](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/b.vandenberg3/Onderwijs/Model_theory_2015/modeltheory_notes.pdf). [Buss, §2.5.6](https://www.math.toronto.edu/swastik/courses/logic-S23/buss-proof-theory.pdf), also distinguishes this all-model condition from uniqueness in one selected model.

This advances the previous factorization test: under a specified logical interface, semantic uniqueness cannot remain an inexplicable extra relation. It has a uniform root-language definition. It need not have an efficient evaluation algorithm, and the theorem itself does not select the root-language interpretation or prove that its definition is a metaphysical ground.

### Corollary B1a — Total unique same-domain extension is a definitional extension

Let \(T_R\) be an \(L\)-theory and \(T\supseteq T_R\) an \(L\cup\{P\}\)-theory. Suppose **every** model of \(T_R\) has exactly one expansion satisfying \(T\). Then there is \(\theta\) as above and

\[
\operatorname{Mod}(T)
=\operatorname{Mod}\left(T_R\cup
\{\forall\mathbf x(P(\mathbf x)\leftrightarrow\theta(\mathbf x))\}\right).
\tag{B3}
\]

**Proof.** Beth gives \(\theta\). Every \(T\)-model satisfies the right side. Conversely, let a model of the right side have reduct \(M\models T_R\). By the existence premise, \(M\) has a \(T\)-expansion; by (B2) its new predicate is exactly \(\theta^M\), so it is the given expansion. ∎

Thus a total, unique, first-order interpretation adds no independent same-domain relational choice. The existence premise prevents empty fibers from disguising a restriction of admissible root worlds. New sorts, domain quotients, higher-order closure and uniqueness only up to a designated isomorphism require their own interpretation contracts; this single-predicate theorem does not automatically cover them.

## 5. A sharper all-truth boundary that does not assume effective axioms

Let \(L\) be a first-order language with a specified syntax coding. Let \(T\) be a consistent classical first-order theory in \(L\cup\{\mathsf{Tr}\}\), where \(\mathsf{Tr}\) is new. Require:

1. The arithmetic/coding part of \(T\) proves the diagonal lemma for **every \(L\)-formula** \(\psi(x)\): some \(L\)-sentence \(\lambda\) satisfies \(T\vdash\lambda\leftrightarrow\psi(\ulcorner\lambda\urcorner)\).
2. For **every \(L\)-sentence** \(\varphi\),
   \[
   T\vdash\mathsf{Tr}(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner)\leftrightarrow\varphi.
   \tag{B4}
   \]
3. \(T\) implicitly determines \(\mathsf{Tr}\) over its \(L\)-reducts in B1's sense.

The first premise is available in standard suitably coded arithmetic extensions; it is stated explicitly rather than inferred from the slogan that the language is expressive. No recursive-enumerability assumption about \(T\)'s axioms is made.

### Theorem B2 — These three conditions are inconsistent

**Proof.** Beth supplies an \(L\)-formula \(\theta(x)\) such that

\[
T\vdash\forall x\,[\mathsf{Tr}(x)\leftrightarrow\theta(x)],
\]

using classical first-order completeness to pass from semantic consequence to derivability if necessary. Apply diagonalization in \(L\) to \(\neg\theta(x)\), obtaining

\[
T\vdash\lambda\leftrightarrow\neg\theta(\ulcorner\lambda\urcorner).
\]

Because \(\lambda\) is an \(L\)-sentence, its instance of (B4), combined with the definition, also gives

\[
T\vdash\lambda\leftrightarrow\theta(\ulcorner\lambda\urcorner).
\]

Classical propositional logic yields a contradiction. ∎

This is a Beth–Tarski consequence, not a new incompleteness theorem. The diagonal step's general syntactic form is documented in [Salehi, *Tarski's Undefinability Theorem and the Diagonal Lemma*, §§1 and 3](https://www.saeedsalehi.ir/pdf/papers/IGPL-2.pdf). It requires consistency and the displayed diagonal/truth conditions, not soundness of \(T\), a recursively enumerable axiom set, or a truth-deciding algorithm.

The unusual strength is specific: dropping effective axiomatization does not rescue **uniform first-order implicit uniqueness of an exhaustive truth predicate for its entire root language**. A proposed all-truth root cannot demand all three premises while remaining consistent.

The scope is equally important:

- A richer set-theoretic root can define truth for arithmetic without satisfying (B4) for every sentence of the richer set-theoretic language. The diagonal sentence would be in that richer language; the required truth instance is absent.
- Externally fixing the standard natural numbers and its externally finite syntax can determine its truth set uniquely. The restriction to that intended structure is not the all-reduct first-order hypothesis of B1.
- Stratified satisfaction, full higher-order semantic conditions, partial truth and appropriate changes of logic are different interfaces. Their existence or adequacy needs its own argument; B2 does not refute them.
- A metaphysical root need not be an internal first-order truth predicate at all. B2 therefore does not show that mathematical truth lacks a ground or that a universal constitutive metaphysics is impossible.

This is a precise architectural restriction on one strengthened formal route. It neither weakens the previous semantic arithmetic result nor turns effective proof generation into truth determination.

## 6. A positive escape already present in the process root

The distinction between unique semantic construction and first-order definition is not merely a remote possibility. It appears in the existing process account.

Let \(E\subseteq S\times S\) be a directed event relation, and let \(E^*\) be its finite-path reachability, including zero-length paths. The [deeper-root result](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/DEEPER-GENERATIVE-ROOT.md#4-a-small-causal-presentation-with-derived-continuation) already establishes that \(E^*\) is the unique least reflexive-transitive relation containing \(E\).

### Proposition B3 — Reachability is not uniformly first-order definable from the edge relation

There is no first-order formula \(\rho(x,y)\) in the language of directed graphs which defines finite-path reachability in every graph.

**Proof.** Suppose such a formula exists. Add constants \(c,d\). Let \(\operatorname{Path}_{\le n}(c,d)\) express the existence of an edge path of length at most \(n\), including equality at length zero. Consider

\[
\{\rho(c,d)\}\cup
\{\neg\operatorname{Path}_{\le n}(c,d):n\in\mathbb N\}.
\tag{B5}
\]

Every finite subset has a model: take a finite directed chain whose endpoint distance exceeds the largest mentioned \(n\). Its endpoints are reachable, so \(\rho\) holds. Compactness gives a graph satisfying all of (B5). There \(\rho(c,d)\) holds but no finite path exists, contradicting the stipulated meaning. ∎

Thus the root's least reachability has a genuine unique construction while failing this uniform first-order formula test. There is no contradiction with Beth: ordinary first-order axioms saying “reflexive, transitive and contains \(E\)” do not enforce leastness. If a first-order theory uniformly selected exactly reachability over every graph, Beth and B3 would contradict one another.

A full second-order leastness condition does select it:

\[
R(x,y)\leftrightarrow
\forall Q\bigl((Q\text{ reflexive-transitive and }E\subseteq Q)
\Rightarrow Q(x,y)\bigr),
\tag{B6}
\]

where \(Q\) ranges over **all** binary relations on \(S\). Alternatively the metatheory can construct the inductive finite-path relation directly. These are real semantic resources, not a first-order renaming of transitivity. An externally fixed restricted family of relations needs a separate check that its intersection is the actual least closure.

This example supports a charitable strong-root programme: determinate mathematical structure can exceed one internal first-order description. It also states the bill honestly. The admissible closure and its quantifier range must be justified; claiming expressiveness alone does not establish them.

## 7. Conservative enrichment clarifies what has actually been added

Let \(T_R\) be a root theory in \(L\), and let \(T^+\) extend it in a larger language. Call the extension **model-conservative** here when every model of \(T_R\) is the reduct of at least one model of \(T^+\). This is an explicit model-extension condition; it is not silently identified with every use of the word “conservative.”

### Proposition C1 — Model-conservative enrichment cannot settle an old undecided content

If \(M,N\models T_R\) give opposite truth values to a fixed \(L\)-sentence \(\varphi\), then some \(T^+\)-models still give those opposite values to \(\varphi\).

**Proof.** Expand \(M,N\) using model conservativity. Reduct preserves interpretation of every \(L\)-formula, so their disagreement remains. ∎

Definitions may eliminate ambiguity about a newly introduced symbol without excluding old root models. They cannot, by themselves, turn a formerly undecided root-language sentence into a common truth of all admissible roots. A stronger principle that does exclude one of the old possibilities can do so, but it has made a substantive choice rather than supplied notation alone.

Likewise, adjoining unconstrained labels or an irrelevant extra predicate creates many different expansions with the same old mathematical truths. Truth preservation cannot uniquely privilege their additional ontology. Excluding surplus primitive structure is a separate explanatory or metaphysical requirement.

The result is not a general ban on learning more consequences from a better presentation: consequences can be revealed that were already implied. Nor does it require every intended mathematical subject to be every root model at once. It tracks exactly whether an enrichment changes admissible possibilities, definitions or merely our access to their consequences.

## 8. One root can support plural mathematical subjects

The positive reconstruction theorem is inherently plural: it ranges over all algebras in a declared family. It does not reduce that family to one preferred model. The reconstructed term operation is one rule with many realizations.

A universal generative interpretation can likewise be a principled scheme over mathematical contexts. Each context specifies its signature, admitted constructions, identity conditions and interpretation; each subject has its own truth relation. Where two contexts designate the same content, their stipulated comparison must preserve that content and its truth. Where groups, geometries or logics designate different subjects, disagreement about the same printed sentence is not automatically contradiction.

This may be formulated as a schema without claiming that all universes form a single set inside one of them. It remains necessary to justify which contexts are admitted, their higher closure, their shared semantic resources and their content transport. Taking every desired subject as an independently supplied input gives representational coverage; it does not yet explain why the common root constitutes all of them.

The strongest positive lesson from N1 is that some apparently additional mathematical structure is recoverable from its entire coherent family of realizations. That supplies a nontrivial model for what constitutive explanation could look like: the operation has no independent choice once the specified concrete structural role is fixed. It does not establish that every mathematical content has such an algebraic role, or that the selected family is already derived from the thin causal root.

## 9. The metaphysical argument can now be strengthened without changing its target

A charitable ultimate-constitution argument can use the following explicit steps:

1. Mathematical subjects are structural possibilities, with identity fixed by their constitutive relations and admissible realization/interpretation conditions.
2. The enriched root determines those possibilities, their identities and admissible closure principles, rather than merely encoding already given truth tables.
3. Their structural roles leave no remaining relevant choice. N1 supplies an actual algebraic example; B1 gives an additional first-order test where its hypotheses apply.
4. Interpreted truth is fixed by those subjects and semantic conditions, including higher-level conditions where needed.
5. Those subjects are constitutive modes of the root, not an independent domain merely described by it; the root also accounts for the semantic resources being used.
6. Every genuinely rival complete constitutive account reconstructs those same roles and background commitments up to independently specified equivalence, or fails an independently justified requirement.

Together, these premises would support the requested claim at the appropriate equivalence level. N1 gives more support for step 3 than simple representability did. B2–B3 identify which implementation of steps 2–4 is impossible and which is coherently available. The present work does not establish steps 1, 2, 5 and 6 for all mathematics.

The final two steps cannot be obtained just by another truth-preservation theorem. One may hold an entire mathematical structure and all its truths fixed while interpreting its ultimate ground as its abstract structural essence, as a generative modal basis, or as a more encompassing reality. Mathematical sameness alone does not decide the priority claim. This is not proof that all such philosophical accounts are equally good: they must still answer explanatory, modal and ontological objections. It identifies what evidence a mathematical reconstruction does and does not supply.

“Uniquely” should therefore concern full constitutive adequacy up to a justified equivalence of grounds, not uniqueness of notation, names, primitive symbols or intermediate explanations. Many intermediate grounds can be compatible with one ultimate ground. Merely necessary structure is also insufficient: every candidate may require distinction and composition while differing over which identities, objects or closure principles exist.

## 10. Exact verdict and validation scope

**New positive result:** for an entire specified algebraic semantics with its concrete maps, coherent operations are exactly generated operations, uniquely recoverable up to the admitted equations. For all deterministic action systems this specializes to one unique finite word. The direction from semantics back to generation is stronger than the earlier construction-only result.

**New restriction:** uniform first-order unique interpretation entails explicit root definability. Consequently a consistent theory cannot both determine an exhaustive same-level truth predicate in that way and validate every truth biconditional for a language in which the required diagonalization is available. No effective-axiom assumption is needed.

**Positive surviving route:** a plural, possibly noneffective and semantically layered generative account can still determine mathematical subjects and truth. The root's own inductive reachability is an explicit example of uniqueness that outruns a uniform first-order definition. Its closure resource must be retained as a premise, not erased by vocabulary.

**Unrestricted claim:** our current root has not been shown to be the unique ultimate constituent of every mathematical truth. This note rules out a precise formal strengthening and supplies a sharper positive reconstruction route. It does not prove that no enriched root could meet the stronger metaphysical target, and does not impose that target on the already accepted conditional foundation.
