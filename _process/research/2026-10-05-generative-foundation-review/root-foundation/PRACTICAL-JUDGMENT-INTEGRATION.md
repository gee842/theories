The affirmative argument links protection to the positive standing of actual life within G0, including contributors' self-good. Otherwise optional expansion could consistently consume the very living conditions affirmed by the shared orientation. J3 addresses actual changes of others' possibilities through undertaking, control or induced dependence, bounded by contributors' lives and jointly feasible burdens. J4 makes authority, fair allocation, timely choice and discharge accountable instead of turning favor into automatic permission.

The full theory retains four substantive rivals: unlimited continuation priority; manipulation of basic-condition thresholds; productive coercion with genuine organizational gains; and irreplaceable care with conflicting basic needs. Their actual losses and gains remain. The response narrows the priority rule, requires classification independent of its desired result, separates generative favor from authority, and allows tragic conflict without inventing replacement. Relevant counterexamples would invalidate correspondence or the proposed priority/allocation principles, not be renamed out of 生.
