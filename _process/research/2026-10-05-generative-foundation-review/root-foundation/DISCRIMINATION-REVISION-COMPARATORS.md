## Primary evidence and its exact scope

Linda McCaughey, Johannes Ziegler and Klaus Fiedler (2026), *Adapting to information search costs in sample-based decisions*, Judgment and Decision Making 21, e20. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1017/jdm.2026.10043). Read the publisher's full-text methods and results for Experiment 2 (§§4.1.2–4.2.2) and Experiments 3a/b (§§5.1.2–5.2.2), with the surrounding discussion. No supplementary data, individual records or statistical models were reanalyzed.

**Source-derived synopsis.** Participants chose how many Bernoulli observations to acquire before deciding, with specified information costs and decision payoffs. Experiment 2 compared continued outcome feedback with its withdrawal after an initial feedback block; balance information was also withheld to prevent substitute feedback. It found no evidence of the predicted difference in within-block sampling adjustment. Experiments 3a/b compared trial feedback with block summaries while each participant's cost–payoff ratio remained constant. Experiment 3b showed a feedback-format main effect on average sample size, but no significant feedback-by-trial or feedback-by-block interaction; 3a likewise did not establish the predicted sampling-adaptation effect. Exclusions included participants who sampled very little. The task, monetary consequences and sampling interface were supplied. These results concern particular feedback contrasts, not every possible consequence-learning mechanism. [Primary methods and results](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/adapting-to-information-search-costs-in-samplebased-decisions/954EF745FC16B0139B12063BE4F78A5E).

## Our methodological inference

This is useful adverse evidence because **changed sampling behavior does not, by itself, identify what changed in the process producing it**. More or fewer observations could arise from different current evidence, a revised estimate of evidence quality, familiarity, fatigue, changed expectations, or a changed criterion for stopping. Those alternatives can produce similar downstream counts. An intervention on feedback can help discriminate them, but a nonsignificant contrast is not a demonstration of exact equivalence, absence of all learning, or impossibility of criterion revision.

The relevant target must therefore be specified independently of the outcome count. Is the changed organization selecting an informative experiment, changing which histories count as distinct, changing confidence under a fixed rule, or changing the continuation that inquiry is meant to support? All can matter. They are not interchangeable explanations.

The comparison does not refute the repository's synthetic mechanism. Nor can that mechanism explain the human findings merely because both involve feedback and inquiry. The empirical task and the constructed learner have different interfaces and identified causal variables. A connection requires a defended correspondence, not shared terminology.

## Why the acquired-suffix intervention is more diagnostic

The proposed extension retains the existing observation-table learner and its **all-future behavior contract**. It tests whether acquired suffix information in \(E\) actually participates in the row distinctions and subsequent inquiry. Its conventional algorithmic ancestry is already recorded in the [acquisition comparison](ABSTRACTION-ACQUISITION-COMPARATORS.md); adding a diagnostic intervention is not a claim to have invented active automata learning.

The useful contrast preserves the acquired table and suffix records while interrupting their specified reader connection. If the faithful reader uses the acquired suffix to distinguish histories and the disconnected reader fails to do so, with the remaining relevant conditions matched, the comparison identifies that connection's contribution. The evidence is then more specific than a correlation between a growing table and a changing query count. Merely deleting the evidence, supplying a different counterexample, changing the target law, or replacing the downstream learner would answer different questions.

This remains a conditional identification proposal until the actual implementation and traces establish it. The diagnostic record should distinguish:

1. **What was acquired:** the observations and counterexample that formed the retained suffix, without an auditor's hidden target state entering the learner.
2. **What was read:** the suffix information available to the faithful reader and the exact interruption in the comparison.
3. **What changed because of that reading:** row discrimination, selected queries, hypothesis or certification status, rather than only a final success label.
4. **What stayed at issue:** the same all-future contract, target assumptions and complete conformance requirement. A correct next output must not replace that stronger undertaking.

If the intervention also changes timing, resource use or other contacts, those changes must enter the interpretation; an efficiency conclusion needs its corresponding comparison. If the disconnected path later recovers through other acquired information, that is evidence of an alternative route. It does not erase the interrupted route's earlier contribution. Conversely, failure to observe a difference can reflect redundancy or an uninformative fixture, rather than establish that retained suffixes never matter.

## Contribution and remaining dependency

The proposed result can establish **acquired distinctions becoming operative in the continuing selection of discrimination** under the unchanged learner. That is a real causal connection even if an encompassing algorithm describes the entire history. The intervention can locate where the retained consequence participates without treating the record, reader and target as fundamentally independent beings.

It must not be described as acquiring the all-future goal itself. Revising which distinctions are necessary under that goal differs from revising the goal, the general stopping standard or its warrant. The [sufficiency comparison](INQUIRY-SUFFICIENCY-COMPARATORS.md) already distinguishes those tasks. The specific reader, update law and evidential contract remain locally supplied unless their formation is separately explained; that status neither places them outside generative activity nor invalidates the result within its scope.

No universal ethical conclusion, empirical superiority or newly acquired favorable purpose follows from this comparison. The useful next evidence is the actual acquired-record → reader → changed-discrimination chain, including its failures and alternative routes.
