## Strongest candidate found: actual reciprocal food delivery

**Schneeberger, Dietz & Taborsky (2012), “Reciprocal cooperation between unrelated rats depends on cost to donor and benefit to recipient.”** Fourteen focal Norway rats experienced particular cooperative or noncooperative partners before roles reversed. The experiment varied tray resistance from 1–5 N, with trained visual workload cues. Pulling frequency and first-pull latency were recorded. A completed pull made one oat flake accessible to the partner alone; the acting rat received no immediate pulling reward during the test. Both animals received food after the preceding experience phase. Cooperative and noncooperative partner identities remained fixed. A separate experiment varied recipient hunger and body mass at constant 1 N. Reported helping varied with partner experience and cost. [Primary article, especially Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2148-12-41).

**Verified scope:** measured resistance and performed actions; experienced information about a particular beneficiary; physical food access rather than nominal points or an unshocked confederate. **Missing:** measured metabolic expenditure, independently logged ingestion or subsequent recipient welfare, identity-specific reversal, and jointly crossed cost × need × information validity. No raw-data deposit or supplement was located on the inspected publisher/PMC pages or targeted search. This is not evidence that none exists. [Primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2148-12-41).

The appropriate interpretation is an empirical example of the **physical interface**, not a complete identification of our proposed mechanism. Food availability, ingestion, physiological benefit and flourishing are different dependent variables. A force in newtons is not energy in joules; converting it to physical work would require displacement, and converting that to metabolic expenditure would require further measurement. No such conversion should be invented for the repository's fuel ledger.

## Stronger memory test, but incomplete reproducibility package

**Kettler, Schweinfurth & Taborsky (2021), “Rats show direct reciprocity when interacting with multiple partners.”** Focal rats encountered four partners across four days, then could provision one previous partner. The study records recipient food-reward counts and action latency, contrasting specific-partner history with the most recent experience of any partner. The “defector” apparatus was blocked during experience: nonreceipt does not establish a freely chosen refusal. The task moves an oat flake to the other compartment; its supplement describes receipt before platform retraction/reloading. It does not measure a force gradient. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-82526-4), [apparatus/training supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-021-82526-4/MediaObjects/41598_2021_82526_MOESM2_ESM.docx).

The linked [R analysis file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-021-82526-4/MediaObjects/41598_2021_82526_MOESM3_ESM.r) was inspected, not executed. It loads `Mastersheet Nina Kaya Kettler.csv`, with focal/partner identity, partner role, prior partner pulls, test count and latency fields. That CSV was not among the publisher's three linked supplements: PDF, DOCX and R. The DOCX contains apparatus/training material, not the master data. Therefore **shared analysis code does not yet mean a runnable public reanalysis**.

Inspection receipts: R, 13,514 bytes, SHA256 `8efc003c0853562c43e999104b3dd66a0cf58ed5f13f658d3c02edef02b1b524`; DOCX, 1,176,277 bytes, SHA256 `3e3c9dbb4c45063e34c715fbca3b2a708ca718e72605fd28b76ed68cabc6b742`. Neither original was imported into this repository.

## A useful adverse comparator already exists

**Gerber, Schweinfurth & Taborsky (2020), “The smell of cooperation.”** The researchers manipulated olfactory access and routed odor from a cooperating rat in another room to a focal rat facing a noncooperative recipient. This increased actual food provisioning to that nearby recipient. Consequently, helpful action can follow a cue whose source identity differs from the beneficiary. The result cautions against treating observed partner-conditioned helping as identification of a correct recipient-specific inference. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7739497/).

Its [Dryad dataset manifest](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.h18931zjd) lists `experiment1.csv` through `experiment4.csv` and `Readme.rtf`, totaling 24.77 KB. The public API metadata and exact response hashes are now [preserved](reason-action-evidence/README.md). Original file downloads returned HTTP 403 (website) or 401 (API). Public HTML preview supplied a complete-looking experiment4 table, transcribed as a separately hashed reading edition; experiment3's preview truncated in row 74. Original CSV bytes and their manifest hashes remain unverified.

The [bounded retrospective check](reason-action-evidence/preview-check.json) verifies all 48 displayed rows, 24 paired focal identities and exact transcription of the DOM cell strings. Raw pull totals are 21 for neutral odor and 53 for cooperating odor; paired counts rise for 17 rats, fall for four and tie for three. The article's experiment4 results report `n=23`, whereas the preview has 24 pairs; that analysis-set discrepancy remains unresolved. No exclusion was invented, no p-value replicated, and no latency inference made. In this experiment the cue provider and food recipient differ, with the cue provider's identity held fixed across conditions. The finding therefore challenges identification of recipient-specific reasoning from helping alone. These data belong to the 2020 study; they do not supply the missing 2012 cost data or 2021 master table. [Primary methods and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7739497/).

## What this changes in our evidence ledger

The prior human examples leave actual recipient benefit unverified. This candidate family provides a materially closer route: physical work can deliver a real resource to a different organism, conditional on experienced social information. It still does not measure the complete concern → warranted command → funded execution → recipient renewal chain.

The conventional comparator should be **recipient-indexed associative learning or a reciprocity policy with an effort cost**, accompanied by a cue-triggered policy that need not bind cue identity to recipient identity. Generalized reciprocity and immediate social-cue responses are further comparators. These are candidate explanatory models, not fitted results from this audit. Do not present a favorable aggregate donation count as discriminating among them.

The useful identification question is whether a model predicts **who receives help when identity, current need, cue source and action efficacy come apart**, with performed work and delivered resource recorded separately. The 2020 manipulation supplies an existing mismatch condition; it may favor a simpler cue mechanism over a correctly bound recipient inference. Even successful matching would establish behavioral specificity, not a normative justification or universal favorable direction.

**Recommendation:** retain the 2012 study as the strongest verified physical-cost/delivery candidate, the 2021 study as a specific-memory comparator, and the 2020 preview calculation as a bounded adverse comparison pending original-data verification and resolution of the analysis set. Do not claim a complete public dataset jointly measures all required quantities. No human candidate satisfying the full conjunction was verified in this bounded search. Existing source work can proceed without starting another study or assuming that biological reciprocity already supplies wisdom.
