# Twentieth review, lens B: redundancy in the Lean core

Reviewer B. Head 121ff8042, which is the same source as 831fcaf73 for the core. Read-only. Scripts and their outputs are in `scripts/B/` (large regenerable outputs not kept):
- `parse.py`: 15,992 declarations, including 11,678 theorems. Its theorem names match all 11,647 spine theorems in THEOREM-CONNECTIVITY.json. The 31 extra are `FallibleInquiry`, which is outside the spine.
- `dups.py`, `defdups.py`: duplicate and near-duplicate statements and definitions.
- `names.py`, `collide.py`: namespaces and collisions.
- `wrappers.py`, `forward.py`: one-line restatements.
- `witness.py`: witness models.
- `sizes.py`: module size and cohesion.

Core path below: `experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/becoming/Becoming/` (abbreviated `B/`).

## 1. Summary

1. **Few statements are redundant, but the modules and namespaces are fragmented.** About 4% of theorems could be deleted without losing a statement: duplicate statements, pure forwarders and the special cases in parallel developments, roughly 450–500 of 11,678. About a third of the modules could be folded into others (≈100 of 297) with no statement lost. A shared witness library could replace about a fifth of the 490 closed witness models with instances. Lines that could go: an estimated 8–12k of 166k.
2. **(major) Several central notions are defined more than once, which breaks "one notion, one name".**
   - The deterministic `Law.*` vocabulary (Recurrence, Attraction) rebuilds the process vocabulary `Branching.*`: 23 shared names, joined by ten `law_*_iff` bridges.
   - `Returns` has four unrelated definitions, `Lossless` and `Merges` three each, and `Settles`/`Rests`/`RestsFrom` seven definitions for at least four notions.
   - The law read through a map is defined twice (`Image` and `Readout.image`) with no bridge between them.
   - The bisimulation and branching-test theory is developed twice: `Labelled`/`Likeness`, then `Composition` for joint frames.
3. **(major) The two-family base produces a layer of reading modules.** There are 53 modules with the suffixes `Runs`, `Frames`, `Worlds`, `Webs`, `Flows` or `Traces`: ≈11.0k lines and 751 theorems.
   - 181 of those theorems are one-liners and 89 only forward another theorem. Some modules say so themselves: `PresentationFrames` reads "none is a further result".
   - The connectivity measure rewards writing them.
4. **(moderate) The same statement is proved again under a new name.** When a later general law covers an earlier case, the connection usually landed as a second theorem with the same statement, not as a new proof of the old one. About 20 such pairs are verified, for example `falls_not_recurring` and `whole_falls_not_recurring`, and `World.arising_iff_ceasing` stated three times.
5. **(moderate) Prelude support lemmas are scattered and re-proved.**
   - 494 theorems state only Nat/List/Bool facts. They sit in 116 modules; only 91 are in the support modules.
   - 57 of these statements are proved 2–5 times each, 79 redundant copies in all.
6. **(moderate) Namespaces collide and witnesses are rebuilt.**
   - 11 namespace collisions go beyond the nineteenth review's N1–N3: `Crossing`, `Cycle`, `Loop`, `Copying`, `Settling`, `Parting`, `Fork`, `Circulation`, `Halving`, plus twin models in `Accretion` and `Passing`/`Shuttle`.
   - Identical witness models are rebuilt: the free process 4×, the empty process 3×, the empty flow 3×, the renewer-and-two-takers model 3×, and the unit carried back and forth at least 3×.

## 3. What is sound and should be kept

- **Statement-level redundancy is low.** For a 166k-line, 300-module core grown by many sessions, ≈4% removable statements is small. Most one-liners are real instances: a law at a named witness states a fact about the model.
- **Exactness witnesses keep their second witness** (`*_exact` theorems), and every calibration (LPO, WLPO, excluded middle) is a single theorem.
- **Some connections are done the right way and are the model for B1/B5/B9**: `Dependence.conditions_alikeAt_iff` (fixed coupling as the instance of the changing one), `withinClosureOf_single` (SelectionPool's single pool gives Selection's law), `Standing.standing_iff_counting`, and Resolution reusing `Shuttle` (`open Shuttle`) instead of rebuilding it.
- **The support modules exist.** `Naturals` and `ListTests` (proved by recursion where the prelude uses `propext`) are the right place; B12 only asks that they be used.
- **The apart register is honest.** It already labels its restatements (16 "restates" roots, with reasons naming the base theorem), which made B1 and B9 easy to confirm.
- **The parser matched the spine exactly** (11,647 of 11,647 names). The core's declarations are regular enough for mechanical refactoring to be safe.

## 4. What I could not check

- **No Lean was run.** Duplicates were found by normalizing statement text: binder names renamed by position, namespace prefixes dropped to the last component.
  - This produces false positives where dotted names on different witnesses collapse. Examples: `World.*_finite` (11 statements), `byOccurrence_dissipative` vs `roles_dissipative`, `Run.withClock_part` vs `ofWithMove_toWithMove`, `MatchedCourses.a_matches_b1`.
  - It produces false negatives where statements differ only in argument order or implicitness.
  - The ≈20 pairs in B9 were verified by reading. The group counts are upper bounds for "same statement" and lower bounds for "same up to instance".
- **Trivial specialization was not decided by unification**, only by the proof shape (one application of another theorem, args only the theorem's own binders). B10's 279 is a lower bound for forwarders and says nothing about statements that are specializations proved independently.
- **"Restatement module" is judged by proof shape and docstrings.** Whether a reading module carries content the source lacks, such as a new witness or exactness, was checked only for PresentationFrames, BoundaryFlows, the census modules and LawThroughChangingCoupling.
