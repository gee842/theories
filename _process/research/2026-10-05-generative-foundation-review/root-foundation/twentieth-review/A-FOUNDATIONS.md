## 1. Summary

1. **Two bases, joined by theorems. Neither is defined from the other.** The README and `Becoming.lean` say the labelled process and the frame "enter as readings" of `Becoming.World`. In the definitions, `Generative.Process`/`Trace`/`Run` (GenerativeCore, Frame) and `World`/`Web`/`Flow`/`Exchange` (World.lean) are independent. They meet only in the 163 statements that use both. The identification is exact in one direction only: a `World` *is* a run of the law it shows, with configurations `WebAt` and moves `Exchange`, and every identity is `rfl` (core `Runs.lean:120–146`). A general process run reaches a world only through `Frame.world`, which keeps activity and drops levels and flows. So the World family is, definitionally, a special case of the Run family. That is the opposite of the README's direction. The prose names three different roots: World (README:20, `Becoming.lean`, §9), the run (`Runs.lean:13,55`), and "the readings in context" (ch:220, D23). The study leaves "whether one is defined from the other" open, "awaits Ren" (study `BECOMING-RENEWAL-READING.md:6002`), and since D45 that wait has no owner.
2. **The same record is defined five times.** The five-field dependence record (level, active, requires, need, draws) exists as `RelationalRenewal.Unfolding`, `Conditions`, `Dependence`, `Web` and `WebAt`. The exchange record exists twice (`Flow`, `Exchange`). `Web F C` has exactly the fields of `Dependence Nat F C`, and `World.web_along` is `rfl`. Making each copy an abbreviation or instance of one indexed record turns the world-side records into the run-side reading (index = step) by definition. That one change makes the two families reach each other.
3. **The layers are hand-labelled and the import graph does not follow them.** Measured on the import graph:
   - The graph has 12 roots and a longest chain of 30 modules.
   - 108 of the 296 core modules (36%) are leaves imported only by the root `Becoming`.
   - 59 modules (≈12k lines, 46 of them leaves) are "…Runs/…Webs/…Worlds/…Traces/…Frames" companion modules. They exist because of the two-base rule of W2.
   - Two hand tables give the layers. The README's covers 273 of the 296 core modules; the REFERENCE table that §9 cites as complete covers 200.
   - Five import edges point upward.
   - 138 modules import the ethical modules (`Metaethics`, `Significance`); about 108 of them never use that vocabulary. The cause is 13 conditional theorems in `Interdependence`/`Feedback`.
4. **Basic notions live in the wrong place and are duplicated.**
   - `Determined`, used by 33 modules, is defined in `Joint`.
   - The kernel-respecting notions (`GoAlike`, `DependsOnlyOn`, `Lumped`, `LumpedOn`, `LumpedWithin`, `GoAlikeAt`, `Law.SameFuture`) all exist, but the general form `GoAlikeUnder` is defined *after* its special case, in `Likeness`.
   - Determined laws (`next : S → S`, Recurrence) have their own `Reachable`/`Returns`/`KeepsPossibilities`, parallel to Branching's.
   - Shared arithmetic exists twice (`Naturals` and the root namespace of `Dependence`).
   - 89 prelude-only statement shapes are proved in two or more modules (213 theorems).
5. **The minimal base is small.** One law (`Process`/`Trace`), one history/run, one indexed standing record, one exchange record and one prelude library state the whole spine. The rest becomes abbreviations, instances, or modules that fold back. The cost is mostly mechanical Lean refactoring with one fresh checker run, plus a docs pass now.

**Two presentations instead?** *Judgment:* an argument exists for keeping two presentations with an equivalence: record-style (World/Web/Flow: amounts per step, no law given) versus law-style (Process: admissible steps). That duality is real; `Becoming.Histories` proves laws up to dead ends correspond to splice-closed families of histories. But the core's own equivalence is `rfl` in the World→Run direction, so the "two presentations" differ only by currying and a type parameter. An equivalence that holds by `rfl` is better written as a definition. What remains a genuine duality is history versus law (`Shown`/`shown_le_iff`, `DeadEnd.no_history`, `Splice.spliced`), and that sits inside the one base. Keeping two definitional roots costs A3's 59 companion modules and the 163-statement meeting zone, and it costs the prose three incompatible claims about the root.

## 4. What is sound and should be kept

- **The pure-core discipline.** GenerativeCore is small (343 lines) and clean: composition, product and transport through `Trace`. Every core identity relating the two families that was checked here (`World.run_world`, `Run.world_webAt/move`, `World.web_along`, `balancedRun_world`) is definitional. That is what makes A1/A2 cheap.
- **The history–law correspondence** (`Shown`, `shown_le_iff`, `DeadEnd.no_history`, `Splice.spliced`, `exists_law_iff`). It is the right core of "law read off the unfolding" and the natural first laws of a history-rooted base.
- **Existing unifying bridges** worth keeping as the pattern: `EdgeClosure.Closure` defined through `Reach`, with `withinClosure_iff_closure`; `trace_ofPartial_iff`; `goAlike_iff_lumped`; `Web.static` with its six equivalences; `GoAlikeUnder` as a general schema.
- **The theorem-connectivity measure.** It is honest and exactly what exposed the two-family structure; its `families_reach_each_other` flag is the right acceptance test for A1/A2.
- W2's achievement: World-side laws need no frame. That stays true under a run-rooted base, because the frame is `Process` plus claims, and the world side needs only histories.
