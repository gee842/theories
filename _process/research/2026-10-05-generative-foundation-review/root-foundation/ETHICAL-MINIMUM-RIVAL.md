# Ethical minimum: which rival actually denies it?

6 October 2026. AI-authored independent review for the connection **an actual patient's adverse condition → an objective consideration favoring an eligible stranger's relief**, within Ren's continuing goal of a universal ethical minimum compatible with plural worthwhile lives. This note builds on the [practical-uptake argument](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-ARGUMENT.md), its [counterreview](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-COUNTERREVIEW.md), and the canonical [unified grounding](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md) and [strongest-claim target](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/STRONGEST-FOUNDATION-CLAIM.md). It adds a conceptual discrimination and bounded primary reading; no empirical result, formal proof or completed ethical foundation is claimed.

**Finding:** a strong motivation-sensitive account can accept objective, actor-directed moral claims even where it denies the actor a reason in its distinctive rational sense. Such an account does not defeat the ethical minimum merely by denying that additional rational status. The remaining opponent of the minimum must reject the patient's contribution to the moral justification of this actor's action, rather than only deny successful persuasion, decisive duty or coercive authority. Agreement among the compared accounts supports a precise shared position; it does not prove that position universally necessary.

## 1. Identify the practical claim before comparing accounts

The target case contains an actual patient, a severe unwanted aversive episode, an informed actor and an available intervention that would relieve that same episode through a warranted permissible means. The actor did not cause the condition or undertake its care. The patient's identified benefit and the act's efficacy do not depend on the actor approving either. Permission, costs, competing claims and relevant limits of understanding must be assessed independently; calling an act *eligible* cannot hide its disputed moral justification.

The proposed minimum says more than that the patient's episode is bad for the patient, that compassion would fit it, or that relief happens to be allowed. It says:

> What this act would do for this patient gives a genuine moral consideration in favor of this actor performing it, independently of the actor's antecedent ends. A change only in those ends does not erase that consideration.

This is an AI formulation of the research proposal, not Ren's quotation. Its practical content includes identifying a feature of the available act that speaks for choosing it; rejecting suppression of an observer's distress as a substitute for patient relief; and revising the proposed act when its supposed benefit proves absent. Calling the contribution moral does not make it a recommendation only for people who volunteer to use an optional moral scoring convention. The proposed claim concerns the actual actor's conduct, including conduct by someone who rejects that judgment.

The account must nevertheless distinguish at least these questions:

| Question | What an affirmative answer adds |
|---|---|
| Patient good | Relief improves something for this actual subject. |
| Fittingness | A patient-directed attitude appropriately answers that subject's condition. |
| Moral favor | The outcome gives a moral justification for this actor's eligible act. |
| Rational authority in the restrictive sense | The consideration belongs to what can rationally address this particular actor under a defended account of practical reasoning. |
| Permission | The act is allowable under the relevant constraints. |
| Duty | The actor is required to act, after the relevant assessment. |
| Enforcement | Someone has justified authority to constrain or compel action. |

These are different relations, not rungs of a single ladder. A beneficial aspect can occur in a prohibited intervention. A duty need not authorize every method of enforcement. A permitted sacrifice may have a patient-grounded justification while remaining optional. A morally justified act can fail to persuade its prospective operator. None of those separations establish the minimum by themselves.

## 2. What the strongest inspected internalist actually denies

**Manne's patient-grounded account.** In *Locating Morality* (2017), pp. 19–23, Manne distinguishes moral claims addressed to agents from reasons passing an additional motivation-based constraint. Claims can still apply when that constraint fails, and ignoring them can remain bad; denying an animal's moral standing can still be mistaken. The denied addition is a distinctive rational demand. This is stronger than admitting only a condition bad for a patient: the claim is directed at conduct. The passage therefore permits convergence on universal moral relevance alongside disagreement about rational authority. [Author-hosted corrected proof](https://www.katemanne.net/uploads/7/3/8/4/73843037/locating_morality_moral_imperatives_as_b.pdf).

**Manne's defense of the restriction.** *Internalism about Reasons: Sad but True?* (2014), especially pp. 96–109 and 111–113, links practical reasons to considerations apt within idealized interpersonal advice. Complete motivational nonreceptivity can defeat that status. The account preserves other moral criticism and acknowledges that its normativity may be expressed with different terminology if the distinction between bad and unreasonable conduct remains substantive. It does not reduce all normativity to successful persuasion. Its explicit argument at p. 109 depends on the further premise that appropriate reason-giving would obtain uptake under ideal conditions. [Author-hosted journal article](https://www.katemanne.net/uploads/7/3/8/4/73843037/internalism_about_reasons_-_km.pdf).

**Foot's useful earlier separation.** In *Morality as a System of Hypothetical Imperatives* (1972), pp. 307–315, Foot distinguishes the application of moral judgments from their rational force. She also allows genuinely other-directed virtues grounded in concern for other people, without reducing them to reputation or private pleasure. Her position in this article is useful against the assumption that all motivation-sensitive explanations are egoistic, or that recognizing a moral judgment automatically supplies the disputed rational force. This note does not describe it as her settled later position. [Primary article facsimile, text-extracted](https://makowski.wdfiles.com/local--files/wprowadzenie-do-analizy-metaetycznej/foot-hip.pdf).

The resulting adjudication is precise. If a rival accepts the moral favor stated in §1 for the actor without antecedent concern, it shares that part of the ethical minimum. We must not manufacture disagreement by translating its restriction on rational authority into a denial of all moral favor. Conversely, replacing the disputed rational relation with a moral relation does not refute its restriction. The larger [strongest-foundation investigation](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/STRONGEST-FOUNDATION-CLAIM.md) continues to seek that stronger result.

This is not a purely verbal resolution. The accounts can agree on the actual relief as morally supported, the persistence of that support under indifference, and the possibility of moral error, while disagreeing whether complete nonreceptivity makes the actor unreasonable in disregarding it. That latter disagreement can affect practices of advice and answerability. It should remain visible without being treated as a refutation of the shared moral claim.

Nor is shared terminology enough. An account that accepts only *our approval of* the actor's action, an optional evaluative convention, or a fitting wish while denying any moral contribution to the act has not accepted §1. Agreement must concern what favors the same act for the same patient and why, including the indifferent-actor comparison.

## 3. Removing a helping intention does not isolate the rival

Consider a capable actor who has no settled plan to help this patient. It may nevertheless care about relieving distress, treating persons fairly, living by defensible judgments, retaining trust, or understanding what follows from its commitments. An internalist can find a sound deliberative route from such broader concerns. It can consequently accept many stranger-rescue judgments and patient-sensitive corrections without treating the reason as dependent on a currently explicit helping intention.

There are three distinct scope claims:

1. All actual actors in a sampled population happen to have some suitable route.
2. Every actor with certain independently warranted capacities necessarily has such a route.
3. Moral favor applies even where no such route exists.

The first is an empirical possibility, not motivation-independent necessity. The second needs a substantive explanation of why the admitted capacities supply the route; defining adequate rationality as accepting universal concern is insufficient. The third is the candidate minimum and can survive rejection of an additional universal rational-reason claim.

The discriminating conceptual contrast therefore removes every suitable route, not merely one helping motive. Compare two operators with the same information, actual control, permissible means and identified patient. The first has a suitable motivational route to relief. The second is stipulated to lack every such route while retaining the other capacities. Ask separately whether relief is morally supported, whether the actor has the more restrictive rational reason, whether nonintervention is permissible, and whether intervention may be compelled.

The stipulation does not establish that such total nonreceptivity is possible for a fully specified kind of actor. Nor can an experiment establish it by observing a few refusals. It defines the conditional contrast on which the accounts disagree. If the proposed combination is impossible, that impossibility must be established by an independently warranted account of agency or experience. An engineered system trained to reject all appeals only instantiates a selected mechanism; it does not settle the full modal question.

A generic desire to do whatever there is reason to do does not itself explain which considerations qualify. If it is used as the common route, the justification of the patient-ground still has to occur elsewhere. Likewise, a desire for factual truth does not by itself supply a practical desire to realize every represented benefit. Neither fact makes such general dispositions irrelevant; it prevents them from completing the ground by a verbal shortcut.

## 4. A live challenge to the rational restriction

An independent reason to respect an actor's freedom supports refraining from coercion and acknowledging what persuasion can accomplish. It does not immediately support a veto over every consideration favoring that actor's conduct. The actor can remain entitled to decide while having a consideration it does not acknowledge; conversely, a duty or an enforceable claim would require separate grounds.

This gives the strongest positive pressure against the restrictive account. We can explain an act's moral justification by the real patient outcome, even when addressing that explanation to its actor has no prospect of changing conduct. A third party's truthful assessment need not be an attempted intervention on the actor. If the account accepts this, inability to persuade has not removed the minimum. If it denies the explanation's further rational status, its restriction must be defended at that further level.

The disputed argument thus contains an additional step: because an ideal practice of reasoning aims at uptake, a consideration is apt in that practice only if ideal uptake would succeed. A practice's aim does not establish every constitutive condition of its correctness. For example, a truthful record can have warranted content even when it will never persuade its subject. This analogy does not prove that reasons have the same structure as records; it identifies the explanatory choice that needs defense. A constitutive account of reasons may legitimately differ. It cannot obtain that difference solely from the fact that unsuccessful persuasion ceases to be useful.

The externalist positive judgment is that the patient-ground can give the actor a reason even when access fails, and that reason-recognition is assessed by the ground. The internalist's positive judgment is that actor reasons are constituted partly by an appropriate interpersonal accessibility relation, while other moral claims survive. Both are substantive. The comparison removes a false inference from conversational failure to no moral favor; it does not prove that every inaccessible moral claim has the additional rational authority.

## 5. The opponent still threatening the ethical minimum

The serious remaining exclusion claim says:

> Even though the patient is genuinely suffering and this act would permissibly relieve it, that consequence contributes nothing to the moral justification of this stranger's action unless a further self, role or motivational relation is present.

That is a real rival to §1. It needs a defense of *zero moral relevance*, not just a defense of extra duties to particular people, optional generosity, motivational limits, or freedom from commands. A person may have a stronger responsibility to their own child without other children counting for nothing. A personal project can outweigh some aid without making its beneficiary's good irrelevant. These are affirmative distinctions about grounds and priority, not a proof that all restrictions fail.

The affirmative case remains the direct patient-ground argument. Effective relief answers the very adverse condition identified as bad for this patient. The corresponding empty act does not. The actor's opportunity makes the distinction relevant to an available choice. This gives the proposal explanatory substance before a full ranking or a demand for sacrifice. The further patient-to-act favoring judgment remains normative and defended; it is not extracted from causal control alone.

Neither the inspected motivational restriction nor the ordinary defenses of special duties supplies a positive argument for the exclusion claim above. That narrows the present dispute. It does **not** show there can be no adequate exclusion account, and it does not constitute independent corroboration each time this same affirmative argument appears in another repository file.

## 6. Plural worthwhile lives need an additional positive argument

The existence of a contributory minimum does not entail genuine latitude. Two opposed structures can preserve every baseline consideration: one can require every life to maximize total relief whenever physically possible; another can permit several substantially different lives under defensible limits. The minimum by itself does not choose between them. This is a conceptual non-entailment observation, not a proof that either structure is ethically correct.

The problem appears even without numbers. Repeatedly introducing a new person who could benefit could make every remaining hour answerable to a new demand. Merely writing *pro tanto* or *not a maximizing theory* does not explain why worthwhile personal commitments may legitimately continue. Ordinary wishes or any chosen project cannot be immunized automatically either: cruel projects remain subject to others' claims.

A strong pluralist continuation should therefore defend why differentiated subjects have positive grounds for self-direction, relationships, rest, cultural creation and other worthwhile commitments, and how these grounds sometimes sustain multiple permissible life courses. It should specify which serious burdens nevertheless override that latitude. This can use the same commitment to actual differentiated subjects as the concern argument, but the extension needs justification. The actor's status as a patient is a source of relevant considerations, not an automatic all-purpose exemption.

At minimum, report three results separately: universal inclusion in moral consideration; warranted constraints against some treatment; and a positively justified range of permissible lives. None entails a single reward number, and the first alone does not prove the third. Compatibility should eventually mean more than the absence of an explicit contradiction: the account should identify real kinds of plural life that its own reasons permit.

## 7. Research verdict and next connection

This round changes the target of the decisive criticism. The strongest inspected motivation-sensitive account cannot be cited as denying all objective moral relevance merely because it restricts rational reasons. That correction reduces a false burden on establishing the ethical minimum while preserving the stronger rational and generative questions.

The present positive conclusion is a defended moral baseline, compatible with several accounts of reasons. Its universal necessity and constitutive derivation remain unproved. The next central work should test the independent grounding of zero moral relevance for the eligible stranger, and defend positive latitude using the actual goods and claims of differentiated subjects. A study of formation and revision can then ask whether acquired dispositions track those independently justified grounds; training success must not certify the standard it was trained to implement.

**Remaining supplied premises:** actual patient and burden identification; warranted benefit, efficacy, permission and comparison; the patient-to-act moral favoring judgment; and any stronger ground for rational authority. Patient coverage beyond the examined experiential case and general permission/priority rules remain substantive work. The accepted conditional generative foundation retains its original completion scope.

**Verdict: no blocking inference or goal substitution was found at the draft's declared scope.** The document retains an action-directed moral contribution independent of the operator's ends, rather than retreating to a claim about the patient's private good. It expressly leaves universally binding rational authority and constitutive generative grounding under investigation. Its reading of the inspected primary distinction is accurate: the action-directed moral claim survives the motivation-sensitive restriction. The disagreement is not dismissed as merely verbal, and agreement is not treated as proof of the common premise. The draft's 2017 section reference, §§1.2.5–1.3, was checked against the source headings.

The draft also respects the gap between counting the helper's good and granting it priority. It recognizes that a maximizing account can count personal projects while demanding their sacrifice. Its cumulative-resource illustration establishes conditional joint infeasibility under a supplied protected requirement; it does not infer that the requirement is justified or that the next rescue must be refused. The draft treats positive grounds for latitude and burden-sensitive requirements as unfinished work, so the whole user objective remains active.

Two scope boundaries accompany this acceptance. First, *objective* in the shared moral minimum concerns independence from the actor's ends, private benefit and role; it is not independence from the patient's actual experience or all subjective conditions. Second, the broad formulation about genuinely adverse conditions is a proposal. The directly developed case concerns severe unwanted experiential burdens; extension to every deprivation, frustrated preference or alleged interest needs its own patient-good argument. The coordinator was asked to make that immediate domain explicit. Neither boundary defeats the proposed minimum; both prevent the breadth of its wording from being mistaken for accomplished coverage.
