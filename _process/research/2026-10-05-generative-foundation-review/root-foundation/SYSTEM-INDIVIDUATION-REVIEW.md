## 1. What the systems route can advance

The strongest proposal begins with independently identified relationships and asks which distinctions, combinations and continuations they sustain. A system boundary then articulates a real explanatory dependence at a stated resolution and time span. It need not be a membrane, an isolated substance, a self-maintaining organism or a fixed membership list. Formation, maintenance, replacement, interaction and dissolution become questions about how these relations occur and change.

This improves the target when the former phrase “organization forms” was read as requiring creation of new organization in every episode. A static arrangement, a finite chain of operations and externally supported activity remain eligible. It does not establish a universal law by changing the name of the explanandum.

Three uses must be kept distinct without treating them as separate realities:

| Use of “system” | What warrants the use | What does not follow alone |
|---|---|---|
| A collection selected for investigation | An explicit question and identifiable referents | Internal causal integration or autonomy |
| An effective system for specified questions | An adequate relation between the retained description and the investigated possibilities | A uniquely privileged physical boundary, identity or mechanism |
| An integrated organization | Evidence for the identified interdependence, joint operation, constraint or maintenance relationship | Perpetual renewal, independent existence, learning or ethical goodness |

An uncoupled collection can be a legitimate mathematical system. Its treatment as a system does not establish the richer integration claim. Conversely, failure of autonomous reduction does not make an open organization unreal. The existing [systems chapter](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/SYSTEMS-COMPLEXITY-CHAOS.md) already establishes exact projection and autonomous-law conditions, assembly effects and regime-sensitive feedback. Reusing those results is appropriate; repeating them under the name “systemhood” would not be a new foundational derivation.

## 2. Substantive rival tests

These are proposed explanatory comparisons, not new formal counterexamples. Each preserves the phenomenon it challenges and specifies what evidence would discriminate it.

**Common drive versus internal integration.** A set of observed processes may vary together because they share a driving condition. A rival can retain the measured histories while contesting an alleged mutual dependence among those processes. To resolve that contest, identify operative connections, controlled changes where available, and consequences of interrupted or replaced connections. Synchrony by itself does not settle the attribution. The larger driven organization may still be a system; the rival challenges the proposed narrower relation rather than systemhood altogether.

**Static, transient and externally supported cases.** A structural assembly can constrain possible displacement without continually rebuilding itself. A finite succession can have an organized dependence and then end. A maintained laboratory preparation can depend on actual human work, power, atmospheric exchange or replenishment. These cases require account of their operative relations, not exclusion for failing biological closure. Expanding the boundary can include some supports, but it must identify them and explain why the expanded question is useful; it cannot invent unobserved renewal to save a universal claim.

**Nested and overlapping boundaries.** The same component can participate in different arrangements and timescales. A relation important to one question can be abstracted in another. Boundaries warranted by distinct questions need not converge to one partition. Where accounts concern the same effect, their mappings and claimed omissions must be compatible with that effect; compatible plurality is not a defect. A theorem about a least support in one fixed representation would not independently make its coordinate system or target uniquely physical.

**Independent aggregation versus joint organization.** A description may correctly retain two independent mechanisms together. This preserves the collection's behavior but gives no further evidence of an integrated organization between them. A richer account should identify the specific joint dependence claimed. Nonfactorization, where proved under a stated admissible domain, is one exact kind of joint restriction; it is not a universal synonym for being a system.

**Constraint and global descriptions.** An equilibrium or whole-history account can preserve all relevant constitutive relationships without narrating each as an input producing an output. Such an account remains eligible. The generative theory's contribution must concern warranted relationships and their consequences, not exclusive ownership of one temporal notation. If a stronger account explains the formation or replacement of a constraint, that adds a target; it does not retroactively make a faithful account of the original target false.

**Redundancy, erasure and changing relevance.** A relationship can contribute in the present arrangement without being individually necessary in every supported configuration. A formerly important contribution may be overwritten or replaced. A proposed support or boundary criterion must disclose whether it asks about one present history, all admitted continuations, available substitutions or selected outcomes. Otherwise a legitimate change of explanatory question can be misreported as a contradiction.

## 3. Primary theoretical comparisons

These sources offer alternatives and methodological comparisons, not empirical confirmation of the generative foundation. They are link-only consultations; no new source import or complete-paper proof audit is claimed.

**Willems (2007), *The Behavioral Approach to Open and Interconnected Systems*.** Directly read the opening and two-tank example, printed pp.46–51, in the [author-hosted article](https://homes.esat.kuleuven.be/~sistawww/smc/jwillems/Articles/JournalArticles/2007.1.pdf). Relations constrain trajectories; interconnection shares variables. Input/output partitions can be derived where appropriate, rather than imposed initially. The hydraulic example retains constitutive and interconnection laws with material parameters. This is a strong conventional comparator for relational modeling: it already avoids an obligatory primitive directional split. Its chosen variables and physical laws still need application warrant. The source therefore supports the admissibility of relational and global descriptions, not an assumption-free derivation of all actual system boundaries.

**Montévil and Mossio (2015), *Biological organisation as closure of constraints*.** Read the abstract and introduction through the indexed [author publication page](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2015-mm-organisation-closure-constraints/) and [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022519315001009). Constraints are characterized through relative conservation while acting on processes at relevant timescales; mutual maintenance supplies the proposed biological closure. This offers a way to derive analytical roles within changing activity. Its biological individuation proposal must not be converted into a requirement for every system. Repeated direct full-page/PDF retrievals failed; this review does not claim to have independently examined its complete formal definitions, examples or boundary procedure.

**Krakauer, Bertschinger, Olbrich, Ay and Flack, *The Information Theory of Individuality*.** Read the relevant formal, boundary-detection and caveat sections of the [2014 author preprint, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.2447v1), rather than treating it as a fresh reading of the later journal version. The proposal evaluates candidate, potentially nested partitions through temporal information. It explicitly begins with an informational characterization of individuality and requires suitable measurements and timescales; it also distinguishes informational closure from causal independence and acknowledges that the abstraction omits mechanisms. This directly tests a tempting shortcut: successful prediction can support an effective individuation, but does not alone identify the physical constitutive relations that the stronger task asks to explain.

## 5. Foundational assessment before the new artifacts

The affirmative route is worthwhile: identify relations independently, derive their consequences, and make boundaries accountable to what they preserve. It explains why system descriptions can be objective in their claims while multiple descriptions remain warranted. It also makes failure informative: a counterexample can show that a chosen boundary omitted a constitutive support, that an alleged dependence was spurious, or that a mechanism changed.

The no-assumptions demand is not discharged by calling the collection, the support relation or the admitted possibilities a system. Conversely, a local choice of description is not automatically a substantive assumption of an ultimately dual ontology. The relevant review question is whether the proposed derivation establishes the reality and scope of the relationships it invokes, or supplies them locally while correctly deriving their consequences. A general philosophical argument could warrant scope without inspecting every case; unique notation, one architecture everywhere and endless recurrence are not completion gates.

**Provisional assessment:** systems individuation can give the foundation a clearer, testable explanatory target and can support genuinely new boundary derivations. The material assessed so far does not establish completion of the stronger assumption-free foundation. This is a limitation of the current argument, not proof that the objective is impossible.
