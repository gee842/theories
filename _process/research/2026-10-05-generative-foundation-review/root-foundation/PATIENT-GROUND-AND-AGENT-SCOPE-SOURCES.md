# Patient ground and agent scope: two primary comparisons

6 October 2026. AI-authored source assessment within the existing root-foundation study. The connection is **experiential adversity → the significance of a possible relief → a reason for an eligible responder**. Actor and patient here name local consequential relations, not independently existing substances. The governing targets remain the [universal ethical minimum](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md) and the [reconstructed ethical grounding](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md).

The useful change in research direction is to separate **whether a relief consideration exists**, **how strongly it counts**, and **whose response it favors**. Investigating the first need not wait for a complete ordering of persons, selves or rights. Conversely, removing an owner from the description cannot by itself settle the third. This note adds one complete primary reading and one explicitly partial inspection; it does not claim a new proof of C1 or C2.

## 2. McMahan: a relief ground before a complete status ordering

**Source synopsis:** Hypothetical sentient beings without memory, conation or psychological continuity still give a suffering-based reason for relief (§2, PDF 2–4). McMahan distinguishes that ground from additional reasons associated with moral status (§3, PDF 4–5); he does not thereby defend equal priority. His replaceability and status claims remain substantive. In §4 (PDF 6), liability or desert may override or nullify a reason **not to cause** suffering. That passage does not establish the nullification of an independent actor's reason to provide permissible relief. The conclusion remains uncertain about comparative weights (§6, PDF 10–11).

## 3. Schroeder: neutrality must not conceal the actor question

Mark Schroeder, “Reasons and Agent-Neutrality,” *Philosophical Studies* 135 (2007), 279–306, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-007-9087-y), [primary archive](https://philarchive.org/archive/SCHRAA-16). Accessed 6 October 2026. **Partial coverage only:** indexed primary text on printed 279, 280, 282 and 285, together with the publisher's abstract. Archive, mirror and direct-file retrieval failed; a full-paper reading is not claimed. The [author's publication page](https://markschroeder.net/reasons-and-explanation) confirms the article and its 2014 reprint.

**Source synopsis:** Schroeder separates reason ascriptions with and without an explicit actor. The quantification strategy explains the latter through actor-addressed, or agent-relational, ascriptions over a domain, rather than obtaining every actor's reason from an omitted grammatical position (280, 282). On 285, merely acting for a reason does not require regarding it as everyone's reason. These inspected passages constrain an appeal to neutrality; they do not provide a new suffering-based ground or establish that every reason depends on desire. The article's complete assessment of competing analyses remains outside this inspection.

This semantic distinction is not the same result as [RootGrounding.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/noninstrumental-concern/RootGrounding.lean). The latter proves an equivalence within an already specified event/operator representation. Neither result licenses identifying the patient's local badness with every eligible actor's practical reason simply by changing notation.

## 4. Positive use in the present derivation

The most promising affirmative target is a response-ground argument about an actual experiential episode and its actual transformation. It should not require the sufferer to formulate a request, possess a narrative self, reciprocate, negotiate, or adopt the responder's project. These are distinct proposed requirements; absence of any one must not be treated as absence of consciousness. An account of experiencing remains necessary. Lack of a primitive permanent owner does not mean that an episode has no experiential location or that its relief occurs everywhere.

Use three controlled comparisons:

1. **Patient capacities.** Hold the adverse episode and permissible effective relief fixed while varying memory, self-description and prospective agency. Establish whether those changes alter the existence of the patient-ground consideration, its weight, or additional claims. This tests the proposed explanation without assuming equal priority or importing a theory of replaceability.
2. **Responder relation.** Hold the patient episode and relief fixed while changing which actual responsive route is available. Justify the favorable relation to each route directly. An actor-free description, a shared world, or a general capacity for rational action is not the reason-generating step. This remains compatible with the canonical account of overlapping cooperative routes.
3. **Justified suffering and permitted mercy.** Keep adversity real and relief independently permissible while examining a proposed reason that the suffering should continue. Distinguish a reason opposing relief from a claim that the patient benefit ceases to favor it at all.

The first comparison must preserve the precise meaning of the canonical “still-unwanted” condition. Experiential aversiveness, an occurrent desire and an enduring reflective preference are not interchangeable. If the defended domain requires a desire, a case without conation is a proposed extension, not an existing instance. If aversive undergoing suffices, that reading needs a positive experiential account. Neither silence nor absence of a refusal establishes it.

The third comparison is the sharpest new stress test. A claim of justified punishment may make a particular intervention impermissible; that does not preserve the current minimum's conditions. A permitted compassionate response may nevertheless be outweighed; that does not remove its favorable consideration. To defeat the present patient-ground claim, a stronger retributive account must explain why actual benefit supplies **no** favorable consideration even when relief remains independently permitted. Equally, the positive account must explain why the burden retains that force, rather than merely labeling it a reason. Do not silently add “undeserved” to eligibility or treat a wrongdoer's suffering as unreal.

This comparison concerns the justification of relief, not permission to inflict suffering, authority to punish, or advice about an actual patient. It preserves both the universal target and the possibility that a sound account of exclusion would require revising it. The source passage motivates the test; it does not decide it.

## 5. Result and next connection

The affirmative route now has a narrower task: defend the patient-ground contribution across changes in personal capacities and independently permitted response routes, while distinguishing additional priority claims and genuine exclusions. A theory of universal rational motivation, equal moral weight, compulsory rescue or comprehensive status is not required to state that contribution. Its truth still needs justification.

Next, examine **permitted relief of justified or deserved suffering: contrary reason or extinguished patient-ground reason?** This is an argument about ethical constitution, not another countermodel obtained by freely changing a predicate. No new law, eligibility gate, Lean result, empirical finding or foundational assumption is introduced here. The existing C1/C2 obligations remain open; the two comparisons identify what the next positive defense and rival must actually establish.
