## What the contrast can support

The central inference is about **a situated route's relation to differentiated information**, not about an immutable capacity inside either person. Communication, physical assistance, interpretation, reporting and the research arrangement are connected activities. Their roles can be distinguished without treating the people as independently complete sources whose interaction is an incidental external link.

A correct response under shared information does not, by itself, distinguish several possible organizations of that route. A change in who has which information can make their predictions differ. A result systematically associated with the information supplied along one route provides positive discriminatory evidence; a mere failure to obtain a correct answer would support a weaker conclusion. This is why a source-separated contrast can advance beyond reporting an accuracy score or assuming that a familiar helper transmits the intended participant's information.

That reasoning must retain the actual outcome variable. A response which includes mediation and interpretation does not isolate a fine-grained motor cause. Agreement between observers about the recorded outcome is compatible with uncertainty about where within the route its content was determined. Reliable recording of a report and reliable transmission of the represented person's information are different correspondence claims.

The experiment compares observed arrangements and their informative consequences. It does not provide both potential outcomes of every individual trial under an identical counterfactual history. Our account should not invent such paired data, a direct measurement of private experience, or a complete causal decomposition of the interaction.

## An excluded use need not erase the event's other significance

A useful conceptual advance concerns the scope of an evidential exclusion. An event can compromise the information separation needed for one attribution question while supplying evidence relevant to a different question about actual expression. These are two uses of the same occurrence within the same interaction, not separate factual and ethical realities.

Accepting the exclusion for the first purpose does not require pretending the event never occurred. Conversely, preserving the event's other significance does not require reinserting it into the original statistic or weakening the information-separation test. The appropriate repair is to retain the event and state separately which claim it can and cannot support. This is an inference about evidential roles, developed here; it is not presented as a new finding of the source authors or as a newly proved mathematical law.

The second use still needs its own correspondence. A meaningful expression in one encounter can count as positive evidence of that expression while remaining insufficient to certify a fully adequate replacement route, stability across settings or the ability to authorize every consequential decision. The source method also has an exception when an alternative expression was not observed by the facilitator, so the repository must not describe every such occurrence as excluded.

This protects both methodological discrimination and ethical inclusion. A research design may properly narrow its outcome analysis without defining the whole of the living interaction. An alignment system which converts “not admissible for this estimate” into “irrelevant to all further judgment” would make an additional, unsupported deletion.

## Counterreview and practical reach

**Could the test alter the activity it measures?** Yes. The response is not to assume an isolated capacity which must remain fixed across every arrangement. The inference must identify what assistance and opportunities were retained, what information access changed, and which remaining changes could explain the result. Familiarity or apparent comfort does not logically eliminate every testing effect. Conversely, invoking a possible effect without explaining the contrast does not automatically defeat the positive source evidence.

**Could the observed route still contain contributions from several positions?** Yes. The relevant conclusion need not be exclusive authorship of every token or an accusation of intentional deception. A route can involve meaningful participation while failing to warrant attributing its reported content to the person represented. The scope of that failed attribution matters particularly when a report is used as consent, preference or authorization; such a further use needs its own justification.

**Does withdrawing an attribution imply withdrawing recognition or support?** No. Under the already explicit G0/J account, the point of calibration is to sustain actual living, participation and response. Failure of a particular attribution neither erases other communication nor proves the absence of mind, interests, potential development or an entitlement to assistance. This is an application of the accepted commitments, not a new ethical axiom inferred from an experiment.

**Does the study already establish a complete replacement?** No. Evidence against one route's asserted property is not a demonstration that another arrangement preserves every relevant function, opportunity and support requirement. The theory may withdraw the unsupported certificate now while retaining the practical task of developing and checking an adequate alternative. Existing classification theorems do not license replacing that unfinished correspondence with the word “alternative.”

**Does one historical investigation establish a universal clinical claim?** No. This audit supports the specified inference at its reported scope and uses it to constrain the theory's architecture. It is not a contemporary systematic review or treatment recommendation. The archival reading adds no new intervention and makes no general inference about all supported communication arrangements.
