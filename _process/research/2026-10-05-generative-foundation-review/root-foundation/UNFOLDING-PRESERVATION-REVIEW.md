## 1. The positive argument and its independent target

The proposed advance is a requirement of factual fidelity to a specified unfolding, not a stipulation that only accounts called generative are adequate. Before choosing an encoding, identify the questions the episode presents: which comparisons were acquired; what the retained evidence allows the interpretation procedure to distinguish; which judgment was produced; whether a changed signal can produce an error with that interpretation unchanged; whether a new record changes the judgment; and what an actual review, loss of access or exhausted support does. These questions concern different, inspectable relations of one episode. They are not separately existing knowing and acting domains.

The mathematical consequence is then that an exact answer-bearing account cannot erase a distinction that changes one of those answers. Its significance depends on the prior justification of the question family. Fidelity to arbitrary analyst-chosen predicates would not justify those predicates as the content of the phenomenon. Nor does an exact account of the episode's final answer automatically undertake to explain its acquisition and correction. The richer requirement is warranted when acquisition, error, review and the conditions of their continuation are what the account purports to explain.

This permits abstraction. An account need not reproduce every coordinate, word or microscopic event. If the mathematical interface demands exact raw boundary states, its necessity claim is correspondingly fine-grained; it must not be reported as a proof that every physical coordinate is indispensable. The preservation obligation is to retain, or make recoverable, the selected distinctions and the supported relations through which compatible fragments continue.

## 2. What the inherited episode actually establishes

In [FormedGrounds.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/universal-ethics/generative-capacity/FormedGrounds.lean), paired contacts constrain a finite family of interpretation rules. Two contrasting contacts settle the decoder; the interpretation procedure receives the acquired pairs and signal, not the actual target, convention bit or reward. A disturbed signal can then produce error while the decoder and compared relation remain unchanged. These are modeled performances and failures with an explicit source law, not observations of a deployed learner.

One important chronological distinction must remain exact. From the mistaken state, `inspectOnly` acquires a receipt without changing the erroneous judgment and exhausts the remaining budget. The alternative `review` event acquires a fresh sample and compares and corrects the judgment in that same event. It does not read the previously saved `inspectOnly` receipt. Consequently the successful review and acquisition-only failure are alternative continuations from the same boundary; executing `inspectOnly` and then trying review fails through exhaustion. Treating them as a sequential acquisition-and-later-uptake success would misdescribe the source.

The general retained-record provenance result and faithful-encoding interpretation result provide more than an isolated successful run, while retaining their stated inputs. The comparison law, paired acquisition, access and support conditions, rule repertoire and revision architecture remain supplied by this local construction. Their successful operation cannot establish every intentional content, universal understanding, ethical warrant or the origin of every process law.

## 3. Strong rivals and the distinctions they test

**Global or atemporal representation.** A single constraint over the whole episode can retain the actual event order, acquired records, erroneous intermediate judgment, support consumption and contrasting possible continuations. Such an account passes factual preservation even if it declines to treat temporal production as metaphysically fundamental. If it also retains the agreed replacement relations, it is an alternative articulation of that content. The study cannot rule it out simply by calling it static. A whole-history table that preserves all these answers also meets the factual criterion; whether it explains rather than catalogs them requires a further argument.

**Common cause and predetermined sequence.** An observed earlier record and later correction do not alone show that the record contributed to the correction. A common condition or a prearranged response sequence may reproduce that chronology. Discrimination requires additional warranted process evidence or supported variations that distinguish the proposals. In the inherited construction, reading the specified event law establishes the modeled connection; it does not turn that connection into empirical identification of every real occurrence.

**Redundancy.** A relation can participate in an actual response even when another route would have preserved the same outcome after its removal. Simple but-for failure is therefore neither an adequate universal definition of participation nor a universal necessary test for it. Conversely, mere presence in a stored history does not show operative participation. The account should preserve the actual route and any independently supported alternatives without inventing exclusive dependence.

**Control and counterfactual scope.** A selectable event label or a syntactically legal intervention does not establish that an actual participant can perform it. A replacement must retain the boundary, access and support conditions under which the comparison is meaningful. Counterfactual sufficiency of one mechanism, its indispensability for this history, constitutive necessity for the target, and universal ontological necessity are different conclusions. Preserving the first does not silently yield the others.

## 4. Two primary comparators

Rubenstein and colleagues' [Causal Consistency of Structural Equation Models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.00819), §4.3, Definition 3 and Theorem 6, compare descriptions through matched interventions and their effects. Their §5.3 also gives a scoped passage from a dynamical description to an equilibrium description. The relevant comparison is fidelity to declared questions, not a blanket requirement to reproduce temporal presentation. Their structural-equation assumptions and particular intervention domains are not results proved by this study.

Beckers and Halpern's [Abstracting Causal Models](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/4117/3995), Example 3.4 and §§3.2–3.3, show why sparse tests, chosen distributions or overly flexible intervention mappings can conceal important differences. Definitions 3.12–3.14 constrain that mapping, while Examples 3.15–3.16 acknowledge limits to unrestricted interventions. The present review therefore requires an independently motivated question and boundary correspondence. The Lean episode interface is not thereby an implementation of their causal-abstraction definitions.

These sources are methodological comparators, not empirical confirmation or evidence that the present formulation is uniquely fundamental. The original [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md) governs the use of local analytical positions: preserve their relationships within the same unfolding and make omissions explicit.

## 5. Formal and receipt review

The generic [UnfoldingPreservation.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/deeper/UnfoldingPreservation.lean) passes semantic review. `May` asks for a completed trace with a specified endpoint property; `Must` constrains every completed endpoint but can hold when no trace completes. `Completes` adds existence, not guaranteed completion along every nondeterministic alternative. Their composition statements use the same reached intermediate state, with distinct existential and universal quantifiers. The explicit branching example retains a real first-stage branch from which the suffix cannot run, even though another branch completes successfully.

The selected-outcome `Account` permits an observation function that omits state coordinates. Its exactness recovers feasible outcomes and their possible/necessary endpoint properties, and a differing answer prevents equal codes. These are relative information-preservation results, not a mechanism for acquiring an exact account. An internal reading can remain unable to answer a question that a mathematical account of the broader represented history can answer.

`SegmentAccount` has the stronger target of every native starting and ending boundary. It contains no composition operation or homomorphism field. `exact_segment_join` transports the inherited native `Trace` joining result into its answers; `equal_segment_codes_substitute` recovers substitution through actual intermediate states. The theorem earns a consequence of exact endpoint fidelity, not the native law or a universal justification for selecting that fine-grained interface. Formal event-list concatenation is total; successful physical joining in this representation still requires admitted traces sharing their boundary. The model does not claim a general theory of concurrent or differently typed joining.

The concrete [FormedUnfoldingPreservation.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/deeper/FormedUnfoldingPreservation.lean) also passes. Its relational wrapper is proved equivalent to the unchanged native run. Closed or exhausted acquisition remains a total no-op, so the separation concerns missing effects rather than an invented absence of the attempted event. The exact-account results need only selected judgment or operative-reading outcomes. An unacquired reference change can preserve that reading yet change the result of the next actual review; this does not give the internal reader direct access to the changed reference.

The successful review retains the disturbed signal. Reapplying interpretation can therefore restore the error. This real regression is retained in the present-answer compression counterexample, rather than corrected away to make the model look successful. The two histories used against reconstruction from an endpoint have equal event counts, genuinely different intermediate acquired receipts and exactly the same final state. Their history difference is not merely unequal list length. The impossibility of an endpoint decoder concerns recovery of these past readings; the native endpoint may still suffice for future evolution. No requirement that every local participant remember every past occurrence follows.
