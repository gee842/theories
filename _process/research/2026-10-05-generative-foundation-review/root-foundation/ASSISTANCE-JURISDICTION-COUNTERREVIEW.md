# Assistance jurisdiction: what a protected practical domain excludes

6 October 2026. AI-authored counterreview of **authority over one's positive life → exclusion of all prospective stranger-relief moral favor**. This continues [unified ethical grounding](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md) and the [elective-concern review](ELECTIVE-CONCERN-COUNTERREVIEW.md). No experiment or necessary-root proof is claimed.

**Assessment:** protected agency can justify more than another first-order weight: it may warrant limits on deliberation, legitimate refusal and source-specific extinction of reasons. These possibilities defeat an overbroad claim that every patient benefit must always govern choice. They do not yet establish that an existing stranger's relief contributes no moral favor. A project-preserving comparison exposes the stronger jurisdiction thesis while providing defeasible positive support for the direct candidate.

## 1. Strengthen the rival before testing it

A serious account starts from the value of sustained, self-directed activity. Plans, attention, intimate relations and worthwhile projects cannot function if every external benefit must continually reopen every decision. Protected domains may therefore authorize an agent to set purposes, use settled procedures and decline invitations to undertake new beneficial projects. The protection could concern how decisions are made, not merely how competing first-order goods are weighed.

Its strongest assistance-specific version adds an asymmetry: unchosen constraints prohibit imposing burdens, while positive purposes become the agent's practical business through adoption or antecedent responsibility. It can value charity, regard suffering as really bad, and correct an adopted project's ineffective methods. It need not declare every reason elective. Its concern is that allowing unchosen benefits to govern an agent's positive direction would convert self-direction into permanent availability for others.

This is a philosophical reconstruction, not a primary author's doctrine. Its defensible elements must not be reduced to selfish preference. Still, requiring a person to evaluate every benefit, requiring that person to assist, and recognizing some favor for an available response are different proposals. The candidate C2 makes the third claim; it does not automatically make the first two.

## 2. Different kinds of exclusion and genuine extinction

| Proposed protection | What it can establish | What remains distinct |
|---|---|---|
| A reason not to deliberate about or act on specified considerations | An independently justified restriction on practical use within its domain | Whether the excluded first-order consideration exists |
| Choice-specific normative disqualification | A consideration lacks active justificatory force for this choice | Whether its underlying value counts elsewhere; this need not be mere nonuse |
| Permission to continue an authored activity | A permissible refusal, even where helping has some favor | Whether the benefit contributes nothing to the comparison |
| A constitutive jurisdiction over positive moral address | That assistance favor itself requires authorization, if this jurisdiction is warranted | This is the stronger contested claim, not a consequence of the first two |
| Valid release from a promise | Actual removal of a directed promise-ground, under a legitimate power | Whether other grounds concerning the same act survive |

The release case is a control against our own argument. Suppose a patient validly releases an actor from a promise to operate an effective relief control. The burden continues; no reliance or other undertaking-derived responsibility remains. The promise-based claim can cease although the patient's condition is unchanged. Genuine normative extinction is therefore a live possibility. The direct account cannot infer permanence of every reason from permanence of suffering. Nor can it assume that a promisor's private repudiation has the same power as the patient's valid release.

The converse inference also fails: removing that claim does not establish that every reason to relieve the burden has disappeared. The authority, object and release conditions of the power matter. An independently justified power over a promise does not automatically become a power over the moral relevance of another's existing condition.

A protected deliberative domain presents a different challenge. A reason may exist although a justified procedure bars acting on it in that context. Such a restriction could preserve impartial judging, confidential relationships or reliable concentration. These are candidate grounds needing assessment, not a blanket claim that all procedures are justified. If the assistance rival accepts the first-order patient reason but excludes its use, it has changed the point of disagreement. That matters: C2 must not be inflated into a duty of constant reconsideration, but neither should it be weakened into an inert label while every practical application is silently excluded.

A stronger protection may genuinely disqualify a consideration for this choice, rather than leave the same active favor unused. If independently warranted in our comparison, it would revise C2's application. Retained patient value elsewhere would not neutralize that countercase.

If a warranted blanket exclusion barred every uncommitted actor from ever choosing for this patient consideration, preserving an abstract first-order mark would not establish the intended prospective practical justification. The weaker existence claim might survive while C2's practical address required revision. A limited, grounded exclusion and that universal barrier therefore need separate assessment; calling both “mere deliberation” would conceal the stronger challenge.

## 3. A comparison within an independently chosen project

The [new argument](ASSISTANCE-JURISDICTION-ARGUMENT.md) and this review converge on a more discriminating case. An actor has independently chosen a worthwhile project and is already informed and considering how to execute it. Two available methods are equally suitable for that project. Both use otherwise permitted means, with no independently prohibited intervention, and preserve the same relevant time, resources, privacy, commitments and opportunities over the actual history. One would additionally relieve a stranger's actual severe, still-unwanted experiential burden; the other would not. No prior helping intention, promise or responsibility is supplied. This does not stipulate overall permission to omit relief; a requiring judgment remains a further question.

This is a stipulated conceptual comparison, not a report that ordinary cases are costless. Being already informed avoids assuming a duty to search continually for beneficiaries. It also does not stipulate away any legitimate exclusionary reason: if a protected method or procedure genuinely applies, its ground must be identified and the case reassessed.

Equal project costs do not answer a principled nonresponsibility or authority objection. They isolate whether the alleged barrier protects those costs or instead governs address independently of them. The latter rival survives the control and needs its own adjudication.

The affirmative judgment is that the real relief makes the first method morally worth choosing for that patient's sake before selection. The actor need not adopt a new life project or abandon the existing one. The judgment concerns prospective favor, not an inferred intention in completed helpful behavior. The absence of conflict does not generate the reason; the actual beneficial difference supplies its proposed ground. The no-benefit method is a contrast that isolates this ground, not a proof that every unopposed act is favored.

This challenges a protection grounded specifically in maintaining chosen purposes: both methods preserve that purpose. Yet it does not remove self-direction altogether. Choosing means can itself express agency, and a serious rival can extend its jurisdiction to implementation choices or evaluative address. That extension now needs defense. Protecting freedom to choose either means can coexist with one method's possessing patient-based moral favor. To exclude even that favor, the rival must explain why the benefit is outside practical address within an already selected project, not merely why another person cannot commandeer the project.

The two accounts thus make a concrete different assessment before adoption. The direct account recognizes a patient-grounded difference between the options. The full jurisdiction account denies that difference for this actor until authorization, while possibly retaining impersonal admiration or permission. An account granting the same prospective patient-based justification already grants the target; an account granting only the latter alternatives does not. This is a substantive disagreement exposed by a controlled case, not a logical contradiction imposed on the rival.

## 4. Doing, allowing and the limits of the affirmative argument

A doing/allowing distinction can help explain why imposing a burden is prohibited while failure to relieve one is sometimes allowed. It need not classify both acts identically. But a difference in prohibition or requiring force does not establish zero favor for aid. In the method comparison, choosing the ineffective option is not redescribed as causing the patient's suffering. The direct judgment is about an available benefit; a later claim that omission is wrong would need additional grounds.

The strongest jurisdiction account can instead claim that allowing belongs to a protected positive domain at the level of reasons themselves. This survives the elementary permission distinction and cannot be dismissed merely for treating assistance differently from restraint. It must explain that classification's normative basis. Finite attention and sustained activity are plausible grounds for restrictions on demands and reconsideration. Their role in eliminating all first-order favor, including within the project-preserving comparison, is less clear and remains contested.

Our affirmative case is likewise bounded. Symmetry does not establish impartial ethics: uniform exclusion of every stranger could be symmetric, while consistent self-relative rules preserve the self/other relation under renaming. Nonarbitrariness alone does not select C2. Nor does absence of a defended exclusion prove its negation. The positive patient comparison must stand as an independent ethical judgment, open to a countercase within its stated domain or a stronger account of protected address.

## 5. Formed boundaries without primitive owners

The concurrent [nondual reconstruction](NONDUAL-GROUNDING-QUESTION.md) asks whether experiential and responsive relations can be grounded without a fundamental me/world division. It does not show that differentiated suffering, accumulated exhaustion, consent or project histories disappear. Earlier operational actor–patient distinctions did not by themselves assert metaphysically independent substances; they need not be rejected as though they did.

The jurisdiction rival can therefore appeal to real continuities of activity rather than ownership by an isolated self. The direct account can locate adversity in actual undergoing rather than an owner's decree. Neither formulation decides which transformations are morally favored. A formed boundary can support coordination or preserve a worthwhile continuity, but its formation alone does not confer authority to erase reasons. Equally, being formed rather than fundamental does not deprive a boundary of every legitimate protection. Those are substantive normative assessments.

**Scoped verdict:** the strongest plausible protections reach particular choice domains, refusal and created claims more directly than they reach universal exclusion of stranger-relief favor. The project-preserving pair supplies a focused affirmative challenge to that further extension. This narrows the dispute beyond the previous observation that creation does not imply exclusivity. It leaves C2 defeasibly supported, with the proposed jurisdiction still open to stronger defense. Actual experience identification, patient-good significance, universal scope and the necessary root remain unfinished; the accepted conditional foundation retains its original completion scope.
