## 2. Strongest substantive rival

Consider a timely common response with accurately understood, unequal basic losses, permitted rescue routes, no relevant prior award and no available route protecting everybody. Reasons favor different routes, and no accepted priority settles the comparison. A rival accepts the full relations, takes each loss seriously and allows an authorized decision to select one of the permissible routes. It denies that this necessarily requires a preceding lottery. The decision need not express contempt, misidentify a need or follow an irrelevant personal preference.

This opponent is stronger than a controller saying that power alone makes its favorite outcome right. Physical control and arbitrary exclusion are not equivalent. A public allocation can exclude a person from this occasion's actual protection without denying that person's ethical significance. The opponent therefore asks why a still-live supporting reason entails an enforceable opportunity to win, rather than respectful consideration followed by a permissible choice.

CP's affirmative reply identifies a further relationship: when exclusion has not been warranted and protection remains permissibly reachable through a common procedure, an actual route by which the request could be fulfilled gives it practical presence which deliberative acknowledgment alone does not. An unavoidable limit on joint fulfillment does not itself settle whether every available route to one request may be closed. This is a defensible additional procedural commitment. It is not a deduction from the existence of a pro tanto reason, and the opponent can reject it while accepting the earlier basic-loss comparisons.

Calling all such direct choices arbitrary would assume the disputed conclusion. The candidate is strongest when it openly says that its account of warranted treatment limits this permission to pick, and explains the actual access relation it protects. It should not claim that the opponent has secretly denied G0 or restored a dualist ontology.

## 3. Conditions that cannot be hidden in the formulation

**No circular exclusion test.** “There is no sufficient reason to exclude” must not mean simply “this request is owed positive chance.” The application should instead assess actual efficacy and feasibility, permission, relevant commitments, warranted priority, costs and common consequences. BC1 and a warranted LR application can supply priority grounds in their stated scopes. This list is not a proof that every possible defeating reason has been enumerated. A live reason can remain after a warranted exclusion from this particular response.

**Inquiry and unresolved conflict differ.** If a timely, proportionate check can reveal that one supposed rescue is ineffective or unauthorized, apparent indecision before that check is not a reason to protect ignorance with a lottery. Equally, sufficient inquiry does not mean all possible inquiry. The affirmative test should preserve a case in which the relevant relations are known and the ethical comparison itself remains unresolved. Even there, incomparability, a current lack of verdict and an exact tie are different claims. None directly determines odds.

**Routes and procedures need distinct permission.** Permissibility of each terminal rescue does not by itself authorize every random mixture, selection method, delay or disclosure. A lottery must be timely and effective in the actual history, with its costs and authority assessed. Existing valid commitments cannot be removed merely to manufacture a new initial choice. The completed chance-revision work already makes history consequential; CP must use it.

**Opportunity is more than an announced number.** The selection and delivery activity must actually leave the relevant protection reachable. A hidden fixed recipient described as an uncertain draw, an unenforced result or a manipulated mechanism fails that application. These are implementation requirements; their satisfaction does not independently prove the shares fair. Biological humanity, a particular psychological response to losing or prior membership in an association is not silently installed as the universal eligibility condition.

**Strict positivity is a lower-level constraint.** CP can reject an unsupported zero while leaving a purportedly token positive share inadequate. It does not follow that every positive share is fair. Nor does the objection to token shares by itself identify a justified numerical floor. Such a floor, a weighting scale, maximum-risk rule or fixed lottery resolution needs its own reason. Actual chance also does not erase a loss when the request is not fulfilled; that distinction is inherited, not the new contribution.

**Opportunity preservation is not an immutable menu rule.** A new joint rescue, changed costs, newly discovered dependence or a valid settlement can change the relevant procedure. Duplicate route descriptions create no new claim. CP must apply to the current formed relations and warranted constraints, not freeze every old ticket or require fixed pairwise odds across all menus. No Luce-style independence condition is supplied by the present ethical argument.

## 4. Primary-source comparison and reading scope

The following source-derived summaries are concentrated here. They inform the substantive tests; none supplies CP's full generative justification.

**John Broome, “Fairness,” *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 91 (1990–91):87–101.** The new targeted reading is §VI, printed pp.100–101 of the [author-hosted original](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~sfop0060/pdf/Fairness.pdf); §§III–V retain their prior reading scope. Broome distinguishes an inability to compare reasons from a separate fairness justification. Indeterminacy can explain needing a decision device without showing why a lottery is owed or superior to a fixed rule. This challenges the inference from unresolved judgment to fair chance, rather than rejecting lotteries generally. His particular illustrative allocation criteria are not adopted here.

**Peter Stone, “The Logic of Random Selection,” *Political Theory* 37(3) (2009):375–397, DOI `10.1177/0090591709332329`.** Read the [author-uploaded primary text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236343886_The_Logic_of_Random_Selection), pp.375–382, 390–392 and notes 11–12. Stone's account explains a lottery's value through blocking inappropriate reasons, while recognizing that it can also block pertinent ones. The relevant decision stage and risks therefore matter. His main treatment restricts lotteries to equal probabilities and sets incomparability aside. It does not establish that unequal CP is mistaken or provide its weights. The useful challenge is to identify which improper influence the actual procedure excludes without losing warranted distinctions.

Both readings used primary text, with no new visual page audit, local preservation of original bytes or empirical validation claimed. Broome's earlier chance discussion and the existing fair-access account receive credit; this review does not invent lottery ethics or present source agreement as a proof. The root researcher's Spiekermann reading and the argument researcher's further primary comparisons belong in their respective declared source ledgers, without duplicating their summaries here.
