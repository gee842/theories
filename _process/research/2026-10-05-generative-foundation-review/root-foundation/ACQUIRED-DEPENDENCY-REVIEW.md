## A common adequate policy already exists for the native task

Keep the original [FormedSelection law](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/formal/FormedSelection.lean). Initially the site is intact, the resident active, and there are at least `n` precursor places. Common fuel is `F`, source is `E`, and the local-store bit is `L`. Require:

1. Increase A by `n`, preserving H and the active resident.
2. After completing that reconstruction, meet `r` specified episodes, each consisting of an imposed damage event followed by restoration of the site.

No other disturbance is imposed in this finite contract. The episodes are independent of the response's preferred route. This is an existential native-continuation question; an arbitrary hostile scheduler may introduce additional losses that it does not cover.

**Claim.** The contract is feasible precisely when

\[
F\ge r+\max(n-L-E,0).
\]

**Necessity.** Net A growth of `n` requires at least `n` A-producing events. The `r` damage/restoration episodes require at least `r` distinct repairs. Every such growth or repair expends one unit of total support, so `n+r ≤ F+L+E`. Each repair additionally consumes common fuel, and common fuel never increases, so `F≥r`. These inequalities are jointly equivalent to the displayed bound. Additional native formation, resident changes, wear or leakage do not evade either ledger.

**Sufficiency.** Use the existing local unit, if present and needed; then use capture/local-copy pairs for as many of the remaining copies as source permits. Bulk-copy the remainder. This uses exactly `max(n−L−E,0)` common units, preserves the resident and H, and never exceeds the precursor capacity. The site remains intact. At least `r` common units remain, which fund the `r` stipulated damage/repair pairs. The empty cases `n=0` and `r=0` retain their usual meanings. ∎

The construction is one fixed native rule, conditional on current local availability, source and the deficit. It need not consult an acquired action-effect archive when those current conditions and the native operation identities are already available. This is not a claim that a costless observer or rule implementation has been physically installed. It establishes that the task itself does not make acquired history necessary for every possible capable controller.

At the actual proposed boundary `F=1`, `E>0`, `L=0`, `n=r=1`, the point is stronger: the fixed sequence `capture → localCopyA`, followed by the stipulated damage and `repair`, succeeds without adapting its reconstruction choice. A comparison solely against bulk-first does not defeat that common adequate rival. Literal capture-first is inappropriate when the local store is already full or the source empty; the general rule above includes those actual guards rather than assuming every context admits capture.

Acquired records could still causally govern the successful route. That would demonstrate one realized acquisition/use connection. It would not establish that learning is necessary for this native task, that the learned policy exceeds every fixed controller, or that the undertaking's purpose has originated in the record.

## A meaningful discriminator: actual command coupling

The proposed two-port interface can make prior acquired consequences operationally necessary **relative to a specified information restriction**.

Let a real control coupling `κ`, fixed throughout each tested history, connect two initially anonymous command ports to the existing native macros:

- local macro: `capture → localCopyA`;
- bulk macro: `bulkCopyA`.

The two admitted couplings exchange which port produces which macro. This must be a difference in the actual command path, not a renaming of two native commands that remain directly accessible to the controller. The material effects themselves remain the original events, and a macro must retain both component events and their timing.

At an intact, active state with one vacant precursor, `F=1`, `E>0` and `L=0`, both macros are enabled. Either replaces one A. The local macro leaves common fuel one; the bulk macro leaves common fuel zero. Under the independently fixed later damage/repair contract, only the local macro admits adequate continuation.

If the controller can issue only the two ports at the relevant decision, the adequate first-port sets are `{0}` under one coupling and `{1}` under the other. They are disjoint. A policy using only information identical across these histories must select the same port and therefore fails one case; randomization cannot give a zero-error guarantee across both. This is the already established relevance argument applied to a concrete native consequence, not a new general impossibility theorem.

The fully informed native-action rule remains a valid rival. A controller with direct native capture/copy access can bypass the ambiguity. A controller born with the correct coupling encoded also needs no acquisition during this episode. The test therefore concerns acquired identification and use of an actual command-effect relation, rather than the necessity of learning for all agents or situations.

The port coupling is additional organization that the seven-coordinate plant state does not represent. A proposed extension must expose its realization, stability and control authority. Calling it a port does not derive its formation, and calling it supplied does not place it outside generative reality.

## What the encounters must discriminate

At the decision boundary both commands are productive. The wrong command does not fail to execute: it restores the current count while exhausting the unit required later. Treating it as a harmless failed query or allowing a retry with restored support would erase the relevant consequence.

Both macros leave the same A/H counts, resident, intactness, empty local store and aggregate energy. A record of only those endpoint variables does not identify which coupling preserved common support. A distinguishing encounter must acquire the common/source consequence, or another genuinely informative physical consequence, and bind it to the port actually issued. Reading the producer's private native-event label from an external trace is not that contact.

One faithful earlier encounter can identify the untested port as well if the admitted coupling family is exactly the two stable permutations. That inference relies on the supplied bijection and stability restriction; it is not discovery of an arbitrary event law from one observation. If extra couplings, stochastic effects or later rewiring are admitted, the inference and the necessary evidence change. Preserve misleading, missing, stale and contradictory records instead of silently assigning an unobserved outcome.

Training must occur under an actual sufficient earlier reserve and material configuration. At the `F=1` boundary, an added operation consuming one common unit eliminates the later repair reserve. The existing source-to-common observation kernel cannot replenish it without changing the law being investigated. The [identification interface](CONTINUING-DEPENDENCY-IDENTIFICATION.md) explains why existing record assembly and archive provenance cannot simply be transplanted as free contact.

Training actions alter the plant, even when their purpose is inquiry. Formation occupies A/H material; stored parts remain unavailable as copying precursor. A reconstructed trial loss must be an independently specified loss, not automatically any A/H part deliberately incorporated into memory. Earlier records, consumed support, native outcomes and the later decision must share an explicit ancestry. Inserting the earlier successful learner state at the later boundary is not a completed join.

## Waiting, bypasses and adequate alternatives

The claim about disjoint first-port sets requires a contract in which the A replacement precedes the independently scheduled later damage. The control protocol must say whether waiting is permitted, whether it supplies information and what autonomous events may occur during it. Mere waiting that leaves the wiring unknown and does not complete the required replacement is not an adequate response. However, if waiting permits reliable uncommanded local copying, or a free informative observation, it can invalidate the claimed first-choice necessity. Do not exclude that possibility by assertion after seeing the result.

Similarly, the local macro contains two native events, whereas the bulk macro contains one. Declaring the later damage to occur only after the macro finishes is a finite scheduling contract, not a consequence of naming an atomic port. A damage event interleaved after capture can change the continuation. Preserve such scheduling limits rather than claiming arbitrary-environment robustness.

The assay should retain a no-new-record or consumer-disconnection control with the same actual earlier encounters and physical conditions. A default-port controller must then succeed in the favorable coupling and fail in the exchanged coupling; it should not be forced to fail everywhere. A selective consumer intervention must preserve storage, relevant signal delivery and execution capacity. An independently instantiated, correctly coupled fixed controller may match or exceed the acquired one and remains an admissible alternative explanation.

## Prospective acceptance

The strongest appropriate positive conclusion would be: **earlier native consequences form retained, port-bound distinctions that later enter command selection, preserving a specified repair continuation in the actual history.** A matched reader intervention and exchanged real coupling would identify the contribution of that acquired relation.

That result remains conditional on contact, stable coupling family, record/reader realization, admissible controls, schedule and continuation scope. It need not derive ultimate purpose, use a separate external evaluator, or require an infinite hierarchy of learned supervisors. The common adequate native policy prevents an inflated necessity claim while leaving a substantive material acquisition/use result possible. Its ethical evaluation remains governed by the whole-process account, not by the synthetic count or repair reserve.
