Theoretical argument, substantive author counterreview and primary-source assessment; **7 new kernel-checked theorems / 15 empty declaration audits**. No independent acceptance, empirical observation or complete fair-lottery theory is claimed.

**Finding:** even the full terminal joint distribution can omit an ethically relevant difference between implementing a settled allocation and replacing it with another lottery. The new connection is **warranted joint arrangement → history-sensitive requirements on revising the selector**. This is a representation and implementation requirement conditional on defended normative premises, not a new derivation of promising or a discovery that unwanted lottery outcomes cannot simply be redrawn.

## 2. One provision history, three continuations

Two positions in the same provision process, A and B, have serious unmet needs. One indivisible opportunity can protect exactly one. For this test the initial equal lottery, the administrator's bounded role, the announced finality of its first valid draw, and its actual proper completion are independently warranted. Both needs, efficacy and relevant burdens remain unchanged. No fraud, error, release, overriding emergency or extra protective route appears. These are substantive case conditions, not implications of randomness.

Represent two independent fair bits by four equiprobable tickets. The first bit determines the announced award. A second bit is available if someone redraws. Unused random bits do not create a second opportunity or a new allocation mandate.

| Continuation | Actual implementation | Terminal chance before either bit | Relation to the first award |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep | Implement the first bit | A and B each one half | Preserved on every ticket |
| Fresh redraw | Discard the first bit and implement the second | A and B each one half | Reversed on the two disagreeing tickets |
| Selective redraw | Stop if B wins; otherwise use the second bit | A one quarter; B three quarters | Outcome-dependent replacement changes the originally announced chances |

The first two procedures have the same **full joint terminal protection distribution**: one half on A-only protection and one half on B-only protection. Equality therefore cannot be repaired merely by adding the correlation of A's and B's final protection. The missing relation is between the earlier warranted settlement and its continuation.

After A has won, Keep gives A certainty for this allocation; Fresh redraw returns both to a half chance. The analogous statement holds after B has won. There is no violation of the earlier equal-chance arrangement in implementing its result: that arrangement concerned the allocation before the draw, not a promise to restore equal chances after every observation. Requiring both unchanged settlement fidelity and fresh equality on every observed branch is incompatible in this model. The checked incompatibility concerns this same resource and these conditions, not all repeated social allocation.

## 3. Why this is a normative difference

The affirmative judgment is to implement the valid settled award in this specified case. The other person's persisting need still supports eligible independent relief; it does not, solely by persisting, provide an additional mandate to rerun the settled allocation. This is precisely the inherited patient-ground distinction. Nor may the administrator claim that its physical control of the random source confers a broader authority to replace the settlement.

The positive ground for fidelity is the particular warranted arrangement through which an initially unresolved joint activity acquires a practicable continuation. Participants can undertake preparation, accept which decision will settle this matter, and organize further activity around it. Its scope permits a genuine result even though not all original needs disappear. Treating every completion as a new tie defeats that particular capacity for joint determination. Material wasted preparation can strengthen the objection, but it is not our sole premise: the inherited agreement account grants actual normative force to a justified settlement even before a further measurable reliance loss occurs.

This explicitly retains a substantive premise: **a properly warranted settlement can create a bounded reason to implement its result**. G0's positive direction and the practical account support its role in sustained joint living; they do not, without the additional defended commitment, uniquely entail every finality rule. No duty is defined as a probability equality or inferred from mere historical occurrence. The formal predicate below describes agreement of outputs only.

The new theoretical consequence is a limit on a proposed revision criterion. A criterion using only current needs and the full terminal protection law cannot distinguish these continuations sufficiently for this warranted history. Such a criterion needs the active settlement relation, or a summary adequate to preserve its practical implications. It need not retain every historical detail. This gives content to history-sensitive generative alignment: formation can change the reasons that a later selecting process must recognize, without changing the bearers into unrelated value owners or postulating a separate realm of obligations.

## 4. Correction remains possible

An arrangement's occurrence does not establish its warrant. A coerced, discriminatory or incorrectly executed procedure is not made valid by having an outcome. Likewise, material new evidence about feasibility, a justified release, or a changed basic emergency may alter what can or ought to continue. The question is which ground changes, which earlier requirement survives, and what the proposed remedy actually affects. Mere novelty is insufficient, because a warranted undertaking may already cover the relevant risk.

Outcome-dependent information is not automatically manipulation. Discovering that the selected route cannot perform its promised protection can be a real defeater, even if the discovery is triggered by the selection. Conversely, a need known before the lottery is not new information simply because its bearer lost. Thus neither “all later revision is forbidden” nor “all outcome-dependent revision is forbidden” follows.

A two-stage procedure announced and independently justified from the outset supplies another control. If its first bit is only a rehearsal and its second bit settles the award, using the second bit can faithfully implement that arrangement. The same terminal distribution and the same pair of physical draws do not give the rehearsal an already settled award. The difference is the actually formed procedure and its scope, not an unconstrained ethical label toggled to manufacture a counterexample. Advance disclosure alone does not make arbitrary terms fair, and a secret post-result declaration that the first stage was provisional is no such control.

For a still-warranted first-stage settlement, a faithful implementation may change its code, custodian or intermediate calculation while preserving its assigned protection. The compositional theorem checks a narrow positive guarantee for such changes. Outside that scope, a correction must explain its changed grounds; fidelity cannot replace that explanation.

## 7. Substantive author counterreview and decision

**Objection: the verdict was supplied as finality.** Correct about the dependency, incorrect if used to erase the distinct implementation result. The normative force is openly inherited from J3 and the agreement account, with the positive defense in §3. Lean checks that terminal probability equality cannot implement that force sufficiently. It supplies no further proof that every settlement ought to bind. Without the normative premise, the mathematical difference remains but the ethical verdict does not follow.

**Objection: a complete consequentialist theory can include history.** Yes. The counterexample targets terminal protection alone, even with all its correlations, not every outcome-based theory. Counting history as an outcome is a possible adequate representation; it still owes an account of which historical relations have significance. Nondual reconstruction similarly has to preserve those distinctions rather than merely assert that everything is connected.

**Objection: correct conditioning already distinguishes Keep from Fresh redraw.** Yes. Their equal terminal laws are evaluated before either draw, not at the same already observed first result. Conditional on that result, the laws differ exactly as the two conditional theorems show. This is no defect in probability theory and no counterexample to a criterion already retaining the relevant observation. The error under examination is using unchanged initial overall chances to certify later revision. Conditioning identifies the changed exposure; explaining why a particular earlier result is binding still requires the warranted procedural history, as the rehearsal control shows.

**Objection: stability privileges winners and entrenches wrong arrangements.** Only if warrant and defeat are omitted. The definite judgment holds in the stipulated valid unchanged case; §4 preserves genuine defects and new grounds. Losing does not erase a living reason, and winning does not provide unlimited authority over the loser. No private ownership theory or absolute winner's veto is inferred.

**Objection: every repeated lottery is now wrong.** A series of genuinely distinct opportunities, or an initially warranted two-stage protocol, is outside the settled single-opportunity comparison. Claims about the whole period's fairness require that larger period's actual relations. Calling every new opportunity the old resource would itself be a misdescription.

**Objection: formal fidelity prevents improved protection.** Not in general. The Bool model has exactly two exclusive routes and deliberately lacks the extra joint route already proved by CrossedClaims. With new joint capacity, retaining the earlier protection while adding another is an available constructive comparison under its own conditions. Equality of the small model's outputs must not be exported as a universal ban on improvement.

**Decision after review:** retain the bounded normative-memory requirement and the positive settled-case judgment. Credit the older finality and patient-ground arguments; reject a claim to have solved initial lottery weights or crossed basic-risk priorities. Beyond the completed LR and symmetric RS cases, the next unresolved normative connection is how unequal claims, or fairer exposure at the price of added basic harm, warrant an initial allocation of chances, and when a genuinely changed basic conflict outweighs a valid settlement. RS §14 already fixes a concrete remaining test: an equal-gravity comparison between (10%,40%) and (31%,31%), with an increased probability of some loss in the second option. Its arithmetic is complete; the remaining task is comparative warrant. Reliable reconstruction of those grounds is an implementation task downstream of that comparison, not a substitute for it.
