# Review of the bounded inquiry sufficiency formalization

6 October 2026. Independent AI-authored assumption and counterexample review. **Accepted at the stated mathematical and verification scope; no blocking issue found.** The prospective acceptance criteria below were fixed before reviewing the source. The reviewer did not author the formalization or rerun its compiler. Source, checker, archived logs and receipt integrity were independently inspected.

## The result being reviewed

The intended result makes the existing [bounded-policy argument](INQUIRY-SUFFICIENCY-MATH.md) precise in the common `Process` interface: when can one observation-contingent, bounded inquiry policy reach an executable response that meets a specified continuation contract in every remaining actual possibility? It must preserve the distinction between that response-relative question and acquiring a model correct for all permitted future experiments. The singleton rule in the existing full-model learner is not refuted by a common response to a narrower question.

The formalization introduces no physical event law. Its process, observations, prefix conditions and response contract are mathematical interfaces to the activity being represented. Their use does not establish that an organism acquires them, that observation costs disappear, or that a desired response is normatively justified. The [shared method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md) requires these distinctions to preserve the inquiry's actual effects, rather than silently splitting information from its material continuation.

## Prospective acceptance criteria

1. A terminal response is one common operation sequence. It is executable from every remaining candidate and satisfies the stated conditions along every actual successor path. An existentially favorable endpoint, or a separate response chosen with hidden knowledge of each state, is insufficient.
2. An admitted query is executable from every remaining candidate. An unavailable candidate cannot disappear merely because it supplies no observation. All successors compatible with the actual observed outcome must enter the next candidate set, including their changed support and other continuing conditions.
3. Initial candidates are nonempty. A live admitted query produces at least one nonempty successor set. Unreachable observation branches may terminate without a response, but their emptiness must be established; empty-set vacuity cannot establish success for a live history.
4. The policy is a separately represented, finite observation-contingent tree. Its uniform query-depth bound permits earlier terminal success. The theorem should connect that representation to the recurrence, rather than rename the recurrence as a policy.
5. The proof records every new theorem's axiom dependencies and does not introduce `axiom`, `sorry`, unsafe proof production or classical choice to conceal a constructive gap. A fresh source-bound verification receipt must be inspected before acceptance.

## Constructive treatment of unreachable branches

For arbitrary state sets, the explicit branch alternative `Empty successor ∨ W n successor` is constructively stronger than the implication `Nonempty successor → W n successor`. The latter does not itself decide whether a branch is unreachable or provide a concrete subtree for it. Finite observation types permit finite assembly of the selected subtrees; they do not alone make arbitrary successor sets decidable.

An unconditional constructive characterization may therefore use the explicit empty/live alternative. Reconciliation with the implication presentation needs a proved or parameterized branch-classification interface, such as that supplied by an explicitly finite, executable state-and-belief representation. This is a theorem scope distinction, not a new physical law or a license to claim the organism itself computes the mathematical classification. Any final account must state which version has actually been proved.

## Five substantive adverse cases

1. **Certainty without an executable response.** A singleton candidate can lack the fuel, access or other support for any acceptable response. Model identification does not imply response sufficiency.
2. **Uncertainty with a common response.** Distinct candidates can admit the same safe continuation even while differing on later experiments. Uniqueness is unnecessary for that response contract, without invalidating uniqueness for the original all-future acquisition target.
3. **Truthful inquiry changes the live question.** In the existing `four_feedback` history, an agreed next reading eliminates neither candidate but advances both. Agreement about the action just completed need not persist for the next action. Successor propagation must preserve this change instead of intersecting an unchanged list of hypotheses.
4. **Disabled alternatives cannot be filtered as evidence.** If a candidate cannot execute the proposed query, discarding it from the successor set can make an unsafe inquiry appear sufficient. The query's common executability condition must rule this out; an actual failure, where modeled, is an outcome with its own successor.
5. **Information can consume its response conditions.** An informative experiment can use the only packet, access or reserve needed for further discrimination or action. The existing [identity inquiry](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/INQUIRY-AND-MATHEMATICAL-IDENTITY.md) preserves this mechanism: pairwise distinguishability does not guarantee one executable identifying experiment. Initial labels must remain represented when the target concerns historical identity even if the current states merge.

These are requirements and already preserved conceptual or source-history counterexamples. This note does not claim five new Lean countermodel theorems unless the eventual source supplies them.

## Source and verification assessment

The [source](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/inquiry-root/sufficiency/InquirySufficiency.lean), including its later additions for seventeen declaration audits, has been independently inspected. No blocking issue was found in its proof structure or semantics. `Completes` requires actual enabledness at each prescribed prefix and safe continuation through every admitted successor, rather than an existentially favorable complete trace. `After` transports every actual successor before observation filtering. `Sound` requires inhabited candidates at live nodes and admits an abort only on a proved empty branch. `Policy` and `Within` are independent syntax and depth predicates; the explicit finite-table witness proof supplies the constructive step in the recurrence-to-policy direction.

`Execution` follows the very same native `Process` steps. The execution theorems establish a possible complete execution from every live candidate, the goal at every complete execution of a sound policy, and a `SafeTrace` recording the contract on every actual query and response edge. Their scope is not a guarantee that an external scheduler executes that policy. The inquiry-depth bound does not count the terminal response's separately finite word. The observation can depend on the whole transition, `sense s a t`, rather than only the endpoint. Its actual production, retention and calibration remain application obligations.

`sufficiency_iff_implication_form` correctly gives the exact equivalence under `ClassifiedThrough`. That interface classifies descendants for every action and observation through the stated depth. It is a sufficient interface and can be stronger than classifying only a chosen policy's reachable branches; necessity is not claimed. `nonemptyDecisionFromList` constructs the required single-belief decision from a list that faithfully enumerates that belief, without requiring equality testing on the state type. It neither finds the enumeration nor proves arbitrary processes have finite executable successor sets. These obligations remain explicit and do not invalidate the unconditional constructive policy theorem.
