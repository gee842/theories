## Exact target needed for the proposed formulas

Use the unchanged [sixteen-event law](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/formal/FormedSelection.lean). The initial configuration is valid and has an active resident. Let `F` be common fuel, `E` source and `L` the initial local-store bit, treated as zero or one. A separately specified prior loss fixes an A deficit `n`, with `n` no greater than the initial unoccupied precursor capacity.

The producer's endpoint increases total A count by at least `n`, preserves total H count and retains the active resident. The constructions below achieve exactly `n`, so the same frontier also applies to exact restoration. The endpoint does **not** additionally have to preserve the initial local-store value or restore an initially damaged site. These qualifications matter. If an initially damaged site must also end intact, the no-repair alternative below is unavailable, including when `n = 0`.

For this target the proposed minimum initial common fuel is:

\[
F_{\min}=\max(n-L-E,0)\qquad\text{if initially intact},
\]

\[
F_{\min}=\min\{\max(n-L,0),\;1+\max(n-L-E,0)\}
\qquad\text{if initially damaged}.
\]

Thus damaged-site feasibility is precisely

\[
n\le F+L\quad\lor\quad
\bigl(F\ge1\ \land\ n+1\le F+L+E\bigr),
\]

subject to the stated material and resident conditions. For `n = 0`, the empty history witnesses feasibility with zero fuel.

## Independent necessity argument

The argument must cover destructive, replacement and formation events as well as the favorable copy sequence.

Only `formA`, `bulkCopyA` and `localCopyA` increase total A count. Association preserves that count, and wear can only reduce it. Therefore a net gain of `n` requires at least `n` A-increasing events.

`formA` and `bulkCopyA` each consume one common unit. The same common account must also fund every repair and any formation, association, H copying or common leakage that occurs. Local A copying consumes the local store; the only operation replenishing that store is capture. Initial local support plus captured source therefore bounds local A copying from above, even when some local support instead funds H copying or leaks away. Source decreases once per capture and has no replenishing native event.

Consequently every successful route satisfies `n ≤ F + L + E`. If the initial site is damaged and no capture occurs, the stronger bound is `n ≤ F + L`. If capture does occur, its first occurrence requires a preceding repair: no other event makes the site intact. That repair consumes a common unit unavailable for A formation or bulk copying. Hence `F ≥ 1` and `n + 1 ≤ F + L + E`.

This lower bound does not assume H or resident preservation; it therefore also bounds paths that temporarily dismantle or replace those structures. Such diversions cannot evade the resource inequality.

## Independent sufficiency argument

With an intact site, use the available initial local unit, then alternate capture with local A copying as needed and available. Use bulk A copying for the remainder. The active resident supplies an A template throughout. The precursor condition supplies space for every required increase; this sequence never wears H or changes the resident.

With a damaged site, one alternative uses the initial local unit and bulk copying without repairing. The other performs one repair and then uses the intact-site construction. These witness the two displayed bounds. Unnecessary capture after the last required copy is not part of the construction.

This is existential finite reachability. It is not a universal guarantee over adversarial event schedules, a probability estimate or a claim that an organism controls every enabled channel. The original [stochastic process](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/process.py) separately specifies competing wear, damage and other rates; those scheduling claims do not follow from the Lean event relation alone.

## Actual-history discriminator and substantive rival

The proposed witness begins at the already established 94-event formed ancestor: active resident, A count 39, H count 39, intact site, full local store, common fuel 293 and source 216. A declared continuation of 292 common-leak events, one local leak and one free-A wear reaches an intact active configuration with common fuel 1, source 216, an empty local store and one A deficit. This is a selected admitted depletion history, not an assertion about typical stochastic behavior or an automatic consequence of repair.

From that same state:

- capture followed by local A copying restores A with common fuel 1 and source 215;
- bulk A copying restores A with common fuel 0 and source 216.

Before a subsequent identical damage event, these endpoints agree on A/H counts, active resident, intactness, empty local store and total energy 216. Their support composition differs. After the damage, the former can repair and the latter cannot restore intactness under any further native history, because common fuel has no replenishment route.

The bulk route is a substantive rival, not a freely toggled predicate: it accomplishes the same immediate reconstruction using an actual admitted mechanism. At the rich ancestor it can also leave ample common support. Therefore capture is not generically necessary, and choosing the bulk route is not generically defective. The later independently specified repair obligation distinguishes the depleted comparison. An aggregate-support-only description omits a causally consequential difference.

The witness does not establish that A copies are an ethical good, that every repair ought to be performed, or that selected leakage and damage represent a justified activity. A process can manufacture burdens or preserve itself at other processes' expense. Those possibilities remain subject to the [whole-process grounding account](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-INTEGRATION.md).
