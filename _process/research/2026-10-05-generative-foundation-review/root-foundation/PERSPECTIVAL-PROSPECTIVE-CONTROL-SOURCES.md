# Prospective agreement and continuing control: primary-source assessment

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored source assessment for the branch proposed in [the ratification integration](PERSPECTIVAL-RATIFICATION-INTEGRATION.md#7-what-is-gained-and-what-is-still-supplied), following the [nondual method](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). Three primary philosophical sources are used. This is neither empirical confirmation nor legal guidance; no mathematical result is proposed.

The universal inference being tested is **whole qualitative realization → the justified scope of prospective practical authority**. Formation through another's contribution can produce genuine powers of commitment, acquired capabilities and continuing dependence together. The question is what within that history warrants a restriction, including one knowingly chosen before the capability existed. Neither causal contribution nor an antecedent act of agreement supplies an unexplained universal answer.

## 1. A serious rival: consent can transform unfair terms

Benjamin Ferguson, “Exploitation's grounding problem,” *Economics & Philosophy* 41(2), 357–375 (2025; first published online 20 November 2024), [DOI and publisher text](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266267124000397). The full publisher HTML was accessible. Relevant passages inspected: §§1–4, 7–10; section locators are used rather than inferring PDF pagination.

Ferguson argues that morally valid consent can override the pro tanto wrong of distributively unfair terms. His argument uses powers over labour and goods, will/interest theories of rights, and analogies with donations and harmful purchases. It is distinct from merely being better off than without an offer. The provider's liberty not to transact matters to the interest-theory objection; §7 makes coercion depend on a morally impermissible threat, so the underlying duties require prior adjudication.

**Exact limit:** §9 expressly leaves other possible wrongs, including transactions involving noncommodifiable goods, unsettled; third-party effects can also defeat overall permissibility. §2 does not deny all inalienable rights. Thus this source challenges dismissal of genuinely transformative consent, but does not establish alienability of every future power. Its rights and consent premises are substantive ethical commitments, not conclusions derived from generative organization.

## 2. Commitment and limits can share a justification

John Stuart Mill, *On Liberty* (1859), chapter V, [primary text in Project Gutenberg's Walter Scott edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/34901/34901-h/34901-h.htm). The inspected discussion is the text's marked pp. 194–198. This is a later reading edition, not an inspected 1859 facsimile. The prefatory introduction is by W. L. Courtney and is not used as Mill's argument.

Mill treats keeping voluntary engagements as the general rule despite later changes of will. He rejects voluntary slavery because comprehensive alienation defeats the liberty that justifies permitting choice. He immediately allows ordinary limitations needed for life; he does not infer that every binding commitment contradicts liberty. On pp. 196–198, he complicates Humboldt's easy dissolution proposal: invited reliance and effects on other people create moral obligations, although not unconditional demands to continue at every cost.

**Exact limit:** this argues from a substantive justification of liberty. Partial control over learned skills is not identified with total slavery. Its extent and justification need a separate argument. Nor does the passage derive the amount owed for training, the legitimacy of a particular restraint, or universal authority for every intelligence.

## 3. Advance delegation need not mean uncontrolled power

Danielle M. Wenner, “Nondomination and the Limits of Relational Autonomy,” *IJFAB* 13(2), 28–48 (2020), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3138/ijfab.13.2.2020-01-08), [author-hosted preprint](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/dwenner/papers/2019WennerIJFAB.pdf). The [author bibliography](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/dwenner/papers.html) verifies final publication; the inspected 36-page preprint is not the final typeset version. Locators below count its title page as physical page 1. This reuses, rather than adds independent evidence to, [the earlier assessment](PERSPECTIVAL-RATIFICATION-SOURCES.md).

In §5, physical pp. 19–21, Wenner distinguishes benevolent noninterference from protection against uncontrolled interference. She also permits controlled restrictions: her examples include legitimate collective procedures and previously chosen medical delegation operating during incapacity. The latter matters here because authorization can persist without contemporaneous exercise of a veto.

**Exact limit:** the passage does not adjudicate permanent guild control or prescribe a universal revocation condition. Its nondomination standard requires substantive defense. We use the distinction between formed choice and organized control within one relational history; her analytical internal/external vocabulary is not imported as an ontological division.

## 4. The distinction the prospective case must earn

The following are our case distinctions and questions, not results reported by these sources. Keep four judgments separate: an offer benefits its accepter compared with no offer; assent meets relevant conditions for choice; the act changes a specified permission or claim; the resulting terms are justified overall. A theory may connect them, but must state the connecting premises. Calling consent “valid” while defining validity to include the desired verdict would conceal the question.

Preserve the strong rival's facts: adequate disclosure and understanding, deliberative opportunity, intact competence, no breached undertaking, and provision that is optional under the rival's proposed account. Do not then defeat it by introducing an unannounced duty to offer unconditional training. The provider's effort, opportunity costs, continuing participation and capacity to undertake further work are actual features of the whole continuation. Their ethical significance must be considered alongside the learner's developed capability. Conversely, describing aid as optional does not yet show that every proposed condition is an authorized object of exchange.

| Prospective arrangement | What must be explained without stipulating the verdict |
|---|---|
| A defined contribution or period of reciprocal service supports the training. | Why the contribution and its completion have this significance, including burdens and what was made possible. Finite duration alone does not prove fairness. |
| A specified premiere restriction supports preparation of one shared performance. | Whether scope and duration track that actual undertaking, and which expectations or contributions depend on it. A restriction can enable meaningful commitment. |
| Continuing affiliation requires participation in an ongoing shared practice. | Whether the particular good exists through that participation. Departure may genuinely end that good; separability cannot be assumed by renaming it. |
| Guild discretion indefinitely controls ordinary uses of acquired skill after the stated formation return is completed. | What the continuing discretion is for, how it is bounded, whose decisions it can govern and why initial agreement can establish that extent of authority. No loss of competence or broken promise is needed to pose this question. |

“Separable skill” here is a specified causal comparison: the learner can exercise the capability without further guild activity or resources, while the guild retains a mechanism for blocking or penalizing its use. This does not imply that the skill had an independent origin, that it has no effects on others, or that separability by itself establishes ownership. The alternative shared-performance case preserves a good whose realization still requires the other participants. These are different continuations within the same formative history, not two fundamentally separate kinds of being.

## 5. The challenge that could change the candidate

The strongest objection is not that the participant failed to understand. It is that future-directed binding is itself a worthwhile exercise of agency, and that a provider can condition optional assistance on that exercise. A candidate condemning every nonrevocable arrangement would fail ordinary commitments and advance delegation. A candidate permitting every clearly disclosed term would owe an account of why comprehensive alienation, effects on others or powers beyond the chosen undertaking nevertheless lie within the relevant authority.

The discriminating next test should therefore hold informed agreement constant and vary **the object, purpose, extent and completion conditions of authorized control**. Permit the favorable comparison to include a restriction that remains binding despite later regret. Then ask whether indefinite discretionary control over ordinary acquired capability is relevantly different after specific reciprocal undertakings have been completed. If the only answer is that indefinite control is disliked, dependent, or unlike an imagined untouched self, the proposed distinction has not been warranted.

A further countercase must remain available: continued broad coordination might protect an actual shared undertaking, or an initially identified completion point might omit a still-relevant contribution or obligation. Evidence or argument establishing those relations would revise the proposed condemnation. Conversely, proof that a training arrangement would not exist without its restrictive term explains feasibility; it does not alone decide whether that arrangement ought to exist. The moral liberty to refuse provision and the moral power to confer continuing control each need a ground.

This branch advances the universal inquiry by locating its substantive dependency: **why should the particular organization of formed agency, contribution and future authority warrant these powers and limits?** Consent, liberty, reciprocity and nondomination are competing or partly compatible proposed explanations. None is established universally by source agreement, by accurately described formation, or by expanding the model's boundary. The sources sharpen the test and block shortcuts; the whole-grounding argument must still defend its ethical relation.
