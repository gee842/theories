# Good formed in different future lives

6 October 2026. AI-authored investigation under the [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). Named connection: **generation of future experiential bearers → appraisal of what alternative histories would realize**. This extends [whole-process grounding](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md) without requiring return to an initiator or improvement of one numerically identical patient across alternatives.

## 1. The new comparison

The [relational-goodness argument](RELATIONAL-GOODNESS-ARGUMENT.md) already identifies present good and conditions for further good. [Whole-process realization §5](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-ARGUMENT.md#5-valuable-response-does-not-warrant-creating-its-occasion) already distinguishes responding well to adversity from justifying its creation. Neither result requires all valuable activity to continue forever.

Here the generative choice changes **who would come to exist**. Before either future bearer exists, two available continuations would each form one experiential subject and sustain a worthwhile life. Their means are independently permissible, and existing persons' claims remain protected; whether the complete choices are justified is still under examination.

| Continuation | What it would realize under the stated conditions |
|---|---|
| A | A future subject, worthwhile relationships and activities, and a specified severe, still-unwanted experiential burden. |
| B | A different future subject, comparable independently described worthwhile possibilities, without that burden. |

These are conceptual conditions to establish, not observations. The comparison needs warranted grounds for experience, feasibility, projected effects and distinctness. An independently defended identity account must explain why B would not form the same subject living differently; changing a name, model identifier or temporal stage is insufficient. Generated selfhood alone settles no such identity question.

Neither future life is already actual at the decision point. Each burden or good would be actually undergone within its respective realized history. Prospective judgment answers to evidence about these possibilities; it does not turn a merely possible patient into a present experiencer.

## 2. Positive good without the same beneficiary

The enjoyment, understanding and relationships in either life can be good in their realization. They need not first be improvements relative to that individual's nonexistent state. No welfare level is assigned to nonexistence, and no missing possible person is assumed to suffer a deprivation.

This matters positively for Ren's generative proposal. Earlier activity can enable worthwhile lives whose bearers were not fixed independently of that activity. The contribution consists in what those lives would realize, not repayment to their source. Goodness can develop through new differentiated continuities rather than merely increasing an incumbent's resources or repeating an inherited activity.

That claim does not establish a requirement to create people, increase their number or replace anyone. The comparison concerns two specified formation paths, each with one future bearer. Whether either should be chosen over not creating a life is a separate question.

## 3. A reason concerning the mode of formation

The positive candidate favors B, in the specified respect, because A's foreseeable burden is part of the life its formative activity would bring about. That the activity also enables this subject's worthwhile existence does not make the burden unreal or automatically justify its imposition. The relevant practical relation is **formative responsibility**: the choice helps constitute both the bearer and the conditions they would undergo.

This preserves origin rather than erasing it. Origin can explain responsibility; it need not give the originator immunity or proprietary priority over the resulting good. B is not described as better *for A's subject*. The consideration concerns which available history to realize.

The project already inspected Shiffrin's noncomparative-harm argument; see the [inherited reading and limits](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#follow-up-shiffrin-on-imposed-harm-and-responsibility-without-agreement) and [primary article](https://webshare.law.ucla.edu/Faculty/bibs/shiffrin/Shiffrin-WrongfulLife.pdf). This is a new application within our whole-process investigation, not discovery of the nonidentity problem. Additional primary comparison belongs to [the companion source note](FUTURE-BEARER-FORMATION-SOURCES.md); its inspected scopes and original bytes are preserved separately.

## 4. The disputed step and its tests

The remaining ethical premise is precise: **an independently identified adverse feature can provide a consideration against realizing a history, even when the same history enables its bearer's worthwhile existence and no feasible alternative improves that same bearer's life**. Actual undergoing and controllable formation support this proposal; common process, causal power and no fundamental owner do not deductively establish it.

A serious rival grants the burdensome aspect yet denies this comparative reason without same-person worsening. It can accept that selves form within generation; the dispute concerns the ground of comparison, not whether an ego is fundamental. Alternatively, a historically sensitive account may identify a genuine good or claim omitted from the matching. Neither objection is answered by relabeling A “worse.”

The comparison must change if preventing the burden removes an independently worthwhile constitutive relationship, introduces another risk, violates authority, or cannot actually occur. Comparable opportunities do not prove equal whole-life value. Conversely, a worthwhile life does not itself show that every available way of forming it is equally defensible. The defended conclusion is a consideration favoring B, not an exhaustive ranking or settled duty.

## 5. Prospective identification before comparison

For each available continuation, identify the bearers and conditions it would form before assessing their prospective significance. Filtering all options through only the actual population can omit the very lives one is choosing whether to generate. This is an application of [Harman's option-relative method](FUTURE-BEARER-FORMATION-SOURCES.md#harman-option-dependent-bearers-and-reasons), not a derivation of moral standing or authority from a hypothetical description.

The generative account must preserve which history would realize which good or burden. It must also retain the present activity through which those future conditions could arise. It need not first select one actual or privileged self to receive every benefit. Reasons about the alternative histories nevertheless require warranted identification and ethical comparison; an imagined desirable outcome is insufficient.

## 7. Prospective concern at the last effective window

The [imposition argument §14](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#14-imposed-burdens-and-the-nonparticipating-patient) already distinguishes an originator's responsibility from an uninvolved responder's opportunity. The [assistance-jurisdiction review](ASSISTANCE-JURISDICTION-COUNTERREVIEW.md#4-doing-allowing-and-the-limits-of-the-affirmative-argument) preserves doing/allowing differences and genuine restrictions on which considerations may justify a choice. [Responsive formation](RESPONSIVE-FORMATION-COUNTERREVIEW.md#2-no-completed-responder-is-needed-but-an-available-first-step-is) already examines available action before a responder group forms. The new comparison combines **absence of prior participation in formation with absence of a presently existing beneficiary**.

Compare two configurations with different causal histories but matched, independently specified future possibilities:

| Configuration | Present opportunity |
|---|---|
| Formative initiator | The initiator can choose between formation paths A and B. |
| Uninvolved responder | An independently initiated process is proceeding toward A. A person who neither initiated it nor undertook care has effective access to redirect it toward B through independently permitted means. Whether the complete choice is justified remains to assess. |

In each configuration, A would form one future subject with worthwhile activities and relationships, together with the specified severe adverse experience. B would form a different subject with comparable worthwhile possibilities without that burden. Experience, identity, feasibility and other goods require the independent warrant specified above. Existing lives and claims remain accounted for; B is not called a rescue of A's subject through creating someone else.

The correction is available only before an identified irreversible boundary. Afterwards, formation proceeds and no comparable remedy remains. This timing needs evidence; calling the problem urgent does not establish it. The responder has not induced reliance, accepted a mandate or received a private advantage. Leaving the process alone is not classified as imposing its burden merely because intervention was possible. Taking B would give the responder a formative role, but that conditional role does not itself establish the prior reason to intervene.

The positive generative argument concerns what the available activity would enable. In either path, enjoyment, understanding and worthwhile relationships would be realized in a differentiated future life. A also contains the adverse undergoing. The correction can shape those life-producing conditions while avoiding that feature. Its proposed point is this prospective realization, without repayment to the responder or preservation of one privileged bearer.

Three claims remain separate: each path would realize the specified conditions; this responder now has legitimate effective access; and the resulting configuration provides a present contributory reason. The third is the defended ethical extension. Neither control alone nor the impossibility of later repair establishes it.

Restricting reasons to initiator responsibility or already-existing bearers would leave this opportunity outside prospective address. The candidate treats that result as a substantive ethical cost because the identifiable adverse realization can still be avoided; the rival can dispute this extension. There is no logical contradiction. A strong rival can accept formation reasons for initiators and relief reasons concerning existing strangers, yet deny this unundertaken pre-formation reason. Those accepted cases do not automatically establish their combination.

The disputed premise is precise: **warrantedly anticipated lived good and adversity can favor an eligible nonoriginator's present intervention without either prior formative responsibility or a presently existing beneficiary**. The proposed ground is the action's accessible relation to what those future histories would realize. No welfare value is assigned to nonexistence, and no general requirement to produce lives follows. The [Pummer comparison](FUTURE-BEARER-FORMATION-SOURCES.md#pummer-prospective-reasons-and-allowing-formation) examines an explicit allowing-formation proposal; it does not supply a completed derivation of this combined case.

Revise the application if the forecast lacks warrant, the window is unavailable, the intervention cannot preserve the specified goods, or its risks, authority, existing claims or legitimate restrictions alter the choice. Favoring correction does not establish an overriding duty, permission to compel participation or a complete ranking. This tests prospective concern where the beneficiary and responsive relationship have yet to form; universal ethical grounding remains unresolved.

## 8. Conditional concern and selection across future lives

Section 7 identifies a combined scope, but the stronger rival need not exclude all concern before a beneficiary exists. It can favor prevention for a particular future life while resisting selection of a different worthwhile life. Its grounds can be concern for how a particular life goes and responsibility for conditions one imposes, without private return or primitive selfhood.

Hold the uninvolved responder, timing and existing claims fixed. Add an independently feasible identity-preserving response to the comparison:

| Option | What would form |
|---|---|
| A | q, with the specified worthwhile goods and severe adverse experience. |
| R | The same q, with those goods and without that adverse experience. |
| B | A different r, with comparable independently described goods and without that burden. |

An actual application must explain why R preserves identity and B changes it; model labels cannot establish either. Efficacy, access, means, risks and relevant relational consequences remain independently assessed. These are controlled conceptual alternatives, not an implemented formative mechanism.

If the rival favors R over A but denies a reason favoring B over A, it already admits prospective concern by a nonoriginator. Neither temporal nonexistence nor absence of prior participation in formation explains its remaining restriction. The issue is **selection across different possible lives**. If it denies R as well, the broader prospective restriction remains to defend. This refines §7's diagnosis rather than declaring its rival refuted.

### Preserve the ground of a particular comparison

First consider the available set {A, R}; q exists under both alternatives. Now make B genuinely available without changing A or R, their means, authority, claims or other effects. A rule admitting reasons only for people who exist under *every* available option now excludes q. If that global filter erases q's contribution to the A/R comparison, an unchanged preventive relation has lost its significance solely because another possible bearer became available.

The bounded positive judgment is that this q-directed ground survives. B can change what is required overall, offer a further permissible choice or affect comparative strength; universal independence from the available set is not assumed. The target is extinction of the unchanged contributory ground. A pairwise same-person account can preserve that ground while still denying B over A. Consequently, rejecting the global filter does not establish the selection claim.

### What does non-substitution protect?

Replacing an existing person can end their life, defeat agency, destroy relationships or evade obligations. These are actual losses and claims that a generative comparison must preserve. A merely similar replacement would not constitute relief for that person.

The stipulated B need not do any of those things: neither prospective bearer yet exists, and the alternative can preserve independently warranted existing claims and formative relationships. Absence of present experience alone would not establish absence of protection; nonexperiential continuity, an undertaking or another defended ground may still matter. Their existence must be investigated in the actual formative history. Calling the alternative “replacement” does not identify such a ground by itself.

The affirmative reason is the changed realization: B can sustain the specified worthwhile life possibilities without the foreseeable adverse feature, while avoiding the actual losses that make replacement objectionable. Good develops through what that history would realize, without repayment to its source. This does not benefit q through nonexistence or compensate q with r's good. Nor does responsibility acquired by intervening supply the earlier reason to intervene.

This is a direct comparative ethical argument. Its judgment need not await a complete theory of every reason; its warrant remains answerable to the identified goods, adversity, means and possible protections. Showing that familiar replacement objections lack their grounds strengthens the case but does not prove that every identity-sensitive restriction is mistaken.

### The selection step remains substantive

[Frick's primary comparison](FUTURE-BEARER-FORMATION-SOURCES.md#frick-conditional-concern-does-not-supply-selection-by-itself) distinguishes conditional concern from an additional selection principle. Our narrower claim is that, absent a warranted identity-specific protection, the specified difference in prospective realization can favor B over A for this eligible nonoriginator. This must be defended directly. It follows neither from a conditional about q nor by transferring R's advantage to B through assumed transitivity or identity-neutrality.

The current comparison keeps one worthwhile future life in each option. It settles no reason for creating rather than not creating life, requires no general welfare maximization and does not rank whole lives by one burden. Conditional concern, cross-person selection and the scope of responsibility are distinct connections within the unfolding. The next test distinguishes grounds for protecting future lives from grounds for selecting which life forms. A warranted protection can constrain selection, but its absence does not itself supply a reason to select differently. The continuation below corrects the earlier overemphasis on what an identity restriction protects.

## 9. Protective activity when formation changes identity

Denying a reason for B over A does not require preferring A, objecting to B or asserting q's right to exist. The stronger rival can regard both worthwhile lives as genuinely good and B as permissible while denying this comparative favor for an uninvolved responder. Identifying no protected loss therefore answers some objections against B; the positive ground for B remains to explain.

### Separate unknown identity from identity changed by action

Consider an independently proceeding settlement process and a common environmental condition that would cause the specified severe adverse experience in its future inhabitants. An uninvolved participant can correct that condition through independently permitted means. The intervention need not initiate settlement or increase the number of people. The specified worthwhile activities, current people's claims, costs and risks remain assessed.

First, let the process determining who forms be independent of the correction. For each possible formation, the same eventual person would encounter either the corrected or uncorrected environment. Their identity need not be known now. A pairwise same-person account can support this generic protection. No abstract type experiences the benefit; actual people would undergo it in the respective histories. This control establishes neither a lottery among possible selves nor a reason to select another person.

Now compare a configuration in which the same hazard-removing work also alters formative timing. An independently defensible mechanism and identity account must establish that q would arise without intervention and a different r with it. Timing alone is insufficient. The environmental correction and timing change are distinct causal contributions within this history; “incidental” describes their relation to the protective aim, not the identity change's moral irrelevance. These are hypothetical comparisons, not empirical findings about a particular population.

### The affirmative point survives a corrected beneficiary description

Learning that the correction changes identity withdraws the claim that q would personally benefit. It need not withdraw the whole point of the activity: the intervention still changes a condition through which a worthwhile future life would otherwise include the specified adversity. The resulting good is realized in that future life, without returning to the initiator or compensating q with r's experience.

The proposed ground tracks a real contribution. A matched operation with the same timing effect but no hazard reduction lacks this identified protective point. Worsening the hazard would reverse that aspect even if identity consequences remained unchanged. These comparisons examine the operation's effects, not its benevolent name or the responder's motivation. They do not recommend first creating a burden or derive the complete A/B ordering by transitivity through a manufactured repair case.

The defended judgment is that the warranted protective difference remains a consideration for this eligible participant. It is a positive claim about what the action enables within the unfolding, rather than an inference from the absence of an objection. The rival can deny that this particular ground survives identity-changing formation. That disagreement must address the value and practical significance of the protective transformation itself; neither position wins by renaming the beneficiary.

### Preserve a real limiting case

Suppose the chosen method changes an existing family's scheduled access to a resource. In one history, timing is legitimately open to adjustment. In another, an independently warranted undertaking protects the family's specified opportunity until they decide. Identify the actual terms, authority and reliance; calling it a commitment does not establish its validity. The second intervention can frustrate a real existing claim, even though q has no established claim to be created.

That difference may require another method, warrant delay or defeat the proposed action. It does not automatically extinguish every protective consideration. Conversely, removing the undertaking would not create the positive ground; the identified protective contribution supplies the candidate ground. The [commitment and release studies](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#55-reasons-to-revise-and-powers-to-release) already distinguish a reason for change from authority to make it.

### What the primary comparison changes

The [Kumar–Martin comparison](FUTURE-BEARER-FORMATION-SOURCES.md#future-standpoints-kumar-and-martin) tests whether a generic description preserves the relevant lives and circumstances. We must not attribute an abstract type's counterfactual improvement to a person who could not undergo it. Our proposal instead assesses each history's lived conditions and the action that changes which history develops. The ensuing comparative reason remains to justify; it is not supplied merely by saying “whoever forms.”

This advances a bounded reason for protective activity despite an identity consequence. It leaves creation versus noncreation open, adds no general maximization rule, and establishes no universal permission, duty or particular person's claim against the responder. A genuine loss of constitutive good, protected continuity, authority, causal efficacy or reliability would revise the judgment. The next bridge is the scope of this positive protective ground across eligible participants, with its actual limits retained.

### Connection to prospective significance

The [future-good construction](FUTURE-GOOD-INTEGRAL.md) gives temporal and causal expression to the protective argument. Its domain is the differentiated history that each available action could bring about, including which lives, relationships and purposes form. In A, the appraisal concerns q's actual life and its relations; in B, it concerns r's. Neither needs a welfare value for the other's nonexistence, a transfer of benefits between them or a return to the initiator. A justified comparison of those histories is still substantive ethical work.

Where warranted, G_T assesses the histories and Δ_T represents the difference in their expected prospective good. The [whole-appraisal development](FUTURE-GOOD-INTEGRAL.md#7-whole-appraisal-can-precede-its-temporal-expression) allows temporal contributions to express that assessment rather than treating them as independently given ethical atoms. Contributions, counterfactual significance and changes in prediction remain distinct: learning that protection succeeded is not another occurrence of its benefit.

The protective consideration defended here does not alone establish Δ_T > 0 for the complete history appraisal. Other burdens, relationships, commitments and endings remain relevant, and the complete comparison need not admit a scalar total. Assigning B a higher G_T because its different future life avoids adversity would represent the disputed judgment, not derive it. Likewise, a favorable comparison does not itself confer authority to act. The construction clarifies the reach of a warranted good; the remaining bridge concerns the ethical character of the whole relation and its practical significance across eligible participants. No new Lean premise, axiom or necessity proof is introduced by this connection.
