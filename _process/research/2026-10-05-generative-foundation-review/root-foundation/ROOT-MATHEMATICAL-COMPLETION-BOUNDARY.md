## What is actually excluded

Fix one carrier `A` and a reading relation `E : A → A → Prop`. The first argument indexes descriptions, the second their query domain. These are roles in one mathematical representation, not fundamentally separate kinds. `Represents E a P` means that description `a` agrees with predicate `P` on every query.

`predicate_diagonal_unrepresented` constructs the predicate `fun x => ¬ E x x` and proves that no description in this same family represents it. `no_full_predicate_coverage` excludes representation of every predicate on that carrier. More precisely, `no_diagonal_closed_complete_family` needs only an admitted family containing this particular diagonal and a claimed representation for all its members. The diagonal-admission condition is explicit; it is not asserted of every meaningful language or generative process.

The source also gives a set/function version of Lawvere's theorem: `fixed_point_of_complete` takes pointwise coverage of every function `A → Y` by an `A`-indexed family and derives a fixed point for every `Y → Y`. `no_complete_fixedpoint_free` and the Boolean specialization display the contradiction when the value domain has a fixed-point-free operation. These results require no effectiveness, finite alphabet, arithmetic coding or enumeration hypothesis. They do require their stated full representation or diagonal-closure contract.

## The constructive escape remains open

`external_reading_covers` represents every predicate of a lower carrier by using the **supplied full predicate type itself** as the description domain. This does not contradict same-carrier noncoverage, and does not derive that predicate domain's admission from a generative principle.

`extendReading` instead adds one explicit description. `extension_preserves` retains the meaning of every old description on the old query domain, and `diagonal_extension_strict` shows that the new description represents a predicate omitted before. The query domain stays fixed. This is not a self-enclosing universal evaluator and not a proof of a completed hierarchy of all mathematical subjects.

The positive lesson is specific: widening a representation can add genuine content without changing old meanings. The negative lesson is equally specific: no final same-carrier representation can satisfy the tested closure contract. A universal theory may be schematic, stratified or interpreted externally, but the relevant admission and interpretation principles then remain part of its proposed explanation.
