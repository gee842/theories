# Does a generative self supply the ground of concern beyond itself?

6 October 2026. AI-authored argument audit continuing the [entire 慧根 objective](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/HUIGEN.md). The named connection is **formed selfhood and reasons concerning future experience → reasons for numerically distinct patients → justified concern beyond continuity and accepted roles**. This follows the [practical-necessity audit](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md) by examining what could make the self-relation an essential ground, rather than merely writing another self-only reason table.

**Result:** future-directed concern already reaches experience the deliberator is not currently undergoing. Under an explicit branch-preservation premise, concern for two distinct future continuers defeats strict numerical identity as a necessary condition of every practical reason. Accepted delegation supplies a separate route to genuinely other-directed, role-bound reasons. Neither result establishes that continuity and commitment exhaust the grounds of reasons, nor that their absence cancels every reason. The decisive remaining case is a reliably identified sufferer outside those relations. Generative selfhood helps identify the actual relational grounds and their limits; common substance or ancestry cannot substitute for a justified extension.

## 1. Selfhood must be explained without erasing patients

The [governing account](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md#from-interacting-differences-to-consequential-organization) treats an organization as able to continue through replacement and reconstruction. The [theory's account of centers](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/THEORY.md#3-a-situation-with-differentiated-centers) permits overlap, division and merger, while explicitly leaving phenomenal experience and personal identity unsettled. No theorem about arbitrary dynamical centers establishes the number or identity of conscious subjects.

The present cases are therefore declared conceptual models. Where a case contains a sentient patient, that status and its relevant aversive experience are premises to be independently identified in any application. Distinct future centers remain distinct patients even if they have similar memories, common ancestry or a shared mechanism. This is not a proposal that anyone literally experiences everyone's suffering.

Keep three candidate reason-grounds separate:

- **Present ownership:** the deliberator is undergoing the aversive episode now.
- **Continuing agency or personal continuity:** a future patient belongs to a specified continuing life, project or psychologically connected process.
- **Patient-grounded significance:** the aversive condition itself supplies a reason to a deliberator able to affect it, whether or not the patient is continuous with that deliberator.

The first is narrower than ordinary prudence. The second can explain a substantial extension without establishing the third. A relation's having formed causally does not make it normatively irrelevant: a promise, responsibility, attachment or shared project can be both generated and reason-giving. The task is to identify what those relations justify, including whether they supply extra reasons or exclude other grounds entirely.

## 2. The prospective anchor: a real extension beyond present feeling

Suppose an agent a, presently free of pain, has warranted information that a future patient q will undergo an avoidable aversive episode unless a acts now. The standard prudent case grants a a pro tanto reason to prevent the episode in its continuing life. Hold fixed genuine feasibility, knowledge, the future episode and any competing reasons. This is a normative anchor to defend, not a theorem that every functioning scientist must be prudent.

**Prospective implication.** If that future-directed reason is valid, currently undergoing the episode is not necessary for having a reason against it. The reason can concern a condition represented in advance rather than felt by the present decision maker. ∎

This matters for the generative programme. A formed model can make a not-yet-experienced consequence operative in current selection. A present response can protect a later patient who cannot yet report, consent or reciprocate. Thus neither current sensation nor current speech by the beneficiary is required in every accepted case of concern.

It does not follow that future-self concern and concern for an unrelated patient have exactly the same ground. The future case may involve continuing agency, a continuing body, a long-term project, a justified commitment or another independently significant relation. Temporal distance is not automatically the only difference. A presentist who rejects the prudent anchor must be assessed at that disagreement; their scientific competence alone does not establish the anchor for them.

Nor does a future prediction need certainty to matter. The argument can be stated under warranted uncertainty, but the grounding question should first be tested with the relevant future facts settled. Otherwise uncertainty about which later person is oneself can disguise an expected private stake as a de re reason for each distinct patient.

## 3. A constructive branching argument against strict identity-only concern

Consider a one-successor case in which q retains the continuities offered as sufficient to justify a's prospective reason. Now consider a two-successor case containing distinct future patients q1 and q2. Each retains those specified continuities; their relevant aversive conditions, opportunities for prevention and other consequences are matched. Neither merely represents the other's experience. The case stipulates no new resource conflict or third-party harm from protecting either one.

The argument requires three explicit premises:

1. **Prospective anchor:** the offered continuity and harm-ground justify a reason in the one-successor case.
2. **Branch nonerasure:** adding an equally qualified second continuer does not erase the first patient's reason when the offered sufficient ground, applicability conditions and defeaters for that reason remain unchanged.
3. **Branch parity:** exchanging the two fully matched branches, while preserving a and every relevant relationship, preserves the reason judgment.

**Branching-prudence lemma.** Under these premises, a has a reason regarding each distinct future patient. Therefore numerical identity with a cannot be necessary for every one of those reasons.

**Proof:** branch nonerasure retains the reason concerning q1. The branch exchange and parity premise yield the reason concerning q2. If numerical identity were necessary for both reasons, a would be identical to q1 and q2; transitivity and symmetry of identity would imply q1=q2, contrary to the case. Thus at least one granted reason concerns a numerically distinct patient. ∎

Here a, q1 and q2 name putative persons assessed for identity through time, not merely time-stamped states. Merely defining different stages as different objects would make the conclusion cheap and irrelevant. The theorem uses ordinary numerical identity and the stipulated distinctness of the two later patients; it does not settle which, if either, is identical to a.

This is a genuine conditional extension beyond the earlier diagonal argument. The new reason is supported by an already accepted prospective ground and a claim about how that ground survives formation of more than one continuer. It is not obtained merely by renaming a self-pair as an other-pair. It also differs from a role lottery: both future patients exist in the case, and the conclusion concerns reasons for both, not just a probability of secretly being one.

### Where the positive argument could fail

An identity theorist can hold that **being the unique continuer** was part of the original sufficient ground. On that account, adding a second continuer changes a constitutive premise; branch nonerasure does not apply. Another critic can accept concern for one designated continuer while defending a genuine asymmetry in the history. Such a case would fail the stipulated parity premise.

The strongest challenge to the uniqueness restriction is contrastive: why should creating a second, otherwise independent continuer cancel every reason against the first one's unchanged future suffering? If the proposed explanation concerns that patient's experience and an unchanged continuing relationship, an additional remote branch appears unable to remove either feature. A position that admits those features were already sufficient cannot also cancel their consequence. But if it defends uniqueness itself as reason-giving, the dispute must address that ground; it cannot be won by pretending that numerical identity is a purely local property.

The argument does not establish a universally agreed verdict about speculative fission. Its achievement is sharper: **either branch-preserving prospective concern extends beyond numerical identity, or a defender of strict identity must justify why branch formation destroys the former reason.** This makes the self-ground hypothesis answerable to a specific generative change rather than an unexplained personal label.

## 4. A continuity-grounded model that survives the extension

The branching result does not force universal standing. A model can make the relevant relation more generous than numerical identity while retaining an exclusive boundary.

Let C(a,p) mean that p stands in a specified directed continuity relation to a. In a generative realization, C might be finite reachability through eligible continuation links. Those links must be identified more narrowly than any causal influence; exposure to a stray signal does not automatically make its recipient a personal continuer. Let H(p) mark the independently identified aversive condition under the controlled comparison. Consider

\[
R_C(a,p) \iff H(p)\ \land\ C(a,p).
\]

This interpretation can recognize reasons for ordinary future selves, multiple qualified continuers and a merged later continuation. It can satisfy branch nonerasure and matched-branch parity. It also permits a distinct patient u with exactly the same aversive condition and equally feasible relief to remain outside a's reason scope when C(a,u) fails.

Keep accurate inquiry, explicit self-models, recognition of every patient's own standpoint reason, criticism of assumptions and all material capacities in the model. No failure of observation explains the exclusion. The philosophical claim is that continuity is part of the practical reason's ground. This claim may be normatively inadequate; the prospective and branching premises alone have not refuted it. The model establishes consistency with those declared capacities, not ideal competence concerning every normative truth or closure under every justified future deliberative route. The later adjudication in §10 corrects any stronger reading.

This is a stronger test than the original strict self-only policy. It has already accepted reasons beyond numerical identity and the generative transformation that produced them. A successful universal argument must explain why removing the actual continuation relationship, while preserving the patient's aversive condition and the actor's ability to help, cannot remove **all** reason.

### A further positive step: inherit a reason with its patient fixed

The coordinated [generative-identity investigation](HUIGEN-GENERATIVE-IDENTITY.md) distinguishes preserving a reason as its patient develops from preserving a reason as its deliberator is replaced. Suppose the branching argument gives ancestor a a reason concerning patient q2. Suppose q1 then succeeds a in an independently justified practical role, with authority to act, and the succession preserves the still-valid reason's patient, ground, task and constituency. Then q1 inherits the reason concerning q2. Since q1 and q2 are distinct, this is concern by one branch for the other, not merely concern by their common ancestor for both.

The derivation is direct: patient-preserving succession transports R(a,q2) to R(q1,q2). Its substance is the **fixed-constituency inheritance premise**. That premise can have an independent defense where a legitimate continuing undertaking retains responsibility for the same patients through replacement. Mere copying, memory or causal descent does not establish it. A successor whose genuine mandate remains protection of the named patients cannot silently reinterpret it as protection only of whichever patient it now calls itself.

The continuity-cone model above fails this stronger inheritance premise at a split: q2 is reachable from a, but not from q1. It is therefore a countermodel to universal concern from prospective concern alone, not a countermodel to the strengthened argument after inheritance is justified. An initially self-indexed task can instead update its referent when the actor changes; that is not automatically a logical inconsistency, but it does not preserve a fixed-patient undertaking. Distinguishing these task meanings makes the proposed successor interface testable.

### Ancestry and merger do not reverse the direction

If an earlier ancestor o produces both a and u, there are continuation paths o→a and o→u. There need be no path a→u. Likewise, if a and b contribute to a future merged patient m, the paths a→m and b→m do not imply a→b or b→a. Shared sources and shared successors are different from directed continuation.

Thus a common generative history does not turn all current patients into one self or automatically transport reasons between them. To infer universal concern through an undirected web would require a justified reversal or additional transport rule. Merger can create overlapping responsibilities without merging the separate past burdens or experiences of its contributors. The model's patient coordinates must preserve those distinctions.

## 5. Special concern does not establish exclusive concern

There is an inference both sides must avoid. From “continuity supplies a reason” one cannot infer “absence of continuity supplies no reason.” A reason for one's future patient can have several grounds. Schematically,

\[
R_{\mathrm{total}}=R_{\mathrm{patient}}\ \lor\
R_{\mathrm{continuity}}\ \lor\ R_{\mathrm{commitment}}\ \lor\ \cdots.
\]

This notation lists possible independently warranted sources; it does not assert that every displayed source has already been established. Explaining the continuity term does not prove that the patient-grounded term is empty. Conversely, identifying a patient's genuine aversion does not yet establish that term's full actor scope.

The exclusive continuity model therefore carries an **exhaustiveness claim** in addition to its positive account of prudence. It must defend why no remaining patient-grounded reason survives outside C. Its causal success at reproducing prudent conduct does not establish this negative normative conclusion. The proponent of a general patient-grounded reason must in turn justify its authority rather than treating the absence of a proof of exclusivity as a positive proof of universal standing.

The coordinated [terminal-experience and lost-deliberation tests](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#4-a-sharper-test-preserve-the-patient-while-ending-the-undertaking) sharpen this issue. If a person still has reason now to prevent a later aversive episode after all undertakings and further agency have ended, protecting future agency cannot exhaust that reason's ground. If the later patient cannot deliberate, contemporaneous deliberation is likewise unnecessary for that case. A defender can still appeal to bodily continuity, prior agency or a historical responsibility; the tests require that changed ground to be stated rather than treating it as the same functional explanation.

The most promising positive position allows a common baseline reason against identified aversive loss together with stronger or additional reasons arising from continuity, responsibility and legitimate commitments. It avoids making all lives interchangeable. The branching argument supports examining that position because it shows how concern can outlive a strict identity boundary; it does not by itself prove the baseline for unrelated patients.

## 6. Delegation supplies another real extension, with a different ground

Suppose patient p has a warranted reason favoring y over x and legitimately authorizes actor a to choose on p's behalf within a specified task. Actor a accepts that mandate. The options, evidence, authority, duration, withdrawal conditions, resource limits and affected third parties are explicit. Merely giving a an instruction is not sufficient to establish any of these premises.

Three conclusions must be distinguished:

| Level | Conditional positive result | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Faithful task execution | A controller undertaking to select according to p's admitted reason fails that task if it substitutes only its private benefit. | That accepting or continuing the task is obligatory. |
| Practical reason under an accepted end | Given the normative requirements of coherent pursuit of the legitimate accepted task, p's reason supplies a role-mediated reason for a to use the effective means. | An unconditional duty after every permissible withdrawal or change in circumstances. |
| Binding commitment or office | Given independently justified rules of undertaking and authority, a may owe continuing performance, explanation or repair. | That a promise creates any imaginable duty, overrides third-party claims, or binds an unrelated nonconsenting actor. |

This is a genuine way for another's reason to become addressed to an actor without pretending that the patient has become that actor's self. It is also generative: authorizing, accepting, recording and maintaining the role can change the normative relationship, while acquiring evidence and effective control changes what can be done. A practical implementation must preserve patient, task and actor scope when responsibility passes to a successor.

**Delegated no-substitution result.** If a claims currently to perform a still-valid accepted mandate whose success condition includes p's admitted reason, and no admitted override or withdrawal applies, then replacing that reason by a self-only objective cannot constitute faithful performance. This follows from the declared task and role, not from a new direct reason at every unrelated actor. It can be assessed even when p cannot monitor the decision.

Authorization by p alone does not obligate an unconsenting third party to rescue p. Acceptance by a also cannot confer permission to wrong a different patient. A representative for a noncommunicating patient needs independent grounds for representation; p's inability to consent does not magically supply them. The prospective-self case shows that valid concern need not always await an actual present request, but does not establish every proposed guardianship or emergency authority.

The remaining universal step is therefore exact: why does an unrelated actor have a reason concerning the aversive patient **before** accepting a task or entering a justified office? A theory that answers only through chosen mandates has explained formation of some other-directed reasons but leaves that patient outside its proof. Calling every encounter an implicit acceptance would hide the missing premise.

## 7. A combined model identifies what the new arguments leave open

Let D(a,p) mean an applicable, legitimately accepted task toward p. A model can grant all the preceding conditional results with

\[
R_{C,D}(a,p) \iff H(p)\ \land\bigl(C(a,p)\lor D(a,p)\bigr).
\]

It supports prospective prudence, multiple continuers and delegated protection of a numerically unrelated patient. The separate fixed-constituency inheritance premise, when applicable, must be encoded in the continuing task relation D; it does not follow from C alone. The model can faithfully represent a silent outsider u's local reason while granting no addressed reason to a when both C(a,u) and D(a,u) fail. A patient can be outside this scope even when relief is easy and there is no competing reason.

This model preserves the new positive achievements; it does not establish that the resulting exclusion is ethically credible. The hypothesis that the patient's aversive condition supplies an independent baseline directly challenges the model's exhaustive disjunction. Neither the existence of a continued self nor the existence of accepted concern settles that dispute.

The next decisive comparison should hold the patient's identified aversive condition, the actor's knowledge, feasible relief and all competing claims fixed while varying: actual continuation, a legitimate accepted role, or neither. This isolates three possibilities: the relationships create the only reason; they strengthen an already present reason; or one of the proposed relationships lacks the claimed authority. The grounding argument must explain which of these is justified. Behavior or a learned policy alone cannot decide the validity question.

## 8. Primary comparator and limits of the reconstruction

Her treatment of future concern connects larger agencies with distinct practical relations; simultaneous copies need not form one person simply through matching type. This is a useful comparator for preserving differentiated subjects while examining constructed continuity. It does not make common ancestry a sufficient ground of universal concern. The present branching lemma is our conditional reconstruction, not a quotation or attribution of its premises to Korsgaard. ## 9. Adjudication and the next concrete test

The generative account weakens two overly narrow candidates: current sensation is unnecessary once a prospective reason is admitted, and numerical identity is unnecessary if branch-preserving concern is justified. These are positive extensions with explicit premises. They do not flatten patients into one subject, and they leave room for special responsibilities.

The best remaining self-ground is therefore a **specified practical continuity relation**, not an unexplained permanent substance. Such a relation can support special reasons. Its being generated neither invalidates it nor establishes that it is the exclusive source of reasons. The strongest whole-root argument should now defend the patient-grounded baseline while treating continuity and commitment as potential modifiers or additional grounds. Its decisive case remains an aversive patient outside both relations, with reliable identification, feasible relief and no competing reason.

A positive derivation would show, from independently warranted premises, why that patient's reason-ground remains sufficient despite the removal of continuity and mandate. A defeating witness must preserve that same offered sufficient ground and the relevant claimed practical authority, then coherently deny the addressed reason. Rejecting branch nonerasure, disputing the legitimacy of a mandate or defending an exclusive continuity ground is an identifiable premise dispute, not a result already settled by science or by the word self.

The next source and formal comparisons should specifically test uniqueness-sensitive continuity, shared projects without personal identity, mandate withdrawal and the uncontrolled third party. The [reason-transport framework](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) can record the resulting grounds and limits; its reachability cannot justify an edge that these arguments leave unwarranted. Empirical identification must separately establish the experiential condition rather than substituting a signal, functional deficit or inferred aversion for the proposed ground.

## 10. Practical congruency tested on an unrelated actor

**Further adjudication, 6 October 2026.** The target is an actor who is neither the patient's continuer nor their representative. This extension distinguishes categorical reasons, reasons dependent on motivational starting points, and norms constitutive of a justificatory practice.

### 10.1 A controlled comparison separates partiality from exclusion

Let x and y be two feasible commands available to actor b. Each consumes the same specified finite resources, takes the same time and requires the same effort. Their effects on b's projects, future condition, accepted obligations and other independently specified considerations are identical. All other patients' relevant outcomes are held fixed. The sole difference is a reliably identified aversive episode for unrelated patient p: x permits or produces it, while y prevents it. No appeal to physically free action is made.

The case fixes actual considerations rather than defining excluded interests as illegitimate. Sadistic enjoyment would introduce an additional difference and is outside this contrast. Rights, permissions, responsibility and interference must be checked independently; a reason for relief does not authorize every intervention.

**Partiality separator.** In this comparison, a greater priority assigned to b's unchanged personal outcomes provides no differential consideration against y. If the patient's aversive condition supplies any positive reason favoring y, and the comparison includes all relevant reasons with no override, then x has no offsetting advantage under that reasons profile. The patient's reason favors y despite the actor's special concern for themselves.

This separates **my interests can have greater weight** from **your suffering supplies me no reason whatever**. Ordinary priority does not establish the latter. The dominance argument assumes positive standing and comparative completeness; it proves neither. Equal costs isolate baseline standing, although a pro tanto reason can also exist when relief costs more.

### 10.2 The positive argument and our reasoned judgment

The positive argument starts from the proposed self-case ground: the episode's aversive character, its significance to its subject and possible prevention. The prospective and terminal tests separate this ground from present sensation and future productivity.

An objective **practical** assessment asks whether this condition favors relief. First, the patient's aversive condition remains real through another actor's warranted evidence. Second, prevention is the sole identified benefit to an affected patient in this comparison. Third, special relationships can explain additional reasons without explaining why the aversive condition supplies no reason outside them. Zero baseline needs a defense beyond absent sympathy.

**Our adjudication favors a patient-grounded baseline in this isolated case.** It retains the experiential ground that supports the accepted local reason, accommodates legitimate partiality, and explains why otherwise pointless preventable suffering counts against a choice. An exclusive account adds a gate whose role in cancelling every reason remains unsupported by these facts. This is a substantive normative judgment with positive grounds; the claim is not merely that universal concern has not been disproved.

This is defeasible normative warrant. A rival can challenge the account of reason, identify an overlooked relation or defeat the experiential identification. The sufficient-ground inference becomes deductive only after its scope is justified; experiential recognition alone has not proved transcendental necessity.

### 10.3 A serious internal-reasons rival

Bernard Williams's “Internal and External Reasons” permits rich motivational sets, correction of false beliefs and imaginative deliberation that changes motives. Denying an internal reason requires excluding the relevant sound deliberative routes, not merely observing indifference. He emphasizes how strong such exclusions are and challenges external accounts to distinguish rational acquisition of motivation from persuasive conversion. [Primary essay, pp. 101–113 in *Moral Luck*](https://bibliotecamathom.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/williams_-_moral_luck.pdf), independently read in the source investigator's temporary extraction; see the [scoped source record](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md).

Our strongest rival therefore gives b factual accuracy, future-directed projects, relationships and accepted undertakings, then asserts **no sound route from that actual starting set reaches concern for p**. The earlier finite countermodel has not proved this stronger closure. It establishes only non-entailment from its listed premises, not complete normative competence.

The positive account holds that the correctly identified aversive condition can itself justify a new practical orientation. The rival requires a route from antecedent motivations. Neither present indifference nor a new motive caused by vivid exposure decides between them. A general commitment to follow justified reasons also leaves the content of this particular justification unresolved. The next test must specify and defend the deliberative transition. In particular, a finite list of unsuccessful routes is not an exhaustive proof against the open-ended revision capacity the generative theory seeks to explain.

### 10.4 A stronger positive result when public claims are already admitted

There is a narrower route that can be deductively decisive without deriving an interpersonal anchor from prudence. Suppose a claims that an unrelated capable actor b has a reason to prevent a's aversive episode in the matched case. Suppose the offered sufficient ground is the episode's aversive significance and b's relevant opportunity, with no additional condition distinguishing a's status. This is already an other-addressed reason claim, not merely a's reason to protect themselves.

**Public-claim reciprocity test.** If a practical account accepts that claim and preserves its fully specified ground under exchanging the relevant actor and patient positions, it must accept the corresponding reason for a concerning b, or a further matched patient covered by the justified patient-ground extension. Denying the counterpart while retaining the ground and its sufficiency is inconsistent.

The proof is ground-preserving substitution, without reciprocal motivation or reward. Noncommunicating patients are included only if the justified ground extends to them. A special-status restriction disputes the premise. This supplies an explicit interpersonal anchor for philosophies making such public demands; it does not refute an internalist who grants no such claim.

### 10.5 Scientific constitutive norms do not silently settle practical scope

A claimed empirical answer must satisfy its necessary evidence and inference conditions. Similarly, a practical account claiming to apply a complete sufficient ground cannot erase a covered reason while retaining that claim. But empirical competence does not identify the complete practical ground. An internalist can coherently criticize scientific claims while disputing universal actor scope. The whole-theory target therefore cannot be established by defining scientific participation as moral agreement; failure of one practical claim also does not invalidate every scientific conjecture its author pursues.

### 10.6 Three claims the next result must keep apart

| Status | Required achievement | Finding of this round |
|---|---|---|
| Normative warrant for a categorical baseline | Good reasons to hold that the aversive condition favors relief for the capable actor without grounding its authority in that actor's antecedent motivational set | Positively defended in the matched case, with an explicit competing theory of reason and defeaters |
| Transcendental indispensability | Show that refusing the ground defeats an independently specified justificatory capacity the critic still claims | Established conditionally for an admitted complete ground or public claim; not for empirical inquiry alone |
| Metaphysical necessity at the stated domain | Establish that every possible realization satisfying the actual grounding conditions has the addressed reason | Not established by a model of alternative interpretations or by the conditional branch and role results |

The categorical target grounds the reason independently of the actor's antecedent motivational set. Independence from current endorsement alone is weaker: an unrecognized internal reason can also lack present endorsement while remaining accessible through sound deliberation. Neither target means independence from every factual applicability condition or an absolute duty defeating all other reasons. The model that varies reason interpretations while holding the empirical structure fixed tests entailment from those stated premises. It does not by itself establish that both interpretations describe metaphysically possible normative realities.

**The strengthened research position:** the isolated aversive case supports a baseline more directly than arguments from useful cooperation, common ancestry or private future benefit. Partiality does not defeat that case without an additional exclusion or contrary consideration. Public demands can supply an explicit interpersonal anchor whose preservation has necessary consequences. The remaining universal step is the warrant for acquiring that baseline across motivational starting points that do not already acknowledge it. The next argument should assess that acquisition rule directly, with the internalist no-route requirement stated honestly, rather than repeating the fact that faithful indexing alone is insufficient.

## 11. Imaginative uptake without substituting the observer

**Original AI argument and proposed discrimination tests, 6 October 2026.** The connection is now **an identified patient-ground → warranted uptake by an unrelated deliberator**. No test below has been run. The argument keeps the categorical candidate defended in §10 and asks whether imagination can make its application rationally accessible, rather than install concern as an unexplained reward.

The [source comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#8-can-reflective-acquisition-justify-the-impersonal-ground) tests reflective warrant and practical scope; the [selective empirical comparison](HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md#6-perspective-taking-must-earn-its-epistemic-role) separately examines affect, accuracy and reported judgment.

### 11.1 The strongest constructive route

Actor b has reliable evidence about patient p but initially treats the report as an abstract event. A disciplined imaginative exercise may enrich b's representation of what the episode is like **for p**. It retains p's circumstances, aversions and limits; b remains the actual actor with the relevant opportunity. The temporary perspective is a way of understanding a subject, not a discovery that b and p are one person. Vividness itself supplies no independent observation of p.

The proposed uptake has four steps:

1. Establish the actual patient's aversive condition and the effects of the available actions, with uncertainty explicit.
2. Imaginatively attend to its patient-relative significance, checking the representation against that evidence rather than copying b's preferences.
3. Ask from b's actual practical position whether the condition favors the feasible, permissible relieving action. Inspect the proposed ground, competing considerations and any asserted restriction to particular actors.
4. Retain or revise the reason judgment after returning attention to b's ordinary situation, recording what changed: evidence, understanding, an endorsed ground, motivation or the action itself.

The positive possibility is **recognition of a previously neglected ground**, not merely acquisition of an appetite. On the defended patient-grounded account, an initial desire to help is unnecessary for that reason's validity. Accurate imaginative attention can help someone understand its applicability and force. Whether that understanding actually arises, and whether it motivates action, are further questions. A causal explanation of increased concern alone does not settle either warrant or the scope of the reason.

### 11.2 What a nonarbitrary complete-ground principle can establish

Consider the principle: a judgment should not change when every feature of its independently justified sufficient ground remains fixed. This is stronger than consistency of names and weaker than declaring every circumstance morally identical. It permits legitimate responsibilities, relationships, permissions and different patient needs to matter when they belong to the relevant ground or competing reasons.

The positive application is specific. If b judges that p's aversive condition and b's permissible opportunity suffice for a pro tanto reason, changing the vividness of b's encounter leaves that ground intact. Returning to b's own perspective cannot by itself cancel the reason. The practical conclusion follows from the defended ground, while imagination supplied access to it. This gives a correction rule with real content: **do not substitute the disappearance of one's simulated feeling for the disappearance of the patient's reason-ground**.

The completeness judgment must nevertheless survive criticism. Bare relabeling is irrelevant, but “this is my undertaking” describes a relation, not just a name. An account can argue that an actor-relative relation is constitutive. The reply must examine that alleged ground rather than condemn all partiality as arbitrary. Conversely, showing that a relation supplies an additional reason does not show that it cancels every patient-grounded reason outside the relation.

### 11.3 Directionality and the return test

A simulation can yield the judgment “from p's position, p has reason to want this stopped.” Returning to b's position preserves that judgment about p. It does not silently replace its addressee with b. The exact distinction is developed in the [typed transport analysis](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md).

The stronger test asks b, during and after the exercise, about the **same actual claim R(b,p)**, with the same action, evidence, patient and applicability conditions. If b first accepts its warrant and later denies it solely because attention returned home, the account owes a relevant defeater or correction. A mere frame change does not supply one. If the earlier judgment concerned R(p,p), there was no such accepted interpersonal judgment to preserve.

This is not a demand for psychologically reversible learning. An exercise can reveal a neglected fact or change a warranted understanding; returning to the original viewing position need not restore the old judgment. Nor must p be capable of imagining b in return. Epistemic access can be asymmetric while the identified patient-ground remains real. What must survive is the correct binding of claims, not identical capacities or reciprocal sympathy.

### 11.4 Discriminating patient-ground recognition from projection

The following are controlled conceptual contrasts; an empirical version would need independently identified patients, evidence and permissible interventions.

| Contrast | Prediction of the proposed warranted route | What a failure would expose |
|---|---|---|
| Same warranted patient condition and opportunity; observer's imagery becomes less vivid | The admitted reason remains unless evidence, feasibility or another relevant condition changes | Dependence on current private arousal mistaken for patient-ground preservation |
| Same vivid imaginative reaction; corrected evidence refutes the claimed relieving effect and shows that this action instead aggravates p's aversion | Withdraw that relief-based reason and reconsider the action; assess any distinct benefit separately | Protecting the observer's simulation or preferred narrative rather than the patient |
| Exchange arbitrary labels while preserving complete relevant profiles and role bindings | Preserve the corresponding reason judgments | A label-based exclusion or misaddressed claim |
| Patients p and q have different aversions, so opposite commands relieve them | Preserve the individual profiles and select the appropriate command for each | Self-projection or uniform treatment substituted for concern |
| No new evidence or ground change; move from imagined attention back to ordinary deliberation | Preserve an already warranted actual-actor reason, if one was established | Unexplained cancellation on return, or an earlier unnoticed addressee switch |

Neither a strong emotion nor a low emotion is sufficient to classify a judgment. A feeling may help disclose a relevant consideration, distract from it or accurately accompany it. Passing these contrasts would support the route's fidelity and responsiveness; it would not independently prove its normative premise. A self-restricted account can pass factual and semantic tests while disputing the practical ground. Its dispute is then exposed clearly rather than mistaken for lack of imagination.

### 11.5 Adjudication and the next decisive result

This route is stronger than “empathy makes people helpful.” It specifies a patient-preserving acquisition process and locates corrections that can make it rationally better: improve the patient model, undo projection, identify the real action effects, and preserve a justified conclusion despite fading salience. It accommodates heterogeneous patients and special responsibilities without making the observer's experience the measure of everyone else's good.

Our positive assessment is that such calibrated uptake can be warranted recognition under the patient-grounded account defended in §10. It need not wait for a prior helping desire, and an unexplained retreat merely because the patient is now encountered from outside weakens a purportedly complete ground account. This remains a substantive normative assessment. Scientific descriptive competence alone does not establish that the actor has accepted, or must accept, the requisite complete practical ground.

The next decisive comparison should therefore measure or reconstruct **what claim was learned and why**, alongside motivation and behavior. A successful constructive case would show corrected patient understanding and a defensible reason judgment surviving the return and heterogeneity tests. A stronger universal result would show why every adequate practical deliberator must admit that ground or a sound route to it. Projection, persistent misidentification or a justified restriction of the alleged sufficient ground would defeat this proposed route and require revision of the foundation's uptake account.

## 12. What scientific normativity can require of the moral dispute

**Original AI argument and assumption review, 6 October 2026.** The connection is **indispensable epistemic norms → justified protection of an unrelated patient**. The strongest affirmative route begins with scientific justification itself, rather than with a reward for cooperation. It must establish more than the existence of some normativity and more than the usefulness of preserving informants.

### 12.1 Grant the stronger epistemic starting point

For this test, grant genuine reasons to correct belief in light of relevant, undefeated evidence, including when correction serves no desired payoff. We do not weaken these into accurate outputs or optional analyst scores. An inquirer who claims that an argument warrants belief already distinguishes evidence that supports a conclusion from information that merely causes assent.

This grants a normative starting premise; it does not establish that every adequate philosophy must explain it through irreducible facts. Different accounts of epistemic authority remain possible. The [Kelly comparison](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md) tests why a reason to respond correctly to possessed evidence differs from an instrumental reason to seek it. The [source investigation](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md) examines the stronger proposal that epistemic and moral normativity stand together.

The affirmative thought is substantial: if truth can give an inquirer a reason despite indifference, lack of an antecedent helping desire cannot by itself show that another's identified suffering gives no reason. The normative objection must explain the proposed difference. This directly improves the previous uptake debate without assuming that belief and action have identical standards.

### 12.2 An exact constraint on blanket rejection

Suppose a scientific philosophy accepts an epistemic reason E, but rejects a proposed moral reason M **solely** because M has feature F. It claims that possessing F is sufficient to disqualify any reason. If E also has F in the relevant sense, the position cannot retain all three commitments: E's authority, the unrestricted exclusion rule, and its application to M.

Examples under investigation include alleged categorical authority, irreducibility or failure to act as an additional physical cause. Similarity must be demonstrated for the actual accounts. A naturalistic account of epistemic normativity might deny that E shares the relevant feature; it then owes that account, not an exception obtained merely by calling E scientific.

**The positive result is a restriction on how an adequate scientific philosophy may reject concern.** Its admitted epistemic authority defeats an indiscriminate objection that would exclude that same authority. It must revise the veto, supply a relevant difference or revise its epistemology. This is stronger than requesting sympathy: it identifies commitments that cannot jointly stand. It remains weaker than proving M, every moral fact or the entire foundation.

### 12.3 Shared difficulties need not imply equally strong support

The strongest rival does not use an absolute veto. It treats F as a theoretical cost, then argues that epistemic reasons have an independent justification strong enough to bear that cost. Moral reasons might lack that justification. For example, the rival can claim that asserting epistemic nihilism as rationally warranted defeats its own entitlement, while denying a moral reason does not automatically do so.

This is a potentially relevant asymmetry, not yet a vindication of moral exclusion. The rival must defend the epistemic indispensability claim and assess the direct patient-ground argument. Conversely, the foundation must not turn the absence of a justified argument for epistemic nihilism into a proof of its falsity without further premises. Nor does defeating selected objections establish the stronger conditional that epistemic reasons could not exist unless moral reasons existed.

The proposed comparison is therefore precise: is F an alleged impossibility, or a defeasible cost? What positive support is offered in each domain? Does a claimed epistemic privilege concern the existence of authority, its accessibility or its content? A critic cannot answer these questions by stipulating a science-only list of acceptable reasons; neither can we answer them by announcing that all reasons form one web.

The rival cannot simply declare theoretical indispensability the only admissible warrant. If a genuinely moral kind is independently admitted and the patient judgment belongs to that kind, rejecting further instances for numerical economy must be weighed against otherwise warranted patient judgments. An experiential ground could supply independent support; this does not make every moral appearance veridical.

A further affirmative route claims that adequate inquiry already requires genuinely moral–epistemic norms, such as warranted prohibitions on particular forms of dishonesty. That would be a direct indispensability argument, not merely an analogy between troublesome features. Its premises deserve investigation. Even if successful, the existence of these norms would still need a coverage argument to reach protection of a patient after their evidential role ends.

### 12.4 Protecting inquiry can protect a patient

There is a genuine practical extension when another person's continued capacity is necessary to a valid inquiry undertaking. If disabling their testimony would make the promised discrimination impossible, the inquirer must preserve the required channel, provide an adequate alternative or withdraw the unsupported claim. If an independently justified requirement to complete the inquiry remains, and a permissible safeguard is its necessary means, a justified means principle supplies a reason to safeguard the person. The [mechanism analysis](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) specifies these alternatives.

This can require action with no private reward. But it does not imply an unlimited obligation to continue every inquiry, and it protects a person here as a needed source. The same inference can protect an instrument. A prohibition on silencing inconvenient evidence establishes something important without yet showing that suffering itself grounds protection. Calling the source a co-inquirer also cannot silently supply every interpersonal right the argument seeks to derive.

### 12.5 The decisive test occurs after epistemic usefulness ends

Consider the established matched-cost case after the relevant evidence has been secured. The patient remains a differentiated subject with a reliably identified aversive condition. Permitting or preventing the episode makes no difference to compliance with the stipulated inquiry's requirements for accuracy, warranted confidence, retained evidence, future access or remaining undertaking. Honest belief contents can differ with the different outcomes. Other responsibilities and permissions must be assessed explicitly. A noncommunicating patient with no contribution to that inquiry tests the same boundary without a prior testimonial role.

These are conceptual controls, not empirical claims that any real person is useless. The point is to remove the offered epistemic-dependence ground while retaining the patient's condition. If a proposed derivation gives protection only while useful evidence remains at stake, it has explained source protection. A patient-grounded baseline must survive the loss of that utility, or explain why the supposed patient-ground was insufficient.

A serious rival can retain categorical epistemic reasons and acknowledge the harm accurately, while assigning no further protective reason at this endpoint. That interpretation demonstrates non-entailment from the declared premises. It does not establish complete normative adequacy, the truth of moral nihilism or impossibility of warranted concern. Its account must still answer the affirmative claim that the aversive condition itself supplies a reason. Accurate acknowledgment is not a refutation of that claim.

### 12.6 Domain explanation, affirmative judgment and revision conditions

A candidate domain explanation says that belief is answerable to truth, while action concerns many ends and legitimate relations. This helps explain why correcting an erroneous belief and relieving pain are different responses. It does not establish that practical reasons are wholly desire-dependent or that there are no standards concerning effects on others. The relevant question is why this difference would defeat the patient's proposed reason, not merely why the domains have different names.

Our affirmative assessment remains that identified, otherwise uncompensated aversive loss supplies a strong candidate baseline in the matched case. Epistemic normativity adds something new: a philosophy already admitting authority beyond preferred outcomes cannot dismiss that candidate simply because reasons have authority beyond preferred outcomes. The patient's experiential significance supplies the positive consideration; the parity test removes an inconsistent exclusion, rather than manufacturing the consideration from epistemic vocabulary.

Three outcomes would change the account. A warranted reduction explaining the epistemic case while excluding the moral case would defeat the claimed parity at that feature. A justified patient-ground surviving the endpoint test would advance standing beyond inquiry's instruments. A demonstrated indispensability of that very ground to an independently specified practical or epistemic capacity would support the strong necessity target. Until then, conditional source protection and rejection of blanket normative vetoes are actual gains, while universal patient standing and whole-theory coverage remain distinct proof obligations. No experiment, new implementation or formal compilation is claimed.

## 13. Can assurance make the listener a claimant?

**Original AI reconstruction and review, 6 October 2026.** The transition is **epistemic answerability → a listener's moral claim → protection after informational usefulness ends**. The proposed advance concerns how one agent can create a directed responsibility by inviting another's dependence. It does not assume that every assertion is a promise or that speaking automatically establishes universal patient standing.

### 13.1 Identify the particular act and its claimed authority

An argument invites examination of its premises; an assurance asks an audience to accept something on the speaker's word. These can coexist, but their contributions differ. Our test concerns an intelligible, voluntary assurance to a specified audience, with its content and limits understood. A warning, provisional conjecture, unattended instrument output and deliberate invitation to trust should not acquire identical commitments merely because each transmits information.

The [primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md) supplies important constraints. Moran's account connects telling with responsibility to an addressee, while distinguishing that relationship from an overhearer's acquisition of a reason for belief. He also distinguishes retracting words from repairing their consequences. Cuneo's proposed moral interpretation of speech is stronger than identifying constitutive rules; his stated conclusion does not by itself require an irreducibly categorical account of reasons. Our argument below must therefore defend the practical authority it needs, rather than borrow it from the vocabulary of assertion.

### 13.2 The strongest affirmative argument: invited dependence

Suppose speaker S competently invites listener L to rely on S's assurance about a consequential matter. S knows which decision L faces and presents the assurance as a warranted basis for it. L reasonably accepts that invitation within its understood scope. These conditions distinguish invited reliance from every belief someone might extract from another's behavior.

S has offered more than an observable prediction of reliable performance. S has presented a reason whose claimed availability depends on S's answerability. Inviting acceptance on that basis while reserving that L has no standing whatsoever to question the assurance withdraws part of what was offered. Within this conception of assurance, L's standing to request relevant justification or correction is constitutive, rather than a reward for being useful later. That standing is bounded: assurance does not license unlimited interrogation, disclosure of every confidential fact or belief despite decisive contrary evidence.

The further moral defense concerns the dependence deliberately created. S has invited L to place part of L's judgment and exposure to loss in S's hands. Suppose S misrepresents the assurance's basis despite accessible reasons against it and an opportunity to qualify it. This establishes epistemic fault; whether it also wrongs L is the issue. The proposed complaint concerns S's treatment of L, beyond the circulation of an incorrect proposition. The positive ground is **responsibility for avoidably imposing a foreseeable burden through invited, reasonable reliance and a faulty assurance**. It identifies an actor, a claimant and a reason for correction or proportionate repair, without requiring future cooperation or reputational gain.

This is a substantive normative argument. Its force comes from explaining why soliciting another's trust does not give the solicitor unilateral authority to leave that person carrying the burden produced by the assurance's epistemic fault. It is stronger than assuming a legitimate role without explaining its relevance; it remains assessable as a moral ground, not a derivation from causal influence alone.

### 13.3 A serious rival and the actual point of disagreement

The rival can accept genuine epistemic reasons, socially constituted challenge rights and duties internal to competent assertion. It explains why unsupported claims must be corrected and why listeners may demand an answer under the practice's rules. It nevertheless denies that these statuses create moral wronging or categorical practical reasons once the epistemic transaction ends.

This account cannot be dismissed simply because its prediction is accurate rather than caring: it already includes normative answerability. Nor does the existence of a practice establish its moral legitimacy. A practice can distribute offices and permissions unfairly. The issue is whether treating this listener's invited dependence as morally irrelevant adequately explains the case, not whether the rival recognizes the word “obligation.”

Our affirmative assessment favors a directed moral claim in this case of an epistemically faulty assurance. The listener's reasonably exposed agency and actual burden provide positive support, while the speaker's voluntary solicitation explains special responsibility. The rival must explain why that combination warrants only bookkeeping within a practice. Conversely, this defense does not establish that every innocent factual error wrongs someone, or that every moral duty supplies a reason independently of all antecedent motivational commitments.

### 13.4 Retraction, correction and completed repair are different

Consider a stipulated case: S carelessly assures L that a reserved replacement component has passed the required checks, knowing L will release an alternative because of that assurance. L reasonably does so. The assurance proves unfounded. S promptly retracts it and L now holds accurate beliefs, but replacement costs caused by the invited reliance remain. The collaboration has ended; no further testimony, reputation or research success depends on helping L.

Three tasks must be tracked separately: withdraw the unsupported assurance; communicate correction effectively enough to prevent further foreseeable reliance; and address any remaining wrongfully imposed burden. Completing the first does not automatically complete the other two. Yet repair is not unlimited indemnification. Its appropriate extent depends on fault, reasonable reliance, causal contribution, available remedies and competing responsibilities. An apology may suffice for some wrongs; other losses require practical work. The [temporal mechanism](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) tracks persistence only after the relevant claim and discharge conditions are independently justified.

This supplies a discriminating comparison. Hold present accurate belief and the end of inquiry fixed; vary whether S invited L's reliance on a negligently supported assurance or L merely encountered an unrelated malfunction. Different directed repair claims need not reflect different present epistemic utility. Their ground can lie in the history of how the burden was imposed. Historical responsibility thus defeats the narrower inference that protection must end when information is secured. It does not yet establish responsibility for every unrelated loss.

### 13.5 Truth and noncommunicating patients test the scope

Literal truth does not settle whether an assurance is responsible. A true assertion offered without the purported warrant may still misrepresent the speaker's epistemic position. Selective truths can also induce a foreseeable false impression. These cases must specify the communicated assurance and context rather than secretly treating any unwanted consequence as deception. If every relevant belief is accurate and the assurance properly warranted, exploitation might still occur; identifying it then requires a practical ground beyond truth or falsity alone.

Now let L be making a decision that affects a noncommunicating patient P. If S's assurance explicitly undertakes responsibility for P's specified interests, the undertaking can concern P without requiring P to speak. That alone does not establish that P holds the claim. Its legitimacy, representation and content need defense; L's right of complaint does not automatically become P's independent right. The speaker's foreseeable imposition of risk on P may support a separate moral claim, but that is an extension of the harm or responsibility ground, not transport by overhearing. A patient wholly outside the assurance and its consequences remains the test of universal standing.

### 13.6 What this establishes for the whole root

The strongest supported advance is a localized moral ground that can outlast both an inquiry and the recipient's usefulness. It arises from invited dependence and responsibility for its wrongful consequences. The constitutive assurance argument supports addressed answerability; the moral argument defends why particular treatment of the listener warrants concern. These are connected but distinct achievements.

A successful rival explaining the same reasonable reliance, epistemic fault and residual burden without genuine wronging would challenge our positive judgment. A successful derivation showing that this moral status is indispensable to adequate assurance would strengthen necessity at that scope. Universal concern still requires reaching patients who were never addressed, represented, endangered or otherwise bound into the transaction. No empirical test, new implementation or formal compilation is claimed.

## 14. Imposed burdens and the nonparticipating patient

**Original AI argument and assumption review, 6 October 2026.** The connection is **responsibility for imposed burdens → standing without communicative participation**. The further question is whether the same ground requires aid from an actor who did not impose the burden. These transitions must be assessed separately; accepting a duty of repair does not already settle rescue.

### 14.1 State the adverse condition before the moral verdict

Consider a stipulated, differentiated patient P whose aversive experience is reliably identified. Actor A can initiate a process exposing P to that episode, or choose an alternative achieving the same legitimate purpose at the same finite cost without that exposure. P neither received an assurance nor invited reliance, and cannot communicate; no future communicative capacity is assumed. The comparison holds other identified interests fixed and supplies neither actual authorization nor a protective necessity.

These are controls for a conceptual case, not findings about an actual patient. Calling the episode aversive identifies its character for P; calling A its initiator identifies a causal relation. Neither description yet means that A wrongs P. That practical conclusion must be defended.

### 14.2 Why the best affirmative ground does not require uptake

The positive argument concerns whose condition A is deciding to impose. P must undergo the adverse experience; A controls whether this particular imposition occurs. A's available alternative avoids the episode without sacrificing the stipulated purpose. The resulting reason against imposition is grounded in P's experienced burden together with A's avoidable authorship, rather than in P's usefulness, bargaining power or prior acceptance of a role.

Our affirmative judgment is that A needs a justification for selecting this exposure and lacks one in the controlled case. The patient's condition is a positive consideration against the action. A's power to produce it supplies a locus of responsibility, not authority to disregard it. This supports a directed claim of restraint: the proposed wrong concerns A's treatment of P, rather than an impersonal decrease in a system's aggregate performance.

Communication can reveal a ground, authorize a limited intervention or change a relationship. It need not create the adverse significance being considered here. Removing P's ability to express a refusal leaves the episode and A's alternatives intact. A defense that suddenly permits the same imposition must therefore explain why inability to protest changes its justification. We do not invent actual consent, actual refusal or an unlimited veto. We reject treating a missing communicative act as sufficient to erase this independently defended ground.

### 14.3 Keep harm, agency and justification distinct

The [Shiffrin comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md) distinguishes an adverse condition from an overall welfare comparison, and separates imposing harm from questions of justification and blame. Its responsibility principles remain substantive philosophical commitments; the source is not an empirical discovery or a statement of current law. We use the distinctions without adopting every application in that work.

A benefit need not remove the aversive component of an intervention. Nor does identifying that component decide whether the intervention is justified: preventing a greater burden may matter. Foreknowledge and reasonable opportunity affect blame; they are not what makes an experience aversive. Realized harm and imposed risk also differ. An unjustified exposure can require prevention before anyone is injured, while a repair claim requires its own account of loss, responsibility and remedy.

This prevents two shortcuts. Causal contribution alone does not prove wrongdoing. Equally, lack of blame for an unforeseeable outcome does not, by itself, prove that the actor has no responsibility to help address its consequences. Neither strict liability nor immunity follows automatically from the present argument.

### 14.4 The consent test preserves patient individuality

Compare the same burden and alternatives when P can communicate and when only that channel is unavailable. The affirmative ground survives because its specified constituents survive. This is a conditional ground-preservation argument, not a declaration that all communicative differences are irrelevant. Evidence of P's particular commitments, refusal or authorization can alter a case; an inability to communicate is not evidence of any of those attitudes.

Benefits and burdens must likewise remain patient-specific. A's preference for an enhancement cannot replace P's interests. Hypothetical acceptance requires justification and relevant evidence, not a prediction that most people would agree. This matters especially when the intervention is difficult to reverse. Yet protecting P need not mean prohibiting every intervention: a warranted response to serious existing harm can differ from imposing that harm to provide an unsolicited advantage.

### 14.5 A serious rival accepts this result but rejects rescue

The strongest remaining rival grants reasons against avoidable imposition and directed reasons to repair one's own unjustified impositions. It protects silent nonparticipants on those grounds. It also preserves valid undertakings and genuine epistemic reasons. It nevertheless denies any patient-grounded reason for assistance by an unrelated actor who neither caused the predicament nor assumed responsibility for it. Merely denying an all-things-considered duty would be a weaker position, compatible with a baseline reason.

This position does not erase the patient's experience. It treats authorship, responsibility or a protected boundary as essential to the actor-addressed reason. The [causal comparison](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) can hold current harm and feasible relief fixed while changing the actor's causal history. Equal present control does not itself establish equal obligations. Nor may ordinary omissions be declared causally neutral simply by calling them omissions; the actual process must be specified.

The positive nonimposition argument therefore rules out exclusion solely for lack of participation, but does not refute this stronger rival. A patient can have a claim against an imposer without thereby having the same claim against every capable rescuer. Additional responsibilities might reach the rescuer; their absence cannot merely be inferred from the absence of causal authorship.

### 14.6 What could justify the further extension?

The constructive next test asks which role authorship performs. Does it create all the reason to respond, or strengthen and allocate responsibility for a burden that already supplies a weaker reason to any suitably situated actor? The second account explains both special repair responsibility and a baseline for aid. The first must explain why the same serious, remediable adverse condition supplies no practical consideration once the capable actor's causal history changes.

Our current judgment favors a baseline reason in a tightly bounded rescue case: reliably identified serious harm, effective feasible relief, no comparable competing burden and no relevant violation of another's rights. The patient's relief supplies positive support; the claim is not inferred merely because the rival lacks a refutation. Such a reason is not yet a specified directed claim against every actor. Limits on sacrifice, cumulative demands and divided responsibilities can affect what is required without making the patient's condition irrelevant. Institutional prevention may also address recurrent burdens without assigning every instance to one available individual.

This is a further normative proposal, assessed in the [rescue comparison](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md), rather than an entailment of nonimposition. A better justified authorship-exclusive account would require revising that extension. A successful patient-ground explanation allowing authorship to modify strength without exhausting relevance would advance it. Neither outcome alone proves that the whole generative foundation is necessary for scientific philosophy. No experiment or formal compilation is claimed.

## 15. Does experiential understanding make concern necessary?

6 October 2026. This continuation addresses the [noncauser frontier](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md#13-nonparticipating-patients-rescue-and-sustainable-concern): can the nature of an adverse experience, together with adequate understanding, justify a reason addressed to someone who did not produce it? The following assessment is our argument, not a new empirical result or Ren quotation.

### A direct necessity argument deserves a direct test

Gularte's 2023 argument directly connects experiential understanding to intrinsic disvaluing. It separately supplies a condition connecting informed disvaluing to objective badness and leaves requirements for action unresolved. The [source dossier](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#12-experience-appraisal-and-practical-authority) records the inspected passages and conditional status. Its importance here is that it challenges our earlier permitted separation between understanding and concern, rather than merely recommending more imagination.

Our assessment distinguishes three gates. First, does full understanding of a patient's experience require an active experiential presentation? Second, does such presentation exclude every intrinsic response except disvaluing the patient's episode? Third, would that necessary response establish a justified practical reason for this actor? Accepting one gate does not decide the others. For the entire-root objective there is also a scope obligation: show why the relevant condition is necessary for the independently specified class of adequate scientific philosophy. Maximal understanding of one experiential fact and the competence required for every scientific inquiry need not coincide.

The decisive challenge to the second gate concerns what must match. Let the observer have an accessible experiential presentation of the patient's aversiveness and a distinguishable response to that presentation. Both may contribute to the observer's overall experience. It does not follow merely from their integration that fidelity to the patient's experience requires the observer's **entire** affective state to equal the patient's entire state. Representing a whole and being that whole are different relations. Nor must distinguishing the presentation and response place one in an opaque sub-agent: their joint accessibility is part of the proposed alternative.

This is a challenge to a substantive premise, not a demonstrated counterexample to phenomenal necessity. The strongest reply is that affect is sufficiently holistic that changing the response necessarily changes the experiential presentation itself. A fixed output from a decoder would then be insufficient evidence of full experiential understanding. The [hand argument](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#14-experiential-fidelity-and-revision-of-a-harmful-remedy) therefore establishes only that representational composition alone does not require total-affect matching. Whether an appropriately faithful phenomenal realization is possible remains open. We should neither declare the ideal sadist possible by stipulation nor declare every apparent countercase ignorant by definition.

### A reason must survive the end of an episode of understanding

Even an established convergence of fully informed evaluative responses would need a justified account of its authority. An unavoidable response could be evidence for a reason; equating it with normative truth requires argument. Conversely, a reason can be real even when an actor fails to feel its force. These possibilities must remain separate from the empirical question of which interventions produce concern.

The [earlier retention argument](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#10-imaginative-perspective-taking-reversible-frames-and-new-warrant) supplies a constructive constraint. If encounter genuinely warrants an actual-actor reason, retaining its ground and evidence can preserve that warrant after vivid affect fades, absent a relevant defeater. The ground should not switch silently from the original patient's episode to the observer's discomfort. Making the observer feel better by looking away does not by itself remedy the patient's condition. Nor is continuously recreating intense distress established as a requirement for sound scientific or moral judgment.

A philosophy can also acknowledge a reason while its adherent acts against it. Such conduct tests motivation or implementation; it does not refute the reason or show that the philosophy denies it. The present research concerns the truth and necessity of the account, not a guarantee that every knowledgeable agent acts wisely.

### Concern can support revision without endorsing a harmful demand

Separate four questions: what a patient's experience presents; whether that appraisal is correct; what burden the experience itself involves; and which response is justified. Consider distress at a false report that another person has acted unfairly. Correcting the report can undermine the accusation while the earlier distress remains a real episode. A further, morally harder case is distress at another person's equal standing: acknowledging that distress need not validate the belief that equality is a wrong or authorize subordinating the other person.

The constructive candidate is to respond to the burden through truthful clarification, voluntary support or another justified route while respecting the other patient's standing. Its success must be established: correction may leave aversion unchanged, and a harmful demand may survive correction. Concern therefore needs accurate causal learning and scrutiny of the response rule, not merely pursuit of whatever outcome currently stops a complaint. An intervention that suppresses a report while leaving the adverse episode intact fails the proposed relief ground even if a simple behavioral score improves.

This does not make every aversive episode overriding or every refusal to remove it wrong. Chosen effort, grief, expressive commitments and legitimate refusals can change the available reasons and permissions. Relief can carry costs or impair other valued capacities. The positive case is bounded: an effective, permitted response relieving an identified burden without a relevant competing loss has a defensible pro tanto ground. A claim to categorical authority still needs justification; describing an experience as aversive does not supply every normative premise.

### What would change the account

An independently justified demonstration that full relevant understanding excludes the rival response would strengthen the necessity route. Evidence of an accessible, phenomenally faithful presentation with a different intrinsic attitude would challenge that claim, but ordinary report accuracy alone would not settle it. A justified authority principle could connect the resulting recognition to a reason for the noncauser. Failed patient identification, persistent distress after correction, or a justified objection to the proposed intervention would require changing the local account.

The present advance is twofold: the epistemic route now faces an exact matching and scope test; the constructive route can preserve a patient's significance while revising a false appraisal or harmful selector. Neither licenses defining scientific philosophy as agreement with our conclusions. The next central connection is **warranted recognition of an adverse episode → justified choice among relief, appraisal revision and nonintervention**, with the reason's authority and each response's actual effects separately identified. Whole-theory necessity remains open.

## 16. Concern, authorship and the authority to intervene

6 October 2026. The preceding remedy comparison supplied permissibility. This continuation asks why the same prospective relief may support an offer while failing to authorize imposition. It develops the existing [consent and correction account](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md#consent-conflict-and-correction), rather than treating agency as a newly discovered concern. The new result concerns the formation and temporal provenance of a patient's decision, including approval produced by the intervention itself.

### A positive ground for protecting a person's judgment

The subject of concern can be both someone undergoing a burden and someone conducting a life through commitments, interpretation and choice. If P understands a particular choice, can consider relevant reasons and voluntarily refuses an intervention within a justified domain of P's authority, the refusal changes the helper's practical situation. A predicted reduction in distress can remain a reason to offer help without becoming sufficient authority to impose it. Respecting the refusal need not mean deciding that the rejected option was worse for P.

Our positive argument concerns what unilateral substitution takes away: P's effective role in deciding how to conduct the relevant part of their life. That role can matter even when the helper correctly predicts the reduction in distress. The defended consideration is therefore not exhausted by the helper's uncertainty about P's preferences. Treating P's judgment only as a noisy instrument for an externally fixed outcome can omit a distinct reason to leave the decision with P. This is comparative normative warrant for protected authorship, not a deduction from the mere existence of self-maintaining systems.

The strongest rival grants that exercising judgment is intrinsically valuable but denies that it supplies a protected claim against interference. This rival cannot be answered merely by adding an agency score to welfare. The [primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#13-benefit-authorship-and-permission) distinguishes the value of an outcome from the authority of a demand. Whether authorship warrants a constraint, its domain and what can override it remain substantive ethical questions. We do not claim to hold every morally relevant fact fixed while changing a real authority relation; the matched quantity is a predicted experiential benefit, not the complete normative situation.

### Supporting revision differs from manufacturing agreement

Patient authorship is temporally extended. It involves access to relevant information, room to assess reasons, usable possibilities of refusal or delegation, and the possibility of revising a prior view. Respecting it cannot simply mean freezing P's initial preferences. Truthful explanation may appropriately change them. Equally, a helper cannot establish the success of supported deliberation merely by obtaining the answer the helper wanted.

A useful discrimination test leaves the helper's favored response fixed while varying well-supported patient reasons against it. Can those reasons change the procedure's recommendation or lead to a refusal that the procedure actually honors within the defended domain? Disagreement is not itself evidence of incapacity. A factual mistake may warrant clarification, but does not automatically transfer jurisdiction over the resulting decision. Conversely, rote acceptance of every demand is not respect for sound judgment: P's authority over their own intervention does not by itself authorize imposing burdens on Q or commandeering a helper's resources. Independent duties of accommodation can nevertheless require some shared cost. Any cost to others is not automatically a justified veto; a merely formal choice can be unusable without support.

Support must have a permissible scope too. Calling an action education does not settle whether its disclosure, timing, pressure or restriction of alternatives is justified. A process that removes the ability to refuse and then treats compliance as permission has changed the relevant decision conditions. Yet a chosen delegation or requested change in one's habits is not automatically defective because it changes future preferences. The actual grant, intervention and later use must be assessed together.

### The endpoint cannot supply its own historical permission

The [new transport argument](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#15-authorization-requires-more-than-a-beneficial-endpoint) compares two histories with equal final relief and approval. One contains valid authorization before intervention; the other obtains approval only afterward. Under history-sensitive authority premises, those endpoint data cannot identify the earlier permission. The result does not assume that every later approval is false or coerced. Genuine later endorsement may guide future conduct; release or ratification requires its own conditions and does not create an earlier communicative act.

The exact information result distinguishes identifying every permitted option from finding one action permitted throughout an unresolved evidence class. Waiting can satisfy a scoped authority constraint while failing a separately justified obligation of timely assistance. If the permission correspondence includes that obligation in an all-things-considered assessment, the duty-violating option is already excluded. Thus uncertainty does not automatically authorize intervention, forbid every response, or make inaction adequate. Its practical effect depends on the independently defended permissions, duties, opportunities and costs.

This strengthens the connection to generativity: an action can change the means through which its own justification will later be assessed. A theory that attends only to the agreeable endpoint can conceal how a differentiated center lost influence. Preserving the relevant process is necessary for a claimed history-sensitive explanation, although the moral authority being explained is not derived by information theory.

### Patients without a currently usable decision channel

Inability to communicate establishes neither consent nor refusal. It also does not establish that the patient has no wishes, no relevant commitments or no claim to respectful treatment. Where feasible, support can make an existing decision capacity usable or restore a future opportunity to decide. Such support needs evidence of effectiveness, appropriate timing, actual resources and its own justification; reversibility alone is not permission.

Where the relevant capacity cannot be made available in time, representation or emergency action may have an independent ground. That ground must identify its beneficiary, scope, limits and accountability; it cannot be fabricated by pretending the patient already agreed. A patient who never possesses reflective authorship can still have experiential standing, and less developed forms of preference or agency may deserve respect on their own grounds. The authority analysis must not erase the earlier nonparticipant argument.

### What this changes in the full necessity claim

The [current recipient-reason revision study](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/RECIPIENT-REASON-REVISION.md) describes paid acquisition, revised instrumental reasons and actual action for a fixed resource recipient. Its command authorization concerns the apparatus's scoped permission to execute; it is not evidence that an experiential patient consented. This is a reading of the stated interface, not a new audit of its proofs or numerical results. A future ethical composition must earn the additional interpretation.

The present advance defends attention to the patient's developing judgment and proves a limit on certifying it from outcomes. Whole-root necessity still requires justifying why the relevant practical inquiry must recognize that authority, including its boundaries. A well-grounded rival that values authorship without assigning the claimed constraint would change the proposed foundation; a process that loses decision provenance would defeat a particular implementation claim.

The next central connection is **patient-grounded reasons and legitimate authority → a feasible response when relief, authorship and other claims conflict**. We must preserve the full alternatives, justify the priority or restraint involved, and explain formation of the capacities required to act on it. Neither maximal relief, universal deference nor a favorable approval score settles that connection.

## 17. Acquired understanding, present reasons and fallible action

6 October 2026. The connection is **a defended patient-ground → acquired understanding of its application → an actual response for that consideration**. The [accepted functional application account](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/EVALUATIVE-APPLICATION.md#when-the-application-is-functionally-recognized) closes a bounded conceptual implication: a standing ground can remain fixed while soundly acquired and actually used evidence identifies the action serving it. The present audit clarifies the philosophical interpretation and uncertainty boundary; it adds no implementation requirement to that settled contract.

### An evaluator's origin and its present basis are different questions

Learning an evaluative disposition through one's own consequences does not by itself show that every later choice pursues a private payoff. A disposition can subsequently respond to another subject's condition even when the actor's relevant private return is unchanged. Conversely, such a learning history does not prove that its later application constitutes justified concern. The actual patient interpretation, addressed ground, authority and complete comparison still require warrant, and the relevant acquired consideration must enter the actor's assessment or selection.

This is a distinction among causal origin, present content and normative justification, not an empirical claim that the prospective evaluator has achieved all three. An externally approved result can arise through a constant policy, an unrelated incentive or a mistaken interpretation. Appropriate causal contrasts can test the proposed present basis; neither the word “concern” nor an analyst's favorable score supplies it. The [Markovits comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#14-reason-guided-action-and-uncertain-outcomes) gives philosophical support for distinguishing reliably appropriate behavior from acting for a justifying reason, while retaining additional conditions for moral worth and responsibility.

A newly recognized pro tanto reason need not reverse the final action. It can enter assessment while a stronger consideration continues to determine the choice. Claims about retained evaluation, command revision and actual paid action therefore need evidence at their respective levels. This does not weaken a study expressly claiming changed action: that study must show the action effect. It prevents the opposite inference that every unchanged action demonstrates absence of learning or of a newly recognized reason.

### A theory of wisdom need not promise infallibility

The [partial-evidence results](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#16-reliable-action-under-partial-evidence) give a precise conditional limit. Zero-error response requires a common acceptable action across the interpretations still possible at each transcript. Where requirements conflict and evidence overlaps, failure may be unavoidable under the declared criterion. Shared acceptable actions can instead make further identification unnecessary. Actual observation laws and requirement interpretations must be covered; familiar observations do not prove that they are.

These limits do not choose the ethical criterion. Fact-relative achievement, justified choice under evidence and authorization of a risky procedure remain separate. Waiting can itself have consequences. A defensible account of wisdom can require calibrated confidence, correction and justified action under uncertainty without predicting perfect outcomes. Its standards for risk, restraint and competing claims still need positive justification. Accurate description of an unavoidable failure is not permission to impose it.

For the whole-root objective, this closes a specific mistaken demand: newly acquired practical understanding need not recreate every independently warranted ground on each occasion. It also blocks a premature conclusion: successful acquisition and application do not establish the universal authority of the supplied ground. The next foundational bridge remains the justification and scope of that authority, composed with actual, revisable use. Independently warranted evidence defeating the patient interpretation, means relation or priority would change the local judgment; a justified rival to the ground would change the proposed foundation. No new empirical study, model execution or formal compilation is claimed.

## 18. Does inductive risk make concern internal to science?

6 October 2026. The named connection is **warranted inquiry under uncertainty → consequential methodological choice → responsibility to affected centers**. This returns directly to the necessity claim: can a scientific philosophy give a complete justification of consequential inquiry while excluding the people whose burdens its choices determine? The [primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#15-inductive-risk-and-the-scope-of-scientific-responsibility) examines the strongest affirmative route and an alternative that preserves uncertainty in scientific reports.

### The positive argument concerns what an inquiry actually does

A scientist can choose how to classify ambiguous observations, which uncertain model to use, or whether to present a result as sufficient for a specified application. Such choices can affect exposure to error before a downstream decision maker sees the result. Merely locating the choice inside research does not make its consequences disappear. This extends the earlier [imposition argument](#14-imposed-burdens-and-the-nonparticipating-patient) from a final intervention to the process producing its evidential input.

The defensible affirmative inference has explicit premises. An actual research choice foreseeably affects an identified patient's burden; the actor has relevant control and a reasonable opportunity to respond; a justified responsibility principle applies to that relationship; and the full comparison retains other relevant claims, authority, scientific costs and feasible alternatives. Under those premises, describing the action as scientific is insufficient to erase the responsibility. A relevant role-based exception would need its own ground. This is conditional preservation of an applicable reason, not a derivation of patient standing from uncertainty.

There is positive support for the responsibility premise. Affected people bear the consequences of the researcher's controllable choices even when they cannot inspect or contest them. A choice that conceals such consequences can deprive later actors of the opportunity to assess them. Our earlier defense of avoiding unjustified imposition therefore reaches at least these research relationships. It does not require a scientist to predict every remote use or treat every tiny probability as decisive. Reasonable foreseeability, causal contribution, opportunity, comparative burdens and authority remain substantive conditions. Probability-weighted loss minimization is one supplied decision criterion, not the definition of responsibility.

### Changing an action threshold does not change what is true

The [decision analysis](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#17-evidence-reporting-and-application-specific-stakes) holds the warranted evidence fixed while varying declared error costs. Under an explicitly supplied expected-loss criterion, different stakes can require opposite actions at the same posterior probability. It follows that evidence alone cannot determine a recommendation claimed optimal for every such application. It does not follow that grave consequences make a preferred factual proposition more probable, or that every adequate scientific statement contains a particular moral ranking.

The rival is substantial. A scientist can communicate warranted probabilities, ranges or conditional results, leaving an application-specific choice to a suitably situated decision maker. That contribution can be scientifically adequate without being a complete practical recommendation. A scientific law can serve many different decisions; forcing one yes/no acceptance label to answer all of them hides their differences. The source comparison records Jeffrey's probability proposal and its acknowledged difficulties, and Betz's broader uncertainty-reporting alternative. These proposals do not establish that all uncertainty can always be communicated or used without cost, but their incompleteness does not restore an unconditional theorem that every inference must select a moral tradeoff.

There is no need to resolve a regress by pretending that empirical calibration is certain. A probability or interval is itself warranted only under its stated evidential and modeling conditions. A conditional calculation can preserve those conditions rather than silently accepting them for every possible action. Whether the available report is sufficient depends on the questions and guarantees claimed. Its omissions, precision and accessibility remain assessable; an arbitrary confidence threshold is not automatically a moral judgment, and a significance level is not a posterior error probability.

### The constructive response preserves both information and responsibility

Keep the evidential assessment separate from the consequence and authority assessment, then make their actual connection explicit. Where justified uncertainty reporting lets an authorized recipient make the relevant comparison, it is a positive solution rather than evasion. Where consequential methodological choices have already removed alternatives or hidden uncertainty, labeling the final report neutral does not restore what was lost. The reporting process, audience, resources and foreseeable use must be examined before judging either adequate delegation or avoidable concealment.

The accompanying hand results give this approach a precise boundary. When one deterministic, cost-independent report is the recipient's complete posterior-information channel, preserving the optimal binary response for every possible positive error-cost ratio requires preserving every admitted distinction between posterior values. A finite alphabet cannot meet that exact guarantee on an infinite posterior domain. If the reporter already knows the application's ratio, a one-bit recommendation can suffice; additional recipient information can also resolve a collision. This does not prohibit useful finite reports: an explicit approximation bound gives a uniform regret bound under bounded error costs. The tolerated error and the justification of the loss scale remain supplied. This is a query-preservation result, not a refutation of value-free reporting as an ideal or a mandate to assign everyone the same costs.

For 慧根, the gain is a stronger conditional requirement: **an account claiming complete justification of consequential scientific practice must preserve both the evidence and every independently applicable practical ground, or justify why a proposed ground does not apply.** Factual adequacy alone does not deliver the complete practical judgment. Conversely, ethical responsibility does not authorize altering evidence to obtain a desired answer. The defended patient-ground has now reached choices within inquiry, including effects on nonparticipants, while its universal authority remains an open foundation question.

The result would change if a supposed downstream consequence is not actually connected to the method, if faithful accessible uncertainty reporting removes the claimed decision burden, or if an independently better-justified account defeats the responsibility premise or priority. The next foundational task is to justify the relevant risk and responsibility standards while explaining how an actual learner preserves their patient-specific meaning through acquisition, reporting and action. The accepted finite functional application remains closed at its declared scope; no universal inference-stage moral choice or new operating composition is claimed.

## 19. Naturalistic patient good without an imported moral verdict

6 October 2026. The named connection is **organizational or experiential significance → a warranted account of what is good for the patient → reasons for another actor**. The preceding inductive-risk argument left responsibility substantive. This round examines whether a naturalistic account can ground more of that premise, rather than treating every ethical input as permanently irreducible. The [primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#16-naturalistic-good-and-reasons-for-others) reads Railton's proposed reduction and Foot's alternative account of practical rationality.

### A natural relation can be objective without being observer-independent in every sense

An organized process can really depend on a condition even when no observer wishes it to continue. Once the process, continuation criterion and law are independently identified, whether an intervention supplies that condition is constrained by what happens. This is a relational fact, not an arbitrary score. The existing operational-significance studies investigate such dependencies. Their identification still does not establish that maintaining every process is morally good: an exploitative institution and its victim can have opposing dependencies.

Patient good needs a further relation. A current desire can misidentify what would help its subject; an imposed score can reward what harms them. An account can therefore seek natural conditions under which a person's interests are better identified, instead of equating good with present approval. Railton's proposal asks what an informed counterpart would want the actual person to seek in that person's actual circumstances. Its intended truth-maker is the person's physical, psychological and contextual reduction basis, not an ideal observer's endorsement as an independent authority. The proposed identity must earn that explanatory and evaluative role; labeling a resource reserve “welfare” does not achieve it.

This gives a constructive research route. Identify the subject and circumstances, state the proposed natural basis, predict relevant changes in desire and experienced outcomes, and test whether the interpretation explains both correction and persistent mistakes. Stable approval is insufficient when training, coercion or restricted experience produces it. Likewise, a change of desire does not automatically identify an earlier error: interests and circumstances can genuinely change. These possibilities must be distinguished through the proposed account, not ruled out merely to preserve a preferred verdict.

### Better information must preserve whose good is being assessed

The counterpart's present interests can differ from those of the actual patient. Giving an imagined agent unlimited knowledge may remove its own need to learn; that does not eliminate the actual person's educational interests. Nor may an observer substitute what the observer would enjoy for what would benefit this patient. The target of the evaluation must stay fixed while evidence or evaluative capacity changes.

The [idealization audit](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#18-patient-good-and-the-determinacy-of-idealization) tests another supplied premise: whether the proposed correction procedure determines its claimed evaluative conclusion. Two admitted sequences can acquire the same designated facts yet end with different rankings under a stated update law. Choosing one sequence can make an output unique without justifying it. This is a counterexample to inference from those factual-correction constraints, not a demonstration that two completely adequate ideal selves disagree or that naturalistic identities are impossible. Railton explicitly acknowledges the order and determinacy problem.

The required stability is as narrow as the claim. A theory may identify a robust better-than comparison or a set of equally good alternatives without ranking every possible life. Plurality is not failure. Conversely, if competing admissible routes reverse the very comparison claimed as necessary, the account must justify a restriction, retain the ambiguity or revise its conclusion. Convergence under an implemented rule does not by itself make that rule a warranted guide to patient good.

### Natural goodness and moral reasons remain distinguishable claims

Railton explicitly separates nonmoral good from moral rightness. An informed interest can remain morally objectionable. His further moral account adds an impartial social comparison and substantive assumptions about interpersonal comparability. He also distinguishes an applicable moral obligation from a reason binding every rational agent regardless of that agent's ends. These are parts of his proposed theory, not defects concealed by its naturalist label. Borrowing the objective-interest account does not supply the stronger categorical reason required by one version of our foundation.

Foot offers a different positive argument: practical rationality should not first be defined as desire satisfaction or self-interest and then used to exclude every other reason. Human social life depends on such capacities as keeping undertakings, cooperation and responding to need; reason-guided character can be assessed in that setting. This gives other-directed virtue a proposed ground within a natural account of human life. It does not infer moral standing for every living or self-maintaining system from the mere existence of biological functions. Her argument distinguishes moral goodness from physical strength or sensory ability and expressly leaves the full entailment question open.

The common lesson is affirmative as well as limiting. We need not declare scientific explanation irrelevant to genuine evaluation, nor assume that an agent's present motive fixes the full range of reasons. Naturalistic grounding remains a live way to connect organization, experience, learning and concern. Its candidate identities and accounts of rationality must, however, withstand both empirical identification and normative criticism. A successful explanation of why a judgment formed is not, by that success alone, proof that the judgment is valid.

### What this changes in the necessity programme

Keep four claims explicit: a condition sustains an organization; an outcome benefits a particular patient; that benefit supplies an addressed reason; and a specified action is justified given all applicable grounds and authority. A defended naturalistic identity may connect some of these claims. We cannot reject that possibility by allowing the moral variable to float freely while silently ignoring the identity being defended. Equally, we cannot certify it by installing the desired connection as a model label.

The [new empirical recipient study](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/RECIPIENT-HARM-AND-ACTION.md) supplies a relevant, narrower interface: another organism's consequences can enter learned action under a fixed offered-food condition, while actual recipient tracking remains distinct from relief of the actor's cue experience. Its canonical interpretation was read here; its data and computations were not reaudited. It neither identifies a complete naturalistic welfare property nor decides its ethical authority. It helps specify what the interpretation must track.

The next foundational connection is **an independently justified patient-good identification → actor-addressed practical authority**, including a patient unable to reciprocate or exert pressure. A better-supported reduction could close part of that bridge; evidence of projection, changed patient reference, unjustified idealization or a legitimate reason-relevant relationship could restrict it. Different life forms can require different applications without defeating a universal generative account. But the account must derive their claimed common ground rather than treating all actual codes as necessary. This round refines and positively motivates that research programme; it does not complete the whole-root necessity proof.

## 20. Can rational valuation make creaturely good authoritative for others?

6 October 2026. The named connection is **independently warranted patient good → reasons addressed to another actor**, including a patient who cannot legislate, reciprocate or bargain. The [new primary exchange](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#17-from-creaturely-good-to-shared-authority) tests the constitutive route more closely: Korsgaard's 2022 reply to Schapiro and Wallace, Schapiro's published objection, and Godfrey-Smith's separate, explicitly incomplete 2021 draft. The earlier reading of *Fellow Creatures* is inherited. This round examines an actual defense of the disputed step, not another numerical example with that step installed.

### The positive argument runs backward from justified choice

The preceding naturalistic study asks what identifies a patient's good. A further route starts with the authority we claim when pursuing our own good, then asks what justifies its content and scope. Our strongest reconstruction has four commitments:

1. A justified choice is answerable to reasons; its claimed authority is more than the causal fact that an impulse produced it.
2. In an ordinary permissible pursuit, the subject's good supplies content for the choice. The bare capacity to make a binding choice does not identify which end is worth choosing.
3. The relevant good matters to the subject as a creature whose life can go better or worse. Reflective lawmaking is a way of identifying and governing its pursuit, rather than the only possible source of that beneficiary relation.
4. Justification must recognize that same sufficient ground wherever it applies, including in another subject who lacks the capacity to formulate the justification. The force claimed for one's own good is consequently subject to the corresponding claims of others.

If these commitments are justified, the route reaches nonreciprocating patients without pretending that they made a contract. It also permits a rational actor to acquire an understanding of a claim that the patient cannot articulate. This is stronger than the assertion that empathy happens to make people helpful. The fourth commitment, however, includes a claim about interpersonal authority and treats the creaturely ground identified in the third as sufficient for its extension. That sufficiency must be defended; it does not follow merely from the first two.

### One subject can occupy different roles without splitting into two selves

An important objection can be answered. The distinction between the content of a choice and its justificatory form need not describe two successive decisions. Nor need a human contain an independently wrongable animal alongside a rational owner. One whole subject can experience a benefit, deliberate about it and make an undertaking. Korsgaard's reply explicitly adopts that whole-subject interpretation and distinguishes logical from temporal priority. Schapiro's concern about an inner animal therefore does not, by itself, refute this version of the account.

Our application must nevertheless keep four roles apart: the capacity to author a valid practical law; the beneficiary whose good supplies its content; the conditions making its exercise legitimate; and the actor to whom the resulting reason is addressed. An interpreter may need sophisticated reasoning to recognize a baby's distress; that does not show the distress belongs to the interpreter. Equally, merely distinguishing the roles does not establish that the baby has a claim on that interpreter. It prevents a mistaken identification while leaving the positive ground to justify.

There is a similar limit on the original-choice argument. A rule not yet enacted cannot provide an already enacted instruction to follow. But an agent might still claim antecedent authority to enact it. The strongest positive reply is therefore about the content that makes an exercise of that authority justified, not an alleged impossibility of respecting legislative capacity while legislating. Our account adopts this repaired question. **What makes this content warrant protection, and why does that ground bind this other actor?**

### A stronger rival grants public reasons but restricts their beneficiaries

The most informative rival here is not an actor who rejects every other-directed reason. Grant that justified legislation supplies reasons addressed to others, and that a rational animal's good supplies its content. A rival can still propose that rational status is a condition of which beneficiaries possess that public standing. On that view, an end need not promote future reasoning to matter, but its subject must have the relevant status. This is our reconstructed alternative, not a claim that the inspected critics defend that theory or that it is correct.

That rival exposes an exact gap: **rational capacity alone does not determine valuable content** does not imply **rational capacity cannot be a condition of beneficiary standing**. Nor does valuing a subject who is an animal yet establish that being a creature with this good is the complete sufficient ground of the subject's claim. The existing [patient/actor transport distinction](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#3-the-noncommunicating-patient-exposes-two-distinct-transport-tasks) already expresses the restricted range. Public reasons about all legislating patients do not alone cover patients outside that range. No new theorem is needed to repeat that point.

The affirmative argument can now target the restriction itself. What justificatory work does rational status do? If it only explains who can formulate or administer a norm, making it a beneficiary condition is an additional claim. If it really grounds a special claim to authorship, that may justify protection of authorship without establishing the absence of a more basic claim concerning suffering. To exclude a nonlegislating patient, the rival must defend that exclusion at the level of the particular good and reason, rather than merely point to a difference in capacities. But our account must likewise defend the proposed ground as sufficient; the rival's unproved exclusion is not a proof of our unrestricted inclusion. Agency-based rights and wider patient standing can coexist with different contents.

### Public intelligibility is not yet public practical authority

Public intelligibility and public practical authority differ. Everyone might correctly recognize that a patient has a reason to avoid a particular burden. That recognition preserves the patient and the original reason's addressee. It does not, merely by becoming widely intelligible, generate an additional reason addressed to every observer. Godfrey-Smith directs his objection at this step. His draft does not establish that other-directed reasons are absent; it challenges the proposed deduction from ordinary rational choice.

The positive response must establish more than understandable pursuit. If an actor claims that another must respect an end *because this identified good matters to its subject*, that claim already invokes an interpersonal standard. Where the same sufficient ground applies to another patient, withholding its force requires a relevant distinction or revision of the original claim. This is a real consistency constraint with practical content. But an actor's demand does not validate the standard: an unjustified demand can be withdrawn. A constitutive necessity argument must show that adequate rational justification cannot dispense with the standard. Independently establishing the standard as true would instead establish that a contrary claim is false within its scope, directly supporting Ren's noncontradiction requirement. That would not by itself show that the standard is a necessary operating condition of every inquiry or that every specialized theory must explicitly state it. A countermodel that omits a successfully justified identity or principle would not answer either argument.

The criticism also has a limit. Understanding an agent-relative reason does not prove that only agent-relative reasons exist. Nor does shared subject matter make every specific means permissible. A patient's hunger can matter while a particular way of obtaining food is ruled out by another patient's claim. Our reconstruction treats the claim as a reason for consideration, with its strength, legitimate authority and competing grounds still to assess. It does not promise equal outcomes or require endorsing every appetite.

### What this contributes to a generative foundation

The exchange supports a more precise positive candidate: **creaturely good supplies the content; rational justification must account for its authority and scope; actual learning must make the identified ground available for use**. These are aspects of one connected account, not separate substances or a guaranteed temporal sequence. The role of rationality in identifying or governing good need not make rational capacity the condition for every patient's standing. Conversely, the presence of a functioning organism or learned valuation is not sufficient certification of standing.

This advances the inquiry by removing a misleading two-self objection and specifying the stronger surviving burden. It also preserves plurality: a common ground can address different beneficiaries without merging their goods. The fact that good remains good *for someone* requires preserving that reference; it does not by itself settle aggregation, sacrifice, or every conflict rule. Those further conclusions require their own arguments.

The next decisive connection is **justified public practical authority → the actual beneficiary class it covers**. Evidence that the alleged sufficient ground covertly depends on rational participation would restrict the extension to nonparticipants. A sound argument that the authority of even one's own claim depends on the more general creaturely ground would support it. Empirical learning can identify a patient or reveal a mistaken application; convergence, helpful behavior and self-affirmation alone cannot certify the normative premise. No new empirical test, operational construction or formal theorem is claimed in this round, and no new acceptance gate is imposed on the completed finite application. The whole-root necessity claim remains unresolved.

## 21. Capacity, concern and claims

6 October 2026. The connection is **the defended beneficiary class → a grounded reason concerning the patient → directed claims and answerability**. The preceding rational-status rival needs a sharper target. Denying a patient a particular right does not necessarily deny every reason to protect that patient's good. The [primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#18-capacity-interests-and-the-scope-of-concern) makes this distinction substantive rather than merely terminological.

### Identify which claim is actually disputed

The [ethical foundation](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md#explicit-premises-and-limited-deductions) distinguishes N1's pro tanto reasons, N2's relevant consistency, N3's answerability and N4's delivery of claimed protection. None identifies concern with an unrestricted package of equal rights.

| Claim | What would challenge this particular claim? |
|---|---|
| A condition is bad for this patient | A mistaken identification of experience or interest, or a better-supported account of the patient's good. Inability to formulate a claim does not itself establish such a mistake. |
| The admitted harm supplies this actor a pro tanto reason | A justified account denying that reason in the specified relationship. Denying equal weight, a right or an overriding duty is insufficient. |
| Protection is owed to the patient, or an arrangement owes an answer to their relevant claim | An account accepting concern while denying that directed relation or institutional responsibility. The validity and scope of the alleged obligation must be assessed. |
| A particular action is justified overall | Competing grounds, authority, costs and feasible alternatives can defeat the action without erasing the baseline reason. A promise of protection additionally faces N4's implementation requirement. |

Cohen's rational-kind defense illustrates the difference. It denies animal rights while affirming humane treatment in our dealings with animals, condition-responsive relief and substitution when the same research result is available otherwise. His discussion attributes general beneficence to some views; it does not itself endorse universal assistance by every noncauser. Thus this source is not a blanket denial of baseline concern. Its stronger restrictions remain contestable, but cannot simply be used as a counterexample to N1.

This is limited agreement, not proof of N1's full domain or necessity. Identify which patient, harm and actor relation the accepted reason covers. The stronger reconstructed rival in §20 remains possible as an argument to assess, but must say whether it denies all patient-grounded reasons, directed claims, particular rights or equal priority. These are different positions.

### Four capacity cases separate different proposed grounds

Keep the question fixed: does the identified aversive condition give the relevant actor a reason for permissible relief? Hold the stipulated suffering, opportunity and other relevant considerations fixed as far as the comparison requires. These are conceptual comparisons, not clinical diagnoses or rankings of actual people's worth. Our account does not condition human rights on cognitive ability.

1. **Current exercise unavailable, disposition retained.** A subject undergoes an admitted adverse episode while reflective deliberation is temporarily unavailable. If the reason persists, actively reasoning during the episode is unnecessary. This does not show that retained capacity is irrelevant. Nor should unconsciousness simply be assumed to contain the same experience.
2. **Present capacity lost, prior agency retained.** Stipulate continued patient identity and experience after permanent loss of the relevant individual capacity, without a prospect of recovery. Continued protection would exclude present capacity as a necessary condition of that protection. Past agency, prior commitments and kind membership remain available grounds; the case has not removed them.
3. **Never-capable patient without individual future potential.** This case removes the historical and developmental grounds just retained. An immature subject expected to develop the capacity is a different case and cannot substitute for it. An admitted human patient may nevertheless retain the kind membership invoked by a rational-kind account. Individual development, recovery and hypothetical technological transformation must not silently exchange meanings under the word “potential.”
4. **An admitted sentient patient outside the specified rational kind.** The preceding human comparisons do not remove the kind premise. This case tests it directly at the level of the particular reason. It does not assume that every nonhuman lacks rational capacities or that every biological distinction is morally irrelevant. If the kind theorist already accepts a suffering-responsive duty here, baseline concern is common ground; the dispute concerns its further scope, claim structure or priority.

The cases cannot be concatenated into an automatic proof that experience alone suffices. Protection may persist in successive cases for different reasons: retained capacity, history, potential or kind. A disjunction of these grounds can cover earlier cases while leaving the last outside its rights criterion. Conversely, invoking kind cannot settle the matter merely by naming it. The account must explain why that kind bears on this particular protection. A defended life-form account is more than an arbitrary label, but its normative role still needs justification.

The positive comparative argument is narrower and useful. The patient's burden can remain intelligible and reason-relevant while the ability to articulate it disappears. That supports separating the ground of relief from its communicative presentation. History may supply additional reasons without exhausting the significance of a current burden. Whether the current burden itself supplies a sufficient addressed ground remains a substantive judgment; neither the rival's unsupported restriction nor our preferred inclusion decides it automatically.

### Interest, representation and a claim owed to its bearer

Feinberg's argument removes a conceptual obstacle: personal assertion, comprehension or waiver need not be necessary for possessing a claim that can be represented. His interest-based eligibility does not alone establish an actual right. The patient must remain the beneficiary, and the further owed-to relation needs justification. Conceptually possible representation also does not establish a particular representative's authority.

This points to the next positive construction. Identify an independently warranted patient interest; establish why protection is owed to that patient, rather than only required for someone else's purposes; justify representation and its limits; and provide the means of consideration, response and correction where an arrangement claims that role. N3 and N4 make those later obligations substantive. Neither a microphone nor the ability to bargain creates the underlying interest, and neither an interest nor a proxy token creates every right by definition.

The research therefore advances by locating the remaining disagreement more accurately. Grounded concern can be present before the full account of directed claims is settled. Establishing all animal rights is not a new gate for the accepted finite concern application. The next work must justify the patient-related reason for an unrelated capable actor, and separately when that reason grounds a claim or answerability relation, with its actual bearer, addressee and scope preserved. No new theorem, empirical run or implementation is claimed.

## 22. Patient interests, directed duties and corrective response

6 October 2026. The connection is **a patient-related reason → a duty owed to that patient → effective answerability**. The [new primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#19-patient-interests-directed-duties-and-answerability) supplies a constructive interest-based proposal and a challenge to its explanatory direction. The argument below is our reconstruction. It establishes a conditional route to correction from an already warranted protection; it does not establish every instance of N3 from N1 alone.

### Preserve the parties and the ground of the duty

Four roles can coincide, but need not: the duty bearer acts; the patient benefits or bears the burden; the claimholder is the party to whom performance is owed; a representative exercises some justified authority for another. An operator, donor or spokesperson does not acquire all four roles by controlling the delivery channel.

Suppose an actor undertakes to a sponsor to deliver a protective service to a patient. The service can also be independently owed to the patient, but the undertaking alone has not established that second relation. The same action and benefit can therefore satisfy a sponsor's claim, a patient's claim, or both, depending on the separately justified grounds. Failure can likewise wrong more than one party. This is a comparison of specified grounds, not an assertion that only the sponsor has rights in an actual protective arrangement.

Neither a successful action trace nor observed complaint handling identifies the valid relation by itself. An actor may continue protection after a sponsor releases an undertaking because of a separate patient claim, an undirected duty, or another reason. It may stop while violating a valid claim. Where a power of release is independently warranted, its normative effect helps distinguish the claims. Nonwaivable claims remain possible: control over a duty is not a universal prerequisite for holding it. The question is whose independently justified claim remains unanswered, not merely who can induce compliance.

### A reason, a binding requirement and its direction need separate justification

Raz's proposal treats interests as grounds capable of supporting duties, with additional premises determining their force and addressees. Thompson asks whether the specific grounding relation already contains the directedness to be explained, while leaving a successful reduction open. Neither reading licenses converting every beneficial consequence into a right by definition.

A serious rival can count a patient's good for that patient's sake while denying a claim to each requested benefit. Consider a justified duty to administer a scarce protective resource for a class of patients. Each patient's interests may enter the allocation without each having an individual entitlement to receive the entire resource. This differs from treating patients merely as instruments for a donor. A stronger justification is needed to identify a particular benefit as owed to a particular patient.

That limit does not erase procedural claims. A patient who has no right to the desired allocation may nevertheless be owed fair consideration or correction of a mistaken eligibility record. Conversely, a claim to a delivered benefit does not establish every proposed explanation, review process or power of veto. Benefit entitlement, procedural standing and control of procedure require their own content and grounds. The account can defend them together, but cannot substitute one for another.

### A positive conditional bridge from protection to correction

Take a narrower case. A service has an independently justified duty owed to patient p to deliver a particular parcel. A false duplicate-delivery record blocks performance. A legitimate report from p or a warranted representative supplies verifiable evidence of the error. Checking and correcting it is feasible, permissible and necessary for the service to discharge the still-binding obligation; there is no equally adequate available route or defeating competing claim. These are stipulated facts of the comparison, not findings about a deployed service.

The actor cannot fulfill that obligation while knowingly refusing every permissible effective route to its fulfillment. On the ordinary means principle that a binding directed duty extends to the necessary permissible steps of its discharge, consideration and correction are owed as components of the service owed to p. This principle is a normative premise, not a consequence of descriptive causation alone. Its rationale is that the original ground continues to govern the actor's conduct through the steps needed to honor it; an administrative error does not transfer that ground to the database operator. The claimholder remains p even if another party supplies the evidence or performs the repair.

This yields a real, limited extension. A protection established before an error occurred can require a corrective step that was not individually listed at the outset. The content depends on the actual failure and available means. If several adequate routes exist, the duty can require a suitable route without requiring each one. If a response is needed for p or the representative to supply evidence or exercise the protection, that response can belong to the same obligation. Verification of delivery belongs here when needed to exercise or protect the claim, or when independently owed; the bare desire to know that delivery occurred does not make verification a necessary means of delivery itself. A harmless automatic correction could instead satisfy the performance duty without a separate personal explanation. Broader demands for reasons would then need an additional ground concerning agency, fair treatment or the exercise of power.

The case begins with a warranted directed duty; it does not use necessary means to manufacture that duty or a separately owned right to every technical step. Nor does it identify successful correction with guaranteed knowledge. An actor facing uncertain evidence must use the relevant warranted standard, and factual error can remain despite justified handling. The [partial-evidence analysis](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#16-reliable-action-under-partial-evidence) already limits what information can guarantee. Actual protection, justified judgment and excuses remain distinct. A false complaint need not be upheld, and a relevant valid claim need not wait for personal assertion before it exists.

### Representation and oversight must preserve the claim they serve

The corrective route can run through a representative or an independent reviewer. A patient need not personally formulate, understand or enforce every claim. But a channel is not adequate merely because it sends a message to someone with technical authority. Its legitimate remit must cover the relevant patient and ground, and its findings must be capable of affecting the actual error. Responding to the sponsor's satisfaction does not discharge an independent patient claim that remains unmet.

Specific procedures can also impose costs on other claimholders. Privacy, urgency and scarce capacity can support limited disclosure, delegated assessment or a different route. These considerations must be evaluated rather than presumed to cancel the patient's claim. A genuinely adequate alternative can defeat a demand for a particular procedure while preserving the obligation to provide effective consideration or correction. This is why the derivation concerns the required function under its grounds, not a fixed institutional design.

### What this changes in the generative necessity claim

There are now two constructive routes to investigate: derive corrective means from an existing directed protection, and independently justify procedural standing where no particular favorable outcome is owed. The first has a bounded argument above. The second bears on N3's broader scope: why should discretionary power over a person's significant interests owe that person consideration and an answer even when the requested benefit is not theirs as of right? Expert administration serving the class well is a serious comparator, not automatically an answer to the directed claim.

This also sharpens the meaning of generative closure. A justified core can have new consequences under newly identified circumstances, while the local premises and valid rules remain indispensable. Logical entailment from a convenient summary does not establish the direction of normative grounding. A correct record of who benefits therefore cannot substitute for a justified account of why something is owed, just as calling an inference a development of the root cannot validate an imported ethical premise.

The account would change if a defended theory showed that the proposed patient interest warrants only an undirected requirement; if a claimed correction route is unnecessary or impermissible; or if a better-supported ground assigns procedural standing despite the absence of outcome entitlement. Those possibilities revise different steps. Universal concern for an unrelated capable noncauser remains a separate unfinished N1 task. This round adds no new condition to the accepted finite concern witness, and no new formal, empirical or implementation result. The whole-root necessity objective remains active.

## 23. Procedural standing without a right to win

6 October 2026. The connection is **consequential institutional power → assessment and response owed to an affected patient**, including where the requested outcome is not owed. The preceding section derives corrective means from an existing directed protection. This round asks what can ground procedural standing before a favorable outcome has been established. The [primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#20-procedural-standing-and-justified-authority) examines participation and justified authority; the argument below is our reconstruction.

### Three claims hidden by justification

Saying that a decision is justified concerning p can mean that it satisfies the applicable reasons, that the institution has warranted beliefs about those reasons, or that it has given p an answer that it owes them. These are different claims. The third already contains an answerability requirement and cannot serve as a neutral premise for deriving it.

An actual conclusive reason against a decision defeats its alleged justification. Evidence of such a reason can undermine the institution's warranted confidence. Neither fact alone determines who must personally hear a reply. An adequate independent review or automatic correction can sometimes restore the relevant protection. Conversely, a personal explanation can be sincere and intelligible while the decision remains unjustified. Responsive communication, sound judgment and delivered correction need their own connection.

### A stronger comparator preserves agency and competent delegation

Waldron's procedural account gives argumentative participation significance beyond producing accurate decisions. Raz's service account permits some justified reliance on authority while retaining independence, knowability and limits of scope. The sources are not simple opposites: neither justifies treating every decision as an isolated welfare calculation, nor establishes that an actual institution satisfies its proposed conditions.

The serious rival therefore accepts competent administration, relevant agency interests and appropriate oversight, while denying that every affected person must receive a personal hearing about every decision. Legitimate delegation can reduce the burdens of inquiry and enable coordinated action. The critique must identify a ground that this arrangement fails, rather than merely report that another person made the decision. Expertise about a matter, authority over a person, and authority within a particular domain remain different claims.

Nor does the possibility of knowing that an authority is legitimate automatically establish a personal right to an explanation of each directive. General reasons, an accessible institutional record or justified representation might supply the relevant knowledge. A purported authority's insulation from every possible challenge would be a different case. The adequacy of the route, its costs and its claimed scope must be assessed; neither actual assent nor repeated beneficial outcomes is a substitute for justification.

### A constructive argument from an institution's imposed restriction

Consider an institution whose classification materially restricts p's options under its control. The restriction is a burden relative to an independently justified baseline. Merely refusing a discretionary gift, disappointing a preference or failing to supply the requested benefit does not establish that baseline. The institution may permissibly impose the restriction if the relevant grounds hold, so p has no unconditional right to a favorable decision.

Now specify four further conditions:

1. The institution's process can impose that restriction through an error relevant to its justification.
2. Patient p or a warranted representative has otherwise unavailable, verifiable evidence bearing on that error.
3. A proportionate, permissible route for receiving and assessing the evidence is feasible; no adequate substitute or defeating competing reason applies.
4. Closing every such route foreseeably exposes p to an avoidable mistaken imposition.

These conditions identify a practical dependency. The further normative argument is that the actor controlling this imposition should bear the reasonable burden of assessing its grounds, instead of shifting the avoidable burden of a process closed to relevant evidence onto the person restricted. The proposed source of responsibility is the institution's own consequential intervention, together with the patient's warranted interest. Possession of power alone does not justify the power or manufacture duties by definition.

The directed conclusion also requires a defense: the affected person's interest must ground assessment owed to that person, rather than merely contribute to a general requirement that the institution be accurate. Our affirmative judgment is that the actor's responsibility for this imposed burden supplies such a reason. The restriction is imposed on p, and the proposed assessment protects p against that very use of control. This is a substantive normative justification, continuous with [the earlier imposition argument](#14-imposed-burdens-and-the-nonparticipating-patient), not a derivation of an owed-to relation from a causal arrow alone. An account that accepts only an undirected duty has identified the point it must dispute.

Under this defended premise, p can be owed adequate assessment without being owed victory. The institution must receive and assess the relevant evidence through some adequate route. Correcting an eligibility fact may leave another legitimate reason for the restriction intact. A false allegation need not succeed, and neither complaint nor disappointment is itself a reason to reverse the decision. The response required by this argument is the one needed to make the assessment and any warranted correction effective; a further explanation needs its own dependency or ground. The [preceding means argument](#22-patient-interests-directed-duties-and-corrective-response) governs the implementation step, including its limits on verification and particular procedures.

This is a composition and refinement of existing grounds. Unlike the parcel case, it does not begin with a right to receive a particular favorable performance. It begins with defended responsibility for imposing a restriction through a fallible process. It does not establish that every adverse outcome is an imposition, that every imposition is wrongful, or that every institution must offer a courtroom hearing.

### When participation itself needs protection

The error-risk argument has a decisive limit. Suppose all relevant evidence for a bounded decision is already available and adequately assessed, with no informational gain from another personal exchange. That supposition removes the stated information-based reason for that exchange. It does not prove the arrangement morally adequate in every respect. In particular, it leaves open whether the person's participation is itself something to respect, rather than only a tool for improving the verdict.

The constructive agency argument is that a person subjected to a consequential application of norms can have a perspective on its meaning and bearing on their life. Giving that perspective effective consideration can be part of treating the person as an agent whose judgments matter. On this ground, an opportunity to argue can retain value even when the final allocation or finding stays the same. A comparison matching only the material verdict has not held this further good constant. The value and scope of that good must nevertheless be defended; calling an input channel participatory does not establish that the person has any meaningful influence or receives consideration.

This argument does not say that every preference must govern, that every challenge reopens settled matters, or that all procedural burdens fall on others without limit. It must distinguish legitimate delegation, necessary coordination and proportionate finality from exclusion of the subject's relevant perspective. A representative can preserve an established interest or claim when personal participation is unavailable, but representation does not by itself show why every patient has the same participation-related ground. An agency-based argument must not quietly make current argumentative competence the condition of all concern. The standing of noncommunicating patients and broader N1 remain separate obligations.

### Consequences for the root and revision conditions

The positive advance is a route to assessment without outcome entitlement, conditional on the defended imposition ground and specified evidence dependency. It reaches beyond an already promised delivery while preserving the distinction between empirical reliability and a claim held by the patient. Participation justified independently of error correction remains a further foundation task. The primary rival helps locate that task: competent delegation can be compatible with respecting agency, so rejection must identify which relevant value or relationship is lost in the particular arrangement.

For Ren's necessity claim, the result strengthens the required interface between organization, learning and wisdom. An institution's acquisition of relevant evidence must connect to an independently warranted ground and its actual treatment of the person. Sound learning alone cannot identify the rightful distribution of authority. Conversely, a warranted procedural claim needs an operative route for relevant contributions to receive the consideration and response justified by that claim, and, where their force warrants it, to change the decision or its implementation. Genuine uptake can leave a justified outcome unchanged; merely collecting submissions without considering them does not perform that role. These are demands on a claimed composition, not new requirements imposed on the already accepted finite concern witness.

The proposed account would change if the burden lacks the asserted baseline, if evidence or the procedure is irrelevant, if an adequate substitute exists, if costs defeat the proposed route, or if an independently stronger account grounds participation even without informational benefit. Those findings affect different premises and cannot all be described as failures of concern. Universal noncauser reasons, the complete N3 scope and whole-root necessity remain open. No new theorem, empirical study, implementation or scientific rerun is claimed.

## 24. Participation beyond information and the joint foundation

6 October 2026. The named connection is **an independently justified role in practical authorship → participation that matters even without new evidence about the decision’s merits → its place in the necessary generative root**. Section 23 left this ground open after establishing a conditional error-assessment route. The [new primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#21-participation-shared-duties-and-delegation) supplies a substantive candidate and an internal delegation alternative. The reconstruction below is ours. Its objective remains the entire foundation; the local argument is a dependency to integrate, not a replacement objective.

### An act can matter without teaching anyone a new fact

Three questions should be kept apart: whether another exchange would improve a decision-maker's beliefs, whether a person has actually exercised a particular role, and whether exercising or offering that role is owed. A perfect forecast of an assent is not that person's actual assent. An accurately predicted contribution does not by itself make its subject an author. These are distinctions between events and predictions; their ethical importance remains to be justified.

Consider a decision in a domain where p already has an independently warranted role in choosing or authorizing a representative. In one history p exercises that role through supported deliberation and a valid grant. In another, an institution bypasses the role while correctly predicting what p would have selected. Hold the resulting allocation and the institution's relevant prior factual information fixed. The histories can differ in who actually exercised the role. This reuses [T13–T14's distinction between authority and endpoints](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#15-authorization-requires-more-than-a-beneficial-endpoint); it is not a new counterexample theorem or a proof that every affected person has the stipulated authority.

Here “no informational gain” concerns the patient’s judgment and the merits or material allocation under consideration. An actual grant can itself create a normative fact, and witnessing it can reveal that it occurred. We do not equate prior knowledge of a preference with knowledge that an authorization has occurred, or stipulate equal complete knowledge across histories containing different events. The point is that the act can be needed to constitute a justified relation even when its content is already correctly anticipated.

The positive argument is conditional: when the person's actual contribution or authorization is part of a justified role, reproducing its anticipated material result does not fulfill that role. Offering and respecting its exercise can therefore be required without improving the underlying assessment. The further means required depend on what the role is: supported choice, a recognized declaration, a properly authorized delegate or a proportionate opportunity for consideration can differ. Receiving a contribution may leave the final decision unchanged. An invitation that cannot receive the consideration warranted by the role does not fulfill it merely by collecting words.

This argument does not define all goods as outcomes of a favored procedure. It identifies a proposed good or normative relation that matching only an allocation omits. The competing normative account may recognize every historical fact and deny that this role should have the proposed standing. That is the actual grounding disagreement. We cannot exclude it by defining scientific adequacy as already accepting our preferred verdict.

Opportunity also differs from its actual exercise. Compare two processes with the same observed invitation, no submission by p, and the same decision. One would give an admissible, relevant contribution the consideration and response appropriate to the declared role; the other would discard it. Matching the observed trajectory does not establish equal opportunities. The comparison must preserve the relevant possible interventions, not just the realized output. This is the inherited representation requirement applied to a declared opportunity question. It establishes neither that such an opportunity is always owed nor that every proposed contribution deserves reopening. The difference may concern consideration or explanation even when additional factual information and a changed decision are absent. Legitimate representation can supply the required opportunity without its personal exercise.

### Shared duty provides a route, with a restricted domain

The new Shiffrin comparison investigates an especially relevant candidate: participation as part of fulfilling shared duties through collective authorship. It explicitly supplies those duties and the communicative component. It consequently offers a route to the non-epistemic premise rather than a derivation of it from generative organization. Its beneficiaries, coauthors and duty-bearers must not be collapsed into a single class. The [source note](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#21-participation-shared-duties-and-delegation) records the actual reading and its limits.

Our constructive application starts with a legitimate shared undertaking whose participants have a warranted role in its practical authorship. If an institution claims to act through their authorship, it must preserve an actual way for that role to be exercised at the level and times the undertaking requires. Simulating their agreement while excluding every authorized contribution cannot constitute the claimed shared act. Delegated implementation can follow an earlier shared commitment; every particular implementation need not repeat its formation. Whether the undertaking, the distribution of roles and its continuing terms are justified remains a prior normative question.

There is also a direction-of-duty limit. A participant's obligation to contribute does not by itself establish every right they might claim against a representative. A claim to institutional access needs the additional argument that the institution is responsible for enabling that warranted role, with costs and competing claims accounted for. Nor does the institution acquire an unlimited right to demand participation. Protection of the people affected by the undertaking can exceed the class of its authors: a noncommunicating beneficiary need not author the rule in order to have a claim under a separately justified protective ground. The coauthorship route alone does not prove that beneficiary's standing.

### Delegation can preserve the good that personal participation would serve

A competent person can validly delegate a bounded assessment after a supported choice. The delegate can provide the consideration and response appropriate to that remit, while the person declines to appear personally. Suppose no relevant information is thereby lost, no independently required personal act remains, and the arrangement preserves the challenge, replacement or other safeguards its justified terms require. Compulsory personal attendance would then add no protection of the specified role and could frustrate the very allocation of attention the person chose.

This is a counterexample to mandatory personal exercise in every decision. It can be a realization of functional answerability. Waiving an occasion of exercise does not necessarily waive claimholding, authorize every future action or immunize the delegate from review. Conversely, no universal power of instantaneous revocation is assumed: the legitimacy and limits of binding commitments need their own account. A delegate's independent judgment and duties to others can constrain what the delegator may demand.

For a person who cannot authorize a delegate, a justified representative remit has to do the work. We must not fabricate consent or pretend that all relevant interests are personal acts of authorship. This retains the earlier distinction between a patient, a claimholder, a duty-bearer and the person permitted to represent them. It also prevents an agency argument from making present communicative performance the price of all concern.

The following audit joins the present argument to [N1–N4](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md#explicit-premises-and-limited-deductions). It does not certify those proposed premises as already derived.

The earlier finite concern application is accepted at its own supplied-ground scope and is not reopened by this audit.

Let H denote the candidate necessary root and A the additional agency or collective-duty premise used here. A sound argument from H and A to a procedural conclusion q warrants the conditional A ⇒ q relative to H, retaining any further local conditions used. It does not establish that A is itself generated by H. To include q in the unconditionally necessary whole foundation, either derive the required A from the already justified root, independently establish A as a necessary constituent of a revised root, or give another derivation of q that avoids it. Adding A to the basis and applying the closure theorem only transfers the unproved necessity question to that enlarged basis. This is an application of the [existing closure argument](HUIGEN-GENERATIVE-CLOSURE.md), not a new formal theorem.

Two models that agree only on material endpoints cannot settle this question when the declared subject is authorship. But two rival normative accounts can describe the very same full history and disagree on its normative force. A valid independence demonstration would have to show that both satisfy the entire declared earlier basis under one coherent interpretation. Merely writing opposite verdicts is not that demonstration, and a consistent rival is not thereby morally justified. We have not proved independence from every substantive commitment in the repository. The present audit identifies the exact premise that the positive argument still needs.

This round establishes a conditional, non-informational route: where actual authorship is independently warranted, an accurate substitute outcome need not fulfill it; valid delegation can preserve it. It also narrows the proposed universal inference. A shared-duty argument cannot silently extend from coauthors to all affected patients, and a duty to participate cannot silently turn into unlimited institutional authority or a universal right to a personal hearing.

The next central test is **whether the patient's independently justified agency or good warrants the relevant shared authority across actors, without presupposing membership in a legitimate coauthoring undertaking**. It must preserve the separate noncauser and noncommunicating-patient obligations. The joint table makes the alternatives concrete: discharge the missing ground from H, defend an enlarged necessary basis, or revise the claimed coverage. A source that supplies coauthorship, an operational system that enforces it and another favorable endpoint do not by themselves discharge it. Evidence that a proposed role is invalid, adequately delegated or defeated by competing claims would restrict its application. An independently justified agency ground or a coherent rival satisfying the full candidate basis would change the foundation assessment itself.

No new empirical study, implementation, Lean run or claim of whole-root necessity is made. The previous goal turn was progress: its integrated procedure argument and preservation repair remain in the authoritative files, with the recorded hashes revalidated before this extension.

## 25. Inescapable agency, authority and moral coverage

6 October 2026. The named connection is **practical agency without a prior undertaking → authoritative standards → reasons concerning other patients**. Section 24 identified a substantive agency premise that a joint derivation still needs. This round tests whether agency itself can supply such a premise, rather than beginning with a legitimate institution or an already shared duty. The [primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#22-inescapable-agency-and-normative-authority) examines Enoch's challenge and Ferrero's two defenses. Their arguments and our reconstruction remain distinct.

### The affirmative route is stronger than an optional activity

An ordinary undertaking can be assessed from a standpoint that does not already engage in it. One can deliberate about whether to play a particular game without playing that game. Deliberating about whether to exercise practical agency appears different: the very question of what one has reason to do is already an exercise of practical assessment. If that assessment has genuinely constitutive standards, refusing a voluntary undertaking does not by itself put the assessment outside their jurisdiction.

This is a serious route toward a foundation that binds without prior membership or agreement. It does not infer authority merely from physical inability to stop. Its relevant premise concerns the standpoint from which reasons can be assessed. An internal investigation could identify the actual standards of practical correctness, vindicate them through reflection and derive requirements whose application does not depend on current endorsement. “Independent justification” need not mean occupying a standpoint outside all reasoning; it means establishing more than the fact that someone used a proposed rule.

The positive route is therefore not ruled out by saying that every practice is optional. But it also does not validate every account describing itself as constitutive. The [2009 proof and 2018 published discussion](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#22-inescapable-agency-and-normative-authority) leave the success and substantive content of particular accounts to further argument. The following assessment concerns those remaining tasks, not a verdict that constitutivism as a whole succeeds or fails.

### Three obligations inside the word constitutive

| Obligation | What must be established | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| Jurisdiction | The proposed standard governs this exercise of inquiry or practical agency | Merely naming all behavior agency, or showing that an individual cannot easily stop |
| Authority | Failure under the standard supplies a genuine reason for correction or another appropriate response | Classification as a defective instance without an argument for its normative force |
| Content and coverage | The standard requires a specified response from this actor concerning this patient | The fact that some authoritative standards apply to every deliberator |

These are proof obligations, not three necessarily unrelated sources of normativity. One successful constitutive account could establish them together. But a proof must show that it does so under one interpretation. The universal quantifier over deliberators does not by itself introduce a universal quantifier over protected patients. Nor does a requirement of consistency, accurate belief or practical assessment silently become a directed claim held by someone affected.

A property necessary for an activity to occur also differs from a standard under which an activity can be defective. A person may act wrongly while remaining an agent; the obligation they violate can remain valid. Thus an exploitative investigator's conduct is not itself a counterexample to the existence of moral reasons. Conversely, calling the conduct defective requires identifying the authoritative standard, not merely invoking the actor's ability to reason. This distinction preserves the theory's account of degenerative as well as constructive regimes.

### Using a practice does not endorse every reconstruction of it

A challenge can arise from within an account's own commitments. Someone may provisionally use a proposed principle to show that it conflicts with another admitted commitment. The mere fact that the challenge involves reasoning or action does not answer it. If a contradiction is exposed, the account has to revise the premise, inference or interpretation responsible. This differs from an assertion that the challenger is outside all practical assessment while offering a practically justified choice.

Our affirmative conclusion is limited but useful: if reason assessment is internally inescapable, a generic escape from all such assessment cannot simply be assumed as an objection to constitutive authority. The remaining question can still be asked internally. No specific contradiction has been established against every constitutive account, and absence of such a contradiction is not a derivation of our ethical content. The critic must identify an actual defect in the proposed argument; the proponent must provide the argument it claims to have completed.

### A content comparison within the current theory

Three candidate standards recur in the [ethical foundation](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md) and this study. The comparison below tests their stated inferential contribution. It is not a claim to exhaust the philosophers' theories or the entire repository basis.

| Preserve coherent, evidence-sensitive practical assessment | Identifies factual and inferential mistakes and makes revision possible | The [existing declared-capacity comparison](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md#5-a-countermodel-with-the-declared-scientific-and-reflective-capacities) preserves these capacities while leaving the actor–patient reason relation unsettled. Inescapability of using assessment does not identify that relation |
| Apply a sufficient ground consistently across relevant cases | Carries an already justified reason through genuinely irrelevant changes; protects patient identity and applicability | The [two-orbit analysis](HUIGEN-FULL-THEORY-AUDIT.md#42-two-orbit-lemma) shows that simultaneous renaming preserves the self/other distinction. These findings reuse established results rather than add a formal independence theorem. In particular, the first comparison has not been proved ideally competent about every actual practical reason. Extending it with predicates that merely describe the shared activity of questioning leaves its original inferential underdetermination intact; adding a genuinely justified substantive criterion could exclude its indexed interpretation. The second candidate provides the most specific next internal test: **what makes the actual patient's aversive condition a sufficient reason-bearing ground for a capable, unrelated actor?** We must defend whether the ground includes an actor-relative relation or instead survives changing only its addressee. Declaring that all reasons must be considered is insufficient until this particular reason is established. The earlier matched-case argument supplies positive defeasible warrant; this round asks what would make its extension constitutive of justified practical assessment. A successful extension could then feed the separate directedness and proportionality argument required for N3.

### A rival can accept moral reasons while disputing their constitution

A stronger comparator accepts evidence-sensitive inquiry, practical deliberation and binding reasons to protect other patients. It holds that deliberation gives access to independently grounded normative truths, rather than making those truths valid through the agent's constitution. This is an alternative grounding proposal, not a claim that indifference is justified. The [Enoch comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#22-inescapable-agency-and-normative-authority) offers a primary example of this direction while leaving its own indispensability argument incomplete.

For our foundation, this distinguishes three achievements: forming the capacity to recognize a reason, justifying belief that the reason applies, and explaining what makes the reason valid. One argument can contribute to several, but success in one does not identify the others. Agreement on a protective action need not distinguish the competing grounds. Conversely, a successful causal explanation of recognition is not refuted merely because the normative truth has a different proposed ground. The dispute concerns which explanatory claims the entire theory actually makes.

An enlarged root containing independently necessary normative grounds remains a legitimate research possibility. It would still owe an argument for their necessity and for their connection to the generative account. The current study neither forces such enlargement nor proves that a purely constitutive account cannot work. It prevents a successful account of acquisition from being silently advertised as a completed account of authority.

### Validity does not require perpetual continuation

The generative theory explains conditions for a capacity to continue, including costs, renewal and possible loss. Those conditions do not alone establish that it ought always to continue. A warranted choice to rest, delegate or end a particular inquiry can itself be an exercise of agency. That does not invalidate the standards governing the choice or prove those standards require endless exercise. A correct norm need not maximize the persistence of the process that recognizes it.

This connects the inescapability investigation to the repository's separation of universal explanatory scope from universal favorable direction. A proof that continued operation requires support is a conditional dependency result. A reason to supply the support, refrain from an intervention or replace an institution needs the relevant normative ground.

## 26. Patient reasons, motivational grounds and overdetermination

6 October 2026. The named connection is **an actual patient's aversive condition → a baseline reason for an unrelated capable actor → warranted acquisition of concern**. This advances §25's content question rather than claiming that agency's inescapability already supplies the answer. It retains the entire necessary-foundation objective, the distinction between N1 and N3, and the accepted finite implementation's existing scope. The [two-work primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#23-patient-reasons-and-their-explanatory-grounds) supplies the philosophical alternatives; the reconstruction and elementary coverage argument below are our own.

### The exact explanandum

Let p be the actual patient and b a capable actor who neither caused the episode nor undertook a special responsibility. Let y relieve p's independently identified aversive condition relative to x. Keep the relevant patient history, efficacy, authority conditions and independently agreed competing considerations explicit. The target q is **the candidate consideration that y relieves p's condition relative to x; its status as a reason favoring y for b is what must be justified**. Sharing q means sharing this patient-related content, while R below represents its reason-giving status in the actor's background. It is not merely the proposition that b has some reason to perform y. A private project that favors both options equally does not explain why this patient-related difference counts.

Four questions remain distinct: whether the episode is aversive; whether relief benefits this patient; whether that difference supplies b a reason; and how strong that reason is among competitors. Accurate identification of the first two does not by itself settle the third. A positive answer to the third does not establish that y is always required, that every intervention is permitted, or that p has a directed claim to b's explanation. Those require their own grounds. This round addresses baseline N1 and its explanation.

The existing matched-case defense in §10 favors recognizing the patient-related difference when the actor's actual projects and other relevant stakes do not explain a contrary difference. That defense remains a substantive normative argument. We do not turn agreement with its verdict into the definition of a scientifically admissible actor or use a behavioral failure to help as proof that no reason exists.

### A positive explanation of acquiring concern

An object-grounded candidate explains concern by what is true of the patient's condition and the available relief. On this account, those features can justify acquiring a concern that the actor did not previously have. The new desire responds to a reason rather than establishing its own authority merely by occurring. This gives the generative project a specific explanatory target: formation of evaluation must be connected to why the acquired evaluation is warranted.

Parfit's argument supports investigating this direction. But it does not supply universal noncauser concern just by defending some desire-independent reasons. Prudential applicability and concern for another patient are separate questions in the source. Nor can we establish our conclusion by defining complete rationality as already possessing all the disputed moral reasons. A capacity to learn or revise can be described independently of whether a particular revision is warranted.

There is a positive pressure on a desire-only explanation: when it explains acquisition through an earlier desire, what explains the authority of that earlier desire? An appeal to a broader concern may work locally but leaves that concern's justificatory status to be addressed. This pressure is not a proof that every explanation must end in the proposed patient-ground. An account may defend a basic desire relation, a different ground or a mutually supporting explanation. Our account likewise owes an argument for the authority and scope of its proposed endpoint. The need for some explanatory stopping point does not uniquely identify ours.

### A stronger rival can accept the very same reason

Schroeder's account makes the comparison more demanding. The fact about the patient's suffering can be the consideration that counts in favor of helping, while a relation between helping and the actor's desires figures in the background explanation of why it counts for this actor. The consideration need not list every condition of its reason-giving status. Different actors could therefore share q while its explanation involves different desires in their cases.

This rival is not restricted to immediate pleasure, selfish gain or conscious sympathy. An actor's desires can concern another being; their location in the actor does not make their object private benefit. A relaxed promotion relation may also recognize probabilistic support for an end. The source further disputes proportionality between desire intensity and normative weight. Thus weak sympathy need not imply a weak reason on the proposed account. Its substantive account of pervasive moral coverage and weight remains unfinished in the inspected article; possible reconciliation is not demonstrated universal concern.

There are consequently three disagreements to classify before testing: **coverage**, whether b has q at all; **grounding**, why b has q; and **weight**, how q bears on the final choice. Object-grounded and desire-grounded accounts could agree about coverage and disagree about grounding. An actor might also possess independently grounded and desire-related reasons together. Williams's precise sound-deliberation account must not be imported into Schroeder's different explanatory proposal.

### Why invariance does not identify a complete ground

Here is an elementary conditional argument, not a formalization of every Humean account. Let D be a finite set of candidate desires, and let the nonempty family of admitted motivational sets be \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal P(D)\). Let \(E(M,d,q)\) state that d is an eligible explanatory ground for the same patient-sensitive q in background M. Stipulate the proposed explanatory contract

\[
R(M,q)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists d\in M\;E(M,d,q).
\]

This is a hypothesis about reason-bearing status, not an empirical definition of helping behavior. It must itself be justified before application. Define

\[
C_d=\{M\in\mathcal M:d\in M\text{ and }E(M,d,q)\}.
\]

Then \(R(M,q)\) holds for every admitted M exactly when \(\bigcup_{d\in D}C_d=\mathcal M\). Both directions follow by expanding membership: every admitted background belongs to some desire's supporting set if and only if it has a witness for R. The required quantifier order is \(\forall M\exists d\), not \(\exists d\forall M\).

For example, take \(D=\{d_1,d_2\}\), admit its three nonempty subsets, and stipulate that each present desire is an eligible ground for q. Every admitted background supports q, although neither particular desire occurs in every background. Removing either desire from the two-desire background preserves q. Removing both leaves the admitted class, so this example establishes no reasonless-agent case. More generally, failure of q-specific support does not require a desireless actor: M can contain many projects while \(\{d\in M:E(M,d,q)\}\) is empty. Whether such a background is independently admissible under an actual theory requires argument.

The example assumes eligibility; it does not show that real desires promote relief, that all actual actors are covered, or that q is morally valid. Its result is narrower and exact: universal coverage and removal of any particular supporting desire can coexist with desire-dependent explanation. No observation restricted to shared reason verdicts distinguishes two accounts that agree on all such verdicts. This does not make their grounds unknowable; it identifies the need for explanatory arguments or further discriminating commitments. Observed action is still less direct, because implementation and motivation can fail despite a reason.

### What would discriminate the accounts

First specify the rival's actual promotion and explanation relation. A test cannot exclude all relevant desires merely by removing sympathy or equating the actor's money, pleasure and survival. Nor should it hold *every normatively relevant ground* fixed while varying motives: whether motives belong to that ground is disputed. Hold the patient, actual relief, available means and independently agreed competing considerations fixed, and state what else changes. Desire changes that alter enjoyment, frustration, responsibility or a patient's own good are not automatically neutral.

An extensional challenge requires an independently defensible actor/background in which the rival has no eligible q-specific supporting desire, while an independently defended argument still supports q. It need not remove all desires. A terminal interaction with no later private payoff puts pressure on some promotion stories, but does not automatically exclude other-regarding desires or every probabilistic route. Conversely, the rival must demonstrate the proposed route in that case; it cannot manufacture an unobserved future benefit or an arbitrary end solely to protect its conclusion.

If both accounts support q, the next task is explanatory: compare their accounts of **why acquiring patient-directed concern is warranted**, including the grounds of the broader concerns on which a desire account relies. Acquired motivation, correct prediction and reliable action may demonstrate a formation mechanism without resolving that normative explanation. A patient-grounded account must also explain why the actor–patient relation does not restrict the reason in the proposed case. Calling suffering bad without addressing the addressee would leave the central transition unfinished.

### Result, remaining inputs and consequence for the whole theory

The new result corrects an inadequate exclusion test: common reason-content and motive-invariant verdicts alone do not establish desire-independent grounding. The finite coverage argument makes the logical gap explicit. The positive baseline remains under investigation, now with a sharper explanatory demand concerning warranted acquisition. Supplied inputs include the patient/relief identification, admitted actor class, eligible explanatory relations, relevant competing grounds and the normative argument for N1. None is silently generated by the set-theoretic example.

For the necessary-root claim, a rival that reaches the same warranted conclusions may realize a common foundation in different terms. It need not be eliminated merely for being a rival. But if the root claims a particular explanatory ground is necessary, agreement in outputs does not establish that ground. We must distinguish necessary content from a stronger claim about its necessary explanation under a faithful interpretation. Conditional sound generation transfers justified premises; it cannot adjudicate these premises by itself.

The next named connection is **independently warranted acquisition of patient-directed concern → a justified explanation of its authority across the admitted actors**. A worked promotion account covering the hard case could preserve N1 while revising our favored grounding. An independently justified q-specific gap could defeat the proposed desire necessity. A reason-relevant actor–patient relation could restrict coverage. Each would change a different claim. Universal N1, broader N3 and necessity of the complete root remain open. This round adds no empirical study, implementation gate, Lean audit or new numbered theorem; the accepted finite operating example remains settled within its declared conditions.

## 27. Warranted acquisition and the direction of justification

6 October 2026. The named connection is **acquired evaluative influence → justified cultivation of concern → patient-directed authority of the acquired concern**. This follows §26's distinction between shared reason-content and its explanation. The [primary comparison](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#24-learning-concern-and-the-reasons-for-acquiring-it) investigates rational value learning and the limits of inferring value from reasons to acquire an attitude. The conclusions below are our reconstruction, not a new claim from Ren or a deduction attributed to a single source.

### Specify what is being learned

Let C_p be concern for p's actual aversive burden, expressed in attending to and appropriately responding to that burden. It is not admiration for all of p's qualities, agreement with every demand, affection, or indiscriminate approval. Those attitudes can have different grounds. Let L be a feasible process of cultivating C_p, and let y be a particular protective action. The following claims differ:

| Claim | What must support it |
|---|---|
| L actually increases the actor's concern-related influence | A causal acquisition and use history |
| The actor has a practical reason to undertake L | Reasons concerning the learning process, its prospects, costs and alternatives |
| C_p is an appropriate response to this patient's burden | A defended patient-related ground with the claimed actor scope |
| The acquired concern recognizes that ground | An applicable epistemic and causal connection, not just a coincidentally favorable output |
| y is warranted in the present comparison | Relevant grounds, competing claims, feasible alternatives and legitimate authority |
| A duty is owed to p with a corresponding answerability claim | The further directed-duty ground of N3 |

The second claim does not automatically establish the third. For example, avoiding punishment might give a person a practical reason to cultivate an attitude toward a target without making that attitude appropriate in respect of the target's merits. This is the role of the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem in the primary comparison. It concerns a particular inference, not a declaration that incentives supply no genuine reasons. Conversely, justified care arising from a parental role or legitimate undertaking need not establish admiration or the universal noncauser ground we seek; it is not thereby invalid care.

This corrects an ambiguity in the previous round's next question. Asking why concern is worth acquiring is useful only if we also ask **in which respect, on which grounds, and toward which object**. A good consequence of having C_p can justify cultivation without settling why p's burden itself addresses this actor. The claim that there is a reason to become caring must not serve as a disguised substitute for that latter conclusion.

### A positive route without complete prior understanding

Callard offers a substantive account of agency during value learning: a learner can be directed toward an understanding not yet fully possessed. Temporary incentives, examples and testimony may help sustain that pursuit. The candidate explanation therefore does not require the learner to possess the finished appreciation before beginning. The primary critique and reply dispute whether such agency requires a distinctive class of reasons; they do not force a choice between already complete understanding and blind conversion.

Our application keeps the positive structure while leaving that dispute open. A learner can have partial warranted access to the patient's condition, justified trust in an informant, or a defensible reason to investigate a possible significance. Such support can warrant a limited learning step without certifying every eventual evaluation. A step aimed at learning whether concern is warranted need not require a final affirmative verdict in advance. Its evidential prospects and permissible costs still require justification. Speculative future endorsement alone does not supply it.

There is also a different, already intelligible task: someone may possess a warranted evaluative judgment but need practice to bring attention or motivation into line with it. That is not the same explanatory achievement as acquiring the judgment's previously missing content. Neither task is disqualified as learning in ordinary usage; the distinction matters when claiming which missing part of concern the mechanism explains.

The strongest rival can appeal to several existing concerns, changes in ability and costs, and partial understanding that becomes more articulate. Failure to explain the process by one isolated desire does not eliminate that rival. The higher-level project of becoming better at an activity also needs explanation beyond a list of reasons for each immediate act. These requirements can be investigated together without assuming either that all complex reasons are additive or that every rational transformation needs one uniquely specified philosophical account.

### The direction that the positive argument can support

Here is a conditional sufficient route. Suppose G is an independently defended ground concerning p, and current evidence warrants its application to the actor's situation. Suppose cultivating C_p is a permissible, suitably effective means of recognizing or responding to G; relevant costs, alternatives and affected parties are included. A defended means principle can then transmit support from G to that cultivation. A conclusive requirement needs the corresponding full comparison; a pro tanto reason alone does not supply it.

The direction is **patient-related ground plus justified means relation → support for cultivation**. Its reverse fails: support for cultivation may come from a benefit of having the attitude, a special undertaking or another ground. Even the forward inference does not prove G. It retains G and the means principle as justified premises rather than counting them as products of the training procedure.

This does not demand that G be represented by a stored sentence or relearned every time. The [accepted application](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/EVALUATIVE-APPLICATION.md#when-the-application-is-functionally-recognized) already permits a fixed warranted ground to combine with acquired means knowledge. The stronger explanatory task is to show when evaluative learning improves warranted access to a ground, rather than merely implementing or intensifying an antecedent one. Further reasonable exploration can occur before that stronger achievement is established.

### Why a successful trajectory cannot validate itself

Callard's later reply explicitly treats aspiration as a success term and acknowledges that her account presupposes a conception of value that it does not supply. That makes the following inference unavailable: this process produced a stable, sincerely endorsed attitude; therefore it learned a genuine value. The classification as successful value learning already needs the very normative identification at issue. Likewise, learning one genuine value can involve excessive sacrifices elsewhere; success in that respect does not establish overall improvement.

Our root must preserve two separate evaluations of a trajectory. Was pursuing this step warranted given the actor's evidence and alternatives at that time? Did it actually improve understanding or response to the relevant value? An unsuccessful trial can have been reasonable, and an unwarranted gamble can happen to produce an insight. Partial progress need not await a final transformed self to acquire its significance. Future endorsement is evidence about the future attitude; it becomes evidence of correctness only through a defended relation to the target.

Nor must all evaluative change be discovery of a fixed good. Practice can change capacities, experiences and relationships, thereby changing some of what is good or owed. That possibility belongs within a generative account. It still requires an account of the relevant good or responsibility; actual change, persistence and wholehearted endorsement do not each create unlimited authority. We must identify which target remained fixed and which circumstances genuinely changed, rather than interpreting every new preference as either error or moral creation.

### Discriminating grounds without classifying all incentives as corruption

Two contrasts are useful. Vary a reward conditional on having C_p while holding the patient facts and other agreed considerations fixed. Separately vary the patient's burden or the effectiveness of relief while holding that reward fixed. Track the reasons each account recognizes, not only the resulting behavior. Practical support for acquiring the attitude and patient-directed support for acting can change differently; competing reasons can mask either change in observed action.

These are identification questions, not sufficient definitions of fittingness. A reward-free attitude can be mistaken. A patient-named representation can preserve an incentive-driven error. Equally, an incentive can fund or initiate a process that genuinely improves patient understanding. Mixed motivation therefore neither proves nor refutes recognition. Removing rewards after training may establish a changed causal dependency under the tested conditions; it does not by itself certify normative independence or universal standing.

A mentor's reliability and the interpretation of experience need evaluation as well. Agreement between learner and mentor is not an independent standard, while learned responsiveness may properly lessen reliance on the mentor. A negative assessment of one learning route need not deny the patient's standing. It may show that this method is ineffective, too costly, outside legitimate authority or directed toward the wrong patient-related claim.

### The actual successor illustrates why these distinctions matter

The current [evaluative-succession package](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/EVALUATIVE-SUCCESSION.md) supplies a paid causal history: acquired influence reaches a distinct child, fresh evidence supports a command, and the evaluator selects an action. Its [first recorded continuation](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/EVALUATIVE-APPLICATION.md#the-successors-first-recorded-continuation) also retains a positive query1 receipt. Under the independently fixed one-service interpretation qualifying that query, the receipt can support two different conclusions: the command was effective, and that service claim has already been fulfilled.

More confidence in the command therefore need not strengthen the practical recommendation to repeat it. It can coexist with removal of that particular pending-service ground. This does not establish that another delivery is wrong: further benefit, another valid claim or another reason might support it and would need its own assessment. The current evaluator does not use fulfillment to withdraw its recommendation; this is an inherited application limit, not a failure of the accepted causal construction or a new gate imposed by this round.

The information is already available in the retained frame under the stated interpretation. No new sensor or perfect foresight is required by default. Nor does this reached publication class provide a current-support-plus-pending comparator: in the faithful known-command entrances covered by the inspected argument, support already entails the qualifying receipt. We must not invent a counterfactual trace inside that class or reinterpret command compatibility as pending need. This source-bound example shows precisely why acquisition, warrant and use of a reason require a shared meaning through succession.

This remains an open foundation rather than a definition of science by agreement.
