## Added transitions and physical backing

1. **Assemble:** convert the spare replacement pair into a finite-state contact and rewrite adapter. Parent material decreases by two and bound adapter material increases by two. The spare is unavailable for later sign replacement.
2. **Inspect:** contact the retained sockets and write “at least one occupied” and “at least two occupied” into the two cleared latches. This reads occupancy, irrespective of sign. A missing channel yields unavailable evidence; a detectable wrong binding yields stale evidence. A zeroed channel yields an actual observed-zero state, not missing data.
3. **Commit:** when the acquired current reading has both bits, change the operative continuation parameter from six encounters to four. The counter stays four. The operation preserves native value, signs, affected stock, remaining source packets and the full earlier history. It clears the reused latches and leaves the adapter bound.

The rule does not compare the acquired report against hidden true occupancy. A coherent false positive can trigger it incorrectly. Any inherited event following a reading invalidates that reading before execution, even a scope event leaving scope unchanged. This is an explicitly supplied lifetime discipline, not automatic detection of arbitrary aging. Reacquisition is a new one-unit operation.

Assembly, reading and commit require the inherited current supported command, active ready core, completed count-four phase, intact scope and service permission; no sign replacement may already have occurred. The permission bit remains a technical service interlock. An independently defended mandate to install and use the revision rule is a separate application premise. Neither the occupied-socket threshold nor its authority is learned by this extension.

Their original applicability and response tests remain operative. Positive links can select effective service; mixed, negative, inert or gated links can still lead to abstention. Revising inquiry does not silently force a benevolent response.
