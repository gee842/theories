## 2. Second-order syntax and the semantic range

In [SecondOrder.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/constitution/higher-order/formal/SecondOrder.lean), a relation of arity `k` is a predicate on tuples `Fin k → A`. A finite list of arities records relation-variable types, while a separate finite individual context records available element variables.

The inspected grammar contains individual equality, relation application to a tuple of individual variables, equality between relation variables of the same arity, negation, conjunction, individual existential quantification and relation existential quantification. Both universal binders have checked derived semantics. `full_sentence_reconstruction` supplies the closed-formula case. Assertions of full formula preservation refer to this grammar. Arbitrary primitive function symbols, their terms and an expanded named signature are not silently included.

The key correspondence is extensional. `related_relation_eq` obtains actual equality of the represented predicates and ideals using the inverse completion maps together with propositional and function extensionality. It does not merely declare membership agreement to count as equality while retaining an incompatible intensional identity. This choice matches the predicate and ideal representations being compared; it is not a result about every possible interpretation of property identity.

Both existential-relation directions expose the essential coverage step. An arbitrary predicate witness is sent to its finite-parts ideal. An arbitrary ideal witness is decoded to its union predicate. Individual binders change the individual environment; relation binders change the relation environment at their declared arity. The inspected indexing preserves this distinction through mixed binders.

The general transport statement matches an explicit predicate-domain condition with an explicit ideal-domain condition. Its full specialization uses every predicate and every ideal. It does not first restrict the domain to computable, named, principal or otherwise finitely described ideals. The theorem also permits arbitrary matching free environments; if an application requires free assignments to belong to selected domains, that is a separate well-formed-model condition rather than an unstated premise of this stronger satisfaction correspondence. `selected_encode_truth` gives the matching selected-range case; `corresponding_fullness` shows that ranges satisfying the stated correspondence are full together. This last theorem is about the ranges themselves, not a claim that every proper restriction must change some sentence of every language.

Arbitrary selected domains should be called **selected semantics**, not automatically Henkin models. The [primary sources](ROOT-MATHEMATICAL-HIGHER-ORDER-SOURCES.md) require the additional interpretation and closure conditions appropriate to their languages. The full correspondence neither supplies nor needs a completeness proof for a selected-domain deductive calculus.

## 3. Finite simple types, full arrows and iterated predicate completion

In [TypedTruth.lean](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/constitution/higher-order/formal/TypedTruth.lean), the type constructors are `base`, `arr` and `pred`. The completed interpretation keeps full arrow domains but represents a predicate type by all finite-subset ideals over the recursively interpreted lower type.

`BiMap` supplies forward and backward base maps with both inverse laws. It does not assume formula-truth preservation. Arrow transport conjugates a function by the appropriate lower-type maps; predicate transport combines those maps with finite-parts encoding and union decoding. The recursive `typeMap` therefore provides both coverage directions at every represented type.

The term grammar contains variables, function application and lambda abstraction. The application case uses the inverse law at the argument type; the lambda case transports an arbitrary argument backward and proves that the extended environments correspond. Formula equality is transported through the induced injection, and predicate application uses the membership correspondence. Typed existential quantification uses both directions of the type map, rather than assuming an embedding reaches every quantified value.

The inspected grammar is a finite simple-type language with equality, predicate membership, negation, conjunction and typed existential quantification, with checked derived universal semantics. It has no object-language truth-value type, no dependent types, no universe variable and no unrestricted internal truth predicate. Nor does the inspected term grammar include a constructor turning any formula directly into a predicate term. These limitations do not invalidate the transport theorem. They fix what “all formulas” means and prevent a claim to have formalized every presentation of higher-order logic.

There is nevertheless a checked **semantic comprehension result**: `formula_comprehension` gives an ideal representing the extension of any formula in an additional variable, at any of the declared types, including formulas that quantify over higher types. It uses the full predicate interpretation explicitly. The distinction between this semantic existence statement and a new predicate-term constructor must remain visible. The concrete `higher_eta_completed` example additionally checks a closed formula involving lambda abstraction, application and quantification over functions from base predicates to predicates of those predicates.

The identity base map gives a concrete same-carrier reconstruction: predicate types at each finite level become completion representations while the resulting formula truths are retained. This is stronger than renaming an arbitrary supplied higher-type carrier. The completion maps are defined from finite parts and unions, and the arrows interact correctly with those changed representations. Fullness still enters through the complete function and predicate domains on which those maps act.

## 4. Three distinctions that the interpretation must preserve

**Full representation and finite naming.** An ideal of finite subsets need not itself have a finite description or be generated by a finite algorithm. The completion theorem covers all ambient predicates because it admits all the corresponding ideals. Calling its pieces finite does not reduce that domain to finite or effectively enumerable data. The source representations do not make that claim.

**Truth transport and truth decision.** The semantic interpretations return propositions and quantify over the stated full domains. A proof that two interpretations agree is not a Boolean procedure deciding every formula. The first-order equality and dense-order algorithms retain their earlier scopes; this extension does not extend their finite witness lists to arbitrary higher-order objects.

**Conditional constitution and ultimate admission.** The reconstruction identifies which complete relational content the ideal presentation retains and how its higher-order interpretation proceeds. It can support the theory’s constitutive reading of that mathematical role. It does not show that the present root independently necessitates every full domain, every universe or the uniquely correct direction of ultimate explanation. That stronger question remains the [earlier completion frontier](ROOT-MATHEMATICAL-COMPLETION-INTEGRATION.md).

These are analytical distinctions within the same generative inquiry. The metatheory’s availability is not evidence that logic lives in a second realm. Likewise, the actual formation of proofs is not by itself the warrant for their mathematical truth. The review assesses the represented relationships and their scope, rather than positing detached subjects or freely varying a grounding predicate.

## 5. Substantive counterreview and acceptance condition

The main mathematical threat is a false preservation claim caused by a changed quantified domain. If only selected ideals were retained while the predicate semantics remained full, the backward witness case would lack its required correspondence. If a mere injection replaced the base bijection while full higher types were still quantified over, the missing surjectivity would have to be addressed. The inspected statements instead expose the required maps and range agreement.

The final second-order source also provides an actual checked domain-change example. On the same natural-number carrier, `full_totalUnary` accepts the closed sentence “there exists a unary predicate holding of every individual,” while `finite_rejects_totalUnary` rejects it when predicates are restricted to finite extensions. `finite_not_full` separately records that restriction’s failure of fullness. This compares genuinely different quantifier ranges; it does not toggle a truth label with the interpreted question held fixed. The finite-only range is expressly not asserted to satisfy comprehension or to be a Henkin model. The example therefore supports the stated range warning without refuting full semantics, Henkin semantics or the generative root.

A structural account can accept every recovered truth and every concrete completion map while leaving the admission principles primitive or explaining them through a different articulation of the same whole. That remains relevant to ultimate grounding, but it does not obstruct this round’s conditional result. The current target does not require winning that larger comparison. Accepting the conditional reconstruction therefore does not weaken the user’s earlier universal goal or falsely mark it complete.
