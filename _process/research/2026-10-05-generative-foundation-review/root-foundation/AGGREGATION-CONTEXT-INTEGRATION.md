## 2. Affirmative argument and selected correction

Changing what can actually be protected can change reasons for action. A purely internal numerical threshold is not itself that justification. A rule must explain the relevance of actual provision, differentiated loss, feasible response, established chances and history. The whole is not a second claimant absorbing every gain; it is the history whose relationships determine what these descriptions preserve.

The checked filter drops every lower-tier contribution when either option can protect a higher-tier case. In a fixed population of seven lower-tier, two middle-tier and one higher-tier claims, improving a route from seven lower protections to those same seven plus the higher protection turns its diagnostic victory into defeat. The other route, protecting two middle-tier cases, is unchanged. Independent permissions and unchanged burdens are stipulated for the philosophical application; the formal module does not infer them.

This supports a definite rejection of the filter as an unexplained implementation of the existing stable comparison route. It does not establish that every context-dependent choice is irrational. A defender can reject the fixed-ranking interpretation, but must explain why this particular increase justifies deleting the old grounds. Nor do the control coefficients establish a correct replacement utility scale. They isolate the filtering effect.

The positive theoretical correction is that **not being strong enough to counterbalance another claim does not establish absence of a reason**. Existing residual-ground work already preserves unmet needs after decisions; this continuation applies that distinction to the internal construction of the decision rule. Keeping those grounds matters when new support later makes further response possible. It also prevents turning pro tanto LC/BC2 comparisons into decisive obligations merely through a strict arithmetic inequality.

These definitions contain neither G0 nor a duty predicate.

| `strongest_cutoff_reverses` | The specified filter favors the original route over its alternative, then that alternative over the improved route |
| `unfiltered_control` | Without filtering, the positive fixed score places the alternative below the original and the original below the improved route |

## 5. Substantive author counterreview

**“Context dependence is not irrational.”** Agreed. The theorem targets an explicitly fixed strict relation and states that premise. A genuinely changed opportunity, duty or fair-chance arrangement can change the grounds. The normative criticism is narrower: the cutoff alone does not explain why retaining old protection and adding more makes the old reasons disappear. Merely indexing each option by a new name would not provide that explanation.

**“The example assumes a complete score after criticizing complete scores.”** The score is the candidate being tested and a control that isolates filtering. The study does not adopt either as warranted ethics. Its positive reason-retention claim is defended in prose from actual living grounds, with numeric interchangeability and tier warrant explicitly left outside the proof.

**“Equal additions may not be morally irrelevant.”** Correct. Adding distinct participants can alter dependence, prior distribution or fair chances even if their counts match. The common-addition premise must therefore be separately warranted, not presumed from copied profile values. The obstruction identifies this possible point of revision. It does not force the full theory to adopt cancellation by definition.

**“Independent local improvements should compose.”** This is a serious challenge to unrestricted contextualism, not something dismissed by saying everything is related. If actual joint implementation preserves the grounds of both comparisons and introduces no countervailing relation, refusing composition needs explanation. The theorem shows the commitments at stake; it does not prove that the required independence obtains in all shared living.

**“Rejecting any premise avoids the obstruction too easily.”** Logical escape is not sufficient. A successful revision must identify the premise rejected, defend the ethical reason, and state how cases without that reason are handled. The current contribution does not claim to have completed such a universal alternative. Its definite correction concerns the unsupported deletion rule, and its next study must address substantive choices rather than another arbitrary threshold.

**“This has refuted J2 or BC1.”** It has not. All three tiers in the diagnostic can be supplied as basic; J2's basic-versus-optional order does not select its coefficients or crossing judgments. BC1's equivalent-rescue conditions do not supply the stipulated three-way strict cycle. BC2 and LC are pro tanto, so applying the strict-choice obstruction directly to them would change the target. Their preserved grounds are precisely why the distinction matters.

**“The absence of a deterministic verdict makes the study empty.”** The study rules out a concrete unjustified implementation, preserves a specified reason and makes the remaining normative decision explicit. It does not claim this is a complete allocation theory. The next central burden is to explain joint choice with chances and crossed severity, including justified composition limits.

## 6. Nondual scope, integration and continuation

Descriptions of groups and joined choices are abstractions of differentiated living within shared histories. An apparent algebraic decomposition is not proof that these lives are independently constituted or that their losses are fungible. Conversely, a universal appeal to interconnection does not defeat a rival whose relevant joint implementation and comparisons are independently specified. This study preserves both constraints.
