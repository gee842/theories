## 1. Relation to earlier results

The [previous interface boundary](CORRECTION-FRONTIER-BOUNDARY.md) was stated for an observation map and an effective command set. It already permitted either a single or a collective source of observations. Ren's proposal asks whether plural systems can change those conditions. The constructive examples answer yes, under explicit relationships. This is a scope extension, not a refutation of the earlier theorem and not a dismissal of the proposal because an old no-go result exists.

The [system-individuation account](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/SYSTEM-INDIVIDUATION.md) already distinguishes causal participation from predictive sufficiency and retains nonunique sufficient regions. [SystemsOrganization](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/systems-complexity/SystemsOrganization.lean) already proves exact projected dynamics, finite-history preservation and composition. This round does not reprove those results as new. The new examples test a particular parent summary under correction interventions and a particular composition of command interfaces. [Joint formation](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/JOINT-FORMATION.md) retains prior credit for separate versus joint feasibility.

## 2. New formal results

| Module and declaration family | Checked scope |
|---|---|
| `CorrectionPopulation`: joint observation, each local observation insufficient | The target polarity is the XOR of two conditions. Each local observation leaves a conflicting continuation; both observations determine a successful command. |
| `arbitrarily_many_duplicate_observations_fail` | Every finite number of duplicate left-only observations still leaves the right condition unresolved. No statistical independence premise is involved. |
| `pilot_transfers`, `target_drift_defeats_pilot` | A shared decoder relation permits transferring a pilot result to the target; changing that relation can defeat transfer. The relation's real warrant is supplied. |
| Independent/shared/mixture error theorems | The product-weight enumeration gives `3p²−2p³`, with strict improvement for `0<p<1/2`. Identical errors retain rate `p`. A common-event mixture has floor `c`; the count-generic floor is conditional on that mixture form, not a general theorem about arbitrary dependence. |
| Contained and uncontained trial theorems | Disabling pilot-local activity preserves the main site; disabling their shared power does not. The specified operations supply their containment properties. |
| `CorrectionParent`: status summary and local intervention | “Both stopped” suffices for the stipulated stop-both action but not for stopping just the left child. Sibling status supplies the missing information. |
| Individual, broadcast and vector correction | Both decoders are individually correctable; a single broadcast command fails for opposite polarities; separate commands work. |
| `joint_correction_from_feasible_commands` | An explicit joint availability witness and an actual child-to-parent outcome bridge imply parent correction. This exposes assumptions; it does not derive availability or justify the bridge from local success. |
| Parent support and delivery | Correct child translations still require common power; restoring it restores delivery in the specified law. |
| Singleton trials and capacity | Capacity-one and capacity-two installations agree on both singleton trials; simultaneous demand distinguishes them. Two units of capacity suffice for every binary demand combination. |

The probability package also checks nonnegative atom weights on the unit interval and normalization of the binary, independent-product and common-event joint laws. The common-event mixture's `p` is the conditional individual error in the no-common-event branch. At `p=c=1/10`, its majority error is `313/2500`, not the independent model's `7/250`. These are different joint laws, not an experiment estimating dependence from real AI samples. The count-generic result only proves a lower bound from nonnegative residual error; no asymptotic convergence or arbitrary-size majority theorem is claimed.

## 5. Substantive author counterreview

**Objection: the joint-observation example only combines bits, so it proves nothing about many actual systems.** Correct as a deployment objection. It proves a constructive possibility and identifies what must transfer to a real application: complementary evidence and usable communication. It refutes neither the earlier theorem nor the need to warrant those relations.

**Objection: the independent-error improvement was put into the probability model.** Independence is indeed supplied. The gain is derived from enumerated outcomes; the comparison with identical-error and common-event laws shows why the independence premise matters. The result is standard reliability mathematics, not a new statistical discovery.

**Objection: calling a pair a parent explains no organization.** Correct. The chapter therefore distinguishes mathematical aggregation from actual joint organization and reuses the existing account of operative participation. The command and capacity examples show particular relationships that a larger description retains. They do not discover a unique universal boundary.

**Objection: the capacity counterexample just forbids the needed joint trial.** No trial is formally forbidden. The result establishes that singleton observations do not distinguish the two capacities. A joint trial or reliable resource measurement would distinguish them. Whether the joint trial's consequences can be contained is a further practical question; it is not silently assumed impossible.

**Objection: one global system means only one sample, so local inference is futile.** That conclusion conflates whole-level histories with samples relevant to a particular invariant or error process. The constructive cases are within one jointly specified process. They show that shared membership permits informative variation. They do not establish an independent sample of civilization-scale outcomes.

**Objection: a successful parent correction can sacrifice children.** Parent success must retain the differentiated outcomes relevant to the claim; a status summary or support relation supplies no ethical priority. The practical account remains responsible for genuine losses, authority and legitimate endings. The formal claims here concern specified Boolean delivery and availability, not ethical maximization.

## 6. Present wall and completion scope

This continuation completes a substantive conditional answer to both questions: how plurality can enable learning/correction, and how group and subsystem descriptions can relate without erasing dependencies. It closes no claim of universal system individuation, real AI independence or full deployment corrigibility.

The wall is the **actual joint variation and transfer range**. A shared parent can support useful local trials while retaining untested common effects. To advance a stronger real-world claim now requires an identified mechanism and evidence capable of discriminating the relevant joint alternatives. Repeating the same successful local probes or choosing a broader system label cannot supply that evidence. A justified general law could supply it; the present study does not prove that no such law exists.

The results, counterexamples and existing systems work are preserved as reusable outcomes. There is no new novelty claim, independent acceptance, external submission or universal prohibition on experimenting within the global whole.
