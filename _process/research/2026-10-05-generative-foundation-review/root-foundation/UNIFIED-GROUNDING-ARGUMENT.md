# Unified grounding: the patient supplies the end, the actor supplies a way

6 October 2026. AI-authored research continuation authorized by Ren's request to continue pursuing unified grounding. This is the mechanism and positive-argument contribution to the named connection **experienced badness → nonexclusive practical significance → a reason for an unrelated capable actor**. It builds on [the naturalistic patient-ground study](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#19-naturalistic-patient-good-without-an-imported-moral-verdict), [the language-relative limit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#28-suffering-and-the-scope-of-the-missing-normative-bridge), and [the latest phenomenal-identity study](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#32-phenomenal-identity-practical-scope-and-the-necessary-root). It does not reopen the [accepted conditional foundation](FOUNDATION-ROOT-ACCEPTANCE.md).

**Result:** a patient-ground can be independent of an actor's wishes while the action reason remains relational: the patient's condition supplies what merits response, and the actor's eligible control supplies who can respond and how. That structure preserves differentiated centers without requiring a free-standing value object or a single collective subject. Applying the existing transport lemma identifies exactly what would make this explanation universal. The surviving challenge concerns whether eligible control activates an already justified practical ground, or whether a further historical relationship is always necessary. Phenomenal identity alone does not decide this.

The contribution is an argument and an explicit discrimination test. No empirical study, software experiment, Lean verification, or metaphysical proof is claimed.

## 1. Begin with a single patient and an actual difference

Fix a patient p and an episode e. In continuation h, p undergoes a severe unwanted experience; in continuation r, that particular burden is relieved. Hold fixed relevant permissions, costs, effects on other centers, and the patient's other interests. The possibility of holding these fixed specifies the narrow target; it is not a claim that practical cases usually have no conflicts.

Write Q(p,e) for an independently identified experiential condition. Neither a negative control signal nor avoidance behavior suffices to identify Q. An account may identify the condition with a phenomenal quality or with a constitutive relation involving sensation and aversion. The issue here does not require deciding between those accounts. It does require keeping all the alleged constituents fixed during a comparison.

Write G(p,e;r,h) for the stronger claim that the actual difference supplies a justified ground favoring relief for p. The phrase “for p” fixes the beneficiary. It does not yet restrict the acting subject to p; nor does it establish that any other actor has a reason. Establishing Q → G is a substantive identification and justification task.

Now introduce an actor a with a permissible intervention y that produces r instead of h. The actor did not cause the burden, make an undertaking, depend on the patient, or stand to receive a benefit. Yet a and p are not unrelated in every respect: a now has an identified capacity to alter p's condition. **The exact dispute is whether this present practical relation suffices, rather than whether a reason could exist in the absence of every relation.**

The positive candidate says that G supplies the end's significance, while this practical relation supplies the address and available means. It does not say that causal capacity by itself creates goodness. The two premises perform different explanatory work.

## 2. Separate the source, scope and strength of a reason

A proposed sufficient ground should answer three questions.

1. **Source:** What fact makes the response worth considering? In this case, relief changes an adverse episode for its actual subject.
2. **Scope:** Which agents have some reason in virtue of that fact? The proposed baseline covers agents with an eligible opportunity for relief.
3. **Strength and resolution:** How much does that reason count alongside other reasons, rights and responsibilities? The present proposal does not give a complete answer.

An actor's relationship to a patient can matter differently at these levels. Causing a burden may add repair responsibility. A promise may create a directed claim. A caregiver role may increase the force of a response or determine who should perform it. These possibilities do not, without a further argument, make such relationships necessary for every reason to help. Conversely, showing that a relationship affects weight does not establish a baseline in its absence.

This distinction also constrains a proposed universal principle. A patient's welfare difference is not a warrant for any actor to impose any intervention. Relevant refusal, authority, means, competing burdens and uncertainty remain part of eligibility or all-things assessment. Nor does identifying a sufficient relief-ground reduce every valuable project to pain relief. The target is one defensible source of reasons, not an exhaustive value theory.

## 3. The strongest positive route is a factorization of practical explanation

The patient-ground proposal is more informative than a declaration that all beings are connected. Its explanation factors into two parts:

\[
\underbrace{G(p,e;r,h)}_{\text{what makes relief matter}}
\quad+\quad
\underbrace{K(a,y,p,e;r,h,c)}_{\text{who can permissibly make this difference}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R(a,p,y,c;e).
\]

Here K includes the capacities, available means and application conditions relevant to the narrowly specified comparison. R is a **pro tanto reason** for a concerning y, grounded in this patient's episode. It is not a command, an all-things duty, blameworthiness, or an enforceable claim against a.

This is a candidate grounding structure, not a deduction from the syntax of the display. Its substantive proposal is that the identity of the actor contributes through K, and is not an additional switch that can erase the patient-ground despite the same eligible opportunity. Proving that proposal would explain actor scope. Putting it into the definition of G would merely relocate the burden.

The explanation has three positive attractions that merit testing.

**It locates the success condition outside the helper's response.** Silencing the helper's distress does not relieve p. Inaccurate empathy can therefore be corrected while the ground of concern remains. This is one reason to identify the source through the patient's condition rather than the actor's reinforcement.

**It makes practical discovery possible.** Learning that an unfamiliar intervention really relieves p can change the appropriate response without creating a new patient good. Likewise, discovering that someone previously regarded as insentient actually suffers could reveal an application, rather than make concern correct only after the observer approves. These are proposed interpretations requiring evidence, not measurements of value inferred from reported approval.

**It explains variation without fragmenting the ground.** Different patients may require opposite actions; different actors may have different access. A common ground can produce differentiated reasons through those facts. Universality does not require merged subjects, identical means, equal burdens, or one aggregate scale.

These attractions establish explanatory work the proposal can do. Some rivals can preserve them by granting only restricted other-directed reasons. The attractions therefore support investigating the factorization; they do not by themselves prove its complete actor scope.

## 4. Apply the existing transport lemma to actor substitution

The [existing exact entailment theorem and cut condition](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#2-exact-entailment-from-anchors-and-warranted-transports) already characterize transport from positive anchors. Its [ground-preserving application](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#4-the-strongest-positive-route-preserve-the-ground-that-justified-the-anchor) explicitly addresses actor scope. The following is an application and restatement of that inherited result, not a new theorem. It requires neither a numerical measure of value nor a complete ethical ordering.

Let X contain practical contexts with fixed meanings for patient, episode, action and actor. In each actor-substitution application, keep the very same p and e throughout the path; the general theorem also permits other context changes only when their separate warrants are stated. Let T be the target contexts with a warranted adverse episode and an eligible relief opportunity. Let R(x) mean that the specified patient-related reason exists in context x. Let A ⊆ T be independently justified anchor contexts. An anchor might concern self-relief, but its warrant must be stated; calling it an anchor does not establish it.

Let E be a relation between contexts. Each edge xEy represents a specified change in actor, means, or circumstance that preserves the allegedly sufficient ground and relevant application conditions. Mere factual similarity does not warrant an edge's normative use.

Assume:

- **Anchor warrant:** R(x) for every x in A.
- **Ground-preserving transmission:** if xEy and R(x), then R(y).
- **Coverage:** every t in T is reachable from some member of A by a finite sequence of E-edges.

**Lemma.** Every target t in T has R(t).

**Proof.** Choose an anchor-to-target path. Anchor warrant gives the initial reason. Ground-preserving transmission preserves it at each edge, by finite induction. Thus the endpoint has the reason. No equality of reason-strength, complete ranking, or common numerical scale is used.

The useful part is the converse diagnostic. In the weakest interpretation satisfying just these constraints, R contains exactly the contexts reachable from A. If some target is unreachable, assigning R only to that reachable set satisfies anchor warrant and transmission while omitting that target. Therefore these premises alone do not establish universal coverage. This counterinterpretation concerns the stated constraints, not a possible world with the correct moral facts changed at will.

Universal actor coverage and universal patient coverage are also different. A patient who never deliberates or acts may have no self-action anchor. A separately justified patient-ground or another warranted anchor, possibly concerning a legitimate caregiver, is needed; actor substitution alone cannot create it. This inherits the [existing two-transport distinction](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#3-the-noncommunicating-patient-exposes-two-distinct-transport-tasks).

The lemma makes the disputed research claim inspectable. A self-relief anchor together with label invariance is insufficient: renaming preserves the actor–patient identity relation, so it never reaches a nonself target. Adding justified promises or responsibility edges may reach more targets while still leaving the stranger case uncovered. A genuine operator-substitution edge would cross that boundary, but its preservation of practical force is precisely what needs justification.

Thus a long chain of individually innocent-looking changes cannot conceal the bridge. At least one edge must establish that an eligible change of acting center does not eliminate the particular reason. Physical continuity of the experiment is not enough to establish normative continuity. The current contribution integrates this inherited requirement with the distinction between fitting concern and practical-end adoption below, and with the concrete naturalistic proposal in §7.

## 5. Why a local experiential ground is not yet an actor-independent reason

Consider two candidate accounts:

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{open}}(a,p,y,c;e)
&\Longleftrightarrow G(p,e;r,h)\land K(a,y,p,e;r,h,c),\\
R_{\mathrm{gate}}(a,p,y,c;e)
&\Longleftrightarrow G(p,e;r,h)\land K(a,y,p,e;r,h,c)\land J(a,p,c).
\end{aligned}
\]

J might require selfhood, responsibility, or a legitimately entrusted relation. Both accounts can preserve the same patient, experiential constitution, causal relief and knowledge. Both can grant that the experience is bad for the patient. Neither must claim the helper's present wishes manufacture that badness.

Consequently, **showing that J is not a constituent of experienced badness does not show that J cannot be a constituent of practical address.** This is a real limitation of a simple locality argument. Our favored account must establish why the eligible relation in K is sufficient; the rival must defend why J is a necessary gate rather than an additional ground of special responsibility.

The burden is not settled by counting predicates. R_open is syntactically simpler in this notation, but syntactic simplicity is representation-dependent and an extra relation might capture a real normative distinction. Equally, the gate is not independently warranted merely because it makes the agent-relative rival formally consistent. Both candidates need substantive explanatory and evaluative support.

Self-regarding examples leave these candidates underidentified. On a test set containing only a = p, any J that is true of selfhood makes them agree. Adding role cases on which J is also true does not fix that problem. The meaningful discrimination must include an eligible nonself, nonresponsible, nonentrusted actor, with actual consent and other relevant conditions preserved. This is a limitation of the argument's evidential coverage, not a claim that normative truth can be learned from approval frequencies alone.

## 6. Fitting concern supplies a useful intermediate target, with a further bridge

One possible defense of the missing edge proceeds through response fittingness:

\[
G(p,e;r,h) \Longrightarrow F(a,p,e;r,h),
\]

where F says that patient-directed concern is a fitting response for an appropriately situated actor. If this is justified independently, recognition can make concern appropriate even without a prior helping desire. That is already substantive actor scope; it must not be introduced as a neutral description of Q.

But the next step needs precision. Compassion, sadness, wishing that a burden would end, adopting its relief as an end, and having a reason to carry out an intervention are different claims. A rival can accept fitting sadness and still restrict action reasons to J. Instrumental rationality alone does not require adopting every end that one can fittingly wish to occur.

A defensible **minimal effectuation principle** would say: where an object-directed consideration gives the actor a reason to pursue an end, a known permissible effective means can inherit a reason from that consideration under the relevant conditions. Its usefulness is genuine, but it already requires a reason to pursue the end. Replacing that premise with fitting sadness would need a stronger, separately defended principle.

We therefore should not claim to have solved the unrelated-actor case by introducing “care” and applying ordinary means–end reasoning. The decisive positive argument must show that the patient's condition calls for practical response, rather than only a fitting spectator attitude. A theory that explicitly grounds such a practical call in the patient's condition would be a substantive contender; an ambiguous use of “concern” would not.

## 7. A concrete naturalistic contender locates the call in the patient

Kate Manne's *Locating Morality: Moral Imperatives as Bodily Imperatives* offers a useful comparison. Her proposed bodily imperatives belong to the patient, and the eligible agents and means are separately represented. She permits normative claims grounded in someone else's conative states. However, she distinguishes those moral claims from agent reasons subject to a motivational accessibility condition. Her account therefore supplies a naturalistic route toward interpersonal claims without independently proving our strongest categorical reason claim. See the [author manuscript, §§1.1.1–1.1.4 and note 7](https://www.katemanne.net/uploads/7/3/8/4/73843037/locating_morality_moral_imperatives_as_b.pdf) and the coordinated [source assessment](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-SOURCES.md).

The original proposal here is to investigate a **patient-side practical specification**: an actual subject's adverse condition and directed resistance can specify which change would answer it, while the external actor's opportunity determines a possible realization. This would make the source more than an observer's approval and the address more than grammatical projection of “my reason” onto another person.

Two constraints prevent premature success. First, having a satisfaction condition does not certify a demand's normative validity: domination and harmful cravings can also specify changes their bearers want. The proposed class must be independently justified, including its treatment of aversion constituted by another person's freedom or achievement. Second, patient willingness can delimit a permissible helper without by itself giving that helper a categorical reason. The relation between a justified patient claim and agent reasons remains substantive.

The promising difference from the simplest causal root is thus **richer constitutive content**, not an unexamined imperative word. A generative account could in principle explain the formation of subjects, identify the actual structure of their experiential and practical conditions, and justify a bridge from that structure. Nothing in the norm-free non-entailment theorem establishes that this richer structure is incapable of grounding ethics. Nor does its natural origin establish that every demand is justified.

## 8. The next test should compare explanations of the gate

Use a fixed patient burden, a willing patient, and a relief intervention available to one actor at a time. Compare the following contexts, retaining the same epistemic quality and permissible effects:

| Context | What changes | Question it isolates |
|---|---|---|
| Self-relief | Patient also performs the intervention | Establish the anchor and why it is justified |
| Entrusted relief | A legitimate commitment supplies another actor | Test role grounds without treating them as universal |
| Independent relief | An informed stranger has the eligible opportunity | Test whether history is an exclusive gate |
| Misleading signal | The actor's discomfort ends, the patient's burden does not | Test whether the candidate tracks the patient |
| Actual relief without emotional reward | The patient's burden ends, actor feeling remains fixed | Separate the ground from reinforcement |
| Refused or harmful means | Patient authority or another burden changes | Test limits without confusing them with zero standing |

These are conceptual discrimination cases and a specification for later empirical identification. They have not been run as experiments. Empirical studies could test what an actual learner tracks and how its representations change. They cannot settle the validity of either normative account by counting which response appears more often.

The most valuable positive result would justify the independent-relief edge by showing that the purported gate contributes nothing necessary to the particular practical ground, while giving a principled account of its legitimate contribution to special responsibilities. It must address the strongest rival: an account that recognizes real suffering and fitting compassion, accepts evidence and moral roles, yet denies every relief reason to the stranger. Calling that rival uncaring describes its verdict; it does not refute its explanation.

Conversely, a principled example in which identical eligible control and patient significance coexist with a justified absence of even a pro tanto reason would defeat this factorization at its claimed scope. A genuine conflict or prohibitive cost would not suffice; those may defeat action without eliminating the ground. A failure to identify actual suffering would instead revise Q → G. These failures should not be conflated.

## 9. What this changes for the unified foundation

The pursuit of unified grounding should continue. The current alternative is not exhausted by a sparse causal theorem on one side and a permanently external ethical axiom on the other. An account can seek a constitutive explanation in the generated subject and a practical relation between distinct centers. That would enrich our understanding of what generative reality contains. Whether its content follows necessarily from the minimal root, follows conditionally when a certain experiential structure forms, or requires an additional irreducible normative principle remains to be established.

This study locates that choice more precisely. The identity of experienced badness, actor scope of fitting concern, a reason to pursue relief, and means to act are four distinguishable explanatory connections. Combining them too soon makes a route appear complete; separating them does not show they are ultimately independent in reality.

**Supplied premises:** an identified patient and burden; warranted relief effects; relevant eligibility and permissions; anchor reasons; and any normative transmission, fittingness or practical-call principle used. The finite induction depends on these stated assumptions. No hidden analyst reward is used to certify the ethical direction.

**Next unresolved bridge:** defend or revise the sufficiency of a patient-ground plus eligible present control for some practical reason, while retaining the explanatory role of special relationships. Source comparison, strongest-rival criticism and empirical identification should be coordinated around that bridge. A successful local defense would advance N1; N3 answerability, conflict resolution and necessity of the full joint basis retain their own obligations.
