# Fair risk, costly equalization and priority near parity

8 October 2026. AI-authored theoretical continuation of [joint choice](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/CHOICE-AND-GENERATIVE-CONTEXT.md#risk-priority-near-parity). **13 new Lean theorems / 21 declaration audits**, with normative warrant and formal scope separately stated. No independent acceptance or empirical calibration is claimed.

**Advance:** exact equality on an inefficient risk frontier requires a stronger commitment than giving the more exposed position some priority. A proposed near-parity principle supplies a reason to stop short of exact equality, while a strong rival shows that symmetry, risk reduction, costless balancing and controlled variation do not entail that principle. The contribution is a defended, contestable normative extension with discriminating diagnostics; it does not infer the right lottery from a chosen utility formula.

## 1. Completed work reused

The [RS continuation](JOINT-RISK-REVISION-INTEGRATION.md#9-continue-through-equal-basic-risk-sharing) already defends a fairness reason for sharing matched basic risks when the loss profile is preserved. Its [asymmetric test](JOINT-RISK-REVISION-INTEGRATION.md#14-push-beyond-symmetry-fairer-exposure-can-add-basic-harm) already fixes the concrete problem: two feasible support plans produce risks (10%,40%) and (80%,10%); mixing them with respective probabilities 70% and 30% yields (31%,31%). The probability of some actual basic loss rises from 50% to 62%, under the supplied law excluding simultaneous loss. Its nineteen risk-sharing theorems and thirty-six audits are retained; its earlier eighteen-theorem joint-risk package remains separate.

[BC1/BC2/LC](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/BASIC-CONFLICT-JUDGMENT.md), [LR](CROSSED-CLAIMS-ARGUMENT.md) and [chance-revision fidelity](CHANCE-REVISION-INTEGRATION.md) are also completed inputs. LR already introduces a contestable local robustness commitment; the present principle is not a new discovery that local ethical stability can be investigated. Its distinct target is the **relative force of risk reduction close to equal exposure**, where a costly equality itself is at issue. A valid prior chance entitlement, unauthorized imposition or changed support threshold would require its own comparison and is excluded from this controlled case, not cancelled by the new principle.

## 2. The whole feasible comparison

The two positions arise in one support process. A plan selects how that common support meets their needs; the joint danger law determines where basic protection fails. Matched loss gravity, recovery, continuing relationships, permissions and implementation costs are substantive conditions of this case. The two risks are not claims owned by independent substances, and their sum is not a new collective claimant.

The initial numerical question must retain all supported mixtures. On a common denominator of 100k, move from exact balance by changing the first plan's ticket count from 7k to 7k+d, where k>0 and 0≤d≤3k. The two loss numerators become:

\[
(31k-7d,\;31k+3d).
\]

The sum falls from 62k to 62k−4d. A reduction of 7d at the initially less exposed position after departure costs an increase of 3d at the other position. These are changes between available alternatives, not claims that the baseline allocation was already rightfully owned. The selection–danger correspondence is the one supplied in RS, not a consequence of mixing labels equally. Finer mixtures must actually be feasible at accounted-for cost.

| Mixture of first plan | Risks | Probability of some loss in the stipulated law |
|---|---|---|
| 100% | (10%,40%) | 50% |
| 90% | (17%,37%) | 54% |
| 70% | (31%,31%) | 62% |

The middle row is a diagnostic witness, **not a recommended allocation**. The new question is why the last increments toward exact equality should have the same or greater priority than earlier sharing, given that their protective price remains 7 for 3. Neither the smallest sum nor the smallest maximum answers that question without a normative commitment.

## 3. NP: a near-parity priority candidate

**NP / 平等附近的优先力度：** for matched basic claims within the specified common support history, a vanishingly small difference in exposure should not, solely by appearing, confer a fixed and substantial extra priority on every further unit of risk reduction at the slightly more exposed position. A finite unequal reduction can therefore favor greater basic protection over exact numerical equality when the exposure difference is sufficiently small and no independently described threshold, history or other relevant relation changes.

NP concerns relative comparative force, not merely small changes in an overall appraisal. It is stronger than continuity of an appraisal and stronger than a finite bound on changes. It does not assert that fairness has no significance near equality, or that every probability reduction has the same force at widely unequal risks. It is an **additional normative commitment**, not a theorem of G0, J2, RS, interchangeable names or physical continuity.

Its intended strength is explicit: for any fixed protective exchange ratio greater than one, a sufficiently small departure sufficiently close to parity can favor the larger reduction over the smaller increase, subject to the matched-history and feasibility conditions. This is a candidate constraint on ethical comparisons, not a proved limit theorem or a numerical calibration. The 7-to-3 consequence uses this declared strength; it would not follow from the looser remark that priority is merely finite.

The affirmative defense has three steps. First, both positions' continuing basic lives remain the reason for protection throughout the transfer. Second, the difference in exposure can justify extra concern for the more exposed position, but in this case it gradually diminishes without changing the kind of threatened loss or the capacities, undertakings and claims at stake. Third, assigning a fixed large priority merely upon crossing from exactly equal to slightly unequal exposure makes exact numerical coincidence do substantial normative work independent of that diminishing disadvantage. We favor requiring an additional ground for that fixed premium, rather than treating it as inherent in every tiny difference. This is a substantive position about fair common protection, not a proof that a rival valuing exact equality is incoherent.

An operational consequence is conditional but definite. If NP applies and adequately fine permissible departures are feasible, the 7-for-3 protection exchange eventually favors a small departure from (31%,31%). **Exact balance then fails to be the best feasible response under this candidate.** This does not select (10%,40%), fix the justified departure size or prove that (17%,37%) is optimal. Without an independently supported neighborhood, no particular finite step is certified by calling it small.

One sufficient numerical interface makes the burden explicit. For a particular local appraisal, let removing risk have a lower force bound L and adding risk have an upper bound U along the actual two-stage comparison. If 7L>3U, the protective reduction wins. The checked theorem exposes these premises. It does not supply L, U, a global utility, or a normative measurement procedure. NP need not be expressed numerically at all; this certificate is one implementation of a comparison once justified, not an equivalent definition of NP.

## 4. A positive model and a stronger rival

Two diagnostic scores demonstrate the additional choice. They apply only to the supplied two-risk description and compare like denominators. Lower is better in each diagnostic. They are not endorsed value functions or measurements of lives.

**Quadratic diagnostic:** Q(a,b)=a²+b². It gives 1658 for (17,37), 1700 for (10,40) and 1922 for (31,31). Thus a model can favor some costly sharing without continuing to exact equality or minimizing total exposure. The checked general identity is:

\[
Q(31k-7d,31k+3d)+248kd=Q(31k,31k)+58d^2.
\]

For d>0 with 58d<248k, the departure has a lower diagnostic score. This is a constructive compatibility result, not the substantive warrant for Q or a calibrated recommendation. Across denominators, Q must be normalized by the square of the common denominator; raw ticket counts at different scales are not comparable scores.

**Corner diagnostic:** C(a,b)=2(a+b)+|a−b|. This equally explicit rival is symmetric, strictly respects componentwise risk reduction, prefers exact balance at fixed total exposure, and has the checked bound:

\[
|C(a,b)-C(c,d)|\leq 3(|a-c|+|b-d|).
\]

Nevertheless, every positive departure in the same family has C=124k+2d, compared with 124k at equality. It retains a first-order premium on eliminating any disparity. The linear change bound holds on arbitrarily fine integer representations of rational chances; bounded variation by itself does not force this premium to disappear. Its normalized score uses the common denominator once, not twice.

This rival defeats the proposed shortcut from symmetry, anti-waste, costless fairness and controlled variation to NP. It could defend the premium through the relational importance of exact equality, rather than claiming one life is worth more. Our objection therefore concerns the warrant and scope of that premium, not a mathematical inconsistency, a freely toggled good/bad predicate, or a cultural origin. The model does not establish that its particular coefficient is right either.

It exposes the welfare assumptions behind inequality measures, derives partial comparisons within a specified class and distinguishes those from completing a ranking. This is important prior credit for the method, not a derivation of our risk ethics: income, utility, exposure and basic living are not interchangeable quantities. We do not claim Atkinson proved NP or our finite diagnostic.

Their pluralist account distinguishes prospects and final outcomes and allows some cost for equality. It blocks treating every costly equality as obviously irrational. Its cardinal well-being framework and illustrative impairment valuations are not adopted here. Our case has supplied probabilities rather than severe uncertainty; this reading supplies a rival and genealogy, not empirical support or a direct endorsement of NP.

Broome and the chance-revision sources retain their previously assessed scopes. Steuwer's publisher abstract was located again but not newly assessed beyond that earlier record; it is not counted as a full-text source. No original source bytes were imported or changed. ## 7. Substantive author counterreview and retained judgment

**Exact equality may be an irreducible relational good.** Yes. The corner rival makes its effect explicit while respecting several other requirements. NP contests that every infinitesimal disparity warrants a fixed significant premium in this controlled basic-protection setting. Neither shared generativity nor symmetry refutes the rival; the affirmative reasons in §3 bear this philosophical burden. NP remains a defended candidate, not a theorem of all intelligence.

**Continuity or smoothness is being disguised as ethics.** The countermodel prevents that shortcut. A uniform change bound does not suffice; a smooth diagnostic is an implementation choice, not its own justification. NP concerns comparative priority, and its substantive commitment is stated independently. No real-analysis theorem or universal differentiability requirement is claimed.

**Equal numerical risks can mask very unequal lives.** Correct. NP's case requires matched loss gravity, recovery, histories, support and permissions. Different dependencies, cumulative burdens or a collapse threshold change the comparison even if percentages coincide. The whole cannot be reconstructed from two numbers alone. The result would need revision if these stipulated matches omitted a material difference.

**A binary menu may contain no small departure.** Correct. The conclusion against the equalized mixture requires actual fine feasible mixtures with accounted-for implementation consequences. A two-option menu or a costly granularity limit is not covered. No machine precision or costless randomization is conjured by the integer parameter.

**The candidate must secretly prefer the pure lower-total-risk plan.** It does not. The quadratic witness favors a partial and genuinely costly sharing step over both endpoints. NP alone does not select that witness, certify some fixed price for fairness or complete the comparison against the pure plan. It narrows what can justify the exact equalizing endpoint.

**Protecting the less exposed person reinstates a privileged prior entitlement.** The comparison neither calls that person's original 10% exposure rightful nor treats a loss of advantage as automatically forbidden. Its reason is continuing basic protection under matched claims. Genuine prior undertakings are retained through the chance-revision account, not created by the display order of alternatives.

**Decision:** retain NP as an explicit candidate and its conditional rejection of exact equalization on this inefficient frontier. Retain the strong corner rival and the unspecified application neighborhood. The next connection is to justify and calibrate relative protective priority across the remaining unequal-risk interval, using actual living consequences and independent comparison cases. A universal scalar, the precise optimal mixture, and the amount of added basic harm justified by fairness remain unestablished. This is narrower than saying the original question is untouched: it identifies an additional commitment, derives its directional consequence, and excludes several insufficient substitutes for its warrant.
