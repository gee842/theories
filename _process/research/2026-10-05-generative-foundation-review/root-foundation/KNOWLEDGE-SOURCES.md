# Primary-source comparisons for the knowledge track

8 October 2026. AI-authored reading record. External sources inform and test the proposal; they do not endorse G0 or certify the generative theory. These are philosophical arguments, not new experiments. No external source bytes are imported. Readings below are link-only; publication and access scopes are explicit.

## Gettier: justification and truth can come apart in their grounds

Edmund L. Gettier, “Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?”, *Analysis* 23(6), 121–123 (1963). [Three-page primary text](https://fitelson.org/proseminar/gettier.pdf). Read the opening conditions and both cases, pp.121–123. The PDF extraction interleaves part of the following article on p.123; it is not included in this assessment.

Gettier argues that truth, belief and the specified justification conditions can coexist without knowledge. His cases make a conclusion true through circumstances different from the supposed grounds that led to it. The paper supports retaining an anti-accident obligation; it neither supplies our positive account nor proves that every instance of knowledge requires an explicit theory of its justification.

**Change to our proposal:** replace an unexplained appeal to “warranted grasp” with an investigation of the actual grounding relation, including how apparent justification may fail to connect to what makes the conclusion true.

## Pritchard: competence, dependence and cognitive integration

Duncan Pritchard, “Socially Extended Scientific Knowledge”, *Frontiers in Psychology* 13, 894738 (2022). [Complete primary article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.894738/full). Read abstract and main argument from Introduction through “Distributed Scientific Knowledge?”; selected footnotes 5–6, 11–14 and 17–21 clarify scope. The primary article takes extended cognition as given for its investigation rather than proving it.

Pritchard distinguishes socially facilitated knowing, socially extended individual knowing and irreducibly distributed knowing. He connects knowledge to cognitive success significantly attributable to relevant cognitive agency, allowing substantial contributions from other people and technology. He warns that collaborative dependence does not by itself establish an irreducible group knower.

**Change to our proposal:** a shared generative history must retain differentiated access and uptake. Shared evidence and successful organization alone do not identify who knows. The source is also a strong compatible rival: it can accept integration and dependence without making our ethical G0 constitutive of knowledge. Its individual/environment vocabulary is a comparator's local decomposition, not a primitive split adopted by our theory.

## Pritchard: current basis and defeaters

Duncan Pritchard, “Anti-luck virtue epistemology and epistemic defeat”, *Synthese* 195, 3065–3077 (2018), first online 31 March 2016. [Publisher page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-016-1074-4). **Access limited to abstract and visible notes**, especially notes 7–10, 16–17; the main subscription text was not read. The separately located 2012 paper's author-hosted Dropbox link was inaccessible and is not counted as a full reading.

The abstract distinguishes risk-based defeat from defects in the explanatory relationship between success and ability. Note 9 directs attention to the currently sustaining basis rather than necessarily the belief's origin. Note 16 cautions against treating every presented error possibility as an epistemic defeater.

**Change to our proposal:** allow a later warranted basis to replace an initially defective route, and avoid making bare imaginability defeat knowledge automatically. The access limit prevents attributing a fuller argument to this reading.

## Collin: necessary truths expose a weak safety test

James Henry Collin, “Towards an Account of Epistemic Luck for Necessary Truths”, *Acta Analytica* 33, 483–504 (2018). [Open-access primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12136-018-0360-9). Read abstract, §1, the necessary-truth problem and attempted-solution passages in §2, and the later calculator/testimony applications; no claim to have assessed every argument for his proposed modal semantics.

Collin argues that ordinary metaphysical possibility formulations have difficulty distinguishing lucky belief in necessary truths. He proposes an epistemic-possibility treatment and discusses randomly correct calculation and transmission through testimony. His discussion motivates a test of any account that certifies a fixed proposition merely because it cannot be false.

**Change to our proposal:** mathematical knowing needs an appropriate proof, computation or testimonial route, not just the necessary truth of its content. Our small Lean result demonstrates only the weakness of a specified fixed-content predicate; it neither refutes all safety theories nor verifies Collin's proposed replacement.

## Burge: representation requires more than registration

Tyler Burge, “Primitive Agency and Natural Norms”, *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 79(2), 251–278 (2009). [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://philosophy.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/BUrge-2009-Primitive-Agency-and-Natural-Norms.pdf). Read the opening discussion, pp.251–255; the paper's subsequent full account of primitive agency is not assessed here.

Burge distinguishes sensory registration from perceptual representation with accuracy conditions. Perceptual constancies concern maintaining representation across changes in stimulation; invariant output or complicated signal processing alone does not suffice. He also proposes connections to the activities and needs of the whole organism, while explicitly leaving part of that constitutive claim undefended here. This is a philosophical comparator, not direct experimental evidence for our theory.

**Change to our proposal:** require a substantive explanation of content and its organization without demanding articulate self-justification from every perceiver. His organism/environment distinction is treated as a scoped analysis of relations within the whole. We do not adopt his taxonomy of animal perception as a result established by this reading.

## Goldman: a correct causal route can still leave relevant error

Alvin I. Goldman, “Discrimination and Perceptual Knowledge”, *The Journal of Philosophy* 73(20), 771–791 (1976). [Primary article scan](https://joelvelasco.net/teaching/4330/goldman76-discrimination.pdf). Read the barn example and relevance discussion, pp.772–778, the initial analysis and dachshund objection on p.779, and selected applications on pp.786–789. No full assessment of every clause of the later analysis is claimed.

Goldman's real-barn case introduces convincing decoys into the surrounding circumstances while preserving the actual successful perception. It challenges an account resting only on the actual causal connection. The paper examines relevant alternatives but expressly leaves unsettled whether circumstances determine a unique correct set. Its later examples also warn against assessing a broad response tendency without attending to the specific perceptual basis.

**Change to our proposal:** connect errors to the actual content, basis and conditions of access. Neither mere logical possibility nor omission from a model settles relevance. Our scope constraints remain a defended proposal, not a solution established by Goldman.

## Sellars: reliable reporting and recognizing reasons

Wilfrid Sellars, *Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind* (1956), §VIII, §§32–38. [Primary text, section VIII](https://ditext.com/sellars/epm8.html). Read this complete section, including the reporting discussion and the proposed response to regress; this is not a fresh reading of the full work.

Sellars rejects bare reliable reporting as a complete account of observational knowledge. He gives the reporter's recognition of authority a substantial role, including general knowledge of reliable indications. His developmental response permits earlier learning episodes that were not themselves knowledge. He also describes empirical inquiry as capable of revising particular commitments without simultaneously suspending everything.

**Change to our proposal:** cognitive uptake needs explanation beyond correlation. We do not adopt his full reflective requirement for every kind of knower; doing so would conflict with our proposed allowance for elementary perception and nonreflective reliance. The comparison therefore records a substantive disagreement, rather than presenting all three readings as endorsing one account.

### Millikan: historical function and mistaken representation

Ruth Garrett Millikan, “Biosemantics,” *The Journal of Philosophy* 86(6), 281–297 (1989). [Author-uploaded primary scan](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ruth-Millikan/publication/235734046_Biosemantics/links/54b9cd880cf253b50e2a9aa5/Biosemantics.pdf). Read pp.281–288 and selected argument on p.291; not the entire paper.

Millikan rejects statistical normality as a sufficient way of selecting content. She connects content to historical function and the organization that uses a representation, while permitting accidental success despite misrepresentation. Historical function is not simply frequent outcome, and content is not identical to the benefit of use. Our candidate takes formation and use seriously without claiming this reading resolves every referential ambiguity. Producer and consumer are analytical roles within one history. An attempted UConn handbook PDF timed out and is not a separate reading.

### Shea, Godfrey-Smith and Cao: informational and functional content

Nicholas Shea, Peter Godfrey-Smith and Rosa Cao, “Content in Simple Signalling Systems,” *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 69, 1009–1035 (2018; online 2017), DOI 10.1093/bjps/axw036. [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://www.nicholasshea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Shea_18_GS_Cao_Content_Sig_Sys_BP18_OA.pdf). Read abstract, §§3.1–3.2, selected cases in §§4.5–4.6, discussion on p.1029 and the appendix's initial definition; no independent recomputation of equilibria.

The authors distinguish functional content from information carried by signals in specified signalling games. Their treatment permits false content and identifies difficult cases involving baseline benefit and divergent interests. States, payoffs, selection and equilibrium conditions are supplied. This is a restricted account, not an exhaustive semantics of thought. Our comparison retains those inputs and does not identify payoff with G0 or ethical goodness.

### Sharpe, Batchelor and Schoenbaum: a measured dissociation

Melissa J. Sharpe, Hannah M. Batchelor and Geoffrey Schoenbaum, “Preconditioned cues have no value,” *eLife* 6:e28362 (2017), DOI 10.7554/eLife.28362. [Publisher](https://elifesciences.org/articles/28362) / [author-lab PDF](https://sharpelab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/185/2018/09/Sharpe-et-al.-2017.-eLife.pdf). Read main text and methods, PDF pp.1–7, plus selected [author-response passages](https://elifesciences.org/articles/28362/peer-reviews); cited references were not independently checked.

After neutral cue pairings and later food conditioning, a preconditioned cue supported anticipatory food-cup responding without supporting the tested new lever response. A second-order procedure supported conditioned reinforcement. Test contexts differed. The authors retain mediated-learning and accessibility questions; absence on one reinforcement measure does not prove absence of every kind of value. This constrains reward-only explanations of acquired relations, without establishing propositional knowledge or ethical value. A related dopamine paper was inspected only selectively and supplies no additional evidence claimed here.

### Hutto and Myin: the content-free rival

Daniel D. Hutto and Erik Myin, *Radicalizing Enactivism: Basic Minds without Content* (2013). [Primary institutional description and abstract](https://researchprofiles.herts.ac.uk/en/publications/radicalizing-enactivism-basic-minds-without-content/). **Abstract/description only; the book was not read.**

The programme challenges representational explanations of basic cognition. It gives this continuation a rival to preserve: adaptive participation may not need truth-evaluable content. This limited access does not justify a detailed reconstruction or refutation of the book, and the present study claims neither.

### Corriveau and Harris: familiarity and observed accuracy

Kathleen Corriveau and Paul L. Harris, “Choosing your informant: weighing familiarity and recent accuracy,” *Developmental Science* 12(3), 426–437 (2009), DOI 10.1111/j.1467-7687.2008.00792.x. [Author-lab primary PDF](https://www.bu.edu/learninglab/files/2012/05/Corriveau-Harris-2009-Choosing-your-informant.pdf). Read introduction, procedures, selected results from both experiments and general discussion including design limitations; not every statistical analysis independently assessed.

Children initially favored a familiar teacher; older preschoolers more strongly revised that preference after contrasting accuracy demonstrations. Correctly identifying prior inaccuracy and adjusting subsequent trust were distinguishable. The demonstration used extreme accuracy contrasts and explicit questions that could prompt attention to accuracy. The result concerns development under this procedure, not universal rationality, a longitudinal mechanism or proof that each resulting belief is knowledge.

### Jaswal and colleagues: correction can be resisted

Vikram K. Jaswal, A. Carrington Croft, Alison R. Setia and Caitlin A. Cole, “Young Children Have a Specific, Highly Robust Bias to Trust Testimony,” *Psychological Science* 21(10), 1541–1547 (2010), DOI 10.1177/0956797610383438. [Author-uploaded manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/46381423_Young_Children_Have_a_Specific_Highly_Robust_Bias_to_Trust_Testimony). Read abstract, both methods/results and discussion through manuscript p.7; references were not independently checked. Direct PMC and DOI access failed; the author upload supplied the text.

Three-year-olds adapted differently to misleading testimony and a misleading arrow despite feedback. Visible speakers also sustained more misleading influence than audio alone. The tasks differ in training history and presentation. The proposed developmental origin remains explanatory speculation, not a mechanism established by these experiments. Our use is bounded: feedback and communicative engagement need not bring belief into line with the evidenced relation.

### BIPM/CCT: quantities, realization and continuity

Consultative Committee for Thermometry, *Mise en pratique for the definition of the kelvin in the SI*, 2019D, current digitalized edition reached from the [BIPM index](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/mises-en-pratique), [primary PDF, version 2.3](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/41489682/SI-App2-kelvin.pdf/cd36cb68-3f00-05fd-339e-452df0b6215e?download=true&t=1748263492487&version=2.3). Read preface, §§1–3, selected §§4.1.2–4.1.3 and §§5–5.1; not all realization methods or annexes.

Practical scale values and thermodynamic temperature are distinguished. Their relationship requires empirical assessment with uncertainty. This supports a case of qualified continuity across changed standards, not a universal account of conceptual change. No measurement data were reanalyzed.

### Kuhn: comparison without complete translation

Thomas S. Kuhn, “Commensurability, Comparability, Communicability,” *PSA 1982*, volume 2, pp.669–688, copyright 1983. [Primary-text OCR reproduction](https://epdf.mx/commensurability-comparability-communicability.html), hosted by a third party. Read §1, substantial §2, selected phlogiston and mass/force passages, opening §5 and note 5. OCR was not fully checked against a scan; no verbatim quotation is used.

Local difficulty translating interrelated terms need not prevent comparison. Interpretation can require learning another vocabulary. Shared terms need not constitute a theory-independent language. The comparison preserves this position rather than attributing wholesale incomparability to Kuhn; we have not assessed his complete philosophical corpus.

### Chang: a stronger operational account of truth

Hasok Chang, “Operational Coherence as the Source of Truth,” *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 117(2), 103–122 (2017), DOI 10.1093/arisoc/aox004. [Author accepted manuscript, 14 February 2017](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/87be8e18-20f1-4554-b5fd-154176e29a36/content) / [publisher](https://academic.oup.com/aristotelian/article-abstract/117/2/103/4321726). Read manuscript pp.1–4, selected pp.8–11, pp.11–15 and the opening of p.16. Remaining argument and subsequent books were not assessed.

Operational coherence is stronger than token success; the proposed sufficient truth condition concerns pragmatically needed belief. Our workshop countercase tests the relation between a belief's enabling role and its specific content. It is a pressure test, not a refutation of every version of the proposal.

### Woodward: causal explanation and relevant variation

James Woodward, “Explanation and Invariance in the Special Sciences,” *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 51 (2000), 197–254. [Primary article reproduced for teaching](https://joelvelasco.net/teaching/5330%28fall2013%29/Woodward00-explinvarspecialsciences.pdf) / [author bibliography](https://www.jameswoodward.org/publications/). Read introduction, the opening intervention account through its main conditions, selected §§3–4, and the opening comparison in §9, pp.228–230. Not the whole 58-page article.

The account connects causal explanation to stability under suitable changes and permits restricted explanatory scope. It does not purport to settle all noncausal explanation. Our reconstruction treats intervention as a relation within the investigated unfolding; no experimental or formal result is obtained merely by adopting this philosophical comparison.

### Hume: inference beyond observed conjunctions

David Hume, *An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding*, [§IV](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/4) and [§V](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/5), Hume Texts Online. Read §IV and §V Part I, plus the opening belief discussion of Part II through §5.12; not the complete Enquiry. Historical text, not a current empirical theory.

Hume distinguishes observed conjunctions from the further inference beyond them and questions a justificatory appeal to past success; custom enters his explanation of expectation. Our fallibilist proposal takes that challenge seriously without claiming to settle all Hume interpretation or to derive induction from generativity. His larger metaphysical claims are not adopted wholesale.

### Pearl and Bareinboim: conditional transportability

Judea Pearl and Elias Bareinboim, “External Validity: From Do-Calculus to Transportability Across Populations,” *Statistical Science* 29(4), 579–595 (2014), DOI 10.1214/14-STS486. [Primary author preprint and metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01603) / [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.01603), uploaded 2015. Read introduction, selected §§2.1–2.4, §3 Examples 1–2 and the opening of Example 3, and selected selection-diagram discussion in §4. General proofs and algorithms were not fully audited or reimplemented.

Transfer depends on specified causal commonalities and differences, including whether a variable is a relevant covariate or a changing proxy. Formal identification does not certify the assumptions' empirical adequacy. Our Lean component does not reproduce the authors' transportability theorem.

### Recht and colleagues: partial transfer in actual evaluation

Benjamin Recht, Rebecca Roelofs, Ludwig Schmidt and Vaishaal Shankar, “Do ImageNet Classifiers Generalize to ImageNet?”, *PMLR* 97, 5389–5400 (2019). [Publisher](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/recht19a.html) / [main PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/recht19a/recht19a.pdf). Read main text §§1–6, with extracted tables noted; separate supplementary appendices, code and raw data were not examined or rerun. No independent figure or numerical verification is claimed.

New test sets produced lower accuracy while largely preserving model order; selection choices affected evaluation. The discussion leaves some forms of adaptivity open. This is a historical, bounded result, not evidence about all current models. Our inference distinguishes transfer of a ranking from transfer of an absolute performance estimate.

### Douglas: consequences of error within scientific decisions

Heather Douglas, “Inductive Risk and Values in Science,” *Philosophy of Science* 67(4), 559–579 (2000), DOI 10.1086/392855. [Primary article reproduced for teaching](https://thehangedman.com/teaching-files/stv/douglas-inductiverisk.pdf). Read introduction, selected §§2–3, selected closing discussion of §6 and §7; the middle dioxin examples and their cited studies were not independently assessed. The historical scientific examples are not used as current health guidance.

Consequences of error can require ethical evaluation in methodological and acceptance choices; wanting a conclusion does not establish its truth. Our account preserves this distinction while separately identifying support for a claim and authorization for a particular reliance. It does not settle all disputes about values, acceptance or knowledge attributions.

### Good: information within a specified decision problem

I. J. Good, “On the Principle of Total Evidence,” *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 17(4), 319–321 (1967), DOI 10.1093/bjps/17.4.319. [Primary three-page paper](https://fitelson.org/seminar/good.pdf) / [publisher metadata](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1093/bjps/17.4.319). Read the complete paper; references were not independently assessed. OCR mathematical signs were treated cautiously; no exact transcription of its inequality is used.

Good's expected-utility argument expressly assumes negligible evidence costs and a specified decision setup. Our inquiry countercase changes elapsed time and response feasibility, so it does not refute that conditional claim. The new Lean result checks a different, probability-free policy interface; it is not a formalization of Good's whole argument.

### Longino: effective critical interaction

Helen E. Longino, *Science as Social Knowledge: Values and Objectivity in Scientific Inquiry* (Princeton University Press, 1990), chapter 4. [Primary book PDF](https://fitelson.org/piksi/longino.pdf). Read printed pp. 75–79 (PDF pages 87–91), covering the four criteria and limits of criticism. ### Fricker: credibility and interpretive exclusion

Miranda Fricker, *Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing* (Oxford University Press, 2007), Introduction, pp. 1–8. [Author-hosted primary text](https://www.mirandafricker.com/uploads/1/3/6/2/136236203/introduction.pdf) / [publisher metadata](https://academic.oup.com/book/32817). Read the complete eight-page introduction, not the later chapters' detailed defenses. The host's recent indexing date is not the work's publication date.

The two forms of injustice described there motivate separate scrutiny of credibility judgments and the available interpretive resources. The introduction also preserves reasons as distinct from power and recognizes limits of individual virtues. Our admission-versus-acceptance proposal is an inference developed here, not a result attributed to Fricker.

### Wynne: a situated case of overlooked knowledge
