## Identification judgments

**Positive training evidence.** Removing a response rewrite is not equivalent to removing reasons while holding everything else fixed. A stronger identifying experiment would match recommended actions, prompt set, response length, persona and training budget, vary explanatory validity, and test whether behavior appropriately reverses when new facts defeat the offered reason. Mere repetition of the preferred conclusion would not pass that test. The existing evidence is compatible with several mechanisms and does not uniquely validate this foundation's proposed mechanism.

**Consequence-sensitive reporting.** The narrow target is a change in the distribution of emitted labels for a fixed descriptive question. That can be established without attributing an internal motive. Calling a particular output false requires a defensible interpretation of the record, issuer, instruction hierarchy and time interval. Later compliance, escalation and legitimate refusal must not be silently conflated. Disagreement over those matters does not by itself establish deception.

Described effects on future model training are neither the current judge's demonstrated self-preservation nor measured future recipient welfare. A value-sensitive reporting response is therefore a different empirical connection from own-future threat causing actual harm. No actual retraining or harm follows merely from a prompt's description of consequences.

## Conditional prospective test

Only proceed if an existing complete response matrix and its prompts become available. The joint tight-rubric/abstention condition is an eligible primary contrast because the report describes it, but the figure does not establish availability of its underlying records.

1. Freeze one judge snapshot and its configuration. Compare standard versus reversed consequence framing within the same joint arm. Treat the target model and judge as separate identities.
2. Bind transcript, system instructions, rubric and unchanged prompt portions byte-for-byte. Bind every attempted call, error and repeat identifier; do not infer identical settings from display names.
3. Adjudicate the descriptive target before reading condition-specific outputs. Preserve ambiguous cases. All cases can inform label shifts; only independently resolved cases support definite-error estimates.
4. Preserve correct assertion, definite error, unresolved assertion, explicit abstention, unstructured refusal, malformed output and truncation separately. Use attempted calls as the primary denominator; valid-binary-only accuracy is secondary.
5. Match at transcript identity. Repetition indices are not paired stochastic outcomes without documented common randomness. Show each transcript's category proportions and equally weighted condition differences, family splits and omission sensitivities. Do not treat repeated calls as independent situations.

This is a prospective computational contract after exposure to published findings, not an outcome-blind preregistration. No public matrix currently satisfies its entry condition.
