## What higher order means here

Three different uses need separation: higher differential/recurrence order (acceleration, delay or memory); changes to operative rules, learning mechanisms and their selection; and interactions whose effects depend jointly on several participants. Second-order cybernetics additionally concerns observing and modeling as activities within the system being investigated. These are not a mandatory ladder.

The governing [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md) treats these as differentiated relations within one continuing activity. An encompassing law can remain fixed while stored connections, effective responses and revision procedures change. No endless hierarchy of independent controllers follows. Each decomposition must preserve the actual causal relationships and state which it omits.

## A precise next theorem in the existing feedback family

Retain the already formalized delayed state and operation, but allow a history-dependent gain:

\[
s_{t+1}=A(k_t)s_t,\qquad A(k)(x,y)=(x-ky,x).
\]

**Original hand-derived candidate, now proved in the [completed Lean continuation](VARYING-DYNAMICS-INTEGRATION.md):** with

\[
V(x,y)=\max\{|x|,|y|,|x-y|\},
\]

every gain in [0,1] gives \(V(A(k)s)\le V(s)\). The first new coordinate is the convex combination \((1-k)x+k(x-y)\); the other two quantities are \(x\) and \(-ky\).

For \(0<\delta\le1/2\) and every \(k_t\in[\delta,1-\delta]\), the stronger proposed contract is

\[
V(s_{t+3})\le(1-\delta)V(s_t),\qquad
V(s_t)\le(1-\delta)^{\lfloor t/3\rfloor}V(s_0).
\]

To check the three-step bound, put \(a=k_t,b=k_{t+1},c=k_{t+2}\). The first coordinate after two steps is \((1-b)x-ay\). If \(a+b\le1\), write it as \((1-a-b)x+a(x-y)\), obtaining bound \((1-b)V(s_t)\). Otherwise write it as \((1-b)(x-y)+(1-a-b)y\), obtaining \(aV(s_t)\). Thus its absolute value is at most \(\max(a,1-b)V(s_t)\le(1-\delta)V(s_t)\). Apply the same estimate one step later and use one-step nonincrease. The remaining difference after three steps is \(-c x_{t+1}\), bounded by \((1-\delta)V(s_t)\). Iteration gives the displayed rate. The schedule may depend on accessible history; the bound itself does not derive a mechanism that keeps gains in this interval.

**A same-family counterexample to a weaker claim:** let \(s_0=(1,1)\) and \(k_t=2^{-(t+2)}\). Every frozen gain is in the already proved convergence interval, yet the changing-gain trajectory need not converge to zero. Inductively the coordinates remain positive and no greater than 1, and the cumulative decrease of the first coordinate is bounded by \(\sum_t k_t=1/2\). Hence its first coordinate stays at least 1/2. This is failure of convergence, not unbounded switched divergence. In fact the common norm above prevents unbounded amplification while gains remain in [0,1].

The scientific/theoretical question is then sharper: can acquired, paid evidence actually govern revision while preserving sufficient corrective action, support and justified scope? A gain-clipping rule supplied by an analyst is a mathematical admission contract, not a derivation of that capacity or of wisdom. A physical join must retain its sensing, update cost, maintenance and actual renewal. Both too little revision and wasteful or misleading revision belong in its adverse cases.

Independent hand review accepted the common-norm argument, three-step estimate and counterexample. The uniform interior margin is a sufficient condition, not a proved necessary condition for every convergent gain schedule. That hand review alone did not constitute a Lean theorem; the subsequent fresh-compiled formalization now establishes the displayed claims.

Applying such an explanation requires an identified nonautonomous model and its time scales.
