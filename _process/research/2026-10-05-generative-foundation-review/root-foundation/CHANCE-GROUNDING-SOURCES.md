## New primary readings

**Kai Spiekermann, “Good reasons for losers: lottery justification and social risk,” Economics & Philosophy 38(1), 108–131 (2022); online 12 April 2021.** DOI: 10.1017/S0266267121000043. Root accessed the [publisher's full-text HTML](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/economics-and-philosophy/article/good-reasons-for-losers-lottery-justification-and-social-risk/32BA61DF3384546EFFD56AF864D36C29), reading the introduction, setup, central section 3 and selected scope discussion in sections 4–5. The LSE PDF access failed; this is not a PDF or whole-article reading claim.

The paper offers a reason addressed to an actual loser: the same person could have won through the same properly applied procedure. Its initial cases already assume equally strong claims and fair probabilities. Thus it helps assess the justification and implementation of a lottery, not establish CP or unequal weights. Modal reversibility is distinguished from probability, so it supplies no minimum chance. The paper's identity and psychological-similarity tests are not adopted as universal criteria for all intelligence or life. Our relational application would require its own argument. These limits matter as much as the affirmative comparison.
