## 1. Scanlon: identifying value does not specify one response form

**Primary source:** T. M. Scanlon, *Understanding Good of a Kind and Good for in Terms of Reasons*, manuscript for the Joseph Raz tribute conference, 22–24 September 2023. [Conference-hosted PDF](https://www.law.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2023-08/scanlon_raz_paper.pdf). Read selected passages in printed pp. 1–12 and pp. 14–16; the PDF page index is one less than the printed page number. The directly accessed copy has 27 PDF pages.

Scanlon presents value through specific reasons and varied appropriate responses: promotion, admiration, emulation and enjoyment need not have the same grounds. His discussion of loyalty includes an actual relationship in the reason, while the courage discussion distinguishes reasons for acting courageously from reasons for cultivating courage. He warns that explaining impartial goodness in terms of others’ reasons to care cannot then explain those reasons merely by invoking that goodness. Good-of-kind standards can also describe hypothetical reasons without establishing anyone’s actual practical reasons. These claims are in printed pp. 2–12. His separation of another’s rescue from benefit to oneself, p. 15, supports avoiding a return-to-self requirement.

**Contribution to this investigation:** test whether the proposed whole relation preserves the form and grounds of response, rather than silently turning every favorable quality into the same promotion ranking. His reasons-first analysis is a substantive rival, not an adopted premise of the generative account or a refutation of co-grounding.

## 2. Portmore: a strong affirmative comparator has a substantive determination claim

**Primary source:** Douglas W. Portmore, *Consequentializing Moral Theories*, penultimate draft dated 22 April 2006, subsequently published in *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 88 (2007), 39–73, DOI 10.1111/j.1468-0114.2007.00280.x. Directly read the [author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227683288_CONSEQUENTIALIZING_MORAL_THEORIES), selected passages in draft pp. 1–10, pp. 16–21, and pp. 29–32. The hosted page identifies author upload; the draft's pagination must not be substituted for journal pagination.

Portmore counts everything that would be the case under an act in its broadly construed outcome. This supports preserving the deciding activity inside the comparison. However, the teleological position also claims that the outcome ranking determines practical status; a matching representation is not the whole claim. Section 5 explicitly separates agreement in verdicts from agreement about their grounds. Ranking outcomes solely by already assigned practical verdicts would forfeit that determination claim. His defense is accordingly a serious comparator, rather than a theorem that all reasons must follow from one prior whole ordering.

**Contribution:** the relevant issue is explanatory direction and independently defended content, not whether a whole-history notation can accommodate restrictions or special responsibilities. No universal maximization premise is imported here.

## 3. S. Andrew Schroeder: representation and ethical explanation can diverge

**Primary source:** S. Andrew Schroeder, *Consequentializing and Its Consequences*, author-hosted uncorrected accepted draft; journal version *Philosophical Studies* 174 (2017), 1475–1497, DOI 10.1007/s11098-016-0768-2. [Author’s college-hosted PDF](https://www1.cmc.edu/pages/faculty/ASchroeder/docs/SASConsCons.pdf). Directly inspected selected passages in draft pp. 1–10, with emphasis on pp. 4–10; the copy has 27 PDF pages. This author is distinct from Mark Schroeder, whose work he discusses.

Schroeder distinguishes arguments that the consequentialized account is superior from arguments that it is merely the same account expressed differently. He challenges identifying goodness with choiceworthiness just because the two can be represented together. An account may assign goodness a role in fitting attitudes or treat it as one contributor to choice. Thus extensional agreement can leave an explanatory disagreement. The paper grants broad representability for argument’s sake; that grant is not an independently established theorem.

**Contribution:** examine whether a proposed universal whole relation makes genuinely shared comparisons, preserves their grounds and distinguishes response forms. This does not impose a separation between ethical and generative reality. Our overlap and response-mode tests are an original application, not conclusions reported from this paper.
