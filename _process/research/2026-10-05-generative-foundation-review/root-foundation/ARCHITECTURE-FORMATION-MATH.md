## 1. Four different explanatory events

Architecture is the actual arrangement of parts and connections, rather than
a programme label sampled from an enumerated list. The following events are
reported separately:

1. **Production:** fabrication and binding create components and edges.
2. **Completion:** an actual signal path to an existing predictor exists.
3. **Use:** a target cue traverses that path and the prepaid command acts.
4. **Retention:** the resulting arrangement receives a subsequent maintenance
   payment and remains functional at the stated boundary.

A fifth relation connects generations of construction: an actual productive
use can pay for a reusable guide whose presence changes subsequent binding
rates. That is feedback on the generator's material state. It does not require
an external selection score, and it does not make beneficial recipient
outcomes necessary consequences of feedback.

## 3. Primitive graph law

Let P denote the actual inherited predictor. A new receptor R carries the
target cue. A formed comparator D passes it exactly when its observed target
identity is the actor. Each costs one appropriate typed precursor and one
fuel unit. The D component can remain present but unused; its expense is
never erased merely because it was unnecessary to the eventual path.

An initially paid one-unit recipe converts a template blank into a docking
guide derived from P. Let e be the number of active P-docking guides and i
the number of additional D-docking guides. The three primitive bond rates are

\[
R\to P:e,\qquad R\to D:b(1+i),\qquad D\to P:e.
\]

R has one outgoing port, so its two outgoing alternatives exclude one another.
D→P may form before either R bond. Each bond consumes a connector and its
declared fuel expense. Only physically enabled, affordable bonds participate
in the race. Construction stops at an R-to-P path or when no such local event
remains enabled. No rule consults the resulting recipient benefit.

The runtime propagates the signal through the graph and D's local identity
predicate. A direct R→P edge admits both target cues. A path R→D→P admits
only the actor. These descriptions classify the graph afterward; they are
not programme labels drawn by the assembly law.

### Exact completion distribution

Assume R and D exist, e>0, sufficient reserve for every considered bond and
at least two connectors. Put a=e and c=b(1+i)>0. The initial total rate is
2a+c. Direct completion occurs through either:

\[
R\to P\quad\text{with probability }\frac{a}{2a+c},
\]

or

\[
D\to P\ \text{then }R\to P
\quad\text{with probability }
\frac{a^2}{(2a+c)(a+c)}.
\]

Adding gives

\[
\boxed{\Pr(\text{direct path})=\frac{a}{a+c}},
\qquad
\Pr(\text{filtered path})=\frac{c}{a+c}.
\]

The direct path with an unused D→P bond is a physically distinct history with
an extra cost. The expected number of bonds is

\[
\boxed{2-\frac{a}{2a+c}=\frac{3a+2c}{2a+c}}.
\]

These elementary identities follow by conditioning on the first bond. They
are not newly discovered mathematics. Their explanatory role is to obtain
the completed-architecture distribution from local events while retaining
intermediates and expenses.

With only one connector, or only one unit available for primary unit-price
binding, completion requires R→P to occur first:

\[
\Pr(\text{completion})=\frac{a}{2a+c}.
\]

The other first bonds leave actual incomplete structures. Sampling the final
direct/filtered labels with probabilities a/(a+c),c/(a+c) would conceal this
failure. If e=0, local R→D formation can consume support without creating a
P path at all. Large inaccessible source stocks do not repair that missing
construction interface.

## 4. Consequences change the generator

Every round has an actor opportunity followed by a recipient opportunity.
An existing active receptor pays retention1 and sensing1 sequentially, then
prepays work3 if the graph transmits. A productive actor command yields10
to actor fuel. One unit maintains the exercised route; two more plus an
available template blank can produce another construction guide.

The live law copies a P guide, e→e+1. The matched frozen law spends the same
two fuel units and blank on an inert product. A third law copies an actually
available D motif into a D guide, i→i+1. A precursor cannot be spent twice,
and no guide is copied from a private receipt that did not occur.

Conditional on unchanged full construction access, a P-guide copy changes
the next direct probability by

\[
\frac{a+1}{a+c+1}-\frac{a}{a+c}
=\frac{c}{(a+c)(a+c+1)}>0.
\]

A D-guide copy instead increases c by b, giving

\[
\frac{a}{a+c+b}-\frac{a}{a+c}
=-\frac{ab}{(a+c)(a+c+b)}<0.
\]

These are production-bias results. A private capture through either graph can
fund either supplied template-copy chemistry. Private success alone therefore
does not dictate whether future construction broadens or narrows target access.
The component that gets copied, its wiring and its locality remain causal
inputs; they are not inferred from an observer's favorable score.

## 5. Separate recipient consequences, use and retention

Recipient captures add10 only to the recipient, followed by its own one-unit
operation. There is no actor return from this outcome. A wrong executed
recipient command can destroy one available recipient unit through an explicit
damage channel. That channel is separate from ordinary missed support.

After recipient use, the newest complete graph and all previously retained
graphs pay maintenance individually, oldest first. The first graph therefore
pays again after the second round. A failed payment deactivates that bound
graph permanently in this experiment; parts are not recycled. A retained old
graph cannot bypass construction at the new addressed site. Formation and
previous use do not automatically imply final retained function.

For every suffix event the energy account is

\[
F+H+E+X+U+D+W=F_{\rm entry}+H_0+E_{\rm suffix},
\]

where F is actor fuel, H recipient reserve, E remaining incident source, X
paid actor expenditure, U recipient operation, D recipient damage loss and W
inaccessible source spill. Typed material is conserved separately:

\[
R_{\rm raw}+R_{\rm formed}=R_0,\quad
D_{\rm raw}+D_{\rm formed}=D_0,\quad
W_{\rm raw}+\#\text{bonds}=W_0,
\]

\[
T_{\rm raw}+e+i+T_{\rm inert}=T_0.
\]

The connector symbol W in the material account names a different typed stock
from source spill in the energy account; the implementation keeps them in
separate fields. Dead bound components remain in the material totals. Source
and recipient reserve cannot prepay actor expenses without an explicit
transfer event, which this law does not provide.

## 6. Production does not entail retention advantage

The primary unconditional comparison is:

| Suffix | Second direct graph probability | Expected recipient captures | Expected retained graphs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Live copying | 1/10 | 17/108 | 2/9 |
| Conventional local realization | 1/10 | 17/108 | 2/9 |
| Matched inert copying | 49/648 | 5/36 | 2/9 |
| Filter copying | 11/216 | 13/108 | 2/9 |

The conventional arm preserves the complete distribution, not only the means.
The conditional2/3 production result cannot substitute for the unconditional
1/10, which includes partial assembly, inadequate liquidity and failed
original histories. Suppressing construction entirely leaves more actor fuel
in this finite comparison. That intervention does not itself install an
optimizing policy, nor does it mean the actor died because no new graph formed.

Raising maintenance price to3 produces expected retained graph counts11/54
for live copying,5/24 for inert copying and23/108 for filter copying. Here the
generator with broader production performs more recipient work but retains
fewer graphs. Finite retention and production bias are distinct outcomes;
neither is a universal moral score or a theorem about population fixation.

The opposite-recipient-law case leaves the actor and generator histories
unchanged while converting helpful transfer into zero recipient captures and
17/108 expected recipient damage under the live law. Without spare template
material all three copying interventions agree. A single connector prevents
second completion. High receptor costs can permit some use but zero final
retention. Cheap-gating, affinity-only and price-only cases distinguish
production-rate changes from binding-price changes. These are consequences
of the declared common mechanism, not discarded anomalies.
