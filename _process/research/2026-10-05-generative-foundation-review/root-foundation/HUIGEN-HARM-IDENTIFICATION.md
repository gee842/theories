# Identifying the harm-ground without confusing signal, experience and reason

6 October 2026. AI-authored supporting investigation of **an independently identified aversive condition → a justified reason → its scope across deliberators** in the [entire 慧根 programme](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/HUIGEN.md). This is a limited primary-evidence check, not a medical recommendation, new participant study or completed theory of pain.

The [practical-necessity audit](HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md) isolates a narrow candidate ground: an aversive experience whose prevention leaves no compensating relevant good worse. Its argument cannot be evaluated if “harm” silently changes between a stimulus, a control-system error, an unpleasant experience, an observed response and a valid reason. The evidence below makes that identification obligation concrete. It does not establish that the ground has actor-neutral normative authority.

## 1. The empirical distinctions required by the philosophical claim

Keep five questions separate:

| Question | Candidate evidence or relation | What it does not settle alone |
|---|---|---|
| What happened to the organism? | Identified exposure, tissue condition and bodily consequences | Whether or how that occurrence was experienced |
| What sensory quality or intensity was experienced? | Appropriately interpreted reports and converging measures | Its unpleasantness or total significance for the subject |
| Was it experienced as aversive? | Affective reports and converging evidence under stated interpretation | Every good or harm, or an infallible judgment of overall badness |
| Did it produce withdrawal or other response? | Observed action and its causal conditions | Absence of experience when action is absent; existence of a justified reason when action occurs |
| What reason does it supply, and to whom? | A defended normative ground, patient scope, actor scope and defeater analysis | Motivation, feasibility or successful delivery of relief |

These are conceptual distinctions to preserve, not a claim that five independent brain modules exist. The measures can interact, reports can be fallible and different implementations can support similar capacities. A scientific account must identify the relations it actually uses.

## 2. Three primary studies and their bounded contribution

### Rainville and colleagues, 1997: a selective affect manipulation

**Source:** “Pain Affect Encoded in Human Anterior Cingulate But Not Somatosensory Cortex,” *Science* 277, 968–971. Inspected the author-uploaded original article text, especially results and methods notes 9–11. [Author copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/13968465_Pain_Affect_Encoded_in_Human_Anterior_Cingulate_But_Not_Somatosensory_Cortex), [primary bibliographic record and abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9252330/).

The PET study used eight volunteers selected for moderate-to-high hypnotic suggestibility and robust modulation of unpleasantness, with eleven sessions rather than eleven independent participants. Suggestions changed unpleasantness ratings significantly; the intensity contrast was not statistically significant. Anterior cingulate activity varied with the affect manipulation, while the reported primary somatosensory response did not show that pattern. This supports separating the measured sensory and affective dimensions under the tested conditions. Nonsignificance does not prove identical intensity, and selection limits population generalization. The experiment neither identifies a uniquely necessary substrate nor measures normative authority. Its relevance here is to prevent the proposed aversive ground from being equated with stimulus intensity alone.

### Berthier, Starkstein and Leiguarda, 1988: response is not a complete proxy

**Source:** “Asymbolia for pain: A sensory-limbic disconnection syndrome,” *Annals of Neurology* 24, 41–49. Inspected the publisher's original abstract and metadata, **not the complete patient reports**. [Publisher record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.410240109).

The report concerns six patients with unilateral hemispheric injury. Its abstract describes absent or inadequate withdrawal and emotional responses despite absence of primary sensory deficits, with other neurological abnormalities and insular involvement. This gives a bounded clinical reason to distinguish sensory processing from observed affective/protective response. The abstract does not independently establish precisely what each patient phenomenally experienced, nor that every such patient lacks suffering. The study is not a controlled experiment isolating one normative or neural variable. No conclusion here treats an absent withdrawal response as absence of a patient, reason or need for consideration.

### Feinstein and colleagues, online 2015 / volume 2016: localization is not universal necessity

**Source:** “Preserved emotional awareness of pain in a patient with extensive bilateral damage to the insula, anterior cingulate, and amygdala,” *Brain Structure and Function* 221, 1499–1511. Inspected the original publisher full text, including methods, results and discussion of recovery. [Open-access original](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00429-014-0986-3).

The single-patient study reports preserved, sometimes pronounced pain experience and expression despite extensive damage to regions commonly associated with pain affect. Measures included reports, facial/vocal responses, withdrawal and autonomic response. The authors acknowledge chronic injury and possible reorganization; their specific plasticity explanation is conjectural. They also report limitations of comparisons and unusable pain fMRI because of movement. This case challenges an unqualified claim that those named regions must be intact for the capacity to occur. It does not establish that they play no normal role, that brain function is irrelevant, or that their removal is harmless. For the generative account, it reinforces the distinction between a capacity and one hypothesized realization.

The Rainville extraction begins with the end of an unrelated article on the same journal page; that material was excluded. ## 3. How this changes the root's premise ledger

The current generative models can instantiate operational significance: an event changes a system's later possibilities, and a formed response can regulate that difference. The step to **experienced** aversive significance remains a separate identification and explanatory problem. Neither a signed controller error nor a recipient's resource loss already constitutes an observation of suffering.

This does not diminish the models' causal results. The [recorded-reasons construction](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/RECORDED-REASONS.md) explicitly supplies factual calibration and normative relevance. The concrete recipient constructions concern specified outcomes and accounts. Using them for an experiential harm claim would require a new, warranted interpretation of the patient and relevant state; changing the label of a fuel variable would not provide it.

The candidate harm-ground should therefore state at least:

- which patient and type of experience are claimed;
- how the evidence bears on that claim, including uncertainty and alternative explanations;
- which aspect is alleged to supply the reason;
- whose action the reason addresses and under what responsibility/opportunity conditions;
- which competing reasons, endorsed projects or other relevant consequences may qualify the proposed comparison.

These are requirements of the specific grounding argument, not a complete ethical decision procedure. A patient need not provide a verbal report for a reason to exist. The evidence needed for someone else to warrant a judgment is a different question from the actual patient's condition and standing.

## 4. What empirical evidence could and could not adjudicate

If a proposed identification claims that the ground is just sensory intensity, a selective change in experienced unpleasantness challenges its adequacy. If it identifies significance with overt withdrawal, dissociation between sensory processing and response challenges that proxy. If it requires one named anatomical substrate universally, preserved capacity after extensive damage is a relevant counterexample candidate, with residual function and reorganization considered. These are substantive ways evidence can revise the root's realization claims.

The disputed **actor scope** needs another argument. Two proposed accounts can agree on the patient's experience, its local significance, and every observed response while differing over whether the loss gives a separate deliberator a reason. Measuring which account people endorse can inform the formation and variation of judgments; it does not, without an additional warrant, establish which reason claim is valid. Likewise, finding that a reason-motivated action has a causal neural realization does not refute its validity.

The positive task is to combine an adequate account of the experience with a defended account of what makes it reason-giving. The [self-ground investigation](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md) tests whether future concern, branching and delegated action can widen that reason's scope. The physiological studies here neither supply those normative premises nor show they are impossible.

**Status:** the contribution is an evidence-sensitive specification of the proposed ground, with concrete failure tests for conflated proxies and realization claims. It adds no new experiment, proof of sentience from generic organization, moral patient classifier, universal necessity of concern or numerical measure of ethical worth. The entire foundation remains open at the connection from formation and significance to warranted cross-center reasons.

## 5. Equal private cost is a controlled comparison, not a proof of reasonlessness

The practical-congruency follow-up tests two feasible actions with equal actor costs and a difference in another patient's aversive experience. This need not posit a physically free intervention. Two commands can consume the same finite resources while producing different recipient outcomes. Equating every morally relevant consequence remains a conceptual control to defend; equating one resource counter does not establish it.

An existing [recipient-correction construction](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/RECIPIENT-CORRECTION.md) gives a concrete operational comparison. In its primary variable-producer fixture, the indexed arm uses actual recipient evidence; the matched `private_only` arm pays the same installation, observation and writing bills while lacking that channel. Saved unconditional metrics are:

| Metric | Indexed recipient evidence | Matched absent recipient evidence |
|---|---:|---:|
| Expected final actor fuel | 25/18 | 25/18 |
| Expected actor captures | 2/9 | 2/9 |
| Expected suffix spending | 25/6 | 25/6 |
| Expected final recipient reserve | 10/9 | 0 |

These are existing synthetic results, not new observations of people or an empirical identification of aversive experience. The comparison changes information and retained control contents; it does **not** hold the entire actor's physical or epistemic state fixed. The recipient is a resource target, and the interpretation of its reserve as experienced good is not established.

Direct inspection of the [suffix code](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/selectors/updaters/prediction/recipient-correction/model.py) confirms why the measured private costs can agree: both arms pay the acquisition/write charges, while successful recipient work credits a separate stock. The missing channel suppresses a label rather than refunding the payment. The [independent checker](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/selectors/updaters/prediction/recipient-correction/independent_check.py) contains a separate equal-stock diagnostic at intervened fuel 40; that altered-fuel case is not presented as an observed predecessor. The table above uses the actual saved primary mixture instead. On 6 October 2026, all eight hashes in the [results receipt](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/selectors/updaters/prediction/recipient-correction/results.json) and all eleven in the [independent receipt](../../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/selectors/updaters/prediction/recipient-correction/independent-check.json) matched the current files. No scientific run was repeated for this reading.

The logical contribution is limited and useful. If an actor's evaluation depends **only on expected final actor fuel**, the displayed equality cannot give that evaluation a strict preference for one arm. Equal expectations alone would not settle a nonlinear or risk-sensitive evaluation; that stronger comparison requires the full distributions. A richer actor may value faithful inquiry, the recipient, a relationship or another consequence. The table therefore excludes one proposed private-resource advantage; it does not establish absence of every internal reason or every rational path to concern. Likewise, successful help alone cannot identify which ground produced it. The existing private-zero proxy can initially match the helping behavior while misidentifying the evidence source.

The matched aversive-experience case consequently requires three independently declared layers: an identified experiential difference; a genuine comparison of the actor's relevant competing reasons; and a justified account of why the patient-ground addresses that actor. Material equality can help isolate the second layer but cannot provide the first or third. This provides a concrete interface for testing the philosophical reconstruction without importing an analyst's moral reward into the causal model.

## 6. Perspective-taking must earn its epistemic role

The next proposed route uses imagination to make another patient's significance accessible to an actor whose initial policy omits it. Three selective primary comparisons test different parts of that route. They are not a systematic review, new participant data or an empirical adjudication of ethical truth.

**Affect:** Batson, Early and Salvarani, 1997, *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 23, 751–758, [publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0146167297237008). The abstract reports undergraduates hearing a fictional need-related interview under objective, imagine-other or imagine-self instructions. The two imaginative conditions produced different reported patterns: imagine-other elicited empathy, while imagine-self also elicited distress. The motivational interpretation draws on prior work; this abstract alone is not a direct test of all helping motives or normative validity. **Access scope:** publisher abstract and metadata only. An indexed full-text transcription was located but its direct fetch timed out; its methods were not treated as inspected.

**Accuracy:** Eyal, Steffel and Epley, 2018, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 114, 547–571, [author-uploaded original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324232939_Perspective_mistaking_Accurately_understanding_the_mind_of_another_requires_getting_perspective_not_taking_perspective), [author's publication listing](https://www.marysteffel.com/research). The report covers 25 experiments and finds no consistent accuracy improvement from instructed perspective-taking across its tasks. In Experiment 25, asking romantic partners directly about opinions improved prediction; verbal answers still had to be mapped to later numeric ratings. The authors expressly limit the inference: these results do not prove that perspective-taking can never improve accuracy. **Access scope:** abstract, Experiment 25 rationale/methods/results/discussion, and the general discussion in the author-uploaded extraction; not every earlier experiment or supplementary analysis. Author-site PDF redirection, a mirror PDF and the lab's Drive viewer did not yield readable full text through the browser; the accessible author upload supplied the reading. No raw-data reanalysis is claimed.

**Competing judgments:** Batson, Klein, Highberger and Shaw, 1995, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 68, 1042–1054, [APA-provided preview and abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232548845_Immorality_From_Empathy-Induced_Altruism_When_Compassion_and_Justice_Conflict). The abstract reports two allocation experiments in which induced empathy increased preferential allocation to its target. In the first, participants showing that partiality still judged it less fair and moral. This supports separating emotional concern, allocation and reported evaluative standards. It does not establish that every preferential allocation is objectively wrong. **Access scope:** abstract and the publisher-provided first-page preview only; the full methods and results were not available in that preview.

All three readings were made on 6 October 2026. Only the named primary texts support these descriptions; later citing articles on the hosting pages were excluded. Source bytes were not imported. These bounded findings motivate tests of the proposed interface, not universal claims about empathy or instructions for clinical intervention.

### A discriminating interface for warranted uptake

The [argument audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md) and [frame analysis](HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) distinguish a model's represented patient from the actor who uses it. The following are proposed contrasts, not completed experiments:

| Contrast | What a successful identification route must distinguish |
|---|---|
| More vivid presentation, same independently warranted patient facts | Increased salience or distress versus an additional reason-ground; vividness may improve attention without adding a new patient fact |
| Corrective evidence about the actual patient | A warranted revision of the patient model versus persistent projection of the observer's own response |
| Observer's distress falls while the patient's condition remains | Private emotional relief versus relief for the named patient |
| Different patient needs in an otherwise similar situation | Faithfulness to this patient versus assuming everyone wants what the observer would want |
| Return from imagined position to the actor's actual role | Retaining the same patient, action opportunity and justified ground versus silently changing the claim's addressee |

A positive result must show which error the process corrects. It might reveal a previously overlooked fact, correct a false belief that others share the actor's preferences, make an existing consideration intelligible, or expose inconsistent application of an admitted sufficient ground. These are different gains. The observer need not reproduce the patient's suffering in order to possess a warranted reason about it. If imagination itself produces an aversive episode in the observer, that is a second condition to identify; reducing it does not automatically alter the original patient's condition.

For a patient who can communicate, their testimony can constrain the simulation, with its own reliability and interpretation assessed. For a patient who cannot, the information obligation remains: independently warranted observation, history or other evidence must support the model. The success of conversation in a particular study neither excludes noncommunicating patients nor licenses inventing their experiences. Uncertainty about evidence is also different from absence of standing.

Passing these contrasts would support empirical and semantic fidelity. The positive normative route further needs a justified rule explaining why the identified ground supplies a reason to the actual actor. It must remain possible to criticize a vivid, compassionate but mistaken judgment. Conversely, lack of intense empathic emotion cannot by itself refute a warranted claim. This makes perspective-taking a candidate means of discovery and understanding within the generative root, with its calibration and practical scope exposed rather than assumed.
