# A universal ethical minimum and the room to live a life

6 October 2026. AI-authored supporting study for the continuing objective: **move toward establishing a universal ethical minimum compatible with plural worthwhile lives**. The named connection is **universal patient-grounded contributory reasons → differentiated requirements and justified latitude for finite, differentiated beings**. This study extends the [practical-uptake argument](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-ARGUMENT.md), [alignment axis](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/ALIGNMENT-AXIS.md), and [strongest foundation claim](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/STRONGEST-FOUNDATION-CLAIM.md). It does not resolve the still-open ground of the patient-to-stranger relation by assuming that relation here.

**Result:** universal admission of patient-grounded reasons does not entail a duty to maximize relief. It also does not, by itself, secure legitimate personal latitude. The positive route to latitude is to identify goods of the actor's life that are real independently of the actor's usefulness to others, including warranted goods of self-direction where those exist; count the cumulative effects of demands on those goods; and defend when patient-directed reasons have requiring force. This yields a substantive case for defeasible latitude and exact limits on two proposed shortcuts. It does not yet establish a universally calibrated boundary between aid and self-directed life.

**最低关怀并不等于无限征用：他人的真实负担可以向我提出理由，而我的生活也有自身的真实好坏。承认两者之后，仍须论证何种理由足以构成要求、哪些余地可以正当保留，以及累积要求会怎样改变整个生命的可能性。** This is this study's formulation, not a quotation from Ren.

## 1. What compatibility with plurality asks for

A universal minimum should be capable of condemning genuine wrongs while leaving more than one worthwhile way of living available in circumstances that can support them. Plurality means a substantively defensible range of lives, relationships, inquiries, practices and commitments. It does not mean that every preference is legitimate, that nobody can be required to help, or that every agent must always retain every option.

There are three different claims:

| Claim | What must be established |
|---|---|
| Logical compatibility | The universal minimum and some genuine personal latitude can both hold without contradiction |
| Ethical justification | The latitude is supported by actual goods and justified normative principles, rather than granted by an arbitrary cap on concern |
| Feasible realization | Available resources, institutions and circumstances can maintain the justified range of lives |

A consistency example establishes only the first. A philosophical defense can advance the second. Neither guarantees the third under every catastrophe or scarcity condition. A theory promising everyone a full range of worthwhile lives in every physically possible situation would promise more than the world necessarily permits. The present objective concerns a justified ethic that protects and supports plurality where possible, including arguments for changing avoidable conditions that unnecessarily destroy it.

## 2. Universal address and mandatory optimization are separate propositions

Let `U` be the candidate already defended in the preceding studies:

> An independently warranted burden supplies some reason in favor of an eligible actor's permissible effective relief, even without private advantage or a prior special relationship.

`U` quantifies over which patients and eligible actors are within the reason's scope. It does not specify how reasons aggregate, which considerations defeat them, or when the strongest available justification becomes a requirement. Quantifier scope is not a decision rule.

For a minimal independence demonstration, consider two available acts, helping a stranger and pursuing an independently worthwhile personal undertaking. The profile includes a patient-grounded consideration favoring help and a different consideration favoring the undertaking. Hold the whole profile of contributory considerations and factual circumstances fixed; their requiring force has not yet been specified. One normative completion can make helping obligatory in this comparison; another can permit either act while retaining the reason to help. Both preserve `U`. Conversely, one can require help in a serious emergency and permit either act in another case without altering the universal scope of patient reasons.

The demonstration stipulates the respective requirement rules; it does not justify either rule. Its exact conclusion is that **`U` alone neither implies maximizing relief nor proves protected latitude**. Adding ever more instances of `U` cannot replace the missing requirement principle.

The [earlier source study](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-SOURCES.md#2-gert-patient-benefit-can-justify-without-requiring) identifies Gert's distinction between justifying and requiring strength. This study uses that conceptual distinction without adopting a thesis that aid can never be required. The source note, rather than a new primary reading, is the basis for this attribution.

## 3. The actor cannot disappear from universal concern

When the actor is also a patient, the actor has actual goods and burdens. The following conditional consequence is immediate:

1. Universal concern admits every patient of the warranted class.
2. The actor belongs to that class and is affected by the available response.
3. Therefore the response must be assessed with the actor's patient-grounded considerations included.

This excludes an interpretation that counts every burden except those imposed on the current helper merely because they are the helper. It supports relief, rest and protection for an exhausted caregiver for the same kind of patient-grounded reason that supports care for someone else. Self-care need not be justified solely as maintenance of an aid-producing resource.

The admitted considerations retain the baseline's exact scope. A principle concerning existing unwanted suffering admits the helper's existing unwanted suffering. Extending it to the prevention of future burdens, or to positive goods beyond relief, requires an additional warranted premise. Those extensions must not enter unnoticed through the phrase “the actor also counts.”

The conclusion is admission, not equal weights, special priority or immunity. A maximizing theory can include the actor's welfare and still require large sacrifices when other benefits outweigh it. Therefore **“the actor also counts” does not finish the argument for latitude**. Nor does this step establish goods of authorship, projects or agency from suffering alone; those require their own identification and defense.

The distinction matters for alignment. A tool's unused compute is not automatically a patient's leisure. Whether an artificial system is a patient, has a self-directed life, or can bear the goods discussed here remains a separate question. User and third-party goods can nevertheless justify limits on a tool's activities and the resources it consumes. We must not create an agent's personal prerogatives merely by calling it an actor.

## 4. A positive ground beyond avoiding exhaustion

An ethic could prevent the helper's extreme suffering while treating every painless moment of their life as available for compulsory service. Showing that such an arrangement is objectionable requires more than a concern for suffering. The candidate additional ground is:

> For beings capable of self-direction, having and exercising a real range of worthwhile possibilities for a life can itself be good for them. Its significance need not consist entirely in relief delivered to someone else.

This includes forming and revising commitments, learning something worth understanding, sustaining reciprocal relationships, and developing practices whose merit can survive the absence of a relief payoff. It does not identify value with whatever the person happens to choose. A project of domination acquires no sufficient justification merely because it is deeply chosen. The case for a particular undertaking must identify its merits and its effects on others. Foregone assistance remains a consideration to assess; it is not removed by naming an activity a project.

Three considerations support this proposed ground.

First, some goods concern the person's actual participation, understanding and relationships. Another person's production of an equivalent aggregate output need not replace them. A friend's relationship with someone else does not, merely by matching an aggregate social score, give this person the friendship they lack. The differentiation of bearers is part of the good's identity.

Second, some goods are partly constituted by how the activity is conducted. A person understanding and revising a commitment is different from a controller assigning the person an outwardly similar sequence. A physical account may explain both sequences accurately while preserving that difference. Identifying the first as a good is a further normative judgment; the descriptive difference alone does not prove its value.

Third, reducing these goods to productive utility would exclude cases in which their particular worth remains while that utility vanishes: an unrecorded conversation, learning without an audience, or a harmless practice that will never improve another person's welfare. The affirmative judgment is that such activities can still be worthwhile for those living them. This is a substantive evaluative argument, open to challenge, rather than a theorem from biological persistence.

These considerations favor recognizing independent goods of lived activity and self-direction. They do not yet establish an absolute right of control, a complete list of worthwhile projects, or a fixed protected budget. In the derivation map, this is a proposed **patient-good identification beyond relief**, followed by the separate question of its force against competing claims. It must not be recorded as a consequence of the relief principle alone.

The coordinated [primary-source comparison](ETHICAL-MINIMUM-SOURCES.md#2-cullity-examine-the-grounds-of-beneficence-and-total-burden) adds a sharper consistency test from Cullity's *Asking Too Much*. Some goods that make assistance matter include the patient's relationships and worthwhile pursuits. If a demand prohibits every instance of such a pursuit merely because it does not maximize assistance, it must explain how making those same pursuits available grounds beneficence. This challenges a blanket dismissal of their value or legitimacy. It does not refute a theory that acknowledges their value while requiring selective sacrifice for greater gains. Nor does welfare value by itself make a pursuit permissible; both the alleged total prohibition and the assistance ground need careful specification.

The [source reviewer](ETHICAL-MINIMUM-SOURCES.md) inspected Cullity's complete article and identifies cumulative rather than isolated sacrifice on printed 413–415, with an explicitly undefended threshold starting point on 414. This study relies on that delegated reading, not a second direct source inspection. The resulting support is for preserving the helper's actual goods and assessing total demands, not a discovered numerical boundary. Wolf's complementary comparison in the same note supports evaluating a life through worthwhile pursuits as well as further altruistic improvement; it does not itself supply the missing priority rule.

## 5. The further case for defeasible latitude

Recognizing the actor's goods supplies countervailing considerations. Why might it also support permission to pursue a worthwhile life when more relief could be produced by choosing otherwise?

The positive proposal is that the relevance of each patient is grounded in that being's own life. A requirement to help should accordingly account for what it takes from the helper's actual life as well as what it contributes to the recipient's. Relating each person to all affected others should not silently transform the helper's goods into goods that count only after everyone else's needs are exhausted. That asymmetry needs a ground beyond the bare fact that another benefit is achievable.

This supports the following **candidate latitude principle**:

> Where an actor has independently warranted goods of a self-directed life, preserving a real range of worthwhile pursuits supplies a defeasible justification for retaining control over time, attention and activity. A patient-directed benefit can justify giving up part of that range. Making its surrender mandatory requires a further sufficient ground, assessed against the actor's actual goods, other claims and feasible alternatives across the relevant history.

The principle does not say that the actor's current wish decides the matter, that baseline concern has zero requiring force, or that the largest sacrifice is never obligatory. Its positive ground is the actor's warranted life, including goods of authorship where those are present. Its scope depends on that ground. The permission becomes weaker or inapplicable when the purported project lacks merit, consumes unjustly held resources, breaks independently valid responsibilities, or faces an overriding justified claim.

There is a genuine additional normative commitment here: the actor's goods can support **permission**, and a reason to produce more benefit does not automatically defeat it. Their existence as goods does not logically prove this force. The defense is that actual subjects have lives to lead as well as benefits to receive and confer, and that requiring surrender of the activity through which those lives are realized calls for sufficient justification. A maximizing rival can acknowledge the same goods and deny the proposed permission. The difference must be assessed on these substantive grounds, not settled by describing the rival as inconsistent.

The resulting latitude is principled without being fully calibrated. It tracks identifiable goods and changes when those goods, burdens or claims change. A fixed percentage of disposable income, a fixed daily rescue quota, or a permission triggered merely by fatigue would require additional argument. None is supplied here.

### Two objections that must remain live

**An altruistic life can be self-authored.** Obligations, external reasons and sustained aid do not automatically erase agency. Someone can reflectively endorse a demanding vocation and live well through it. The alleged harm is an actual foreclosure of warranted possibilities or goods, not the fact that help occupies much of the life. The argument cannot manufacture a loss of authorship by declaring every other-regarding requirement foreign to the self.

**Opportunity costs are often morally relevant.** A peaceful personal project may have real value while a rescue claim outweighs it. The proposal does not license ignoring such costs or guarantee that an attractive personal option remains permitted in every emergency. Its target is an unjustified conversion of all possible beneficial action into compulsory action, not every demanding moral conclusion.

## 6. A finite life is not a fresh resource at every request

Suppose each of many assistance opportunities costs a small amount of time. Considered against an isolated moment, each may look easy. Considered as one history, the requests can consume rest, relationships, sustained inquiry and the possibility of any continuing project. The difference is real even when no single request crosses a dramatic threshold.

Let `F` be the set of physically feasible histories for the actor over a specified horizon. For proposed categorical requirements `D₁, …, Dₙ`, let `Sᵢ ⊆ F` be the histories that satisfy requirement `Dᵢ`. Under the independently supplied principle that joint categorical requirements must admit joint compliance, an adequate assignment must satisfy:

\[
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n} S_i \ne \varnothing.
\]

Separate individual feasibility, `Sᵢ ≠ ∅` for every i, does not establish that condition. A person with one available hour can individually perform either of two hour-long rescues before the same deadline, while being unable to perform both. Universal reasons for both rescues do not, by themselves, imply two jointly performable obligations. One needs a defensible allocation, alternative requirement, priority judgment, or account of unavoidable failure. This study uses the joint-compliance principle; it does not settle theories that allow genuine moral dilemmas with jointly unsatisfiable obligations.

Joint feasibility is only the first test. A history can be feasible yet destroy the actor's life as a bearer of independent goods. Those goods must be assessed at the same historical scale as the demands. Restoring one day's physical energy does not replace a relationship lost through months of permanent availability. A new requester does not reset earlier costs to zero.

This produces a concrete requirement on any proposed account of duties: **when individually small interventions accumulate, the assessment must retain the history that changes their actual burdens, opportunity costs, commitments and feasible alternatives**. A threshold or requirement that ignores those changes is applying its own premises to the wrong case.

This is not a proof that there is always a moral stopping point before serious sacrifice. A widespread disaster can leave all feasible histories grievous. It establishes that cumulative sacrifice must be justified as cumulative sacrifice. Repeating a small-cost argument while discarding its accumulated effects is not such a justification.

## 7. Why aggregation remains an explicit additional bridge

Including the actor's independent goods does not guarantee robust latitude under simple maximizing rules. Consider a stylized comparison in which the actor retains a good of finite assessed magnitude `g > 0`, or instead relinquishes it to confer a benefit assessed as `b > 0` on each of `N` patients. Suppose all effects are identified, the magnitudes are commensurable, the benefits sum without limit, and maximizing that sum is mandatory. Then the maximizing rule requires the sacrifice whenever:

\[
Nb > g.
\]

For any finite g and positive b there is an integer N for which that inequality holds. This elementary consequence is conditional on additive measurement, replication of the benefit profile and mandatory maximization. The reason principle `U` supplies none of those three assumptions. The construction is not evidence that actual populations or interventions have that profile.

Nor is the conclusion automatically objectionable: saving many people can justify great sacrifice. Its methodological significance is that **universal admission, aggregation and requiring force do different work**. An argument for preserving ordinary latitude cannot quietly accept unlimited additive requiring force and then assume actor inclusion has protected the actor. Conversely, rejecting unlimited aggregation does not establish that numbers never matter.

A stronger protection against sacrificing a whole life for arbitrarily many individually minor advantages would require an independently defended principle concerning severity, aggregation, individual claims or authority. The latitude principle in §5 supplies an initial defense of a permission; this study does not claim to have completed all of those comparisons. Replacing them with a numerical cap would hide the unresolved bridge.

## 8. Institutions and responsibilities can make plurality feasible

Universal concern can support differentiated responsibility. Requiring everyone to intervene independently may duplicate costs, undermine competent care, or consume the conditions of further useful action. Coordination can protect patients while leaving actors a genuine range of lives.

Several reasons favor a warranted allocation: relevant competence, actual capacity, avoidance of harmful duplication, continuity of care, prior responsibility and commitments, and the need to make enduring relationships and plans possible. These factors can explain why different people should do different things without making the unassigned patients worthless.

The ethical authority of the allocation remains to be defended. A fair-share label does not make a burden fair; the distribution of resources, existing disadvantages, dependants and access to alternatives matter. Having completed one's share does not necessarily defeat a newly encountered urgent rescue claim. Others' wrongful refusal does not automatically authorize unlimited transfer of their duties onto the conscientious person. Both transitions require a further assessment.

The constructive direction is therefore to reduce avoidable conflict between concern and plural life: prevent recurring emergencies where possible, distribute burdens through justified arrangements, preserve competence, and keep correction available when the arrangement excludes actual patients. These are conditional practical implications once the relevant goods and claims are warranted. A system's survival or an institution's efficiency cannot certify their legitimacy.

## 9. Discriminating cases

These are conceptual comparisons and proposed evaluation cases, not performed behavioral experiments or evidence of universal moral truth.

| Case | What the account should preserve | What remains to justify |
|---|---|---|
| A stranger's severe suffering can be relieved without relevant sacrifice or opposing claim | A genuine patient reason under the candidate baseline | Whether the reason makes relief required; the baseline alone settles neither requirement nor blame |
| A helper's health is harmed by continued assistance | The helper's actual burden inside the patient class | Priority between these and other patients' burdens |
| Every painless hour is assigned to useful service | Potential losses of independently worthwhile activity and self-direction | The reality and force of those goods beyond a suffering-only standard |
| A person reflectively chooses a demanding caring vocation | The possibility that care realizes that person's worthwhile life | Whether particular sacrifices are authorized, informed and compatible with other claims |
| Many individually small requests erase a continuing project | The cumulative history, including actual foregone goods | When the resulting reason supports refusal and when a stronger claim overrides it |
| A lucrative personal project is exploitative | Chosen commitment alone does not confer legitimacy | Independently warranted constraints and the actual effects on victims |
| A person invokes their fair share while alone beside a preventable emergency | Prior contributions without treating them as automatic immunity | The additional current claim and the true costs of response |
| A system reduces suffering by forcing everyone into one chosen lifestyle | Actual patients, consent and independently grounded goods of agency | The agency and authority principles that a relief metric omits |
| An AI invokes its own leisure to abandon an authorized task | No automatic equation of computational capacity with patient welfare or authorship | Whether this system has relevant standing; otherwise assess the affected users and third parties |
| Catastrophe leaves no feasible option preserving everyone's projects | A tragic conflict without declaring the sacrificed goods unreal | Which costly response is justified and what repair remains possible |

Passing a supplied test suite would show conformity to supplied judgments. It would not derive those judgments, establish subjective experience, or close the patient-to-stranger bridge.

| Joint categorical requirements must admit joint compliance | Explicit normative consistency premise for §6; theories of genuine dilemmas require separate treatment |

| A comparative reason establishes coercive authority or culpability | Rejected without additional premises about claims, authority, knowledge and control |

The study imports no benevolent reward or selection law. It makes the additional ethical judgments explicit so their role can be challenged rather than concealed in a controller's scoring function.

## 11. What changes in the foundation and what could overturn it

The new result is an articulated connection between patient-grounded universal concern and differentiated lives. Universal concern cannot consistently exclude the helper from the admitted patient class. A sequence of claims cannot be assessed by repeatedly deleting the history that changes its burdens. But these two constraints still leave room for maximizing theories; a defended protection for plurality requires additional grounds concerning worthwhile activity and the force of reasons.

The positive candidate is therefore **universal relevance of actual patients together with defeasible protection for the actual goods through which differentiated beings live**, followed by justified requirements that remain answerable to both. This provides a route to a universal ethical minimum that does not treat every life as merely a means of delivering benefits. It does not claim that every conflict is soluble, every burden is small, or every good is equally weighty.

Three findings would materially change this account. A successful argument that the identified authorship goods have only instrumental value would remove one proposed ground of latitude. An independently defended maximizing requirement could override the proposed permission in a wider class of cases than we currently judge appropriate. A justified reason to restrict patient-to-stranger address would narrow the universal baseline itself. All are substantive possibilities; consistency of a stipulated rival is insufficient to establish any one of them.

The next new bridge is **from independently identified goods and contributory reasons to justified requiring strength in a complete finite-history comparison**. It should begin with a serious rescue claim, actual cumulative costs, a genuine worthwhile alternative, and independently specified authority. Compare a demanding but justified intervention, an optional sacrifice, and an impermissible imposition without defining the verdict into “worthwhile,” “reasonable” or “eligible.” That comparison would advance a requirement theory; another repetition that suffering matters would not.
