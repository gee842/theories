## 2. Proof acceptance

**A1: ideal completion and compact basis.** The use of nonempty directed lower sets is consistent throughout. Principal ideals are nonempty and directed; their inclusion reflects exactly the original order. An inclusion-directed union of ideals is again a nonempty directed lower set. Each principal is compact because membership of its generator in the union supplies one witnessing ideal. Conversely, every ideal is the directed union of its principal subideals, so a compact ideal must be principal. These arguments establish both compact generation and the absence of additional compact generators.

The further assertion about an existing finite nonempty join in the basis is correct. An ideal containing every member of the finite set has an internal common upper bound; lower closure therefore includes their least upper bound in the basis. This argument does not cover arbitrary old directed suprema, and the note explicitly preserves that limit.

**A2: free continuous extension.** For monotone `f`, the image of each ideal is nonempty and directed, so its supremum exists in the codomain. Both sides of the asserted continuity equation are suprema of the same collection of finite-image values. The principal value is `f(p)` because it is the maximum of its principal image. Uniqueness follows from directed generation of each ideal by principals. No effective computation of a supremum is implied.

**A2a: positive finite-content readings.** The indicator of `p ≤ q` is monotone into the ordered two-element domain. Its extension is one exactly when some member of the ideal extends `p`, equivalently when `p` belongs to the ideal. These readings separate different ideals. The proof concerns this positive observation family, not all sentences about the completed carrier. In particular, the indicator of an infinite word is not continuous: it is zero on every finite prefix and one at their supremum.

**A3: characterization of the whole completion.** The two maps `x ↦ {p : e(p) ≤ x}` and `I ↦ sup e[I]` have the declared domains. Order embedding and lower closure give one inclusion for their composite. Compactness supplies the converse by locating a witnessing member of the directed image. Directed generation gives the other inverse equation. Any isomorphism preserving the basis must preserve its directed suprema, so it is the displayed one. The converse uses A1. This is unique realization of a specified structural role, with no remaining arbitrary completed point choices.

The independence examples are valid. With `P = D = N ∪ {ω}` and identity embedding, directed generation and completeness hold but the named `ω` is not compact. The ideals `N` and `N ∪ {ω}` then have the same supremum, defeating the asserted one-to-one recovery. Embedding a singleton basis as zero of a two-element chain instead preserves compactness and order but leaves the upper point outside generation by that basis. Both examples identify an actual omitted condition rather than freely toggling an interpretation label.

**A4: word ideals.** Two elements of a prefix-ordered ideal have a common word extension and hence are comparable. A bounded-length ideal is principal; an unbounded-length ideal determines one consistent symbol at every position. Lower closure ensures all its finite prefixes are present. The reverse constructions give ideals, and their orders agree. No finite alphabet assumption is needed for this classification.

**A5: subset ideals.** Every subset determines the ideal of all its finite subsets. Conversely, directedness combines the finitely many members witnessing a finite subset of an ideal's union; lower closure admits that finite subset. Nonemptiness ensures the empty subset is included. Thus the inverse maps are correct even when the underlying set is empty or uncountable.

**Effective admission.** Computably produced increasing finite sets yield exactly computably enumerable subsets of the natural numbers. The diagonal subset given in the note is not computably enumerable. Its finite sections exist in the stated background mathematics without forming a computably supplied sequence. This distinguishes unrestricted mathematical chain admission from effective enumeration without claiming that a completion as a whole cannot have a finite description.

## 3. What “conservative” can mean here

The canonical account correctly limits preservation to the finite approximation order, its stated finite-order consequences, and interpretations satisfying the monotonicity and directed-continuity conditions. It does not claim an elementary embedding or preservation of every finitely written formula. A short quantified formula can change when the carrier expands: all finite unary words have proper extensions, whereas the completed infinite word is maximal.

Even an existing directed join in the basis can change under the principal embedding. In `N ∪ {ω}`, the union of the natural-number principals is the ideal `N`, strictly below the principal of the old `ω`. The construction treats every named basis element as compact; it is not simultaneously an automatic preservation of old noncompact limit identities.

The right-append warning is also essential and correct. From the empty word being a prefix of `0`, appending `1` gives `1` and `01`, which are not prefix-comparable. The earlier chronological action-word theorem therefore does not automatically instantiate A2. Prefixing a fixed word is monotone on the full word language; restricted languages additionally require closure under it. Temporal execution and information refinement are different relations whose correspondence must be demonstrated.

These limitations prevent a false inference that the extension theorem already reconstructs every mathematical predicate or truth. Ordinary semantic interpretation remains a separate part of the [root-frontier account](MATHEMATICAL-ROOT-FRONTIER-MATH.md).

## 4. Mathematical completion and concrete history

The countdown example correctly preserves a substantive native law. The final proof and canonical presentations begin from a single initial configuration that makes an infinitely branching choice of finite budget. The proof also retains the equivalent variant whose initial possibilities are the arbitrarily large finite post-choice budgets. These are equivalent ways of locating the omitted consequential choice. Every finite projected action prefix is possible, but every actual chosen budget supports only finitely many actions. The ideal infinite word is consequently not a concrete run of that countdown law.

The positive lifting theorem A4b is sound in the stated classical background. A finite nonempty initial set and finite successor sets for each required label make the tree of matching, prefix-closed admitted finite concrete histories finitely branching. Existence of every prefix gives arbitrarily deep nodes. Repeatedly choosing a child with unbounded-depth descendants produces a branch. The budget example violates finite initial choice in one presentation and finite branching at the initial choice in the other. The final proof explicitly preserves all modeled finite-prefix conditions while excluding an unsupported claim about conditions arising only at an infinite limit.

The conclusion is existence of a coherent run under the complete supplied transition relation. It is not observation of an enacted infinite history, an effective selection algorithm, or a proof that actual physical laws remain correct forever. Conditions represented only at an infinite limit are not established merely because all finite-prefix conditions are met. The [source companion](MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSIBILITY-SOURCES.md) preserves this last restriction explicitly.

Under the nondual method, the failed infinite-run inference identifies an omission in a projection: it forgets which finite budget was chosen. It is not evidence that the fuller process intrinsically separates knowing from activity, nor that no mathematical idealization is legitimate.

## 5. Is the philosophical admission argument more than a restatement?

Yes, at its declared relative scope. The canonical argument begins with an independently intelligible purpose: represent unrestricted coherent approximation while preserving the basis and admitting no extra points beyond its profiles. The requirements articulate that purpose without listing the individual completed objects or their full atomic tables. A construction proves the role realizable in the background mathematics; the characterization proves that every realization preserving it is the same subject up to the specified isomorphism. The interpretation-extension theorem then constrains how its values can depend on finite approximation.

That supports introducing this mathematical subject where the completion role fits the intended inquiry. It provides a constitutive structural explanation of its identity: no further hidden bearer has to be selected to distinguish two otherwise role-identical completions. This is a defended interpretation accompanying the mathematics. Unique role realization alone does not prove that the role is the uniquely appropriate one for every mathematical subject or the uniquely necessary metaphysical basis of truth.

The canonical account does not use absence of finite exclusion as a proof of mandatory admission. It explicitly allows finite-only, effective, physically realized or otherwise restricted subjects where their intended roles justify those restrictions. Such alternatives fail the full directed-completion contract, but can be mathematically and explanatorily legitimate under a different contract. The account first defends the proposed subject's role; it does not use the resulting uniqueness theorem to establish that unrestricted role by circular inference.

The location of infinity is especially clear in the subset example. For each external subset `S`, its family of finite subsets is directed, and full directed completeness requires its supremum. The resulting power-set scope therefore uses precisely the all-families admission premise. It does not derive powerset strength from finite unions alone. Ordinary set-based construction of the entire ideal domain also employs the surrounding mathematics. These dependencies are substantive and acknowledged; being locally supplied does not put them outside generative reality.
