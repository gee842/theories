# Why agreement can change practical authority

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored affirmative argument and counterreview continuing [prospective control](PERSPECTIVAL-PROSPECTIVE-CONTROL-INTEGRATION.md), under the [nondual method and terminology correction](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md#keep-foundational-language-at-the-right-level). **Scope:** philosophical explanation and constructed discriminators; no mathematical theorem, empirical finding or completed universal proof.

**Finding:** agreement can partly constitute a worthwhile way of arranging a shared continuation, rather than merely announce preferences or predict cooperation. This supports a candidate reason for fidelity, while the full directed duty and power of release still need explanation. It supports legitimate finality as well as change. It does not yet establish a universal limit on deliberately accepted subordination: the account must defend which forms of arranging shared life have this favorable character and which powers they can confer.

## 1. The universal inference and the proposed ground

[Elective concern §§2–4](ELECTIVE-CONCERN-ARGUMENT.md#2-four-different-things-voluntary-choice-can-do) already allows genuine normative novelty and distinguishes a power's warrant from its being merely a trigger for antecedent reasons. This is inherited. The new test concerns whether a specified shared relation explains its directed authority when reliance losses are absent.

The inference remains **whole qualitative realization → differentiated practical force**. The object is not an independent will creating norms from nowhere. Through communication, response and subsequent conduct, an unfolding practice can acquire a specified direction that its participants have actually undertaken together. Their differentiated capacities and positions form within the same history. This formation can change what someone has reason to do, whose demand has standing, and what a release would accomplish.

The candidate favorable quality is **participation in actually settling how a shared continuation will proceed**. It differs from merely obtaining an agreeable outcome. Participants can select one among several permissible arrangements; its becoming their actual undertaking can supply a reason that the equally attractive unselected arrangement lacks. The proposal therefore grants agreement genuine normative work instead of treating it as evidence for a completely antecedent ranking.

Why favor carrying out this arrangement? Because continuation responsive to what was undertaken can realize the particular relation being formed: the contribution of others to determining the shared activity becomes effective, and each can organize further conduct through that settled relation. Disregarding its specification can impair this realization even where the substituted outcome has similar useful properties. This is a substantive account of fidelity's point. It does not derive that point from causal coordination alone or assume that every announced arrangement deserves realization.

## 2. A discriminator that does not reduce agreement to induced loss

Consider collaborators who jointly settle which of two otherwise permissible designs to complete. Both know an available fallback will protect material resources and prevent interrupted livelihood if the selected design is abandoned. They understand this protection and nonetheless undertake the selected design as their shared work.

One collaborator can privately replace the selected design with the equally useful alternative. The fallback prevents the identified material losses. Does the absence of those losses erase every reason to follow the agreement or seek a release?

**Affirmative judgment:** no. There can remain a reason concerning the actual shared determination and its faithful continuation. Jointly settling the design has changed the practical relation; the alternative's equal utility does not itself realize that settlement. A request to reconsider can acknowledge this ground without making release compulsory. This judgment need not imply decisive obligation where unforeseen burdens arise.

The rival says all justification lies in independently valuable consequences or preferences, so once these are matched nothing remains. It must then explain why the actual agreement's specification could matter except as an instrument or a desired object. Our candidate identifies another proposed good: how the activity proceeds through a determination its participants made effective together. The two accounts disagree about that actual relationship, not about an independently toggled obligation.

This test narrows a merely loss-based explanation. It does not prove that all consequences or ethical goods are matched: the affirmative expressly identifies a relational difference. A rival that counts that difference as a consequence may agree substantively. The disagreement is over its favorable character and practical force, not the vocabulary of consequences.

**Exact sufficiency limit:** a favorable relational aspect does not yet establish a duty owed to this collaborator, that collaborator's particular power to release it, or priority over contrary reasons. Those would require explaining how the actual settlement entrusts a specified part of the continuation to particular participants and why that entrusting has the claimed practical force. A general reason to preserve worthwhile shared activity would not by itself supply this differentiated authority. Encoding those powers into “shared settlement” and then extracting them would leave their warrant assumed.

## 3. Stable commitments, finality and chosen subordination

If the account required participants to retain maximum choice at every later moment, it would undermine its proposed ground. Settling a continuation can matter precisely because some questions cease to be open for routine reconsideration. A fixed performance date, a final allocation of scarce rehearsal time or an irreversible completion can be warranted. The historical undertaking can continue supplying a reason after present enthusiasm or one original instrumental purpose ends.

A stronger countercase is deliberate subordination. Participants might knowingly entrust an expedition's navigation decisions to one leader for a specified period, accept asymmetric control, and undertake to follow decisions they later dislike. They can value reliable coordination and the experience of this disciplined relation. Their contribution to arranging the future can consist in granting discretion, rather than retaining an equal vote on each action. Later unilateral disagreement does not automatically restore the relinquished choice.

The account should accept such a case when its substantive conditions are warranted. It cannot declare that any loss of future discretion negates the value of participation. Nor can it assume that every valid delegation requires a continuously usable personal veto. Some finality and constraints on reconsideration may be constitutive of what was legitimately settled.

The harder rival extends this explanation to enduring allegiance whose point includes submission itself. It argues that informed adoption can confer broad authority even after productive cooperation or material return is complete. This is a genuine extension of the affirmative's reason-creating proposal. Rejecting it requires a defended limit on the kind or extent of relation that may be established; simply repeating the value of arranging one's continuation does not suffice, because the rival claims to instantiate that value.

A lifetime confidentiality undertaking provides a further control. Entrusted material can remain sensitive long after active collaboration ends, and a warranted promise can continue protecting that relation. Indefinite duration and foregone opportunities alone do not discredit it. The actual content, exceptions, surviving ground and legitimate release still require assessment; perpetual restriction is neither automatically immunity nor automatically valid.

## 4. Finality is different from immunity of authority

**Finality** settles a question through an independently warranted history. It can rule out repeated reopening, even when present preference changes. **Immunity** would make the authority's current declaration sufficient to establish the validity of its own scope, regardless of the relationship alleged to justify that scope. These are different claims.

The distinction does not entail that every power must be freshly defended before each exercise. A stable historical ground, a justified allocation of discretion or a legitimate procedure can settle the relevant matter. Nor does a justified result require an unlimited channel of appeal: repeated challenge can destroy a worthwhile completion, and some questions may properly be closed.

But a procedure established to settle navigation has not thereby established that it can decide every unrelated issue. If it can legitimately determine parts of its own jurisdiction, that further discretion must belong to the actual justified relation. Its mere success in maintaining compliance does not establish it. The claim concerns normative scope, not which challenges must be heard at every moment or who has permission to intervene.

This provides a real but limited constraint. Agreement can establish a ground that outlasts current wishes; a current declaration cannot alone substitute for that ground. Yet it does **not** prove the guild's disputed enduring veto invalid. A rival may assert that the initial undertaking itself conferred the breadth now exercised. We must assess that asserted ground rather than call every broad historical settlement self-certification.

## 5. Positive limits and the strongest remaining objection

The proposed explanation gives a reason to distinguish continuation of a worthwhile undertaking from using the resulting relation as a license for otherwise ungrounded interference. Its positive basis is the realization that actually justified settling the relation, including the participants' differentiated lives, entrusted matters, burdens and claims beyond the agreement. Scope is therefore contentful: agreement to a joint completion is not automatically agreement to unrelated demands.

Where an arrangement explicitly includes the disputed demands, however, interpreting its content is no longer enough. The affirmative must explain why such a relation should not receive the claimed authority. It may appeal to the actual ordinary living it obstructs, treatment of people beyond its legitimate reach, or a substantive limit on permanently surrendering particular forms of participation. Those are ethical grounds to defend; they do not follow merely because agreement has a history.

The strongest objection is that we have praised participants' power to make a chosen future practically binding, then withdrawn that power when its chosen content displeases us. The reply must identify a difference in ethical realization, not merely a less attractive choice. Some chosen losses, restrictions and endings are part of worthwhile continuations. No general axiom of expanding capacities, increasing options or preserving every current form is admitted.

**Adjudication:** the shared-arrangement explanation supports new practical authority and its differentiation more directly than a model of agreement as information about preferences. It also supports respecting legitimate finality. Its general ceiling remains unproved. The previous ordinary-use-veto judgment therefore retains its particular substantive defense; it is not newly derived from this explanation of agreement.

## 6. Normative inputs and revision conditions

Three proposed ethical premises carry the argument: an actual shared determination can have favorable significance beyond preventing disappointment; faithfully continuing a warranted determination can supply a directed reason after preferences change; and that reason's scope depends on the ethically justified relation, including genuine commitments formed through it. Their common constitution within the whole is the explanatory proposal, not a proof of their necessity.

Revise the candidate if the supposedly shared determination never occurred, if the specified practice assigns no such settling role to the exchange, or if fulfillment loses every alleged relational good once other actual stakes are held fixed. Revise its practical scope where a valid release, justified delegation or final settlement changes what is now owed. Accept a chosen-subordination case if the affirmative cannot identify a relevant adverse difference without imposing a blanket preference for independence.

The result changes the next obligation: investigate the value and limits of **the actual relation created by informed undertaking**, rather than repeat another harmful term or another explanation of preference formation. Universality would require this ground to address every qualifying intelligence through its actual role, without requiring identical duties or present endorsement. That scope, and the ethical constitution of the shared realization itself, remain to be established.
