# Patient-ground after fair allocation

6 October 2026. AI-authored counterreview of **a patient's continuing local badness → prospective moral favor for an eligible independent actor's response**. This continues the [universal ethical minimum](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md), with no new axiom, formal result, empirical observation or claim of necessary grounding.

**Finding:** fulfillment of a fair-chance claim can settle one allocation without extinguishing the patient-ground for a newly available, independently permissible relief opportunity. Three nearby cases distinguish that affirmative judgment from reopening the allocation or taking its winner's resource. This is a new application of inherited distinctions, not a new general theory of reasons. Genuine moral disqualification remains a serious possibility; the review does not reduce it to ordinary weighting.

## 1. The inherited boundary

The [earlier self-ground audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#23-procedural-standing-without-a-right-to-win) distinguishes procedural standing from entitlement to a favorable outcome. Its preceding section already explains why including a patient's interests in a scarce allocation does not guarantee that patient's receipt. [Evaluative application](../../../../theories/shengsheng-buxi-generative-existence/unified-2026-10-05/EVALUATIVE-APPLICATION.md#correct-command-withdrawn-application) likewise distinguishes fulfillment of a specified service from the absence of continuing need or other grounds. Neither distinction originates here.

The [assistance-jurisdiction review](ASSISTANCE-JURISDICTION-COUNTERREVIEW.md) already admits that a consideration can be disqualified from justifying a particular choice. The [constitutive closure audit](ETHICAL-CONSTITUTION-CLOSURE.md#6-the-formal-interface-needs-a-declared-meaning-of-current-justification) identifies the consequence for C2: an abstract patient value with no warranted prospective application cannot substitute for the intended practical justification. Our task is to apply that challenge to a concrete history without confusing the object of the allegedly fulfilled claim.

## 2. One lottery, three subsequent options

P and Q have independently identified severe, still-unwanted experiential burdens. One indivisible opportunity can reliably relieve either person. For this comparison, grant that a fair lottery is a warranted way of allocating this opportunity and that its procedure has been properly completed. Q wins. This grants a local procedural justification for the test; it does not derive lottery fairness from equal standing or establish it as a universal allocation rule. P received the promised chance, with no guarantee of relief. P's burden continues. Participation did not waive every possible future offer of assistance.

Compare three continuations. Preserve P's patient identity, episode and unwanted burden; state each changed resource or action relation rather than pretending all three are the same option.

| Continuation | What is now proposed | The question it actually tests |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Divert Q's allocation** | Give P the opportunity already allocated to Q. | Whether taking that allocation is permitted, and how Q's claim and continuing need bear on the act. P's real benefit does not settle those questions. |
| **B. Repeat the same draw** | Reopen the completed allocation solely because P's unchanged need remains, with no new defect in the process or new relevant evidence. | Whether that need justifies reconsidering this settled procedure. Repeated draws until P wins would alter the original chance and displace Q's result. The need is not newly discovered merely because its bearer lost. |
| **C. Use an independent opportunity** | A previously uninvolved, informed actor can now relieve P through a newly available means. It does not take or delay Q's allocation, reopen the draw, or impose another burden. Permission and efficacy are independently warranted. | Whether completion of P's fair-chance claim removes even patient-directed moral favor for this new response. |

In C the actor has no helping commitment, role, private return or responsibility for the original suffering. The new means has no relevant competing claimant, hidden history of wrongful acquisition, or continuing availability commitment. These are declared conditions of a conceptual test, not conclusions obtained by calling a resource free. The actor's inquiry and complete-history costs are accounted for. No intervention is inferred to be permissible simply from a patient's silence.

## 3. The positive judgment in C

Our affirmative judgment is that C's actual relief can justify the actor's response for P's sake before the actor adopts concern. It would change an ongoing adverse experience for its bearer. Leaving the new means unused would leave that burden in place. The completed lottery changes neither this beneficial difference nor the independence of the new opportunity from Q's award.

The point is not merely that no objection has been found. Actual relief supplies a positive explanation of what would make this option worth choosing. An otherwise identical empty intervention would not have that patient-directed justification. The earlier chance was a different good with a different satisfaction condition: receiving a fair opportunity to obtain relief is not receiving relief. A receipt establishing the former therefore does not establish that the latter patient-ground has disappeared.

This judgment can preserve the finality of the first allocation. Helping through C does not require treating P as the true winner, declaring Q's selection unjust, or reopening every completed procedure whenever unmet need remains. Nor does it infer an additional right against the lottery's administrator. An independent reason for the new actor and a further directed claim against that administrator are different propositions.

This supports a limited conclusion: **the fact that P received the warranted chance does not, by itself, defeat the proposed patient justification in C**. It is a substantive judgment applied to the controlled history. It is not a deduction of C2 from C1 or a proof that every possible procedural history permits this response. A stronger contrary verdict would need a defended ground that covers the new opportunity, not just the settled allocation.

## 4. Preserve genuine exclusion without enlarging its scope

A serious rival may defend A or B as cases of moral exclusion rather than mere outweighing. It could maintain that a settled, justified procedure removes certain considerations from the current allocation decision. This review does not establish that such a description is false. Conversely, the continuing existence of P's suffering does not by itself show that it can presently justify undoing the award. That would repeat the very inference under review.

The rival has a further task if it denies all moral favor in C. It must explain why the authority that settles allocation of the original opportunity also governs an independently available relief option, with no injury to the winner or the procedure. Reclassifying C as another attempt to win the original lottery would change its facts. A genuine waiver, incompatible undertaking or justified rule covering the new resource would instead be a different case requiring assessment of that ground and scope.

The inherited [Adams comparison](ASSISTANCE-JURISDICTION-SOURCES.md#1-adams-an-exclusion-can-affect-justification-itself) matters here. His account concerns genuine justificatory exclusion within a specified choice, not merely motivation or less attention. Its restrictions are narrow and can themselves be reconsidered. His suffering-animal example also retains reasons concerning other responses. This supports neither blanket patient exclusion nor an automatic inference that every excluded ground survives for the very same choice.

An institutional justification and an actor's overall moral justification must also be kept distinct. A patient's benefit might be inadmissible as evidence of guilt or as an official reason for awarding a prize, while still giving the official a moral reason to arrange assistance separately. That surviving separate response does not rescue an overbroad claim about the verdict or award itself. Equally, exclusion from that institutional judgment does not show absence of every moral reason for the actor. Which choice is under evaluation must remain fixed across the argument.

## 5. Means, taint, fulfillment and chance

The diagnostic has four practical limits.

- **Disqualified means:** if diverting Q's allocation is impermissible, A does not preserve C's independently permissible response. Permission cannot be inferred from a real benefit; prohibition alone also does not establish that every beneficial aspect is absent.
- **Moral taint:** a new opportunity procured through exploitation, coercion or a breach of trust is not C as specified. Present use might owe restoration or affect other people. A theory could also defend disqualification that remains after those effects are controlled; that would require its own argument, not dismissal as ordinary costs or acceptance merely because the history is called tainted.
- **Fulfilled claims:** completion removes support arising specifically from the completed fair-chance or service claim. It neither supplies a second instance of that same demand nor certifies that all patient-grounded considerations ended. Conversely, if actual relief has already occurred and another action would provide no further relevant benefit, the original relief comparison is no longer present.
- **Ex ante chance:** reasons to allocate chances before a draw and reasons concerning available acts after its result have different opportunities and information. C does not establish an expected-value rule, disregard fair chances, or show that a larger probability always warrants intervention. Its new response is stipulated effective; uncertainty would require a further account.

## 6. Research consequence and review scope

The new discriminating result is the contrast between preserving a completed allocation and extinguishing the beneficiary's practical significance. C supplies a positive challenge to the latter inference while A and B retain genuine constraints. This narrows one substantive jurisdiction rival; it does not discharge the patient-to-independent-actor ground.

The account would change if an independently justified moral exclusion covered C even with its declared permissions and beneficial difference intact. The needed argument must concern current moral favor for that response, not just the absence of a new entitlement to the original allocation. Conversely, defending the continued favor in C supports the minimum at this history-sensitive scope without establishing all actor scope or ultimate necessity.

Realization of the experience, C1, efficacy, permission, the procedural justification and the positive favoring judgment remain supplied or defended at their stated levels. No eligible-actor definition is expanded to mean that C2 is already true. No formal contract or scientific check is changed.
