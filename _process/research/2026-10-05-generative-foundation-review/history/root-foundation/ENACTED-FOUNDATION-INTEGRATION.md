## 2. Affirmative argument and remaining ground

An enacted inquiry provides a candidate ground for saying that some activity occurs without assuming the truth of its asserted content. Its occurrence and its content's truth are different aspects of the same episode. This distinction introduces no independent mental and physical substances. Nor does it imply that the occurrence is groundless merely because the formal description takes it as an input.

To reach the content of 生生, examine what actually participates in subsequent inquiry: available expressions, retained consequences, operative discrimination, correction and use. Where those connections are established, the earlier activity helps form the later conditions. The current source–compiler–correction history is a modest actual example under ordinary tool and record correspondence; existing joined learning examples provide richer conditional mechanisms. Neither supplies ultimate justification of all inference or all law.

The decisive unresolved move is from these particular grounds to universal necessity. Universal quantification over all meaningful inquiry is already stronger than one actual witness; universal scope over all reality is stronger again. A local ending, meanwhile, neither establishes nor refutes cessation of the whole. These differences stay inside the shared explanatory investigation rather than becoming separate ontological domains.
