**How often couplings act: settling, runaway and its rate (P123, 16 October 2026).** P120 left three questions in what-a-cut-loses' next step and the chapter's §8: whether every schedule settles, whether every loop of recurring couplings makes the most difference that can arrive run away, and how fast a runaway is. New core module `Becoming.Frequency` (imports Closing; 83 theorems, 98 declarations, empty axiom reports), no premise beyond the strengths each statement names.
- **The rate.** A schedule `on` marks steps, and `Frequency.count on t` counts those before t. With the part giving no gap after the start, starting gaps at most D, and the strengths into each condition at most M at each step and nothing at unmarked steps, the carried amount at t is at most D·(1 + M)^a, a the marked steps before t (`carried_le_count`); with every condition listed, the steps at which anything couples are such a schedule (`carried_le_coupling`). A self-loop coupling with strength M at the steps of any schedule couples exactly there (`Frequency.SelfLoop.couplesAt_iff`) and carries exactly D·(1 + M)^a (`Frequency.SelfLoop.carried_eq`). The rate bounds every gap of every law with the strengths (`gap_le_count`).
- **Runaway needs frequent couplings, not strong ones.** A coupling reading any earlier level adds at least what had reached it there (`carried_step_ge_at`). A chain in time order carries what reached its start to its end (`ChainInTime`, `carried_along_in_time`); every return around a loop (`Returns`) at least doubles what had reached it (`carried_doubles_return`), and R returns multiply it by 2^R (`carried_returns`). Returns with 2^i outgrowing every polynomial in their steps run away whatever the strengths (`runaway_of_returns`); where 2^a is polynomial so is the carried amount, of degree nM (`carried_le_of_rare`); a self-loop is polynomial exactly where 2^a is, whatever its positive strength (`Frequency.SelfLoop.polynomial_iff`).
- **A recurring loop need not run away.** The sparse schedule marks 0, 1, 3, 7, … (`Frequency.Sparse`): 2^a ≤ 2(t + 1), and it marks steps as late as one likes. The self-loop coupling there recurs, so every finite order is inexact (`Frequency.Sparse.inexact`), yet it stays within D·2^M·(t + 1)^M (`Frequency.Sparse.polynomial`).
- **Settling.** A coupling that stops by N (`StopsBy`) does not recur; with every condition listed, where each coupling recurs or stops, the schedule settles (`settled_of_each`). Every schedule settles exactly where every decided two-place family has every row true somewhere or some row false throughout (`RecursOrStops`; `settled_of_recursOrStops`, `settled_iff_recursOrStops`, `settled_every_iff`). Two witnesses carry the converse. The self-loop at the first true step of a decided sequence couples at most once, and its settling decides the sequence (`Frequency.First`, `lpo_of_settled`). The self-loop reading level s once every row up to s has been true somewhere recurs exactly where every row is (`Frequency.Rows.recurs_iff`). The dichotomy gives `LPO` (`RecursOrStops.lpo`).
- **Instances.** P118's self-loop couples at every step, and P122's onset at the steps from T (`Frequency.SelfLoop.always_carried`, `Frequency.SelfLoop.onset_amount`).
- **Readings (the AI's, pending Ren).** A runaway is set by how often couplings act, not by how strong they are; strength sets only the degree. Ren called the AI's reading of "only a loop makes a difference run away" right, a reading that left couplings at only some steps open; that case now closes both ways. Only a loop of the couplings that go on can make it run away, and such a loop need not; for a self-loop, how often it couples decides exactly. That every schedule settles is a principle of reasoning, not a fact the theory asserts or denies of the unfolding.
- **Boundaries.** Natural amounts: a positive strength is at least one, and with real amounts strengths shrinking fast enough could keep frequent returns from running away. Not stated: whether, beyond the self-loop, the most difference that can arrive meets the bound by returns or the bound by coupling steps; the spectral radius of given strengths.
