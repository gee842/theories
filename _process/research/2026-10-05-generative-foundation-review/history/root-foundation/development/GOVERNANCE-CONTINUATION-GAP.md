## Recommendation as sent

The following reproduces the final recommendation verbatim; its closing statement describes that message's time of delivery.

> Recommend **strategic protection when present decisions change future oversight and resources**.
>
> The [settlement proof](../../../2026-10-05-revisable-concern/settlement/formal/REPORT.md) assumes future opportunities and contexts are independent of prior harms, admissions and records. Its protection argument then cancels equal continuation values. That leaves a concrete governance gap: suppressing correction may itself increase future control or income.
>
> A bounded deliverable would audit and extend that existing argument. For each deviation, require:
>
> \[
> \text{current protection margin}
> >
> \delta\,\text{future advantage from deviation}.
> \]
>
> Separately retain sound evidence, justified authority, funded payment prefixes and feasible renewed correction. This uses standard dynamic-game reasoning; it needs no new apparatus or benchmark and does not duplicate the live succession study.
>
> **Falsifier:** an allowed deviation yields enough future advantage—or destroys correction capacity—that the existing immediate margins no longer sustain protection.
>
> The result would identify exactly when the accepted governance mechanism survives endogenous changes in power, rather than assuming those changes away. No files written.

The proposed extension would have to identify the actual persistent decision maker, admissible state transitions and credible continuations, including off-path states. If `g(a)` is protection's current payoff advantage over deviation `a`, the relevant comparison is `g(a) > δ(E[V after a] − E[V after protection])`, or a valid upper bound on the right side. This is a prospective one-step incentive condition, not a completed equilibrium proof. An arbitrary favorable continuation function cannot certify itself.

Resource viability and strategic choice are separate. Future rents can favor a destructive deviation even while both paths remain funded. Conversely, a favorable incentive comparison cannot replace lost audit access, legitimate authority or unaffordable reconstruction. Sound reasons and ethical standing do not follow from profitability. Any eventual study should reuse these accepted distinctions and identify which new transition or bound is independently warranted, rather than inventing a payoff to select the desired result.
