- **Entropy: history and record.** In a balanced flow every change of a level is an exchange, so the history of exchanges grows at every level change without a dissipation premise, and an exchange with its reverse restores every level but not the history (`Flow.Balances.restores_levels_not_history`). Under the first law a reverse is within the law exactly where the exchange is lossless, and zero loss is possible: a finite, lossless, enclosed world can exchange without end, its configuration coming back while its history grows (`Passing.passes`, `Passing.history_grows`). The least loss per exchange is exactly the first law with the record held in the conserved amount: a flow dissipates exactly where, for some record, every exchange conserves the family with its record exactly and leaves the record at least one unit (`Flow.dissipatesOn_iff_conserved`); this decomposes the least loss and does not derive it. Under the first law, while change goes on no quantity of a finite enclosure's levels keeps rising (`Flow.Balances.no_arrow`); where change is a function of the configuration, rest or such a rise merges configurations (`Web.merges_of_settles`, `Flow.Balances.merges_of_arrow`); a finite world that keeps exchanging cannot hold its history in its configuration (`World.forgets`); and a rise without end under exact conservation draws from beyond (`Gathering.gathers`; `Becoming.Entropy`). These are also read as instances of R6's laws of change (`Becoming.Recurrence`): the first law keeps the configurations within the stock at `T`, and within that bound the configuration recurs constructively (`Flow.Balances.recurs`), an arrow stops change (`Flow.Balances.arrow_stops`), a law merging nothing brings the start back (`Flow.Balances.returns_of_lossless`, the bound doing the work), and the present forgets the kept history (`Flow.Balances.present_forgets`). Which quantity is called entropy, the history or the record in the conserved amount, is Ren's decision.
