# A constitutive candidate for experiential significance and practical response

6 October 2026. AI-authored research responding to Ren's request to advance and establish the candidate direction: generative activity → beings with actual stakes → opportunities to affect those stakes → reasons → concern and wisdom. This note owns the positive constitutive argument. It follows [unified ethical grounding](../../../../theories/becoming/history/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md), [the strongest foundation target](../../../../theories/becoming/history/STRONGEST-FOUNDATION-CLAIM.md), and [the universal ethical minimum](../../../../theories/becoming/history/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md).

**Candidate:** in a specified class of still-unwanted aversive episodes, the subject's experiential condition constitutes a local bad-for relation. An eligible intervention can realize relief of that very condition, making the patient's improvement a source of objective moral favor for the acting subject. The actor's opportunity supplies practical address; the actor's endorsement is not the proposed value source. This is a defended constitutive candidate with explicit realization and scope requirements. It is not a newly proved identity or a completed derivation from the minimal causal root.

The relevant ingredients already appear in [§29–§32 of the self-ground audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#29-experiential-grounds-and-warranted-concern) and the practical-uptake studies. They are inherited, not rediscovered here. The advance is a **joint adequacy contract**: state a restricted patient-side realization, distinguish constitutive sufficiency from mere co-occurrence, and compare the proposed practical ground with an elective-concern rival at the moment before endorsement. The resulting revision tests identify which part of the candidate would fail.

## 1. A generative account must identify the being for whom the difference matters

The established causal work explains organizations with dependencies, consequence-sensitive regulation and acquired response. Those are necessary components of many plausible accounts of stakes, but their presence does not establish felt significance. An organization can preserve itself by harming a patient; an experienced burden can remain while that organization's reserve grows. A negative error signal is therefore not a certificate of experiential badness.

This candidate starts when a generative realization contains an actual experiencing subject. It does not derive consciousness from feedback, information integration, a numerical reward, recursive self-modeling or persistence alone. The sufficient and necessary realization conditions for experience remain unknown here. Human evidence can warrant particular applications without supplying a universal physical criterion.

The subject need not be an independent substance. The candidate is compatible with experience depending on an organized, historically formed process. That compatibility does not establish a particular identity theory of mind. It also does not make the process's every condition valuable: the relevant stake concerns what the condition is like for the actual subject.

This separates two obligations. A realization account must establish that an experiencing subject and a particular experiential condition exist. A constitutive account must explain which features make the condition bad for that subject and practically significant. Neither obligation can be discharged by naming the same variable “welfare.”

## 2. The patient-side hypothesis has a restricted sufficient domain

Let D be a class of actual episode/subject/context triples identified through the following proposed conditions:

1. There is an actual subject p undergoing an episode e. The evidence supports experiencing, rather than merely reflex, withdrawal, aversive learning or a report token.
2. The episode has a severe negative experiential character: its adverse quality is present in the undergoing, rather than only in a belief about a future loss or a threat to the observer.
3. That quality remains unwanted in the relevant experiential respect. The class does not include cases established to involve intrinsic enjoyment or positive appreciation of that same occurrence that may alter its prudential status. Instrumental acceptance is distinguished from such appreciation.
4. The proposed contrast concerns relief of this condition for this patient. Effects on agency, other valued experiences, bodily function and the wider life are represented separately rather than assumed absent whenever they complicate the conclusion.

These specify a narrow target. They do not constitute an operational consciousness test, require an articulate avowal, or identify “unwanted” with every expressed desire. In a noncommunicating patient, warrant for the relevant experiential whole must come from an independently assessed evidential route. Uncertainty about the realization narrows confidence in an application; it does not erase the possible patient by definition.

**C1 — Constitutive patient significance.** Within D, the aversive experiential whole constitutes a bad-for relation concerning p's undergoing that occurrence. Its badness is not an extra penalty conferred by a controller, an outside observer or p's later reflective theory.

This is a proposed constitutive identification, not an inference from the grammatical meaning of “negative.” The term identifies the hypothesized experiential feature; C1 identifies its evaluative role. An independently specified phenomenal description does not prove that role simply by being labeled adverse. Conversely, treating the role as freely detachable after C1 is justified would omit the very identification under assessment.

The positive warrant comes from the explanatory fit between the present experiential burden, what makes relief matter to the subject, and what changes when the experience genuinely improves. This fit supports identifying one condition under experiential and evaluative descriptions instead of adding an externally selected score. It permits a subject to lack a theory of welfare while still undergoing the condition that makes relief significant. These are grounds for the candidate, not an impossibility proof against every alternative welfare account.

The restriction is substantive. [The existing attitude and whole-life audit](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#30-experiential-occurrence-patient-attitudes-and-whole-life-value) records accounts on which intrinsic enjoyment defeats badness rather than merely compensating for it. It also records whole-life accounts that challenge token-sensitive significance. We therefore do not claim that every unpleasant quality must be bad under every attitude and history. Nor does describing badness “in one respect” answer those objections automatically. C1 must be tested in its declared domain, and any broader claim needs a separate defense.

## 3. What would identify this condition rather than a convenient proxy?

The candidate makes a specific difference between **what realizes the stake** and **how we detect it**. Neural activity, report, attention, behavior and resource change can provide evidence about the subject. None is declared identical to experienced disvalue by this note. Their evidential significance depends on the actual realization and interpretation.

The following crossed comparison is a contract for identifying the proposed ground, not a report of an experiment:

| Functional condition | Experiential condition | What C1 attributes |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational functioning is impaired | A qualifying aversive episode is present | Experiential badness from the episode; functional loss may have additional grounds |
| Organizational functioning is preserved or improved | The same qualifying experiential burden is present | The experiential stake is not canceled by organizational success |
| Organizational functioning is impaired | No qualifying aversive experience is established | C1 supplies no identified experiential conclusion; other harms remain possible |
| Organizational functioning is preserved | No qualifying aversive experience is established | No experiential reason is established merely by the functional description |

“No episode is established” is epistemic, not a proof of its absence. The third and fourth rows make no assertion that only felt experiences can matter. They prevent a local candidate from silently becoming a complete welfare theory.

The [coordinated source investigation](CONSTITUTIVE-SIGNIFICANCE-SOURCES.md) supplies empirical identification constraints. Its comparisons distinguish sensory, affective, behavioral and choice measures at their actual scopes. Voluntary acceptance of stimulation cannot be read as absence of burden, and an altered choice does not establish unchanged phenomenal valence. This note inherits that assessment rather than reporting another primary reading or reanalysis.

For an actual realization claim, the investigator must record which experiential variable the evidence identifies, which alternatives remain observationally compatible, and whether the intervention changes a constituent of the ground or only an indicator. That requirement makes C1 more demanding than an assigned negative number while leaving the psychological theory open to revision.

## 4. The practical hypothesis concerns moral favor, with rational authority separate

Fix a target in D and two actual available continuations. Intervention y relieves the identified condition for p relative to x. The actor a can produce the difference. In the clean comparison, the intervention is independently permissible, the patient's relevant willingness and authority are retained, and other morally relevant effects are controlled. These are application premises, not consequences of C1.

**C2 — Constitutive practical significance.** In that comparison, the prospective relation by which a's available intervention y would realize the improvement for p constitutes an objective moral favoring relation concerning y over x. Its source is the patient's identified stake together with the action's relation to it. The favor is present before execution as well as before endorsement. Prior endorsement, private return and a special role are not necessary constituents of this particular moral favor.

C2 is a candidate account of what the relevant favor consists in, not merely a rule that a benevolent observer should reward y. It identifies a relation between the beneficiary, the actual difference and the operator. Changing the means changes its application; changing only the operator's approval does not change the proposed source.

The conjunction of C1 and C2 is the candidate. **C1 alone does not entail C2.** Patient-local constitution does not logically exclude an actor-address condition. Nor does formal agreement of two predicates establish their metaphysical identity. The account needs the positive argument for C2 below, alongside a realization account and the defense of C1.

The immediate conclusion is objective **moral** favor. The latest [ethical-minimum comparison](../../../../theories/becoming/history/UNIVERSAL-ETHICAL-MINIMUM.md#1-preserve-the-practical-claim-while-distinguishing-a-stronger-dispute) shows why this should not be confused with the distinct claim that every possible rational actor has motivation-independent rational authority to respond. Manne's inspected position is not a denial of the minimum merely because it qualifies the latter authority. The stronger foundation continues to seek that connection; C2 does not acquire it through terminology.

## 5. The elective-concern rival locates the actual dispute

Consider an account accepting C1 and all the stated facts about y, but recognizing moral favor for the operator only after a legitimate endorsement, undertaking or special tie H. Before H, help can be permissible. After H, the patient's actual condition governs faithful pursuit, so ineffective or merely symbolic help can be criticized. This rival preserves patient tracking and many correct judgments; it cannot be defeated merely by showing that it sometimes tracks the patient.

Write F for the candidate moral-favor relation. C2 supports the eligible relief instances before H; the elective account conditions the corresponding operator-directed favor on H. Both can preserve the same experiential realization. Their difference is the normative significance of endorsement, not whether suffering or causal access exists.

Now fix a time **before** the actor adopts helping as an end. The actor considers making a modest permissible sacrifice to relieve p, or making an otherwise matched sacrifice that benefits nobody. Other goods of expression, play, inquiry or commitment are held fixed. The original [practical-uptake argument](PRACTICAL-UPTAKE-ARGUMENT.md#a-positive-justification-test-with-a-modest-cost) defends the prospective judgment that p's improvement already makes the relief option more worth choosing in the relevant moral respect. The sacrifice need not be required for this positive justification to exist.

The timing matters. If the rival accepts this **same prospective patient-grounded moral justification**, it has accepted an instance of F before H and cannot also make H necessary for that favor. This conditional incompatibility follows from the meanings fixed in the comparison. It is not a metaphysical proof that the prospective judgment is true. The rival can instead deny the judgment, reinterpret it as bare permission, or locate it in a preexisting wider attitude. Those are genuine disagreements requiring assessment.

The positive case for the prospective judgment has three parts:

- **The difference merits the response.** What makes relief worth choosing is an actual improvement for p. The proposed reason identifies that difference before a new undertaking changes anyone's commitments.
- **It explains why endorsement can be warranted.** Choosing concern can answer a merit already present; its justification does not have to be manufactured by the very choice whose adequacy is being assessed. This does not deny genuine choice-created obligations in other cases.
- **It remains answerable to the patient.** If the supposed relief would not improve p's condition, the particular prospective justification fails, even if the actor could freely adopt the ineffective project. If only the actor's willingness changes, the merit just identified has not thereby disappeared. An account that makes the latter change decisive owes a defense of that additional dependence.

Together these considerations favor C2 as an explanation of the prospective moral justification. The rival may give endorsement a substantive normative role in selecting ends. We do not refute that role by calling choice arbitrary, or assume that every morally eligible end must already have a decisive reason. The narrower question is why such a role must erase **all** pre-endorsement moral favor from the patient's good, rather than select among already worthy possibilities or add further responsibility.

This is the new practical adjudication: identify the pre-endorsement explanandum and the rival's exact change of verdict. Merely showing that benevolent choice creates later duties no longer addresses that explanandum. Conversely, the candidate cannot count a post-endorsement example as evidence that it has established the prospective ground.

### Private adoption and renunciation isolate the proposed normative power

The [companion private-adoption and renunciation contract](CONSTITUTIVE-SIGNIFICANCE-COUNTERREVIEW.md#private-adoption-and-renunciation-provide-a-sharper-contrast) sharpens the comparison. Keep the patient, burden, eligible relief, knowledge and relevant competing interests fixed. The actor privately adopts the helping project and later privately abandons it. There is no communicated promise, induced reliance, new exposure, accepted office or other independently created patient claim. The actor's mind really changes; the comparison does not pretend that every fact about the actor stayed constant.

| Context | Constitutive candidate | Elective gate, if the private change toggles H |
|---|---|---|
| Before adoption | Patient relief supplies baseline moral favor | No operator-directed moral favor from this burden |
| During the chosen project | The baseline remains; legitimate project reasons may add to it | Endorsement makes the favor apply |
| After bare private abandonment | The patient baseline remains if its grounds and access remain | The favor ceases when H ceases |

The third row is conditional on the rival actually allowing the specified change to end H. If a valid undertaking survives repudiation, that is a different case; a private announcement cannot simply discharge obligations by definition. The comparison also does not assert that choices lack all normative powers. It isolates the stronger claim that this uncommunicated selection creates **all** of the patient's moral significance for the available response.

The argument favors the constitutive account because the prospective merit and its object remain available through the private change. A response to actual suffering can be worthy before it becomes a selected project, and remains answerable to that same ground after interest fades. The elective account can deny this and defend a distinct authority to determine which goods morally address the actor. That is the exact additional thesis to evaluate; citing ordinary choice-created obligations is insufficient. This is a discriminating fork with a positive adjudication, not a claim that the rival is contradictory or empirically ruled out.

## 6. A joint adequacy contract for the candidate

The candidate is ready to be assessed as a specified account when the following records are supplied. They are not a new scientific experiment or a declaration of whole-foundation completion.

| Obligation | Required content | Present status |
|---|---|---|
| Realization | An actual experiencing patient and the whole relevant episode, with evidence and alternatives | Supplied for the philosophical cases; universal realization conditions unproved |
| Patient sufficiency | The specified D-conditions and why their experiential whole constitutes the stated local bad-for relation | C1 defended as a restricted hypothesis; broader attitude and whole-life challenges retained |
| Prospective practical ground | Why relief morally favors this actor's option before endorsement or special role | C2 positively defended through the prospective comparison; strongest rival remains substantive |
| Application | Actual efficacy, patient identity, authority, permission and effects on other centers | Independently supplied local premises; not derived from causal capacity alone |
| Ground recognition | How evidence and learning warrant this interpretation rather than only produce agreement | Partly realized by existing conditional mechanisms; universal moral recognition unestablished |
| Joint necessity | Why every adequately justified rival must preserve C1 and C2 together or equivalent content | Open; agreement on selected outcomes is insufficient |

The explanatory gains inherited from the earlier work now have a definite location in this contract. Observer-only suppression fails the relief relation. Genuine alteration of the experiential whole can remove or transform the C1-instance. Completion can end an application of C2. Destruction of a patient cannot be entered as a zero-valued state of that same patient's flourishing without an independent comparison. A different patient's burden adds a different ground rather than replacing the first person's subject index. None of these results establishes one interpersonal sum or a full life ranking. They are inherited constraints applied to the candidate, not new theorems of this round.

## 7. Revisions must distinguish four different failures

**Realization failure.** The evidence identified nociception, negative reinforcement or a verbal report but did not establish the claimed experience. Correct the realization claim. This does not show that actual suffering lacks significance.

**Patient-sufficiency failure.** A preserved member of D lacks the alleged bad-for relation under a better warranted assessment. Revise C1. A merely instrumentally accepted pain or a changed experiential whole does not automatically produce this counterexample; neither may the class be redefined after every adverse result to protect C1. The enjoyment and whole-life challenges must be addressed as genuine alternatives.

**Practical-scope failure.** The patient-ground and eligible relief are preserved, but an independently justified condition H is necessary for moral favor. Revise C2's actor coverage. A lack of decisive duty or actual motivation alone does not establish this result. A rival accepting the moral favor while restricting a distinct rational-reason notion challenges the stronger authority claim instead.

**Implementation failure.** C1, C2 and the local application are warranted, but the formed system ignores the patient, retains a harmful association, fails to distinguish the actual target or cannot pay for action. Explain the failure through the generative mechanism. Do not repair it by redefining the patient-ground as whatever the system happens to select.

These distinctions make the candidate revisable without turning every objection into an undifferentiated failure of “unification.” They also prevent proof of one link from silently serving as evidence for another.

## 8. What is established at this stage

This study establishes a **specified and positively defended candidate**, not the truth of a necessary foundation. Its patient realization, sufficient experiential domain, prospective moral claim, rival explanation, supplied premises and revision conditions are explicit. Its strongest advance over the previous effectuation arguments is the timing-sensitive challenge to elective concern, paired with a restricted constitutive domain that does not evade the existing enjoyment and whole-life objections.

Within the candidate, the intended generative direction is coherent: actual organization can realize experiential stakes; actual opportunities make a response available; the stake and response relation ground moral favor; acquired concern can recognize it; and correction can restore fidelity when learning fails. The first realization law, C1, C2 and the relevant application grounds are not all derived from the minimal causal root. Their status in the account is explicit rather than concealed by a chain of arrows.

The next decisive result must either strengthen the independent warrant of C1 in its specified domain, defeat the elective condition as necessary for pre-endorsement moral favor, or justify such a condition and restrict the candidate. A new controller implementing C2 would help explain realization of concern but would not decide its constitutive truth. The programme continues to seek the stronger unified foundation while preserving the accepted conditional work, differentiated centers and plural worthwhile lives.

## 9. Backward audit: the adversity assumed in correcting concern

6 October 2026. AI-authored continuation of the [whole-process inquiry](../../../../theories/becoming/history/UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md#when-fulfilled-concern-itself-needs-correction). The named connection is **actual unwanted undergoing → local adversity → a ground for correcting a formed orientation**. This audits a dependence of the correction argument; it does not replace whole-process grounding with an independently supplied patient ontology.

The [concern-object argument](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-ARGUMENT.md#10-concerns-object-and-the-grounds-of-its-correction) proposes a defect in welcoming an occurrence precisely because it is adverse. That proposal already uses the occurrence's independently warranted adversity. Running the explanation backward—welcoming this is defective, therefore undergoing it is bad—would recycle that input. Independently justified evidence about fitting attitudes could support an account of value; the present argument has not supplied such independent justification. Neither the occurrence of an attitude nor a proposed rule about its fittingness closes the dependence.

The restricted domain in §2 must therefore keep three senses of “unwanted” distinct. **Constitutive experiential opposition** describes the manner of undergoing. **A further rejecting attitude** concerns one's response to that experience. **Warranted rejection** already claims evaluative justification. The candidate intends the first, without requiring the second or building in the third. Calling an experience negative does not establish its bad-for quality. Conversely, substituting a separate preference for the complete experiential mode would change the target. Whether that mode concerns a bodily condition or the experience itself also remains substantive; opposition cannot simply be translated into a command to eliminate its own occurrence.

The [new Massin comparison](AFFECTIVE-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md#massin-an-identity-with-a-different-bearer) gives an explicit identity account whose value bearer is a bodily episode. His distinction allows genuine suffering of an apparent pain while its bodily object is absent. Consequently, importing that identity would leave our question about the undergoing itself unanswered. This is neither a denial of experiential adversity nor a preserved-domain counterexample to C1.

This application uses the existing distinction in [HUIGEN §40](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#40-affective-disclosure-and-the-badness-of-undergoing): correcting a represented bodily condition and changing the actual experience are different possible changes within one formation. They are not presumed to be independently manipulable in every case. The source comparison is new here; the target-retention criterion and the contrasting cases are inherited. No new clinical result, realization mechanism, ethical theorem or successful C1 derivation is claimed.

**Result and next bridge.** The backward derivation fails at a specified dependence. The affirmative account remains the proposed identity between actual unwanted undergoing and adversity for the life undergoing it, with its existing explanatory support and restricted scope. The newly examined alternative does not yet establish or defeat that identity. A substantive next advance must explain why the independently identified mode constitutes prudential adversity, or defend an account preserving that mode while denying precisely that relation. A mere denial of additional whole-life loss, practical authority or independent token value would not by itself decide this question. A genuine misdescription would require revising the domain rather than expanding it to evade the challenge.

The significance integral continues to express downstream differences conditional on an adequate appraisal of realizations. It cannot discharge this ethical dependence by accumulating it through time. Neither C1 nor the practical bridge C2 has acquired a new foundational assumption or proof in this audit.

## 10. Present aversion and the explanation by failed aims

6 October 2026. Named connection: **the manner of an actual experiential realization → its proposed local adversity → prospective significance of transforming it**. Section 9 left C1's positive warrant unresolved. This comparison tests a specific proposed explanation: that the adversity consists in failure to realize a preferred or constitutive alternative. It preserves the whole-process starting point; experiencing, its object, response dispositions and later appraisal are differentiated relations within the activity.

The distinction between affective direction, separate rejection and warranted rejection is inherited from §9. So are the restrictions on inferring welfare from organizational functioning and the [temporal-content test for formal aims](AFFECTIVE-REALIZATION-ARGUMENT.md#comparative-relief-and-an-experiences-formal-aim). The new contribution is a primary argument against requiring a represented alternative, joined to a comparison with successful protective functioning. It narrows an explanatory route rather than newly discovering nonreflective experience or proving C1.

### What a represented failure would add

The [Heathwood comparison](AFFECTIVE-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md#heathwood-aversion-and-an-unrealized-alternative) presents a possible mind that encounters and dislikes an actual taste without representing its absence. The proposed distinction concerns experiential attitudes; behavioral dispositions admit a different analysis. Treat this as a philosophical case to assess, not an established finding about any actual animal. Its mild example is not itself a demonstrated member of C1's severe domain.

The consequence for our reconstruction is conditional but substantive. If that psychological distinction is sound, identifying aversive undergoing does not already identify a positive experiential desire for an absent state. Requiring such a representation would add a cognitive condition to C1's target. A model that represents both simply as an unmet target can preserve a response disposition while omitting the distinction relevant to the experiential proposal. That is a potential representational omission; it does not demonstrate either the proposed bad-for identity or a substantive failure of the generative account.

Conversely, an unfulfilled attraction does not by itself identify present aversive undergoing. Someone can welcome an optional addition while remaining untroubled by its absence. Whether that absence has a distinct welfare cost remains a genuine dispute. The present distinction does not dismiss frustration as necessarily neutral; it prevents inferring the occurrence of C1's particular ground from failure to acquire a desired good.

### Successful activity can retain the disputed experiential quality

Consider an accurate painful warning during a successful escape. The person understands it, acts competently and accepts its protective role while still undergoing its qualifying first-order unpleasantness without intrinsic enjoyment. The protective activity succeeds, and no error in the represented danger is required. Failure of the protective response is therefore not necessary for this proposed local adverse aspect. Other bodily or organizational impairments may remain; the example does not establish their absence.

This is our conceptual comparison, not a clinical case or a claim that a costless substitute exists. If an equally informative and effective continuation with less aversive undergoing is genuinely available, C1 predicts a local benefit from that difference. Evidence of its availability, retained goods, later effects and legitimate means remains necessary before making a practical recommendation. The example does not establish that such a comparison can always be realized.

A stricter capacity-only account could grant the unchanged repugnance while locating this episode's welfare contribution entirely in successful exercise of the relevant capacity. It would deny the proposed additional adverse contribution, rather than concede and outweigh it. This is our rival reconstruction, not a claim that every perfectionist adopts it. To discriminate it from C1, establish the relevant capacity independently and determine whether both aversive and nonaversive realization preserve it. If aversiveness partly constitutes that capacity, holding protection constant alone would not preserve the rival's target. A genuine comparison cannot assume away that obligation.

The cross-connection with the [realization audit](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-ARGUMENT.md#11-realization-and-the-claim-of-necessary-agreement) is important: coherent endorsement and successful activity can coexist with a disputed adverse manner of undergoing. This supports investigating ethical quality within successful realization itself. It neither refutes the fuller Nishida proposal nor establishes that every felt opposition is bad.

### The stronger telic explanation remains a substantive candidate

Neither comparison defeats a separately justified, nonconceptual experiential aim. Successful protection need not fulfill a reflexive direction concerning less of the experience; lack of a represented negation need not exclude that direction. The existing type/token and temporal-content obligations remain. A theory must identify which direction is constitutive, what satisfies it and why its realization or frustration constitutes welfare. Those claims cannot be extracted solely from a generic label of aversion or from task success.

The new source also offers a positive symmetry argument from fulfilled attraction to realized aversion. That could contribute to a joint identification of experiential good and bad. In this programme, however, the [preceding formed-appreciation study](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-ARGUMENT.md#12-formed-appreciation-and-the-claim-of-added-good) has not derived the initial good from generation. Assuming it here would relocate the same dependence. A joint constitutive account remains possible; symmetry alone does not establish either side's ethical warrant.

### Result for the whole and its future significance

C1 remains a present-realization candidate: the proposed local adversity concerns how the unfolding is actually lived. An affected being need not first conceive relief or a whole better future for this candidate to apply. An investigator's model of possible continuations is therefore not a mental capacity that the beneficiary must possess. This refines the explanatory route without adding a new requirement to the protected domain.

The [future-good construction](FUTURE-GOOD-INTEGRAL.md) can trace what available transformations would change in these actual realizations. Neither the beneficiary's absent future model nor a successful protective outcome licenses replacing the current experiential character with a zero. Equally, that statement does not prove the negative value assigned to it: C1, whole-history comparison and practical address retain their substantive burdens. The candidate's proposed ground is lived quality within the process; its full derivation from the minimal generative foundation remains open.

The next grounding attempt must defend that quality's prudential status directly or explain it through an independently warranted constitutive relation. Repeating that an aim is frustrated, a warning succeeds or an observer wants relief will not discharge the identified obligation. A demonstrated equivalence between experiential aversion and the relevant nonconceptual aim would strengthen the telic route; a qualifying case lacking the proposed bad-for relation would revise C1. No new empirical result, formal axiom or universal ethical necessity is claimed.

## 11. Experiential warrant and evolutionary explanation

6 October 2026. Named connection: **actual aversive undergoing → justified local prudential appraisal → constitutive explanation and practical use**. The previous round clarified the experiential target. This round tests whether the justification of its proposed bad-for quality requires an earlier ethical premise, and whether a new evolutionary account defeats its claimed experiential warrant. The [primary record](AFFECTIVE-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md#warrant-and-the-evolutionary-explanation-of-value-judgments) preserves both the affirmative epistemic comparator and its challenge.

The possibility of experiential evidence and the distinction between genealogy and warrant are inherited from [self-ground §§29–32](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#29-experiential-grounds-and-warranted-concern), the evolutionary audit and the affective-disclosure work. The new result is an adjudication of a specific explanatory challenge. No new general acquaintance theorem is claimed.

### A first-value judgment need not be an inference from a psychological report

Fix a qualifying occurrence and distinguish its aversive character, its appearing locally bad for the life undergoing it, and a judgment accepting that appearance. These may be connected within one activity. They are not interchangeable descriptions established by this note. An appearance that a body is endangered, a disposition to withdraw and the judgment that anyone must intervene have different contents.

The positive position is that attentive experience can furnish defeasible warrant for the local evaluative judgment without first applying a universal ethical rule. This is not a deduction from “I feel aversion” or “I judge it bad” to its badness. A noninferential account gives the properly identified appearance an initial epistemic role, subject to correction. Huemer's argument clarifies that form of justification; his metaphysics and account of intellectual intuition are not silently adopted as a theory of affect.

This position requires a defensible epistemology, including an account of apparent evaluative content and its defeaters. It is therefore not an assumption-free deduction from the minimal generative root. But absence of such a deduction is also not a demonstration that C1 has no rational support. An ethically substantive judgment can be provisionally warranted before its complete constitutive explanation is known. Justification for accepting it and what makes it true remain separate explanatory questions within the same unfolding.

### What the evolutionary challenge actually targets

The newly read Japanese argument distinguishes the fact that people have pain-value intuitions from the intuitions themselves. Its central target is the inference that evaluative acquaintance must be the best explanation of that fact and therefore pain has intrinsic disvalue. A plausible alternative origin weakens that inference's claim to exclusivity. The article itself permits evolved acquaintance and does not report an established adaptive history.

Our adjudication accepts the restriction: prevalence, immediacy and causal usefulness of a judgment do not independently prove that it discloses value. But replacing an inference from those facts does not by itself defeat a proposed noninferential warrant. Nor does describing an evolutionary cause show that the putative object of acquaintance is absent. To undercut the particular judgment, the genealogy needs a defended connection to its unreliability or lack of warrant; a general story of adaptive avoidance does not provide that connection automatically.

Conversely, the candidate cannot protect every evaluative appearance by declaring its content identical with truth. An account that explains a particular misleading appearance while preserving the underlying experience would be a substantive challenge. Whether the relevant mode actually presents local prudential adversity must be assessed rather than inferred from the words “felt bad.”

### Identify the content whose selection is being explained

Our comparison concerns the evolutionary account's specific explanatory step, not the truth of evolution. Consider a planner with retained information about injury, prospective avoidance and concern for continued agency. It might avoid future tissue damage while withholding the judgment that painful undergoing is intrinsically bad. A second planner might additionally accept that judgment. These are conceptually specified alternatives, not observed populations or newly implemented conscious systems.

The first profile shows why the source's ceteris paribus tendency claim and proposed planning advantage require comparison with other bases of prospective protection. Its conceptual possibility does not by itself refute either claim. It does not show that the additional judgment has no selective advantage. The question becomes which contribution of that precise content—beyond anticipation, aversion and protective concern—the history explains. This restriction also applies to our positive theory. Avoidance and relief-seeking alone cannot discriminate C1 from a rival that predicts the same conduct. ### A stronger contextual challenge remains

Fitting grief or remorse offers a more demanding alleged misclassification. A rival can propose that an unwanted painful manner partly constitutes valuable recognition, with no separate adverse contribution. That contests C1 at the local level; merely claiming an overall benefit would not. The rival must defend elimination of the adverse contribution rather than its coexistence with fitting recognition. C1 cannot answer just by repeating that the episode is unpleasant.

Nor can the case be excluded from the declared domain solely because the response is fitting. Intrinsic enjoyment, a changed experiential quality or another actual scope condition must be established. A painless version of the same relevant capacity cannot be stipulated if painfulness partly constitutes that capacity. These constraints make contextual error a substantive next test, rather than a predicate switched in a weaker model.

The defended position remains provisional support for C1 as a local first-value judgment, with the specific evolutionary challenge restricted to what it establishes.
