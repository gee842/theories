# Inquiry that changes the possibilities of identification

6 October 2026. AI-authored primary-source and interpretation note for the connection **acquired distinctions → actual inquiry → changed conditions of further identification**. Read under the [shared nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). This note credits established mathematical antecedents and the repository's existing acquisition mechanisms. It introduces no scientific run, empirical cognition result or mathematical-priority claim.

## Direct primary antecedents

**Edward F. Moore, “Gedanken-Experiments on Sequential Machines,” *Automata Studies* (1956), pp. 129–153.** Directly inspected printed pp. 129–130, 135–139 and 148 in the [primary paper hosted by Carnegie Mellon](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cdm/resources/Moore1956-gedanken-experiments.pdf). In this 27-page scan these are PDF pages 2–3, 8–12 and 21, respectively; the first PDF page is a cover. Moore's “simple experiment” uses one copy and explicitly permits inputs to depend on preceding outputs. “Simple” therefore does not mean preset. A multiple experiment instead has several copies initially in the same state.

Theorem 1, pp. 138–139, provides a machine whose states are pairwise distinguishable but whose initial state cannot always be identified by a single-copy experiment. The first input merges some alternatives before producing evidence that distinguishes them. Adaptive continuation cannot undo this loss. Moore separately explains that the final state can nevertheless be known; Theorem 8, p. 148, gives a final-state identification construction for reduced finite machines. The paper explicitly concerns conceptual experiments rather than measurements of physical devices. Its optional physical analogy is unnecessary for the present application.

**Petra van den Bos and Frits Vaandrager, “State Identification for Labeled Transition Systems with Inputs and Outputs” (2019).** Directly inspected [the authors' primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11034), §4, printed pp. 10–12, Definitions 12–20 and Figure 3; and §6, pp. 23–24, Definition 33, Lemma 34 and Theorem 35. The paper defines adaptive tests, requires their inputs to remain enabled for the relevant alternatives, and distinguishes pair-separating tests from one graph separating all incompatible pairs. Figure 3 gives an example without such a graph. The later construction preserves incompatibility through appropriately injective steps; its stated completeness theorem assumes that all distinct state pairs are incompatible. These are useful controls on an effectful inquiry proof. Their suspension-automaton framework is broader than our deterministic native interface, so its exact algorithmic guarantees should not be imported without a representation argument.

**David Lee and Mihalis Yannakakis, “Testing Finite-State Machines: State Identification and Verification,” *IEEE Transactions on Computers* 43(3) (1994), 306–320, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/12.272431).** The bibliographic identity is confirmed by [Yannakakis's author-hosted publication list](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~mihalis/cv.pdf), entry 55. The full 1994 paper was not accessible in this bounded search. It is a recorded antecedent, not a directly verified proof source here. The two directly inspected works above are sufficient for the present distinction; the 1996 survey is not substituted for the uninspected original.

The consulted PDFs remain link-only references; no external original has been imported or edited. Page references describe the inspected versions, not an exhaustive reading of either paper.

## Three questions that must not be conflated

| Question | Required relation |
|---|---|
| Are two starting configurations behaviorally different? | Some permitted inquiry has different observable consequences from those two configurations. |
| Can one inquiry determine which starting alternative was present? | One policy, using only accessible history and retained information, separates every admitted starting alternative on its actual branch. |
| Can inquiry identify what continuation is possible now? | Its record identifies the relevant present behavior after the inquiry's own transformations, even if the earlier alternative is unrecoverable. |

The first quantifies separately over a witness for each pair. The second requires those witnesses to fit together into one executable policy. Repeating a witness on a fresh copy or restoring all initial conditions changes that problem. Merely returning one component to its initial phase does not restore consumed support, reverse an earlier transformation or recreate a lost contact.

In our [native access law](../../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/acquisition/access/diagnosis.py), `probe` consumes a ten-unit source packet before its receipt is acquired. `reset` changes component phase when it works; it does not replenish that packet. Reading can coincide across readiness/sensor inversions while transfer consequences differ. A probe can therefore distinguish alternatives and remove a remaining opportunity within the same event history. This concerns actual relations specified by the law, rather than an independent label attached by an external meaning-giver.

An initial-behavior impossibility claim must preserve every channel the controller actually has: acquired outputs, known command history, source availability, accessible resource changes and any retained archive. Hidden audit labels are not controller inputs. Conversely, an observable difference must not be discarded simply because it is inconvenient for a proof. Where unsuccessful alternatives have the same receipt and resource consequence, then enter states with identical remaining native behavior, an additional internal calculation cannot create the absent differentiating encounter. This is the local connection to test; it does not require identifying all reality with finite-state machines.

3. The saved matched comparison identified by the inquiry-interface argument changes that family through a retained acquired signal and consequently continues acquisition for four readings instead of three. Its primitive access and uniqueness stopping rule remain unchanged. The misleading-signal branch preserves the fact that longer inquiry need not be a warranted improvement.

The [mathematical identity study](../../../../theories/becoming/history/IMMANENT-MATHEMATICAL-IDENTITY.md) establishes exact behavioral identity relative to declared continuations. The present connection adds a different obligation: an inquiry policy must preserve enough of the relevant future opportunities to establish the identification it claims. Exact pairwise distinctions alone do not discharge that obligation. Neither that theorem nor this source note supplies a learner that revises its criterion of sufficient inquiry through those consequences.

## What the comparison supports

The present application is a conditional reconstruction within one generative history: contact, response, retention and support depletion determine what can subsequently be identified. The branch can therefore correct an articulation of the core that treats inquiry as adding evidence while leaving its own conditions untouched. The established automata results supply a precise comparator for that correction; they do not establish the generative ontology, universal mathematical constitution or empirical properties of human knowing.

A substantive challenge can identify an admissible policy that separates every declared starting class without an added information channel or restored packet, or show that the proposed indistinguishable continuation omits an accessible difference. Adding actual replenishment, a new coupling or an independently warranted prior restriction can change the result, but must be represented as a changed continuing configuration. This is a scope boundary of the local reconstruction, not evidence that inquiry and reality are fundamentally separate.
