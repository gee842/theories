# Recursive generative selection: primary-source assessment

7 October 2026. AI-authored evidence assessment of Ren's proposal that generative organizations continue and reproduce, degeneration undermines their future, and selection across connected scales can shape constituent activity. The strongest candidate concerns **renewal of selection conditions themselves**, not a claim that every local advantage immediately benefits the whole. This note follows the [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md).

## Existing connections and present target

[Generative selection](../../../../theories/becoming/history/GENERATIVE-SELECTION.md) already distinguishes common support from differential transmission and describes possible constructive feedbacks. Its common-return cancellation results do not exclude selection through unequal group renewal. [Liangzhi evolution](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/LIANGZHI-EVOLUTION.md) already connects actual consequences, transmission and developmental reconstruction; its [primary-source assessment](LIANGZHI-EVOLUTION-SOURCES.md) supplies distinct care/development interfaces. These achievements remain credited. The present sources specifically examine whether collective continuation can change the selection of constituent activity, and when that feedback can fail.

This advances a premise of the universal ethical investigation: whether recognition and practice supporting further formation can have an endogenous evolutionary explanation. It does not identify evolutionary success with ethical quality or establish the practical authority of the resulting orientation.

## Three primary works

### 1. Local disadvantage can coexist with global increase

John S. Chuang, Olivier Rivoire and Stanislas Leibler, “Simpson's Paradox in a Synthetic Microbial System,” *Science* 323 (2009), 272–275; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1166739), [institution-hosted published PDF](https://www.icts.res.in/sites/default/files/simpson%20paradox-leibler-2009.pdf).

**Reading:** complete article text, 272–275, and figure captions through searchable PDF text; the five-page file includes a cover and unrelated preceding-article material. Supplementary methods and raw data were not read.

**Reported experiment:** engineered *E. coli* producers generated a signal enabling antibiotic resistance. Nonproducers increased within mixed groups, but producer-rich groups grew more. Appropriate pooling therefore increased the overall producer fraction. Repeated dilution, growth and pooling raised producers from 10% to over 95% across five rounds. Small founding populations generated the required compositional variance. Allowing groups to reach stationary phase instead removed the relevant growth difference.

**Scope:** this directly supports a causal route from collective productivity to differential transmission, despite opposing within-group change. The laboratory supplied repeated group formation and timing. It demonstrates neither spontaneous formation of every such cycle nor inevitable cooperation under arbitrary mixing, and it measures cell frequencies rather than ethical quality.

### 2. Selection can shape a collective reproductive cycle

Katrin Hammerschmidt, Caroline J. Rose, Benjamin Kerr and Paul B. Rainey, “Life cycles, fitness decoupling and the evolution of multicellularity,” *Nature* 515 (2014), 75–79; [primary publisher page](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13884), DOI 10.1038/nature13884.

**Reading:** the publisher's abstract and displayed Extended Data Figure 1 caption; the full main text, methods and supplement were not available in the inspected page. A repository-handle attempt failed. No claim of a full-paper reading.

**Reported experiment:** bacterial collectives were propagated under a regime rewarding collective persistence, either incorporating or purging cheating types. Incorporation enabled a two-state life cycle and an evolved developmental switch. The authors report improved collective fitness with constituent-cell fitness unchanged or reduced; the purging treatment did not show the same decoupling. Collective fitness nevertheless improved in both treatments, through different associated traits.

**Scope:** a constructive route connects the recurrence of a larger organization to changes in its constituent organization. “Cheater” names a role in a specified interaction; a changed life cycle can alter that role. The experiment's propagation regime remains part of the explanation, and these readings do not establish unassisted emergence of the regime or selection of an entire biosphere.

### 3. Selection can also destroy its continuation conditions

Kalle Parvinen and Ulf Dieckmann, “Self-extinction through optimizing selection,” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 333 (2013), 1–9; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2013.03.025), [institutional PDF](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/id/eprint/10708/1/IR-13-058.pdf).

**Reading:** complete main text, 1–7, and displayed Appendix A.1–A.2/start of A.3, by public HTTPS download and local text extraction. The file contains the published article after a later institutional cover. Web/PMC openings encountered browser challenges. Remaining appendix text and references were not systematically read.

**Reported models:** explicit population laws include an Allee effect: sufficiently low density undermines further reproduction. Selection favors increased harvesting in a coupled consumer–resource model, or increased growth in a modified Ricker model, yet a viable population attractor can disappear. The authors also discuss tradeoffs, joint evolution and spatial structure as possible preventive mechanisms.

**Scope:** these are published conditional modeling results, not observed extinctions, our numerical replication, or new Lean-checked proofs. They challenge guaranteed correction before collapse under those laws. They do not refute the possibility that broader recurring organization selects against such histories when alternative continuing lineages and suitable transmission actually exist.

## Reconstruction: what recursive selection would require

The following synthesis is our research interpretation. Local destructive success does not settle the recursive proposal: the relevant continuation can include formation, dissolution, dispersal and reformation of the local group. A history that looks favorable over one phase may contribute little to later organization. Conversely, an activity costly within that phase can become common through the recurrence it enables. The formation of the larger cycle is therefore a substantive explanatory target, not merely a new label for a pre-existing collection.

“Pressure from the whole to its parts” should identify actual relations: which larger patterns recur, which differences affect their continuation, and how that continuation changes constituent reproduction, development or opportunities. These scales select aspects of one connected unfolding; they are not independent levels receiving commands from a separate whole. The experiments show informative realizations, while their supplied propagation conditions locate the next derivation obligation.

Three claims must remain distinguishable:

1. **Filtering:** exhausted or extinct lineages cease contributing descendants. This fits Ren's proposed exclusion of degeneration, but does not show that viable alternatives remain or that losses are repaired.
2. **Recursive renewal:** the formation process sustains variation, faithful-enough transmission, appropriate grouping and the conditions for another effective cycle. This is a stronger positive mechanism, and it can itself be subject to further selection where that larger recurrence exists.
3. **Universal favorable direction:** the connected unfolding reliably produces richer viable organization, and that direction has justified ethical significance. Neither repeated local success nor retrospective study only of survivors establishes this conclusion.

The next discriminating test should trace the same histories through multiple formation cycles while varying how consequences enter later transmission. It should retain successful renewal, delayed consequences, lost variation, destructive spread and complete failure. At every proposed recursive step, identify the continuing alternatives and the actual route from collective consequence to constituent formation. Mere resemblance across scales cannot substitute for that connection. Where the whole does not reproduce as a population of wholes, a claim about whole-level Darwinian selection needs a different account of recurrence and differential continuation; it cannot silently inherit a laboratory group's reproduction rule.

This leaves a strong affirmative hypothesis: sustained generativity may be selected through recursively renewed organizational relationships, including transformations of what counts as a reproducing unit. Its empirical range and the ethical warrant of the resulting orientation remain questions to investigate together.
