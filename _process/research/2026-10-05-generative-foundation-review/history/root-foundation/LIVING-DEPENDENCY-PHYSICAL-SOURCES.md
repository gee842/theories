This examines whether physical explanation can provide the support/demand correspondence missing from the general continuation proof. ## A physical-scale derivation and its limits

Pankaj Mehta and Jané Kondev, *What do the fundamental constants of physics tell us about life?*, [author preprint, arXiv:2509.09892v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09892v2), 8 November 2025.

**Direct reading:** abstract and main text, including the maintenance section and conclusion, plus Methods §1.8 on membrane voltage, current and power. Other Methods sections and cited originals were not independently inspected. The PMC version returned a browser check. This is a link-only assessment, not an imported source, replication or new experiment.

The authors estimate energetic and kinetic scales for chemical self-replicators. Their maintenance account connects membrane leakage to compensatory transport, with membrane composition, geometry, temperature, concentration differences and pore formation entering the estimate. Methods §1.8 explicitly calculates maximum modeled pore conductance using minimum viscosity, then estimates expected pore occurrence. This provides a possible physical explanation of maintenance demand. It does not establish a common positive lower bound across every living realization or every time interval. The paper's qualitative physical estimates should not be promoted into an assumption-free universal renewal law.

## Consequence for the present derivation

The constructive route is to retain the operative relationships that a particular physical account supplies. In our selected maintained-transport regime, incoming load, compensatory export and the support consumed by that export can be represented separately and then composed. The resulting account can explain why a preserved macroscopic condition still depends on actual activity. This is more informative than merely naming a stored quantity and requiring that it decrease.

The proposed mathematical bridge must expose its premises: actual transport balance, an energetic coupling, and the condition being maintained. It should allow finite histories using inherited tolerance or stored support, and should derive replenishment only when the represented actual demand exceeds what those inherited conditions can sustain. A stochastic biological description must not be silently replaced by a deterministic maximum lifetime. These are requirements for our representation, not further reported results of the preprint.

The quantities selected for that representation describe relations within one history. A cell boundary is a materially formed relation; it is not a primitive separation between a completed subject and an independent world. Available chemical support can itself be inherited from earlier formation or made available through other organisms' changes. An added input to one selected account is not thereby generation from outside reality, novel life, or an inexhaustible source. An account of the supplying relations must retain their own changes and losses.

The biological investigation and independent coverage review test those correspondence obligations. No theorem or experiment is newly proved in this source note. A checked proof of a transport abstraction would establish its conditional inference, while the correspondence and claimed coverage remain jointly assessed through mechanisms and evidence.
