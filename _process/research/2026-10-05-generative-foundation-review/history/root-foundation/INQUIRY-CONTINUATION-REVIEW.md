## What the existing work already establishes

The inquiry-interface argument already credits a matched history in which an acquired consequence changes a retained method, and the altered method changes the duration of actual sampling. Its supplied uniqueness stopping rule remains unchanged. This continuation must not rediscover consequence-dependent sampling by assuming it was absent.

The mathematical identity result defines equivalence through all admitted continuations. It expressly distinguishes that semantic relation from a learner's effective access to it. The present issue is therefore a separate implication to test: if every pair of initial possibilities can be distinguished by some continuation, must one ongoing inquiry identify which initial possibility obtained? The nondual reconstruction matters directly. Sensing, transfer, depletion, retention and further questioning are consequences of the same native operation history. ## Why the negative branch really erases an opportunity

Every pair of A, B and C has a distinguishing probe: output-1 distinguishes A from B and C; output-0 distinguishes B from C. Non-probe operations cannot distinguish these classes because phase, sensing parity and reset behavior are shared. Thus every initially distinguishing native inquiry has a first probe, after a common acquired history.

At that first probe, the learned output is either zero or one. In either case two different initial classes produce nonarrival. The source loses ten units before the later receipt operation. Both negative outcomes have the same zero arrival, zero credit, ten units wasted and zero source remaining. Their phase does not change. Subsequent reads, actions and resets still agree. A later probe first incurs its native operation demand, then reports `source_unavailable`; it does not read the latent readiness or blockage factor. The discarded opportunity is consequently part of the native law.

The claim survives access to the full actual fuel balance and failure reports. A history-dependent policy makes the same choices and internal updates after equal observations. On the paired nonarrival branch its costs, remaining fuel, capacity demands and later denials therefore agree. Funding failure before the first probe yields no distinguishing target-dependent effect. Receipt acquisition failure on that negative branch likewise has equal conditions and consequences. The fixture's limited retained archive is not needed for the impossibility argument: granting the complete transcript does not restore the absent encounter.

This is not a frozen-label objection to a stronger constitutive theory. The pre-probe classes are independently specified by native transfer consequences, and the argument preserves the actual source effect, response law and available commands. It challenges the proposed implication from pairwise contextual difference to one guaranteed identifying procedure. It does not refute the nondual account or declare meaningful distinctions external to activity.

## The target must remain explicit

The conclusion concerns identification of the **initial** native behavioral class. After the negative probe, the two remaining initial alternatives can belong to the same current behavioral class under the depleted interface. Inquiry has changed what can happen. Consequently, failure to identify the initial class is compatible with knowing the current continuation class, often called homing in the relevant formal literature.

Replenishment, access to another specimen, a direct readiness sensor, a side channel for the blocked factor, or restored trial conditions would change the admitted continuation structure. They are substantive possible revisions, not refutations of the original scoped result. Conversely, preserving only the original class names after changing that structure would not establish that the original target is still being investigated.

## Conditions on the general belief-space theorem

For a finite deterministic transition model, attach each initial target label to its evolving configuration. A belief is a nonempty set of `(initial label, current configuration)` pairs. An action and its observed outcome update configurations and filter pairs while preserving the labels. Outcomes must include relevant denials and failure consequences; an operation that changes conditions before failing cannot be represented as an effect-free undefined action.

The depth-zero winning beliefs are those with one target value. Inductively add a belief when some available policy action takes every nonempty observation branch into an earlier winning set. For this finite deterministic setting, the least fixed point characterizes existence of a finite worst-case terminating procedure that correctly identifies the target. A proof must establish both directions: an added belief supplies a decision tree; any finite successful decision tree places its root in the corresponding stage. Finite belief space gives termination of the calculation and permits a choice depending only on the current belief.

This theorem solves a specified control problem. It does not explain the origin of the transition class, target, stopping condition, storage or action repertoire. It must not be applied to an arbitrary infinite-resource model merely because a finite quotient was convenient; the quotient must preserve outcome-conditioned continuation and the selected target. Initial labels are bookkeeping for a legitimate historical question, not an assertion that present activity must preserve every previous distinction.

## A constructive change and its scope

With two admitted packets and enough support for every required operation, probe first at learned output 1. Receipt one identifies A. Receipt zero leaves B or C with a packet still available; move to a learned output-zero phase and probe again. Receipt one identifies B; receipt zero identifies C. The same native law now supports an identifying tree for all three classes. The blocked raw codes remain indistinguishable.

This constructive comparison identifies the relevant missing continuation: another realized probe opportunity. It is not an actual successor of the original one-packet history unless its provenance and supporting resources are supplied. It also does not learn when initial-class identification is worth pursuing. A locally possible identification can be irrelevant, harmful, or inferior to preserving a different continuation; those are further questions about the operative criterion, not conclusions of this decision tree.
