## What this adds

The prior [evaluative extension](EVALUATIVE-EXTENSION-INTEGRATION.md) distinguishes common comparison from uniform undefeatedness. This module addresses a different interface: a selection rule changes or compresses its **family of represented applications**, potentially changing which practical objections remain visible. It characterizes when the resulting comparative certificate transports back to the old family, while allowing a change of model types or labels.

It then proves a stronger condition for transport across every menu in a fixed plan domain. Finally, an arbitrary finite-prefix construction preserves the complete represented profiles on every audited plan while defeating extrapolation to the next plan. The positive all-menu theorem and negative finite-audit family specify different evidential burdens; neither establishes universal impossibility or automatic alignment.

## Exact interface

`PossibleDefeat live menu better a` means that some admitted review identifies an eligible plan strictly superior to `a`. `Clear` explicitly requires `menu a` and requires `a` to belong to the comparator's frontier in every admitted review. It says nothing by itself about absolute protection, implementation, authorization or whether choosing an undefeated plan is justified under uncertainty. An empty family is not prohibited by the definitions: it makes the comparative part vacuous, but does not erase eligibility.

`CertificateCoverage oldLive newLive menu oldBetter newBetter` says that for each eligible `a`, an old possible defeat implies a new possible defeat. The representing review and defeating plan may both change. It preserves **the existence of a represented objection sufficient to block this certificate**. It does not preserve all supporting reasons, trace their provenance, prove an adequate actual description, or prevent false objections. A family which spuriously rejects every plan can satisfy one-way soundness while being useless. Semantic fidelity and practical usability have separate premises.

The all-menu result quantifies over a **fixed, interpreted plan domain and fixed comparison families**. Newly admitted facts, new basic claims, changed meanings, newly formed dependencies or changes to G0/J can change these relations, so they require new correspondence. A proof of the global pairwise condition can cover arbitrarily many later menus within its stated domain. Merely passing finitely many menus does not provide that condition. Bidirectional exactness can be established by applying the one-way result in both directions, with the respective no-self-defeat conditions; no separate additional theorem is counted for this corollary.

## Concrete J1/J2 controls

The finite three-plan control reuses `PracticalJudgment.Better`. Both admitted applications agree that `output` has a secondary realization absent from `routine`. Each gives `routine` and `output` the same basic profile, so output is favored in both. Compressing the two reviews to the first is exact and nonvacuous on that old menu.

A newly eligible `repair` restores the basic condition under the omitted application, while failing that condition under the retained one. Consequently the compressed family still certifies output and the full family does not. Output's forgone realization remains true; restoring the omitted objection does not erase it. The old certificate keeps its old-menu scope. Adding a new alternative has not retroactively falsified the old result.

The existential objection summary restores the correct negative certificate without claiming that the two causal applications are now one coherent ranking. These profiles and eligibility changes are supplied mathematical conditions. They do not execute the acquisition of a repair, determine its real authority or identify which application is empirically warranted.

## Arbitrary finite audit and its limit

For every natural number `n`, let plans have intensity `k`, with shared secondary claims `i < k`. Two admitted support applications use the same comparison rule:

- The retained application protects the basic condition for every intensity.
- The omitted application protects it exactly when `k ≤ n`.

This is a simple supplied support-capacity family. Its quantity is a local intensity and secondary-realization count; neither life nor ethical quality is defined as that count. For every audited `k ≤ n`, the complete represented protection profiles agree; the gain function is identical by definition. The theorem `every_finite_prefix_has_exact_compression` proves exact certificate correspondence for every plan under the audited menu.

Once intensity `n+1` is eligible, the compressed family certifies it. Under the omitted application, intensity `n` preserves the basic condition while `n+1` loses it, and J2 favors `n`. The extra secondary realization at index `n` remains an actual loss when taking that lower-intensity alternative. `every_finite_prefix_has_a_failing_next_plan` checks all these claims together for arbitrary `n`.

The quantification is **for every audit bound, this family supplies a case with a failing next plan**. It is not one fixed finite-capacity process which hides the same loss beyond every bound. It is not a theorem that every finite intelligence must fail, that all finite evidence is inadequate, or that known finite domains and justified structural laws cannot support reliable choice. The all-menu theorem states one positive structural route. Whether a real new plan fits such a law remains the substantive correspondence burden.
