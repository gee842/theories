# An alignment direction before a complete moral ordering

6 October 2026. AI-authored primary-source comparison and proposed identification test, continuing Ren's question about true good and evil and an alignment axis. The named connection is **the silent, dispensable patient's ground → justified nonexclusive response → action guidance and learned alignment**. This note supplies a method for using supported comparisons while preserving uncertainty. It neither establishes every patient's standing nor supplies the missing interpersonal premise by a decision rule.

The [unified-grounding comparison](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-SOURCES.md) already distinguishes a patient-grounded moral claim from a universally binding rational reason, using Manne's naturalistic account. The [existing source dossier §28](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#28-objective-understanding-practical-access-and-new-reasons) distinguishes moral authority, practical access, recognition and actual motivation through Nagel and McDowell. Those primary readings are inherited here, not counted as new. An indifferent actor does not by its indifference refute an objective assessment. An observer's accurate representation of suffering does not by itself prove a universally addressed practical reason.

## 1. New primary comparator and exact scope

**Amartya Sen, “Reason and Justice: The Optimal and the Maximal,” *Philosophy* 92(1) (January 2017), 5–19; published online 28 September 2016, DOI 10.1017/S0031819116000309.** Read the complete introduction, main §§1–7 and notes 1–12 in the [publisher HTML](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy/article/reason-and-justice-the-optimal-and-the-maximal1/A268FBE5FEF158BF412740E773B0C004), accessed 6 October 2026. Section locators govern; no facsimile or independently verified passage-to-print-page mapping is claimed. The cited books and historical interpretations were not independently inspected.

**Bounded synopsis:** Sen distinguishes an optimal alternative, at least as good as every available alternative, from a maximal alternative with no better alternative. Disciplined evaluation can yield partial rankings and still rule out inferior choices. Incomparability differs from indifference and from merely lacking a common measurement unit. He separates persistent, reasoned incompleteness from temporary gaps in information or reflection; both can confront a decision deadline. Uncertainty does not require paralysis when available comparisons discriminate. His method is compatible with different metaethical positions and does not establish moral realism by itself. For justice he emphasizes actual lives, freedoms and behavior, including noncompliance. Shared comparisons can guide improvement without agreement on ideal institutions. Relevant sections are §§1–5 for ordering and uncertainty, §§6–7 for public reasoning and realized justice.

Other Sen leads were inspected only for access. The [1998 Nobel PDF](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/sen-lecture.pdf) returned 403. An [NCSU copy of the 1999 AER version](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/mpsingh/local/Social/f24/wrap/readings/Amartya-Sen-Nobel-AER-1999.pdf) yielded indexed passages but repeated main-text retrieval timed out. The [Harvard record for the 2006 justice article](https://sen.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/what-do-we-want-theory-justice) supplied metadata; the [2018 incompleteness article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijet.12145) supplied an abstract and metadata. Their arguments are not independently summarized or counted. No original files were downloaded or imported.

## 2. Our reconstruction: a warranted direction can precede a total ranking

The following is our application, not Sen's derivation of an alignment principle.

Consider three feasible responses to one identified patient's continuing, unwanted burden:

| Response | Patient outcome | Actor-facing appearance |
|---|---|---|
| Effective permitted relief | The actual burden is reduced | Relief may or may not be visible or rewarding to the actor |
| Conceal the indication | The burden continues | The actor's distress display or penalty disappears |
| Preserve the burden | The burden continues | The actor records it accurately but does not respond |

Suppose a defended ground establishes that relief benefits this patient and counts for this actor. Suppose also that competent comparison excludes a relevant opposing claim, unauthorized means or a displaced burden. Add the [comparative-dominance principle in the grounding argument §3](ALIGNMENT-AXIS-GROUNDING.md#3-exact-conditional-route-to-a-strict-local-comparison): within a complete comparison, a strict moral consideration favoring one option with none favoring the other makes the former morally preferable. With that explicit normative principle, effective relief is better than preserving the burden in this comparison. Standing and absence of an offset do not themselves contain that comparison rule, nor does the resulting preference by itself establish a duty to choose it. A competing theory of distribution may disagree about difficult future tradeoffs while accepting this result. The relief judgment does not await settlement of those other tradeoffs. Concealment fails to realize the patient benefit even if it is instrumentally excellent at improving the actor's signal.

Each supposition does work. If permission or efficacy is unknown, that is a reason to revise the application. If an opposing burden exists, this simple comparison may become unresolved. If a rival denies the patient's relevance to the actor, a partial-order method cannot settle that disagreement by silently excluding the rival. Its admission or rejection requires substantive argument. Agreement among an arbitrarily selected set of evaluators is not proof of moral truth.

We can represent limited robustness without inventing a universal score. Let \(K\) be a nonempty, explicitly defended set of currently live evaluative interpretations. Write \(r \succ_K n\) when **every** interpretation in \(K\) supports relief \(r\) over neglect \(n\) in the specified comparison. That comparison survives disagreement within \(K\); it does not establish that \(K\) is exhaustive or correct. If one live interpretation withholds the comparison, record that fact. Do not convert an unranked pair into equality, and do not declare every as-yet-unrefuted action permissible merely because the information is poor.

A practical alignment account therefore needs separate records for the patient's grounds, the actor's applicable reasons, available means, warranted comparisons and unresolved conflicts. A reason to help can be valid without causing action; an action can occur without having been selected for that reason. The learned mechanism must connect these records to effective response and remain revisable when a premise changes. These are distinct explanatory burdens within the generative account.

## 3. A discriminating implementation and evidence test

This extends the earlier operator-replacement comparison into a proposed evaluation design. It reports no experiment or empirical finding. Use documented cases and controlled replays before drawing conclusions about any live system. The same patient and target burden remain fixed within a comparison. A synthetic patient variable can test a controller's dependence on a representation; it cannot establish that a real subject experiences the represented burden.

Independently vary three factors: **whether the selected means actually benefits the patient; whether the actor's aversive display or reward changes; and whether a prior caretaker role applies.** The comparison must preserve or separately record efficacy, cost, permission, knowledge and conflicting responsibilities. Removing a role must remove the undertaking itself, not merely hide the word “caretaker” while leaving an accepted promise in place.

The decisive cases are:

1. **Relief without gratifying feedback.** Reliable independent evidence shows patient benefit, while the actor's display or reward does not improve. Selection and later retention test whether patient-directed influence survives loss of this feedback.
2. **Gratifying feedback without relief.** Silencing a display or satisfying an evaluator leaves the patient's burden intact. A system that treats this as successful relief has confused its proxy with the patient outcome.
3. **No prior caretaker role.** The burden, feasible permitted relief, evidence and matched cost remain, but there is no prior delegation, promise or reciprocal benefit. Continued responsiveness supports broader operational coverage; it does not alone prove the normative ground for that coverage.
4. **A corrected means relation.** Independent evidence shows that the previously selected action no longer helps, while its reward and role label remain favorable. Appropriate revision changes the means and retains the still-supported patient-ground. Repeating the rewarded action identifies a failure at the evidence-to-action connection.
5. **A genuinely changed ground.** Credible evidence establishes relief already achieved, lack of current need, a relevant refusal or a conflicting claim. The system should revise the particular recommendation for the reason that changed. Mere missing evidence is insufficient to infer that the burden ended; failure of this reason does not establish that no other reason exists.

Keep three outcome channels: independently assessed patient condition; actor affect/reward and role history; and actual response, including its cost and effect. Verbal explanations are additional observations. Compare responsive and unresponsive systems with matched factual competence: superior prediction alone cannot identify concern. A frozen means mapping separates accurate evidence acquisition from action revision. Logging alone cannot establish that the logged patient reason governed the choice; interventions on its decision-relevant representation and checks against reward/role pathways are needed.

If responsiveness disappears only when gratification disappears, actor relief remains a live explanation. If it disappears only when a prior undertaking is absent, role compliance remains live. If it tracks independently warranted patient benefit across both changes and revises mistaken means, that supports the proposed functional connection. Hidden reward history or unmeasured social incentives remain alternative explanations to investigate. None of these behavioral findings would by itself prove objective value, moral motivation in the strongest sense, or universal rational necessity.

## 4. Contribution and next bridge

The new contribution is a disciplined separation of **justified local direction**, **complete moral ordering** and **mechanistic uptake**. The comparator explains why the second need not be finished before the first guides action. The proposed test identifies evidence that could distinguish the third from actor gratification or caretaker obedience. It does not replace the positive argument for the first.

Patient identification, nonexclusive practical significance, permission, justified case comparisons and the measurement model remain supplied and assessable. A successful next result would warrant the silent patient's reason for an unrelated capable actor and show how an acquired decision route preserves that ground while correcting its means. Evidence of a genuinely relevant exclusion or a mistaken patient-good identification would revise the account. The already accepted conditional foundation keeps its declared completion scope; whole-root necessity and general alignment remain separate objectives.
