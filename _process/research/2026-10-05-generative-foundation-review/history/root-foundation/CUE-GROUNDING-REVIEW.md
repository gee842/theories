## Verified source and bounded empirical premise

The article is Gerber, Schweinfurth and Taborsky (2020), *The smell of cooperation*, DOI [10.1098/rspb.2020.2327](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2020.2327), not the initially suggested `.2371`. [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7739497/) is the primary methods source; the named data deposit is [Dryad 10.5061/dryad.h18931zjd](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.h18931zjd).

The trained female rats repeatedly encountered experimental conditions. Pulling made food accessible to a neighboring animal; frequency and first-pull latency were recorded. Noncooperator training differed, and the platform was blocked during noncooperative experience. In experiments 3a/3b, the manipulated odor originated from another room, independently of the neighboring nonhelping recipient; cue timing differed. Experiment 4 compared the same odor donor with pulling access against a neutral situation without access. These manipulations do not independently measure ingestion, welfare, energy expenditure or future survival. The published statistical analysis accounts for repeated focal animals. [Methods, §§2a–e](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7739497/).

The transition under examination should therefore be named **externally acquired social cue → recipient-directed food-access action**. “Target-specific cognition” would add an unmeasured claim: routing a pull to the sole available recipient does not demonstrate discriminating among possible beneficiaries.

## Contrast contract before inspecting local results

1. **Primary identifying contrast:** experiment 4, within focal animal, cooperative-access odor versus neutral/no-access odor from the same donor. Report paired count differences in their original units, individual paired differences, sample size, zero differences and uncertainty. It is informative about dependence on the donor's current experimental condition beyond its fixed identity. It is not an isolated intervention on benevolent intent, cooperative chemical content or recipient benefit.
2. **Supporting contrasts:** experiments 3a and 3b separately, cooperative-source versus noncooperative-source odor while the local recipient's observed role is held fixed. Their chief relevance is the separation of cue source from action beneficiary. Report both pull counts and first-pull timing; do not pick the favorable endpoint after seeing significance. Do not count 3a and 3b as independent animals without a verified identity map.
3. **Timing and modality context:** experiments 1 and 2 may clarify sensory access and experience/test timing, but do not pool their rows into an apparently large independent sample for the primary contrast. Analyze their actual factorial and repeated-unit structure if used. Treat direct and generalized paradigms separately unless a prespecified interaction addresses their difference.
4. **Primary interpretation independent of p-values:** estimate the observed paired contrast. A positive contrast would support cue sensitivity in this apparatus; a small or unstable estimate would restrict that support. Neither result establishes an internal representational format. An insignificant contrast cannot certify equivalence, and a significant count contrast cannot certify a welfare effect.

These are identification priorities, not a commitment to one statistical package. A defensible simpler paired analysis is preferable to an unstable fitted model, provided its estimand and difference from the authors' analysis are explicit. Any exact sign-flip/randomization claim needs the actual assignment/exchangeability justification. If only a signed-rank approximation is available, disclose zero/tie handling and its assumptions. Selection of a simpler method after reading rows must be recorded.

## Mechanisms that these contrasts can and cannot separate

The competing models should make different observable predictions before receiving theoretical names. A strictly local-recipient-history-only model with no odor input predicts no remote-odor contrast when local history is fixed; a robust contrast would disfavor that **restricted** model. It would not disprove recipient modeling generally: such a model can also admit external context as input.

A cue-triggered action propensity, generalized reciprocity, associative learning and a richer context-sensitive recipient model can predict the same aggregate pull difference. The present contrast alone cannot identify which one operates. A single beneficiary also prevents separating a general increase in pulling from correct beneficiary selection. Experiment 4 addresses a fixed donor-identity explanation, while the access manipulation can still change exertion, arousal and other contextual cues. The active chemical component and its causal specificity require additional interventions.

“Noncooperator” is an experimental treatment label. Blocked performance and differential training do not establish refusal despite capacity, moral blame, deception or a stable hostile intention. Receiving a remote cue and helping a different animal can be a valid generalized policy; calling it cognitive misbinding requires an independently justified target-specific objective and evidence of the animal's attempted attribution.

The apparatus supplies training, action opportunity, food and social exposure. The reanalysis does not derive these supports from the proposed foundation, measure their formation, or test whether continued cognition requires every external relation. Any claim about resource-dependent cognition must distinguish a causal enabling condition from the information content manipulated here.

## Acceptance and revision conditions

Accept a completed **bounded reanalysis** only if provenance, units, contrasts, missingness, estimates and uncertainties are reproducible and the wording respects the limits above. Reproducing an author p-value is insufficient without an interpretable contrast; failing to reproduce it does not alone invalidate the original experiment when models differ.

The result can change our account by ruling out the inference that helpful delivery identifies warranted recipient-specific reasoning. It can also supply actual observations against which a narrowly specified cue-independent policy fails. It cannot establish a new experiment, general rat cognition, an ethical justification, human governance, empirical AI alignment, the complete concern-to-renewal chain or a universal favorable direction. Physical food access, actual consumption, organismic benefit and preservation of a future must remain separate claims.

The next discriminating empirical design would vary cue source and beneficiary identity independently, include meaningful alternative beneficiaries, and separately record action, delivery and recipient consequences. That is a proposed design, not work performed here. The present reanalysis should first establish exactly what the public observations warrant.

## Review of the proposed identification statement

The root proposed an elementary observational-equivalence argument. It is valid at the following scope. For a fixed observational design, let `O` denote recorded cue condition, `H` the permitted observed history and `Y` the count outcome. Any richer model, including one with an unobserved recipient representation, induces a conditional distribution `K(y | o,h)` on the observed contexts. A descriptive response model assigned that same kernel has the same conditional count predictions on those contexts. Values outside observed support are arbitrary and receive no empirical identification from this argument.

For **whole-sequence** equality, `H` must retain the complete relevant observed past, or the alternative process must otherwise preserve shared dependence. Equality of a one-trial marginal kernel does not by itself reproduce correlated repeated trials or shared-donor effects. With the full observed history, ordinary chain-rule factorization supplies the claimed sequence equivalence under the fixed design. This is an existence argument for a saturated descriptive alternative, not evidence that a memoryless cue rule fits all data or that the alternative is a simpler biological mechanism.

Consequently these restricted observables alone cannot identify recipient-indexed internal representation or moral concern without further model restrictions or discriminating interventions. This does not show that the animals lack such capacities, erase the empirical cue contrast, or establish equivalence under new beneficiary-choice or source-mismatch interventions. The argument concerns identification from a specified observation scheme; it is not a new empirical experiment or a completion of cognitive explanation.
