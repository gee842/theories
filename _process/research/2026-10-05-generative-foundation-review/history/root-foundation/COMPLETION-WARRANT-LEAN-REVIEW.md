7 October 2026. This is independent of those six modules' authorship, not external peer review. The reviewer read their complete statements and proof bodies against [A1–A5 and the completion examples](MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSIBILITY-MATH.md) and [W1–W6](COMPLETION-WARRANT-MATH.md). Final clean compilation belongs to the integrated runner; this statement review does not substitute for it.

**Decision:** accept the reviewed mathematical correspondences at their explicit conditional scope. No conclusion has been hidden in a stronger representation premise than the corresponding hand theorem. The scope limits below must remain visible.

## 1. Ideal admissibility: W1, W2 and W2a

`Ideal` contains a predicate on an arbitrary supplied poset, nonemptiness, lower closure and directedness. `DirectedFamily` is genuinely nonempty and inclusion-directed; `familyUnion` constructs an ideal and its two order bounds are proved. The principal-family reconstruction is established independently before it is used in the admission proof.

`FiniteRefutable C` is stated as a finite-content exclusion witness. `ScottClosed C` is separately stated through lower closure and arbitrary nonempty directed unions. Their equivalence is proved rather than defined. The lower-basis representation, basis uniqueness and equality of permitted principals with the union of admitted finite content follow from those premises. The formalization does not assume that an arbitrary admission predicate already has this form.

The hull is *defined* by all ideals contained in the union of old finite content. Its closedness, containment, leastness, uniqueness and preservation of finite content are then proved. This is a legitimate construction, not a definition of “least” that evades the required argument. `hull_joint_content` also checks the incompatibility boundary: one new ideal cannot combine two approximations without an old admitted ideal containing a common extending approximation.

For W2a, `AvoidingBasis` excludes approximations extending an independently specified forbidden element. The equivalence with ideals containing no forbidden element and the principal classification are substantive proofs. `respects_maximal` unfolds the natural maximality formulation; the separate uniqueness result correctly requires both finite refutation and the exact permitted-principal classification. The prohibitions alone are not promoted to a unique admission class.

The lower-subtype ideal construction has actual restriction/lifting maps, inverse identities and order correspondence. Empty predicates and even an empty supplied poset are covered without inventing an empty ideal. The formal scope is slightly more general than the nonempty-poset hand statement.

## 2. Compact approximation role: A1, A2, A2a and A3

`Dcpo.sup` ranges over every predicate with a nonempty directed-family proof, not merely finite sets, computable sets or countable chains. `Compact` quantifies over all those directed subsets. This preserves the substantive full-admission and compactness premises of the hand argument.

A1's embedding, union supremum, principal compactness, principal generation and characterization of every compact ideal as principal are proved. A2's extension is the supremum of the monotone map's image on an ideal. Continuity and uniqueness are both proved; agreement on principal generators alone is not treated as sufficient. Arbitrary nonmonotone or noncontinuous interpretations receive no extension guarantee.

For A2a the truth-value target is `Prop` ordered by implication. `positive_reading_membership` identifies the extended reading with membership of the named approximation, and `positive_readings_separate` proves separation of ideals. This is a faithful proposition-valued formulation. It does not formalize all mathematical truth or an effective decision procedure.

`BasisRole` assumes exactly the A3 conditions: a faithful order embedding; compact images; a nonempty directed set of named approximants below each target point; and that point's reconstruction as their supremum. In particular, `generates` is an explicit original A3 premise, not a newly smuggled assumption that the target is already isomorphic to the ideal completion. `profile_extend` uses compactness and order reflection to prevent extra finite information; `extend_profile` uses the declared generation premise. The maps' order behavior, inverse identities and uniqueness fixing the basis are then proved. The ideal construction is separately shown to inhabit this role.

The proof does not derive full completion from bare generativity, physical realization from mathematical existence, or a uniquely necessary metaphysical interpretation. The relevance of the named role to an actual mathematical subject remains an explanatory application.

## 3. General examples and counterexamples

`FinitePart` is an extensional subset predicate with a finite-list witness. The maps between ideals of finite parts and arbitrary subset predicates have inverse and order proofs for any type, establishing the A5 order correspondence without enumerating a finite powerset. `boundedPart` covers every natural-number subset by increasing bounded finite parts; its classical definition does not purport to enumerate a noncomputable subset effectively.

`ideal_finite_upper` and `finite_lub_preserved` establish actual finite-LUB conservation. They even handle an empty list when an original least element supplies that LUB. The countdown obstruction includes a concrete fixed-budget transition relation, an induction identifying the state at step `n`, arbitrarily long finite runs with appropriately selected budgets, and impossibility of one infinite run with a fixed budget. Thus it records the relevant quantifier difference within a concrete transition model, not just an unrelated logical formula.

The infinity reading is proved not continuous at a directed union of principal natural-number ideals. The original natural order's lack of a greatest element and the completed order's greatest ideal establish the underlying contrast in the first-order example, without claiming a general formal semantics of first-order theories. Right append has an explicit prefix-order counterexample.

`CompletionBoundaries` now checks the two dependency witnesses as well. The noncompact-infinity carrier is represented by the ideals of the natural order. It has a checked supremum of the finite principal points; completing this carrier again gives a different supremum from the old infinity's principal image. Distinct ideals collapse under the extension of the identity interpretation. Faithful comparison and generation by predecessors persist, while compactness fails. This verifies the claimed mathematical obstruction in that explicit carrier; it does not rely on an unproved topological analogy.

The missing-generation witness is `Unit` embedded as `False` in the truth-value order. The embedding is faithful and compact, its below-point approximation families are directed, and their supremum at `True` is proved to be `False`. The exact A3 generation condition therefore fails. Both examples check the distinct work of the original premises without pretending that dropping a premise automatically invalidates every possible completion.

## 4. History carriers and finite-branching lifting

`HistoryIdeals` defines finite words as natural lengths with coordinate functions and infinite words as full traces. Every ideal is classified by bounded versus unbounded lengths. Bounded ideals are proved principal; unbounded ideals produce one consistent union trace through directed compatibility. Surjectivity, injectivity and all four order comparisons establish the full finite-plus-infinite correspondence. Restriction/lifting through a lower finite-word language gives A4 for arbitrary prefix-closed admitted languages, rather than only the full tree. No infinite-extension condition is silently imposed on a finite dead end.

`FiniteOnly` supplies the exact W4 ideal example. `PropertyClass K` includes every finite principal history and exactly the infinite histories satisfying `K`; its lower closure, full Scott hull and exclusion of any failed infinite property are checked. The recurrence instance therefore proves the exact W5 ideal claim, in addition to the earlier infinite-only prefix-topology claim. The W6 ideal instance does not require prefix realization by infinite represented outputs: every finite history is separately admitted by this carrier's definition. That is correct and differs from the infinite-only theorem, where prefix realization remains necessary. The closed-language boundary theorem connects the infinite-only topology to the ideal-language result without conflating the carriers.

`FiniteBranching` proves the finite-list unbounded-candidate principle from depth monotonicity, then constructs a ray from an unbounded node by classical selection. Finite initial roots and arbitrary finite depth give the stated lifting conclusion. The labelled theorem retains the requested position and concrete state together, proves that the position advances exactly once per step, and only then projects the resulting path to concrete states. This is an actual requested-word lifting argument, not the invalid exchange of separate finite witnesses for one coherent path.

The generic relation can be instantiated on complete admitted concrete histories, so prefix-closed obligations are retained in the nodes and extensions. If finite branching is known only on reachable histories, use that restricted history carrier; the theorem's `children` hypothesis ranges over the supplied carrier. Its list-valued enumeration is mathematical data and can be chosen classically. Nothing in the checked conclusion supplies an effective infinite-branch selector, indefinite physical enactment, or a guarantee of an added limit-only obligation. The role of full-history correspondence remains an explicit application requirement. `CompletionBoundaries.guarded_concrete_lifting` additionally filters roots and extensions by an arbitrary admitted-node predicate and proves that every node of the constructed branch retains it. Instantiating nodes by histories gives a formal preservation interface for the declared prefix conditions; it does not prove a condition on the infinite limit merely from its finite restrictions.

## 5. Interpretation for the normative priority

The preservation inference is accepted only in the following conditional form: if an ongoing obligation has independent warrant and a proposed continuation claims to preserve it, checking only its finite-prefix compatibility can be insufficient. W5 supplies a genuine counterexample to that proposed sufficiency test. It does not establish that every obligation is global or that recurrence is itself ethically required.

A finite Lean derivation and a finite segment of observed behavior are different evidence. `PrefixSafety.allTrue_recurs` explicitly proves an infinitary recurrence statement with a finite kernel-checkable proof. No result licenses “Lean cannot prove liveness.” A generating law, invariant or verified refinement can support an infinite guarantee, with its actual premises retained.

The informed-finality rival in the [current normative integration](NORMATIVE-WARRANT-INTEGRATION.md) survives. It can recognize serious burdens, fallibility and the difference between suppression and an answer, yet argue that a warranted completion or a costly assessment justifies stopping. The closure theorems do not refute that claim: the dispute concerns the condition's authority and scope. A legitimate resolution can fulfill a claim; a justified change can alter its scope; an adequate substitute can preserve its function without keeping an incumbent channel. Demanding that every inherited obligation continue forever would assume the normative conclusion and erase these cases.

The meaningful next test retains the actual claim, burden, completion cost and proposed authority while asking whether the claimed finality answers the relevant reasons or merely prevents their effective appearance. A mathematical preservation certificate can expose a mismatch between an unchanged declared obligation and its implementation. It cannot by itself determine whether the obligation should remain unchanged, who is entitled to revise it, or whose loss may be imposed. Those remain questions within the same generative relationship, not facts assigned to separate ethical and causal realms. The formal continuation therefore supports the normative priority through a precise implementation requirement; it neither displaces normative warrant nor proves universal practical authority.

Its correspondence table and argument preserve these limits, retain the informed-finality rival and explicitly keep first normative warrant unresolved. Its validation claims remain conditional on the root's fresh integrated build; this statement review did not run that build. The suggested clarification concerning accurate technical learning was sent to root: such learning alone is insufficient, but learned facts together with a checked generating rule can supply an infinite guarantee.
