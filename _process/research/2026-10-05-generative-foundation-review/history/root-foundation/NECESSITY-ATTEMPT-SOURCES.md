## 3. Three inferences that must stay separate

First, rejecting an ultimate proprietor does not remove the actual differentiation of undergoing. A continuing experiential history can be constituted within one unfolding. Its adversity need not belong to a metaphysically independent substance to remain real. Imagining wholly disconnected flashes would change this target; no inference about our restricted case should depend on accepting that substitution.

Second, special concern and exclusive concern differ. Continuity, privileged access and responsibility may justify stronger attention to some outcomes while leaving other outcomes with positive practical relevance. Showing that an account permits special concern does not establish that a stranger's relief contributes nothing. Consequently, a criticism of equal weighting or unlimited altruism leaves the present minimum open. This is a substantive scope correction, not an inference that the minimum is already true.

Third, desire independence is not ground exhaustion. A sufferer's reason can precede any reflective ownership judgment or adopted self-care project. It still remains possible that the reason is jointly constituted by adversity and a real self-related continuity. Answering “because it hurts” can identify the salient ground without settling that completeness claim. A rival need not posit an ultimate self to make this proposal.

The positive candidate instead treats the experiential adversity as identifying the response's point, and the practical relation as locating an eligible way of answering it. The patient reference preserves whose undergoing would change; it need not function as a restriction on who can have reason. That is a meaningful explanation, but its nonexclusive scope needs vindication. Neither classical authority nor replacing “private” with “impersonal” supplies it.

## 4. The discriminating test

Retain the same severe, still-unwanted experiential episode and a genuinely effective, permissible, patient-preserving relief possibility. Compare suitably informed response positions while preserving relevant consequences and protections. Do not substitute a different patient, erase a continuing life or infer permission from benefit alone. Compare the same kind of contributory reason; a prudential reason cannot silently become an ethical reason merely because the operator changes.

The candidate predicts that changing the operator does not erase the response's patient-grounded contribution. The restrictive rival predicts that an independently described relation can constitute the difference between benefit and practical favor. Global renaming cannot decide this: a rule indexing each agent's reasons to its own continuity survives such renaming. Nor does the imagination of being the sufferer remove the self-relation.

The explanatory question is whether continuity accounts for access, priority and special responsibility, or also constitutes the existence of all baseline favor. The rival must justify the stronger role; the candidate must justify its alleged redundancy. Equal origin in generation is insufficient because the same unfolding also forms destructive processes. A successful generative history is not thereby a good one.

The existing adoption and operator comparisons already pose this test. This reading sharpens their target: preserve differentiated continuity while investigating whether its special normative role is exclusive. It does not introduce a new experiment or claim that a controller comparison decides moral truth. Coverage of sufferers who never possess practical agency also requires direct grounding; transfer from their own action-reasons cannot be the only route.

## 5. Result and review

The primary comparison removes an overstrong obstacle: failure to derive equal altruism does not establish failure of minimal universal favor. It also blocks a shortcut: ownerlessness and desire independence do not establish exhaustive patient-based grounding. This leaves a narrower, constructive necessity target rather than a proof of permanent normative irreducibility.
