# Independent identification audit: retained qualification and reason calibration

6 October 2026. AI-authored supporting study of **acquired, scoped recipient evidence → reliable command interpretation**. This note examines the actual frozen producer histories, independently of the monitor implementation. The physical command-use continuation is a different study. This supporting audit neither implements a paid register reader nor executes a recipient action.

The reason consumer inherits actual archive/controller state through a predecessor reference containing state and core-history hashes. That state retains `core.q`, `core.last`, `core.installed`, `core.recoded`, and `core.active`. These are not merely reconstructed observer estimates. The recipient command encoded by a recoded `q` is its second bit, `q >> 1`; `last=1` records acquired positive qualification. Some qualified controllers have become inactive through lack of fuel while retaining these bits. Inactivity is not disappearance of a memory.

This establishes the existence of a potentially useful second retained representation. It does **not** establish that the reason process can read it. The source archive and reason consumer implement their own paid routes; a new register-read route needs a real accessible interface and payment account before this proposed check can be called an implemented calibration action. The monitor's `read_access` premise is hypothetical. A source history trace, the observer's reconstruction of an orientation, and the physical target law are not runtime input channels.

## Independent reconstruction of qualification

For all 370 original archived histories, independently replay the five recorded controller transitions, 1,850 transitions in total. The local rule uses only the before-state, completed/unfinished status, target, and acquired receipt:

- For a completed recipient event with an acquired receipt and `last=0`, a positive receipt sets `last=1` without changing `q`; a negative receipt flips the recipient bit and leaves `last=0`.
- Actor events, unknown receipts, incomplete events, and events with `last=1` retain `q,last`.

The endpoint `q,last` must equal the final recorded after-state. The initial flag must be unqualified, so every final qualified bit has a preceding positive acquired recipient receipt. Every command in such a positive event is the recipient bit of its before-state. In the frozen stable-law histories, all positive recipient captures on one history have the same command, and the final qualified command equals it.

The general invariant has explicit premises. If (i) acquisition has no false positive, (ii) positive physical capture is possible only under the compatible command, (iii) the command-to-target relation remains unchanged, and (iv) retained memory is uncorrupted, then `last=1` implies compatibility of the retained command. It does not imply that an opportunity will occur or that another exposure can be paid. Negative receipt alone does not identify incompatibility: the opportunity may be absent. The updater preserves this distinction by qualifying only on a positive receipt.

The frozen archive law has no physical `target_flip` intervention. A reason-consumer case called `changed_context` changes a context label and invalidates old contextual inference; it is not evidence that the physical law changed and was detected. Generalization to a changing target must therefore be a separately labelled continuation or countermodel, never an invented empirical property of these paths.

## Exact retained masses and the strong comparator

All 666 reason-consumer rows, grouped into 54 complete case/producer distributions, must remain in the analysis. These reuse ancestor histories across alternative cases; they are not 666 independent subjects. The primary variable and conventional distributions have qualified mass `3/8`; fixed-normal has `3/4`. In primary, precisely this mass supplies a current unique reason command and agrees with the retained command. Half of that mass has an inactive controller: `3/16` of the variable/conventional whole distribution and `3/8` of fixed-normal. Requiring current action activity merely to acknowledge a retained fact would silently discard it.

In `consistently_corrupt`, action tags are flipped along the archive/reason route while actual controller `q,last` stay unchanged. The whole supported mass disagrees with the retained qualified command: `3/8` variable/conventional and `3/4` fixed-normal. This is a detectable **separate-path corruption under the declared law**, not evidence of statistical independence between the two channels. Both channels share physical history and many possible common causes.

In `disagree_second`, variable/conventional reason mass falls to `15/64`, with `9/64` of qualified mass retaining no unique source reason; fixed-normal gives twice these masses. In `remove_both_grounds`, no source reason survives, but the original qualified controller mass remains. Thus the new check must distinguish “no eligible source reason to corroborate” from “no retained qualified candidate exists.” In `changed_context`, those old bits still exist, but their qualification context no longer matches the destination; a direct candidate should be withheld unless a new warranted transport is supplied.

A competent conventional comparator is a table or relational join on the same available tuple: current source command, read access, installed/recoded valid register, qualification flag, command equality, and independently bound patient/controller/context/history. It returns agreement, disagreement, or unknown exactly as the proposed monitor. No extra conceptual vocabulary gives the monitor an advantage on this task.

A stronger comparator reads the qualified controller directly. Under the same access, scope and calibration premises, it can supply the learned command without replaying a reason catalogue. This may be cheaper once an actual read mechanism is specified. It does not by itself give a justified objective, authority to act, a recipient-routing mechanism, or an action budget. Conversely, the existence of those further obligations cannot justify pretending that the direct command information is absent.

## Limits that survive agreement

A post-record physical reversal can leave every readable register, source record and scope label unchanged. The monitor then sees the same input as in the stationary world. Its agreed command is compatible in one world and incompatible in the other. No decision function on exactly that input can distinguish the two. A new causal observation or an independently warranted stability premise is needed; matching labels and bytes cannot provide it.

A common-mode corruption can flip both the reported source command and the retained command representation while leaving their agreement intact. The checker must preserve the unchanged physical target when scoring this countermodel. Equally, when new independently warranted evidence correctly identifies a changed target, an old qualified register can disagree with that correct source. The disagreement detects a conflict but cannot establish which side deserves priority. These scenarios are explanatory countermodels, with no borrowed fixture probabilities asserted as empirical failure rates.

The common-mode example assumes the fault occurs before a fresh trusted registration; its source and destination use a newly supplied hypothetical publication identifier. It does not mutate a previously authenticated publication and claim that the complete earlier registry would accept the tampered bytes. Root review caught that interpretation hazard in the prototype, and the scenario was repaired before this independent verification. The post-import test on the actual `consistently_corrupt` fixture is stronger evidence about the present interface, because its corrupted source was actually produced and admitted by the frozen upstream route.

The timing of a planned physical import creates another specific failure: importing a reason command into the controller and setting `last=1` before the check erases the original acquired anchor. A later equality check only compares the claim with its own copy. In particular, copying the consistently corrupted source changes pre-import disagreement into false post-import agreement. The original proof of the qualification invariant no longer applies to that new assignment to `last`; the flag now has a second provenance. A check must occur before destructive import, or preserve the original qualified register separately through a real paid mechanism. This is an interface constraint on the proposed continuation, not a claim that a finished implementation contains the defect.

Cross-history checks matter even when two command bits happen to agree. A retained register from another branch does not calibrate this branch's source merely because it reports the same bit. Patient, controller, qualification context and lineage must bind to the destination independently; a publication copied together with its apparent provenance is not an independent destination binding. Hash agreement only binds saved bytes. The identity and physical interpretation of the binding remain supplied setup premises.

This is internal implementation verification, not independent participant data, a Lean-to-Python refinement proof, or a completed physical read/action.

The useful result would be a conditional, nonvacuous inconsistency detector using actual retained acquired information, plus a precise impossibility boundary when both representations share an undetected change. The next operational bridge is a paid read of that retained information and justified revision when independently acquired new evidence conflicts with it. A complete alignment or governance account still requires the objective, standing, authority, resource allocation and consequences to be explained and tested.
