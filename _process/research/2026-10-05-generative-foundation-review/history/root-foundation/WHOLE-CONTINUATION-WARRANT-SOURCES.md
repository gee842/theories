# Whole continuation and practical force: primary-source assessment

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored supporting assessment for the [universal normative-grounding agenda](NORMATIVE-WARRANT-INTEGRATION.md#universal-derivation-now-governs-this-session). [Ren's current instruction](../../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-07-universal-intelligence-session-goal.md) makes universal meta-ethics of all intelligence the objective. The shared-performance comparison is a discriminator for that objective, not a replacement local finish line. This note adds two directly inspected primary texts to the [earlier source review](NORMATIVE-WARRANT-SOURCES.md); it is neither a new formal proof nor empirical validation.

**Result:** these texts sharpen two positive routes—ends and their revision within activity, and practical rationality grounded in forms of living—while blocking two shortcuts. A successful selected undertaking does not by itself justify its whole continuation. A consideration's ethical significance does not, merely by being called ethical, determine the final ranking. Neither text establishes practical force for every intelligence, and neither settles whether a formed role may exclude every baseline reason arising from an otherwise eligible response.

## 1. John Dewey: an end belongs within its continuing consequences

John Dewey, *Human Nature and Conduct* (1922), Part III, “The Nature of Aims,” pp. 223–237. [Original text reproduced by the Mead Project at Brock University](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey1922_20.html). Inspected the chapter's text, especially the retained printed-page markers 223–229, 232–233 and 235–237. The university-hosted transcription is a primary-text reproduction, not a scan checked against an original copy. The website's editorial introduction is not attributed to Dewey.

**What the text argues:** aims form within conduct as anticipated consequences guiding its next direction. Reaching a harbor redirects activity rather than ending all activity. Treating one favored outcome as self-contained obscures other consequences and can give apparent justification to success at any price. Dewey includes natural and moral aims in this criticism. He also treats means and ends as perspectives on the same actuality and requires attention to the conditions through which an envisaged result can occur.

**Contribution to this investigation:** the performance's completion, ordinary living, recovery and altered commitments can be assessed as related aspects of a continuing history. Ending the performance does not end that history. This supplies a close antecedent for the scope discipline in [the shared method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md), without establishing our complete ontology.

**Our challenge, not Dewey's asserted result:** the informed finality rival already includes the impairment and still ranks completion higher. A criticism of selective attention therefore does not yet answer that rival. The missing defense concerns the ethical character and comparative force of the retained consequences. Nor does revisability establish that every commitment must be reopened indefinitely or that no stable ethical ground is possible. A stable wider concern can require revision of this concrete pursuit. The actual costs and grounds of stopping remain within the same appraisal.

## 2. Philippa Foot: a reason is different from a final verdict

Philippa Foot, “Rationality and Goodness,” originally *Royal Institute of Philosophy Supplements* 54 (2004), pp. 1–13; [publisher bibliographic record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/royal-institute-of-philosophy-supplements/article/abs/rationality-and-goodness/6F40C132DD287D617E3906C21D7D2C55). Direct reading used the article's **2022 reprint**, in Anthony O'Hear (ed.), *Moral Philosophy*, printed pp. **1–21**, available in this [publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781009118408_A45553410/preview-9781009118408_A45553410.pdf). The following page references use the reprint, not the original pagination. Inspected pp. 1–2, 4–6, 8–14 and 17–21 in the extracted author text. This is not a claim to have read *Natural Goodness* in this round, or an endorsement of the preview's editorial foreword.

**What the text argues:** pp. 1–2 assume justice's moral status and distinguish the rationality question from that prior dispute. Pages 10–12 distinguish contributing considerations from final judgments; a consideration called moral has no automatic trumping status. Pages 13–14 acknowledge that merely grouping reason categories leaves a unification problem. Pages 17–19 appeal to requirements of human life beyond growth, survival and reproduction; pp. 20–21 leave open rational nonhuman beings with different practical reasoning.

**Contribution to this investigation:** this directly supports separating baseline favor from final ranking. Its life-form approach also challenges the presumption that another consideration must first serve an intelligence's self-interest before it can be a reason.

**Our challenge:** Foot's initial ethical premises are not a proof of first goodness. The universal programme must additionally explain why the relevant whole realization has practical force across intelligences with different forms of life, rather than infer that force from human reason categories. The source's possibility of different rational forms is a scope warning, not a counterexample disproving every universal relational ground.

## 3. What these sources change in the current argument

The following is our assessment of the repository's candidate, not additional doctrine attributed to either author.

[The actual performance comparison](SITUATED-ETHICAL-DISCERNMENT.md#continuation-test-changing-a-successful-pursuit) preserves a genuine completion under C and genuine loss under R. R instead supports recovery from specified serious sustained impairment; its additional burdens remain bounded and represented. [The whole-grounding account](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#35-revising-a-pursuit-that-still-succeeds) correctly retains the adverse effect, lost achievement and continuing relationship. Calling C technically successful therefore cannot settle the whole appraisal. Calling R protective cannot settle it either: the proposed priority still requires ethical argument.

The same test separates four questions that must not be merged into one premise:

| Question | What the present source assessment contributes | What remains to defend |
|---|---|---|
| Does the differentiated realization have ethical quality? | A route to investigate quality through living and its actual relations rather than selected output or private return. | The constitutive identification itself against a rival retaining the same realization. |
| Does this quality favor an eligible response? | A challenge to treating self-interest or prior endorsement as automatically privileged sources of reasons. | Why the whole relation suffices for baseline practical favor across every intelligence in the stated response position. |
| Which continuation is warranted overall? | A disciplined distinction between relevant reason and final verdict. | Comparative priority amid the real achievement, impairment, completion, permissions and feasible alternatives. |
| Who may act, compel, or end inquiry? | No automatic answer from a goal's importance or from recognizing a reason. | Actual jurisdiction, responsibilities and justified constraints; these may alter permissions without automatically erasing every baseline reason. |

“Eligible response” must be specified through actual capability, access, means and relevant relationships, without defining eligibility as already owing the desired response. The practical relation is differentiated within the whole history, not a bridge between fundamentally separate owners of value. An intelligence's assent, refusal or learning changes that history; none alone determines whether its proposed ground was sufficient.

The strongest role-sensitive rival can grant favorable quality yet contend that a particular responsibility excludes that consideration as a practical basis. The proposed universal account must explain why a warranted exclusion only differentiates force or permissible means, or why and when it may remove baseline favor. The rival must independently justify its boundary; merely naming a jurisdiction cannot settle its scope. Conversely, source support for whole consideration cannot prove that all role boundaries are illicit.

This identifies a useful universal test: **compare two substantive explanations of the same whole history, including its formed role, relevant realization and actual response opportunity.** One account denies baseline favor on independently specified role grounds; the other explains why that same role changes legitimate means or priority while leaving a reason intact. Their accounts must address the relationship itself, not freely toggle a detached predicate. If the role changes the realization's proposed ethical quality, return to that first identification rather than stipulate quality unchanged. This is a proposed philosophical comparison, not a formal countermodel or an executed experiment. It neither presumes a fully connected world nor treats inability to recognize a reason as exemption from its ground.

## 4. Access, evidence and validation limits

Both original-author texts above were directly inspected on 7 October 2026. Their selected passages supply arguments and distinctions, not independent observations of the hypothetical performance. No full external source bytes were imported into the repository. An attempted additional reading of Scanlon's *Rights, Goals, and Fairness* reached Harvard's bibliographic record and image-only scan, but usable text extraction failed; it is therefore **not counted as a read source** and supplies no claim here. This access limit does not concern Scanlon's argument's merits.

The current case's feasibility, impairment and burden comparisons are specified conditions, not medical findings. An empirical application would need evidence for those conditions and for omitted effects or alternatives; such evidence could defeat the application without alone deciding the universal ethical ordering. The source comparison does not warrant a claim of novelty or consensus.

No new mathematical proof is claimed. Any subsequent formal inference must comply with [Ren's Lean requirement](../../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-07-lean-proof-requirement.md), expose its hypotheses and be checked. Formalizing the chosen ethical premise as an assumption will verify conditional consequences, not discharge its substantive warrant.

**Research implication:** pursue the universal connection from defended whole quality to practical favor through actual response relations, with roles and assent as explicit tests. Use the performance and closure cases to discriminate that inference. Neither a source survey, a stronger mechanism nor another successful local cancellation closes the universal objective.
