## 1. Positive argument

The protected object is a living realization within a shared unfolding: its ongoing organization, changing activities, affected relationships and actual losses. A particular institution, provider or molecular arrangement can be one means. Retention, reconfiguration, substitution and ending must be assessed by the histories they make possible and the histories they interrupt. Neither a common whole nor a dependency makes one position owner of the others.

A decision must therefore carry three relationships together. First, an independently identified living activity depends on particular functional relations and joint conditions over a stated horizon. Second, G0 gives living and further generation favorable significance; J2 supplies the explicit scoped priority of basic protection over optional enrichment when other basic claims are preserved. Third, J3/J4 constrain authority, burdens, assignment, participation, selection and discharge. Evidence connects the description to actual circumstances. None of these roles is silently discharged by encoding a desired verdict in a predicate.

The new comparative inference starts from a candidate set that can contain failures. If each failure of the selected basic claim has an eligible restoration preserving all the basic claims the failed plan protects, J2 makes the restoration better. J4 excludes the failed plan from the undefeated choices. Nonempty choices must be separately established. Thus the target becomes a collective requirement **through a disclosed comparative argument**, rather than by deleting all failures from eligibility at the outset. Actual organization/support requirements then transport to the chosen histories as necessary means. Specific assignments follow only after the further role, legitimacy and joint-capacity assessment.

The unrestricted statement “dependency entails duty” is rejected. The sufficient inference requires the actual living correspondence, normative standing, adequate candidate coverage and a restoration that preserves other basic claims. If maintaining a crop or service destroys a contributor's basic living, the simple restoration inference no longer applies. The response is a recorded conflict, additional support, changed means or horizon, mitigation or justified ending under the existing practical account. It is not redefining the contributor's loss as secondary.

This is the universal inference advanced: a common conditional reason structure can apply across differentiated intelligent and practical positions while leaving their particular responsibilities different. It does not establish motivation, compliance or unique necessity of the present vocabulary. The explicit normative-axiom route is retained; proving G0 without premises is not a completion gate for this work.

### No empirical substitution by definitions

`Scheduled` is a local finite service contract. It permits zero-demand epochs and does not require a constant positive output in life. A stable competent count abstracts retention without damage during this particular horizon. `timedProcess` adds both an upper bound and an epoch-realization law: every admitted bounded batch is treated as schedulable within that epoch under the modeled conditions. Extra duration, occupancy and interference must be ruled out or represented; they are not excluded by the count bound itself. Dynamic organization is covered separately by the general batch bridge; those untimed batches must not be identified with clock epochs without another correspondence. The two interfaces are compatible tools, not a claim that one experiment establishes both.

`protect a claim ↔ target a` is an explicit strong correspondence premise. In a real application, the target must include the consequences necessary for that basic living claim, not just a reactor output. The toy example's target is a declared service, and its two-unit contributor ceilings are stipulated spare capacities. Neither a human burden threshold nor the moral adequacy of output is thereby measured or proved. Plan-indexed `ds` and `need` must preserve the same independently identified living claim across alternative means, or carry a separately justified revision. Reducing a demand or moving a deadline until it fits cannot count as fulfilling the original claim. Contributing spare support may lose an optional realization; this is retained by `optional_loss_remains`.

`eligible` is not an arbitrary approval flag in application. It must retain consent/authority, feasible routing, all relevant support and capacities, existing obligations, contributor lives and harms. The restoration premise also requires adequate alternative coverage: leaving out a relevant affected claim or repair route changes the inference. The theorem checks the argument once these meanings are justified; it does not certify them.

A `Trace` is an admissible model history, with concrete intermediate states. Constructing it proves realization **in the model**, not that people have already performed it or molecules took that order. Actual implementation requires the observations listed in the evidence note. “Actual” in inherited theorem names refers to the represented step/trace certificate.

## 4. Adverse cases and substantive rivals

1. **Quantity without competence:** adequate substrate and support cannot execute a positive catalytic demand from zero active organization in the restricted production-only family. Admitted assembly routes change this conclusion, as the existing organization module shows.
2. **Partial competence and deadlines:** the checked pair `[2]` versus `[1,1]` has equal total output and support but different per-epoch competence requirements. Relaxing a deadline is a genuine change of target; it cannot retroactively fulfill an earlier missed need.
3. **Individually feasible but not jointly feasible:** one support unit suffices for an isolated one-step episode but not a two-step continuation from the same stock. No repeated reset is permitted.
4. **Optional loss remains:** the concrete support choice forgoes an actual optional realization. J2 supplies its priority; the lost realization is not erased or declared unreal.
5. **Plural providers:** the target can be a collective requirement without either provider individually being indispensable. Once a justified assignment is settled, the inherited undertaking principles additionally apply; the alternative does not erase that commitment.
6. **Overload and conflicting basic claims:** no full allocation exists beyond shared capacity, and a restoration sacrificing a different basic claim does not satisfy the comparison premise. Existing incomparability/conflict results apply; neither all-or-nothing abandonment nor unlimited personal service follows.
7. **Productive domination:** a coercive arrangement can produce real organization. Rejecting it requires J3/J4 or an identified basic loss, rather than pretending it produces nothing. Output success and legitimacy remain distinct questions within the same history.
8. **Institutional perpetuity:** successful service through another route can release the old provider under the existing handoff conditions. The requirement concerns the living realization and warranted residual responsibilities, not preservation of every old organization.

A serious opposing ethical account can accept all causal evidence and give optional aggregate output priority over a contributor's basic conditions. G0 alone does not logically refute it. Our reason for J2 is that living conditions are already realizations within the favorable whole, not an indefinitely expendable input for optional production. J3/J4 extend this inclusion to standing, bounded burden and authority. That is substantive defended normative content, not a mathematical consequence of a supply balance.
