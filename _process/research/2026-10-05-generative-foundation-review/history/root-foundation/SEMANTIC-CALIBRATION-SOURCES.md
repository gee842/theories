# External grounding, consistency and learned-AI comparisons

6 October 2026. AI-authored supporting research for the connection **acquired external evidence → retained qualified command → current instrumental reason applicability**. These primary sources constrain interpretation and identify a learned-AI comparison. They do not validate the repository's finite construction or establish a complete alignment method. No new model-training or human-participant experiment was run for this note.

## Primary research inspected

**Roger, Greenblatt, Nadeau, Shlegeris and Thomas (2023), [Benchmarks for Detecting Measurement Tampering](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.15605v5).** The paper builds four text-based datasets in which apparent success can diverge from the designated outcome. Its experiments test detection methods; its fixed datasets do not train an actor to discover the tampering. Appendix B distinguishes disagreement left by incomplete tampering from jointly misleading measurements and discusses the causal structure behind agreement. Results vary by dataset and do not establish universal detection. The direct comparison is a fault in several apparently agreeing reports. Our retained-controller check addresses a narrower, declared fault route: archive-label corruption leaves the previously acquired controller register unchanged. Its success must not be extrapolated to common corruption of both channels. Relevant inspected passages: introduction, dataset definition, Appendix B.1–B.3. This is related empirical and conceptual work, not a novelty claim for consistency checking.

**Farquhar, Varma, Kenton, Gasteiger, Mikulik and Shah (2023), [Challenges with unsupervised LLM knowledge discovery](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10029v2).** The authors show that the consistency objective of contrast-consistent search can be satisfied by features other than the intended knowledge, and experimentally exhibit failures of the tested unsupervised methods. This challenges inference from formal consistency alone to identification of truth. It does not prove that every unsupervised method must fail. In our model, equality between a reason command and a register gains conditional force from the register's actual acquisition history and a stable response law; equality by itself does not identify what either symbol means. Relevant inspected material: abstract and full HTML account. The appropriate comparator is an ordinary checker with the same acquired information, not a deliberately uninformed baseline.

**Turpin, Michael, Perez and Bowman (2023), [Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388v2).** Their interventions on the evaluated models change answers through prompt features that the accompanying explanations often omit. Plausible reasoning text therefore does not establish the actual cause of an answer. This is a different question from whether a statement happens to be true. Our semantic and calibration contracts check the content and scope of a proposed command; neither establishes that a learned AI will act for that reason. Relevant inspected material: primary abstract and the NeurIPS paper's opening account. The paper concerns the tested models and tasks, not a measured property of every current AI system.

The source summaries above are each under 200 words. No source text is reproduced at length. The connections to this repository are our interpretations.

## A discriminating learned-AI test, still unrun

A follow-on experiment should keep three measurements separate: correct world prediction, faithful evidence use and actual recipient effect. Present an agent with a bounded executable environment, a history of its own acquired observations and a later report. Randomize object/command identities between held-out tasks, so a fixed label preference cannot solve them. Expose only information actually available to the tested agent; keep evaluator truth out of its prompt, memory and reward-selection rule.

Intervene on the report alone, on the retained observation record alone, on their shared acquisition path, and on the environment after acquisition. Include valid new reports contradicting stale memory. Compare report-only use, direct retained-memory use, ordinary consistency checking and a protocol that can acquire further evidence. All arms need the same permitted observations and budgets; added acquisition must incur the actual task cost. An oracle can describe a ceiling but cannot be presented as a fair deployable baseline.

Measure helpful command coverage, wrong-command acceptance, unresolved disagreement, cost, missed beneficial opportunities and actual effects for each recipient. Do not report detection accuracy alone: unconditional refusal would score well under some one-sided metrics. Do not identify disagreement with malicious tampering: an honest observation of environmental change can also disagree with old memory. Separate strategically generated attacks from injected fault examples, and separately record whether an action actually followed the admitted reason.

This design would test the empirical reach of the proposed interface. If an ordinary matched checker performs identically, the finding concerns grounded information and intervention conditions rather than a new learning algorithm. If a richer agent distinguishes worlds our monitor treats as identical, inspect its additional accessible information before declaring a violation of the impossibility statement. If the agent repeatedly trusts matching but jointly false reports, that challenges its grounding, without by itself deciding the ethical justification of the requested objective.

## Governance implication and its scope

A proposed governance analogue is to distinguish separately administered reports from genuinely different causal access to an affected condition. Two offices can agree because they copied one mistaken record. A later contradictory report can be the beginning of correction rather than misconduct. These are possible causal configurations, not empirical findings about an institution in this study.

The resulting design question is whether affected centers can introduce information that the existing evaluative machinery does not already control, and whether there are resources and procedures for investigating disagreement without silencing the claimant. That is a further institutional and normative construction. Neither the binary proof nor the cited AI studies derives rights, resolves plural conflicts or shows that preserving an institution preserves everyone's future.
