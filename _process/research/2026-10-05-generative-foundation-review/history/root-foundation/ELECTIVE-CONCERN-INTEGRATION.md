## Result and inherited premises

The new work separates selecting an option, creating a project reason, forming an actual relational good and undertaking a directed responsibility. It allows an exercise of will to be proposed as a genuine ground, without requiring every power to be an event triggering a prior complete norm. The theoretical warrant for a power remains assessable without demanding antecedent reasons that guide every detail of its exercise.

Its restricted inferential result is sound: establishing one created ground does not establish the absence of another ground for the same available act. The stronger rival can accept given self-regarding, noninterference and role reasons while restricting positive assistance to authored connections. The canonical therefore does not substitute a refutation of unrestricted voluntarism for an assessment of that narrower claim. It retains the explicit burden on the direct account to justify C2.

The affirmative conclusion remains comparative and defeasible. Actual patient relief is defended as a reason to consider the response before commitment, while self-direction can create additional grounds and support protected choices. The current argument finds the case for created reasons stronger than the case for their exclusive control of assistance favor. This is neither a contradiction proof against the rival nor a derivation from causal generation.

The prospective identification refinement is new to this round: when intentional helping is used to identify the supposed elective condition, selecting successful helping also selects that condition. Successful action and subsequent correction then cannot distinguish a reason preceding adoption from one created through it. Independent characterization of the power and prospective assessment of the option are required. This limits an observation route; it is not a behavioral result or a normative verdict produced by an experiment.

The restricted patient-domain hypothesis, prospective relief judgment, private adoption/valid-withdrawal comparison and reason-transport result are inherited. The current contribution refines their use rather than proving them again. Realization of an experiencing subject, C1's bounded bad-for identification, eligible means and C2's interpersonal scope remain substantive premises. The legitimacy, conditions and interactions of reason-creating powers also require warrant. Adding a proposed baseline can change whether a particular power's exercise conditions hold; the canonical explicitly rejects automatic preservation of all previous comparisons.

The concurrent rescue requirement is correctly treated as downstream: it starts from moral favor and adds a requiring comparison. It cannot be reused as an independent proof of that starting favor. Moral favor, practical or rational reasons, permission, duty, authority, motivation and implementation remain distinct. No complete concern, wisdom or priority account follows from this round.
