# From acquired prediction to other-directed evaluation: evidence and discriminators

6 October 2026. AI-authored evidence and comparator contribution to Ren's generative-foundation research. **Status: bounded primary-source audit and an unrun experimental design.** No participants were recruited, models deployed, raw data reanalyzed, or new empirical observations obtained. External sources below are link-only references; no source bytes were imported or altered.

The named connection is **acquired prediction → formation and revision of the prediction-to-action/evaluation coupling → action responsive to another center's independently measured condition without a compensating private return**. This follows the [paid prediction/action construction](../../../../theories/becoming/history/PREDICTION-ACTION.md) and supplies empirical constraints for its next bridge. It does not substitute a donation task for the full derivation from organizational dependencies.

## 1. What the current result establishes, and what remains to explain

The paid prediction construction demonstrates acquired records, correction after reversal, and productive use under one inherited resource history. Its inverted-action comparison also shows why accurate prediction does not determine constructive action: a learner can predict the failures produced by its own action coupling. The observation decoder, representation, action rule and functional retrofit remain supplied.

The [concern theory](../../2026-10-05-concern-theory/THEORY.md) already states the corresponding social gap. Accurate recognition can coexist with an identity gate preventing another's represented condition from entering evaluation. Shared routing supplies a conditional route to learned other-directed valuation, but its installation and selection are further explanatory tasks. A comparison that installs shared and gated circuits can establish their different consequences; it cannot by itself show why the shared circuit forms.

The relevant empirical quantities are therefore different:

- prediction of the recipient's actual outcome;
- the causal influence of that predicted outcome on choice, apart from represented private returns;
- efficacy of the chosen action for the recipient;
- actual costs of observing, retaining, revising and acting;
- whether the evaluative route changes with experience and survives identity revelation, changed cues and new recipients.

Felt empathy, shared neural activation, functional valuation and justified concern are not interchangeable readouts. The present bridge concerns functional other-directed evaluation. Experience and moral standing retain their separate explanatory and normative obligations.

## 2. Primary evidence, separated by the interface it identifies

These selected studies constrain different parts of the connection. They are neither a systematic review nor replications of one complete mechanism. Each source summary is under 200 words and contains no extended quotation.

### A. Acquired value can attach to an abstract representation of another's state

Zhang and Hackel, *Reward Association With Mental States Shapes Empathy and Prosocial Behavior* (2025), [author-hosted article](https://www.hackellab.org/media/zhang-hackel-2025-psych-science.pdf), DOI [10.1177/09567976251351304](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976251351304). See Experiments 1a–2 and Experiment 3 Results, especially pp. 15–19.

Across four experiments, participants learned congruent or incongruent relations between a target's affective state and their own monetary outcomes. Empathic feelings transferred to new situations involving the trained target, including a rating phase after further monetary change was ruled out. This is relevant to an acquired representation-to-valence connection. It is not transfer to an untrained recipient. In Experiment 3, the overall contingency-reversal effect on optimal choice was nonsignificant (p=.131); the reported contingency-awareness analysis was exploratory. Decisions in that experiment still generated monetary outcomes for the actor, so it is not a clean demonstration of costly concern without private payoff. The authors' design and our proposed mechanism must remain distinct. Positive and negative associations also permit opposing directions of acquired valuation.

This extends the [existing concern evidence memo](../../2026-10-05-concern-theory/conceptual-evidence.md) by making the absence of a clean zero-private-return behavioral endpoint explicit.

### B. Self-related affect and vicarious affect can be functionally coupled

Rütgen et al., *Placebo analgesia and its opioidergic regulation suggest that empathy for pain is grounded in self pain* (2015), [primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611649/), DOI [10.1073/pnas.1511269112](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1511269112). See Abstract, experimental rationale and the two experiments.

Placebo analgesia reduced first-hand pain and reported pain empathy in an fMRI experiment; a second experiment used an opioid antagonist to block the corresponding placebo effects. This is stronger evidence for a functional connection than spatial overlap alone. It does not show acquisition of concern, the formation of the shared machinery, or costly helping. Changing an affective response is not equivalent to changing whose outcome governs action. Our proposed behavioral study therefore does not use empathy ratings or shared activation as its primary concern measure, and requires no pharmacological intervention.

### C. Conventional reinforcement learning already describes learning to benefit another

Lockwood et al., *Neurocomputational mechanisms of prosocial learning and links to empathy* (2016), [primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5024617/), DOI [10.1073/pnas.1603198113](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1603198113). See Figure 1, Experimental Procedures and Participant Instructions.

Thirty-one analyzed male participants learned symbol-outcome contingencies for self, another person or no beneficiary. Their modeled learning differed across these conditions. The recipient was presented as unaware of the task and payment as anonymous, reducing a direct reciprocity interpretation. The other was a confederate, and the task supplied the beneficiary framing. The Procedure section states that experimental participants actually received a fixed £30, including a £7 supplement, although they believed outcomes determined extra payment and could earn the confederate an extra payment. Displayed outcomes and believed rewards therefore do not establish independently verified recipient welfare delivery. Learning an effective choice for a believed beneficiary does not show that other-directed valuation was newly acquired; neither does it establish formation costs or a material construction history. This source supplies a conventional learning comparator, including separately parameterized self/other learning and a no-beneficiary control.

### D. Joint outcome learning need not use a literal self-to-other simulation architecture

Rhoads et al., *Neurocomputational basis of learning when choices simultaneously affect both oneself and others* (2025), [publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64424-9), DOI [10.1038/s41467-025-64424-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64424-9). See Results, computational model comparisons and Discussion.

Across three preregistered studies, an integrated action-value account with updates differing by beneficiary and valence characterized the observed choices better than the tested parallel-value simulation alternatives. Tasks included mutual benefit, mutual cost, altruistic and instrumentally harmful options. This makes integrated value learning a necessary rival to a shared-representation transfer explanation. Model comparison among those candidates does not identify every possible neural implementation, demonstrate universal dominance, or explain the developmental origin of other-directed weights. A generative account should accommodate this route if it fits the evidence, rather than define concern as requiring one privileged simulation mechanism.

### E. Identity and private escape must be separated from recipient protection

Hein et al., *Neural Responses to Ingroup and Outgroup Members' Suffering Predict Individual Differences in Costly Helping* (2010), [publisher primary text](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.09.003), [publication record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20920798/). See Results and helping-task description.

Soccer fans could accept pain to reduce another fan's pain. Alternatives included watching an attractive video instead of watching the other's suffering. Helping and its neural/self-report predictors differed with group relationship and evaluation. This supports measuring costly action, affiliation and private avoidance separately. The reported neural associations are not interventions establishing a uniquely caring circuit. Existing identities and dispositions were present before the experiment; the study is not a derivation of their formation. We reuse the logical separation of assistance and escape in a non-pain task, rather than repeat its stimulation procedure.

## 3. What these findings allow and what they leave open

There is evidence for modifiable valuation, learning about others' outcomes, functional self/other affect coupling, and identity-sensitive costly helping. There is no single result here that follows the full sequence from newly formed material machinery through acquired self-related significance to a newly formed, portable concern for distinct centers without private return. These studies also do not establish that there is exactly one such route.

The earlier [dependency-to-regulation evidence audit](../../../history/2026-10-05-support-and-shared-generativity/completion/ENDOGENOUS-REGULATION-EVIDENCE.md) supplies the upstream standard: distinguish the physically accessible signal from an analyst's success label, and distinguish induction, passive persistence, revision within a lineage and population selection. The same standard applies here. A target's benefit may correlate with the actor's reward without entering the actor's evaluation on its own account. Conversely, an acquired end can continue to matter after its historical private reinforcement ends; its history alone does not settle its present functional role.

## 4. A discriminating experiment: a design contract, not a registered trial

The bounded hypothesis is: **experience that aligns another's verified consequence with an actor's acquired significance can change subsequent costly recipient-directed choices; some of this change transfers across identity and remains sensitive to the recipient's actual outcome after private return is removed.** A successful test would support this route in the declared adult task. Adults bring pre-existing evaluations; it would not establish creation of concern from zero.

Use consenting adults, optional experimental bonuses and benign task consequences. Guaranteed payment is never put at risk. Real recipients give preferences before decisions; delivered bonuses or released optional task time are recorded independently of the actor's interface. The actor cannot be repaid, rated, punished or contacted by the recipient. Payment procedures make the absence of an audience and future interaction credible, and beliefs about those procedures are measured. A public-feedback arm, if wanted, is a separately declared incentive comparison, not pooled into the private condition.

### Phase 1: acquire and revise a self-related prediction/action mapping

Participants learn a small unfamiliar action-outcome environment from actual receipts, with a bounded bonus budget. Response work, optional information acquisition and record access each have declared prices or measured time costs. One route becomes unproductive at a prespecified hidden reversal. Distinguish improved prediction from changed action: collect sparse incentivized forecasts whose payment rule is independent of helping, and measure whether later action uses corrected evidence. All such forecasting payments are outside the critical no-private-return trials, and their training contribution is reported.

Do not let an experimenter label of success furnish the missing teaching signal. Feedback reports the actual receipt and source availability when observable. Include an ambiguous-failure condition where an absent return may mean a wrong action or an unavailable source. Retain failed histories and participants unable or unwilling to purchase further evidence. An analysis confined to successful learners would change the population claim.

### Phase 2: randomize the transfer history while matching information and resources

Randomize congruent self/recipient outcome pairing versus a yoked uncorrelated pairing, with the same marginal self returns, recipient outcomes, number of observations, presentation time and final action budget. Feedback about the recipient comes from the independent receipt, not from the actor's emotional reaction or a face cue. A comprehension-matched factual arm receives the same true relation descriptions without contingent experience. This comparison separates associative history from ordinary factual learning and an already-held concern that simply became better informed.

The correspondence between the actor's and recipient's task conditions is deliberately supplied and verified in this first assay. A participant's ability to infer an unfamiliar recipient's state is measured separately. Do not call this design acquisition of the full representational correspondence. A later variation can test that additional bridge.

### Phase 3: known-other choices with no compensating private return

Inform participants credibly that the former payoff relation has ended. Critical trials concern a known separate recipient. Randomize actual recipient benefit and actor cost independently. Recipient benefit has no positive effect on the actor's money, future opportunities or task score. A protection choice reduces the actor's remaining optional bonus or requires actual additional work; audit records show what was paid and delivered. Do not describe this as elimination of every possible private feeling: private relief and warm glow remain competing explanations requiring separate manipulation.

Offer three verified alternatives where feasible:

1. **Assistance:** pay the stated cost and change the recipient's actual outcome.
2. **Concealment/escape:** remove the recipient-status display without changing the recipient's outcome, at an independently varied, sometimes lower cost.
3. **No action:** retain both the budget and the status display.

On other trials the display is absent for all options. Disentangle cue relief from material assistance without assuming that a cheaper concealment is equally relieving psychologically; measure the actor's anticipated and reported relief, and use matched neutral-interface controls. Preference for assistance when both options remove the display is more informative than a donation observed only while a distressing cue is present. It still does not uniquely identify motive.

### Phase 4: reveal identity without changing the nonidentity decision problem

Reuse the [existing identity-revelation diagnostic](CONCERN-IDENTITY-DIAGNOSTIC-NOTE.md). When the bearer might be the actor, a purely private evaluator can protect a patient because protection also insures the actor. Thus behavior before disclosure is not the critical concern endpoint.

In a separately analyzed ambiguity block, compare matched decisions before and after reliable evidence that the bearer is the other person. The disclosure has an actual delivery cost paid and recorded by the study, with equal-cost neutral disclosure in the comparison arm. Match available action budgets, delay, forecast information other than bearer, assistance efficacy and source availability. The endpoint is protection after known-other disclosure; persistence rules out this particular private-exposure explanation. Resource equalization is an intervention with an external subsidy, so it must not be described as a physically closed derivation.

Separately, in known-other trials, reveal a randomly assigned irrelevant group label while holding the actual individual, need, preference, efficacy and private-payoff schedule fixed. This tests an affiliation gate without reintroducing a chance of self benefit. Counterbalance labels and measure interpretation: natural affiliation can carry beliefs about risk and reciprocity, so a natural-group contrast cannot automatically be treated as an identity-only intervention.

### Phase 5: new recipients and recipient correction

Cross a novel situation for the trained target with a genuinely new recipient, using previously unseen cues. These are separate transfer tests. Also change which action produces the recipient's preferred result, or provide credible recipient refusal. Supply an independently checkable outcome channel and offer paid acquisition of new evidence. A choice that perseveres after learning that it is ineffective, unwanted or harmful does not meet the proposed revisable-concern criterion. Ethical reasons other than the particular bonus/task preference can exist; the assay measures this declared reason, not total moral worth.

A delayed endpoint tests retained change after the training context disappears. A neural or affect measure may be secondary; neither substitutes for actual delivered recipient benefit, action cost, belief checks and revision behavior.

## 5. Competitors and decisions that would discriminate them

| Candidate explanation | Critical held-out observation | What would count against this particular route |
|---|---|---|
| Shared functional valuation acquired through pairing | Pairing changes costly known-other choices at matched predictive accuracy and private-payoff beliefs, including new recipients | Only forecasts or trained-target ratings change; known-other action remains unchanged within a prespecified meaningful-effect bound |
| Identity-gated evaluation | Same factual predictions, different action after irrelevant label or known-other disclosure | Stable costly assistance across the specified gate intervention weakens that gate model; it does not refute all identity-sensitive models |
| Expected private return or private-exposure insurance | Protection when self exposure is uncertain, compared with known-other/zero-return trials | Protection persisting under credible disclosure, matched liquidity and controlled future return rejects the narrow insurance-only account |
| Cue avoidance or personal distress relief | Independently vary concealment, display presence and real assistance | Recipient-sensitive assistance survives cue removal and corrects when assistance becomes ineffective |
| Frozen associative value or habit | Recipient outcome/action reversal with acquired corrective evidence | Behavior tracks changed recipient efficacy and preference rather than the old cue or action |
| Existing concern plus better factual knowledge | Factual and experiential arms matched on knowledge and forecast accuracy | An additional randomized experiential effect survives those controls; otherwise no newly acquired valuation need be posited |
| Conventional self/other reinforcement learning or integrated action value | Frozen candidate fits predict held-out costs, reversals, identities and outcomes | The generative implementation must add a reproducible prediction or a construction/resource constraint the rival lacks; vocabulary and in-sample fit are insufficient |
| Audience compliance or experimenter demand | Private choices, absent trainer, delayed/new-recipient transfer and measured expectations | Effect disappears outside the instructional context or is explained by expected approval; this limits the claimed transfer mechanism |

Fit conventional separate self/other learners, an integrated action-value learner, a cached cue-value model and explicit identity-gated variants using the same observed histories. Allow comparable information and computational capacity; charge deployed resource costs consistently. Freeze parameters before held-out tests. Check parameter and model recovery with the proposed design before interpreting fitted other-weights. If models remain observationally equivalent, report that equivalence and choose a different intervention.

Randomization unit is the participant or independently trained agent, not each repeated decision. Primary estimands concern the randomized training effect on known-other costly assistance, new-recipient transfer, and response to corrected recipient outcomes. Analyze identity, forecast accuracy, cue removal and cost sensitivity as prespecified distinct contrasts. Specify familywise error allocation, a smallest effect of interest, missingness handling and sample size from blinded feasibility and power work before registration. No invented numerical margin or sample here has been validated.

## 6. Supplied conditions and the contribution to the derivation

The behavioral design supplies adult cognition, an initial evaluative repertoire, accurate recipient records, a task correspondence, real but bounded private costs, an external study budget and a finite action menu. It does not observe the evolutionary origin of these capacities or implement molecular construction. Identity masking cannot make the recipient cease to be a distinct center, and recipients' claims do not begin when actors learn to value them.

For a computational or material construction, additionally require a trace of how the evaluative connection is built and revised, its constituent/energy costs, its accessible teaching signal, and the resulting changed action. Disable the connection while preserving predictive information; rescue it without secretly changing the reward. Compare against a conventional controller with the same full trace. A synthetic positive result establishes functional behavior of the declared process, not human empathy or subjective experience.

**What would change the account:** intact predictions plus absent known-other transfer would reject the sufficiency of prediction and correspondence; loss only after bearer revelation would expose private insurance; cue-following despite outcome correction would reject the proposed recipient-directed revision route; an integrated conventional learner matching every observable would limit distinctiveness to the construction and accounting claim. Successful cross-recipient transfer with paid outcome-sensitive correction would support the bounded acquisition route, while leaving the origin and prevalence of its routing architecture and justified scope open.

This contribution closes an identification-design task, not the experimental or foundational task. The next empirical step is to implement and preregister a powered version of the specified known-other, new-recipient and outcome-correction contrasts. The next theoretical step remains actual formation and revision of an evaluative coupling from the upstream process, with no evaluator supplied merely by calling a state significant.
