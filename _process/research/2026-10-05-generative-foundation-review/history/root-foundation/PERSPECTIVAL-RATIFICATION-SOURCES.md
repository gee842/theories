# Induced dependence, present ratification and transition: source challenges

7 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. AI-authored primary philosophical source assessment for the next test in [the trajectory counterreview](PERSPECTIVAL-TRAJECTORY-COUNTERREVIEW.md#8-integration-verdict-and-next-bridge). Read with [the affirmative argument](PERSPECTIVAL-TRAJECTORY-ARGUMENT.md) and the latest [whole-practical-ground integration](WHOLE-PRACTICAL-GROUND-INTEGRATION.md). **Status: discriminating philosophical comparisons; no empirical finding, mathematical proof, legal interpretation or completed universal ethical grounding.**

The target is **induced dependence → present authorization → warranted continuation or transition** within the same developing association. The sources challenge different shortcuts; they do not form a consensus from which our verdict follows. Their accounts of wellbeing, autonomy and freedom retain substantive commitments requiring assessment under the [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md).

## 1. Khader: criticism need not deny the current participant's agency

**Serene J. Khader (2012), “Must Theorising about Adaptive Preferences Deny Women's Agency?”, Journal of Applied Philosophy 29(4), 302–317.** [Published record](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-5930.2012.00575.x); [author-hosted manuscript](https://www.serenekhader.com/uploads/5/6/6/4/56647983/penultimate_must_theorizing_about_aps_deny_womens_agency_v02.pdf). Locators: manuscript §2, pp. 3–4; §4, pp. 7–10. These are manuscript page numbers, not claimed matches to journal pagination.

Khader separates an account of adaptive preferences from an account of autonomy. Reflective choices can perpetuate deprivation because available options are restricted; diagnosis cannot simply identify defective agency. Her alternative invokes basic wellbeing and a causal relationship to unjust formation. It explicitly relies on a weak perfectionist premise, rather than deriving its standard from the agent's endorsement. Page 8 allows informed self-sacrificing preferences that persist under improved opportunities to be genuinely the person's own. Pages 9–10 support attention to agency and dialogue, without categorically excluding every coercive response.

**Challenge:** a candidate that invalidates ratification merely because it was formed under dependence is too strong. But recognizing agency does not establish that the surrounding arrangement is justified. The account's wellbeing premise must be defended; it is not evidence that the generative theory has already derived that premise.

## 2. Christman: withdrawing support can undermine the agency a reform claims to respect

**John Christman (2014), “Relational Autonomy and the Social Dynamics of Paternalism,” Ethical Theory and Moral Practice 17(3), 369–382.** [Published record](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10677-013-9449-9); [university-hosted article reproduction](https://www0.sun.ac.za/philosophy/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Student-Philosophy-Journal-22-2021.pdf). Locators: printed pp. 374, 375 and 379; access limitation below.

Page 374 presents historically informed acceptance without deep self-alienation. Page 375 distinguishes context-dependent support for agency from claims that particular relationships constitute it; Christman ties the latter necessity to the person's understanding of herself through those relations. Page 379 argues that noninterference alone can withhold the dialogical support needed for autonomy to develop, and defends autonomy-facilitating interaction.

**Challenge:** creating an unassisted opportunity to leave is not always an adequate response to dependence. Abruptly dismantling valued relations can damage capacities for deliberation or practical identity. This does not require preserving every existing authority or establish an unlimited duty to maintain an association. His argument concerns support for agency; it is not a general ratification rule. Its normative commitment to valuing autonomy remains explicit.

## 3. Wenner: current endorsement and protection against uncontrolled power are different tests

**Danielle M. Wenner (2020), “Nondomination and the Limits of Relational Autonomy,” IJFAB: International Journal of Feminist Approaches to Bioethics 13(2), 28–48.** [Published DOI](https://doi.org/10.3138/ijfab.13.2.2020-01-08); [author-hosted preprint](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/dwenner/papers/2019WennerIJFAB.pdf); [author's bibliography](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/dwenner/papers.html). Locators: preprint §4 and §5, PDF pages 14–16, 19–22 and 24–30, counting the title page as page 1.

Wenner challenges attempts to distinguish acceptable from oppressive social formation through autonomy alone. In §5, an adjusted preference can leave an earlier restriction uncorrected; benevolent noninterference can coexist with another's power to interfere at will. She redirects criticism toward uncontrolled power over options while retaining the possibility of autonomous agency under oppression. Her control example also permits some authorized restrictions rather than treating every reduced option set as objectionable.

**Challenge:** sincere ratification does not by itself specify who can subsequently revise the terms. Conversely, the theory must not use a favored account of freedom to dismiss inconvenient preferences as inauthentic. This is a substantive rival account, not a proved equivalence between non-domination and whole ethical goodness.

## 4. What the source comparison requires us to revise

The following is **our integration and proposed scope test**, not further findings attributed to those authors.

Two simple candidates fail the comparison as adequate research formulations:

- **Dependence invalidates endorsement.** Dependence may enable meaningful activity and reflective choice; it may also restrict them. The relevant history must identify which relations do what. Neither an unformed self nor an isolated ideal chooser supplies the baseline.
- **Informed endorsement validates the arrangement.** Endorsement can be an actual exercise of agency, but the validity, object and reach of an authorization need specification. Valuing a craft, agreeing to a schedule, and empowering an office to change every condition of participation are different activities.

Our revised candidate is consequently narrower: **take current participants' judgments seriously while separately examining the relations through which those judgments can authorize particular future terms and the claims those terms affect.** This does not require that every current preference survive an imagined world stripped of its constitutive relationships. Nor does it imply that expanding options is always the ethical aim.

Wenner's presentation distinguishes internal agency from external conditions. Here those labels are not imported as fundamental domains. The relevant distinctions are between learned dispositions, enacted opportunities, recognition and control within the same organized history. Christman's and Wenner's disagreement over constitution versus conditions is a substantive explanatory question to preserve, not settle by replacing their terminology.

## 5. Removing an obstruction versus ending a constitutive relationship

Return to the association from the counterreview. Compare two specific interventions.

**Remove an obstruction:** an office stops withholding an already acquired qualification solely to prevent departure. The craft, genuine record of competence and voluntary ties can remain. Whether the qualification is actually earned, who may certify it, and whether withholding is unjustified are open facts and normative questions; the description does not establish them by naming a barrier.

**End a constitutive relation:** the association dissolves its shared practice, so its mutual participation, a distinctive apprenticeship and some relationships cease. Giving a former member portable resources need not reproduce those goods. Calling the loss an exit cost does not explain whether it is an avoidable penalty, an actual consequence of ending this particular relation, or a mixture.

An adequate test asks whether a proposed alternative preserves the relevant good and capacities, transforms them into something different, or ends them. It also asks whether the office is actively obstructing a feasible route or lacks the power to provide an irreplaceable relationship. An actual good's nonportability neither makes its loss fictitious nor proves that its authority structure must continue. A warranted transformation can involve genuine loss; there is no presumption of a costless repair or permanent maintenance obligation.

This distinction can revise the initial proposal. If the purportedly less controlling alternative destroys the very shared realization participants are authorizing, it does not support a claim that the same good is available under better terms. The comparison must be redone with the loss retained. If an independently usable achievement survives, an assertion that all departure losses are intrinsic to association becomes correspondingly weaker.

## 6. Present ratification needs an object and a scope

The following are candidate moral questions, not a statement of contract law or a sourced complete theory of consent.

| Putative authorization | What must be established | What it does not establish merely by being expressed |
|---|---|---|
| “I choose to continue this practice.” | The current choice, understood terms, relevant capacities and actual conditions of refusal. | Agreement to every future change of terms. |
| “This office may make specified decisions.” | The powers conferred, their bounds and legitimate ways of exercising or contesting them. | Authority over every domain of the participant's life. |
| “I release a claim concerning what happened.” | That the speaker holds this claim and that the particular release is adequately authorized. | Release of another person's claim or proof that the earlier decision was justified when made. |
| “We ratify the association.” | Who participates, how dissent is treated, and any legitimate representative authority. | Universal agreement inferred from the approval of selected members. |

Later ratification can be normatively significant: it may constitute a new permission or relationship rather than merely report a preference. The candidate must allow that possibility. Yet the physical fact that later assent occurred cannot establish the scope of the moral power exercised. A theory claiming retrospective ratification changes an earlier act's status owes an argument for that effect; the current study has not supplied one. The earlier absence of authorization also remains a fact in the history, even where present claims or permissions change.

The hard case therefore concerns a competent participant who endorses continued membership after gaining a credible alternative and relevant support, while another declines. A serious affirmative account must allow the first response to change its diagnosis of present membership. It must independently assess the second person's remaining claims and whether the first has any authority to settle them. A serious association-based rival must explain those limits rather than generalize from selected assent.

## 7. Contribution to the universal inference

The sources require the investigation to hold together **formed agency, actual good, authority and response** without treating one as a proxy for all four. Their strongest challenge to our earlier candidate is that a reformer can misdescribe both the good and the person whose situation is being changed. A whole appraisal must include the reform's own constitutive effects and its claimed authority; benevolent intention or a wider field does not resolve them.

This narrows the next affirmative burden: explain why specified relations of induced vulnerability, undertaking and control warrant a particular response, while allowing valid present authorization to change that response's scope. It does not derive universal first practical favor from the special responsibility of an office that induced dependence. The [whole-practical-ground distinction](WHOLE-PRACTICAL-GROUND-INTEGRATION.md#4-ethical-assessment-of-the-candidate) remains: an additional historically directed responsibility is not yet an exclusive account of every possible moral reason, and demonstrating that exclusivity is underdefended does not prove its universal opposite.

These are philosophical source challenges. No empirical occurrence, prevalence or effectiveness is inferred from the authors' illustrative cases or from their agreement with any part of the repository's account.
