## 2. Where the initial force would reside

The candidate concerns a severe, still-unwanted experiential burden, not every adverse signal or unpleasant moment. Actual relief changes that undergoing; suppressing a report, satisfying a controller or replacing the patient may leave the proposed ground unanswered. These identifications remain independently assessable before accepting the moral conclusion.

There are two possible explanations of a proposed reason. One makes the occurrence's adversity, together with an eligible transformation, the ground of contributory favor. Another additionally requires a particular relation, commitment or authority before the transformation can favor this responder's choice. Both can recognize the same local experiential improvement. Their disagreement is about practical scope, not whether an ultimate ego owns the episode.

The first explanation has a clear positive point: the response is worth choosing for what it can change in the affected undergoing. It need not acquire this point from a helper's prior wish to help. That is the constitutive hypothesis to defend. Calling the improvement a reason is warranted only if this account of its favoring force succeeds; the word cannot do the argumentative work.

“Bad for this patient” therefore admits a crucial distinction. The patient reference can locate whose experience would improve without restricting which choices the improvement addresses. But it can also appear in a proposed restriction on practical reasons. Removing a substantial owner does not decide between those interpretations. A universal rule giving each responder only self-indexed reasons can respect formal symmetry. Its defensibility must be assessed through its proposed grounds and consequences, rather than dismissed as a labeling error.

## 3. What an additional authorization could mean

Suppose the constitutive account has actually established an option-specific contributory moral reason, including its present scope. Asking why it has any contributory moral force then either reopens that original justification or demands a different conclusion. The existence of a reason does not logically require an additional proprietor to license its first favoring force.

This is a conditional clarification, not a shortcut from badness. Someone who grants only local improvement has not granted the supposition. Nor has someone who argues that a legitimate exclusion disqualifies this consideration in this choice. Neither objection is answered by calling the request redundant. Further grounds may explain the reason; the claim is only that a second authority is not mandatory merely because the first warranted reason exists.

Felt urgency is another question. A responsive process may fail to register a valid consideration, while intense aversion or identification may support an erroneous response. Cultivating concern can change access and motivation without creating the first reason. Conversely, demonstrating changed motivation does not vindicate its object. A demand for compulsory conduct, overriding duty or categorical rational authority also exceeds the established contributory moral favor. These differences prevent an apparent explanatory regress from being manufactured by changing the target midway.
