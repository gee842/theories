# Constitutive significance: identifying the patient ground

6 October 2026. AI-authored empirical comparison for Ren's continuing unified-grounding study. The named connection is **generative organization → genuine patient stakes → practical reasons**. This note addresses identification of a candidate experiential stake. It does not treat an experiment about experience as a derivation of ethical authority.

The existing [harm-identification dossier](HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md#2-three-primary-studies-and-their-bounded-contribution) already assessed Rainville 1997, Berthier 1988 and Feinstein 2015/2016. Those remain inherited evidence at their recorded access scope, not new readings or independent replications here. The additional comparison below tests whether sensation, experienced burden and accepting an outcome can safely be collapsed into one regulatory quantity.

## 1. Rainville 1999: an explicitly limited abstract comparator

**Pierre Rainville, Benoît Carrier, Robert K. Hofbauer, Catherine M. Bushnell and Gary H. Duncan, “Dissociation of sensory and affective dimensions of pain using hypnotic modulation,” *Pain* 82(2), 159–171 (1999), DOI 10.1016/S0304-3959(99)00048-2.** [Primary abstract in PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10467921/).

**Bounded synopsis:** Across three experiments, the authors report that suggestions targeting unpleasantness modulated its ratings largely independently of intensity, whereas suggestions targeting intensity produced accompanying unpleasantness changes. In one experiment, stimulus-evoked heart-rate change correlated with unpleasantness rather than intensity. These findings support distinguishing measured sensory and affective dimensions while allowing interaction. They neither establish exact independence nor identify ethical reasons. Abstract-only access does not warrant a fresh appraisal of sample selection, full methods, uncertainty estimates or participant overlap with earlier work.

**Exact reading scope:** publication metadata and the complete author abstract, especially its experiment-design paragraph and the following three results paragraphs. The full article, tables and supplementary material were not inspected. This adds a bounded comparator about affect, sensation and autonomic response; it is not counted as a verified independent replication of the 1997 result.

## 2. Becker 2017: acceptance and experience need separate measurements

**Susanne Becker, Wiebke Gandhi, Yan Jun Chen and Petra Schweinhardt, “Subjective utility moderates bidirectional effects of conflicting motivations on pain perception,” *Scientific Reports* 7, 7790 (2017), DOI 10.1038/s41598-017-08454-4.** [University of Reading published-version PDF](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/72139/1/s41598-017-08454-4.pdf); [institutional provenance](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/72139/).

**Bounded synopsis:** In 21 healthy volunteers' placebo-session data, accepting money with electrical stimulation varied with reward: 22.0% of low-win trials and 78.1% of high-win trials. Participants also rated the stimulation. The study reports context-dependent modulation of those ratings. Crucially, its scale combines painfulness and unpleasantness; it cannot isolate affect from sensation. Acceptance therefore cannot serve as an unexamined substitute for experienced-burden measurement. The experiment does not establish that valence stayed fixed when choices changed, or that choosing the package endorses its painful component. Some reported contrasts are nonsignificant; “no effect” is not an equivalence finding.

**Exact reading scope:** the complete main-article text, article pages 1–10, read by PDF text extraction. Especially page 2, “Participants” and “Rating scales”; pages 3–5, decision task and statistical analysis; pages 5–8, results; pages 8–9, discussion and its combined-scale limitation. The institutional file has two cover pages, so article page 2 is PDF page 4. Figure captions were read; image facsimiles, underlying data and supplementary analyses were not inspected. No new statistical analysis or replication was performed.

## 3. An identification contract for the constitutive candidate

The following is our proposed research contract, not a result attributed to either experiment.

Keep six targets distinct: organizational viability, sensory discrimination, experienced negative valence, avoidance or reinforcement, reflective acceptance of a whole episode, and the proposed ethical significance of that episode. Distinction does not mean that these must always vary independently. A constitutive account must explain their connections without identifying them merely because they usually covary.

Use **experienced negative valence** initially as a phenomenological target: how an episode feels to its subject, described independently of whether anyone judges relief morally required. Do not operationalize it as “whatever supplies a reason,” because that would build the disputed conclusion into identification. Human reports, interventions and convergent measurements can constrain the target; none is automatically an infallible readout. Extending identification to another kind of system needs an independently supported account of experience there. A learning rule, negative reward value or successful withdrawal policy alone does not supply that account.

Three proposed contrasts make the contract substantive:

| Proposed contrast | What the account must distinguish | What would defeat a shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Similar sensory input with differing experienced unpleasantness | Sensory quantity from experiential burden | Evidence that their relation varies defeats identifying burden with input magnitude alone |
| A painful episode accepted for another benefit | The local experienced component from the value of the whole package | Continued negative experience despite acceptance defeats identifying burden with refusal alone |
| A report or avoidance response suppressed while experience remains | Successful control of an indicator from relief of the patient | Persistent negative experience defeats identifying relief with eliminating the indicator |

These are conceptual discrimination requirements, not three newly completed experiments. The second contrast must allow that appraisal can change experience itself. If the episode becomes nonaversive, the model should revise its attribution of this particular burden, rather than insist that the same stimulus must carry it. Conversely, acceptance of a larger beneficial package does not by definition erase every local cost. Actual cases require evidence about both components.

The target is one contributory experiential stake, not all welfare or all possible reasons. Painless functional damage may matter on other grounds. Relief of this stake must also be distinguished from ending the patient's existence: removing a bearer is not automatically the same outcome as improving that bearer's experience. Neither source tests or licenses that substitution.

## 4. What survives, and what remains supplied

The simple identity **regulation = ethical stakes** is insufficiently discriminating: it cannot tell a regulatory signal from an experienced cost unless it adds a justified identification theory. A narrower candidate survives: some generatively realized episodes may have an adverse experiential character whose badness-for the patient is constitutive rather than imposed by an observer's success score. The empirical comparisons constrain what such a realization could be; they do not establish this philosophical identity.

Two further questions remain. First, why does the proposed generative organization realize experience of this kind at all? Second, why does that patient-ground itself contribute in favor of permissible effective relief by an unrelated capable actor before that actor endorses the end? Both require argument beyond these measurements. Failure of a stimulus, behavior or anatomical proxy would revise the proposed realization or its indicator; it would not by itself prove that ethical normativity is irreducible. Equally, a realization theory cannot protect itself from criticism by defining every contrary report as error.

What would change the account is an independently supported dissociation between its specified constitutive organization and the experiential target, or a justified explanation showing that its alleged experiential burden is only an attributed regulatory objective. A separate normative challenge would preserve the identified burden while denying its pre-endorsement practical relevance. The present sources adjudicate neither that denial nor the strongest universal grounding claim.
