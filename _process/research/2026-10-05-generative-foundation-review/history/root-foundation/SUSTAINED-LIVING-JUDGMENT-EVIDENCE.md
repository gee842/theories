## Primary research account

Elinor Ostrom, “Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems,” *American Economic Review* 100(3), 2010, 641–672, section VI.A, printed pp. 656–657. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.3.641); [university-hosted published reading copy](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf).

Read section VI.A through the browser's extracted PDF text. Ostrom reports a research programme assessing infrastructure condition, seasonal tail-end water and agricultural productivity. Farmer-managed systems performed better on these reported measures with environmental controls; an expanded 229-system database supported the earlier comparison. Communication, agreements, monitoring and enforcement occur in the described arrangements. This is a research participant's synthesis of field studies, not our independent examination of Lam's data or a randomized comparison. It supports examining coordination alongside material delivery; it neither establishes universal superiority of local governance nor validates a particular community's burdens or consent. The Nobel PDF and interview pages returned 403; the university reading copy was accessible. No claim of whole-paper, appendix or raw-data review is made.

## What follows for our application (our inference)

The sources jointly motivate identifying **staged living activity → actual delivery and operative organization → coordination and maintenance → differentiated consequences**. They do not themselves supply G0, J2, J3 or J4. Distinct evidence is needed for a household's basic claim, labor burden, ecological effect, authority and consent. A crop-yield outcome is not identical to protection of every living claim in a history.

The proposal's independent target is continuing the specified living activity during the relevant horizon, including the lives of those maintaining it. Functional measures and affected people's evidence must be retained together. Infrastructure inventory, assigned staff or total withdrawals are possible predictors; they cannot serve as definitions of successful protection.

## Discriminating assessment protocol

For an identified practice, record the decision and horizon before comparing alternatives. Record actual delivery by location and time, functional capacity and repair downtime, relevant living-stage outcomes, maintenance contributions, cumulative burden, available alternatives and the process of authorization. Identify which claims are basic before ranking plans; leave contested classifications visible. Preserve lost optional activities and uncompensated earlier harms.

Compare at least: (a) the best supported supply-only explanation; (b) an explanation incorporating operative organization and delivery timing; (c) an explanation additionally incorporating arrangements that sustain maintenance and correction. Use relevant existing data or an authorized intervention; do not withhold indispensable support to manufacture an experimental contrast. The present source comparison does not estimate causal effects among these models.

A verified organizational repair followed by failed delivery challenges its sufficiency. Effective delivery followed by absent living continuation challenges the claimed functional correspondence. Continued living without the claimed necessary route challenges its necessity after assessing genuine alternatives. Successful delivery achieved through basic deprivation of contributors defeats the J2 all-other-basic-claims premise even if the production model is accurate. Excluding those contributors from the claim register is not a valid repair.

No field dataset, prospective decision, burden calibration or completed institutional implementation is supplied by this round. The application is an evidence-constrained worked argument and test specification. It establishes what must be checked to use the formal connection, not that the conditions already hold everywhere.
