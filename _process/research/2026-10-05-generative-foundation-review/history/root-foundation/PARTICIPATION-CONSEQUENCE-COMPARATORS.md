# Participation, retained consequence and changing effective range

6 October 2026. AI-authored primary-source comparison under [Ren's project-wide nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). Named connection: **formed and revisable participation → acquired consequence distinctions → changes in affected continuation**. No new model, controller, experimental run or complete foundational derivation is reported.

## The configuration and the existing connection

The unit of explanation is an organism continuing through material exchanges, movement and changes in its own capacities. A retained difference can be part of the machinery that changes later contact, rather than a detached description delivered to a separate actuator. Distinguishing encounter, structural retention and later use preserves their order and causal roles; treating them as independently complete things would omit the proposed formative relationship.

This develops the [genealogy's](NONDUAL-TREE-GENEALOGY.md) account of consequences becoming conditions again and the [existing material history](NONDUAL-TREE-MECHANISM.md), where an actual consequence enters retained, action-relevant organization. It complements the [thermal applicability comparison](NONDUAL-APPLICABILITY-COMPARATORS.md): changing what makes a response useful is distinguishable from acquiring a lasting capacity to respond differently. These are differentiations within ongoing activity. They are not proof that either relation is outside a common generative account.

The two sources below were chosen for structural participation and changing effective range. The first is the central comparator. The second is a useful limit case for distinguishing changed performance from identified learning. Initial searches of tool use and habituation were source selection, not a systematic review or additional evidence in this note.

## Primary evidence, with measured and inferred parts separated

**Kramar and Alim (2021), “Encoding memory in tube diameter hierarchy of living flow network.”** [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2007815118); [preserved author PDF](../../../../sources/38-participation-consequence-2026-10-06/original/kramar-alim-2021.pdf).

Kramar and Alim imaged Physarum networks after local heat-killed bacteria or glucose–leucine stimuli. Quantitative analysis used trimmed, initially stable networks. Diameter hierarchies changed within roughly 15 minutes and persisted through the 45-minute quantified window; a longer sequence showed remodeling and foraging after food consumption. A proposed advected softening agent and closed-tube mechanical model reproduce aspects of the dynamics; the agent's identity is unknown. Naturally differing contraction frequencies, rather than randomized flow perturbations, support one prediction. Figure 5 compares existing thick tubes at different flow-travel distances from the new stimulus. It does not compare experimentally assigned prior-stimulus histories: the authors explicitly decline that direct test. The identified readout therefore combines measured geometry-dependent responses with an interpretation of existing hierarchy as past information. It is not a demonstrated controlled acquisition–retention–later-use chain for two assigned histories. The main example's 2,165 tubes are within-network measurements, not 2,165 independent organisms. No survival advantage or ethical valuation was measured. Main text, methods and Figure 5 were inspected; supplementary movies, large appendix and raw data were not reanalyzed.

**Sonoda et al. (2012), “Hermit crabs perceive the extent of their virtual bodies.”** [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2012.0085); [preserved primary XML](../../../../sources/38-participation-consequence-2026-10-06/original/sonoda-2012.xml).

Sonoda and colleagues tested 72 terrestrial hermit crabs in six conditions, twelve per condition, without pre-training. They compared natural shells with shells bearing a plastic extension, across corridor clearances. Attached crabs had ten minutes before testing. Turns varied with clearance and extension; during left turns the plate did not contact the partitions. This weakens a simple collision-forces-turning explanation. The study compares separate groups rather than retaining a pre/post learning trajectory for every animal. Scoring omitted first/last corners and certain body-contact cases; stopped crabs could be restarted and non-restarting trials cancelled. No separate food-for-correct-turn reward is described. The intervention establishes rapid adjustment of movement to changed bodily geometry, but does not uniquely distinguish newly learned dimensions from an existing flexible multisensory policy. It neither measures a retained neural representation nor independently establishes subjective ownership. Full primary XML, methods and discussion were read; no raw trajectory analysis was performed.

## A published challenge to the memory interpretation

**Reiber and Nogueira Júnior (2021), “Missing evidence for memory in the monocellular slime mold.”** [Primary letter](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2105928118); [preserved PMC HTML](../../../../sources/38-participation-consequence-2026-10-06/original/reiber-nogueira-2021.html).

Reiber and Nogueira Júnior accept the reported remodeling and flow-based account but dispute evidence of experience-dependent facilitation, learning and retrieval beyond immediate chemotaxis. They regard nonneuronal learning as an important research target, discuss plant priming and question what functional benefit the proposed stored location adds. They also challenge the terminology of accurate readout and advocate ecological context. This is a critical letter, not a new experiment or a reanalysis showing that the measured diameter changes disappear.

**Our assessment.** The evidential challenge is material: retained shape plus a response to a present stimulus does not, by itself, isolate the causal contribution of a specified earlier encounter. The later discrimination proposed below must preserve that challenge. A description in terms of material organization can be correct while a stronger claim about acquired retrieval remains underidentified.

The challenge does not require cognition to exist apart from physiology. Nor should the letter be recast as a blanket denial of nonneuronal learning. Its stronger implication that readout requires error-free representations is unnecessary for the bounded memory claim considered here. Fallible causal use of a retained distinction is coherent. Likewise, demonstrating change throughout an ecosystem is not a universal prerequisite for establishing a specific local retention/use relation; ecological effects become necessary evidence when the claim itself concerns those effects. Those are scope judgments about adequate tests, not a dismissal of the criticism by redefining every response as memory.

Full letter text and references were inspected. A [bounded reply search](../../../../sources/38-participation-consequence-2026-10-06/metadata/criticism-reading.json) identified no author reply; that is not a finding that none exists. Austin's earlier commentary, DOI `10.1073/pnas.2102056118`, is not a Kramar–Alim reply.

## What these comparisons change in our formulation

The following is our interpretation and proposed discrimination, not further reported results.

**Changing participation need not mean adding or deleting a graph edge.** A framework that counts only adjacency can miss changed capacities of existing relations. Geometry, conductance, timing, load and available movement may alter effective reach even when a coarse graph stays unchanged. The relevant question is which material change affects which subsequent continuation, not whether the analyst has named a new object.

**A fixed dynamical law need not imply a fixed response or no memory.** A coupled physicochemical account with hysteresis and retained structure can preserve the whole encounter–retention–use proposal. If it explains the observations, it is a possible realization, not automatically a refutation. Conversely, labeling every lasting change “learned understanding” would erase the stronger action–consequence identification problem. Distinguish a memoryless response to present stimulation, history-dependent adaptive organization, and acquisition of a relation that transfers under changed contingencies. The second does not require the third; the third needs evidence beyond the second.

**Physical support differs from a success label.** A nutrient or a traversable opening can change what an organism can actually do. That relation is not manufactured by assigning a high numerical reward to an output. Nevertheless, a demonstration of successful approach does not by itself measure net nutrition, continued viability or an organism's own assessment of those consequences. The organism's metabolism, sensory capacities, developmental history and experimental access conditions remain starting points of these local explanations. Calling them supplied here does not place them outside generative reality, and calling them immanent does not explain their formation.

The explanatory advance is therefore modest but concrete: retained history may operate as changed material coupling, and bodily extent may be a revisable relation of practical control. The unresolved empirical question is the contribution of a particular acquired history to later use, with present stimulation and competing physiological changes adequately distinguished. This is an unresolved identification in the stronger interpretation, not evidence against all material memory or the reconstructed nondual account.

## A discrimination that would materially advance the named connection

A useful comparison would trace different prior contacts into retained organization, then test their consequences under a common later challenge. It need not produce two literally identical organisms. Randomized histories, repeated measurements, controls for direct residual stimulation, and an explicit model of variability can identify a contrast without that impossible requirement.

For a structural-memory claim, the central question is whether changing the proposed retained material relation changes later transport or action, while preserving the relevant present inputs and avoiding an unaccounted general injury or energy difference. Establishing an effect of assigned history and establishing mediation by structure are different claims. One must not statistically remove the entire structural consequence of history and then conclude that history has no effect. An intervention that destroys the organism's general capacity also cannot uniquely identify the stored relation.

For the stronger claim of **acquired consequence discrimination**, vary which encountered relation actually provides usable support or passage, separately from a familiar cue or configuration, and test whether acquired history supports appropriate revision or transfer. Independently measured uptake, transport, collision and continuation are candidate consequences; an analyst's preferred route alone is inadequate. A successful comparison against a present-input-only response would establish history dependence. Matching performance by an ordinary stateful adaptive mechanism would narrow any claim of distinctive cognition, while potentially explaining the very relationship being investigated.

Failure matters too. Persistent commitment to a formerly useful relation after its consequences change would bound adaptive significance, even if a structural memory remains. A representation that cannot express that adverse regime would omit something required by the target. Neither improvement nor failure in these limited domains determines how concern for others, legitimate authority or ethical warrant arises. Those relationships remain part of the common inquiry and require their own adequate reconstruction; they are not conclusions encoded in a physiological success measure.
