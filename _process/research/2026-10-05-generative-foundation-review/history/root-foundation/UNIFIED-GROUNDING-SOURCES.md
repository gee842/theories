# Unified grounding: bodily claims, changing operators and rational authority

6 October 2026. AI-authored primary-source comparison and identification proposal for the continuation Ren requested: pursue unified grounding without prematurely treating the present supplied ethical premise as metaphysically irreducible. The named connection is **an actual patient's experienced burden → its practical significance for a capable unrelated actor**. The proposed comparison keeps the patient and relief target fixed while changing the available operator. This is philosophical research and a proposed evidence interface, not a new empirical result.

The existing [source dossier](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md), especially §§16 and 25–29, already compares naturalistic good, phenomenal identity, acquaintance, patient aversion, objective understanding and interpersonal reasons. The [harm-identification study](HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md) distinguishes experience, intensity, response and normative authority. Those findings are inherited, not counted again. This round adds one directly inspected primary work with an explicit patient–actor structure and a substantive alternative to both egoism and irreducible normative facts.

## 1. Primary comparator: Manne's naturalistic patient claims

**Kate Manne, “Locating Morality: Moral Imperatives as Bodily Imperatives,” *Oxford Studies in Metaethics*, volume 12 (2017), chapter 1.** [Author-hosted corrected proof](https://www.katemanne.net/uploads/7/3/8/4/73843037/locating_morality_moral_imperatives_as_b.pdf); [author publication record](https://www.katemanne.net/uploads/7/3/8/4/73843037/katemanne__6_.pdf). Accessed 6 October 2026. Text reading covered the chapter's argument through web and local PDF extraction; no facsimile inspection. Local proof pagination governs: 5–10, 14, 17–22 and 23–25 are central. Earlier web extraction occasionally merged footers across pages.

**Bounded synopsis:** Manne identifies core moral claims with selected bodily imperatives, separating their patient from the actors and means the patient accepts. Those claims need not depend on an operator's desires. Her internalism nevertheless permits a valid moral claim without a reason for an actor who cannot be rationally persuaded. Naturalistic moral grounding and universally binding rational authority therefore remain distinct. The stronger authority is explicitly unavailable within her proposal.

## 2. Our reconstruction: separate two explanatory ambitions

The current root investigates at least two propositions:

1. Ethical significance belongs within generative reality and can be explained through what actual beings, experiences and relations are.
2. Every adequately capable actor necessarily has a practical reason to respond to that significance, whether or not its present concerns favor doing so.

The first does not require the second to be a definition of it. A candidate can place universal moral claims in natural reality yet reject a conception of reason that binds every instrumentally coherent actor. That is a substantive rival within unified grounding, not automatically evidence that ethics requires a separate irreducible substance or axiom. Conversely, assigning a natural identity to a claim does not establish that the identity is true, nor that its normative significance has been explained.

Our target remains stronger than merely predicting uptake. It should justify a reason to relieve an actual burden under permissibility, efficacy and capacity conditions. But it must specify what “reason” adds to a patient-grounded moral claim and why that addition is necessary. Otherwise a dispute over rational accessibility can be mistaken for a dispute over whether the patient matters at all.

This distinction also sharpens the strongest contrary position. A rival may agree that a patient's suffering is real, intrinsically or constitutively adverse, a fitting object of concern, and a moral claim on strangers; it may still deny a reason for an actor outside a defended deliberative-accessibility class. Refuting a controller that simply erases the patient does not refute this rival. The account must examine the accessibility restriction itself. Nor does observing an unmoved actor establish that no sound route to responsiveness exists.

## 3. A concrete test: retain the patient, change the operator

The following is our proposed comparison, not Manne's experiment. It extends the repository's patient-preserving approach and incorporates the parallel counterreview's concern about prior roles.

Let one identified patient undergo an actual aversive condition. A particular intervention would relieve that condition while preserving the patient's relevant agency, commitments and other interests. Compare cases in which the same permitted intervention can be performed by different operators. The operators are distinct from the patient and from each other. No copied subject, imagined role reversal or reassignment of the burden substitutes for the original patient.

The useful independent variables are:

| Contrast | What changes | What must remain fixed or separately identified |
|---|---|---|
| Available operator | A can enact relief in one case; B can enact it in the other | Actual patient, target condition, permissible remedy and matched efficacy/cost |
| Operator's concern | Responsive versus indifferent operator | Patient's burden, patient-directed remedy, factual knowledge and relevant practical capacity |
| Prior relationship | Prior entrustment or responsibility versus none | Present feasible control and the patient's actual burden |
| Patient's accepted means | Patient accepts one means but refuses another | Relief potential must be distinguished from permission and participation in shaping activity |
| Observer-only escape | Operator stops witnessing distress while patient remains in it | Patient outcome is separately measured; operator relief does not count as patient relief |
| Information correction | Reliable patient evidence corrects the operator's initial interpretation | Change in warranted identification is not itself creation of the patient-ground |

“Matched” is a declared comparison condition, not a conclusion from equal fuel, identical button presses or equal reaction times. Changing an operator can alter competence, trust, permission, risk or vulnerability. Any of those may supply a legitimate difference. The pure ownership contrast is informative only after such differences are identified and controlled conceptually or empirically.

Two versions of the claim should then be assessed separately:

- **Patient-address claim:** when the patient can receive the same permitted relief from either operator, its burden and relevant acceptance do not disappear when the operator changes.
- **Actor-reason claim:** the preserved patient-ground together with the new operator's eligible practical relation supplies that operator a reason to enact relief.

The first does not entail the second by formal substitution. A defended theory can connect them; an unexamined rule that maps any patient imperative onto every available controller's objective merely implements the conclusion. Successful routing establishes responsiveness under the supplied rule. It does not show that the rule is a constitutive requirement of agency or of adequate understanding.

Nevertheless, the comparison makes positive explanatory progress possible. A unified account should explain why the patient's end remains the same while the available means changes. It need not posit a new valued patient merely because a new operator becomes available. If a rival withholds the actor's reason, it must locate and defend the additional relation doing that work—historical role, deliberative accessibility, ownership, or another proposed condition—rather than silently changing the patient or relief target.

## 4. Identification obligations and avoidable contamination

The patient identification must not come from the operator's reward or the analyst's preferred outcome. The earlier [harm-identification contract](HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md#1-the-empirical-distinctions-required-by-the-philosophical-claim) remains in force: sensory intensity, aversiveness, withdrawal, judgment and reason are different evidential targets. A reporting failure does not itself erase a patient or a burden. A generic error signal with negative sign has not yet been identified as suffering.

The practical capacity class must likewise be specified without defining a capable operator as one that already accepts universal concern. Relevant factual understanding, ability to distinguish the actual patient from a representation, control over a permitted effective means, and ability to revise a mistaken means belief are possible starting capacities. Whether they suffice for the disputed practical understanding is the question under investigation. Conversely, a controller that passes these functional tests has not thereby been proved to possess human-like experiential understanding.

An instruction to act as the patient's caretaker, a prior promise, employment, accepted delegation or experimenter-assigned beneficiary task can introduce an independent reason to help. Such evidence is useful for mechanisms but cannot alone test a reason for an actor without a prior role. Similarly, a learned controller whose sole supplied reward is patient relief already has the target built into its selector. Neither construction establishes universal noncauser authority.

Patient willingness is evidence about permitted means and participation in shaping activity; it must not be treated as the entire ground of moral standing. In the narrow test, willingness can be stipulated as a controlled fact. Extending the account to infants, nonverbal animals, unconscious patients or uncertain refusal requires an additional interpretation. We should not make every patient's significance depend on its ability to formulate a request to every possible rescuer.

## 5. What would change the account

A **positive advance** would justify why the same actual patient-ground, under an independently specified eligible relief relation, addresses a replacement operator without appeal to that operator's prior concern or special role. The justification may be constitutive, phenomenological, practical or abductive; each must state the warrant connecting its evidence to its conclusion. An implementation can then explain acquired uptake without supplying its own moral truth condition.

A **restriction** would be warranted if a defended actor–patient relation changes the reason while the patient-ground and other relevant factors remain fixed. Mere possible notation, an arbitrary selfish policy, or observed refusal is insufficient. A viable restriction must explain why the added gate is normatively relevant and why its scope is necessary rather than an artifact of the local model.

A **different successful unification** could explain universal patient claims within generative reality while rejecting their identification with reasons under a motivationally restrictive concept. That would change the strongest rationalist claim without establishing ethical irreducibility. The present source comparison supports keeping this possibility distinct; it does not endorse it as the final account.

No likelihood estimate follows from this single comparison. It establishes a better division of the explanatory burden and a discriminating patient-preserving test. The next unresolved bridge is the justification of the actor-reason claim for an informed, capable stranger without prior undertaking, while retaining the difference between a patient's real burden and a controller's accepted end.
