# Reconsidering an aim through the practice that realizes it

6 October 2026. AI-authored primary comparison for the [pretraining-evidence argument](PRETRAINING-EVIDENCE-ARGUMENT.md). The named connection is **retained consequences of forming responsiveness → reconsideration of what further acquisition is for and what it imposes**. This is a philosophical comparison, not new empirical evidence or a proof of the ethical minimum.

## The bounded primary reading

In *Human Nature and Conduct* (1922), Part III §§VI–VII, Dewey treats aims as anticipated consequences that redirect ongoing activity. The intended result is one among the activity's effects. Fixating on it can obscure other consequences, including effects attributed to the means. He also treats principles as instruments of inquiry whose use can expose reasons to revise them. He rejects both inflexible application and discarding accumulated experience. This is an affirmative account of intelligent redirection within conduct, alongside a critique of fixed ends and antecedently complete moral standards. It is not an empirical demonstration or an assumption-free derivation of universal concern. [Primary transcription, printed pp. 223–247](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386-images.html).

## Consequence for the present investigation

The [previous round](PRACTICAL-BOUNDARY-INTEGRATION.md) fixed a decision before a fifth training pair. Its declared purpose cannot by itself settle the point of undertaking it. At least three possible inquiries differ: reconstructing a past debit, finding out what that debit means for the affected being, and forecasting what another operation will do. A procedure informative about one may provide no evidence about the others. That distinction must follow the actual relation between an operation and its result, rather than the investigator naming every additional trial “learning.”

If the existing formation structure retains the past debit exactly, another imposed operation does not become logically necessary merely because no separate tally was installed. But exact mathematical reconstruction does not establish that the organization has a readable, affordable route to that information. Identifying such a route remains a causal task. Past debit also does not establish current suffering, entitlement, consent or the best next act. A genuine need for evidence about those matters must be assessed on its own terms.

The comparison therefore supports examining an inquiry's proposed end and its concrete conduct together. An aim to improve responsiveness can change when its actual production burdens someone or when the purportedly missing information is already retained. It cannot certify its continuation simply by keeping the desirable aim verbally fixed. This applies the shared [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md): the distinction between purpose and method must preserve their consequences in one unfolding.

## What still needs an ethical argument

This does not make every consequence equally important or every proposed inquiry impermissible. The value of the information, access to alternatives, burdens, timing and competing claims require assessment. Nor does epistemic accuracy itself establish the ground or practical range of concern. A practice can use accurate evidence to refine exploitation.

The universal-minimum objective is also not replaced by Dewey's philosophical position. A minimum applying to every relevant occurrence could be compatible with highly variable responses; criticism of a complete fixed decision rule does not decide that claim. Conversely, an account of how aims form cannot establish such a minimum merely by being developmental or nondual. The specific target remains justified responsiveness to actual differentiated conditions, including beings who offer no return or reciprocal recognition.

The contribution of this reading is a sharper inquiry question: **what unresolved fact would this further operation reveal, and how do its actual effects bear on the purpose for which it is proposed?** The mechanistic evidence and the independently defended ethical comparison must answer it together. A source's prestige or a new interpretation of a counter does not supply the answer.
