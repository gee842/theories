## Timing and row provenance

The source records a timestamp, acquires both readings, solves, and issues heater requests in serial order. Under source fidelity, an order-preserving clock and successful serial progression, the request lies between that timestamp and the next iteration's timestamp. The timestamp is not the readout-retrieval instant. The scripts order the `.T1`/`.T2` calls; they do not preserve the historical driver/firmware or underlying hardware conversion timestamps. A hardware-sample-age interpretation additionally requires that sampling occurred within the retrieval bracket, or a separate bound on prior internal age. This clarification is a correspondence limit, not a change to the timing arithmetic. For ARX, `Q1s[i+1] = Q1.NEWVAL` and `a.Q1(Q1s[i])` genuinely introduce a previous-plan dependency; physics and ANN assign and issue index `i` in the same iteration.

The age results use acquired command-associated rows with a following acquired timestamp. The nonlinear runs contribute 297 intervals each; each distinct ARX run contributes 597. Upper endpoints are respectively **2.028091669 s**, **2.643852472 s**, **2.939590931 s**, and **2.325789213 s** for physics, ARX Summary, ANN and ARX illustration. All are below three seconds historically. At a two-second timestamp deadline, ARX Summary has 310 certified-within and 287 undetermined intervals; physics and ANN have 297 undetermined each. Crossing the bound is not evidence of an actual missed deadline. ARX's timestamp-age lower bound is not a physical acquisition-age lower bound; its upper endpoint bounds retrieval-to-issue delay under the ordering premise. Extending that statement to hardware sample age requires the additional sampling correspondence just stated.

The ARX arrays initialize index 0 before the timed loop, acquire indices 1–599, compute a final planned value at index 600 without issuing it, and retain initialized temperatures at that last row. Its last timestamp is taken after plotting and shutdown. Both ARX records preserve this pattern. Excluding these positions from acquired-row metrics is justified by provenance, not by favorable outcome selection. The original mean sampled error is 11.515500 for physics and 11.587354 for ARX Summary; acquired-loop-only means are 11.554013378 and 11.546911519, reversing their small ordering. ANN changes from 11.351500 to 11.389464883. No causal or statistical method ranking follows from these single histories.

The source's tracking sum divided by row count has units °C, whereas Table 3 labels it °C/sec. Movement divided by the final timestamp does have percentage-points/second units. The original values reproduce the rounded table: tracking 11.5, 11.6, 11.4 and movement approximately 2.0, 3.3, 2.5. The report correctly retains the original calculation and labels the dimensional discrepancy. It also avoids treating an interval containing sleep, acquisition, solving and other operations as an isolated solver-duration measurement.

## Why the exposure comparison is warranted

The shared window begins at **4.169826745986938477 s**, the latest second-loop timestamp, and ends at **600.5725736618041992 s**, the earliest last acquired timestamp. Its duration is **596.402746915817260723 s**. In every run a preceding request has already been issued before the window begins. The analysis therefore needs no undocumented initial command-hold timing and makes no assumption about post-loop holding or shutdown.

Within timestamp bracket `i`, the previous requested value is row `i−1` and the newly issued value is row `i`, including ARX: the queue affects which measurement formed that value, not the row in which it is issued. For a clipped bracket, clamp the unknown switch position to its clipped endpoints. The old-command hold duration remains in `[0,width]`; exposure lies between the all-old and all-new endpoint integrals. `segment_midpoint_bound` proves this exact enclosure. Cross-channel sequencing may correlate switches, so not every point of the product domain need be realizable. Using the containing product domain is conservative and does not invalidate a strict separation throughout it.

This preserves dimensions; it does not multiply the physical interval twice. `separated_history_exposure` then directly consumes the preceding strict interval-comparison theorem.
