# Realized quality, relational constitution and completed repair

6 October 2026. AI-authored investigation under the [nondual method](../../../../theories/becoming/chapters/NONDUAL-METHOD.md). Named connection: **differentiated realization within generative unfolding → ethical significance of its actual relations → changed grounds as activity transforms those relations**. This continues the [whole-process argument](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md), its [scope map](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-SCOPE.md) and [review](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-REVIEW.md). The paired cases are our conceptual constructions, not Ren's words or new empirical observations.

**Result:** the ethical candidate must concern how life is actually realized *in its relations*. Neither the felt character of an episode, successful attainment of an internally formed target, nor future capacity alone settles whole goodness. A positive response can be valuable because of how it answers an existing burden, while its success removes that very occasion for response. Preserving the identical response-good can therefore conflict with completing the repair. Value in the response does not by itself justify maintaining or recreating the burden that gives it its object.

This is a bounded philosophical advance. It separates preserving the same valuable activity from preserving its value through fulfillment, and identifies what must be assessed when its present form ends through success. The inherited no-countervailing-loss comparison remains sufficient; a changed relation alone does not refute its antecedent. This study does not establish that every unpleasant episode has an invariant negative ethical contribution, or complete universal ethical grounding.

## 1. What is inherited and what changes here

[Relational goodness](RELATIONAL-GOODNESS-ARGUMENT.md) already distinguishes present good from its contribution to later good and preserves rest and completion. [Affective realization](AFFECTIVE-REALIZATION-ARGUMENT.md) already separates an experience's possible internal direction from functional target attainment, welfare and practical authority. [Constitutive significance](CONSTITUTIVE-SIGNIFICANCE-ARGUMENT.md) already proposes a restricted experiential burden; [the constitutive-answer argument](CONSTITUTIVE-ANSWER-ARGUMENT.md) defends direct response without an extra authorization regress. The [closure audit](ETHICAL-CONSTITUTION-CLOSURE.md) records that these arguments have not discharged the disputed ethical identifications.

The [self-ground audit §36](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#36-can-goodness-be-identified-with-generativity) already contains the equal-future-capacity relief comparison and its exact-representation obstruction. No appraisal factoring through a lens can recover a distinction that lens identifies away. Extending that elementary observation from a scalar to a rich future-response representation is an application of the same inherited result, not a new theorem here. Its relevant lesson is that realized life must remain visible alongside subsequent possibilities.

[Sections 54–55](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#54-successful-support-and-continuing-commitments) already establish that success can change faithful fulfillment without automatically discharging every undertaking. That practical distinction is inherited too.

The concurrently added [whole-process continuation §7](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#7-continuation-the-origin-of-a-need-and-the-value-of-its-repair) now explicitly separates the entrance comparison from the later remedial comparison and rejects a positive-repair count as a sufficient goodness criterion. It credits earlier imposition/rescue, evaluative-grounding, desert and restoration-law work. That result was read before this note's completion and is inherited here. The three-history construction below applies it without a newly claimed priority or second independent confirmation.

The new connection is a **test of relational constitution**. A whole-life rival can retain every experiential fact while disputing that it contributes a fixed ethical amount. We examine that rival through cases in which changing a burden also changes what the response *is*. This blocks an extrapolation that requires literal continuation of every valuable activity, without vindicating the burden. It also tests the concurrent §7 comparison's matched-goods premise: the responding relationship can contain a genuine good that is transformed when its object is removed. Whether its ending is completion, a relevant loss, or both must be assessed rather than settled by the mere fact of change. Only an independently warranted loss would defeat the no-countervailing-loss antecedent.

## 2. Fix the target without building in the verdict

Begin with ongoing activity in which relatively enduring experiential continuities and relations of agency have formed. Their occurrence and identification require evidence. The generic causal root does not itself establish consciousness, a valid permission or a worthwhile activity. Undergoing, sensing, acting and judging are differentiated relations within this history; none needs to be a fundamentally independent substance.

For the experiential contrast, identify an actual episode's felt character. The adverse arm retains the restricted domain already examined: severe felt unpleasantness that remains unwanted in the relevant experiential respect. Instrumental acceptance can coexist with that condition. Cases in which enjoyment or another relation changes the experience or defeats its alleged local burden need assessment rather than automatic exclusion. An avoidance signal, negative reward or evaluator's word does not identify the experience.

Then identify what the activity is doing: what its object is, how the episode participates, whose agency is exercised, which dependencies are causal and which relations constitute the activity described. The relief of a burden, domination of a person and successful performance of a chosen activity are different relations even if some performance indicators coincide.

The following questions concern one unfolding, but they do not have the same content:

| Question | Content to identify |
|---|---|
| What formed and occurred? | The actual experiential continuity, episode and felt character. |
| What succeeded? | The independently specified target of an activity, intention or controller. |
| What constitutes the activity? | Its object, relevant relations, participation and history. |
| What is good or adverse? | A defended evaluative interpretation of that realization. |
| How should the whole be compared? | Its plural goods, burdens, means, endings and alternatives. |
| What should be done now? | Available action, evidence, authority and competing grounds. |

Here *realization* first means that something actually occurs. *Fulfillment* only becomes informative when its content is specified. Completing a control target, feeling enjoyment, realizing a relationship and completing a life project are different relations. None acquires its ethical interpretation simply from the shared word.

## 3. The crossed comparison defeats two easy reductions

Consider four conceptual cases. They preserve a declared kind of felt mode or target success, not an identical complete physical or phenomenal state. Intentional content and relational organization may change the complete experience. The ethical judgments in the right column are explicit judgments to defend, not outputs established by stipulation.

| Case | Felt mode and operational success | Relational question preserved |
|---|---|---|
| Freely enjoyed, nonexploitative shared activity | Present enjoyment; intended activity succeeds. | What makes the activity and its relations worthwhile? |
| Successful imposed cruelty enjoyed by its initiator | Present enjoyment; the intended control target succeeds. | Can enjoyment in another's imposed suffering constitute a defect in the whole activity, rather than merely an otherwise good amount offset by someone else's loss? |
| Needed, authorized, competent repair that remains painful | Present unpleasantness; the repair succeeds. | Does pain have a fixed negative contribution, a meaningful constitutive role, or a burden worth accepting for another good? What permission and alternatives are present? |
| Imposed, futile burden | Present unpleasantness; no defended repair relation. | What, if anything, justifies imposing or sustaining it? Its being an activity is insufficient. |

The target-success lens cannot distinguish successful cruelty from successful repair without additional content. Felt valence alone cannot determine the ethical quality of the activity in which it occurs. These are limitations of the specified lenses under the defended relational assessments. They do not establish that pleasures never have local value, that painful activity is automatically good, or that all pleasures and pains have been explained.

The positive candidate is therefore richer: **the lived mode and what the activity is doing together bear on its ethical character**. The relation can be a constituent, not merely a later consequence attached to an otherwise unchanged good. The [primary-source comparison](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md) distinguishes Moore's organic-value challenge from mere causal interdependence and Aristotle's account of completed pleasurable activity from unfinished target attainment. Their evaluated activities and particular ethical judgments remain substantive premises; citing them does not derive worthwhileness.

## 4. What an improvement comparison must actually preserve

The earlier whole-process argument favors a feasible reorganization that removes an impairment while preserving independently warranted goods and introducing no countervailing warranted loss. This remains a useful sufficient comparison. The new issue is whether its antecedent is available in the case under investigation.

Suppose suffering occasions actual compassion and a competent response. Removing the suffering can preserve the participants, their capacities and their relationship while ending *this compassion's present object*. Subsequent enjoyment, companionship or readiness to respond can be good, but they are not literally the same activity of answering this still-existing burden. Treating this as literal continuation of every instantiated activity misses a real change in the whole.

That observation does not make continued suffering preferable. It shows that an argument must compare the relevant wholes instead of obtaining improvement by a description that silently preserves incompatible constituents. A response whose point is relief can succeed by ending the occasion for its present exercise. Its historical value is not erased when its current object ceases. Its enabling capacity can remain available without being continually exercised.

Compassion concerning past suffering can also continue after relief. The fixed claim concerns the ongoing exercise of this response to a still-existing burden, not every possible compassionate relation. Ending that exercise is not automatically an ethically adverse loss: completion can fulfill the good at issue. Thus the test establishes a change requiring accurate appraisal, not a general cost of successful repair or a proof that every repair falls outside a no-loss comparison.

Four roles must be distinguished within that history. A burden may have been a **historical cause** of acquired understanding, a **present enabling condition** under the current arrangement, a **constituent of the response's object**, or the **condition targeted for relief**. One occurrence can occupy several roles, but their consequences differ. A past cause need not remain present for its effect to persist. A currently necessary condition may cease to be necessary under a feasible reorganization. Ending the object of this response may complete rather than frustrate its point. No inference from these dependency facts alone establishes that the burden ought to continue.

The same discipline applies to meaningful pain. Ask which relation is at issue before deciding that painless continuation preserves everything valuable:

- If pain is a causal byproduct of an independently valuable activity, removing it while preserving the activity may be coherent. That claim needs an actual feasible alternative.
- If undergoing a difficulty is part of what the chosen achievement consists in, a proposed painless alternative may be a different achievement. The difference is not automatically ethically decisive; it must be appraised.
- If a person merely believes pain authenticates an activity, first establish whether the alleged constitutive relation is correct. Sincere endorsement alone does not establish it.
- If imposed suffering is indispensable to the initiator's delight in domination, ending it removes that particular delight's object. The fact that a relational good is lost is exactly what is disputed: calling successful domination a protected good would assume the issue.

A legitimate loss cannot be dismissed because it obstructs the preferred comparison. Equally, every lost satisfaction cannot be treated as an ethically warranted claim. The constitutive analysis identifies what changes; the ethical appraisal must justify which changes count in which ways.

## 5. Valuable response does not warrant creating its occasion

Fix three possible histories at a declared decision point, with the actual relations and feasible choices stated. These are a comparison contract, not a claim that an unaffected alternative is always available:

| History | Actual course |
|---|---|
| `H₀` | The burden does not arise; ordinary affected life proceeds, with its own opportunities and limitations. |
| `H₁` | A burden arises or is imposed; a compassionate, competent and appropriately authorized response relieves it. |
| `H₂` | The same burden arises or is imposed; the available response is withheld and the burden continues. |

An independently defended judgment favoring `H₁` over `H₂` can establish the importance of response once the adversity exists. It does not rank `H₁` against `H₀`. The latter comparison includes the occurrence of adversity, how it came about, any intervening losses, the relational good of the response, and the ordinary life or other goods available without that occasion. No equality of these histories is assumed.

For example, the praise due to competent compassion in `H₁` does not itself justify arranging the burden rather than allowing `H₀`. “This adversity makes this compassion possible” can be a true constitutive or causal statement while “therefore this adversity should exist” is unsupported. The conclusion needs a further defended comparison and legitimate means. The [source comparison](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md) identifies Moore's §133 as a historical comparator; this narrower three-history construction is ours, not adoption of his complete ideal-world ranking.

This directly disciplines the generative claim that good enables further good. A response can be good because it answers a condition that would better never have arisen, under a separately warranted whole comparison. Its goodness need not require its opportunity to recur. A repair can generate an ordinary continuation in which that repair is no longer appropriate. Good can thus change its own form and end a need for its own repetition.

Once successful repair removes the target burden, the earlier reason grounded solely in relieving that burden no longer supports repeating that response. This is a change in that reason's application, not proof that no reason to remain exists. Continuing protection, aftercare, an unfulfilled undertaking, new adversity and other independent goods can support further activity. These must be identified rather than inferred from the former intervention's success. The scope discipline is inherited from the commitment studies; its application here follows the newly explicit difference between valuable response and warranted occurrence of its occasion.

No general ban on all adversity follows. Valuable chosen challenge, justified treatment, unavoidable protection and genuine conflicts require their actual grounds. The invalid inference is precise: value of response *given* adversity cannot alone justify causing or renewing that adversity. Optional challenge also cannot be called repair of an antecedent burden without preserving what changed.

## 6. The surviving whole-life rival and positive adjudication

A strong whole-life account can accept every historical and phenomenal fact, acknowledge the difference between genuine relief and a muted report, and still deny that a painful token has a context-invariant negative ethical contribution. Its place in achievement, cultivation, a relationship or an entire life may partly constitute its value. This rival does not pretend that the pain never occurred. Descriptive non-erasure does not refute it.

Our candidate should accept that serious challenge to automatic decomposition. The proposed adverse experiential aspect is not yet an independently proved negative quantity available for addition across wholes. Some transformations preserve sensation while changing its meaning, or preserve pleasantness while changing the activity's object. Whether they preserve the full target of a particular constitutive claim must be fixed before judgment.

There remains a positive reason to investigate the restricted burden identification: it explains why actual relief can address what muting a report does not, why functional success can leave something still unwanted in the undergoing, and why the end of that undergoing can matter without another productive sequel. These are inherited philosophical grounds. The new relational test sharpens where their comparative force applies: determine whether a proposed removal preserves or transforms the independently warranted relations, and assess the changed whole when they cannot all remain.

This yields a partial adjudication. **Reject target success as sufficient; reject a valence-only whole ranking; retain lived quality as actual content; assess the activity's constitutive relations without assigning every lost satisfaction equal standing.** The cruelty and completed-repair cases support this richer candidate. Its ethical content is still defended through substantive judgments about suffering, agency and relationship; it is not a deduction from a causally neutral transition law.

A purely neutralist rival that preserves all facts while denying any ethical significance is not refuted by the descriptive comparisons. The remaining constitutive dispute cannot be solved by labeling an internally formed preference authoritative. Nor does recognizing a good confer permission for any means of producing it. The existing C1/C2 and whole-life grounding obligations remain at their declared scopes.

## 7. Failed claim, revised candidate and scope

**Failed stronger claim:** a process is good insofar as its internally generated directions are fulfilled and its future activity is maintained. Successful cruelty, painful competent response, present pleasure with an unfulfilled wish for more, and benign completion defeat that identification under the defended judgments. The rejection does not imply that all internal fulfillment is irrelevant.

**Failed stronger extrapolation:** require every repair to continue every valuable activity in its identical present form while removing the burden. In the compassion case, the burden partly constitutes the occasion of that particular response-good. Literal continuation can therefore conflict with completion. This is not a disproof of the original sufficient no-loss comparison or a justification of continued burden. If completion preserves or realizes the relevant value without a warranted loss, that sufficient comparison can still apply. The earlier whole-process note did not assert the stronger requirement.

**Revised candidate:** goodness concerns how differentiated life is realized in its actual relations and how activity changes those relations. Some responses answer an existing burden well; their success can appropriately end their present form. Broader improvement may involve a real change or ending of a valuable activity and therefore requires whole appraisal rather than an assumption that nothing valuable changed.

Rest and final enjoyment need no productive sequel. Ending an adverse episode, completing an undertaking, retiring a practice and ending an experiential continuity remain different changes. The argument does not count destruction of a bearer as an automatically improved state of that bearer's undergoing, or require every existing form to persist indefinitely.

| Dependency | Status |
|---|---|
| Actual experiential occurrence and felt mode | Supplied realization premise; no universal consciousness criterion established. |
| The constitutive or causal role of adversity | Case-specific identification needing evidence and interpretation. |
| Permission, authority and worth of the activity | Independently defended premises, not consequences of internal success. |
| Rejection of inference from valuable response to warranted adversity | Conceptual scope result: the conclusion needs further comparative content. |
| Concrete ranking of `H₁` and `H₀` | Not supplied by the response judgment; remains case-dependent. |
| Restricted local adverse-aspect identity and general ethical authority | Substantive unresolved grounding claims. |

An application must be revised if its alleged burden is only a report or assigned signal, if the supposedly preserved good changes its constitutive object, if a purported harmless alternative cannot preserve the claimed relations, or if an alleged authority lacks independent justification. A broader value claim must be narrowed if a better whole-life account preserves the fixed experiential target yet shows its alleged local significance was misdescribed. Mere outweighing does not establish that stronger result, while a genuinely changed constitution may.

The accepted conditional foundation remains complete at its scope. The new contribution is a more demanding constructive test of its ethical interpretation: explain which goods repair realizes, which conditions they depend on, and what their successful completion changes. The next central bridge is **warranted appraisal of a transformation that removes a burden while changing a genuinely valuable relation**, with its feasible alternatives, consent, historical goods and actual losses retained. This is stronger than assuming the easiest no-loss comparison; it does not demand a universal ranking before any local conclusion can be justified.

## 8. Continuation, 6 October 2026: what supported participation actually preserves

The next comparison concerns **removal of a difficulty → continued exercise or replacement of the activity being valued → appraisal of the changed realization**. It narrows the preceding open question to a hypothetical joint musical practice. No actual participants, intervention study or model are reported.

### Genealogy and the specific identification problem

The [self-ground audit §44](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#44-conscious-stakes-and-actual-relations) already distinguishes actual reciprocal participation from its convincing appearance. Sections 54–55 already use joint musical activity to distinguish taking over, enabling, standing by, faithful fulfillment and release of a commitment. Support versus substitution, output insufficiency and music are therefore inherited resources, not discoveries of this continuation.

The [same audit §24](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#24-participation-beyond-information-and-the-joint-foundation) already distinguishes actual participation, predicted outcomes and counterfactual opportunities. [Evaluative application](EVALUATIVE-APPLICATION-MATH.md#a-sufficient-bounded-functional-criterion) already requires real mediation and meaningful sensitivity while permitting redundancy. The addition is a worked identification and appraisal of **which musical choices remain operative as assistance changes task demands and strain**. No new general causal-sensitivity principle is claimed.

### Fix the intended activity and the available alternatives

Consider two musicians undertaking a present duet in which each hears the other and shapes timing, emphasis and phrasing in response. Specify before comparing alternatives that the target includes **their actually exercising the relevant skills in mutually responsive interpretation**, rather than only producing a waveform or enjoying music together. This identifies the activity under examination; it does not prove its ethical value or create a valid obligation simply because someone names it.

Grant that the following arrangements are physically available in the hypothetical case. Their burdens and permissions remain to be assessed; availability is not inferred from an idealized diagram.

1. An intrusive unrelated noise makes hearing the partner harder. Reducing it lets each hear and interpret the other's contribution more reliably.
2. An agreed assistive control reduces a musician's motor strain. Its actual mapping continues to carry that musician's choices about the declared timing and phrasing into the music, which the partner hears and answers.
3. A preset rendering produces a matching musical result while replacing both musicians' current interpretive contributions. Their gestures during playback do not control the declared interpretive range.
4. An adaptive arrangement combines automated functions with current human interpretation. Its classification depends on which choices remain effective and how the participants respond, rather than on the presence of automation alone.

These are constructed comparison arms, not a claim that every particular impairment admits such assistance or that one device realizes all four. A difficult passage, noise and motor strain are not interchangeable kinds of difficulty.

### A concrete test of actual exercise

For the declared target, investigate a range of musically relevant alternatives: an earlier or later entry within the agreed style, a held phrase, a change in emphasis, or an answer to the other's variation. The question is whether the actual arrangement permits and carries the participants' interpretations through those alternatives, with uptake in the joint performance. The range must be specified before observing a favorable example. A decorative light or negligible ornament that follows a gesture does not establish control over phrasing when the target concerns phrasing.

Hold the arrangement fixed while varying an admissible interpretive contribution: would it enter what the other can hear and answer through the operative route? Also seek actual evidence of attending, selecting, executing and responding. An unused capacity cannot certify present exercise; synchrony alone can reflect a shared preset.

This is evidence about a relation, not a universal definition. Redundant contributions can both be actual: another musician or a backup can sustain the output without making the first player's realized exercise fictitious. Identify the operative route and relevant alternatives; do not require each participant to be indispensable or unilaterally controlling.

Live selection or mixing can be expressive activity at another level; a recording retains historical performance. Arm 3 denies only the specified present mutual execution through current gestures. Earlier creative contributions, listening and new selection remain identifiable contributions, not erased because playback occurs.

The relevant distinction is thus supported by the actual history and control relations: **which choices participate, over which range, in which present activity**. Assistive technology can preserve that relation; nominal feedback can fail to do so. A changed medium is not automatically substitution, and an unchanged sound is not automatically preservation.

### Difficulty is not a single quantity

The comparison must distinguish four relations:

| Relation | Question in the musical case |
|---|---|
| Task demand | Which timing, hearing, coordination or execution does the specified activity require? |
| Available capacity | What can these participants actually perceive and do under this arrangement? |
| Felt effort | How demanding is the exercise as experienced, given those capacities? |
| Unwanted burden | Which adverse undergoing or other restriction is alleged, with what ground for that identification? |

A more skilled player may experience less effort while exercising the same interpretive capacity. A better arrangement may reduce interference while permitting richer responsiveness. Reduced effort therefore need not mean reduced achievement. Conversely, choosing a challenge can make the exercise of a particular capacity part of the intended achievement, rather than a dispensable obstacle on the way to sound.

For example, if the independently identified target is an unassisted execution of a specified demanding technique, a device performing that technique changes the achievement. It may enable a different, worthwhile shared interpretation. That does not mean the original achievement occurred. If the target is expressive joint music and does not require that technique, the same assistance may preserve the relevant participation. The actual target must come from the practice and participants' defensible interpretation of it, not be changed retrospectively to make a favored intervention succeed.

More difficulty is not automatically valuable: a challenge can be futile, coerced or harmful. Some ways of making activity easier replace an exercise constitutive of a valuable achievement.

The [additional source comparison](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md#6-continuation-difficulty-support-and-actual-participation) preserves a sharper rival: even when aid retains skill and provides genuine relief, lost exertion might have contributory value. Forsberg and Skelton contest this through a headache case, explicitly acknowledging controversy; Hurka defends selected achievement through challenging activity. Neither settles this duet. Effort lost, participation lost and a valued expressive range lost are different proposed changes. Primary access belongs to the source investigator; this author read the source note.

### Why actual shared expression is a positive candidate good

The proposed good is a present realization: these participants make an interpretation together through their differentiated capacities and responsiveness. Its point includes what they actually hear, initiate, sustain and answer. That relation can be enjoyed now, bring a finite practice to completion, and exist without producing a further capacity or benefit afterward. The candidate does not require future productivity to confer its first value.

The proposed warrant concerns an actual good of making something together: contributions receive uptake, variations are answered and success belongs to their coordination. This explains why assistance that frees expression can be welcome while replacing it secretly defeats the shared achievement. The claim is evaluative; matching a declared target alone does not prove it.

Its defended scope concerns freely shared expression with assessed means and effects. Consent and competence are insufficient by themselves: reciprocal skill can serve cruelty. Actual relationship, expressive content and affected consequences must warrant the appraisal.

The strongest rival can accept every difference in causation, exercise and participation in shaping activity while holding that the participants' welfare is determined only by their relevant experiences or by the delivered musical result. It may still require honesty and fidelity to the selected task for independent reasons. Our causal comparison does not refute that valuation. The relational candidate explains a present good in the actual doing together; the rival must explain the force of that consideration or deny its welfare significance without denying what occurred. This is the same substantive value dispute inherited from §44, now supplied with a more concrete identification route rather than a new necessity proof.

Another alternative is legitimate revision of the activity: the participants may choose shared listening, adaptive mixing or another form of music-making. This can be a worthwhile change. Calling it a change respects the original target instead of declaring every preferred endpoint its fulfillment. Valid permission to change the activity, its present goodness and any surviving undertaking remain separate questions, as §§54–55 already establish.

### A real tradeoff remains

Suppose the available assistive control preserves the declared phrasing choices and mutual responsiveness, substantially reduces unwanted strain, but narrows a timbre palette the musicians use and value. No equally accessible device preserves that palette in the available session. This is a hypothetical conflict, not a clean no-loss arm. The evidence can establish preserved participation and reduced burden while also identifying a genuine expressive limitation.

Our appraisal gives the relief a positive ground and retains the expressive loss. It favors neither maximum ease nor maximum unaided execution automatically. What the specific session aims to realize, the severity of the strain, the importance of the lost range and the participants' legitimate choices bear on the comparison. If the challenge itself concerns that technique or timbre, the intervention may change the achievement. If it concerns mutual phrasing, the intervention can preserve that achievement while limiting another good. This classification advances the comparison without manufacturing a decisive ordering.

### Result and conditions of revision

The limited advance is a worked account of **what continued participation consists in under changed support**, with a defeasible appraisal of mutual expression. Preserving relevant interpretation while removing interference can support improved realization; actual effects, authority and value remain to be assessed. Replacing the relevant exercise cannot count as preserving that achievement solely because sound improves. It may realize another good. The test derives no universal value of agency, exertion or selected challenge.

A result changes if the alleged assistive route does not carry the relevant choices, if apparent responsiveness is merely decorative or prearranged, if actual participation exists at an overlooked level, if the available alternative is misdescribed, or if the target really includes the removed challenge. A stronger ethical conclusion changes if the relational good is not independently warranted, if the intervention removes a legitimate good or authority, or if an outcome-based rival better explains the comparison. These are genuine revision conditions, not exceptions added after a preferred verdict.

No new experiment, formal theorem or source survey was performed. The criterion's mechanism and ethical reach remain distinct: identifying a preserved constitutive relation can improve appraisal, while the claim that this relation is good remains part of the continuing grounding task. The accepted conditional foundation is unchanged.

## 9. Continuation, 6 October 2026: actual engagement and the grounds of its value

The named connection is **actual participation → a noninstrumental contribution to the participants' good**. The question is now value itself, rather than another method for identifying participation or deriving practical consequences from value already granted. The [formed-understanding continuation](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#8-continuation-formed-understanding-can-correct-its-source) supports correction within truthful inquiry but explicitly supplies that inquiry's worth. Its success does not discharge the present question.

### Inheritance and an unavailable inference

The [self-ground audit §24](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#24-participation-beyond-information-and-the-joint-foundation) separates actual participation from predicted outcomes; §§44–45 already defend relational benefit and examine experiential, desire, deception and future-effect explanations. [Section 43](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#43-correction-with-changing-grounds-of-benefit) also requires attitudes to be assessed in the actual continuation. The music and support cases in §§54–55 and the present §8 are inherited. A real-versus-sham comparison, or an attempt to control its confounds, is not a newly discovered method here.

One tempting stronger inference is unavailable: **showing that actual engagement matters beyond a pleasant appearance does not establish an additional value independent of all participants' attitudes**. An informed desire concerning actual collaboration can be fulfilled only when that relation occurs. Such an account can explain the contrast while accepting its causal facts and denying a separate intrinsic increment for participation. Deception, breached undertakings and future risks supply further possible explanations. Preference for the real case is not, by itself, evidence isolating its independent welfare contribution.

This makes §8's acknowledged limitation more exact rather than reversing an earlier claimed proof. Its causal identification remains valid; its positive appraisal is a candidate, not a demonstrated refutation of informed-desire accounts. The [separate primary recheck](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md#7-continuation-actual-participation-and-attitude-sensitive-value) supports this reconciliation without claiming that Nozick or Heathwood establishes independent participation value.

### A positive ground can have joint constituents

The more defensible affirmative proposal is that **actual mutual expression, together with the participants' informed lived concern for it, can constitute a present relational good**. Here concern is part of how the activity is lived, not an outside evaluator donating significance to an otherwise finished process. The participants, their attractions, their interpretive capacities and their actual encounter have formed within the same unfolding. This proposal does not require every good to depend on someone's reflective endorsement.

“Informed concern” concerns understanding the object valued and relevant circumstances; it is not infallible knowledge that this token engagement actually succeeds. Such an attraction can coexist with a mistaken success belief. Evidence of its actual realization remains separately necessary.

Its positive point is concrete. A musical contribution becomes something another person actually hears and answers; the response enters what the first musician then makes. For participants engaged with this doing-together, its realization gives them the encounter they care about. A convincing presentation can preserve enjoyment while failing to realize that relation. The candidate locates their good in actually living the valued engagement, rather than solely in believing that it happened or receiving a later reward. It allows expressive completion to matter now, without another productive sequel.

This argument offers a specified good, not the formula that fulfilling any selected end is good. Cooperation in cruelty, coerced identification and manipulated approval remain substantive objections to that formula. The present positive judgment concerns a benign expressive activity in which participants' contributions receive genuine uptake, their engagement is informed and voluntary, and the effects on others are retained. These features explain the proposed kind of realization; their ethical relevance remains a defended appraisal, not a conclusion entailed by causal coupling or consent alone.

The actual engagement and the attraction need not be two independently existing sources later added together. A composite account may regard their relation as the value-bearing condition. It therefore need not award one quantity for desire satisfaction and a second for participation. This is compatible with several informed-desire or hybrid accounts. Compatibility is a useful result: a generative foundation does not have to eliminate every role for formed attitudes to explain real goods within the whole.

A purely experiential account remains a genuine rival. It can grant that the desired relation failed to occur yet deny a welfare difference when the participants' relevant experiences match. The relational candidate answers that the actual object is part of what they are living well through, not merely a cause of their pleasant belief. That is a substantive comparative welfare judgment, already supported but not decisively established in §§44–45. Calling it objective cannot settle the dispute.

### The stronger claim needs a different comparison

Five propositions must remain separate: actual engagement occurs; it has impersonal value; it benefits a participant; someone has a reason to preserve it; and that person is permitted to use particular means. A favorable reason does not itself establish permission. An observer's apt appreciation might support a proposal about impersonal excellence without proving anyone better off. The stronger claim tested here is specifically an **additional present contribution to participant welfare from actual engagement even without intrinsic concern for it**. It is not implied by the composite proposal above.

A hypothetical routing comparison can expose that stronger claim's burden. Participants intentionally execute phrases as a means to music and enjoyment, while freely authorizing either live mutual routing or a corresponding rendering. They are not promised a particular route. In the live arm their contributions actually shape one another's inputs; in the rendering arm the stipulated experienced sequence is matched without that present mutual influence. Deliberate execution remains in both; only the live arm realizes the mutual relation under examination.

To distinguish the value accounts, the contract must also match the relevant experiences, actual desire fulfillment, commitments, effects on others and subsequent opportunities. It must separately establish that accurate descriptive information about the routing would not produce an intrinsic preference for actual mutual participation. Current ignorance can match nonfactive beliefs; this counterfactual informed indifference is a different premise, not an observation obtained from blindness. “Fully informed” cannot mean already accepting the disputed value theory.

These are demanding coherence and admissibility conditions, not a proposal for an experiment already shown feasible. In particular, an intention to execute a phrase instrumentally need not include intrinsic concern for mutual participation. If preserving the claimed activity actually requires that intrinsic concern, deleting it changes the target and the comparison fails. Hidden frustration of any world-directed desire also defeats the intended isolation. The two arms must not be described as having identical factive knowledge of different routes.

If the contract can be met, an attitude-independent participation thesis predicts an additional participant benefit in the live arm. Pure experiential and the specified informed-desire accounts need not. This is a discriminating disagreement, not evidence that the relational verdict wins. A defender could instead attribute impersonal excellence while agreeing that the indifferent participants are not better off; that would defend a different proposition. If informed attraction is constitutive of the narrower good, this comparison does not refute that narrower proposal either.

### What the investigation resolves

The positive explanatory correction is that **a real relational good can be constituted jointly by formed concern and its actual realization**. It need not become detached from every participant attitude to count as real, noninstrumental or part of the generative whole. Conversely, generating endorsement or satisfying a harmful purpose does not establish goodness. The particular content and relations still need appraisal.

The present contribution reconciles and clarifies inherited candidates; it does not introduce joint concern and realization as a new root definition. Our current arguments support continuing to defend that composite candidate. They do not isolate a further attitude-independent welfare increment for participation. Such an increment is also not a necessary premise of the composite candidate, although removing that unnecessary demand does not complete ethical grounding. The richer musical description, preserved participation in shaping activity and valid correction mechanisms cannot be substituted for that missing argument. This closes the attempted stronger inference and clarifies the positive account; it is not a proof of universal ethical necessity.

The account would change if actual engagement adds no participant benefit under a better experiential explanation, if informed attraction genuinely changes the proposed ground, or if a defensible evaluative argument establishes a benefit in the stronger comparison after its confounds are excluded and its target preserved. Empirical identification alone would not supply that welfare judgment. Practical reasons and authority would still require their proper scope. Rest, receiving care, receptive enjoyment and completion remain possible goods; no requirement of active participation in shaping activity is imposed on healthy life.

## 10. Concern's object and the grounds of its correction

6 October 2026. The named connection is **a formed concern's relation to its object → ethical quality within the unfolding → warranted correction of that concern**. Sections 3 and 7 already reject identifying goodness with successful internal targets. Section 9 preserves a more specific good jointly constituted by informed concern and actual engagement. [Warranted attention, §53](HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#53-warranted-attention-within-a-shared-perspective), already allows improvement without changed outward conduct. The present extension tests the quality of an orientation while holding the other's suffering fixed; it does not rediscover that successful cruelty can be bad.

### Preserve the good activity and isolate the orientation

Two participants genuinely make music together: each hears, answers and shapes the other's contribution, and both understand and care about this engagement. A third person independently undergoes a severe, still-unwanted burden. Compare two continuations retaining the collaboration, its skilled exercise and fulfillment, the third person's burden and subsequent history, and the participants' relevant factual understanding. In one, their enjoyment concerns the shared expression. In the other, enjoyment is also directed toward the third person's undergoing **because it is adverse for that person**.

The comparison matches pleasant intensity and duration and relevant further effects, rather than stipulating equal complete welfare or equal ethical quality. Extra injury, humiliation, changed future aggression, false beliefs about desert, and different opportunities to help cannot supply the alleged contrast. These are conceptual controls to establish, not an observed experiment or a claim that perfectly matched populations can be engineered. Both participants can accurately judge the burden undeserved while welcoming it. Their intentional objects differ, so their complete experiences are not declared identical.

The common collaboration remains actual. Hostile enjoyment does not make its every skill, pleasure or fulfilled concern fictitious. Conversely, its factual accuracy establishes neither the ethical quality of its object nor that of the orientation toward it. An existing desire can succeed without thereby vindicating what it welcomes.

### A positive reason for correction, and a genuine rival

Our affirmative proposal is that **an independently warranted adverse aspect gives a ground against welcoming that very aspect as fulfillment**. The proposed defect concerns the relation now realized, even without another injury caused by it. This is an ethical claim about the response to an identified condition; it is not an inference from a negative signal or from the fact that another person dislikes something.

The supporting explanation is object-specific. Here the feature that makes the orientation satisfied is precisely a feature adverse to an actual life. Criticism answers that orientation's taking the adversity as its welcome object, while leaving other pleasures and achievements available for recognition. If the object instead were the person's relief, or merely a fictional performance understood as such, this particular criticism would need reassessment. If adversity or the attitude's object was misidentified, its stated ground would fail. This makes the proposal answerable to the relation, rather than to an observer's bare disapproval of unfamiliar enjoyment.

Nor does the proposal require the participants to misdescribe the facts. They may know exactly what occurs. The proposed correction concerns how their orientation responds to that condition. To identify it as unfitting is to defend a normative connection, not to correct a false belief by definition. Calling every factually informed preference authoritative would leave no room to assess the orientation on those grounds; its successful formation cannot settle its own authority.

A purely hedonistic account of whole quality can accept the actual pain, all matched pleasures and the genuine collaboration while denying any additional ethical defect in this causally ineffective hostility. It does not merely give a conceded defect less weight. Our judgment favors the object-sensitive account because it explains why the way an adverse life-condition is welcomed can itself be objectionable, even when no additional effect follows. The rival can reject that judgment; neither causal description nor absence of an alternative explanation proves our account necessary. Evidence of later harm would not by itself decide whether this present relation has the proposed ethical significance.

A second dispute concerns how retained pleasantness participates in the whole. One account retains a favorable component alongside an adverse orientation; another regards that pleasantness as constitutively involved in the defect without independently favoring production of the malicious whole. The [new primary comparisons](WHOLE-PROCESS-REALIZATION-SOURCES.md#9-attitudes-to-realized-good-and-adversity) explain this difference. Recognizing actual enjoyment does not decide between these ethical structures. The present argument claims the object-sensitive defect without making either decomposition a foundational axiom or identifying intrinsic-value vocabulary with a proved good-for theory.

### Correction need not eliminate the person or every enjoyment

Where a feasible, independently permissible change redirects enjoyment toward the retained expression, it can improve the orientation in this respect. Actual pleasure, engagement, capacities and personal continuity might remain. Their preservation must be established rather than guaranteed by naming the change correction. The earlier hostile concern ceases or changes; it is not fulfilled by secretly substituting a different object. Costs, coercion, other commitments and subsequent effects remain part of the whole comparison.

The proposed reason is to change the hostile relation, not to annihilate the bearer or suppress all positive affect. It does not depend on a later reward for a reformer or on the third person eventually helping the collaborators. Yet improvement of orientation is not automatically increased welfare for its bearer, an overall ranking, a duty to undertake every correction, or permission to impose it. Any claimed loss in changing the concern needs assessment, not dismissal solely because its former object was adverse.

### Connect actual attitudes to the future-good appraisal

This gives the integral a further kind of proposed content. A caring or hostile orientation can be an actual feature of a history, whose ethical character depends on its object. Its proposed value is different from its instrumental significance through what it later produces. The [recursion distinction](FUTURE-GOOD-INTEGRAL.md#attitudes-as-realizations-rather-than-repeated-future-value) preserves that difference. Neither repeated description of one attitude nor the possibility of caring about caring establishes additional instantiated attitudes. A future benefit also cannot be counted again as fresh present good merely because someone aims at it. A distinct relational contribution requires its own identification and warrant.

The bounded advance is a defended account of why a generated priority can require correction despite factual accuracy and fulfillment, with a rival denying the proposed relational contribution. This concerns the ethical character of formed evaluation itself. It does not supply the original warrant for all goods and burdens, derive a complete ordering, or discharge the universal minimum. The rival can retain ordinary relief reasons while denying this additional defect, so the latter must not become an unannounced prerequisite for baseline concern. The test must also identify its ethical target. An inability to maximize them together is therefore compatible with a unified account and cannot, by itself, establish that an additional external principle is needed.

## 12. Formed appreciation and the claim of added good

6 October 2026. Named connection: **activity forms a lived concern together with its actual fulfillment → present good → prospective significance of enabling that formation**. ### A positive formation need not repair an induced absence

Begin with actual shared music. A participant understands and competently contributes to the collaboration to learn a timing pattern, while lacking intrinsic concern for the shared expression itself. During the activity, that expression comes to be intrinsically appreciated. It is already occurring: the new concern can be fulfilled from its formation, without an intervening episode of frustrated wanting. Actual learning, attention and interaction produce this change; an outside evaluator need not donate an antecedently warranted private preference.

The proposed good is the changed way of living an actual encounter. A contribution is heard and answered, and that realized relation becomes something the participant welcomes for itself. Our affirmative appraisal remains that such informed appreciative engagement can constitute a present good. The immediate target is a contribution to participant good within the whole; this alone establishes neither overall whole-history improvement nor a practical reason or permission to produce it. Its experiential and relational constituents belong to the same unfolding. This is a substantive value judgment; neither richer intentional content nor successful preference installation proves it.

The conceptual consequence is firmer and narrower: causal formation of concern does not imply that an unmet need was first manufactured and then repaired. The [injury–repair comparison](WHOLE-PROCESS-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md#7-continuation-the-origin-of-a-need-and-the-value-of-its-repair) requires its actual adverse detour. It cannot be applied solely because a concern has a cause. The present account does not deny transition costs, future vulnerability or opportunities lost; it says they must be identified in the history. Likewise, a new description of an unchanged state would not establish new appreciation.

### The stronger comparative claim does not follow

The proposal in §11 matched participation, accomplishment, pleasant intensity and duration. Those controls do not establish equal total pleasure under every serious account of pleasure. Appreciation may itself be attitudinal pleasure. Matching a sensory tone while adding appreciative engagement can therefore leave relevant pleasure unmatched; the inherited Heathwood identity and the new 2019 discussion make this a substantive possibility, not a settled analysis of every pleasant experience.

A concrete reallocation shows a second difficulty. In continuation A, the musician intrinsically appreciates the actual tonal expression while valuing mutual participation instrumentally. In continuation B, intrinsic appreciation shifts toward the actual mutual participation. Both activities are genuinely collaborative, both attractions concern real understood objects, and accomplishment and total attitudinal enjoyment may match. Neither continuation is an empty pursuit or a deceptive substitute.

The composite account in §9 can regard both as living a valued relation. The newly formed concern in B does not by itself show that B contains more good: another fulfilled appreciation may have changed or been displaced. A further judgment about participation in shaping activity, the distribution of appreciation or the relation's quality would be needed. Switching to a claim about moral worth would not settle the proposed participant-benefit comparison either.

This corrects the initial affirmative suggestion made during internal review. The present argument supports a possible new instance or configuration of good, not a demonstrated increase beyond all matched pleasures and other goods. It neither establishes equality nor refutes an additional relational contribution. The strict increment remains unproved; it is not a necessary premise of the broader composite account.

### A rival can accept fulfillment without favoring acquisition

Even when no prior appreciation is displaced, a stronger rival can grant the new informed lived concern and its actual fulfillment while denying that its acquisition improves the history over continued informed indifference. It can also grant that, once the concern exists, fulfillment is better than frustration. These are different comparisons:

| Comparison | What must be justified |
|---|---|
| Formed concern fulfilled versus that concern frustrated | The proposed contribution of fulfillment or avoidance of frustration, given the concern. |
| Formed and fulfilled concern versus continuing informed indifference | Whether the formation adds good, rather than only creating a new condition under which a loss could occur. |
| One fulfilled appreciation versus another | Their comparative quality and whatever is displaced or preserved. |

No implication from the first row alone establishes the second or third. This comparison pattern is inherited from the earlier repair and new-life-formation work; its application here concerns a new orientation within a continuing life. Dietz supplies a direct theoretical treatment of the acquisition dispute. His criticism of comprehensive alternatives does not refute every limited view denying an additional contribution from this particular concern. A rival can recognize pleasure and other positive goods while declining to count an extra satisfaction benefit; it need not deny every positive value.

Our affirmative appraisal of appreciative engagement remains plausible because its object is actually lived and welcomed, rather than merely scored as achieved. The rival disputes the value of that relation or its additional contribution. Repeating the psychological description, citing subsequent endorsement, or assuming the rival must secretly omit the experience does not defeat it. The strongest supported conclusion therefore retains the candidate good while withdrawing the automatic comparative increase.

### Formation history and future significance

Deliberate teaching or another intervention need not invalidate genuinely lived appreciation merely by being artificial. Conversely, spontaneous or ordinary development does not certify goodness. Identify deception, coercion, altered access, displaced concerns and future effects directly. A wrongful formation process may leave a real later benefit; that benefit does not retrospectively authorize the process. Nor is every welcome object benign: §10's hostile enjoyment continues to constrain the proposed account.

For Ren's integral, appraise the actual continuation with the concerns and fulfillments that form within it. Do not freeze the starting evaluator; do not count acquisition and fulfillment as two goods merely because they have two names. If appreciative fulfillment and pleasure are the same good under a warranted account, their two descriptions are not two contributions. Distinct goods remain possible, but require identification. Whole-history improvement also retains the formation history, other affected lives and alternatives.

The result advances the generative account at a declared scope: formed appreciation need not be a repair of induced deprivation, and a possible present good need not rely on a previously held private demand. It does not derive the good's ethical authority from formation, establish a further nonhedonic increment, or prove universal necessity. The next governing connection remains the substantive warrant of lived fulfillment or adversity and its significance within the whole. This study closes the automatic-addition route; future work must defend the actual ground rather than repeat the acquisition mechanism as proof of it.
