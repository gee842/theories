## 1. The correction that makes this a new test

The preceding case relied on a warranted undertaking to issue a completed competence record. The present comparison removes that ground: all proposed terms are disclosed accurately before training, no relevant undertaking is broken, and the learner understands and deliberately accepts them. This means understanding the arrangement, significant consequences and relevant uncertainty, not possessing advance experience of every appreciation that training will form. Genuine agency and understanding are part of the case. We do not obtain an adverse verdict by silently restoring deception, incompetence or an unfulfilled delivery promise.

The proposed distinction between an intrinsically shared good and a separable skill is useful descriptively but inadequate ethically. A restriction on an operationally portable skill can sustain a worthwhile common performance or an actual teaching arrangement. Conversely, dependence can be constitutive of an objectionable practice. Nor does repayment automatically end a fixed-term commitment: prospective agreement can form a further ground, including shared reliance and meaningful self-binding, which is not exhausted by the provider's recovered expenditure.

These corrections matter to the universal account. A theory that made every acquired dependence illegitimate would misdescribe constructive formation. A theory that made every accurately accepted relation authoritative would leave the ethical character of the formed authority unexplained. The task is to defend the actual difference, including the significance of commitment itself.

## 2. One formation history and several powers

A teaching association brings about skill through learners' activity, teachers' work, shared tools, accumulated knowledge and surrounding practices. The capacities to teach, learn, undertake and evaluate differentiate through these relations. Neither an independently complete provider nor an independently complete learner enters as the foundation. Their contributions and practical positions nevertheless differ: a teacher may supply scarce time, learners may undertake effort and expense, and an office may control recognition or participation.

Consider an offer that produces a real craft, valued affiliation and a usable capacity. The learner can understand and decline the offer. Suppose the association could actually provide the training under a specified finite return arrangement, with its real labor, risk and resources accounted for. This feasibility is a stipulation of the constructed comparison, not evidence that every real school can finance an open alternative. An ethical application would need that evidence. The learner's acceptance does not by itself waive other people's claims or establish authority over them.

Distinguish four proposed arrangements. None is classified solely by its name or the existence of assent.

| Arrangement | Actual relationship requiring assessment | Proposed scope of authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Finite reciprocal undertaking.** Training accompanies a clearly specified period of contribution to the common practice. | Teachers' work, learners' effort, provision risk, actual reliance and a shared finite undertaking. | The specified contribution; earlier cost recovery need not discharge the whole undertaking. A claim to performance and permissible ways of enforcing it still differ. |
| **Shared completion.** Participants agree to postpone separate use of a jointly developed performance until its first common presentation. | The preparation and timing of this particular achievement. The individual skills may be portable while the agreed completion remains unrealized. | A restriction with that content and duration can have a substantive ground. Operational portability alone does not refute it. |
| **Continuing affiliation.** Members deliberately remain within an ongoing practice with demanding shared standards. | Actual belonging, reciprocal participation and a form of life they may reasonably value. Departure changes or ends some of these goods. | Authority over participation may continue; departure alone establishes no claim to a duplicate relationship. Its scope and any surviving commitments, including specific transition undertakings, still require appraisal. |
| **Continuing ordinary-use veto.** After the specified formation return and shared undertakings are fulfilled, the office retains indefinite discretion to block unrelated ordinary uses of the acquired skill. | The earlier contribution and assent, possible allegiance or identity goods, the office's continuing power, and concrete lost uses in later living. | The disputed power extends beyond current participation and identified unfinished cooperation. Disclosure describes its content; it does not settle its warrant. |

In the last case, the association can block a former member's competent repair work in an independently available workshop, work that would meet actual ordinary needs. Relevant tools and access there actually exist. The disclosed arrangement attaches an effectively enforced penalty to ordinary use without the office's permission; the moral authority to impose this penalty is disputed, with no claim about any actual legal regime. The use does not falsely claim affiliation, disclose protected shared material or create the stipulated safety risk. The association has received the stated finite contribution; no further promised service, third-party reliance or specific shared project remains unfulfilled. These are case conditions, not facts inferred from silence. We still retain its members' actual preference for durable allegiance and the learner's earlier commitment to the veto itself. Eliminating every competing consideration by labeling it false would evade the rival.

The compared change removes this ordinary-use veto while preserving the identified finite obligations and permitting continued voluntary affiliation. It does not preserve guaranteed allegiance, the feeling of irrevocable membership or the original institution's entire identity. Those losses are real candidates for assessment. Nor does the change require continued teaching, duplicate facilities or an unlimited subsidy. If lifting the restriction itself entails significant costs or destroys another genuine cooperation, the comparison must include them. The organizer's alternative design before training and the office's later cessation of enforcement are different acts at different times. Feasibility of either does not establish feasibility or authority for every participant's unilateral action.

## 3. The affirmative argument and its ethical dependence

The affirmative candidate permits real prospective authority. In a worthwhile reciprocal undertaking, acceptance can help constitute the shared organization through which teaching, contribution and completion become possible. Keeping an undertaking may realize fidelity and participation in practical decisions even when breaking it would increase an immediate option count. Its force need not depend on a presently favorable attitude. These are substantive claims about the quality of such relationships; neither their occurrence nor their efficiency proves them universally.

For the ordinary-use veto, the candidate favors removing the office's discretionary restriction while retaining the specified fulfilled history and any distinct surviving claims. Its reason concerns a concrete continuation: a developed skill can now participate in actual independent work and ordinary living, while the office can prevent that realization without performing further teaching or protecting the named common achievement. The gain is this contentful use and the changed relation of dependence, not the mere number of available options. The original generosity and genuine belonging remain part of the history; they do not disappear when the power changes.

The decisive ethical claim is that **the value of this disclosed self-binding and guaranteed allegiance does not, in the specified circumstances, justify indefinite discretionary control over those ordinary uses**. It does not deny that commitment can supply a reason. It denies that its agreed breadth fixes the breadth of justified interference. We support that assessment through the contrast in what the powers realize: the finite restrictions organize actual reciprocity or a particular completion; the residual veto makes the exercise of an already developed capacity depend on continued institutional leave, despite the named cooperative grounds being fulfilled. Protecting affiliation and recognition within the continuing practice does not by itself explain authority over unrelated work after departure.

This is a substantive ethical comparison, not a theorem obtained from portability or the word “whole.” It gives weight to the actual independent work and living, to reciprocal achievement and to warranted commitment; it limits what allegiance can justify. A rival can dispute that weight or defend a stronger normative power of self-binding. Generative description has made the disagreement exact but has not yet supplied a universally necessary ordering. The absence of a demonstrated justification alone would not establish a prohibition; the adverse judgment here relies on the positive assessment just stated.

Its immediate practical object is the office's blocking intervention. Rejecting that power does not by itself extinguish every possible claim concerning the learner's undertaking: a narrower fidelity obligation, a reason to seek release or a responsibility concerning the changed relationship may still require adjudication. A claim to performance, permission to impose a penalty and a participant's permission to disregard every commitment are different conclusions. The present argument does not derive all three from the rejection of this veto.

Two invalid shortcuts are excluded. Distributed formation does not entail that exclusive authority is always illegitimate: many justified arrangements allocate control within co-produced conditions. Equally, a provider's causal contribution does not itself establish authority over everything it helped enable. The scope must be explained through the actual practical relationship. This sharpens the inherited rejection of role self-certification rather than presenting it as a newly discovered universal ground.

## 4. The strongest rival: chosen commitment has value of its own

The rival says that the association may permissibly decline to teach and that a competent learner can prefer the fully disclosed restrictive offer to no offer. A lasting commitment can also be part of the good sought, rather than an unfortunate price paid for skill. Ending the restriction can therefore change the undertaking's good even when the craft survives. A whole account must preserve these points; it cannot call every preference for durable allegiance a cognitive error.

The response distinguishes the reasonableness of accepting the available offer, the legitimacy of the offer's terms, and the authority to implement a particular later interference. An offer can improve someone's situation without that comparison alone settling its conditions. Conversely, a term's being objectionable does not automatically entail that outsiders should prohibit the transaction, that the provider owes the same service without conditions, or that the learner's agreement has no force. Those further conclusions need their own grounds and actual alternatives.

The hardest disagreement survives after these distinctions. If freely adopted allegiance itself is claimed to justify the indefinite ordinary-use veto, the rival owes an account of why that act of adoption warrants so extensive an ongoing power. The affirmative must justify its proposed limit on the authority that agreement can confer; simply assuming unrestricted future independence would beg the question in the opposite direction. A defended normative power of consent is not circular merely because its exercise changes what is permitted. The question is whether it extends to this kind and scope of interference. Our supported judgment is the bounded comparison in §3. We have not proved that every lifelong undertaking is invalid, that every permissible restriction must be continuously revocable, or that the lesser power always prevails.

This also corrects an attractive but excessive rule: **“restriction ends when its original productive purpose ends.”** Formation can create a further commitment whose ground is no longer reducible to that original purpose. What must be assessed is the full current ground, including the actual earlier undertaking. Time elapsed, money repaid or a new preference is not sufficient by itself to erase it. A stable procedure can settle a scope without reopening every decision indefinitely; its legitimate finality is one of the goods and authorities requiring explanation.

## 5. What this adds to subjective fields and trajectories

The learner's favorable field can include teachers, peers, the craft, the institution and enduring allegiance. Its breadth does not specify how those attachments confer authority. A very wide identification with the association can coexist with extensive control of former members; a bounded commitment can support reliable cooperation and a legitimate ending. A moral direction cannot be read off field width, satisfaction or persistence.

The relevant candidate trajectory is more specific: **successful formation of skill and attachment → a supported claim to reciprocal contribution → expansion or retention of control beyond that claim → later approval treated as authority for the expanded scope**. The present case tests the retention variant, whose broad term was disclosed from the start, rather than an unannounced enlargement. Each arrow is a hypothesis requiring evidence in a real application. A constructive comparison can sustain the actual undertaking, credit its fulfillment, permit continuing affiliation and constrain a remaining power on the grounds it actually has. Neither trajectory is classified merely by whether the originating institution survives.

This connects Ren's karma-like direction to changes in the practical organization of consequences. What earlier activity produces includes goods, skills, expectations and powers; these change what subsequent activity can realize and what it demands of those participating. Formation can leave a reason for gratitude or fidelity without leaving the same enforcement power. Conversely, a genuine ongoing common undertaking can retain authority after initial costs are recovered. Counting skill, approval or dependence alone loses these differences.

The concurrent [ethical-identification study](WHOLE-ETHICAL-IDENTIFICATION-ARGUMENT.md) supplies a further control: genuinely ending a striving or attenuating a capacity can realize contentment. This study's favorable comparison concerns the specified useful work and living, not an imperative to keep every capacity growing or exercised. A participant's worthwhile completion or chosen ending remains possible; it does not automatically authorize an office to impose that ending through a different history.

For learning and self-modification, track changes in the grounds, their recognition and their implemented scope separately. A later preference for wider control does not alone expand authority; a completed undertaking can genuinely change it; a new warranted undertaking can form it again. This is a refinement of the existing ground-sensitive preservation requirement, not a newly proved invariant. The earlier formal package can preserve supplied distinctions only if its state and comparison include the relevant history; it does not discover these ethical grounds or establish a meta-attractor basin.

## 6. Integration and the remaining universal connection

The accompanying [substantive review](PERSPECTIVAL-PROSPECTIVE-CONTROL-REVIEW.md) supplies the formation-and-commitment rival and changes the comparison in three ways: portable skills can be legitimately restricted; cost recovery does not discharge every formed commitment; and loss of guaranteed allegiance must be counted rather than renamed as no loss. Its final critical pass also narrows the verdict from erasing all claims to rejecting a specified exercise of control, preserves possible transition undertakings and corrects the scope of one participant's authority concerning others. These revisions are incorporated above.

The [correspondence audit](PERSPECTIVAL-PROSPECTIVE-CONTROL-CORRESPONDENCE.md) distinguishes operational portability, preservation of a particular good, actual access and disputed authority. It separates available designs before formation from feasible release or transformation afterward. It also requires a concrete mechanism of interference and retains the ethical-identification countercase to generic capacity superiority. These are constraints on faithfully stating the case, not independent proofs of the verdict.

The [primary-source assessment](PERSPECTIVAL-PROSPECTIVE-CONTROL-SOURCES.md) adds three specific challenges. Ferguson's [“Exploitation's grounding problem”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266267124000397) defends consent's power to change the standing of distributively unfair terms; his account leaves other wrongs, noncommodifiability and third-party effects open. This strengthens the rival from a preference for allegiance to a proposed normative power. Mill's [*On Liberty*, chapter V](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/34901/34901-h/34901-h.htm) defends ordinary engagements and reliance while limiting total alienation through his justification of liberty. That substantive limit cannot simply be transferred to partial guild control. Wenner's [author preprint](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/dwenner/papers/2019WennerIJFAB.pdf) permits controlled delegation during later incapacity, challenging any universal requirement of continuous unilateral revocation. Her nondomination criterion remains a substantive candidate. The source reviewer inspected the passages and records editions and access; this integration does not claim an additional independent reading of each original. These are internal AI research contributions and philosophical comparisons, not external validation or empirical evidence.

The bounded advance is already sharper than the initial proposed test: **neither separability nor fulfilled expenditure sets the extent of authority; prospective agreement can form a genuine further ground, whose practical reach remains a substantive ethical question**. This permits constructive dependence, real completion and meaningful commitment while exposing the specific claim needed to justify control beyond them. It does not settle universal normative grounding through an appealing local example.

The next substantive connection for this supporting branch must address that remaining ethical content rather than repeat another no-promise story. The precise rival is that competent adoption of an enduring allegiance can by itself justify the disputed breadth of control; the affirmative holds that adopting a commitment can change practical grounds within the whole realization without automatically settling the extent of justified interference. A further advance would defend or revise one of those accounts of what agreement can authorize, with the actual goods and losses retained. The universal programme and other authorized branches keep their wider priorities. More detail about how approval reproduces would be mechanism evidence, not an answer to this normative disagreement.
