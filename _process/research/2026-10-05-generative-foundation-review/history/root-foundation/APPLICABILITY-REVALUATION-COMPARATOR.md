## What this identifies

The useful explanatory dependency is **historically acquired outcome significance participating in present selection**. A proposed account that preserves only established action strengths and current deprivation misses a relevant relation: experience can change what an anticipated consequence contributes to selection before the action is reinforced again. This is more specific than observing less behavior after a general manipulation.

A common scheduled condition must not be read as matched complete physiology. Extinction can expose dependence on learned relations while changing what actions actually deliver. A mechanistic comparison must represent both distinctions; neither identical bodily state nor an unchanged delivery law follows from this design.

The retained effect can be described as a change in whether a previously useful action remains attractive in the current condition. That is an operational applicability distinction. It is not yet a demonstration that the organism represents a proposition of the form “this undertaking no longer applies,” abandons an encompassing purpose, or discovers a new purpose. Those stronger descriptions require their own evidence.

A manipulation spanning acquisition and use cannot uniquely locate encoding, retention, retrieval or integration with action selection. It is not a phase-specific memory-reader disconnection. Interaction evidence and nonsignificant comparisons also answer different questions; the latter do not establish exact equivalence. When eligibility depends on acquisition, inference from the retained sample must not silently expand to all initially assigned animals. These are limits on our interpretation, without a new analysis or numerical replication.

## The strongest ordinary rival remains admissible

An ordinary controller can retain learned action–outcome relations, acquire outcome values conditional on encountered conditions, and use those values in later choice. Its encompassing algorithm can remain fixed while its operative evaluation changes. Such a model is a candidate explanation of the acquired organization, not a refutation of its existence.

Consequently, “the behavior cannot be a fixed policy on new inputs” is too broad. If those inputs include learned memory or the entire history, that description may reproduce the change. The meaningful restricted contrast is against an account whose stated accessible state omits the acquired outcome-specific relation. It must not covertly reintroduce that relation as an unexplained current input.

Food-directed activity, consumption, changing bodily condition and later action are differentiated parts of one history. Describing these parts separately preserves the experimental intervention and its temporal dependencies; it does not establish an independent meaning-giver assigning value to an otherwise disconnected organism. Conversely, the experimental account does not explain the formation of every biological sensitivity it uses. Feeding regime, available outcomes, action repertoire, training arrangement and the organism's existing machinery remain given at this explanatory scale. That limitation does not place them outside generative reality.

## Constraint on the current applicability question

The [acquired-discrimination study](DISCRIMINATION-REVISION-COMPARATORS.md) concerns changing which distinctions participate in inquiry under an unchanged undertaking. The present comparator addresses another dependency: acquired experience can change the outcome's contribution to selecting an established action. The [earlier applicability identification note](COMPLETION-APPLICABILITY-IDENTIFICATION.md) separately distinguishes fulfillment, episode binding and surviving grounds. Neither kind of revision should be inferred from the other merely because action eventually changes.

A useful mechanistic test would preserve an acquired action–outcome relation, vary subsequent experience of the outcome without reinforced retraining of that action, and test selection under a common specified current condition. The mechanism must expose how the acquired change enters selection. A selective intervention could then interrupt that use while preserving sufficient means knowledge and action capacity. Where another outcome still supports action, global shutdown would not count as selective revaluation.

This is a discriminator for a proposed implementation, not a newly required universal architecture or a request for another controller. A changed recommendation supplied directly by an analyst would bypass the connection. A faithful acquired-state update that changes selection would realize it, even under a conventional algorithm. Failure to differentiate after adequate acquisition would challenge the claimed mechanism; missing access to the relevant acquired relation would instead locate a representational limitation.

The empirical comparison does not establish warranted concern for other beings, reliable ethical revision, or the origin of all criteria. Its legitimate contribution is to constrain a concrete explanatory bridge between experienced consequence and later goal-sensitive action, while preserving both biological premises and ordinary computational alternatives.
