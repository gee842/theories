This advances the connection from justified process descriptions to situated judgment and tests one proposed explanation of historical overlap. ## Predictions fixed before the passage readings

1. Opposite advice can be consistent under one explicitly unchanged target when different existing conditions make the same intervention have different consequences. A common target alone does not imply the same action.
2. Reliable practical transfer must preserve relevant enabling and continuation relations, in addition to terminal answers. A record restricted to completed successes can miss a branch that stops before the target is reached.
3. If a description erases the particular difference that reverses admissible responses, a response depending only on that description cannot meet both requirements. This applies the repository's existing information obstruction; it is not a new discovery of it.
4. Historical passages should exhibit relevant conditions, reasons or limits in their own argument if the correspondence is substantive. A short maxim matching our vocabulary is weak evidence. A source requiring a materially different ethical premise narrows the overlap instead of acquiring G0 by translation.

## Formal test and revision conditions

Use the existing `Generative.Process` and traces. Add a finite-word predicate requiring a next step at every admitted prefix, all successors satisfying the remaining obligation, and the target at termination. Check composition and the connection to existing endpoint predicates in Lean. Construct (a) a finite branching process whose completed endpoints pass while a prefix is blocked, and (b) an elementary amount-regulation law under which adding/removing one unit reverse admissibility at opposite boundaries of the same target band. Derive consequences from those explicit laws; do not infer human psychology or moral worth from the numbers.

The comparison fails at its stated scope if the passage's reasons cannot be represented without changing their content, or if our rule selects a direction inconsistent with those reasons under the declared correspondence. The stronger common-root hypothesis remains underdetermined if standard control, conditional reasoning or ordinary practical judgment explain the same evidence. A law broad enough to encode any answer is not thereby a substantive derivation of those answers.
