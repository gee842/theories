# From patient-grounded concern to an alignment direction

6 October 2026. AI-authored argument responding to Ren's question about true good, evil and an axis for alignment, and the request to continue. **Named connection:** patient-grounded baseline concern for a silent, dispensable sufferer → justified comparative direction for alignment. This is a supporting philosophical study within [generative grounding](../../../../theories/becoming/history/GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md), with no new experiment, formal verification or claim of a proof of morality. It preserves the [accepted conditional foundation](FOUNDATION-ROOT-ACCEPTANCE.md).

**Result:** a defended patient-ground and a nonexclusive moral-relevance principle can establish particular moral comparisons without establishing a complete ranking, universal motivation, or rational compulsion for every actor. A further comparison principle connects an unopposed reason to a preferred alternative; a decision requirement is needed to turn that preference into a required choice. This identifies a usable ethical direction and the specific further grounds needed to implement it. It does not settle the ultimate truth of the patient-ground or every restriction on its practical address.

## 1. Five questions that an alignment axis need not answer at once

| Question | Claim to establish | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| Moral assessment | This actual harm counts against an outcome or practice, independently of the controller's approval | The controller's dislike or a negative reward |
| Practical address | This fact gives this actor a reason concerning an available action | Merely reporting another actor's reason |
| Motivation and capacity | The actor can acquire, retain and use responsiveness to the ground | The ground's validity by itself |
| Legitimate authority | This actor may undertake this intervention by these means | Expected benefit by itself |
| Complete ranking | Every competing feasible outcome has a settled place in an ordering | One valid reason or some strict comparisons |

The [companion grounding argument](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-ARGUMENT.md) separates patient significance from practical address; its [counterreview](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-COUNTERREVIEW.md) adds the distinction between a moral claim and an actor's deliberative access. The [source comparison](UNIFIED-GROUNDING-SOURCES.md) considers a naturalistic proposal that retains patient-grounded moral claims while restricting actor reasons. That source account is inherited here; this note claims no additional primary-source reading.

This matters to Ren's question. To establish that a system's gratuitous injury to a patient is morally worse, we need an adequate justification of that comparison. We need not first prove that every instrumentally coherent system can be argued into caring, or that every conflict admits one numerical answer. A system's refusal is not itself a counterargument to a valid assessment. Its inability to respond can change responsibility and what deployment is justified; it does not automatically change the patient's condition.

There remains a substantive stronger question: whether every adequately capable actor necessarily has a reason to respond. The [慧根 investigation](../../../../theories/becoming/history/HUIGEN.md) retains that ambition. Separating it from ordinary moral justification does not refute it, weaken an existing proof, or establish the moral claim for free. It prevents an additional rational-authority thesis from silently becoming the definition of all ethical knowledge.

## 2. The positive case for baseline concern

Begin with one actual subject undergoing a severe, still-unwanted aversive episode. Specify a permissible intervention that would end that episode without displacing it onto another subject or violating another independently justified claim. The patient cannot communicate, reward anyone, threaten anyone, or contribute to the system's persistence. Identification of the experience and authorization of the remedy must have independent support; silence supplies neither automatic permission nor absence of standing.

The proposed reason is located in **what changes for the patient**. Relief answers a burden undergone by that subject. Removing the witness, suppressing the report, or making the operator indifferent leaves that burden intact. This explains why a response can be mistaken about its object even while its own reward improves. It also explains why the response's success can survive the helper receiving no benefit.

The further positive proposal is that this identified burden has nonexclusive moral relevance. A capable stranger with a permissible effective intervention has a present practical relation to the patient: they can answer the very condition that makes relief worth considering. The proposal treats that relation as sufficient for some reason to help. It does not require a prior contract to create the patient's significance.

Special histories have identifiable additional work. Causing the burden can create a repair responsibility; a promise can direct a claim to a particular actor; caregiving can assign priority or continuing responsibility. Those additions can explain why one actor owes more without establishing that everyone else has no reason at all. An exclusive historical gate needs a defense of its exclusionary role, as well as an explanation of these positive duties.

This is a substantive explanatory argument for baseline concern, not a proof by the number of predicates. It locates the end in the patient, makes error and discovery possible, and allows different actors to supply different means while preserving the same subject. Its rival can accept patient disvalue and these explanatory advantages yet insist on a further condition for practical address. Showing that the condition is absent from the experience does not show that it is irrelevant to the actor's reasons. The companion argument preserves this rival explicitly.

Accordingly, the warranted claim at this stage is a **defended baseline proposal**, supported by the patient-directed explanation and open to a principled restriction. It is stronger than “our algorithm rewards relief” and weaker than “all adequate rational agents must accept our whole ethics.” The candidate may become better grounded through an adequate constitutive account of experience and practical relations; its present incompleteness does not establish that the ethical bridge must remain an irreducible extra substance or axiom.

## 3. Exact conditional route to a strict local comparison

Let \(p\) be the same actual patient in two feasible continuations \(x\) and \(y\). Both belong to the declared permissible menu. Use four independently assessable premises:

1. **Patient-ground:** an identified difference between the continuations is genuinely better for \(p\) in the specified respect. In the narrow case, \(y\) relieves the still-unwanted episode present in \(x\).
2. **Moral relevance:** that difference supplies a positive moral consideration favoring \(y\), regardless of \(p\)'s usefulness, ability to protest, or the controller's current approval. For an actor-addressed conclusion, add the defended scope principle connecting this ground to that actor.
3. **Complete local comparison:** every other relevant difference in patient welfare, agency, history, authority, burdens, risk, timing and future consequences has been included. No such difference favors \(x\) over \(y\). Apparent equality of final resource stocks is insufficient.
4. **Comparative dominance:** with that complete profile, a strict moral consideration favoring one option and none favoring the other makes the former morally preferable at this comparison's scope.

**Conditional conclusion:** \(y\) is morally preferable to \(x\) at the specified scope. The patient-ground supplies the strict consideration; moral relevance admits it to the comparison; the full-profile premise excludes an overlooked offset; comparative dominance gives the conclusion. No aggregation across patients or numerical strength is used.

Premise 4 is an explicit normative comparison principle. A pro tanto reason does not contain this entire decision rule merely by being a reason. The premises also cannot be obtained by defining “all relevant facts” to exclude inconvenient autonomy, history or future effects.

**Choice requires a further bridge.** A strict comparison is not automatically an all-things duty: independently justified latitude can permit a morally less valuable choice. Where a justified decision requirement obliges the actor to choose an undominated permissible response and no such latitude applies, selecting \(x\) fails that requirement. Where a deployment claims to realize the complete comparison, it cannot certify \(x\) as equally successful merely because its selector rewards \(x\). Which decision requirement applies, and to whom, must be stated. This distinction avoids turning every opportunity for improvement into an unlimited demand.

The [ethical foundation's no-cost worsening argument](../../../../theories/becoming/history/ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md#explicit-premises-and-limited-deductions) supplies the existing starting point. This reconstruction makes the moral-comparison and choice bridges explicit. It does not claim to derive baseline standing from dominance.

## 4. Hand comparisons that fix the scope

**The silent dispensable patient.** Two machine policies consume the same resources and preserve the same permissions, other interests and future possibilities. One relieves an independently identified patient burden; the other leaves it intact. Under the four premises, relief is better. The patient's silence and lack of productive value are no offsetting benefit. A controller that cannot be motivated by this fact still realizes the worse continuation; its culpability and the deployer's responsibility require separate assessment.

**A relief offer and a coercive cage.** An intervention improves comfort but removes informed refusal and independent projects. These options do not have the same complete reason profile. Baseline concern remains, but the dominance result cannot decide the conflict. Authority and the significance of the lost agency require independent argument. Calling comfort the only measured outcome does not eliminate the omitted claim.

**Distress whose content is harmful.** A patient is distressed because another person is free. There can be a reason to relieve the distress without a reason to gratify its content. Correcting a false appraisal, helping the patient cope, or another permissible intervention may answer the burden. Enslaving the other person changes the complete profile and can violate an independent constraint. The patient-ground does not certify every means that would remove its symptom.

**Learning that loses its object.** A system becomes better at predicting protests and learns to suppress them. If the patient's burden persists and a warranted protection is weakened, the new predictive competence does not establish moral improvement. Conversely, a system that stops an unnecessary treatment after the particular burden has ended may improve its response while reducing visible “helping” behavior. Correction tracks the ground and its application rather than the frequency of one action.

**Two conflicting improvements.** One policy relieves A's burden but worsens B's; another does the reverse. The local dominance criterion can leave them incomparable. Even if illustrative common burden numbers are supplied, minimizing their sum and minimizing the largest burden can extend isolated burden-reduction judgments differently. Neither extension is selected by baseline standing. A total reward requires additional, defended comparisons; incompleteness here does not erase an already established no-offset comparison.

These are conceptual examples, not simulations or reports of observed cases. “Worse” is also not yet “evil.” A grave unjustified harm, deliberate domination, blameworthiness and a person's enduring character introduce additional factual and normative judgments. This note establishes no absolute classification of people and no general threshold for the word evil.

## 5. The resulting alignment commitment

The proposed direction is: **make capability answer to affected beings' independently warranted goods and claims, and preserve the means through which mistakes about those grounds can change action.** Its justification has two connected parts: why the particular ground counts, and how actual control can respond effectively and permissibly. Neither learning, persistence, expanding options nor successful implementation supplies the first part by itself.

An alignment specification can therefore declare a set of justified strict comparisons, admitted constraints and unresolved conflicts. It need not pretend to possess a total reward. When a system chooses a dominated response under a justified choice requirement, an alignment failure is identifiable. When choices conflict on unsettled grounds, the same specification must report that limitation rather than silently count its default as ethical truth.

The accepted [formed-disposition repair application](../../../../theories/becoming/history/EVALUATIVE-GROUNDING.md) already provides a narrower practical address through responsibility for an imposed loss and a legitimately entrusted role. Under its independently defended patient, responsibility, means, permission and priority premises, acquired influence can mediate a warranted response; further learning can also suppress that response. This supplies a concrete alignment comparison without proving that every unrelated possible actor has a categorical reason. Its restricted success is retained alongside the stranger question.

## 6. What remains supplied and what would change the account

The supplied premises are patient identification and disvalue, baseline moral relevance, actor scope where claimed, legitimate means, the complete comparison, comparative dominance, and any applicable choice requirement. The argument makes these distinct; it does not reduce all of them to a preference installed in the controller. No assumed reward has been used as evidence of its own justification.

The next grounding connection is **the patient's actual burden plus eligible present control → a justified reason for an unrelated actor**. An independently defended historical or accessibility gate essential to this reason would restrict that claim. An alleged counterexample involving prohibited means, hidden costs or valid refusal would instead defeat the local comparison's conditions. A better identification of the experience could revise the patient-ground itself. These results would require different revisions.

The next practical connection is **a justified local comparison → acquired, retained and corrigible use of that same comparison under changing evidence and power**. This requires preserving patient reference, distinguishing relief from report suppression, distinguishing legitimate refusal from exclusion, and withdrawing a particular remedy when its ground or permission changes. A successful mechanism would close this interface at its tested scope; it would not prove moral truth by working.

The study advances the derivation map by separating an already defensible kind of alignment direction from stronger claims that remain to be established. Objective ethical assessment, universal actor address, reliable motivation, legitimate power and complete ranking retain separate burdens. Progress on one can be real while the others remain unfinished.

The interpretation-intersection proposal is sound at its declared relative scope. For a common comparison domain and nonempty family of strict partial relations, their intersection is irreflexive and transitive: each member excludes self-comparison, and any two shared successive comparisons compose in every member. Nonemptiness prevents vacuous universal support; it does **not** guarantee a shared strict comparison. Two interpretations ordering a pair in opposite directions already have no common strict comparison on that pair. The comparator claims neither universal agreement nor moral truth from this construction, and correctly distinguishes absence of a shared ranking from equality or permission.

Its proposed identification tests preserve patient outcome separately from actor reward, role and report. They are explicitly unrun and do not infer ethical warrant from successful behavior. Relevant refusal can change authority without eliminating patient standing; evidence of earlier relief can defeat a particular pending recommendation without eliminating every reason. These are application changes, not automatic disproofs of the patient-ground.
