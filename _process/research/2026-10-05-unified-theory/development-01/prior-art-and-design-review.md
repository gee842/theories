# Formation of effective correction through a maintained alternative

Independent local-work and primary-literature review, 5 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. Prepared for the next development of Ren's unified theory. This is a targeted audit and prospective design review, not an exhaustive novelty search or a claim that the proposed model has been implemented. The mathematical observations below are elementary comparisons.

## What would advance the local theory

The strongest available step is to join **the material construction and continuing renewal of an alternative, strategic responsiveness to credible withdrawal, and the dated resources needed to establish and transmit that alternative**. It should report which of these joins has been completed and which control primitives remain supplied.

Three propositions already exist locally and should not be repackaged as new:

1. The [continuing-participation experiment](/Users/ren/theories/experiments/continuing-participation/README.md) already checks refusal at reached information histories, distinguishes initial agreement from continued acceptance, accounts for joint ownership of compensation, and derives exact current-cash thresholds. Its 92 selected comparisons include cases where compensation works, where available cash prevents it, and where aggregate gains are insufficient. Atomic payment conditional on execution and refusal rights are supplied. No selected policy bought a diagnostic.
2. The [selection continuation](/Users/ren/theories/sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-05-selection-and-continuity-of-renewal.md) already distinguishes operating viability, transmission, invasion, and functional replacement. It derives a maintenance-copying extinction result, shows how rewarding contributors consumes shared support, and calculates replacement gaps and overlap requirements. Adding a positive imitation coefficient or a delayed transition alone would repeat existing work.
3. The [formation-coupled experiment](/Users/ren/theories/experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/gap-resolution/formation-coupled/RESULTS.md) already links complaint, costly records/inquiry, a generated response within a supplied grammar, ratification, execution, turnover, and training. Its [reopening extension](/Users/ren/theories/experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/gap-resolution/formation-coupled/REOPENING-RESULTS.md) permits continuing revision. Ratification/admission/selection rules remain supplied; the protected arm is an imposed benchmark. A funded complaint channel alone is therefore insufficiently new.

The prior [continuing-participation synthesis](/Users/ren/theories/sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-05-continuing-participation-and-funded-correction.md) explicitly recommends an earned-output-funded private backup versus an equally nominal but revocable earmark. It also says the alternative should remain usable during cooperation, and that private custody does not explain protection against confiscation. The present extension should acknowledge that exact genealogy.

## Primary sources and their boundaries

### Self-enforcing promises and lost future rents are established

Jonathan Levin, [Relational Incentive Contracts, 2003](https://web.stanford.edu/~jdlevin/Papers/RIC.pdf), analyzes relational contracts in which promised contingent compensation is sustained by the value of continued trade. The paper's model and introductory results were inspected. It distinguishes moral hazard, hidden information, and subjective evaluation, and makes self-enforcement a restriction on possible incentives. Its fixed salary is contractually enforceable and its outside opportunities are specified inputs. It does not derive every enforcement technology from physical control, and its stationary simplifications rely on its economic assumptions. Our payoff condition comparing safeguard cost with lost relational rent is a familiar incentive comparison, not a new theory of self-enforcement. A current irreversible safeguard followed by voluntary input is also distinct from a promise to safeguard after input has been delivered.

### Investment, expropriation, and limited liquidity are already dynamically coupled

Jonathan P. Thomas and Tim Worrall, [Dynamic Relational Contracts under Complete Information, 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022053118300413), with an accessible [author manuscript](https://homepages.econ.ed.ac.uk/~jpthomas/DRC.pdf), study two agents' costly joint investments, division of benefits, and opportunities for expropriation. The published abstract/introduction and author manuscript were inspected. Their dynamic efficient contracts can feature asymmetric early investment and backloading under limited liability. The model has no accumulating productive asset; investment depreciates each period. Its results are not evidence for our parameters, but rule out novelty claims based merely on coupling financing, investment, and endogenous willingness to continue. A distinct local addition would retain and depreciate a physically usable fallback, cash needed before revenues, and actual transfer to successors.

### Norms and sanctioning rules can themselves emerge in explicit models

Kenji Itao and Kunihiko Kaneko, [Self-organized institutions in evolutionary dynamical-systems game, author version 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07249v2), published under the plural title [Self-organized institutions in evolutionary dynamical-systems games](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2500960122), evolve decision functions in a resource-harvesting environment. Those functions use environmental and partner states; response to another's harvesting can degrade the common environment, generating norms and punitive responses. The full author text was inspected. This is close prior art for “responses that change their own enabling environment produce institutions.” Its represented strategy family, observation access, matching, fitness, and transmission remain specified. Our proposed two-party sequential example is simpler and cannot claim to solve institutional origins more generally. Its potential value is a transparent accounting of fallback construction, funding, strategic response, and succession in one restricted mechanism.

### Funding and auditing institutions already interact with capture incentives

Taylor A. Kessinger and Joshua B. Plotkin, [Institutions of public judgment established by social contract and taxation, author version](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11199v2), with [published article](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2506537122), analyze tax-supported reputation institutions, tax evasion, bribery, and audits. Full author text and the tax/audit sections were inspected. Their results depend on tax, audit, and strategy-update rates; a more valuable cooperative institution is not automatically protected from corrupt strategies. Detection and the removal of a corrupt official are explicit modeled mechanisms. This is direct prior art for the need to sustain the institution that sustains cooperation. It does not supply a fully endogenous origin of audit authority or bootstrap all required capacity from zero. Our model should make equally explicit what monitors, seizure constraints, and replacement powers it assumes.

### Monitoring costs change real behavior, rather than being a harmless label

Timo Goeschl and Johannes Jarke, [Second and third party punishment under costly monitoring, 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167487016000398), with an accessible [university working-paper version](https://www.wiso.uni-hamburg.de/forschung/archiv/workingpaper/wpneuesdesign/wp6.pdf), report a laboratory experiment where conditioning punishment on behavior requires an additional fee. The publisher's abstract/introduction and university manuscript abstract were inspected. Costly monitoring produces less discriminating enforcement and weaker cooperative incentives in their experiment. It does not establish the effects of a particular real-world reform. If our extension assumes perfectly observable safeguards, it should explicitly leave costly diagnosis outside its proved result rather than imply that all epistemic costs are already modeled.

### Better independent alternatives do not universally improve cooperation

Jörg Gross and Robert Böhm, [Voluntary restrictions on self-reliance increase cooperation and mitigate wealth inequality, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33122435/), study groups' choices about individual self-reliance in a public-goods setting. The indexed primary abstract and article material were inspected. Under their experimental conditions, participants sometimes voluntarily restrict self-reliance, increasing cooperation and reducing wealth inequality. This is a useful counterweight to any monotone claim that stronger outside options always improve cooperative performance. It does not justify coercively degrading someone's fallback, and the present model must separately measure welfare, participation, actual choice, and safety. The empirical result concerns a supplied laboratory choice structure, not a general verdict against autonomy.

### Persistence of harmful institutions is an explanatory target

Daron Acemoglu, [Modeling Inefficient Institutions, 2006](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11940), explains how actors with political power can prefer inefficient policies through extraction, factor-price manipulation, or political consolidation. The NBER primary abstract was inspected; detailed theorems were not independently audited. It is a direct precedent for modeling incentives to damage others' alternatives rather than assuming an institution's productive benefits select it. The present proposal's blocking/seizure option is valuable only if its cost and effect are explicitly accounted for. Giving blocking a high cost is a condition for protection, not itself an explanation for why the cost is high or why the control structure is legitimate.

## Review of the proposed strategic stage

The coordinator proposes a participant who receives benefit b from safe cooperation and b−h from harmful cooperation, an operable alternative with payoff o, and an inactive fallback o₀. In the intended strict region,

\[
o_0<b-h<o<b.
\]

The operator can implement a genuinely effective, observable safeguard costing c before the participant supplies an input. The participant then chooses the shared activity or the alternative. If withdrawing the input costs the operator rent r relative to its best feasible independent continuation, the operator prefers safeguarding to losing the participant when c<r. If the operator can instead remove the participant's alternative at current cost χ and retain the input under harmful conditions, safeguarding beats that route only when c<χ as well. This yields the familiar strict comparison c<min{r,χ}, subject to the complete game order and accounting.

The result is useful if the following issues are resolved explicitly:

**Do not disable the fallback artificially when cooperation is chosen.** If it gives additive benefit o under both cooperation and refusal, the comparison becomes b−h+o versus o. The new asset cancels, and may not change refusal at all. A clean physical story is one scarce unit of labor or input that the participant may allocate to either shared production or their own maintained backup. Both paths remain available; the same input cannot be used twice. Another valid story requires a specified substitution technology. The model must identify which it uses.

**Describe timing as a causal mechanism.** The safeguard must be completed and durable for the current production period before input delivery. An observable announcement, revocable earmark, or cost already sunk without effective protection does not establish that condition. If the operator can remove it after receiving input, that deviation belongs in the game. Giving the participant a later retaliation threat requires a separate dynamic argument.

**Rent is a counterfactual difference.** r must measure what this participant can actually cause the operator to lose relative to its best replacement or independent path, not gross output. Cheap replacement, stockpiled input, or partial production can make r much smaller. r=0 is the critical case in which the affected person lacks this particular source of influence, even though their welfare still matters.

**Seizure is not just an extra fee.** Does seizure cost χ destroy capital, transfer it, suppress access temporarily, or change custody permanently? Who receives any seized value? Can the operator first accept maintenance funding and then seize? The comparison c<χ is conditional on the chosen answers. A cost paid from someone else's account is not an operator incentive cost of the same magnitude.

**Strict inequalities avoid hiding selection.** At o=b−h, o=b, c=r, or c=χ, several best responses may coexist. Report the full set or an explicit tie rule. A single selected path at equality is not robustness. If backup upkeep modifies the participant's net b or the operator's net r, use the updated quantities rather than inherited nominal payoffs.

**Funding feasibility is not the choice to form.** A physically reachable backup need not be privately worthwhile to build. Compare complete continuation values of building and not building, including setup, maturation, maintenance, foregone output, and the operator's response. A participant-only optimizer does not establish joint agreement; an omniscient social optimizer does not establish formation under the declared strategic decision rights.

**Succession must include decisions and access.** A transferred object whose successor cannot operate it is not a functioning alternative. Conversely, an inherited rule with no funded object cannot discipline an operator. Charge training and custody transfer where required. If the original founder is allowed to bind every successor's expenditure forever, describe that as a commitment assumption.

## A discriminating finite study

Freeze the following contrasts before inspecting the final parameter grid. Keep the production technology, preferences, and accounting identical wherever possible; interventions that necessarily change resource availability must report that incidence.

| Contrast | What it identifies | Necessary counterweight |
|---|---|---|
| Materially usable alternative versus nominal revocable earmark | Actual execution and control | Charge equal nominal resources, record any seized value |
| Prebuilt alternative versus earned-resource formation | Installation/transition obstruction | Same mature capability and maintenance costs |
| Sufficient versus insufficient current cash at matched future value | Dated liquidity | Include an explicit priced-credit relaxation if claiming broader financing relevance |
| High versus low operator rent loss | Dependence and replaceability | Keep harm and participant preferences fixed |
| High versus low seizure cost | Vulnerability of the alternative | Report actual seizures and who pays |
| Same capital with and without successor operating capability | Transmission of effective capacity | Charge training and prevent free inherited knowledge |
| Exclusive input allocation versus additive backup | Why the fallback changes refusal | Demonstrate cancellation in the additive control |
| Useful alternative b−h<o<b versus o≥b | Protection versus exit | Do not treat safe independent exit as a failed ethical outcome |

The crucial success is a joined witness: a participant chooses and can afford to build an alternative from actual resources, construction completes before an irreversible loss, operator behavior then changes for the predicted strategic reason, and successor decisions maintain the implemented capacity. The crucial failure witnesses are: a good mature equilibrium unreachable from the current cash state; a functioning backup that does not make the operator depend on the participant; seizure cheaper than protection; and a nominal inheritance that loses operation.

The program should report at least separate series for productive output, each person's consumption/utility, realized harm, usable capital, liquid cash, outside performance, and input allocation. A rise in cooperation can conceal worse fallback or higher harm. A fall in cooperation can represent a person successfully avoiding exploitation. Do not combine these into an unexplained wisdom score.

## Strongest warranted advance and remaining frontier

If those checks succeed, the warranted conclusion is that **effective responsiveness can be caused by a materially built and maintained alternative when the affected participant controls a consequential input and the operator cannot cheaply neutralize that alternative**. This explains one route from productive resources to a capacity to refuse and alter another actor's behavior. It is not a universal movement toward wisdom, an origin of every property right, or an ethical justification for including only economically indispensable people.

The result would also sharpen the next frontier: how can beings whose loss is inexpensive to dominant actors gain durable standing without making their protection depend solely on market indispensability? Coalition formation, reciprocal commitments, public guarantees, representation, or norms could supply mechanisms, but each brings formation, financing, control, and capture questions of its own. That frontier should remain visible, rather than being hidden inside a benevolent planner or an unexplained enforcement parameter.
