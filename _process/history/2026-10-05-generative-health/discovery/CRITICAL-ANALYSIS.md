# Creating functions, not only preserving a repertoire

Critical research memo, 5 October 2026. This develops the unresolved discovery question in [ANSWER.md](../ANSWER.md). The examples and proposed test are conceptual constructions; no new experiment or external empirical validation has been performed.

## The answer needs a generative mechanism

The current core can explain important conditions of invention: inquiry needs support, experimental consequences need to reach a revisable understanding, and an alternative needs a feasible route into operation. It does not yet explain which new functions will arise or why this process produces them. “Maintain the capacity to reorganize” becomes circular if that capacity means whatever would have produced the successful invention.

A useful extension is therefore a causal hypothesis:

> Supported differences in experience and practice can produce new distinctions, constructions, and proposed purposes. Consequential trials can establish previously unavailable relations between those constructions and their surroundings. Continuation becomes generative when it carries forward the means of forming and testing further relations, including means usable by successors and differently situated participants.

This is more than maintenance but less than a universal invention theorem. Variation, construction, interpretation, authorization, and establishment need separately identifiable mechanisms. Their connection across organisms, technologies, inquiry, and institutions is a proposed comparison, not an established equivalence.

## What the existing models actually establish

The three local studies isolate genuine components while leaving the present question open:

| Study | Demonstrated object | Supplied boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Situated discovery](../../../../experiments/situated-discovery/README.md) | A learner generates contextual distinctions from records, tests policies on fresh randomized cases, and encounters separate admission and deployment gates. | A finite predicate grammar, two actions, common purpose, and known attribute vocabulary. |
| [Prospective revision](../../../../experiments/prospective-revision/README.md) | Paid preservation can assist a later question; fresh validation can outperform archive-first investigation; anomalies need not precede useful inquiry. | Supplied later questions, response efficacy, and a finite workflow family. |
| [Revisable inheritance](../../../../experiments/revisable-inheritance/README.md) | A summary sufficient under one family can erase distinctions needed after model expansion; readiness and silence also transmit information. | The expanded family is supplied at one handoff, with common losses and an established communication protocol. |

These are not experiments creating new action ontologies, new purposes, or a self-maintaining discovery institution across repeated succession. In situated discovery, linked records and completed evidence persist without further payment. In revisable inheritance, the protocol is optimized rather than learned or institutionally established. Those simplifications cannot be silently carried into an explanation of how discovery reproduces its own conditions.

## Four meanings of novelty

An unfamiliar answer, a new representation, a new effective action, and a newly valued purpose are different achievements.

1. **Learning:** a previously unknown relationship becomes known within the learner's current vocabulary.
2. **Representational invention:** the learner creates a distinction or explanatory organization absent from its active model.
3. **Functional invention:** a constructed artifact or practice makes a consequential response executable that was unavailable through the actual prior interface and budget.
4. **Formation of purposes:** participants come to value, pursue, or contest a way of being that was absent from the previous evaluation.

A new classifier may demonstrate the first two without the third. A new tool can demonstrate the third while serving an unchanged purpose. A community finding meaning in a practice concerns the fourth; lowering its old loss function does not establish that change in meaning.

Fixed physical laws do not make functional invention unreal. A device can create a usable intervention even if an analyst could have encoded every possible device in an enormous initial program space. Conversely, using an unbounded grammar does not explain why any generated program acquires a function. The explanatory question is how construction changes actual relations among materials, agents, environments, and purposes.

For a bounded study, declare the prior repertoire, interface, resource limits, and evaluation questions. A candidate function must causally extend what can be accomplished under those limits. This is relative novelty, but it is inspectable. Changing the comparison boundary after seeing the invention would make the claim unfalsifiable.

## Counterexamples the extension must survive

**Novelty theater.** A generator creates many new strings, forms, or institutional names without changing anyone's effective responses. Repertoire size and syntactic distance are insufficient measures of functional creation.

**Inventive domination.** An institution repeatedly creates new surveillance and extraction tools, funds research, and replaces its researchers. Its discovery machinery persists while affected people's ability to refuse or develop alternatives disappears. Invention plus renewal is not shared health.

**The museum of first success.** A successor inherits a brilliant tool and a complete account of its successful deployment. It lacks experimental access, calibration skills, funding, or authority to challenge the tool. Inherited performance survives; further discovery does not.

**The archive that cannot answer.** If two histories map to the same retained summary but a later question distinguishes them, no decoder using only that summary can recover the distinction. A more creative successor does not reverse erased information. Full recording also has costs and cannot preserve measurements never made. Fresh inquiry may supply an alternative route, but cannot recover every historical fact.

**The benevolent evaluation monopoly.** All proposals are judged solely by their immediate contribution to the incumbent's score. A proposed new purpose is rejected because it is not an improvement by that score. Giving the same evaluator a larger search space does not remove this exclusion.

**Protected waste.** Insulation from immediate returns funds endless unproductive trials, while others pay their costs. The existing prospective study already rules out a universal archive quota or universal superiority of inquiry. Protection must enable consequential assessment and possible suspension, not make a favored institution permanently unanswerable.

## A mechanism worth testing across domains

The proposed common mechanism has several distinguishable transitions: supported variation produces encounters; encounters can expose inadequacies or new possibilities; participants form distinctions and constructions; trials connect them to consequences; functioning constructions acquire support; successors receive means to repeat and challenge that process.

The distinctive object is the **reproduction of discoverability**, not just reproduction of the discovery. Its carriers include instruments, materials, records, interpretive skills, opportunities to encounter unfamiliar situations, authority to propose questions, and resources to run consequential tests. Which carriers matter is an empirical question. A fixed universal inventory would repeat the compression problem at another level.

Partial independence can matter because a practice that must first prove its worth in the incumbent's vocabulary may never receive its first trial. Yet it needs real contact with consequences and an eventual route into operation. These are testable tensions, not a prescription for maximal independence, maximal novelty, or perpetual activity.

Successors need not inherit every theory or preserve the predecessor's identity. They need workable access to inquiry and the ability to challenge what was inherited. Plural futures additionally require that this access not belong exclusively to the current winner. Those standing requirements are normative commitments; the causal loop does not derive them.

## What an honest next test would show

Use a construction environment with generic materials and tools rather than a short menu of finished solutions. Let participants encounter a difficulty for which the initially deployed repertoire has a demonstrated limitation. Candidate artifacts or practices must be constructed by the learner, paid for, and validated on fresh cases. Retain matched baselines that receive equal total resources and initial competence but differ in how inquiry, evidence, and proposal rights survive turnover.

The decisive test comes **after succession**. Remove the predecessor and introduce a materially different challenge requiring another construction or revision. Compare:

- inheritance of the successful artifact or policy;
- inheritance of artifact plus records;
- inheritance of usable experimental tools, skills, records, resources, and proposal authority;
- loss or capture of one of those supports.

Track first and second functional gains, discovery and establishment times, actual expenditure, failed trials, service interruptions, affected-party burdens, and the successor's ability to reopen a rejected question. Do not count copying the first solution as renewed discovery. Where readiness, silence, behavior, or selective archive absence reveal information, successors must be allowed to use those channels; otherwise an inheritance effect may be manufactured by artificial ignorance.

Vary support independently of candidate quality where feasible. A no-authority arm whose code forbids deployment demonstrates the specified gate, not a discovery about endogenous institutional capture. To investigate capture, implement or observe a process that allocates authority and then test its consequences. Likewise, do not advertise an underfunded arm's arithmetic inability to finish a trial as emergent evidence that the inquiry mechanism failed.

For plural futures, allow at least two differently situated parties to propose independently meaningful questions. Assess whether each has an executable inquiry route and retain separate burdens and refusals. Do not reduce the outcome to how many proposals the strongest party accepts. Incompatible requests may require acknowledged negotiation; no mechanism guarantees all imaginable futures simultaneously.

A human or organizational study is needed to substantiate formation of new purposes. A simulation with fixed rewards can test new action construction but cannot establish genuine evaluative change merely by renaming a reward coordinate. A defensible study can preregister procedures for documenting and contesting new aims without pretending to enumerate their content beforehand.

## What success would and would not establish

Success would show that identified supports causally enable new effective constructions and preserve that ability through a specified succession and changed challenge. It would advance the current work beyond selecting a policy in a supplied finite grammar.

It would not certify creativity under unrestricted novelty, legitimate every newly adopted purpose, or prove the same mechanism across all domains. Failure should distinguish absence of a useful construction from failed discovery, lost evidence, blocked authorization, and unaffordable establishment. These are different explanations with different remedies.

The strongest revision to the core is therefore: **further becoming requires not merely workable alternatives, but maintained ways for encounters, constructions, and participants to change what counts as an alternative—and ways for successors to continue that work without inheriting compulsory allegiance to its earlier results.** The causal hypothesis is now concrete enough to test; its universal and normative reach remains limited.
