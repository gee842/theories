# Discovery that preserves the conditions of further discovery

Prepared 5 October 2026. Focused primary-source research and project-precedence audit. Five sources were examined beyond their abstracts, including the mechanisms and qualifications identified below. This memo proposes a sharper causal hypothesis; it does not establish a universal novelty theorem.

## Main finding

**Current performance does not determine future generative capacity.** Two arrangements can do the same thing now while enabling different subsequent changes. An intermediate can even perform worse now while making a later capability reachable. Consequently, continually selecting the presently best arrangement can destroy the routes by which a better arrangement would arise.

The previous answer's supported variation, consequential correction, and lasting support needs a temporal distinction: **retaining an intermediate for further exploration is different from certifying it for operational adoption**. A candidate can deserve neither current deployment nor immediate destruction. Supporting its continued investigation requires actual resources and bounded exposure, not presumed future success.

## Primary evidence and limits

### 1. March: assimilation can consume the differences from which learning proceeds

James G. March (1991), *Exploration and Exploitation in Organizational Learning*, Organization Science 2:71–87. [Full paper](https://hiveresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/12/march_1991_exploration_and_exploitation_in_organizational_learning.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2.1.71). Read especially pp. 74–81.

In the mutual-learning model, individuals adopt an organizational code while the code learns from better-informed individuals. Rapid assimilation improves individuals initially but extinguishes differences available for collective learning. Slower assimilation, mixed learning rates, or moderate recruitment can preserve variation; excessive turnover can damage learning. This is a mechanism for retaining discovery sources rather than maximizing novelty. Crucially, the model supplies reality-based rankings of individuals: it does not explain how an institution reliably identifies superior knowledge. The finite belief dimensions, simplified learning rules, and absence of direct investigation limit transfer to real institutions. No universally optimal exploration fraction follows.

### 2. Wagner: preserve a function while changing the structures from which novelty is reachable

Andreas Wagner (2008), *Robustness and evolvability: a paradox resolved*, Proceedings of the Royal Society B 275:91–100. [Full paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2562401/). Read results on neutral networks and finite populations, §2(f), and discussion.

In computational RNA folding, connected sequences can produce the same structure. A population can move through this neutral network while preserving that phenotype, encountering different neighboring phenotypes along the way. This provides a concrete route from present robustness to future variation. The relationship depends on level: robustness of an individual sequence can reduce immediate phenotypic novelty, while robustness of a phenotype can increase population access to variation. Finding an arbitrary specified target is a different measure and was not strongly predicted by starting robustness. These results concern RNA secondary-structure models; accessible variation is not automatically useful innovation or organismal health.

### 3. Lenski and colleagues: later functions can recruit earlier components and temporarily inferior intermediates

Lenski, R. E., Ofria, C., Pennock, R. T. and Adami, C. (2003), *The evolutionary origin of complex features*, Nature 423:139–144. [Author-hosted full paper](https://cse.msu.edu/~ofria/pubs/2003LenskiEtAl.pdf). Read lineage reconstruction, mutation reversals, treatment controls, and discussion.

Digital organisms assembled complex logic functions from instructions previously involved in simpler functions and replication. Mutation-reversal interventions showed that some immediately harmful precursors were necessary for the later function in those particular genetic backgrounds. Simpler rewarded functions supplied useful intermediate organization, although no one intermediate function was essential across all successful lineages. This demonstrates context-dependent reuse and nonlinear interactions rather than foresighted selection. The experiment used supplied instructions, computational rules, resources, and rewarded functions. It establishes pathways to specified forms of complexity in that environment, not unrestricted functional invention, a guarantee of improvement, or a reason to preserve every harmful variant.

### 4. Leon and colleagues: an innovation must survive competition long enough to be refined

Leon, D., D'Alton, S., Quandt, E. M. and Barrick, J. E. (2018), *Innovation in an E. coli evolution experiment is contingent on maintaining adaptive potential until competition subsides*, PLOS Genetics 14:e1007348. [Full paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1007348). Read knock-in assays, phylogenetic mapping, population-context analysis, and discussion.

The researchers reconstructed an initial citrate-utilization mutation in historical genetic backgrounds and measured changing fitness effects. Some backgrounds made this rudimentary innovation beneficial, others strongly harmful. They combined these measurements with models of competing beneficial mutations to infer when the initial variant could persist until a refining mutation arose. An improvement over one's own parent could still lose to competitors improving the existing niche. Genetic effects were experimentally assayed; the historical competition explanation partly relies on model comparison. This case supports separating appearance, persistence, and refinement, without proving that reduced competition universally promotes innovation.

### 5. Taylor: search, new functions, and revised descriptions must be distinguished

Tim Taylor (2019), *Evolutionary Innovations and Where to Find Them: Routes to Open-Ended Evolution in Natural and Artificial Systems*. [Full preprint, version 4](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.01883v4). Read §§2.1.1, 3, 4.2–4.3, and 5.

Taylor separates exploration within a described phenotype space from novelties requiring expansion of the model or its concepts. Proposed mechanisms include exploiting additional properties of existing components and compositions whose behavior is not additive. Fixed genetic descriptions can still yield unexpected functions through their physical realization and context. In this account, the physical possibilities need not have expanded; the description can have omitted them. The framework distinguishes phenotype generation, evaluation, and reproduction, including environmentally mediated changes to each. Its routes beyond exploration are conceptual proposals, not sufficient conditions or proofs that any computational system achieves indefinitely expanding novelty.

## What this project has already established

The earlier [answer](../ANSWER.md) explicitly assumes a specified workable successor in its paid-establishment theorem. That boundary is useful but supplies neither its discovery nor the survival of uncertain intermediates.

The [situated-discovery experiment](../../../../experiments/situated-discovery/README.md) already generates candidates from records without receiving the hidden rule, pays for evidence, validates on fresh randomized cases, and separates evidence standing from implementation authority. Its [protocol](../../../../experiments/situated-discovery/PROTOCOL.md) supplies 73 predicates, two actions, and a fixed objective. It does not study a sequence of intermediates changing what can subsequently be generated. Discovery and paid validation must not be presented as additions invented here.

The [prospective-revision experiment](../../../../experiments/prospective-revision/README.md) already distinguishes routine anomalies from future usefulness, pays for preservation before a question arrives, and shows that direct fresh validation can outperform archive-first inquiry. It uses supplied worlds, questions, candidates, and response efficacy. Thus “investigate before demonstrated returns” and “retain what current questions omit” also have project precedents.

The opening account of [Keep the Possibility Space Open](../../../../theories/keep-the-possibility-space-open/README.md) already distinguishes generativity from option maximization and names the danger of competitive success disabling correction. The present contribution is a more specific proposed mechanism and test, not a replacement principle.

## Sharpened hypothesis and discriminating test

**Generative capacity depends partly on the supported paths between arrangements, not only their current outputs.** The hypothesis is that sustainable discovery benefits from preserving some structurally different realizations and some bounded investigations of imperfect intermediates, while retaining rigorous standards for operational use. Reuse and composition can then make new capabilities accessible. Which variants deserve support cannot generally be read off present performance alone.

A useful next experiment would compare two candidate-retention rules under matched resource budgets and identical generation operators:

1. Retain only the best current performer.
2. Retain a limited set of currently equivalent or inferior candidates with different structures, paying their storage, experimentation, and maintenance costs.

Both arms must generate subsequent candidates from what they actually retained. Test discovery, independent validation, deployment costs, and the capacity to repeat discovery after turnover. Include worlds where intermediates enable later useful compositions, worlds where they lead nowhere, and worlds where their costs destroy the funding needed to finish. Track changing reachability explicitly; a larger archive is not by itself a result.

This would add something distinct from the existing supplied-predicate experiments. It would still demonstrate discovery within the implemented generative rules. To claim a new function, define the function independently of a convenient label and show the causal behavior; to claim an expanded representation, specify what the former description could not express. No finite run can certify endless novelty, and discovery that persists only through unmodeled outside support has not explained the maintenance of its own conditions.
