The strongest answer supported by this investigation is **maintained capacity for viable reorganization**. A process can carry its functioning into a changing future when its enabling conditions remain available and it can establish altered arrangements before indispensable conditions fail. Present activity can renew those conditions, obtain continuing provision, transfer functions to different carriers, or remove a dependence. The arrangements themselves need not remain the same.

The generative part is the production and renewal of this capacity to reorganize. It includes supporting alternatives, letting consequences affect their development, and making workable alternatives executable. For shared health, this must be evaluated against the futures and burdens of affected beings together. An organization can reproduce and revise itself while systematically closing other people's futures.

Some necessities below follow from explicit premises about loss, information, and transition costs. The establishment model has an exact proof. The broader connection among organisms, inquiry, institutions, and other processes is a proposed causal synthesis; the research does not prove their equivalence. The judgment that affected beings should retain worthwhile futures remains a normative commitment.

## What the proposed invariant preserves

The underlying relationship is that **activity helps make the conditions for subsequent activity available, including conditions for changing how that availability is achieved**. Continuity can lie in this relationship while its particular realizations change. Repair, substitution, succession, migration, and release from a dependence are different mechanisms with different costs. Calling all of them renewal would conceal what an explanation needs to identify.

There are two meanings of invariance here. A faithful change of description preserves what the same situation can support. This is already proved under explicit assumptions in the [root foundation](../2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/ROOT-FOUNDATION.md). Actual activity changes the situation and therefore changes what is possible. Generative continuation concerns how those changes preserve or recreate adequate capacity to respond and reorganize. It is not a claim that an identical possibility set, identity, or numerical quantity remains constant through time.

A particular capacity can disappear when it is no longer needed. A successful transition can end an institution and give its participants independent means of continuing. Conversely, relabeling successors does not erase losses suffered by particular people. The continuity being claimed must name its bearers and protected consequences.

The word universal is strongest when applied to conditional constraints. If an indispensable support suffers losses beyond its finite reserve, provision or change of dependence is necessary. If a response requires distinguishing two circumstances, accessible information or an action adequate to both is necessary. If transformation has costs and takes time, a feasible path must pay those costs before obligations fail. No single organizational design follows from these necessities.

## The organization that makes transformation possible

The simplest enabling structure has a continuing return from activity to the conditions enabling activity. A biological precedent is organizational closure: constraints participate in maintaining one another even as their particular realizations vary. This is a theoretical account of biological organization, not proof that every causal cycle is adequate or that all healthy systems are internally self-sufficient. [Montévil and Mossio 2015](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2015-mm-organisation-closure-constraints/).

Continuation alone does not establish responsiveness. Di Paolo's account of adaptivity adds regulation of tendencies toward loss of viability and allows the regulators themselves to change. It provides a precursor for distinguishing ongoing operation from the condition of the processes preserving it. [Di Paolo 2005](https://ezequieldipaolo.net/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/autopoiesis_teleology_2005.pdf).

A useful proposed mechanism is a regulator materially supported by production but partly decoupled from its immediate dynamics. It can change operating regimes while remaining functionally connected to what supports it. Bich and colleagues develop this biological architecture; its transfer to social or epistemic systems is an inference to investigate. [Bich and colleagues 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-015-9497-8).

These contributions suggest a causal hypothesis: **variation needs supported room to occur, consequential correction, and a way for workable changes to acquire lasting support**. Excessive dependence on an incumbent can prevent alternatives from developing. Complete separation can leave alternatives unable to sustain themselves or affect the functioning process. Finding an alternative is also different from financing, authorizing, learning, and installing it.

This is not a demand for constant experimentation. Relevant change can be rare, and dormancy can preserve potential. Nor is every variation helpful. The organization and variation literature treats stabilization and functional change as interacting questions rather than equating novelty with improvement. [Montévil and colleagues 2016](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2016-mmp-theoretical-principles-variation/).

The supporting capacities of inquiry, regulation, and succession belong inside the causal account. Their operators can leave, evidence can become unusable, and access can be withdrawn. Their persistence must be supplied by the actual network and its stated outside inputs. Introducing a permanent, perfectly informed supervisor would leave the central problem unanswered.

## Why perpetual operation can prevent a successor

The investigation derives a precise separation between current viability and the ability to establish another arrangement. This goes beyond saying that a handoff must be feasible: it calculates a feasibility boundary in a declared model.

An incumbent provides a required service while functioning capacity `c` remains at least `c_*`. Effort can maintain it or build a successor. Assume

\[
\dot c=\alpha u-\delta c,\qquad
\dot x=\beta(1-u),\qquad 0\le u\le1.
\]

Here `u` is the maintenance fraction, `alpha` is renewal productivity, `delta` is depreciation, `x` is successor progress, and `beta` is construction productivity. Initially `c=c_0 >= c_*` and `x=0`. A successor becomes functional at `x=K`. The successor is already specified and assumed workable: this model establishes access to its installation, not the discovery of a novel form. Its readiness, including required permission and commissioning, is assumed to be captured correctly by `K`.

Define

\[
p=\frac{\delta c_*}{\alpha}\le1,
\qquad t_0=\frac{1}{\delta}\log\frac{c_0}{c_*}.
\]

The incumbent can be kept operating indefinitely. Yet the maximum successor progress achievable by deadline `T`, while preserving service, is exactly

\[
X_{\max}(T)=\beta\left[\min(T,t_0)+(1-p)\max(T-t_0,0)\right].
\]

The successor can be ready in time if and only if `K <= X_max(T)`. The [mathematical analysis](MATHEMATICAL-ANALYSIS.md) gives the proof and earliest completion time. Construction can be paid by an inherited usable reserve, represented by `t_0`, and an ongoing margin after maintenance, represented by `1-p`.

If `c_0=c_*` and `p=1`, every unit of effort is needed to sustain the incumbent. It can operate forever, but no positive successor project can ever be completed. This is a counterexample to treating survival as sufficient evidence of generative capacity. It requires neither malicious leadership nor poor information.

In normalized units with `delta=c_*=beta=1`, a successor needing `K=2` gives these cases:

| Renewal productivity and initial support | Current operation | Successor establishment |
|---|---|---|
| `alpha=1`, `c_0=1` | Can continue indefinitely | Impossible for every positive project size |
| `alpha=2`, `c_0=1` | Can continue indefinitely | Earliest completion at time 4 |
| `alpha=2`, `c_0=e` | Can continue indefinitely | Earliest completion at time 3 |
| `alpha=1`, `c_0=e` | Can continue indefinitely | Can build at most 1 unit, however long it waits |

These are model consequences, not measured social facts. The model assumes known conditions and construction that neither fails nor decays. Its optimal construction schedule spends the initial buffer first and then maintains the incumbent at its floor. That is optimal only under those assumptions; it is not advice to eliminate reserves. Shocks, construction failures, learning effects, or a protected reserve margin change the problem.

Independent finite-control enumeration reproduced the frontier in 96 exact rational comparisons. A further 1,000 admissible sampled schedules respected its upper bound. Those checks support the calculation; the proof establishes optimality within the model. See [verification results](VERIFICATION.json).

The general implication is concrete: **the resources that make today's arrangement viable may leave no capacity to make tomorrow's arrangement possible**. A proposed successor needs a paid transition path, not just desirable properties after installation. Repeated transformations cannot reuse a reserve already spent; the next starting capacities must actually be delivered.

## How this develops the existing foundations

This investigation adds a test of access to changed forms. It does not rediscover the project's existing support, invariance, or switching results.

| Existing result or perspective | What the present inquiry asks of it |
|---|---|
| Continued realizability | Do actual responses preserve or establish the capacity needed for later responses? |
| Renewal under vulnerability | Can support be renewed, replaced, or made unnecessary before the dependent function fails? |
| Selection of practices | Does actual selection retain the means of changing practice, or merely an incumbent that reproduces itself? |
| Informed correction | Are information and execution available soon enough, and are their own implementations replaceable? |
| Switching between operating regimes | Can transition costs and the immature phase be paid, and what capacities arrive at the destination? |
| Keeping possibility open | Which responses remain executable for which participants, rather than merely imaginable or available to someone else? |

The common structure is a candidate explanation across domains. A biological regulator, a scientific method, and an institutional amendment process have not thereby been shown to be mathematically equivalent. A useful mapping must identify what each preserves and which extra mechanisms and commitments it introduces.

## Shared health requires joint feasibility and standing

A process can reproduce local power by consuming shared support or excluding others from correction. Hence both the boundary of the account and the distribution of executable capabilities matter.

Suppose two parties each require one unit of an indispensable resource every period, but only one unit exists and neither has reserves. Each party has a feasible continuation considered alone. Their continuations are not jointly feasible. Shared health cannot be established by combining incompatible individual certificates.

Access matters too. A reform technically available only to an incumbent is not automatically an effective capability of those subject to it. The actual means to initiate, contest, refuse, fund, and execute change belong in the model. The existence of a good policy establishes a possibility; a mechanism that installs and retains it establishes actual behavior.

This distinguishes local adaptive persistence from shared generative health. The former can be described causally. Calling the latter desirable requires judgments about whose lives, capacities, burdens, and standing matter. Physics does not choose those commitments. Once commitments are explicit, causal analysis can show whether they are jointly supportable and where claimed benefits depend on omitted losses.

This also prevents a permanent-incumbent bias. Resilience research separates persistence within a regime from transformability when that regime becomes untenable, and recognizes that undesirable arrangements can be resilient. [Walker and colleagues 2004](https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol9/iss2/art5/main.html). A worthwhile continuation may require ending the current organization while protecting particular people and commitments during the transition.

## What the answer rules out

The [counterexample analysis](COUNTEREXAMPLES.md) tests the candidate against dormancy, durable supports, adaptive domination, destructive novelty, blocked succession, and legitimate endings. Four restrictions are especially consequential.

First, more options is not a universal objective. A commitment can close options and create capabilities. Many destinations reachable now also need not provide a policy that keeps appropriate responses available until the relevant information arrives.

Second, local success cannot establish indefinite renewal. For any finite observation period, a sufficiently large hidden external reserve can reproduce the same local performance as recurrent supply. Continued provision needs evidence about the mechanism beyond observed output.

Third, feasible alternatives are weaker than actual corrigibility. Selection or authority can prevent every feasible corrective action from being implemented. The machinery selecting, funding, and transmitting practices is part of the problem.

Fourth, no result here guarantees response to unrestricted novelty. A finite menu of successor forms can demonstrate transformability within that menu. Creating new functions, observations, or aims exceeds it. Supported experimentation and revisable representations are a proposed architecture for that wider task, not a certificate against every unknown future.

## The answer and its empirical test

The causal answer is **timely provision together with executable changes in how dependence is organized**. Where choosing an adequate change requires distinguishing circumstances, the process needs access to the relevant information; where changes incur costs, they need resources and time; where collective action is required, they need actual participation and authority. Fixed robust or scheduled responses can suffice without new inquiry in other cases. The means of making required changes must themselves be supported and replaceable. Calling the resulting continuation healthy additionally requires joint, justified commitments about affected futures.

The phrase renewal of the capacity to become remains useful as a short name. Its explanatory content now lies in distinguishable mechanisms: sources and losses, information and response, paid establishment, actual selection, and joint consequences. This is a relation among processes, not a new inventory of universally fundamental substances.

A useful empirical test would measure maintenance productivity and establishment costs independently, then predict an untested transition deadline. A broader comparison would match present performance and exposure while varying support for correction and succession, then test another disturbance after the first transition. Recurring performance after turnover would be stronger evidence than a single rescue. External subsidy, inherited reserve, and changes in who bears costs must remain visible.

The investigation establishes a conditional mathematical boundary and sharpens a cross-domain causal hypothesis. It does not establish a universal ethical theorem, a scalar health measure, or a completed theory of unrestricted novelty. Source claims and their status are recorded in the [primary evidence memo](RESEARCH.md); detailed proofs and internal checks remain separately inspectable.
