# Conceptual review of revisable concern

5 October 2026. Independent review of `THEORY.md` and `START-HERE.md`, checked against `conceptual-evidence.md` and the definitions and scope results in `scope-math.md`. No root or canonical files edited.

## Overall judgment

The chapter makes a defensible advance: a stated reason can acquire an operative domain beyond its formerly admitted cases through a specified, inspectable transfer procedure. This is genuine **procedural scope revision** in the constructed system. It is not evidence that a human acquired a new intrinsic concern, that the procedure discovered the correct moral features, or that graph consistency establishes ethical truth. The main text generally preserves those boundaries.

The traceable challenge is the appropriate conceptual center. It exposes a conflict between an acknowledged reason, a defended relevance commitment, and a particular exclusion. Its three responses are accountable ways of addressing that conflict, rather than three equally justified moral conclusions. The text explicitly permits refusal and evasion as causal possibilities without misclassifying them as successful repair.

No blocking conceptual error found. The three precision edits below would make the account harder to misread. The third especially prevents the summary of warrant from collapsing the distinction developed in the mathematics.

## Recommended precision edits

1. **Name the finite result as admitting an existing reason for a new case.** THEORY §4 currently says “An explicit finite procedure for admitting a new reason.” The recurrence does not generate a new kind or ground of reason. Suggested heading: **“An explicit finite procedure for admitting a reason for a new case.”** The section body and §11 already state the narrower result correctly.

2. **Keep the test of expansion conditional on retained premises.** The first test in THEORY §13 currently says that a warranted matched case should change admitted scope. The protocol also permits openly revising or retracting the previous commitment, although openness does not make that revision justified. Suggested replacement: **“If the positive anchor and consistency commitment are retained, a warranted matched case should change the rule's admitted scope; a supported disanalogy should preserve a justified distinction.”** Otherwise the test can inadvertently count every nonexpansion as a failure of the protocol rather than distinguishing expansion, justified difference, candid retraction, and evasion.

3. **State the fallibility of examination explicitly in §3.** Its warrant bullet describes relations and reasons surviving a declared examination. A bad audit can certify an error; formal soundness cannot come from the word “declared.” Suggested sentence after the three bullets: **“Passing a declared examination is fallible. The formal specimen assumes sound certificates; that assumption does not turn procedural acceptance into a proof of ethical truth.”** In the mathematical idealization W represents warranted equivalences; K is what the process has audited and retained, and may be mistaken outside the sound-certificate case. D concerns operative delivery. The closing paragraph's “validity” should continue to refer to the defended reason, not merely to the institution's positive verdict.

These are local clarifications, not requests to add another disclaimer section. START-HERE is sufficiently qualified as written. If the same precision is desired there, “to resolve that conflict under its stated consistency commitment” can qualify “it must”; the following sentence already recognizes refusal, so this is optional.

## Requested distinctions: review results

| Issue | Finding |
|---|---|
| Consistency versus truth | THEORY §1 correctly gives the uniformly zero evaluator as a counterexample to favorable direction from consistency alone. §§2 and 4 expose relevance and anchor assumptions; conflicting anchors are not resolved by averaging. The suggested audit-fallibility sentence would complete the distinction in §3. |
| Direct intake | THEORY §2 explicitly rejects resemblance, self-advocacy, usefulness, or an existing beneficiary's approval as conditions of standing. New testimony, representation, investigation, or a new stake can introduce another starting reason. This is an architectural requirement, not a theorem that the current finite recurrence invents a new moral concept. §11 correctly retains that limit. |
| Contextual reasons versus total priority | Nodes are cases in context for a named reason. Equal reason recognition permits different all-things-considered actions; special duties, needs, preferences, uncertainty, and feasibility remain distinguishable. The text does not require emotional homogenization or identical treatment of whole people. |
| W, K, and D | Their functions remain distinct in the main argument and appendix. W supplies the idealized warrant relation, K retained support and its provenance, D what can currently enter an operative update. They must not be read as three names for one graph. |
| Blockade versus retained knowledge | THEORY §§3, 7, and 8 correctly prohibit deriving lost standing or lost knowledge from a communication cut. Previously retained reasons and surviving public records change the initial state. Erasure, retrieval suppression, revoked authority, or implementation failure need their own mechanisms and costs. |
| Retraction | Defeating a certificate calls for invalidation of dependent derivations and examination of independent support. Merely blocking a valid channel does not. Withdrawing one derivation is not a verdict that the recipient lacks standing. The leakage counterexample explains why indiscriminate forgetting cannot do this work. |
| Paid pathways | Costs represent operations and institutional or technical means needed for delivery, audit, retention, authority, and maintenance. They do not measure a person's worth or buy ethical truth. Construction and upkeep are explicitly distinct from an adversary's disabling cost. The finite example states its cost-resistance relation as a supplied technology. |
| Actual scope revision | The Boolean update changes the set of cases for which the named reason is procedurally admitted. The incremental procedure can admit new certified relations and thereby remove an operative exclusion. This is more than revising efficacy while retaining the same gate. It remains an implemented conditional transfer policy, rather than demonstrated spontaneous acquisition of a policy or alteration of ultimate ethical grounds. |
| Novel cases | “Appropriate novel cases” is defensible as new instances meeting the declared reason and certificate criteria. The graph results establish finite closure, not unlimited out-of-distribution generalization or discovery of previously unrepresented relevant properties. §11 already makes that boundary explicit. |
| Motivation versus conduct | THEORY §§1, 5, and 11 distinguish information, procedural admission, emotional uptake, action, and institutional constraint. An official's compelled action is not automatically acquired direct concern. |
| Strategic preservation versus justification | The controller model asks whether claims retain effective routes against a specified profitable attack. Neither an expensive-to-suppress claim nor a robust institution thereby becomes just. The finite payoff, affordability, timing, and attack-menu qualifications preserve the distinction. |
| Universal direction | The text supplies conditional mechanisms and countertrajectories, not a law that reality tends toward inclusive concern. START-HERE states the missing premise explicitly. No universal directional conclusion is smuggled into closure or convergence. |

## What the advance does and does not close

This continuation closes a particular implementation gap: given an acknowledged positive reason, a defended relevance specification, and a commitment to apply it consistently, it states how a previously excluded case enters the operative rule and how the resulting support can persist, be corrected, arrive too late, or be strategically suppressed.

It does not yet close the origin of that procedural commitment, discovery and justification of all morally relevant features, formation of new kinds of reason, or legitimate arbitration of plural reasons. Those are not merely missing parameter estimates. They are distinct explanatory and justificatory tasks. The chapter names them instead of silently treating a positive anchor or valid certificate as their solution.

No additional dialogue is necessary: the familiar-versus-unfamiliar injury example and the two-claim bottleneck already perform different, useful explanatory jobs. An added scene could obscure the distinction between the logical reason challenge and the strategic route-preservation calculation.
