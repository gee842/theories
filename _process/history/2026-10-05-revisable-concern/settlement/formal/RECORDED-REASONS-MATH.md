## Exact finite closure and its price

Let `E(u,v)` mean that some positively decoded candidate edge connects `u` to `v`. On the three-node domain, every rooted reachable non-root node has one of these simple paths:

\[
a\to p,\quad a\to q,\quad a\to p\to q,\quad a\to q\to p.
\]

These paths are sufficient even with self-loops or repeated edge endpoints: any reachable node has a simple path, and a three-node simple path uses at most two edges. Conversely every admitted path is rooted reachability. The Lean `closure_exact` proves that equivalence for the three-node domain; the independent graph grid checks every ordered triple of non-self candidate edges and all eight Boolean certificate assignments. This is not arbitrary-size graph closure at constant cost.

Each paid audit activates the workspace, senses context, decodes three queries, then checks all four paths. The respective charges are `1+1+3+1+1+2+2=11`. Path charges are prepaid even for failed or redundant paths. A path event is a bounded circuit macro, not an unpaid validity oracle. The reader's finite functional capacity, wiring, query table and reliable invalidation interlock are supplied by the declared recipe.

For a valid dormant state with an installed reader, idle writer and available archive, the fixed audit word is feasible exactly when common fuel is at least eleven. Its endpoint preserves archive and source, spends eleven common units, and publishes the exact rooted closure of the decoded available evidence. This does not assert completeness for unknown facts outside that evidence. Positive semantic soundness follows by induction on rooted paths from the supplied root and relevance rules.

Access loss before sensing/decoding can block the audit without producing false certificates. After complete decoding, replay reads the cache and can finish despite subsequent archive inaccessibility. Delivery loss likewise does not refute a retained reason. The source stock is not an alternative upfront payment mechanism in the audit law.
