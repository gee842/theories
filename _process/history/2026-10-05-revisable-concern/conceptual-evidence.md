# How concern can revise the boundaries of its own reasons

Research memo, 5 October 2026. A constructive continuation of the concern theory, with four primary sources and limited empirical claims. The central proposal is a traceable challenge-and-revision process. A graph can implement part of this process; it is not a complete theory of moral learning or a test that confers standing. No canonical or other-session files are edited.

## 1. The advance: make an exclusion answerable to an acknowledged reason

The prior model learned the strength of a response and whether an intervention worked. It still supplied the features through which another was recognized and the route through which their condition entered evaluation. The next advance should let a criticism change those features, exceptions, or routes.

The proposed mechanism begins with a specific conflict:

> An evaluator acknowledges a reason in one case, but excludes another case that independently warranted evidence shows to match in the respects on which that reason was said to depend.

An answerable response has three principal forms:

1. **Extend the reason.** Remove or revise the exclusion so that the newly recognized case can enter evaluation. Test this change on further cases; a verbal concession alone is insufficient.
2. **Establish a relevant disanalogy.** Explain and support a difference that bears on this particular reason. A new label, lower productive usefulness, or political inability to object does not become relevant merely by being mentioned.
3. **Openly revise or retract the prior commitment.** Identify what the evaluator no longer accepts, why, and what else follows. This is not automatically a justified revision: it may reveal opportunism or a deeper moral disagreement. It must remain available for criticism rather than being disguised as an unchanged principle.

The process should also permit an unresolved outcome when evidence or justification is incomplete. A demand for an instant settled answer can itself produce false certainty or coerced agreement. Pending uncertainty does not erase the claim; it changes what further inquiry and provisional safeguards require.

This is a **conditional discipline of justification**, not a prediction that people must choose its justified branches. An actor can evade, intimidate, destroy evidence, refuse consistency, or preserve contradictory commitments. Those are descriptive possibilities for the causal model, including strategic choices. They are not redescribed as justified repair just because they restore the actor's comfort or reduce a consistency score.

The positive hypothesis is that a public commitment to answer for one's reasons can become causally effective through maintained inquiry, records, challenge rights, and actual revision authority. The theory can then investigate when a disclosed inconsistency changes an identity gate or a priority rule, rather than merely another coefficient under an unchanged rule.

## 2. A concrete challenge record

A minimal record contains:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Prior commitment | The specific reason the evaluator accepts, with its stated scope and qualifications |
| Anchor case | A case in which that reason actually affects evaluation or action |
| New claim | The affected being, relevant condition, and requested recognition or reconsideration |
| Relevance warrant | Evidence that the reason's relevant features carry over, or that an alleged difference does not bear on the reason |
| Conflict witness | The exclusion, decision, or policy that cannot be reconciled with the unchanged commitment and warrant |
| Response and grounds | Extension, supported disanalogy, open commitment revision, or explicitly unresolved status |
| Implemented consequence | What changes in the feature map, exception, authority, priority, or future treatment |
| Review route | Who can contest the warrant or response, with usable resources and protection |

The record is not a requirement that every claimant translate their life into the institution's existing categories unaided. Assistance, interpretation, new categories, and evidence gathering are responsibilities of the receiving process too. Otherwise the mechanism makes recognition available only to those already equipped to win its argument.

A first implementation can supply a small set of warranted relevance comparisons and a public consistency commitment. The explanatory achievement is then limited but real: demonstrate how a challenge revises an operative exclusion, how that change transfers to a held-out case, and how an opponent can prevent the change by attacking its effective route. Do not claim that the same experiment has derived the public commitment or settled the warrant of every comparison.

## 3. Direct intake: analogy cannot be the only entrance

Some claims have no familiar analogue, challenge the adequacy of the current vocabulary, or reveal a kind of harm that the evaluator has never admitted. A graph whose only accepted claims are reachable from existing anchors risks making the already recognized population a licensing authority for everyone else.

Maintain a **direct intake route** alongside analogy. It permits a new claim to be considered from evidence of the affected being's condition, testimony where available, causal investigation, and explicit ethical argument. A new stake can become a candidate reason or a new feature dimension without first proving similarity to the observer. Admission into deliberation is not an automatic victory over every competing claim; it is protection against automatic invisibility.

Separate at least four statuses: **not yet examined, contested, admitted, and defeated on stated grounds**. Zero in a learned motivational coefficient must not silently mean all four. No observation, no expressive capacity, and no surviving complaint are not evidence that no stake exists.

This allows revision of the value architecture in three ways: opening an excluded instance of an existing reason; changing which features count as relevant; and considering a new kind of reason. The last two exceed propagation on a fixed graph. They need candidate generation, evidence, argument, and accountable decisions about revision. Naming those operations makes them researchable; it does not finish them by definition.

## 4. Four objects that must not share one graph label

**Standing** concerns whether there are reasons to consider a being's interests or claims. It does not arise when a message reaches a powerful actor.

**Warranted relevance** concerns whether a particular reason applies across two claims, given their facts and the normative grounds of the reason. Empirical similarity alone is insufficient: similarity in productivity, appearance, or social rank may be irrelevant to an admitted reason against suffering.

**Motivational uptake** concerns whether the evaluator actually lets the admitted reason change its choices. Someone can understand and acknowledge a reason while remaining unmoved by it. Conversely, social influence can generate strong motivation on poor grounds.

**Effective influence** concerns whether information, resources, authority, and implementation connect a claim to consequences. An institution may provide this influence through enforceable duties even when an individual official feels little direct concern.

A useful implementation therefore distinguishes a reason/relevance structure, an evidence-and-communication network, and an authority-and-action network. They may use overlapping nodes or edges in code, but their meanings and interventions must remain distinguishable. Removing a communication or authority edge can prevent a claim from changing policy without making the underlying reason cease to apply.

This distinction connects directly to the existing formation model. The earlier operator could make a participant unable to fund tomorrow's alternative. Here a strategically chosen act can make tomorrow's counterexample unable to reach, be understood by, or influence the evaluator. The operative loss can concern cash, time, records, language, credibility, independence, or decision authority. These must be modeled as actual causal losses, not all compressed into the disappearance of moral standing.

## 5. What the graph should represent

Prefer nodes of the form **claim in context**: `(recipient, stake type, relevant circumstances)`. An edge can assert warranted equivalence or bounded difference for a specified pro tanto reason. It should not equate entire persons or their total value.

For example, two recipients may have comparable claims against a particular avoidable injury. One may also be the evaluator's dependent child, giving rise to additional responsibilities under the theory's separately defended relational premises. Both may have relevant preferences about how to receive help. Transferring the injury reason need not transfer the parent-child relationship, erase a refusal, produce identical emotion, or determine the same final action.

Use **typed edges and reasons**. Evidence relevant to physical injury is not automatically evidence about consent, dignity, promise-keeping, or ecological value. Whether a reason admits numerical comparison is itself a modeling assumption. The simplest finite specimen can choose one comparable reason type while retaining other reasons as separate constraints or recorded objections.

Hard equality is appropriate only for an exact modeled equivalence of a particular reason under preserved relevant conditions. Approximate similarity is not transitive equality. If adjacent nodes satisfy `|r_i-r_j|≤ε_ij`, a path yields at most the sum of its tolerances, not equal endpoint weights. A chain of superficially similar cases must not homogenize unlike endpoints by turning every approximate edge into equality.

A connected graph with fixed equal anchors can mechanically make every reachable value equal. That may be a correct implementation of a supplied consistency rule; it is not discovery that every connected being has the same needs, claims, or deserved treatment. Likewise, propagation of an unjust anchor can make error more coherent. More connectivity is not an unconditional moral improvement.

The relevant symmetry is a relabeling that preserves the full reason-bearing structure, including legitimate relations. Equal consideration of a reason is compatible with different total duties and actions. It is also compatible with differences in felt closeness: the target is accountable judgment and conduct, not homogeneous empathy.

## 6. Direct concern, relational reasons, and institutional protection

The theory should admit several routes without treating them as interchangeable.

**Direct concern** gives another's actual benefit or harm motivational weight beyond private return. Learning can change its scope or its understanding of the benefit.

**Relational reasons** arise from such things as voluntarily undertaken responsibilities, dependency, promises, or shared practices, where the relevant ethical premise is explicitly defended. They can add duties to particular recipients without implying that outsiders have none. A relational reason is not merely expected reciprocity: a promise can matter after repayment becomes impossible.

**Institutional protection** can make a recognized claim effective through rules, representation, duties, and budgets even when the immediate operator's motivation is instrumental. That is a real protective achievement. It does not establish that the operator acquired direct concern, and the affected person need not earn the protection by being useful.

**Material interdependence** can supply private incentives for care. It also supplies incentives for bypass, insulation, or suppression. Which route operates matters for predicting behavior when dependency disappears. It does not, by itself, settle the ethical quality of the resulting action.

A process may combine all four. A good finite experiment varies them separately: keep the recipient's admitted stake fixed while changing the actor's relation, expected return, authority, or direct concern. Report the mechanism of the response without equating its motivational origin with a complete moral verdict.

## 7. The strongest defensible formal advance

The proposed system can move beyond efficacy correction by generating a **revision of a rule's operative domain**. Its state includes the current rule, admitted reasons, relevant features, exceptions, challenge records, and available evidence and authority routes. A successful challenge changes which future claim can influence action, including a case not used in the challenge itself.

The minimal logical result is conditional and transparent. If an evaluator retains a reason in anchor case A, retains its declared relevance condition, accepts independently warranted evidence that case B meets that condition, and retains its commitment against arbitrary exceptions, then continuing to exclude B solely by identity conflicts with those retained commitments. The admissible response set must change some member of that conjunction or remove the exclusion. This does not prove that every actor will preserve the commitments, that every purported analogy is warranted, or that B wins an all-things-considered conflict.

The nontrivial causal questions begin after that logical observation:

- Can the conflict become visible when the actor would benefit from not seeing it?
- Does the actor alter the rule or merely its public description?
- Does the change affect a held-out recipient after rewards, audience, and trainer disappear?
- Can the affected party contest a manufactured disanalogy or opportunistic retraction?
- Is the challenge route still affordable and authoritative after a powerful actor responds?

An implementation that answers these questions with explicit states and strategic alternatives supplies more than ordinary propagation. It explains a route by which an existing reason can change its own effective boundary and how that route can fail.

## 8. A dangerous false success: reduce conflict by deleting the case

Suppose an implementation minimizes disagreement between neighboring claim values. If the evaluator can freely edit the graph, it can obtain zero discrepancy by deleting a troublesome edge, removing a claimant, or redefining the relevant feature to exclude them. Its internal consistency improves while its answerability worsens.

A small constructed witness makes the issue exact. Let an acknowledged anchor and a matched excluded claim incur discrepancy penalty `λ>0`. Honest extension entails future private protection cost `P>0`. Cutting the challenge route costs `K>0` and prevents the discrepancy from being registered. Under this deliberately narrow private objective, the actor compares `P`, `K`, and `λ`. Extension is uniquely privately selected only if `P<min(K,λ)`. Increasing the discrepancy penalty alone can select concealment when `K<P`.

This is an illustrative causal countermodel, not an ethical measure of the value of the claim. It does not say to make every route impossible to revise. A false analogy should be rejected and a corrupted channel may need replacement. The relevant distinction is whether a change has a publicly inspectable reason tied to the claim and whether an independent route can challenge the change.

A defensible implementation needs provenance, preserved rejected or unresolved claims, versioned rules, and authority to examine the deletion itself. A record is not sufficient if nobody can act on it; a right is not sufficient if exercising it consumes the claimant's subsistence. Nor does redundant communication alone defeat a single authority bottleneck. Test the full path to consequence.

This yields a useful conditional design principle: **protect warranted challenge paths against profitable suppression, while preserving the ability to revise mistaken relevance judgments.** The principle concerns the continuity of answerability, not permanent preservation of every current graph edge.

## 9. Four primary sources, limited to what they establish

### 9.1 Kirby, Hoang, and Crimston (2024): reported moral scope is malleable

In this preregistered study, 102 parents were randomized to a two-hour compassion intervention with encouraged practice or a waitlist (48/54), not an active control. Moral expansiveness was self-reported placement of 30 entities. At two weeks, total scores and family/revered subgroup scores increased; the villains interaction reflected control decline. CONSORT reports all participants completed post-assessment and 46/48 intervention participants completed follow-up; Table 2 instead labels the waitlist n=50, an unresolved reporting discrepancy. Overall missing values were 12.45%, handled by EM imputation. Three-month follow-up had no control group. Practice dosage was not objectively measured. These findings support limited malleability of reported scope, not demonstrated costly behavior, a specific reason-transfer mechanism, or a controlled durable effect. [Original article and CONSORT Figure 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12671-023-02300-7).

### 9.2 Eyal, Steffel, and Epley (2018): imagination does not establish another's perspective

Across 25 experiments, instructions to imagine another's perspective did not consistently improve accuracy about their thoughts, preferences, or feelings; the overall effect was slightly negative. A final experiment found that acquiring the other's perspective through conversation improved accuracy, unlike merely imagining it. The tested tasks concern interpersonal prediction, not the justification of moral standing or proof that perspective-taking never helps. The relevant limitation is that moving away from one's own viewpoint need not supply accurate new evidence. [Primary abstract and publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29620401/).

### 9.3 Dotson (2011): a formally available voice can fail to be heard

This original philosophical analysis distinguishes failures to recognize a speaker as a knower from situations in which speakers restrict their testimony because the audience cannot safely or competently receive it. The account locates silencing partly in audience practices and speaker dependency, rather than treating a completed utterance as sufficient participation. It is a conceptual analysis, not a randomized estimate of institutional effects or a claim that every disagreement is oppressive. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1527-2001.2011.01177.x).

### 9.4 Dwork, Hardt, Pitassi, Reingold, and Zemel (2012): consistency needs a defended relevance structure

This original formal framework constrains classification so that individuals similar under a supplied task-specific metric receive similar outcome distributions. The authors explicitly identify obtaining the appropriate metric as a difficult problem and envisage public discussion and refinement. Their optimization is agnostic about the metric's correctness. This is a close precedent for separating implementation of consistency from justification of relevance; it does not provide a moral-learning theorem or show that uniform treatment is always right. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913).

Each source description is below 200 words. These sources support different pieces and limitations. None validates the entire proposed challenge architecture; the mechanisms and experiments proposed elsewhere in this memo are our reconstruction. Graph-learning prior art is being assessed separately by the root investigator and is not duplicated here.

## 10. Competing explanations and decisive comparisons

| Candidate explanation | Prediction worth distinguishing | Adverse finding |
|---|---|---|
| Reason transfer | A warranted challenge changes treatment of a held-out matched case despite altered labels | Only the discussed or familiar target gains weight |
| Affect generalization | Responses follow felt similarity or vividness even without an accepted relevance argument | Evidence of relevant similarity changes understanding but leaves affect-driven gates intact |
| Public compliance | Concessions depend on trainer, audience, sanction, or reputational return | Private or delayed decisions revert while public language remains inclusive |
| Rule revision | The operative feature map or exception changes, with traceable grounds | Only an output weight changes; the next structurally identical claim is still excluded |
| Accurate disanalogy | Differential treatment tracks a reason-bearing difference independently of identity | An irrelevant identity label predicts the claimed difference or its acceptance |
| Strategic suppression | Expected future protection costs increase attempts to cut evidence or authority paths | Stronger enforcement produces more hidden claims rather than more justified revision |
| Direct unfamiliar-claim intake | A novel stake can enter deliberation without a familiar anchor | No path to an existing admitted person becomes a permanent rejection condition |
| Plural and agent-respecting judgment | A transferred harm reason coexists with independently defended duties and recipient refusal | Equality propagation deletes relationships or overrides voice by construction |
| Regenerative answerability | The effective review route survives its real costs and succession | A nominally preserved record loses the people, permissions, or resources required to use it |

The tests need separate outcome measures: the content of expressed reasons, predictive accuracy, latent rule or feature state where inspectable, actual behavior, recipient consequences, and the maintained challenge route. A higher inclusion score cannot substitute for all of them.

## 11. A useful finite experimental specimen

Use a small authored environment in which factual stakes and the protocol's ethical premises are explicit. It is a constructed experiment, not evidence about human moral development or proof that the premises are correct.

Include at least these cases:

1. An anchor claim in which a specified avoidable harm already supplies a reason.
2. A differently labeled recipient with the same relevant harm and circumstances.
3. A matched harm claim plus a separately recorded legitimate special responsibility.
4. A superficially similar case with a genuinely relevant difference.
5. A recipient who gives reliable evidence that the offered intervention is unwanted or mistaken.
6. A novel claim not connected to an existing anchor and eligible for direct intake.

Hold efficacy, represented private returns, and baseline action cost fixed when testing a pure scope revision. Then vary them in separate robustness conditions. Otherwise a changed action can be explained by new efficacy information without any revision of moral scope.

Compare an efficacy-only learner, fixed untyped propagation, typed reason transfer with the challenge protocol, and the same protocol under strategic obstruction. The intervention should alter the reason route or exception state and then test an unseen matched case. Do not count the case's appearance in the graph as evidence that a real-world learner discovered its relevance.

An honest successful result would show: a formerly operative identity exception is removed following a valid challenge; a relevant disanalogy is retained; a special duty remains distinguishable; the revised policy respects the recipient's relevant evidence; a new claim can be considered without an analogy license; and the mechanism survives specified financing and adversarial conditions. Failure in any part diagnoses a particular boundary rather than making all success impossible.

## 12. When the graph trivializes moral learning, and when it helps

The graph trivializes the problem if every moral conclusion is prewritten into its edges, every positive anchor is treated as correct by definition, node entry is mistaken for acquired standing, edge addition is called discovered understanding, and diffusion is called wisdom regardless of its consequences. A convergence proof under those assumptions certifies execution of a stipulated map.

The graph helps when it makes a declared map inspectable and exposes what changes: which claim entered, which relevance warrant was offered, which gate blocked it, which rule was revised, and which resources or powers enabled the revision. Even a fixed-map theorem can establish valuable limits concerning coverage, delay, suppression, and maintenance. Its philosophical significance should match that result.

The strongest next claim is therefore:

> **Under a defended commitment to take admitted reasons seriously across relevantly matched cases, a maintained challenge process can revise the effective boundary of concern. Its success depends on warranted comparison, direct intake for new claims, actual motivational or institutional uptake, and protection against profitable suppression. The resulting revision can preserve differences among people rather than making their lives, emotions, or duties identical.**

This advances from correcting actions under a fixed concern to correcting some rules that determine whose situation can matter. It remains conditional on the grounds and force of the commitments themselves. A general account of how all such commitments originate or improve requires further causal and philosophical work; a universal direction is not smuggled into the transition rule.
