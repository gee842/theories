## Exact references and the mixed-history theorem

The primary preparation has M=3, no A or H, no resident, a damaged site, no local fuel, four common fuel units and one incident packet. Unit formation, association, repair, capture and local-copy rates are supplied. The zero-loss reference has:

| Law | Exact terminal E[A−H] | Expected A local copies while H is already present |
|---|---:|---:|
| Local return | 17/150 | 7/150 |
| Shared return | 4/147 | 0 local copies; primary copying uses common fuel |
| Repair disabled | 0 | 0 |
| Equally paid ineffective repair | 0 | 0 |

These values were independently derived by complete exact recursion. The finite-time mixed witness is H formation → A formation → A association → repair → capture → A copy. Its competing exit rates are 24,15,8,3,1,1 and its branch probability is 1/60. The endpoint contains A=2 and H=1. Each precursor and fuel debit belongs to that same path.

The analytical bound follows correctly: impose waits of at most two, use e²>7, and restore nuisance rates ε=10⁻⁶ through a coupling whose total nuisance intensity is at most 19ε. The bound includes every reachable state, not just the successful path: c≤1, population≤3, usable fuel≤5 and P(A+H)≤2. The range of Z is [−3,3], so the two-arm comparison loses at most 2·6·19ε·12. This proves a causal abundance gap greater than **28482021/7353062500**, exceeding **0.00387**, relative to each repair control.

The mixed-copy mark lies in [0,1] because one incident packet funds at most one local copy. Its separate one-arm bound exceeds **0.00638**. Thus the conclusion does not rely solely on A-first clonal histories. The smaller M=2,F=3 diagnostic is preserved: its exact reference abundance contrast is 1/18, but its mixed-copy count is zero. That narrower fixture alone would not have established the requested mixed-history connection.

## Controls must be interpreted by their actual statistics

At H=12 in the positive-rate primary fixture:

| Arm | E[A−H] | Expected A local copies with H present |
|---|---:|---:|
| Active local return | 0.113318292 | 0.046659551 |
| Repair disabled | 0, by label symmetry | 0 |
| Equally paid ineffective repair | −0.0000000587 | 0 |
| Shared return | 0.027209847 | 0 local copies |
| No copying | 0, by its generator identity | 0 |

The shared arm is not neutral in this abundance statistic. Its common fuel can correlate with composition because only A restores capture. The earlier stopped-frequency martingale assumed an already seeded contest without continuing untemplated formation and concerns a different observable. It cannot be substituted here. Likewise the ineffective-repair arm lacks full label-swap symmetry: only A burns common fuel through futile work. The analytical proof correctly uses a two-arm perturbation instead of assuming that arm's positive-rate expectation is zero.

Average individual-population fraction can also mislead. The no-copy arm has equal mean A and H counts but expected within-run A fraction approximately **0.514444**, above one half. The ineffective-repair arm's fraction is approximately **0.510444** even while its abundance contrast is negative. Empty-population probabilities in these cases are tiny; the empty-half convention does not account for the difference. Averaging a ratio over populations of differing sizes and histories is not taking the ratio of mean counts. A fraction above one half alone therefore cannot establish the claimed consequence-dependent copying mechanism.

The comparison preserves equal initial resources and event prices across arms. Realized expenditures need not be equal after histories diverge. No-association and no-repair controls block new capture; disabling both copying routes leaves symmetric formation and wear. Initial common fuel below three blocks bootstrap; below four blocks the restored-site/mixed-population configuration in the local-return law. The independent graph checks reproduce these obstructions.

## Source-dependent failure and paid takeover remain visible

The work-cost fixture uses the same σ=10⁻⁴ and nuisance rates ε=10⁻⁹ in the two environments. Changing only incident source from one to zero changes the expected abundance contrast from approximately **+0.113297003** to **−0.0000113329**. The note's independent analytical bounds, respectively greater than 0.0443 and less than −0.00000496, also check. A catalytic outcome differs from the zero-work reference with probability at most σ because its zero-nuisance history has at most one repair attempt. Source-free histories instead acquire a negative drift through the actual A-consuming work branch. These are explicit reaction costs, not observer penalties.

A separate paid-takeover path shows that locality does not guarantee permanent credit to the useful type. Starting with M=2,F=5,E=1 and both types absent, follow:

**Form A → form H → associate A → repair → resident A wears → associate H → capture → H copies.**

The independent checker verifies all competing hazards and a strictly positive exact path probability, carrying the repaired site and all fuel through the succession. The endpoint has A=0,H=2 and no remaining usable or incident fuel. This is a positive-probability possibility, not a reliable finite-deadline outcome. The primary F=4 fixture's fuel restriction cannot establish general immunity to such inheritance of another type's productive work.

With positive wear and finite support, all molecules eventually disappear after finitely many births; terminal A−H is zero. The positive and negative selection effects concern the declared finite horizon. They establish neither indefinitely retained advantages nor inevitable favorable direction.
