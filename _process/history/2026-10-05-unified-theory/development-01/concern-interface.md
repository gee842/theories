# A precise interface between learned concern and constructed alternatives

Research note, 5 October 2026. This is a new, explicitly specified one-period model joining two completed lines of work. It does not assert faithful equivalence of their entire dynamics or autonomous formation of all enabling conditions. Its sources are the concern branch's `learning-formal.md`, `dynamics-formal.md`, and `CLAIMS.md`, and this development's `math-proposal.md`.

## 1. What is composed

The concern branch distinguishes learned recipient valuation v=gw from estimated intervention efficacy z. The learned value w and alignment g can start at zero and grow under its supplied teaching, shared evaluation route, and paid evidence. Its action score uses z because an imperfect intervention leaves residual recipient harm. Neither knowledge nor accurate prediction alone forces that evaluative route.

The constructed-alternative game instead explains how a participant's funded rival use of its input changes an incumbent's strategic choice. Its current protective condition is c<min(r,chi), conditional on observable implementation, physical refusal, and costly blockage.

Here take v=gw>=0 as the incumbent's current direct valuation of a distinct patient's condition. This patient can be a third party, so adding this ledger does not silently change the input participant's previously proved preferences. Keep the participant's receipts b>x, available alternative o with x<o<b, startup and upkeep costs, and the incumbent's economic payoffs unchanged. The patient suffers H>0 under harmful joint production, (1-z)H under safeguard S, and zero under exit following unblocked H, where 0<=z<=1.

For the exact outcome interpretation, z is the known current efficacy. If z is instead a learned estimate, the following equations characterize perceived choice; actual safeguard harm is (1-Q)H with separately measured true efficacy Q. An incorrect estimate cannot establish actual protection. All actions and current payments are feasible; learning, audits, and their costs are outside this one decision and must be separately financed.

Recipient damage is not another private cost or an income debit already counted in r. The term v times patient harm is explicitly other-directed valuation. The different parties' utilities are not added into a supposed social objective.

## 2. The active-alternative theorem

With an active alternative, the input participant joins S, joins blocked harmful production B, and exits unblocked harmful production H. The incumbent's evaluated payoffs are therefore

\[
U_S=r-c-(1-z)vH,
\qquad U_B=r-\chi-vH,
\qquad U_H=0.
\tag{C1}
\]

Consequently S is uniquely chosen exactly when

\[
\boxed{c<\chi+zvH\quad\text{and}\quad c+(1-z)vH<r.}
\tag{C2}
\]

**Proof.** The difference U_S-U_B is chi-c+zvH; U_S-U_H is r-c-(1-z)vH. Both must be strictly positive. These comparisons are also sufficient against the entire declared three-action repertoire. Equality permits another optimal action and is not guaranteed safeguarding. ∎

The first inequality combines expensive interference and direct concern for the harm the safeguard prevents. The second compares continuing imperfect joint production with harmless exit. Replacing the first inequality's zvH by a single concern score and dropping the residual term in the second would silently change what exit does to the patient.

For perfect protection z=1, put q=vH. The condition reduces to

\[
\boxed{c<\chi+q,\qquad c<r.}
\tag{C3}
\]

When v=0 this recovers the original one-period incentive threshold. It does not recover the original multi-episode equilibrium without its further information, resource, and horizon assumptions.

## 3. Complementarity, substitution, and exit

If no alternative is active, the input participant joins harmful H. Blockage adds a positive fee without changing any outcome, so B is dominated. The incumbent then compares S above with r-vH. Thus

\[
\boxed{\text{unique inactive safeguarding}\iff zvH>c.}
\tag{C4}
\]

This concerns the declared repertoire: the incumbent is not given an additional unilateral shutdown action. Adding harmless shutdown supplies another option and can change the result, especially when joint production has no incumbent value.

There is a strict complementarity region. For example, let r=4,c=2,chi=1,H=1,z=1,v=3/2. Concern alone is insufficient: inactive harmful production gives 5/2, exceeding safeguarding's 2. The alternative alone is insufficient: at v=0 blockage gives 3, exceeding safeguarding's 2. Together, safeguarding gives 2, exceeding blockage's 3/2 and exit's zero.

This is an incumbent action result, not a claim that the participant can afford or will build the alternative. If h=b-x and upkeep is m, constructing it still needs the separate private gain h-m and every funding prefix when inactivity remains harmful. When concern already induces inactive safeguarding, activating an otherwise redundant facility costs m without improving the participant's current receipt. The channels can therefore be complements near a threshold and substitutes after either alone secures the outcome. Prudential insurance against future change is a further dynamic question.

For imperfect protection 0<z<1, greater concern need not produce more joint operation. Writing C=vH, safeguarding requires

\[
\frac{c-\chi}{z}<C<\frac{r-c}{1-z},\qquad C\ge0,
\tag{C5}
\]

with the lower inequality automatic when its right-hand threshold is negative. As concern grows, residual damage can make harmless exit preferable. For r=4,c=2,chi=1,z=1/2,H=1, v=3 uniquely selects S, while v=5 uniquely selects exit. Actual patient harm falls from 1/2 to zero. Counting only ongoing safeguards would misleadingly call that transition a loss of concern or correction.

## 4. Limits and the next genuine bridge

The assumption that exit leaves this patient's harm zero is substantive. If exit damages the patient, gives up another important benefit, or shifts harm elsewhere, that consequence belongs in its own ledger. For general predicted harms L_S,L_B,L_E, the exact active conditions are c<chi+v(L_B-L_S) and c<r+v(L_E-L_S). Ethical standing cannot be settled by whatever this small ledger happens to include.

The concern branch's acquisition and succession theorems do not transfer unchanged. Even if v and z increase along their separately validated learning paths, the residual product (1-z)v need not be monotone. Paid teaching, operative evidence, participant formation, strategic interference, operator succession, and actual patient outcomes would have to be represented in one process before claiming a closed developmental theory.

The new result is narrower and concrete: direct learned concern and constructed leverage can jointly change a current choice when neither suffices alone; their exact interaction depends on the consequences of exit and intervention. A proof of strategic protection, a proof of learned valuation, and an ethical reason to protect remain distinct claims connected through this declared interface.
