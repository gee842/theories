# What strategic formation adds to the unified foundation

5 October 2026. Conceptual and ethical review of the current mathematical proposal. This note supplies an exact countermodel and assesses interpretation; it is not independent certification of every game-theoretic proof. No claim below establishes universal moral direction or empirical validity.

## The central advance

The new result explains a limited formation process that the earlier funded corrective gate did not. A participant can choose to construct and maintain an actual alternative. An incumbent then chooses protection because the alternative changes what the participant will do and what the incumbent can obtain. In the current-action model, protection is the unique response exactly when \(c<\min(r,\chi)\). This is an operational explanation of how a protective response can enter a privately selected process without a supplied benevolent command.

The persistent-incumbent result makes the deeper contribution. An action can have a current consequence and a different consequence for **who will be able to affect later choices**. Blocking once can be more expensive than safeguarding now while being cheaper than safeguarding throughout. It can therefore be privately optimal because it removes the participant's future ability to fund challenge.

In the specified two-episode class, the distinction is exact:

\[
W_{\mathrm{aff}}=K+m+\ell,
\qquad W_{\mathrm{res}}=K+2m+\ell.
\]

The first threshold finances entry. The second leaves enough reserve after the specified interference to remove its strategic advantage. These are different causal properties, not two estimates of the same budget. The extra \(m\) changes the future game, and through that change alters the present action selected by the incumbent.

The equilibrium implication is especially important: below the second threshold the participant delays operation; above it the incumbent safeguards from the start. Neither selected path contains actual blockage. Absence of an observed suppression event therefore does not establish absence of strategically consequential power to suppress. Conversely, an unused alternative can be causally effective by changing the conduct selected in its presence.

The result should retain its exact scope: two episodes, the stated repertoire and preferences, supplied control and observation, no borrowing, and the displayed inequalities. “Strategically resilient” here means sufficient against the specified financing attack in this equilibrium. It does not mean proof against any coercion, future theft, repeated interference, or policy change.

## The foundation becomes more discriminating

The earlier foundation asked what current activity leaves possible. This result refines that question: **which future possibilities remain usable by which agents, and how does that distribution of capacity change what others choose now?** Capacity enters both as a future causal resource and as a present determinant of expectations and incentives.

Three distinctions become indispensable:

- **Protection delivered now and protection made renewable.** A currently safe choice may leave later correction fragile; a reserve can protect even before it is spent.
- **An available intervention and the ability to keep choosing it.** Paying for a first operation does not establish control over continuation.
- **Continuation of assets and continuation of effective influence.** A facility can remain intact while its owner loses the reserve needed to activate it. Retaining equipment alone can preserve a powerless inheritance.

This also clarifies why “longer horizon” is not a general moral improvement. A longer-lived actor can value future cooperation, but can also invest in removing future resistance. The sign depends on which future consequences the actor can appropriate, whose costs enter its evaluation, and what constraints remain. The new example proves the second possibility in a small class; it does not establish that replacing incumbents is generally better.

## Does the proposed wisdom refinement over-narrow the theory?

“Wisdom must track how present choices change who can challenge future choices” is a strong institutional application, but it is too narrow as an unconditional definition of all wisdom. A solitary inquiry, medical understanding, skilled attention, or a finite personal decision can be wise without involving a contest between parties. Some situations also require finality or appropriately limited contestation.

The more general formulation should be:

> **Where continued responsiveness matters, wisdom tracks how present action changes the conditions under which later understanding, choice, and correction can occur.**

The institutional and ethical specialization is:

> **Where decisions shape others' lives, ethical wisdom also asks whose claims can influence later choices, how that capacity can be weakened, and what would keep justified challenges effective.**

The words “where” and “justified” matter. Not every decision requires an indefinitely maintained reviewing institution; not every interference with a harmful actor's leverage is wrong. The protected claim and the authority to decide remain objects of ethical justification. A predator's capacity to resist restraint is not automatically something to regenerate.

This refinement connects multiple domains without equating them. An investigator can erase data needed to criticize a model; a learner can adopt an interface that suppresses anomalous observations; an institution can deprive its critics of operating means; a successor can inherit conclusions without the means of rechecking them. The causal question is common, while the relevant carriers and ethical claims differ.

## An exact countermodel of insulated regeneration

The following deliberately small construction isolates what productive selection alone cannot guarantee.

Let \(R_t>0\) be an organization's productive support. Fix \(R_*>0\), \(0<\lambda<1\), and explicitly supplied recurrent resources sufficient for replacement. The production arrangement spends \(\lambda R_*\) per period renewing this support, with dynamics

\[
R_{t+1}=(1-\lambda)R_t+\lambda R_*.
\]

On the admitted operating interval \(R_t\in[R_*/2,3R_*/2]\), suppose gross production is \(Y>\lambda R_*+c\). The interval is invariant, and

\[
R_t-R_*=(1-\lambda)^t(R_0-R_*),
\]

so the support converges geometrically to its maintained level. The recurrent supply and constant output on this interval are explicit premises, not a claim that a closed system produces resources from nothing.

A separate affected patient undergoes independently described harm \(h>0\) each period unless the selector installs that period's safeguard \(a_t=1\). The safeguard costs \(c>0\) from surplus; \(a_t=0\) costs nothing. Patient harm is

\[
d_t=h(1-a_t).
\]

The selector's private receipt is

\[
u_t=Y-\lambda R_*-ca_t.
\]

Assume patient harm and patient responses cannot change production, access to renewal inputs, the selector's continuation opportunities or rewards, or third-party actions. The selector assigns the patient's harm no direct weight. Both actions remain physically feasible because their costs fit the stipulated surplus. There is no hidden retaliation, reputation effect, sanction, reciprocity, conscience, or learning signal that could alter the selector's incentives. These are substantive insulation assumptions, not claims about all real patients.

For any finite positive horizon, or an infinite discounted horizon with \(0<\delta<1\), choosing a safeguard at any date reduces the selector's payoff by \(\delta^t c>0\) without changing any other component. Therefore

\[
a_t=0\quad\text{at every date}
\]

is the unique payoff-maximizing action sequence. This remains true for a selector with perfect knowledge of the harm and arbitrary computational capacity. Productive support converges to \(R_*\), maintenance continues, and the patient remains unprotected. A patient can exist within the same modeled world without having a causal route to change the selector's objective or constraints.

This is an exact counterexample to “maintained productive regeneration necessarily develops responsiveness to every affected being.” It does not establish that any particular real institution is completely insulated, nor that all forms of selection lack inclusive outcomes. It identifies conditions under which this route to protection is absent.

A slightly more general statement follows immediately: when a safeguard has positive private cost and leaves the selector's reward-relevant future and feasible alternatives unchanged, a purely private maximizer has no incentive to choose it. Dropping any premise can change the result. Direct concern, an affected party's credible substitution, collective constraints, duties backed by enforcement, or other consequential responses can make protection enter selection. Each proposed route requires its own causal account; listing a possible route does not show that it forms or persists.

## Standing and leverage must remain different

The new constructive mechanism works by leverage: an affected party controls an input the incumbent cannot replace without losing rent, and can operate an alternative that is sufficiently costly to block. Those facts explain why protection may be chosen. They do not explain why the party deserves protection.

The ethical bridge developed separately gives standing through admitted goods, harms, relevant consistency, and answerability. A patient can lose every source of leverage while retaining those claims. Conversely, an agent can gain leverage without acquiring a justified claim to use it in every way. Bargaining power is a causal resource, not a moral entitlement.

This distinction prevents three misleading conclusions. First, successful equilibrium bargaining does not establish that its outcome is fair. Second, inability to induce protection does not show that the patient's claim is weak. Third, an institution that protects only effective contributors may be a considerable operational achievement while leaving the larger ethical question unresolved.

The compact relation is:

> **Standing explains why a claim deserves consideration; leverage helps explain whether a selector responds; legitimacy concerns whether the response and the powers producing it are justified.**

A complete theory must investigate all three without replacing one with another.

## Practical and experimental implications

Within the model's stated class, the next comparison should distinguish startup funding from funding delivered capacity after interference. The exact extra-reserve intervention makes a discriminating prediction: a change in resources can shift current selected protection even when no interference occurs on either realized path. This is stronger than correlating protection with expenditure on actual complaints.

The relevant outcomes should be recorded separately: patient protection, installed facility, active operation, reserves at every prefix, available alternatives, actor choices, and successor conditions. Aggregate production cannot serve as a proxy for these. A protective outcome should also not be scored as discovery, consent, or moral legitimacy unless the model independently represents those properties.

A further useful perturbation is to vary whether participant withdrawal actually removes a valuable input. The leverage mechanism predicts different incumbent behavior when it does and when the participant is readily replaceable. The insulation construction predicts no protective response if all relevant channels are removed and direct concern remains absent. Testing that distinction would connect the positive and negative models through a precise causal condition.

Finally, none of these results yet explains invention of the alternative, reliable recognition of previously unknown harms, acquisition of de facto control, joint organization by weak patients, or the emergence of direct concern. These are the next formation questions. The present work advances the unified theory by locating one exact bridge and one exact boundary, rather than announcing that every remaining bridge follows from their existence.
