## Verdict

The preferred architecture delivers a real, useful optimum for an explicit problem. It does not merely advise choosing a model pragmatically: N1 gives a least sufficient partition, N3 gives a sharp dimension bound, and N4 supplies a finite refinement construction. The ordering is openly chosen rather than presented as a law of nature. The document correctly avoids inferring an optimal scientific theory, ethical objective, or causal ontology from these results.

The main outstanding correction concerns **effective computation**, not conceptual architecture. Recursive definability and mathematical finiteness do not, by themselves, imply that the resulting model is executable on a computer. Correct the two phrases below before describing the unrestricted results in those terms. No other blocking conceptual or normative finding was identified.

## Required wording corrections

1. **Section 15, line 322: “yields an executable predictor.”** N2 establishes that the update is well-defined; the supplied response law and equivalence classes can be uncomputable. Prefer “yields a well-defined recursive predictor,” adding effective representation assumptions when execution is claimed. Sections 1 and 5 can continue to discuss executable implementations as a further requirement; they should not imply that N2 alone meets it.

2. **Section 15, line 322: “computable optimal faithful quotient.”** N4's finite refinement is an effective algorithm when its labels, action set, and kernel comparisons are effectively given. Finite rational transition tables suffice. For unrestricted real kernel entries, exact equality is not automatically decidable, even when entries have computable approximations. State the effective-input assumption or describe finite refinement with exact comparison operations as assumed primitives. The constructed rational example is unaffected.

These corrections preserve the achieved optima. They prevent a stronger implementation promise from being attached to a representation theorem.

## Recommended statement to the user

We now have a preferred foundation with proved representational optimality under explicit requirements. The advance is that consequential distinctions determine the state representation, while causal mechanisms explain how alignment's conditions are reproduced and public commitments determine what must be protected. This improves the formulation; it does not prove that the generative theory is the uniquely best account of every system.

The substantive next scientific test remains whether this integration identifies neglected dependencies, makes better predictions, or improves intervention design. That is a test of the generative explanation, distinct from the completed representation theorems.
