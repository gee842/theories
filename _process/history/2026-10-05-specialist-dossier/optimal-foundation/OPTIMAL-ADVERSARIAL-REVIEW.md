## Timing example

The six-class result is correct for the supplied transition system. Active reserve levels are distinguished by one `wait`; at a common reserve, `wait` repeated `r−1` times followed by `dispatch` distinguishes the two delay levels. The badge has no effect. These are executable open-loop test sequences even though the controller does not observe the hidden reserve and delay.

The reported nine faithful partitions are also structurally consistent with the code. For each fixed delay, the two badge values can remain merged at both reserve levels, can split only at reserve two, or can split at both levels. A split at reserve one forces a split at reserve two because `wait` preserves the badge. This gives three possibilities per delay and therefore nine faithful partitions. The unique minimum merges badges at both reserves and both delays. This independent reasoning agrees with the supplied exhaustive enumeration of the 4,140 active-state partitions; it does not require rerunning the calculation.

The only substantive interpretation restriction is already stated in [the macrostep definition](/Users/ren/theories/_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/OPTIMAL-FOUNDATION.md:224): `dispatch` is an indivisible intervention whose retained consequence is timely completion. The example preserves decision-epoch outcomes; it does not preserve elapsed physical time, intermediate communication, interruption opportunities, or travel hazards. For any of those claims, holding-time laws, intermediate states, or an expanded interface would be needed. It likewise does not prove that arbitrary geography reduces to delay; [line 232](/Users/ren/theories/_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/OPTIMAL-FOUNDATION.md:232) correctly excludes that inference.
