# Inherited evidence, model revision, and the limits of a sufficient summary

Mathematical working memo, 5 October 2026. AI-generated research assistance under Ren's research direction. This memo derives small finite examples and conditional information constraints. It introduces no new universal law and implements no experiment. The known-model inquiry and succession calculations remain in [recurrent-inquiry-math.md](recurrent-inquiry-math.md).

## 1. Historical truth does not imply an unchanged prescription

Separate three objects: an observation of what happened, a model relating that observation to future conditions, and a decision using that prediction together with current losses and constraints. The observation can remain true while the latter two change.

This is not a theorem that history or states cannot recur. The binary chains below revisit both states indefinitely with probability one: conditional on any allowed positive switching rate, independent switch events occur infinitely often. A fixed transition law, loss, information state, and feasible action set can also justify reusing the same policy. What is invalid is transferring a policy solely because an old observation remains true, without checking the assumptions connecting it to the current decision.

The new mathematical object is uncertainty about the transition model. Learning which member of a supplied finite family applies is still not unrestricted formation of a new model or purpose. An explicit second counterexample will show why compression sufficient for that finite family can fail after the family is expanded.

## 2. Unknown switching rate: exact likelihood and posterior

Let a fixed but unknown switching rate be

\[
H\in\{h_L,h_H\}=\{.05,.45\},\qquad
\Pr(H=h_H)=\pi\in[0,1].
\tag{1}
\]

Initially \(S_0\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), independently of \(H\). Conditional on \(H=h\), let the switch indicators \(Z_i\) be independent Bernoulli\((h)\) variables and set \(S_i=S_{i-1}\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}Z_i\). Suppose the predecessor has actually observed the consecutive, error-free trajectory

\[
X=(S_0,\ldots,S_N),\qquad
K=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf1\{S_i\ne S_{i-1}\}.
\]

Each observation can be costly to acquire; the likelihood below is conditional on this acquisition having occurred. It does not assume an affordable or optimal observation policy. A particular ordered history has likelihood

\[
\Pr_h(X=x)=\tfrac12 h^{K(x)}(1-h)^{N-K(x)}.
\tag{2}
\]

No binomial coefficient belongs in the likelihood of one ordered history. The distribution of its aggregate switch count has the binomial coefficient; that coefficient cancels in the posterior odds. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Pr(H=h_H\mid X)}{\Pr(H=h_L\mid X)}
=\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}
9^K\left(\frac{11}{19}\right)^{N-K}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

For interior priors write the resulting high-rate posterior as \(w_{K,N}\). If the last observed state is \(s\), the probability of need after another \(d\) transitions is

\[
\boxed{
p_d(s,K,N)=\tfrac12+(s-\tfrac12)
\left[(1-w_{K,N})(.9)^d+w_{K,N}(.1)^d\right].
}
\tag{4}
\]

For one transition after a negative finding,

\[
p_1(0,K,N)=.05+.4w_{K,N}.
\tag{5}
\]

The old observation \(S_N=0\) is not repudiated. Its current implication depends on the evidence about persistence and change.

## 3. Why the last state alone loses model evidence

Because \(S_0\) is uniform, \(S_N=S_0\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(Z_1\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}\cdots\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}Z_N)\) is uniform even conditional on the whole switch sequence and \(H\). Hence

\[
S_N\ \perp\ (H,Z_1,\ldots,Z_N),
\qquad \Pr(H=h_H\mid S_N)=\pi.
\tag{6}
\]

The last state alone carries exactly zero information about which switching rate applies in this acquisition design. An old action instruction computed only from the last state and a fixed assumed rate also carries no more information about \(H\). Such an instruction might additionally discard the identity or age of the observation.

This conclusion changes if the initial distribution depends on \(H\), if measurement times are selected adaptively, if the instruction itself uses other historical evidence, or if visible spending and capacity choices reveal history. It is a statement about the specified channel, not about every object called a conclusion or instruction.

**Sufficiency within the declared family.** The factorization (2) shows that \((K,N)\) preserves the entire likelihood for \(H\). Appending \(S_N\) preserves the terminal state needed to predict the future. Consequently

\[
T(X)=(S_N,K,N)
\tag{7}
\]

gives the same posterior predictions as the full trajectory for every future event generated by this homogeneous Markov family, under the same prior. The same factorization works for a prior over a continuum of homogeneous switching rates. It does not require retaining every raw row to answer these specified questions.

Under known \(h\), only the last state and elapsed time are needed for the same prediction. Under unknown homogeneous \(h\), the transition evidence becomes relevant. The appropriate summary therefore depends on which uncertainty is admitted.

## 4. A smallest paid-inheritance comparison

Take just two historic observations, \(N=1\), equal model priors, and a successor making one decision after one more transition. Treatment costs \(c=.3\), prevents a loss \(L=1\) when need is present, and otherwise has no effect. Without a fresh successor diagnostic, the Bayes operating loss at need probability \(p\) is \(g(p)=\min(.3,p)\).

A historic switch has probability \(.25\), and gives

\[
w_{1,1}=.9,\qquad \mathbb E[H\mid K=1]=.41.
\]

No historic switch has probability \(.75\), and gives

\[
w_{0,1}=\frac{11}{30},\qquad
\mathbb E[H\mid K=0]=\frac{59}{300}\simeq.196667.
\tag{8}
\]

When the final observation is negative, a switched history recommends treatment next period, while an unchanged history recommends restraint. When the final observation is positive, both histories recommend treatment. With only the last state, the switching-rate prior remains unchanged, so a negative predicts need probability \(.25\) and a positive predicts \(.75\).

The exact expected operating losses are

\[
R_{\mathrm{last}}=\tfrac12(.25)+\tfrac12(.3)=.275,
\]

\[
R_{\mathrm{transition}}
=\tfrac12\left[\tfrac34\frac{59}{300}+\tfrac14(.3)\right]
+\tfrac12(.3)=.26125.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus inheriting the extra switch bit yields gross gain

\[
R_{\mathrm{last}}-R_{\mathrm{transition}}
=.01375=\frac{11}{800}.
\tag{10}
\]

For this one future action, an additional acquisition and transmission cost above that gain would defeat the net benefit, absent other uses. If both observations are already acquired, only incremental retention/transmission costs belong to this comparison. Conversely, raw history and the sufficient summary have identical operational value under the homogeneous family, so a cheaper faithful summary dominates a more expensive raw archive for this particular task.

These are fixed-channel comparisons. They assume measurement dates and public traces do not reveal the missing switch bit. An adaptive communication rule or a paid capacity choice might signal that bit through another channel, changing both the likelihood and the optimal cost. Equation (10) must not be transplanted unchanged into such a joint signaling experiment.

## 5. A sufficient old summary can become insufficient after model expansion

Consider the two length-five histories

\[
x_A=00010,\qquad x_B=01000.
\tag{11}
\]

Both end in state zero, have \(N=4\) and \(K=2\), and therefore produce exactly the same likelihood under every homogeneous switching rate. Their switch sequences are respectively \(0011\) and \(1100\).

Now admit two different temporal models with equal prior probability. In \(M_{\uparrow}\), the first two transition rates are .05 and all later rates are .45. In \(M_{\downarrow}\), the first two are .45 and all later rates are .05. Each model has independent transition switches and a uniform initial state. The late regime continues for the successor's next decision.

The likelihood ratio for \(x_A\) is

\[
\frac{\Pr(x_A\mid M_{\uparrow})}{\Pr(x_A\mid M_{\downarrow})}
=\frac{(.95)^2(.45)^2}{(.55)^2(.05)^2}
=\left(\frac{171}{11}\right)^2
=\frac{29241}{121}.
\tag{12}
\]

For \(x_B\), it is the reciprocal. Thus the high-next-rate posterior is \(29241/29362\) after \(x_A\), and \(121/29362\) after \(x_B\). The corresponding need probabilities after their common final negative are approximately .448352 and .051648. At \(c/L=.3\), the optimal next actions are opposite.

The old summary \((S_N,K,N)\) cannot distinguish these histories. Indeed its distribution is identical under these two temporal models: the switch count sums two low-rate and two high-rate Bernoulli variables under either ordering, and the last state remains uniform independently of that count. Conditional on this summary, the two temporal-model probabilities remain equal. The missing order cannot be reconstructed by doing more computation on the same summary.

This is an exact counterexample to model-independent sufficiency. It is not evidence that all raw history must always be stored. An enlarged summary preserving switch counts separately for the early and late segments suffices for this expanded family. A still different future model could require another distinction.

The broad statistical issue is established prior art. [Robert, Cornuet, Marin, and Pillai (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.4432) show that statistics sufficient for parameter inference within candidate models need not preserve the information required for model comparison; their likelihood-factorization argument identifies the missing model-dependent factor. Our finite Markov example is a separate elementary construction illustrating that boundary, not a claim to have discovered it.

## 6. A precise decision criterion for adequate compression

Let \(Y\) be a specified future binary event, \(X\) a finite historical record, and \(T=T(X)\) a retained summary. Under an explicitly declared joint model, write

\[
p_X=\Pr(Y=1\mid X),\qquad
p_T=\Pr(Y=1\mid T)=\mathbb E[p_X\mid T].
\]

For a treatment threshold \(\theta=c/L\), the expected Bayes operating risks obey

\[
\boxed{
L\mathbb E[\min(\theta,p_T)]
\ \geq\ L\mathbb E[\min(\theta,p_X)].
}
\tag{13}
\]

Proof: \(p\mapsto\min(\theta,p)\) is concave; apply conditional Jensen's inequality. Free access to the richer record cannot worsen optimized decisions because the receiver can ignore it. Paid access can still have negative net value.

For finite records, equality for every threshold \(\theta\in[0,1]\) holds if and only if \(p_X\) is a function of \(T\), apart from zero-probability histories. If two positive-probability histories merged by \(T\) have different predictive probabilities, choose a threshold strictly between them. The concave function has its kink inside that conditional range, producing a strict conditional risk gap. This gives a concrete, decision-relative test for whether a compression loses useful predictive content.

No noninjective finite compression can preserve answers to every imaginable historical binary question: two merged histories can be distinguished by a question about which occurred. That elementary information limit does not show that any particular omitted distinction will matter, that full recording is feasible or ethical, or that a record of the past identifies all possible futures. The substantive work is specifying plausible future questions and model revisions, their likelihoods, and the cost of preserving the relevant distinctions.

## 7. Admission of a model is different from updating its probability

In the finite family (1), both transition likelihoods are positive for every finite binary trajectory. If \(\pi=0\), Bayes' rule gives

\[
\Pr(H=h_H\mid X)=0
\quad\text{for every finite history }X.
\tag{14}
\]

Acquiring and preserving more records cannot give positive posterior mass to this excluded atom. Introducing it requires a change in model support or prior, or another explicitly specified revision procedure. That change is not explained by the ordinary posterior update itself. Conversely, a small positive prior allows evidence to shift the probability, although finite evidence may be insufficient to change action.

For \(\pi=\varepsilon>0\), any \(N\)-transition history has high-rate posterior odds at most

\[
\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}9^N.
\tag{15}
\]

After a final negative, treatment at \(c/L=.3\) requires \(w>.625\), or posterior odds above \(5/3\). Therefore

\[
\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}9^N\leq\frac53
\tag{16}
\]

certifies that no such finite history can make treatment strictly optimal under this prior. Equality is indifference. A low prior is not identical to a zero prior, and this finite-evidence bound is not a theorem of permanent closure. Nor does it apply to singleton probabilities under a continuous prior: continuous Bayesian learning can concentrate around a parameter whose individual point initially had zero probability. The support restriction here concerns an excluded member of a finite model family.

## 8. What the joint inquiry-and-succession model must retain

If observations are separated by gaps \(d_i\), the observed transition likelihood is

\[
\Pr_h(S_{t_i}=b\mid S_{t_{i-1}}=a)
=\frac{1+(-1)^{\mathbf1\{a\ne b\}}(1-2h)^{d_i}}{2}.
\tag{17}
\]

The number of observed switches without their gaps is generally insufficient. Failed diagnostics, their reliability, observation dates, and the distinction between unobserved and observed-negative states must remain in the observation model. For complete consecutive histories acquired under a known stopping rule, parameter-independent selection factors can cancel in the likelihood; this does not justify discarding timing or selection information in arbitrary partial records.

More generally, if \(C\) denotes surviving public traces and \(M\) an archive message, the successor must use the joint likelihood. In a fixed-length fully observed example it has the form

\[
\Pr(H=h,S_N=s\mid C,M)
\ \propto\ \Pr(H=h)
\sum_{x:x_N=s}\Pr_h(X=x)\Pr_{\mathrm{policy}}(C,M\mid x).
\tag{18}
\]

The policy kernel includes evidence-dependent capacity decisions, spending or invoices if visible, selective recording, and message failure. If those choices depend on other hidden variables, those variables must also be integrated. A last-state payload plus public trace need not have the same informational value as last state alone. An absent archive is not a generic reset to ignorance.

An optimizing team may intentionally use a feasible capacity choice as a signal as well as a physical investment. That is legitimate in a model permitting this channel, but its value must not all be attributed to physical maintenance. Costs and receiver access determine which such encodings are actually available. The [common-information approach of Nayyar, Mahajan, and Teneketzis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1695) supplies established machinery for policies with shared and private histories.

## 9. Exact joint choice of readiness and evidence publication

The next finite model can join physical readiness and communication without a forgetting shortcut. Fix a retention format \(r\) before the data: public last state only, total switch count, early/late switch counts, or full switch word. Let \(A_r\) be its fixed acquisition-plus-retention cost, paid before the sender's handoff decision. In the \(N=4\) comparison the final state \(Z=S_N\) is always publicly available. The sender sees only \(Z\) and the actually retained statistic \(t\); erased parts of the history are unavailable to it as well as to the successor.

For the model family admitted at handoff, calculate

\[
\mu_r(t\mid z)=\Pr(T_r=t\mid Z=z),\qquad
p_r(z,t)=\Pr(S_{N+1}=1\mid Z=z,T_r=t).
\tag{19}
\]

The sender chooses ready capacity \(u\in\{0,1\}\), costing \(m u\), and whether to publish a truthful complete-statistic payload \(P\in\{0,1\}\), costing \(f_rP\). The successor sees the format, last state, readiness, and payload or silence, and knows the agreed sender prescription. It does not see an additional private invoice or a cached unretained history. Rebuilding cannot serve the current decision, so only \(u=1\) permits a fresh perfect diagnostic at cost \(d\).

There are three possible successor responses: no treatment, treatment, or diagnostic followed by state-contingent treatment. Separate their operating risks from diagnostic expenditure:

\[
R_0(p)=Lp,\qquad R_1(p)=c,\qquad
R_{\mathrm{test}}(p)=cp.
\tag{20}
\]

An episode-total inquiry cap requires, in each realized branch,

\[
A_r+mu+f_rP+d\mathbf1\{a=\mathrm{test}\}\leq B,
\qquad a=\mathrm{test}\Longrightarrow u=1.
\tag{21}
\]

Ordinary treatment is financed separately but remains a cost in (20). This cap differs from the preceding recurrent model's per-period limit and from an expected-spending constraint.

For each public \(z\), there are six possible deterministic silence decoders: the receiver selects either 0 or 1 when \((u,P)=(0,0)\), and selects 0, 1, or test when \((u,P)=(1,0)\). Write one such pair as \(\delta=(\delta_0,\delta_1)\). A published complete statistic reveals \(t\), so its best feasible receiver response can be chosen directly. The exact joint optimum for the fixed format is

\[
\boxed{
V_r=A_r+\sum_z\Pr(Z=z)
\min_{\delta\in\{0,1\}\times\{0,1,\mathrm{test}\}}
\sum_t\mu_r(t\mid z)
\min_{(u,P,a)\in\mathcal F_r(\delta,z,t)}
\left[mu+f_rP+d\mathbf1\{a=\mathrm{test}\}+R_a(p_r(z,t))\right],
}
\tag{22}
\]

where \(\mathcal F_r\) enforces (21), requires \(a=\delta_u\) for silence, and permits any feasible response to published \(t\). An empty feasible set has infinite cost.

This finite minimization is exact because fixing the receiver's silence decoder makes each retained type's sender choice independent of other types: the objective is additive and feasibility is branchwise. Conversely, every deterministic sender/receiver team policy is represented. Private randomization cannot lower the optimum: hold the receiver strategy fixed and replace each randomized sender action by a minimizing deterministic action; then choose minimizing deterministic receiver responses to the resulting observations. Expected cost cannot increase. This argument would need modification for additional cross-type constraints or an expected-budget tradeoff requiring mixtures.

After choosing a prescription, compute the receiver's actual silence posterior using its selected type partition, rather than assigning a belief directly. For example,

\[
\Pr(S_{N+1}=1\mid z,u,P=0)
=\frac{\sum_{t:\sigma_z(t)=(u,0)}\mu_r(t\mid z)p_r(z,t)}
{\sum_{t:\sigma_z(t)=(u,0)}\mu_r(t\mid z)}
\tag{23}
\]

for a positive-probability silent observation. This is a verification condition as well as an interpretation. A globally optimal team decoder must minimize the receiver's feasible conditional loss on every reached observation; otherwise replacing that response would lower the joint objective.

Readiness now has both a physical role and a potential signaling role. An archive-free optimum can therefore be well informed, rather than ignorant. Under the ordered family, however, total switch count and the public last state are independent of the regime orientation. A sender restricted to that information cannot make readiness or silence informative about orientation by manipulating them. The data-processing constraint rules out creating model information from an uninformative retained type. Readiness may still be valuable because it makes a new diagnostic physically available.

The homogeneous and ordered families are analyst-supplied alternatives. If the expanded family is announced only at handoff, comparing earlier fixed formats under that family is an ex post robustness comparison. Calling a format ex ante optimal requires a prior over which later family or question will matter. The exact coordinator benchmark explains feasible information use, not the social emergence of its common objective or update rule.

## 10. Positive-cost certificates and what they do not establish

For \(N=4\), equal priors, one successor decision, \(L=1,c=.3\), and public final state, the last-state-only operating risk is .275 in both families. With the complete statistic and no fresh successor diagnostic, the gross benefit in the homogeneous family is

\[
D_{\mathrm{hom}}
=\tfrac14\left[.15\{1-(.55)^4\}-.25\{1-(.95)^4\}\right]
=\frac{143841}{6400000}=.02247515625.
\tag{24}
\]

Here, after a negative final state, one or more historic switches make treatment optimal; zero switches do not. In the ordered family, the likelihood ratio from timed counts is

\[
\frac{\Pr(K_E,K_L\mid M_{\uparrow})}
{\Pr(K_E,K_L\mid M_{\downarrow})}
=\left(\frac{171}{11}\right)^{K_L-K_E}.
\tag{25}
\]

After a negative final state, treatment is optimal precisely when \(K_L>K_E\). Under \(M_{\uparrow}\), this event has probability \(103797/160000\); under \(M_{\downarrow}\), it has probability \(4917/160000\). Therefore

\[
D_{\mathrm{ordered}}
=\tfrac14\left[.15\frac{103797}{160000}-.25\frac{4917}{160000}\right]
=\frac{143403}{6400000}=.02240671875.
\tag{26}
\]

These give concrete sufficient positive-cost bounds. A feasible policy that acquires and retains timed counts, always publishes them, buys no readiness, and has total incremental expense \(A_r+f_r<D_{\mathrm{ordered}}\) improves on the baseline that uses only the public last state and buys neither publication nor readiness. All prices can be strictly positive. For example, acquisition .004, four retained bits at .002 each, and publication .003 sum to .015, below the bound. This comparison does not claim superiority to the jointly optimal last-only format, which may purchase readiness and a new diagnostic. Nor is it a sharp archive threshold for (22), whose signaling and selective-publication opportunities can reduce costs.

With known \(N=4\), fixed-length binary encoding needs three bits for total count (five possible values), four bits for the timed pair (nine values), and four bits for the raw switch word (sixteen values). Under this exact per-bit pricing, timed counts are not cheaper than the raw word. They have the same inference value for the ordered family, and raw order within each segment is ancillary to its orientation. Thus they should give the same optimum in (22) when all other prices and feasible actions match; extra ancillary detail cannot improve a finite common-interest optimum merely by supplying private randomization. Different encoding conventions or additional future model questions can change that equivalence.

## 11. Compatibility audit of the implemented joint model

The implemented `experiments/revisable-inheritance/model.py` and protocol add two public decisions before the private retained statistic is read. Retention is already paid. After observing \(Z\) and the admitted model family, the caretaker chooses whether to pay for archive inspection \(I\), and whether to establish a message channel \(O\). Both choices precede private inspection and are public. Thus they cannot reveal private type beyond the already public information used to choose them.

For a format with \(b_r\) bits, retention price \(\rho\), inspection price \(k_I\), and channel setup price \(f_0\), the public-stage expenditure is

\[
B_r(I,O)=\rho b_r+k_I I+f_0O.
\tag{27}
\]

Conditional on \(Z=z\), the implementation takes the minimum of (22)'s continuation over permitted \((I,O)\), substitutes \(B_r(I,O)\) for its fixed base cost, and permits a payload only if \(O=1\). When \(I=0\), it replaces the sender's private-type distribution by a single uninformative type with the correctly marginalized \(p(z)\). It does not let an uninspected archive inform readiness or disclosure. The episode cap includes retention, inspection, setup, readiness, payload, and a successor diagnostic. Each is charged even when it turns out not to help; setup is paid on silent branches as well.

Three generating families are evaluated separately: homogeneous, ordered, and their four-model union with uniform prior. For that union, each model's likelihood is still constant within a retained pair \((K_E,K_L)\). Timed counts and raw words therefore have the same posterior predictions and, with the implemented equal prices and feasibility rules, the same joint optimum. Total count can distinguish some mixture components in the expanded family, even though it cannot distinguish the two orientations in the ordered-only family. These two assertions must not be conflated.

The implemented six-decoder search agrees with the finite-team argument above. Its separate brute encoder search checks that result for two-transition cases. It also verifies receiver best responses after recomputing the actual selected silence partitions, checks component costs and branch caps, and conditions published complete types on the appropriate family likelihood. No simulator-supplied true model index enters the sender's or receiver's decision. The supplied family and prior do enter, as the declared optimal-team benchmark.

All five implementation tests were independently rerun successfully. They cover family-relative sufficiency, the same-count opposite-action histories, decoder search versus exhaustive sender-rule enumeration, paid feasibility, and control restrictions. No experiment code was changed by this audit.

**A positive-price case where readiness is purely a signal.** In the ordered-family control with no payloads and no successor diagnostic, take the implementation's \(N=4,L=1,c=.3\), retention .003 per bit, inspection .01, and readiness .005. Retain timed counts or the equally priced raw word. For public \(Z=0\), inspect and buy readiness exactly when \(K_L>K_E\); for public \(Z=1\), do not inspect or buy readiness. The receiver treats on a ready signal at \(Z=0\), and always treats at \(Z=1\).

Under the equal model mixture,

\[
\Pr(K_L>K_E\mid Z=0)
=\tfrac12\frac{103797+4917}{160000}=.33973125.
\tag{28}
\]

The overall readiness probability is therefore .169865625. The policy reproduces the full-information operating risk .25259328125 without any payload or diagnostic, at total expected cost

\[
.25259328125+.012+.005+.005(.169865625)
=.270442609375<.275.
\tag{29}
\]

Here .012 is four-bit retention, .005 is inspection on half the cases, and the final term is paid readiness. With a nonbinding branch cap this is also optimal in that control: positive differences \(K_L-K_E\) give need probability at least .425824, exceeding treatment plus readiness cost .305; nonpositive differences give probability at most .25. The alternative signaling polarity sends the more frequent class and costs more, while constant decoders discard the useful distinction. At \(Z=1\), all types recommend treatment and inspection cannot help.

This mechanism depends on the receiver seeing readiness and knowing the agreed prescription. Its improvement is information conveyed by a paid physical choice, not the option value of actually using the capability. The distinction is essential when relating this joint model to the earlier example of maintaining inquiry for a later diagnostic. If only total count is retained in the ordered family, no such signal can recover the erased orientation.

The central result is a conditional design constraint: preserve enough evidence to support the revisions the institution claims to permit, or explicitly acknowledge the questions its compression can no longer answer. A posterior under a supplied family, admission of another family, acquisition of new observations, and legitimate revision of purposes are separate operations. The finite examples and joint team formulation clarify their boundaries; they do not combine them into a universal model of becoming or an alignment solution.
