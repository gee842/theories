# Joint formation without hidden benevolence

This analytical memo preceded the completed [joint construction](JOINT-FORMATION.md). Its ally examples are exploratory proposals, not the parameter ledger of the subsequently chosen game. The negative results and acceptance conditions guided that construction.

5 October 2026. Independent conceptual and mathematical proposal for Development 2. The target is a conditional joint account of acquired concern, reliable evidence, and effective protection of a patient who supplies no valuable input. This note critiques the premises needed to solve that target; it is not itself a completed dynamic study or a novelty claim. The latest `CONCERN-AND-CORRECTION.md` bridge has been read.

## The exact question worth solving now

A tractable question is:

> Given a declared material and cognitive repertoire, can initially unconcerned actors choose and finance the learning, evidence, and control arrangements that subsequently protect a noncontributing patient, while a strategic operator can interfere and each successor must actually rebuild or inherit the relevant capacities?

“Given a repertoire” means supplying possible learning updates, available work, observable signals, and physically possible interventions. This is legitimate model specification, like specifying reaction laws. It must not mean supplying the desired actions, their funding schedule, truthful reports, benevolent allocation, or guaranteed survival. Within that repertoire, entry, evidence purchase, control construction, protective action, attack, renewal, and succession must be actual choices or physically forced transitions.

A positive answer requires a single sequential process with both resource and incentive inequalities. A negative parameter region must be allowed to prevent formation. If the positive region is nonempty and a strategy/certificate covers every relevant continuation, this conditional question is solved. It should not be dismissed as unfinished merely because a physical repertoire remains supplied. Conversely, independent success of several prescribed schedules does not answer it.

## Obstruction 1: an unfunded and unrewarded bootstrap cannot select itself

Consider a finite horizon, or an infinite discounted horizon with bounded returns and 0<delta<1. An actor begins with no other-directed valuation. Suppose a baseline policy obtains private returns p_t, and any policy that creates concern, audits, or institutional capacity:

1. incurs a nonnegative private expense k_t, with positive expense on at least one positively weighted date;
2. cannot increase any private return or remove a privately valued loss;
3. receives no subsidy, enforceable contribution, reciprocal benefit, or other initially valued consequence.

Under the actor's initial evaluation, which assigns zero weight to the patient's condition, any such policy has value

\[
\sum_t\delta^t(p_t-k_t)<\sum_t\delta^tp_t.
\]

Therefore no optimizing initial actor chooses the costly bootstrap. This is an elementary dominance theorem. Announcing that the trained actor will later care is not an additional private return in this theorem.

If choices are evaluated by future, changed coefficients instead, the preference convention must be stated. For the particular criterion p_t-k_t-v_tH_t, with nonnegative v_t,H_t and zero baseline concern, the same no-bootstrap conclusion holds: acquiring a harm penalty cannot outperform the costless zero-penalty baseline when private returns are unchanged. Other conventions can yield different results. Adding a coefficient-dependent constant to a utility representation preserves one-date choices but can change ex ante preferences over acquiring that coefficient. Thus state-dependent utility normalization cannot be silently used to manufacture motivation to learn concern.

A valid positive mechanism must break a premise: privately useful apprenticeship, costly ignorance about one's own exposure, private performance benefits, a committed donor with existing concern, a subsidized institution, or an explicitly modeled coalition can do so. These are different mechanisms and should not be grouped under a single mysterious emergence force.

## Obstruction 2: self-only evidence cannot identify other-directed routing

Let two evaluation architectures have identical self-task predictions, learning observations, training expenses, private consequences, and allowed interventions on all histories available to the selecting actor. One routes the resulting value to corresponding other-patient features; the other inserts an identity gate and routes those features to zero. Suppose the selector's criterion depends only on the stipulated identical histories and costs.

Then its evidence and criterion assign the same score to both architectures. No such selector can uniquely prefer shared routing on those grounds. Arbitrarily many repetitions of the same self-only evidence do not change this equivalence. An identity-blind tie rule can favor sharing, but the rule is then an additional premise; it is not evidence identifying the moral truth of the shared architecture.

A positive result needs a discriminating ground: a demonstrated computational cost of gating, private performance under a self-relevant transfer distribution, a structural constraint of the implementation, or other evidence admitted by the selector. The common feature map can be selected for self reasons and then transfer evaluation to another. The transfer's accuracy on the other remains a claim to validate. Moral standing does not depend on the learner's feature similarity or chosen architecture.

In particular, comparing a shared predictor with a context-gated predictor is insufficient if a third candidate can preserve the same successful self predictor while gating only other-directed evaluation at no additional cost. The architecture menu must include that adversarial alternative or clearly state why the specified implementation cannot realize it cheaply.

## Obstruction 3: paying for observations does not make them informative

Take two still-possible physical states theta_0 and theta_1. Suppose every affordable observation and every admitted paid audit produces the same history distribution in the two states, while the actions meeting the patient's protection requirement have disjoint sets. Any history-based policy has the same action distribution in both states. Its support cannot lie in both disjoint safe sets. Hence no policy can guarantee protection in both.

This is the familiar information obstruction applied to an institution's critic. It survives perfect computation, sincere concern, and an audit budget. Payment supplies an observation action, not its discriminating content.

If a strategic operator can forge every affordable observation at sufficiently low cost, reliability also needs a strategic condition. A protected critic must have a route whose signals actually depend on the relevant patient outcome and whose attack consequences are covered in the game. It is legitimate to specify a physical sensor with exact response under declared conditions. It is not legitimate to assume that a bill labeled “audit” reveals truth regardless of capture.

For an exact finite model, one clean option is a paid sensor with an explicit tampering action and price. If one attack can save the operator at most G in continuation value, an attack price tau>G makes that attack strictly unprofitable, assuming no other benefit and sufficient prefunding for all available actions. This is a conditional deterrence result, not proof that the sensor is invulnerable or that any price can be chosen by decree. Sensor construction and the source of that price need physical implementation premises.

## A minimal positive route: privately useful learning creates an ally

The most economical extension introduces a third-party patient and an ally who owns a rival productive input. The patient owns no such input, supplies no repayment, cannot threaten, and changes no material production parameter. The ally's support can therefore matter without giving the patient its own leverage.

A self-relevant apprenticeship first creates two products: a private capability with actual return G, and a shared evaluative feature learned from primitive self valence and independently supported correspondence. The learned coefficient begins at zero. Paid learning produces q=vH for the patient's harm. This does not mean that primitive valence, informative evidence, or the shared feature architecture arise from nothing; their acquisition choices and resource costs must be visible.

A gate preserving self competence while suppressing other-directed transfer must be included if it is physically available. Let its additional implementation expense be D. Let the total finite-horizon material cost of the shared architecture's subsequent ally behavior be at most L. If D>L, the gate is materially more expensive even before the actor acquires concern. If apprenticeship costs A and G>A+L, a sophisticated initially self-interested actor can prefer the apprenticeship while anticipating the later cost of its changed preferences. These strict inequalities describe a nonempty conditional route to selection of shared learning; they do not establish that gates usually cost that much. If cheap gating reverses the comparison, that adverse region must remain in the results.

This structure handles a subtle issue: scheduled self-training can be privately selected at zero concern, whereas patient-specific teaching and evidence bought solely for another cannot be assumed privately attractive before any evaluative route exists. Patient alignment evidence must either be part of the privately useful transfer task, already available as a paid byproduct, or separately justified at the date it is purchased.

### Exact ally response thresholds

Suppose both safe and harmful joint production give the ally the same cash receipt b. Only the third-party patient suffers H under harmful production. With an active alternative, the ally receives o if it withdraws; without it, the fallback is o0, where o0<o<b. Withdrawal prevents this particular production-caused patient harm. The ally's one-period evaluations are:

- safe cooperation: b;
- harmful cooperation: b-q;
- active withdrawal: o;
- blocked or inactive withdrawal: o0.

For

\[
\boxed{b-o<q<b-o_0,}
\]

the ally cooperates after a safeguard, refuses unblocked harmful production, and accepts harmful production after its alternative is blocked. This restores the formation model's response pattern through other-directed valuation, rather than through an injury debit in the ally's own cash.

The purely private operator therefore again compares r-c, r-chi, and zero. It uniquely safeguards when c<min(r,chi). The ally's active safe payoff, after upkeep m, exceeds inactive harmful payoff by q-m. For T stationary episodes, no discount, unchanged q, and a guaranteed safeguard response, construction has incremental evaluated value T(q-m)-K. Actual opening cash must nevertheless cover K+m+ell and every later cost prefix. Concern cannot pay a bill.

This is protection of a patient with no own leverage, through an ally whose learned valuation motivates use of the ally's leverage. It is not a claim that leverage has ceased to matter in the mechanism, nor a claim that leverage gives the patient standing.

Crucially, harmful production still pays the ally b. A blockade therefore does not automatically reduce the ally's cash from b to x as in the original personally harmed-participant game. The original W_res financing-attack theorem cannot be copied into this third-party model. Attacks must instead explicitly impair paid evidence, support resources, the ally's control, or subsequent learning, and their continuations must be solved anew.

### A transparent bootstrap witness

For a small candidate instance, use b=5, o=4, o0=1, q=3, m=K=1, ell=2, and two operating episodes. The ally response interval holds: 1<3<4. With r=4, c=1, and chi=3/2, a fresh operator safeguards. The ally's evaluated operating benefit net of construction is 2(3-1)-1=3. Its total extra material expenditure on the institution is K+2m=3.

An apprenticeship with private return G=5, expense A=1, and an available identity gate costing D=4 therefore has these initial private comparisons, provided the cash prefixes and timing are executable: shared learning followed by anticipated ally expenditures yields private gain 5-1-3=1; gated learning yields 5-1-4=0; declining yields zero. The shared route is strictly selected on these stated initial preferences.

This witness is not the finished joint theorem. It supplies a testable core whose missing transitions are explicit: how the paid course realizes q, how efficacy evidence is obtained and challenged, how the operator can attack either course or institution, and what occurs at replacement. The completed model must include those transitions and recompute the resulting preferences; it must not preserve these provisional payoffs if an added attack changes them.

## Capture and succession are continuation choices

A reliable initial critic is insufficient when the operator can later buy its disappearance. Every attack changing the future evidence, funding, learned coefficient, or action repertoire must appear before those future benefits are credited. Its profitability includes the avoided future safeguards, not merely the current protection cost. The earlier persistent-operator result supplies exactly this warning.

Likewise, a learner retaining a positive q is not an inherited institution. The next ally may need resources, paid records, preserved correspondence, sensor access, control of a rival input, and incentive to use them. Two legitimate mechanisms differ:

1. Paid transfer preserves enough learned state and capability for the first successor decision, with a voluntary or enforced transfer choice actually modeled.
2. A new ally independently reconstructs them because the same private apprenticeship opportunity recurs. This can be genuine regeneration through replacement, but it requires resources and time for reconstruction and cannot protect a patient during an unpaid or unprotected gap by assumption.

Neither route requires explaining the ultimate origin of every physical regularity before proving a conditional continuation theorem. Both require the actual first successor bill, decision rule, and vulnerability interval. A departing ally's gratuitous bequest should not be hidden in a transfer variable.

## Acceptance conditions for a completed Development 2

A conditional solution should exhibit a fully specified process and at least one strictly positive region where all the following hold together:

- The first enabling contribution is selected while the learned concern coefficient is zero.
- The competing identity-gated architecture is represented or excluded by an explicit physical restriction; its cost and private advantage are compared.
- Learning produces a consequential coefficient from declared data, rather than renaming an initially positive fixed moral weight.
- Evidence has a causal identification route, a real bill, and explicit capture options; operating outcomes and audit burdens are separately recorded.
- At least one patient without its own productive leverage becomes protected because an ally chooses to contribute or act.
- The strategic operator's best responses include attack and concealment where feasible; no one-period rule is silently repeated for a persistent actor.
- All learning, control, audit, operation, attack, and succession costs draw on the actual shared resources at their proper dates.
- Replacement delivers either maintained capacity or a financed reconstruction path, with any protection gap counted.
- Counterregions show failures of entry, recognition, evidence, control, or continuation under the same model.

This list defines the question to finish, not additional universal requirements that can be introduced after success. If these conditions are fulfilled, the declared joint-formation problem has a conditional solution. The universality, natural prevalence, ethical adequacy, and empirical explanatory advantage of that solution remain different claims. Reporting those limits is appropriate; treating them as grounds to deny the achieved conditional result would move the destination after the work.

## Implication for the unified foundation

The deeper object is not an ever-expanding list of benevolent capacities. It is the selection and renewal of the relations that make affected consequences capable of changing action. An initially self-useful activity can construct those relations, but the same activity may also select a gate, a misleading signal, or a cheaper route around the patient. The theory becomes explanatory when it derives which of those routes is chosen and maintained from explicit circumstances.

The ethical claim remains independent: a patient's harm can supply a reason even where none of these mechanisms forms. The proposed model explains one way that reason can become effective in a process. It must not identify the failure to acquire concern with the absence of anything worth caring about.
