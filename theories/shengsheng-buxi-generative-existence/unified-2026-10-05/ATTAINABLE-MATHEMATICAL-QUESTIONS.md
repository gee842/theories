This is a conditional mathematical result about an existing apparatus, not a new learner or a completed explanation of criterion formation.

**The new result:** each of two questions can be answered exactly by a feasible inquiry while no feasible inquiry guarantees their combined answer. Consequently, the available inquiries need not have a single most informative member. This strengthens the [preceding identification result](INQUIRY-AND-MATHEMATICAL-IDENTITY.md) into a constraint on a proposed rule: “first acquire as much information as possible, then decide which differences matter.” Sometimes there is no strongest available answer to acquire first.

## One history supplies the obstruction

Keep the original access law, including its consumed source packet. After the sensor readings, the retained alternatives have three distinguishable initial behaviors:

- **A:** the transfer channel is open and readiness follows the learned output.
- **B:** it is open and readiness reverses the learned output.
- **C:** it is blocked. Two underlying parameter configurations, differing in readiness and sensor reversals, remain behaviorally identical under this interface.

The known sensor parity and reset condition are held fixed. Ordinary sensor histories do not separate these alternatives. The informative action is a probe at a selected learned phase:

| Initial behavior | Probe at learned output 1 | Probe at learned output 0 |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 1 | 0 |
| B | 0 | 1 |
| C | 0 | 0 |

Each probe consumes the sole source packet, whether its result is positive or negative. Phase reset does not replenish it. The table describes alternative first experiments, not two readings available from one actual packet.

The first experiment answers **“was the initial behavior A?”** exactly; the second answers **“was it B?”** exactly. Their joint answer would identify A, B or C. But after a negative first result, two initial behaviors remain possible and produce the same remaining native observations. No adaptive continuation can recover the missing answer under the unchanged law. The earlier proof covers arbitrary observation-dependent strategies, not only a fixed two-command schedule.

This concerns initial identity. A changed current configuration can have a settled future behavior even while its initial cause remains unknown. Replacing the initial question with that current-state question would change the claim being certified.

The mathematical calculation grants sufficient ordinary operating support, complete retention, computation and knowledge of the admitted family. These grants remove possible obstructions while preserving packet consumption. Thus the impossibility applies also to the actual apparatus. Positive attainability in this more permissive calculation does not establish that an installed native controller can afford and retain the answer. The saved original 36 retained probe results and 12 retention failures remain unchanged.

## The exact information ordering

A question partitions the admitted initial labels according to its possible exact answers. Say that question P is no finer than Q when Q's answer determines P's answer. A combined question records both answers; its partition is their common refinement, written P∨Q.

Under admissible answer projection, attainable questions are **downward closed**: an inquiry that distinguishes more can answer a coarser question. But effectful inquiry need not allow combination of two separately attainable questions. In the one-packet case the exact family, on A/B/C, is:

\[
\{\{ABC\},\ \{A\mid BC\},\ \{B\mid AC\}\}.
\]

The last two questions are incomparable. Their common refinement is \(\{A\mid B\mid C\}\), which is not attainable. There is no greatest member of this family. Even **“was it blocked?”**, the partition \(\{AB\mid C\}\), cannot be guaranteed with one packet, despite being a binary question.

The [proofs](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/INQUIRY-CRITERION-MATH.md) establish the general implication: if two attainable questions have an unattainable common refinement, no attainable question can dominate all attainable questions. For finite target sets the converse also holds: a nonempty downward-closed attainable family has a greatest member exactly when it is closed under pairwise common refinement. “Maximal” and “greatest” differ here: several questions can each admit no feasible strict improvement while none determines all the others.

These statements describe exact information, not a numerical reward, an expected value or a duty to investigate. They assume that projecting a retained answer is permitted; they do not give an unfunded physical decoder for free in a stronger implementation claim.

## When combined inquiry becomes possible

If an actual restoration procedure preserves the initial target and the first result, and makes the next experiment commonly executable with its full support and retention conditions, the inquiries can be composed. Their retained answers then determine the common refinement. For a finite target set, closure under this composition yields a greatest attainable partition.

The conditions carry the explanatory work. A reset that restores only the phase does not restore the original source. A repeatable observation that erases the first answer also fails the required composition. Separate affordability from the original start does not establish that both experiments can be completed in one continuing history.

A hypothetical initial stock of two packets does suffice in this particular abstraction. It admits all five coarsenings of A/B/C identity and has that identity as its greatest attainable question. Neither packet count distinguishes the two blocked parameter configurations. This is a changed resource condition, not renewal discovered in the original history.

## The timing of relevance matters

Suppose the later demand is either “initial A?” or “initial B?”. If the demand is available before spending the packet, its corresponding experiment answers it. If an experiment has already consumed the packet before the demand arrives, a negative outcome can leave the other demand undecidable. The quantifiers differ:

\[
\forall d\;\exists\pi_d\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\exists\pi\;\forall d.
\]

Here the left side selects an inquiry after knowing the demand; the right side requires a prior inquiry whose retained result supports either later demand. The negative result has this precise event ordering. If waiting for the demand while retaining the packet is allowed, waiting and then selecting the appropriate inquiry works in the abstraction. No deadline, passive loss or obligation to probe is supplied by the source, so none is inferred.

This does not challenge logical conjunction. Once two correct answers have actually been obtained and retained, their conjunction remains available at the stated decoding scope. What fails is combining **separate possibilities of acquisition** into one executable acquisition history. The target truths are not made contradictory by the inquiry constraint.

## What this changes in the generative account

An inquiry is a part of the activity it investigates. In this example, generating evidence also removes a condition of generating other evidence. The separation between an initial target, a contact, an acquired record and a future question preserves these different roles within one law-governed history; it does not posit fundamentally independent knowing and world.

The result rules out a general explanation of criterion formation that assumes there is always one greatest feasible information state, obtainable before any relation to continuing activity matters. It does **not** prove that relevance must be externally assigned or that all choices are equally grounded. A concrete continuation may depend on one distinction and not the other. Its actual dependency can favor an inquiry without introducing a universal scalar objective. That connection must be identified and shown to influence the operative arrangement.

Existing positive results remain credited. [Abstraction acquisition](ABSTRACTION-ACQUISITION.md) already uses prediction failure to form a distinguishing suffix and change a represented partition; its all-future equivalence contract remains supplied. [Method revision](METHOD-REVISION.md) changes a candidate generator and the duration of inquiry under its singleton rule. The separate [inquiry-revision construction](INQUIRY-REVISION.md) makes acquired retained consequences change an actual encounter requirement. The present result does not replace these mechanisms or declare criterion revision wholly missing. It specifies a constraint on extending them to competing consequential questions.

The next stronger connection is **an independently identified consequence for continuing activity → acquired change in which of several attainable distinctions the procedure requires → subsequent inquiry and use governed by that change**. A discriminating test must separate this change from ordinary elimination of candidate models: where possible, hold the accessible candidate information fixed and compare the operative criterion and resulting inquiry. Preserve misleading evidence, interrupted implementation, demands arriving too late and alternatives whose joint acquisition is impossible. No endless hierarchy of controllers is required by this statement; the causal connection may be realized within an existing organization.

The current [conditional universality target](GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md#minimal-grounding-and-universal-scope) asks what follows for every admitted process under independently stated law properties. This result contributes such a conditional constraint. It neither establishes a uniquely necessary root nor proves that the root uniquely constitutes every mathematical truth. That stronger metaphysical claim remains separate from the active conditional programme.
