## 1. Plurality changes the earlier question

The earlier impossibility theorem concerns the information and effective actions available at a particular correction interface. It does not require there to be only one physical system. If the combined observations of several systems distinguish previously indistinguishable continuations, its adverse premise no longer holds. If a contained trial changes what can be known before a larger commitment, the original decision interface has changed.

The new Lean example makes this positive claim explicit. The effective command polarity depends on two Boolean conditions. One observation reveals the first, another reveals the second. Either observation alone leaves an incompatible pair of commands unresolved; combining them gives a correct command for every admitted configuration. The gain comes from their complementary relationship. Statistical independence is not required for this deterministic result.

The opposite control also matters. An arbitrary number of copies reporting only the first condition still leaves the second unknown. The proof covers every natural-number count. Multiplying observers need not multiply the distinctions available to the correction process.

This advances the one-process account: knowing becomes distributed across differentiated activity, and communication can form an effective distinction that no local position previously had. Communication availability, accurate uptake and compatible timing remain substantive requirements; a tuple in a formal model is not a proof that real participants can exchange the relevant information.

## 2. When trials elsewhere can teach us about this case

A pilot can reveal a command-decoding relationship without initially imposing the command on the target. Lean checks a successful transfer when the tested pilot and target share that relationship. It also checks failure when the target's relationship changes. The equality in that small theorem is an explicit transfer condition; its real warrant must come from construction, calibration or supported causal invariance.

A useful trial therefore needs three connected grounds: what its result discriminates, why that result bears on the target, and how its consequences remain within the claimed scope. These grounds need not be absolute isolation or perfect similarity. A known difference can be modeled and compensated; bounded dependence can support a bounded claim. An unknown difference cannot be erased by calling the sites samples of the same system type.

The literature already provides a strong comparison. Peters, Bühlmann and Meinshausen use variation across experimental settings to identify relationships under stated invariance assumptions. Their results support investigating transferable mechanisms while making clear that multiple settings do not by themselves establish invariance. This chapter uses a much smaller exact example, not a replication or extension of their statistical method. [Primary paper, introduction and §1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.01332).

The ability to sustain useful trials is itself a capacity of the joint organization: it requires preserving sites, evidence routes and alternatives through which comparison can change subsequent action. Experimental plurality can be formed, maintained or lost. This is an AI-authored explanatory inference from the specified relations, not an additional universal theorem.

Containment is a different relation from transfer. The formal installation has pilot activity, main activity and shared power. A pilot-local test preserves main availability; a test that removes shared power disables the main activity too. Both occur within one joint installation. A test may be informative yet uncontained, or contained yet uninformative about the target. Those possibilities should not be collapsed into a single label such as “independent experiment.”

## 3. Several samples can be one source of error

In the exact probability construction, each of three readings has error probability `p`. Under the supplied independent product law, majority error is

`3p² − 2p³`.

Lean proves this is less than `p` when `0 < p < 1/2`. At `p = 0.1`, it is `0.028`. When all three errors are the same shared event, majority error remains `p`, or `0.1` in the example. This is a difference between joint distributions with the same individual error rates; counting devices or measuring their separate accuracies does not resolve it.

A further model lets a common event force all readings wrong with probability `c`; in its absence, the independent experiment applies. Error is then

`c + (1 − c)(3p² − 2p³)`.

The checked lower bound is `c`. With `c = p = 0.1`, the value is `0.1252`; here `p` is the conditional error probability when the common event is absent, not the unconditional individual error rate. The analogous floor holds for any number of devices if the same mixture structure and nonnegative residual error are supplied. Increasing the population can reduce a residual term without removing the common event.

These formulas are conditional mathematical results. No actual AI error independence, common-event rate or effective sample size has been measured here. They show why sample plurality and common-failure unity must be assessed for the particular error process.

## 4. When a group can count as one system

There is no contradiction in being one organized group and several distinguishable subsystems. The [existing individuation account](SYSTEM-INDIVIDUATION.md) identifies systems through operative relationships, and the [warrant account](SYSTEM-WARRANT.md) explains how actual participation and admissible variation are established. This continuation applies those results to correction and trials rather than reopening their completed explanatory work.

A substantive claim of unity should identify **what the group does together, which relations realize that capacity, and which distinctions must remain visible under the interventions being considered**. A jointly realized capacity can justify a group-level description. Merely naming a collection supplies a mathematical aggregate, but does not establish an organized unity. Conversely, needing continuing support does not disqualify a real open system.

| Question | What makes a grouping adequate for that question? | What does not follow automatically? |
|---|---|---|
| Can we describe its dynamics as one process? | The joint law retains relevant interactions and explicitly represented inputs over the chosen scope and timescale. | That each component has an autonomous law. |
| Can a simpler parent summary replace the detailed state? | States with the same summary have corresponding relevant consequences under the admitted operations. | That a summary adequate for ordinary operation remains adequate for every intervention. |
| Do these sites supply multiple informative trials? | Their records discriminate relevant alternatives and have a supported relationship to the target. | Independent errors, transferable conclusions or contained consequences. |
| Do these sites constitute one correction dependency? | Shared support, communication or command constraints affect whether corrections can succeed together. | One central controller is necessary or entitled to rule them. |

These are accountable analytical distinctions within the same unfolding. A group can be jointly organized, statistically dependent and locally controllable in different degrees. Boundaries may be nested, overlapping or temporary. Shared infrastructure can participate in several organizations; forcing all such relationships into a unique tree can hide the very dependency being studied.

Simon's near-decomposability analysis provides a prior account of why some subsystem descriptions work over one timescale while longer-term behavior retains wider dependence. It supports timescale-sensitive analysis, not a universal sharp cut or an assertion that every group is nearly decomposable. [Original discussion, “Nearly Decomposable Systems”](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~ztatlock/599z-17sp/papers/complexity-simon-62.pdf).

## 5. How a subsystem relates to its parent

A parent description gathers a joint organization, including relations that are absent from a description of one child alone. A child contributes to that joint organization and may depend on support organized through it. Neither direction need be exclusive: a child can have substantial local regulation while participating in parent-level constraints.

The checked intervention example uses two subsystems and the parent summary “both have stopped.” This summary correctly predicts the result of stopping both. It cannot, on its own, predict what happens when only the left subsystem is stopped: the result depends on whether the right one was already stopped. Two detailed configurations have the same initial parent summary and different intervention outcomes. Retaining the sibling's status resolves the ambiguity.

Thus the parent can be a legitimate system while a particular parent summary is inadequate. The failed summary is not evidence that the two children are unrelated, that the parent is unreal, or that every parent requires a complete microscopic description. It identifies a specific missing distinction for a specific intervention. Exact parent/child causal descriptions must respect intervention effects; this requirement is already developed in causal abstraction research. [Rubenstein et al., definition 3 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.00819).

Correction also has a composition requirement. The formal children have different command polarities. Each can be corrected individually, but one shared broadcast bit cannot correct both. Separately addressable commands can. A generic Lean lemma makes joint command availability and the bridge to the parent outcome explicit; it does not derive those conditions from separate local successes.

Even correct translations can fail together if their shared power is lost. Restoring that parent-level support restores delivery in the model. The parent is therefore neither an extra mysterious cause nor merely a list of children: the larger description retains their operative coupling and support.

No ethical rank follows from nesting. A larger system's persistence does not automatically override losses among its constituents, and being a subsystem does not establish an unlimited duty to support the parent. Those judgments require the already developed [practical principles](PRACTICAL-JUDGMENT.md), including legitimate transformation and ending.

## 6. One shared global continuation still permits learning

Even if we treat humanity's connected activity as one global continuation, that does not make every local observation one statistical sample. Local preparation, intervention, comparison, historical variation and natural experiments can occur within it. Their evidence may support a particular relationship without requiring another complete civilization as a control.

Ren's concern remains important at a different scale. A local test does not reproduce every consequence of a globally shared intervention, and a common failure can remove the sites needed for later learning. The relevant distinction is therefore between **variation whose consequences and transfer can be investigated within the continuing whole** and a claim about a whole-level change for which that correspondence has not been established. We have not measured or proved that the contemporary world is exactly one unit for every such question.

The new shared-capacity example makes the limit concrete. Two installations have capacities one and two. Both pass every singleton demand test. When both subsystems demand service together, the first fails and the second succeeds. The same successful local trials therefore support different joint predictions. The outcome comes from an explicit load/capacity law, not a freely changed success predicate. Measuring the shared capacity or establishing another reliable bound could resolve it; merely repeating singleton trials cannot distinguish these two laws.

This credits the existing [joint-realization results](JOINT-FORMATION.md): separate feasibility was already distinguished from joint feasibility. The new use concerns what local trials establish about a parent-level intervention.

## 7. The wall reached in the plural-system round

Plural systems do improve what can be learned and corrected when their relationship supplies complementary information, transferable responses, contained trials or jointly feasible alternatives. Those constructive cases are now formally checked. The previous adverse class does not cover them unchanged.

The remaining wall is **warranting the joint relationships at the scale of the intended intervention**. Successful local trials alone do not establish absence of a common failure, pilot-to-target invariance, containment of trial consequences, or compatibility of all local corrections. The explicit paired-capacity example demonstrates one such identification failure. It is a scoped limit on the evidence, not a theorem that these relations can never be known.

To cross it for a real case, the investigation needs an identified joint mechanism and evidence that discriminates the relevant alternatives: for example, an observed shared resource bound, an inspected command-routing relation, or interventions that vary the suspected common dependency without consuming the conditions needed to learn. No concrete global mechanism or deployment is specified here, so claiming a universal grouping rule or a guaranteed safe global trial would go beyond the evidence.

The contribution of this round is a connected, checked answer to Ren's new direction: **one whole can sustain experimentally useful plurality, and the adequacy of parent/subsystem descriptions must be tested against both their dependencies and the interventions they are used to justify.** Independent mathematical novelty and unrestricted AI corrigibility remain unestablished; established causal, reliability and systems methods retain credit.

## 8. Start with relations and derive the correction boundary

Ren's follow-up asks us to think from the relational side. Begin with ongoing activity and the conditions through which its consequences become possible. A command, a decoder, a request and a support route are differentiated participations in that activity. Their roles depend on the relationships actually maintained. We then ask which portion can be described together without losing what matters to a proposed correction.

**可纠正性不能只归给一个孤立的部件。** 它通过辨认、请求、接续、作用及共同支持的关系实现。某个部分保持原样，关系变化也能使纠正变得可能或不可能。父系统不是另加在这些关系之上的统治者；它的描述应保留共同活动怎样实现、怎样影响各参与处，以及怎样能够被修订。

For a specified feasibility question, write `R(i,b)` for the admitted joint realization of retained internal detail `i` and boundary conditions `b`. These are two descriptions within one process. The boundary must include every crossing dependence consequential to this question; it is not assumed to be a physical membrane. Eliminating internal detail gives the interface

`B_R(b) ↔ there exists i such that R(i,b)`.

The new Lean result establishes an exact criterion: **two descriptions support the same feasible combinations with every context expressed solely through `b` if and only if their boundary relations agree.** Inclusion of boundary possibilities similarly characterizes preservation of all such feasible combinations in one direction. The converse uses contexts that select an individual boundary assignment; these are mathematical tests, not a claim that every such test is physically available.

This provides an accountable sense in which a group can be treated as one interface. It does not establish that every arbitrary collection is an actual organized system. That stronger identification still requires formation, continuing participation and effects, as in [system warrant](SYSTEM-WARRANT.md). Nor does it establish a unique boundary. Different questions can retain different relations, and the same support activity can cross several group descriptions.

Composition is checked by joining relations on shared boundary values. Regrouping three such relations preserves the resulting relation, and replacing one region by an equal boundary relation preserves feasible coupling to a remaining region. Here a parent description is obtained by retaining the consequential joint interface of its participants. The algebra assumes that this interface accounts for the crossing dependencies. It does not make an omitted dependency disappear.

Joint interface information is essential. A region admitting only `(false,false)` and `(true,true)` permits either value at either position. Summarizing those positions separately loses their equality requirement and falsely admits an unequal pair. Thus an adequate parent interface may need a relation among several boundary values, rather than one success bit or a list of local possibilities.

## 9. A relation can be repaired without replacing the participants

Consider command values coupled around a closed chain. Each connection either preserves the bit or flips it. Every connection accepts either incoming value and has a unique corresponding outgoing value. Nevertheless, **the whole cycle has a consistent assignment exactly when the number of flips has even parity**. Lean proves this for every finite list of connections and either requested root value, by relating joint assignments to propagation through the list.

The smallest displayed obstruction is `a=b`, `b=c`, `c≠a`. Every individual relation has full projections onto its two positions, but there is no joint assignment. Changing one operative translation restores compatibility. Appending a converter whose polarity matches the composed parity restores both possible root assignments for an arbitrary finite chain. The converter is a change to the realized connection, not a renaming of an unchanged relation. Its availability, support and permission remain explicit application requirements.

There is a further constructive gain: **within this translation class, an end-to-end probe starting with `false` returns the entire composed parity**. That one observed value determines the chain's response to either input; it does not identify every internal connection. Lean proves that equal probe results are equivalent to equal complete endpoint relations in this class. A converter selected from the current probe therefore restores the required closed-chain compatibility. A retained probe from an earlier configuration can fail after a connection changes.

This advances the previous evidence wall. Knowing every internal detail is not a general prerequisite for a justified group-level correction; a warranted composed relation can suffice. The proof supplies an explicit sufficient observation for one structural class. It does not say that one probe identifies an arbitrary nonlinear, stateful or adversarial installation. The path recursion solves simultaneous compatibility equations; it is not a physical timing model or a stability theorem. A real probe also needs a permitted, informative and appropriately contained realization.

There is no need to freeze every internal connection. A further exact theorem says that the retained converter remains correct **if and only if the current composed relation agrees with the measured one**. In the concrete revision law, two adjacent translations change polarity together. The internal relations change, while their combined effect stays the same. Lean proves preservation of the complete endpoint relation and the retained correction through every finite history of such revisions, allowing different pairs at successive steps.

The scope of “together” matters. If those two changes occur separately, the first intermediate change reverses the composed relation and defeats the old converter. Lean checks that failure for arbitrary surrounding connections. Thus preservation at completed revisions does not establish safety at every physical intermediate state. A real implementation needs coordinated updates, protected delivery during the change, or a separately effective route. The result preserves room for continued internal transformation while identifying exactly what must remain effective.

**关系的改变本身可以是纠正的对象。** 这里的成功不是替换所有参与者，也不是增加样本数，而是辨明并修订它们的接续方式。整体关系可以被有根据地概括；需要保留多少细节，取决于要保证什么以及概括在哪些变化下仍成立。

The underlying local/global consistency issue has established precedents. Beeri, Fagin, Maier and Yannakakis distinguish agreement on overlapping projections from global consistency and give structural conditions connecting them. Our cyclic translation and repair proofs are scoped applications, not a new discovery of that distinction. [Primary paper, printed pp.485–487](https://s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/res-files/500-jacm83a.pdf).

## 10. A possibility interface is not yet a correction contract

A more consequential limit appears after the exact interface result. Let the requested output be `b`, an internal command be `a`, and a currently hidden mode be `h`. Compare a fixed translation `a=b` with a switchable translation `a XOR h=b`. For each mode, each requested output is possible in both installations. Their projected boundary relations agree, and Lean proves they pass exactly the same feasibility-context tests.

Yet the fixed translation can realize either request with one command chosen without seeing the mode. The switchable translation cannot. It needs opposite commands in the two hidden modes. Revealing the current mode supplies a successful rule; simply retaining the same projected possibility set does not.

The distinction is between

`for every hidden condition, some successful realization exists`

and

`one rule using the available observations succeeds in every admitted condition`.

The first does not establish the second. This is a failure of using a feasibility interface as a control contract, not a refutation of its exact feasibility theorem. The earlier [observation-based boundary result](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/CORRECTION-FRONTIER-BOUNDARY.md) supplies the general common-action criterion; it is credited rather than re-counted. The present result shows how an apparently adequate parent interface can erase that distinction during aggregation.

A correction interface must therefore retain how a realization can be selected: what can actually be observed, which choices can be coordinated, which conditions may vary, and what remains effective until delivery. Existing [protected delivery](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/CORRECTION-FRONTIER-BOUNDARY.md) and [handoff](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/CORRECTION-HANDOFF-INTEGRATION.md) results address parts of this execution requirement. No theorem in this round combines them into one distributed, continuously reconfiguring implementation.

This emphasis has a strong predecessor in interface automata: compatibility and refinement account for assumptions about interaction and distinguish who controls a choice. That work is richer in temporal composition than the present single-decision separation. Its component/context distinction can be read here as an accountable analytical cut within shared activity. [de Alfaro and Henzinger, introduction](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~heineman/html/teaching_/CS562/p109-de_alfaro.pdf).

## 11. Alignment consequence and the remaining wall

The relational account now gives a constructive research target: **maintain and revise the relationships through which legitimate correction becomes jointly realizable, observable and effective**. Expanding the number or capabilities of participating AIs is only one possible change in that organization. The correction problem also concerns how they share information, translate requests, coordinate changes and sustain the sites from which further correction is possible.

This is an implementation direction under the already stated ethical premises, not a deduction of moral authority from compatibility. Some destructive arrangements are internally consistent; some warranted changes properly end an existing arrangement. Actual losses, authority and responsibilities remain governed by [practical judgment](PRACTICAL-JUDGMENT.md). Preserving a parent structure is not automatically the ethical aim.

The advance beyond the prior round is precise: we now have exact relational grouping and replacement criteria for feasibility, a general finite cyclic obstruction with a constructive relational repair, and a proof that an exact feasibility abstraction can still conceal failure of observable correction. Aggregate relational evidence can suffice without a complete internal map, and the measured correction can survive internal revisions that preserve the composed relationship.

**The remaining wall is a supported strategy through changing relations.** The probe and retained converter now cover every finite history of the stated paired revisions. The converter remains supplied, and a pair is modeled as one coordinated step. Splitting that step exposes an actual failing intermediate relationship. The observation must match the relevant composed effect when correction takes effect, even though internal details may change. The earlier impossibility reappears if permitted unobserved changes can destroy every common effective response. To advance the implementation, one must identify a realizable observation-and-delivery relationship that survives the admitted changes, or a fallback whose effectiveness does not depend on them. Calling the group one system, adding more copies or existentially hiding its internals supplies none of those relationships by itself.

[The proof and counterreview record](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/RELATIONAL-CORRECTION-INTEGRATION.md) contains **39 new Lean theorems / 55 audits**, with 38 empty dependency reports and 17 using only `propext`. Independent novelty, full distributed execution and unrestricted AI corrigibility remain unestablished. The paper-specific counterexample from the preceding work retains its separate candidate-contribution status.

## 12. Correction depends on the request's history

The next continuation advances the coordinated-update boundary above. There is a stronger difficulty than an invalid intermediate configuration: **even an instantaneous replacement preserving the entire endpoint relation can break a request already in flight**. Suppose an old path flips a command twice, and its replacement leaves it unchanged twice. Both complete paths preserve the command. But a request that crosses the old first connection and the new second connection is flipped once. Its actual history belongs to neither complete path.

Lean proves this mixed-history failure for either repeated polarity and either requested value. This does not invalidate the previous feasibility interface. It identifies the extra relation a correction contract must preserve: how an ongoing request continues through change. A parent description adequate for completed configurations can omit precisely what is needed for an intervention already underway.

**纠正依赖的关系包含历史。** 两种整体配置各自都能正确传达，不等于在它们之间转换时，已经进行的请求仍能正确到达。需要说明请求进入了哪一组接续关系、还依赖哪些关系，以及这种依赖何时结束。这里的历史不是额外添加的观察者叙事，而是请求实际经历的作用次序。

## 13. Preserve a request's route while building its replacement

The new construction keeps two banks of translation paths within one installation. A replacement is assembled one connection at a time, with its composed polarity accumulated from those actual modeled writes. It is published only when complete. Admission captures the active route, encodes the request and records its use of that route in one protected step. Each later hop reads the captured route, even when another route becomes active for new requests.

An old route can be reset or reused only after every registered request referring to it has completed. Thus the shared organization can change while preserving the concrete relationships on which existing correction depends. The entire handoff need not be indivisible; protected acquisition, individual operations and the lifetime of referenced storage remain explicit premises. Merely retaining a version number does not suffice.

**33 new Lean theorems / 62 audits** establish initialization, actual assembly, each permitted operation, correct delivery after arbitrary finite prefixes, and completion of a head request after its remaining hops plus one consecutive execution step. The prior relational and handoff modules contribute **62 inherited theorems / 100 audits**, counted separately. Counterexamples preserve failure from switching a request's version, reusing its route too early and registering its dependency after capture. The proof also connects a prepared route to the prior checked channel; it does not claim a full refinement of every earlier protocol.

Executable checks cover **4,512 reachable states / 63,168 transitions**, 100 repeated handoffs and 80 controlled shared-memory thread trials. Correctly pinned requests succeed; each of the three deliberately broken controls delivers the wrong result under its forced schedule. These are checks of an authored translation program, not empirical AI validation. [Proof, code, dependencies and counterreview](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/VERSIONED-CORRECTION-INTEGRATION.md).

Per-request consistency and delayed reclamation have established predecessors. Reitblatt and colleagues' network-update work already explains why atomic whole-network replacement is weaker than preserving a packet's processing history and supplies a versioned update method. [Primary paper](https://reitblatt.com/downloads/consistent-updates-sigcomm12.pdf). Linux RCU similarly separates removal from safe reclamation after existing readers finish. [Primary documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/RCU/whatisRCU.html). The present contribution is their scoped integration into this relational correction account with checked proofs, not a claim to have discovered those methods.

## 14. The new boundary: supported history and finite resources

Safety through permitted revisions now has a concrete construction. Its resource limit is equally concrete: with two banks, the active path and an unfinished request on the other path can leave no storage available for another build. A full request queue also blocks admission. Lean proves these conditions. Completion follows under the specified execution schedule; arbitrary safe pauses do not establish progress, and no wall-clock or unlimited-throughput guarantee is claimed.

For growth and alignment, the resulting design question is whether transformation continues to support the histories through which warranted correction is being carried out. More internal change is compatible with safe delivery in this model, but storage, protected acquisition and execution time must actually be available. Those supports are relations within the whole, not free resources supplied by naming a parent system.

The remaining wall is establishing this correspondence for an identified evolving AI installation: the actual command effect, faithful writes, protected route lifetime, scheduling and resources, and the authority of a request whose circumstances may change. The model keeps requested meaning and authorization supplied; it does not prove that completing an outdated request is ethically right, nor that every intervention should preserve the old organization. The existing practical-judgment results retain that scope. No actual AI deployment or independent novelty is established by this continuation.
