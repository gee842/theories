# Why affected beings matter within a generative world

5 October 2026. Proposed ethical development for the unified theory. This is an original AI-assisted argument for this research program, not a reconstruction of Ren's exact words, a theorem of physics, or a claim of universal philosophical agreement. It extends the explicit commitments already recorded in the [conscience source](../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-05-conscience-and-universal-morality.md) and [unified theory, Section 10](THEORY.md).

## The positive bridge

A living or experiencing being can undergo deprivation as a loss to that being. The loss does not first become significant when an observer finds it useful to prevent it. Pain can be bad for the sufferer; loss of the means to act can obstruct that person's projects; coercion can make the apparent pursuit of a life answerable to another's power. These are claims about what happens to a subject, not merely about the subject's contribution to an organization.

The first ethical premise is that at least some such subject-relative goods and harms provide reasons that an agent deliberating about shared action should take into account. This premise can be defended by attention to the experience and claims of affected beings and by consistency with the reasons we already recognize in our own case. It is not derived from the fact that matter continues to transform.

The second premise is that a reason's force cannot be canceled merely by changing the arbitrary identity of the person to whom it applies. If pain is a reason because of what the experience is like for its subject, the same kind of pain does not cease to supply any reason when experienced by an unfamiliar or politically powerless subject. Differences in urgency, responsibility, uncertainty, relevant relationships, and available means can change what should be done. A difference has to bear on the reason being assessed; naming someone an outsider does not itself supply that connection.

The third premise is that shared coercive arrangements owe a justification responsive to the claims of those whose lives they govern. This is a substantive commitment to answerability, not a disguised consequence of efficient coordination. Affected people can reject a proposed justification; answerability does not guarantee consensus or grant everyone an unlimited veto. It requires that their relevant claims can enter the reasoning, receive an intelligible response, and affect the arrangement when that response reveals an avoidable wrong.

Together, these premises support **standing without a usefulness test**. A child, an injured worker, an outsider, or an animal capable of suffering can supply reasons even when the productive organization would function just as well without them. The route from generation to ethics is therefore:

> Generative processes create and alter conditions in which beings can fare well or badly. Their consequences supply ethical reasons under the standing premise. The causal theory identifies what realizing those reasons requires; impartial justification constrains whose reasons may be ignored.

This bridge gives causal investigation a moral object without making causal success its moral standard. It also makes the ethical claim contestable at its actual foundations. Someone who denies that anyone else's suffering supplies them any reason has not been refuted by a maintenance equation. The disagreement is with the ethical premises, not a missing step in the arithmetic.

## What generativity contributes to the ethical argument

“More future possibilities” cannot be the whole good. More ways to exploit, torment, or deceive do not become desirable because they enlarge an action set. Nor must a person endlessly generate, improve, or persist to deserve care. Rest, enjoyment, love, grief, completion, and chosen withdrawal can matter without producing a further achievement.

Generativity makes a more specific contribution. It directs attention to the **production and destruction of conditions through time**. An action can benefit a person today while installing a relationship that makes tomorrow's refusal unaffordable. A reform can announce protection while exhausting the institution that would implement it. A productive arrangement can move its damage to someone outside its accounting boundary. These are morally relevant causal features because they change how affected beings can live, act, and challenge what happens to them.

On this account, ethical wisdom combines three distinguishable capacities:

1. **Epistemic discernment:** identify consequential facts, uncertainty, causal dependencies, and inadequacies in the current description.
2. **Practical competence:** find and implement workable responses with actual resources, authority, timing, and succession.
3. **Ethical judgment:** recognize affected beings as sources of reasons, assess conflicts without arbitrary exclusion, and remain willing to revise one's commitments and exercise of power.

One person or practice can integrate all three. The distinction identifies failure modes: an accurate oppressor has discernment; a reliable coercive institution has competence; a compassionate but systematically mistaken intervention may fail in both while remaining sincerely concerned. Ethical wisdom requires their coordination, not maximal intensity along any one dimension.

A compact proposed formulation is:

> **Ethical wisdom is situated, revisable judgment that takes affected beings' claims seriously and makes its responses effective without concealing who bears their costs.**

This leaves a real evaluative task in “takes seriously”; the following premises and constraints give it content rather than defining every desired outcome as wise.

## Explicit premises and limited deductions

The proposal needs a declared domain of moral patients and a list of relevant kinds of claims. A center in the dynamical model is not automatically a moral patient. Evidence of experience, interests, agency, relationships, and vulnerability must be considered on their own grounds. Membership uncertainty requires a reasoned policy for uncertainty; it cannot be settled by calling every feedback loop conscious. Duties concerning ecosystems or nonexperiencing entities may require additional value premises. Future persons also raise identity and population questions not solved here.

For a specified application, use the following premises:

- **N1 Standing:** some independently described goods and harms of the admitted moral patients supply pro tanto reasons for action. “Pro tanto” means a reason with weight that can be outweighed, not an absolute command.
- **N2 Relevant consistency:** a judgment may change across persons or situations only on the basis of a morally relevant difference. Arbitrary identity and bookkeeping labels are not such differences; responsibilities and relationships can be.
- **N3 Answerability:** an arrangement exercising consequential power over affected persons owes consideration and a response to their relevant claims; their practical inability to force a hearing does not cancel this requirement.
- **N4 Feasible implementation:** when an arrangement claims to provide a specified protection, it must provide the causal means required to deliver that protection in the claimed circumstances. This is a consistency requirement between promise and implementation, not a theory that inability automatically excuses an avoidable failure.

These premises support limited deductions:

**D1 No costless worsening.** If two feasible options are identical in every relevant respect except that one imposes an additional admitted harm on a patient, the harmed option has a reason against it that the other does not. If all relevant reasons have been included and no contrary reason remains, choosing it cannot be justified by that reasons profile. This is a conditional dominance judgment, not a universal welfare sum. Its force disappears if the allegedly identical options conceal a relevant difference.

**D2 No arbitrary exclusion.** Renaming a patient, changing an administrative boundary, or removing that patient's productive usefulness does not by itself remove the reasons arising from their admitted goods and harms. Changes in usefulness can affect what others can afford or require; these enter as additional reasons and constraints, rather than retroactively making the patient have no standing. N1 is necessary here: impartiality alone is also compatible with treating everyone's interests as worth zero.

**D3 Account for effective access.** Under N3 and N4, a claimed right of challenge is defective when its exercise predictably requires resources, information, protection against retaliation, or response authority that the arrangement withholds. The necessary means depend on the case; this conclusion does not require that every complaint succeed or that every means be unlimited.

**D4 Trace displaced burdens.** If an option appears preferable only because a bookkeeping change removes an affected patient's admitted harm from the record, the evaluation is inconsistent with N1 and N2. A larger boundary does not repair this automatically: a total that hides the distribution can make the same mistake within a larger ledger.

**D5 Preserve claims through infeasibility.** If the feasible set contains no option satisfying all admitted protections, feasibility does not decide whose claim ceases to matter. The conclusion is that the protections cannot all be delivered under the stipulated conditions. Judgment must then examine changes to those conditions, the necessity and distribution of unavoidable loss, and responsibilities for repair. A declared priority rule can choose among options, but its normative premise must be separately defended.

**D6 Make revisability effective where error matters.** Under N3 and N4, an arrangement claiming to respond to material mistakes requires a usable route for detecting, contesting, and correcting the relevant errors. The appropriate safeguard depends on expected seriousness, time, uncertainty, and costs. There is no deduction that every act must remain reversible or that all decisions need an infinite hierarchy of reviewers. Where irreversibility is unavoidable, justification may require stronger prior scrutiny, limited exposure, representation, or repair duties instead.

The last three deductions link ethics directly to the formal core: observational distinguishability, liquidity, authority, prefix safety, installed policies, and delivered succession can be necessary for an ethical commitment to become an actual practice. The mathematics remains conditional on the commitment; the ethical argument becomes answerable to what can actually be delivered.

## A precise identity consistency check

Let a decision context contain patients, circumstances, relationships, available actions, relevant reasons, and feasible consequences. Let \(D(x)\) be the set of actions the evaluator accepts as justified in context \(x\). If \(\sigma\) is a pure renaming that preserves the full morally relevant structure, N2 requires

\[
D(\sigma x)=\sigma D(x).
\]

This condition is useful for finding favoritism that depends only on labels. It does not force equal treatment of unequally situated persons, resolve conflicting claims, or prove N1. An evaluator indifferent to everyone satisfies this symmetry. Conversely, a special duty to one's child can survive the check when the parent-child relationship is preserved under renaming; whether that special duty is legitimate requires another argument.

A practical test is to hold the causal consequences and legitimate relational reasons fixed while changing only the names, status labels, or jurisdictional classifications. If the judgment changes, identify the additional reason or acknowledge the inconsistency. This is a test of a declared justification, not a machine for producing the whole of ethics.

## Consent, conflict, and correction

Consent matters because it can express agency, authorize otherwise impermissible interference, and supply evidence about what a person values. These functions are distinct. Apparent agreement can also arise from ignorance, threats, dependent access to necessities, or a worsened refusal option. Accordingly, a model claiming voluntary participation should name the participant's information, options, means of refusal, and the parties able to alter those options.

No single participation inequality establishes all these conditions. A favorable expected payoff does not establish informed authorization. A formally available exit does not establish a usable exit. Consent by a majority does not erase minority claims. Conversely, newborns and people unable to consent retain standing, and emergencies may require justified action without prior agreement. Guardianship and emergency authority introduce responsibilities for representation, proportionality, disclosure, and review rather than a general exemption from answerability.

For genuine conflicts, use a sequence whose steps expose the reasons instead of settling them by definition: identify the independently described interests at stake; verify necessity and search for less harmful feasible alternatives; identify who has authority to decide and why; make the burdens visible; apply and defend the relevant priority, compensation, or fair-allocation rule; and specify review and repair appropriate to the risks. A good-faith process can still reach a wrong decision. Its value lies partly in making mistakes corrigible and affected claims consequential.

Revision also has two targets. New evidence can change which action best serves a standing commitment. Ethical criticism can challenge the commitment, protected domain, or allocation rule itself. If a procedure only allows complaints that affirm its existing ranking, it offers bounded policy correction, not full ethical revisability. Neither kind of revision guarantees improvement: capture can rewrite a protection to conceal a violation. The grounds and consequences of amendments must be examined.

## Counterexamples that the unified theory must retain

**The dispensable victim.** Production and institutional persistence are identical with and without one person's avoidable suffering. This defeats an inference from persistence to adequate moral concern. Under N1, the suffering remains a reason even when it produces no destabilizing feedback.

**The benevolent cage.** An authority maintains people's health and material comfort while preventing informed refusal, criticism, and independent projects. A capacity floor alone does not establish ethical wisdom. A defense of agency and answerability supplies additional considerations; the example does not imply that autonomy is the only good or that every restriction is wrong.

**The regenerative predator.** A coercive organization renews its personnel, learns from resistance, and preserves its correction machinery. It is regenerative and epistemically capable. Its target and burden distribution can remain ethically objectionable. “Correction” must therefore always name what is being corrected and for whom.

**The destructive rescue.** Shutting down a harmful institution, restricting an action, or ending a particular cycle can protect affected beings. Maximizing continued activity or preserving every available option would recommend the wrong object. The ethical reference is the independently justified goods and claims, not the persistence of whichever process currently exists.

**The impossible promise.** Two admitted protections cannot jointly be maintained with the available resources. Neither demanding both nor proving their incompatibility completes the ethical work. One must address the source of scarcity, feasible change, burdens, priorities, and repair. Unlimited rhetorical inclusion without means can itself mislead the protected parties.

## What this permits the next causal study to claim

An institutional model can now separate three measurable achievements: the formation of a channel that changes decisions; its continued operational capacity; and its responsiveness to separately specified claims of affected patients. Only the third gives the channel the relevant ethical direction, and the claim must be limited to the protected dimensions actually measured.

A mechanism that spreads because it protects valuable contributors has not yet explained protection of dispensable patients. A mechanism that protects them only because an analyst assigns their losses to its objective has operationalized a normative premise rather than explained the formation of that premise. Both can be worthwhile models if described correctly.

A stronger next result would identify conditions under which agents construct and renew protections for affected others even when aggregate production does not require those others. It should compare explanations—direct concern, reciprocal exposure, coalition-building, institutional commitment, reputational pressure, or learning about dependence—without equating their motivational differences with the final ethical verdict. Protective behavior can have mixed motives; declared compassion can fail to protect.

There is also a decisive negative result to seek: environments in which a stable learning and production process has no endogenous route that makes a dispensable patient's harm alter selection. Such a result would specify when the universal-direction hypothesis fails and what additional condition could change the conclusion. Ethical justification can still demand change in those environments. Causal inevitability is not a prerequisite for having a reason to act.

## Remaining philosophical work

The proposed bridge gives a positive defense of non-exclusion and answerability, plus concrete consistency constraints. It does not resolve the scope of sentience, population ethics, interpersonal comparisons, the full status of nonexperiencing nature, legitimate partiality, rights conflicts, or the ultimate grounds of moral reasons. These are substantive open questions, not gaps to conceal by using “generativity” broadly enough to cover every answer.

Its strongest contribution is a disciplined connection: **standing determines whose consequences require justification; causal inquiry discovers how those consequences are produced; effective correction makes justified concern able to change the world.** This connection can become more explanatory and more humane without pretending that either mathematics or sincere conviction has finished the other one's work.
