**An action can succeed for someone without settling every affected claim. A successful episode can also leave later protection unsecured.** The distinction matters when connecting concern to effective action and succession: fulfilled claims, retained reasons and sustained protection are different achievements.

## Which claims an actual action supports

Keep each claim's patient, context, reason and addressee explicit. The verification ledger distinguishes:

- independently admitted claims;
- claims whose fulfillment is soundly certified by the actual execution;
- admitted claims whose fulfillment remains uncertified.

The last category includes incomplete information as well as failed or unaffordable delivery. It does not establish that harm occurred, and it does not defeat the underlying reason. Revising a reason requires evidence or argument bearing on its ground; lack of fuel alone is not such a rule.

For partial evidence, the retained possibilities must include the actual situation. The chosen action requires separately justified permission, an actual enabled trace and a sound link from that trace to the addressed claim's fulfillment. This narrower result does not require the settlement theorem's complete current audit of every candidate edge. It also does not select an ethically correct action from conflicting claims: the reasons and choice remain explicit inputs.

The concrete action theorem uses the existing charged events, a shared initial harm baseline and the supplied apparatus and permission conditions. It proves the certified effect and actual bill without an additional full audit. It does not produce the evidence or promise executable completion from every possible starting state. The separate theorem connecting justified reasons to action retains the parent's audit, calibration and positive-ground requirements.

The ledger is a mathematical verification device. It does not construct storage, pay for a new deliberation algorithm or establish a motive to care. Preserving the admitted ledger is an explicit interface contract. The physical conclusions below follow from the imported event law and reached histories.

## Protection through time has a stricter composition law

The existing process records cumulative harm separately for each patient. Every event preserves or increases each counter. For actual consecutive histories `s → u → t`, a patient has no new harm over the entire history exactly when that patient has no new harm in **both** segments.

Consequently, the patients protected throughout the concatenation are the **intersection** of the patients protected in each segment. A union of certificates from separate episodes can overstate continuous protection. Earlier certificates remain evidence about their original intervals; their targets and times cannot silently expand.

The accepted parent supplies a concrete test. Its first action protects both patients and ends with harm counts `(0,0)`. Its revised action protects q and ends at `(1,0)`; the later successor episode ends at `(2,0)`. The initial protection of p was real. It does not establish p's protection over the longer history. The same states reached through resource use, events and energy accounts are used throughout.

This is a theorem about the declared cumulative-harm ledger. It does not claim that injuries cannot heal or that welfare can only decline. Likewise, certificates of completed one-time services can accumulate when their meaning and fulfillment persist. That requires an explicit persistence condition and a shared assessment horizon.

## Failure and restraint remain visible

In another actual parent history, cutting delivery leaves the installed reasons intact while allowing a release that harms both patients. A reason's continued representation is therefore insufficient for its effective protection. Conversely, withdrawal can avoid new harm without supplying a positive service. No certificate, no service and actual harm must remain separate observations.

The result retains the full feasible repertoire. A shortage for the armed protocol does not prove that voluntary protection or withdrawal is impossible. It supplies neither an interpersonal harm sum nor a priority rule for deciding whose unsatisfied claim should prevail. The [ethical foundation](ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md) and [finite decision account](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-revisable-concern/settlement/normative/decision-math.md) state the additional normative commitments such choices require.

In the concrete two-patient annotation, the addressed set is supplied separately from the current reason publication. Keeping p in that set does not restore the parent's retracted derivation for p. Continued ethical warrant needs its own ground, just as the choice to act needs legitimate permission.

Here p and q are the protection model's two patients. They are not the fresh-recipient study's two alternative commands for one recipient. The new verification interface does not identify those different meanings or claim a physical join between the two producers.
