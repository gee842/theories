## Review and corrections

Three parallel readers reviewed mathematics, inquiry and evidence, and relational direction and correction. They then examined the main synthesis and proof appendix. Their notes are preserved under the repository's [_process research record](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-unified-theory/). This is independent reconstruction by collaborating AI agents, not independent human peer review.

The reviews produced substantive corrections:

- Finite support does not rule out indefinitely positive **summable** activity. The necessity statements now require unbounded cumulative loss or the relevant positive lower bound. The energy result bounds cumulative activity and excludes a positive sustained long-run average.
- The finite information-state theorem's equivalence now requires exact compatibility sets and exact successor updates. Overapproximations provide sound sufficient certificates but can falsely exclude feasible policies.
- A finite policy witness still needs an affordable implementation of its memory and computation. These are explicit assumptions of the physical reading.
- Recurrence uses delivered transition events. Parallel transitions with the same endpoints but different event labels cannot be collapsed. Physical interpretation also requires time divergence.
- The succession recurrence now states that the delivered configuration is observed or is an exact implementable information state, and that the option summaries capture all resource-dependent feasibility.
- A failing trajectory of one installed policy is distinguished from a proof that every available policy fails.
- The main account preserves the negative inquiry results, both null reasoning pilots, decision adequacy despite incomplete knowledge, and historical truth beyond future-response equivalence.

The final review accepted the amended formal passages without a remaining mathematical blocker in its stated scope. Acceptance is a review outcome, not certification of every possible application.

## Original prospective integrated inquiry study

The following broader design was proposed to test the **joint causal account**, while allowing a simpler model to win. It remains prospective where it requires unknown hazards, uncertain sensing, discovery of new interventions, and the specified policy comparisons. The later strategic constructions do not constitute this whole experiment or an approved human-subject study.

Use a bounded simulated service institution with two affected groups, a productive resource, an observation channel, a replaceable operator, and a corrector. The initial operator's ordinary observations sometimes leave a group-specific failure indistinguishable from a harmless situation. A paid linkage or instrument change can reveal it. A corrective action can then alter provision if the actual authority permits it. All inquiry, review, ordinary service, correction, and handoff draw from explicit dated accounts.

The key extension is to make construction and renewal of the information-to-action pathway actual decisions. A system may spend on inquiry, keep it recoverably dormant, fund a participant-controlled alternative, or invest in shared production. A successor receives only the serialized package, rights, resources, and instructions actually left behind. Constitutional authority is still an explicit model input unless a separate formation and enforcement mechanism is added; this boundary must be stated rather than renamed away.

Vary four mechanisms independently: diagnostic access, effective authority, initial reserve, and recurring funding. Include a second comparison between an irrevocable participant-controlled fallback and a revocable promise with equal nominal funding. Keep the accounting treatment of ownership and transfers explicit. Use paired environment seeds as the sampling unit, held-out conditions for evaluation, and equal declared initial resources. Report realized expenditures and outcome differences instead of assuming every policy spends equally.

Include stationary harmless worlds, temporary opportunities, delayed hazards, hidden group-specific damage, withdrawal of temporary support, and operator succession. Compare the integrated adaptive policy against an optimized fixed schedule, a performance-only policy, and a policy using the same information and budget with simpler decision rules. Separate ablations remove one route at a time. Do not supply a perfect corrective proposal to a model claiming to explain its discovery.

Predeclared predictions should be conditional:

1. Extra diagnostic money will not restore protection when the actionable evidence remains indistinguishable or effective authority is absent.
2. Recurring support can sustain repeated corrective expense beyond what an equal initial endowment can cover over sufficiently long horizons; neither helps after an irreversible deadline.
3. A participant-specific observation can outperform an aggregate when it reveals an unstable consequential difference, and add only cost when the hidden difference is harmless.
4. A recoverable pause can outperform continuous inquiry under low change rates but fail when restoration is too slow.
5. An adoption rule including affordable reversal can beat a current-gain-only rule under relevant drift, while sacrificing resources in stationary worlds.
6. A protected alternative can preserve meaningful refusal; improving apparent agreement by worsening outside options is recorded separately.
7. A joint certificate should predict failures that remain invisible to isolated module scores. If it adds no out-of-sample predictive or intervention value, its extra complexity is not justified for that application.

Measure participant losses, service delivered and denied, useful discoveries, false adoption, correction latency, dated solvency, retained ability to refuse, and successor performance separately. The study does not invent a universal wisdom score. Freeze the implementation and analysis before the final evaluation; record failed predictions and all planned comparisons.
