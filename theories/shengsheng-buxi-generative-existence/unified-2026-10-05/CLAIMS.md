## How the theory can be challenged

A formal challenge can exhibit an omitted premise, invalid derivation, or counterexample within a theorem's stated class. An application challenge can show that a claimed dependency is absent, that an alleged obstruction has an unmodeled substitute, or that a simpler model predicts interventions just as well. A normative challenge can contest the scope of standing, protected consequences, or revision procedure. These are different challenges and should receive answers appropriate to their grounds.

The overall proposal would lose scientific value if its extra distinctions failed to improve predictions, diagnoses, or interventions over simpler alternatives at comparable cost. Reinterpreting every outcome afterward as “generativity” would evade that test rather than satisfy it.
