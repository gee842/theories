## The existing advance and the next question

The [current inquiry-interface argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/INQUIRY-INTERFACE-ARGUMENT.md) already establishes consequence-dependent sampling. The remaining connection is therefore more specific than making observations affect the next command. It concerns the criterion for **sufficient inquiry**, including what its own effects leave possible. A fixed rule of continuing until one candidate remains can be inappropriate when the permitted experiments cannot achieve that condition, or when a less discriminating question already suffices for a justified continuation. The formation and warrant of such criteria remain to be explained.

Under the [nondual method](NONDUAL-METHOD.md), an experiment belongs to the activity it investigates. It can alter contact, retained organization, available support and subsequent possibilities. The following local model gives those relations a precise representation. Its packet, fuel and fault names belong to that realization; they are not foundational entities or a definition of inquiry.

## One existing history and three possible behaviors

The [access study](EXPERIMENTAL-ACCESS.md) continues an actually acquired three-phase model. Its native phase readings are `001`; one action advances the phase. Reset, when effective, returns that component to its initial phase. A transfer attempt consumes one ten-unit source packet and either transfers it or leaves it unavailable. **Reset does not restore the packet.** Actual transfer occurs before acquisition of its receipt, so an interrupted record cannot undo the event.

The expanded access family admits both readiness reversal and transfer blockage. After the sensor sequence, the learner has identified the current phase, reset condition and combined readiness/sensor inversion parity. It still admits four fault templates. At fixed parity, these give three classes of native behavior while the packet remains:

| Initial class | Transfer relation | Probe at learned output 1 | Probe at learned output 0 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | Open path, ordinary readiness | Arrival | No arrival |
| B | Open path, reversed readiness | No arrival | Arrival |
| C | Blocked path, either readiness reversal | No arrival | No arrival |

The two blocked templates are distinct proposed causes but one behavioral class for these questions. Sensor inversion compensates for readiness inversion so that their readings match the fixed known parity. Reads, advances and resets alone cannot distinguish A, B and C within the remaining family.

The classes are specified through the original native law and its consequences. They are not three freely assigned labels on an otherwise identical structure. A probe at learned output 1 separates A from B or C; a probe at learned output 0 separates B from C. Every pair therefore has a permitted distinguishing experiment when its ordinary execution conditions are met.

## Why the separate experiments cannot always be combined

Before the first probe, every candidate gives the same sensor transcript and undergoes the same phase and support changes. An inquiry using that accessible history must therefore make the same first probe choice in all remaining candidates.

If it probes at learned output 1, arrival identifies A. No arrival leaves B and C. Both consume the sole packet, receive no replenishment, and undergo the same observation and record operations. Their resulting phase, sensor parity, reset behavior, source availability and accessible fuel consequences agree. Further native readings agree, and further attempts encounter the same exhausted source. No later calculation or choice among those commands can distinguish the two initial classes.

If it probes at learned output 0, the symmetric argument leaves A and C indistinguishable after no arrival. An inquiry that never probes obtains no class-discriminating evidence at all. Thus no one policy can identify the initial class in every case. The obstruction survives granting unlimited computational capacity and removing fuel restrictions: neither concession restores the consumed source under the unchanged law. Zero-error randomization cannot supply a uniformly successful policy either.

This is the difference between

\[
\forall c\ne d\;\exists\text{ a test distinguishing }c,d
\]

and

\[
\exists\text{ one executable policy identifying every initial class}.
\]

The first statement does not entail the second when tests change the future configuration. The [proof note](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/INQUIRY-CONTINUATION-MATH.md) gives the exact argument and preserves the initial class throughout each candidate's changing state.

This distinction has a direct antecedent in [Moore's 1956 sequential-machine experiments](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cdm/resources/Moore1956-gedanken-experiments.pdf), Theorem 1. Moore's experiments already permit adaptive branching. The present contribution is its application to the repository's acquired model and consumable native interaction, not a claim to have discovered the underlying automata result. ## The past and the present pose different identification problems

After the unsuccessful probe, the remaining alternatives can have the same current native behavior. That does not mean they had the same behavior before the probe, or that the learner has discovered which initial class occurred. Inquiry has transformed the situation so that the old difference no longer changes any available native continuation.

This is why the proof keeps pairs of **initial target label and current configuration**. Merging equal current configurations while deleting their initial labels would falsely report that a historical question had been answered. Conversely, demanding that every historical alternative be resolved before making any claim about current possibilities would also be too strong. A record can correctly establish the current limitation while retaining uncertainty about its cause.

That distinction can constrain a criterion of sufficient inquiry. If all retained alternatives agree about the current continuation actually at issue, exact historical diagnosis need not be required to answer that question. But discarding a historical distinction requires attention to later demands: new support or a changed coupling could make its consequences relevant again. Such restoration is absent from the current native interface and must be represented if claimed. Neither remaining ambiguity nor depletion automatically warrants an action or makes the preceding inquiry wise.

## A positive criterion for an identifying inquiry

For a finite declared process, retain all compatible pairs \((\ell,s)\), where \(\ell\) is the initial answer sought and \(s\) is the current configuration. An action updates each configuration and partitions the candidates by its acquired outcome. Its changes to subsequent opportunities must be included; it is insufficient to partition an unchanging list of hypotheses by predicted answers.

Let \(W_0\) be the nonempty candidate sets whose initial labels already agree. Define

\[
W_{n+1}=W_n\cup
\{B:\text{ some admissible action has every possible outcome successor in }W_n\}.
\]

Then \(B\in W_n\) exactly when a policy can identify the target in at most \(n\) further actions under the stated transition and observation conditions. The union is the least fixed point of this rule. On a finite candidate-state graph it stabilizes and supplies a terminating policy wherever identification is possible. The proof treats an action with a consequential failure through its actual outcome and successor, rather than silently erasing the failure.

This criterion asks whether a complete identifying continuation exists. A test that divides the present alternatives can still be unusable for that goal if one branch destroys all remaining separators. The rule does not require an analyst's uncertainty score. It does require a specified target, accurate candidate transitions, an adequate observation interface and representation of relevant feasibility. Those dependencies remain local explanatory premises; a computed decision tree is not an installed or maintained mechanism.

For the concrete three-class problem, a **hypothetical two-packet configuration** makes the positive result visible. Probe at output 1. Arrival identifies A; otherwise use the remaining packet at output 0, where arrival identifies B and no arrival identifies C. At most two probes suffice. This changes the initial source condition and assumes sufficient support for the necessary actions and records. It is not an extension silently funded from the original one-packet endpoint, and it still cannot distinguish the two blocked causal templates.

More generally, reusable pairwise experiments do identify a finite set of \(m\) targets in at most \(m-1\) experiments when each experiment can execute and terminate for every remaining candidate, its record excludes at least one selected alternative, and restoration preserves the initial target and the conditions for later tests. These are substantive continuation and retention premises. A component command named `reset` does not establish them. The fixed-point criterion permits weaker sufficient arrangements where full restoration is unnecessary.

## Contribution to the common account

The earlier identity result characterized distinctions through possible continuations. This continuation shows why access to those distinctions must also be explained through **one actual evolving inquiry**, whose effects condition what can be investigated next. It advances the bridge from mathematical identity to warranted acquisition without assuming that inquiry stands outside its subject.

Three obligations now have precise scopes: a mathematical relation can distinguish configurations; a feasible policy can acquire the distinction; and a formed organization can realize that policy while maintaining the relevant conditions. None alone proves the others. They describe related requirements within generative activity, not separate realities. The first-order truth obstruction and the unfinished argument for universal mathematical constitution remain unchanged.

The next shared connection is **consequences changing the operative criterion for sufficient inquiry**, including preservation or transformation of the possibilities on which further understanding depends. The current result supplies an exact test for one criterion, full initial identification, and shows where it fails. A stronger constructive history would have to show the criterion or inquiry arrangement changing through acquired consequences, then affecting actual continuation. It must preserve justified uncertainty and failed changes rather than supply a desired answer or a favorable score.

An admissible one-packet policy identifying all three initial classes, or an overlooked accessible channel distinguishing the negative pair, would defeat this local result. Genuine replenishment, a new interaction or an independently warranted restriction of candidates would instead change its premises. The obstruction is therefore a property of the reconstructed inquiry, not a failure of generative reality or a denial that richer understanding can form.
