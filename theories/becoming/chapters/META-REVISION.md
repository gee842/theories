## Why future questions matter to retention

Let \(m(h)\) be everything accessible after history \(h\), including any resource or clock information. Let \(\mathcal F\) be the future questions that must be answered exactly. Exact decoding for every such question is possible precisely when

\[
m(h)=m(h')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f(h)=f(h')\quad\text{for every }f\in\mathcal F.
\]

Thus memory must preserve the joint answer profile of the intended questions. If those questions distinguish every admitted history, the memory must distinguish every history too. Restricted questions can require much less. This is an information requirement; it does not guarantee reliable storage or affordable decoding.

A small counterexample makes the selection problem concrete. Acquire two independent bits, but retain only one bit before learning whether the next question asks for the first or second. Preserving the first is exact for one demand; preserving the second is exact for the other. No alternative one-bit encoding can recover both coordinates for all four histories.

The [finite audit](../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/mathematical-abstraction/acquisition/access/revision/metarevision/README.md) checks all16 encoders and all16 pairs of decoders:256 designs. None answers all eight history/question pairs correctly; the largest answer count is six. These are logical coverage counts, not a measured task distribution, native reward or implemented retention policy. Competitive, minimax, average-case and domain-specific guarantees remain possible under their respective criteria.

The implication for our theory is specific: “preserve what could matter later” does not by itself determine an executable retention choice under incompatible demands and limited memory. The relevant future dependence, admissible loss or justified priority must enter the explanation.

## Why a generator needs more than past agreement

Any finite observed binary sequence can be continued by infinitely many different finite-state processes: append different numbers of zeros, then a one and an absorbing zero state. A finite generated list cannot contain every compatible continuation. A supplied bound or structural law can make a finite certificate possible, as in the earlier learning study; agreement alone does not certify unrestricted coverage.

This is compatible with a finite program enumerating an infinite class. Describing all candidates does not supply unlimited computation or determine which unobserved continuation is actual. Selecting a rule that fitted the past therefore still requires an account of the conditions under which that evidence bears on later use.

## The missing causal connection

A positive selector claim must demonstrate an actual route from a consequence to changed later procedure. Under comparable future opportunities, an acquired consequence must change a retained selector state, and that state must change which generator or retention recipe is enacted. Sensing, writing, switching and subsequent use must all be paid. A different reported score or stored label is insufficient; a halted arm alone does not show selection between two executable recipes.

This criterion does not require an abstract numeric reward. Differential physical support could preserve one mechanism, or an acquired consequence could modify a selector within one continuing system. Those are distinct mechanisms. Either needs its actual coupling identified. A route can also respond to misleading evidence and make future behavior worse; causal efficacy does not establish wisdom.

The [primary comparisons](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/META-REVISION-COMPARATORS.md) show that metareasoning, bandit selection and online retention already offer algorithms and guarantees under specified utilities, reward channels or request/cost models. Their availability does not derive the missing organizational consequence. Conversely, unknown future requests do not make every useful retention policy impossible.

There is a further limitation in the present resource model: it imposes no passive loss or externally timed duty while the controller does nothing. Simply minimizing expenditure would favor inactivity. To explain active inquiry, we must identify an actual dependency, task, deadline or return affected by the inquiry, rather than add an analyst's preference for more correct predictions.
