## Identity must preserve the declared continuations

For a fixed deterministic process, let \(s\) and \(t\) be configurations. Let \(A\) specify actions and \(Q\) specify observable questions. Write

\[
s\equiv_{A,Q}t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every finite allowed action sequence has the same definedness and answers from }s\text{ and }t.
\]

The empty sequence includes current answers. With partial actions, an unavailable action is distinguished from a successful action with an unfavorable answer. The [mathematical note](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/IMMANENT-MATHEMATICAL-IDENTITY-MATH.md) defines these observations explicitly.

The theorem admits all finite words over the selected actions; any protocol restrictions must be retained in the state or separately justified. A failed attempt that changes the configuration is itself an event with an outcome, not an undefined action whose consequences disappear. A read that consumes resources or changes its target likewise belongs in the activity being represented. Mathematical definedness alone does not give a learner a decidable physical test for it.

This relation is the coarsest equivalence preserving the questions, action availability and recursive action updates. Its quotient therefore has well-defined operations. Any exact representation of those continuations must retain at least these distinctions. The characterization is inherited from the earlier behavioral-quotient result, with its partial-action scope made explicit here.

The criterion is accountable: specify the allowed interactions and the consequences to preserve before constructing the quotient. If two supposedly identical represented states give different answers in one admitted continuation, the identification fails. An omitted but subsequently relevant consequence calls for an enlarged scope. Merely giving different names to two states supplies neither kind of challenge.

This is a semantic condition on possible continuations, not a statement that every continuation has been performed or that a finite learner knows the full relation. Acquired records provide evidence for a proposed abstraction. Their adequacy requires a separate argument, such as the earlier bounded-state certificate. The nondual reconstruction includes the formation of the interface in its explanatory task; the present theorem does not claim to have derived that formation.

## A reached state and a reusable operation have different identities

From a fixed entrance, histories \(u\) and \(v\) can leave the same future response pattern. That permits appending the same continuation to either. It does not automatically permit replacing one history by the other after an arbitrary prefix.

Consider a deterministic recognizer whose only accepted word is `ab`. After either `b` or `aa`, it is in the same dead state: no suffix can produce acceptance. But prefixing `a` gives `ab` and `aaa`, which have different answers. The histories have the same residual behavior from the entrance while the words are different operations on the whole reachable system.

For operations to be interchangeable, require the same result from **every reachable starting state**, preserving subsequent observations and definedness. In the total case this is equivalent to

\[
u\equiv_{\mathrm{op}}v
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall x,y,\ F(xuy)=F(xvy),
\]

where \(F\) is the response from the fixed entrance and \(x,y\) range over action words. Both preceding and following contexts matter. This relation is a two-sided congruence: equivalent operations can replace one another inside compositions. The resulting operation classes act faithfully as transformations of the reachable behavioral quotient. The proof also treats partial transformations with explicit domain preservation.

This clarifies two different senses of compositional identity. A state abstraction needs well-defined action updates. An algebra of interchangeable operations needs the stronger substitution condition. Neither should be silently substituted for the other. The distinction is standard in automata and algebraic language theory; its role here is to specify what an acquired mathematical identification actually warrants.

The [earlier action-word reconstruction](MATHEMATICAL-ROOT-FRONTIER.md#a-positive-reconstruction-of-operations) has another scope: uniform operations across **all** deterministic action systems correspond to unique words. Within one particular system, distinct words can act identically. For example, three advances act as identity on a three-cycle. Enlarging to all systems removes equations supported only by that particular realization.

## Expanding participation can refine an exact identity

Suppose one interface retains a set of actions and questions, and a larger interface preserves those laws while admitting additional actions or questions. The richer behavioral equivalence refines the earlier one. There is a canonical surjection

\[
S/{\equiv_{\mathrm{richer}}}\longrightarrow S/{\equiv_{\mathrm{earlier}}},
\qquad [s]_{\mathrm{richer}}\longmapsto[s]_{\mathrm{earlier}}.
\]

It preserves the old questions and actions. It need not make a newly accessible action well defined on the old quotient: that new action can be precisely what separates two previously equivalent states. The old abstraction can remain correct within its declared scope.

Three different changes therefore require different explanations:

| Change | What needs revision |
|---|---|
| New evidence defeats an identification within the original scope | A previously incorrect hypothesis about that scope |
| Newly relevant actions or consequences distinguish previously equivalent states | The scope and its exact abstraction |
| The process itself changes its response law | The earlier process model and its applicability |

The previous acquisition example includes the first kind. This theorem addresses the second while fixing the underlying process and old laws. The three/four-state environment alternatives in the method study must not be mislabeled as mere refinement of one unchanged process. Actual histories can combine these changes, but their composition requires an account.

For a directed family of expanding interfaces, every finite action word and question eventually occurs together at some stage. Consequently,

\[
\equiv_{\mathrm{union}}=\bigcap_i\equiv_i.
\]

Directedness matters: separate access to `a` in one scope and `b` in another does not establish a scope containing the mixed continuation `ab`. The equality above concerns all allowed finite continuations. If the final quotient has only finitely many classes, some stage already separates them all. The proof does not give a learner an effective stopping rule or a warranted bound on when that stage occurs.

## Compatible finite descriptions need not realize an infinite object

The refining quotients supply compatible descriptions of each actual configuration. Taking all stages together identifies its class under the union interface. The resulting map into the family of compatible profiles is injective. It need not be surjective.

An elementary example uses finite counts \(n\in\mathbb N\), the increment operation, and the stage-\(k\) observation

\[
q_k(n)=\min(n,k).
\]

At stage \(k\), every count at least \(k\) belongs to one tail class. Increment is well defined on these classes, so these are genuine compositional quotients. Later stages distinguish more counts. All stages together distinguish every pair of finite counts.

The profile whose stage-\(k\) value is always \(k\) is nevertheless compatible with every projection. Every finite collection of these requirements has a witness: choose a sufficiently large finite count. No finite count satisfies all requirements, since stage \(n+1\) distinguishes \(n\) from its tail class.

Thus exhaustive refinement recovers the identities of the existing finite counts while the full space of compatible profiles also contains an ideal infinite element. Adjoining that element is a coherent mathematical completion with an enlarged domain. It does not follow merely from each finite requirement being realizable in the original domain. The example neither denies infinite mathematical objects nor derives arithmetic from premise-free activity.

The exact realization condition is that each compatible family of classes has a nonempty intersection in the intended carrier. Compactness with suitable closed class fibers is one sufficient condition. Such a condition must be established where used. Calling all profiles expressions of one unfolding does not itself establish the intersection property.

## What this contributes to mathematical necessity

Within a specified interaction structure, an exact identity is constrained by all its admitted contexts. Its force exceeds observed repetition: a compositional proof can warrant unperformed finite continuations. Exact minimal representations then agree up to structure-preserving renaming. This is a precise form of representation-independent content within the declared scope.

Necessity across a class of realizations is stronger. An equation is necessary for that class only when every admitted realization satisfies it. The three-cycle equation does not become a law of all deterministic systems merely because it is exact and useful in its original setting. Likewise, neither a finite learning success nor an infinite compatible profile selects every intended mathematical domain.

The [core/tree rearticulation theorem](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/CORE-TREE-CONTINUATION.md#a-branch-can-become-a-root-articulation-without-gaining-independent-authority) supplies a related invariance within proof practice: replacing a basis by its closed derived tree preserves contextual derivability. A branch can become a reusable starting point while retaining its original warrant. This concerns deductive presentations; the interface-refinement theorem concerns process descriptions. Neither identifies all generative activity with deduction or proves that the physical learner executes that calculus.

Together these results support a more substantive constitutive candidate: **mathematical identity can articulate invariance under specified generative transformations, while mathematical practice forms, tests and revises its access to that invariance.** That proposal preserves a distinction between what is the case and what a situated learner has established, within one account of activity. It is a local account of behavioral and operational identity, not yet an account of every mathematical object, truth or necessity.
