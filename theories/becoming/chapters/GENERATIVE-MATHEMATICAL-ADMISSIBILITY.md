## Begin with finite constructions and their actual relationships

Let P describe specified finite partial constructions, ordered by extension: p ≤ q means that q preserves and extends the content of p. For a word, this can mean being a prefix. For a partial collection, it can mean inclusion in a larger finite collection.

This order is a modeling choice with a declared purpose. It is not the claim that every physical event accumulates information, that damage cannot occur, or that every later belief is better than an earlier one. Actual activity can erase a record or invalidate a prediction. A history prefix remains a prefix of its historical continuation even when the later situation loses the means to represent it. Which finite constructions are admitted, and which extensions preserve their meaning, must be established in the chosen application.

Call a collection I of such approximations coherent when it is nonempty, includes the prefixes or weaker approximations of its members, and any two members have a common extension **within I**. Thus it retains their common construction rather than combining incompatible branches by declaration. In order theory this is an ideal of P.

A proposed completed object can be characterized by exactly the approximations that belong to it. The completed infinite binary word `010101…`, for example, would have all its finite prefixes as its approximation profile. No individual prefix is the entire infinite word. Nor is a physical process required to finish infinitely many actions in order for us to specify the mathematical role of that word.

The [earlier identity analysis](IMMANENT-MATHEMATICAL-IDENTITY.md) already distinguished a coherent infinite profile from its realization in an original carrier. The present question is different: **when a completion is wanted, what conditions determine which completion it is?**

## A completion role that determines its own domain

Consider four requirements on a domain D containing faithful representatives of P:

1. **Preserve the finite order.** The embedding neither merges distinct finite approximations nor invents extension relations between them.
2. **Admit directed limits.** Every nonempty family whose members can be jointly extended within that family has a least upper bound in D.
3. **Finite content has a witness.** If a represented finite approximation lies below such a limit, it already lies below a member of the approximating family. A limit cannot make a new finite piece appear without a finite-stage witness. This is order-theoretic compactness, not a claim about spatial size or finite physical cost.
4. **No additional content beyond approximation.** Every object of D is the least upper bound of the nonempty directed family of represented finite approximations below it.

The [mathematical result](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSIBILITY-MATH.md) proves that these requirements force

\[
D\cong\operatorname{Idl}(P),
\]

where \(\operatorname{Idl}(P)\) is the collection of all nonempty directed lower subsets of P, ordered by inclusion. A finite p is represented by all approximations below p. A completed x is represented by all finite approximations below x. Directed completeness constructs an object from each coherent profile; the finite-witness condition recovers exactly that profile; the final condition excludes additional objects not determined by such profiles. The correspondence is unique when the designated finite embedding is preserved.

There is a second positive result. Every monotone interpretation of P into another domain admitting directed limits has a unique extension that preserves those limits:

\[
\widehat f(I)=\sup_{p\in I}f(p).
\]

The completed interpretation therefore cannot independently choose new values at the limits while retaining that continuity contract. This is the standard ideal-completion construction from domain theory, applied here as a test of mathematical admissibility. [Primary sources and exact antecedents](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSIBILITY-SOURCES.md) distinguish the theorem from our philosophical interpretation.

The qualification to monotone interpretations matters even for ordinary action words. Appending the same action on the right is not generally monotone in the prefix order: `a` is a prefix of `ab`, but `aa` is not a prefix of `aba`. Thus the result does not automatically extend the chronological action operations of the earlier free-word construction. Prefixing a fixed word is monotone; right appending needs a different interface or a separately proved interpretation. Temporal continuation and preservation of an approximation order are distinct relationships within the account.

The preservation claim is precise. It preserves the finite order and these limit-preserving interpretations. It is **not** preservation of every logical sentence. The finite unary words each have a proper extension; their completed domain contains an infinite word with no proper extension. Moreover, the predicate “is infinite” is false at every finite prefix and true at the infinite limit, so it does not preserve directed limits into the ordinary two-valued order. The extension theorem does not reconstruct arbitrary predicates or every truth from finite observation alone.

## What warrants this mathematical admission

The positive argument concerns a subject sought under an independently intelligible role: **represent all coherent extensions of the given finite content, without changing that content or introducing objects distinguished only by something outside it**. The four requirements articulate that role. They are not selected to name a prechosen completed carrier; the theorem derives the carrier and its comparison maps from them.

Within ordinary background mathematics, constructing the ideals proves that the role has a realization. The uniqueness theorem then shows that a different realization preserving the role is another presentation of the same completed subject. This supplies a defensible mathematical admission: the subject is the specified coherent-completion role, rather than an additional hidden bearer behind it. Its identity does not depend on which inscription, calculation or physical representation introduces it.

This applies the [earlier constitutive interpretation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/MATHEMATICS-GROUNDING-RESOLUTION.md) to a new, exact completion principle. The argument for adopting the role is that it answers a particular mathematical question without adding unexplained finite distinctions or unobservable duplicate completion points. That is a reason for this construction where the question is appropriate. It is not an argument that every mathematical subject or every real process must satisfy the same role.

In particular, the requirement to admit all coherent profiles is substantive. One could instead investigate only finite objects, computably described infinite words, or limits admitted by a restricted scheme. Those are different subjects. Their exclusion of some profiles may be justified by their own role; it is not refuted merely because the full ideal completion is available.

The result therefore changes one dependency: **we do not have to supply the completed domain independently once its finite basis and completion role are fixed**. It does not eliminate the need to justify that role, the finite basis, or the mathematical resources used to construct its ideals. This is relative constitutive explanation, not a derivation from a mathematics-free starting point.

## The word every carries the remaining infinity commitment

Two exact examples expose the reach of the principle.

| Finite basis and extension order | Its full ideal completion |
|---|---|
| Finite words, ordered by prefix | Finite words together with infinite words |
| Finite subsets of a fixed set A, ordered by inclusion | All subsets of A, ordered by inclusion |

For the second case, a subset S determines the directed family of its finite subsets. Conversely, the union of an ideal of finite subsets determines exactly that ideal: every finite part of the union is contained in a common member and belongs to the ideal by downward closure. Thus the correspondence is exact.

For unary word lengths, the first completion is \(\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}\), not the ordinary natural-number domain. Finite counting and completed approximation therefore specify different mathematical subjects even when they share the same finite constructions. A universal account cannot adopt this one completion role for every subject without preserving that difference.

This recovers full subset admission from an independently stated completion role. It also reveals where fullness entered. Requiring a supremum for **every** directed family includes the family of finite subsets of each external S. Restricting the families to those already computably describable would change the principle and would not justify full power-set admission by this proof.

The construction uses ordinary set-based mathematics, including a domain of subsets in which the ideals can be collected. That background cannot be erased by presenting the result as a construction. The advance is a characterization and explanatory organization of the commitment, not a proof of that entire background from the generative root.

## A coherent completed description need not be a possible infinite execution

The distinction matters to the existing [finite-history root](DEEPER-GENERATIVE-ROOT.md). Its history-language realization preserves which finite event words have an execution; it expressly does not promise to preserve infinite paths of the original process.

Consider a supplied process with one initial state. Its first event `c` chooses a finite natural-number counter n. Each later event `a` decreases that counter by one; at zero the process stops. For every finite k, the word `c a^k` can occur by choosing a sufficiently large n. Yet every individual execution has only finitely many `a` events.

The finite-word language has a coherent ideal containing every `c a^k`. Its completion therefore contains `c a a a…`. That is a legitimate completed word, but it is not an infinite execution of the supplied countdown law. The finite witnesses use different initial choices. Treating them as one execution would exchange

\[
\forall k\;\exists\text{ a compatible finite execution of length }k
\]

with

\[
\exists\text{ one infinite execution with all those prefixes}.
\]

The counterexample retains the actual event law and shows what the finite-word projection omits. It is a dynamic instance of the previous compatible-profile nonrealization result, not a refutation of the fuller nondual account or a claim that mathematics must always be physically instantiated.

There is also a positive conditional bridge. If the initial configurations are finite in number and each concrete configuration has only finitely many successors for the next required event, then feasibility of every prefix of a fixed infinite word yields a single coherent infinite path. The finite execution tree has arbitrarily large depth and finite branching, so König's lemma supplies that path. The [proof](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSIBILITY-MATH.md) states the classical background and its exact scope. This is a possible path of the complete declared transition law, not a physical guarantee that a path is enacted or a computable controller can select it.

The countdown process violates finite branching at its initial choice. No contradiction is hidden: the positive result identifies a concrete law property under which finite approximations do warrant an infinite path, and the counterexample shows why that property cannot be omitted without replacement.

## The result for the stronger root claim

The initial positive proposal can now be stated more sharply:

> **Where a mathematical subject is the full directed completion of a specified finite generative basis, faithful finite embedding, witnessed finite content and generation by approximations determine its completed domain uniquely. Under additional finite-branching realization conditions, coherent infinite word profiles also have paths in the supplied process.**

> **若一个数学对象的角色是某个明确的有限生成基础的有向完备化，并要求忠实保留有限结构、有限内容具有近似见证、每个对象都由其近似确定，那么这些条件可将完备域唯一确定到保留基础的同构。若再满足有限分支等实现条件，相容的无限词轮廓也对应于所给过程中的路径。**

Both formulations are AI-authored. The nondual account can use these results to explain mathematical idealization and its relation to actual generation without identifying the idealization with an enacted history. A supplied completion condition in this representation is not thereby outside generative reality; its origin and warrant are precisely the remaining explanatory question.

The direct attempt does not derive unrestricted completion from activity alone. “Activity can continue” does not entail “every coherent family has a completed object,” and even the latter does not identify one ultimate metaphysical ground. The independent scope of the completed conditional foundation remains unchanged.

The next substantive question is **which features of a generative context warrant a particular completion contract, and what explanatory work they do that an alternative structural account cannot match**. This study closes the relative identity and interpretation-extension part for directed completion. Extending it to all mathematical possibility and establishing unique ultimate constitution remain separate obligations. A contrary result would need to violate the proved characterization under its actual hypotheses, or show that the proposed completion role misdescribes the intended subject; changing the admission contract produces a different question.

Hand proofs and [independent review](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/MATHEMATICAL-ADMISSIBILITY-REVIEW.md) supply this round's mathematical validation. No finite enumeration is offered as evidence for an all-ideal theorem, and no previous experimental or formal result is rerun or replaced.

## When the subject warrants completion

6 October 2026 continuation, following [Ren's further instruction](../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-06-completion-warrant-continuation.md). The preceding theorem says what the completion role determines. The next argument tests **when an independently specified admissibility criterion requires that role**, then compares the explanation with a substantive rival. Its [proofs](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/COMPLETION-WARRANT-MATH.md), [review](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/COMPLETION-WARRANT-REVIEW.md) and [primary comparisons](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/COMPLETION-WARRANT-SOURCES.md) preserve the original completion result.

There is a precise condition that can warrant admission from finite grounds:

> **Whenever a candidate is excluded, some finite approximation it contains already makes every candidate containing that approximation inadmissible.**

This is finite refutability of admissibility. It is a condition on the intended subject, not a rule that whatever we have not managed to disprove must exist. The witness is a semantic reason for exclusion; an actual observer may lack a procedure for finding or recognizing it.

Let C be the admitted objects within the ambient ideal completion of P. Let Q contain exactly those finite approximations whose principal ideals belong to C. The new characterization establishes:

\[
\text{finite-refutable admission}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C=\operatorname{Idl}(Q),
\]

where Q is a lower subset of P and the ideals use its inherited order. Equivalently, C is closed under passing to weaker approximations and under nonempty directed unions. An excluded completed profile must contain an excluded finite piece; a profile all of whose pieces are admitted cannot be rejected only at its limit.

The reasoning is direct. If all finite pieces of an ideal are admitted, their principal ideals form a directed family. Closure puts its union in C. Conversely, if admission means containing only pieces from Q, any excluded ideal contains a piece outside Q, which is the required finite witness. The complete proof shows the equivalence with finite refutability, including empty admissible families. These are established domain-theoretic and safety-property ideas applied to the admission question, not a claim of mathematical priority.

This narrows the previously supplied completion premise. **For a subject whose exclusions are entirely finitely witnessed, full completion of its admissible finite basis is required, rather than merely one available choice.** The ambient mathematical universe in which the ideals are considered remains part of the metatheory. The theorem selects admission within that universe; it does not conjure the universe without prior commitments.

## Where finite grounds are warranted

A concrete case is the mathematical subject “all histories obeying these stated initial and transition conditions,” with every relevant state and intermediate relation retained. If those are the entire admission criteria, any violating history has an invalid initial state or an invalid step at a finite position. A history passing every such condition satisfies the specified subject's requirements. The completion rule is warranted by what the subject is required to preserve.

That argument has two important boundaries. First, it concerns full concrete histories. Projecting away which finite counter was chosen in the earlier countdown example loses the relationship needed to maintain one execution. The same observable word prefixes can then pass separately without forming a concrete path. The earlier realization conditions still apply.

Second, a violation at a finite position need not be ascertainable from finite measured information. For example, if admissibility requires an exact real value to be nonzero, then the exact value zero violates it. But every finite-precision interval around zero can still contain nonzero values. A finite list of exact states and a finite record of measurements are different approximation bases. Temporal locality alone does not make a constraint finitely refutable in every observation interface.

Thus the positive argument uses an independently identified contract: the given initial, transition and prefix conditions exhaust the relevant notion of valid history, and the chosen approximations preserve those conditions. If the intended subject also requires termination, fairness or another global property, that property must remain in the contract.

## Global requirements defeat the unrestricted inference

Consider infinite binary histories. Suppose a specified recurring-service requirement says that `1` must occur infinitely often. Every finite word can be extended to a history satisfying that requirement: append infinitely many ones. Yet the infinite all-zero history violates it.

No finite all-zero prefix proves that the recurring requirement will fail, because a later continuation can still satisfy it. This is a genuine global restriction with a clear role; it is not an arbitrary omission from an otherwise acceptable universe. Its short formulation does not turn it into a finitely witnessed violation.

Within the domain of finite and infinite words, retain every finite word as a partial candidate, and admit as completed histories only those with infinitely many ones. The directed chain

\[
\epsilon\leq0\leq00\leq000\leq\cdots
\]

consists of admitted partial candidates, but its limit is excluded. Requiring full directed closure would remove that restriction. In fact the least family closed under weaker approximations and directed unions and containing any given family C is

\[
\operatorname{Idl}\!\left(\bigcup_{I\in C}I\right).
\]

For the recurring-service example, every finite word occurs, so this closure admits every infinite binary word. The completion retains the finite possibility information while losing the global requirement. It cannot be advertised as preserving the original subject in full.

The finite-only subject supplies another case: every finite unary word is admitted while the infinite word is excluded, without an excluded finite prefix. An intended class of terminating executions likewise needs a termination contract, including an adequate account of final states; it is not automatically the full completion of its finite prefixes.

These cases establish a definite result: **the proposed universal inference from absence of a finite obstruction to admission fails.** They do not refute the conditional finite-refutation theorem or the possibility of one richer generative account explaining both local and global conditions. A finite description can specify an infinitary requirement. The stronger account must retain that content rather than silently replacing it with finite-prefix closure.

## A substantive rival preserves the positive result

The explanatory comparison should preserve what the generative account has achieved. Consider a structural-role account with the following commitments:

| Explanatory task | What the comparator retains |
|---|---|
| Mathematical identity | The same approximation order, compact basis and identity conditions |
| Completion | The same ideal construction, finite-refutation criterion and global counterexamples |
| Interpretation | The same continuous extension theorem and separately specified semantics |
| Actual mathematical practice | The same laws for formation, acquired distinctions, retained records and their use |
| Differentiated knowing and activity | The same situated relationships; no external spectator is introduced |

This account identifies the mathematical subject with its structural role and takes the role and its semantic relations to supply the explanation of the relevant truths. It treats the ultimate status of those structural or modal resources as a further question, rather than identifying them with the full substantive generative root. It is compatible with a nondual account of practice and need not introduce inert objects opposed to an active observer.

The comparator is an explicit philosophical construction for this test, not a claim that every historical structuralist endorses exactly this package. It is more substantive than changing an unconstrained grounding predicate: it states what explains the subject, how that explanation relates to practice, what resources it uses and where it withholds the additional identification.

At this interface, both accounts can derive the same completion and admit exactly the same objects under the same contract. Both need the basis, the constraint semantics and the background mathematics. Our generative account's unification of formation, inquiry and interpretation is an explanatory virtue, but this comparison has not shown that the rival cannot preserve those connections. Calling that whole package one root does not, by itself, reduce the commitments it contains.

Nor does this establish that the accounts are equally good in every respect or that the rival is a completed metaphysics. A demonstrated difference in explanatory dependencies, modal adequacy, economy or correspondence could favor one. The present completion argument supplies no such discriminator for **ultimate exclusivity**. If their structural content is shown equivalent, that establishes equivalence at the specified mathematical role; it does not alone decide the direction of ultimate grounding.

## What this continuation settles

There is now a positive criterion for a class of completion choices: **when all exclusion is finitely witnessed in an adequate approximation basis, the admissible completion is forced by the admitted finite content**. This is stronger than merely stipulating an arbitrary completed domain. The argument is warranted for the full-state, local-law validity role just described and can be tested for other subjects.

Two stronger inferences have been tested and do not follow. Finite formulation does not make every admissibility condition finitely refutable; global requirements preserve counterexamples. Unique realization of the completion role does not make the generative interpretation its uniquely adequate ultimate ground; the structural comparator remains live.

For this route, the remaining claim should therefore be stated as an explanatory programme rather than a deferred routine proof: explain why particular mathematical contexts have their actual local or global admissibility conditions, and whether their constitutive relationships require the substantive generative account rather than a matched alternative. A further completion theorem alone cannot settle that comparison. The established conditional foundation and the earlier positive constitutive interpretation retain their scope.

A finite proof is not a finite observed prefix. Lean can check a finite argument that a specified rule entails an event after every future position. It can also check that recurrence as a property of arbitrary histories has no finite bad-prefix characterization. These results coexist: the proof reasons from the full stated rule, while the observation records only a finite part of the history. Neither result says that every true infinitary claim has a finite proof in the chosen system.

The difference matters for the repository’s [normative first priority](UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md#first-priority-universal-normative-grounding). Suppose a defended ethical reason requires eventual or recurring response within a particular relationship. A revision that preserves every finite opportunity to respond can still permit never responding. The checked recurrence counterexample shows the mathematical failure of that proposed preservation criterion. An implementation must retain the complete requirement whose preservation it claims, including relevant global content.

That argument does not establish the first ethical worth of the response. Its scope, burdens, exceptions and legitimate discharge still need substantive defense within the whole relationship. Some undertakings properly finish, some claims are fulfilled, and some continuing channels should close. A universal instruction to retain every possible continuation would ignore those distinctions. The formal completion theorem cannot decide which ethical admission contract is warranted.

This analysis remains monist and relational. Proof formation, retained evidence, changing conduct and experienced consequences are differentiations within the unfolding under investigation. The finite-witness distinction classifies properties of those same histories; it does not posit a separate ethical realm or assume constraint and flow as independent kinds. What must be explained is the relationship through which a particular consideration has practical force, and how that force can remain faithfully represented or legitimately change.

The informed-finality rival can accept all of these mathematical proofs and still argue that a particular achievement or burden warrants ending assessment. Answering it requires defending the relevant ethical priority, not imposing recurrence or openness as the meaning of goodness. Likewise, the structural rival can accept the checked completion theorem without accepting uniquely generative metaphysical grounding. Formal verification strengthens the mathematical evidence; it does not by itself resolve either philosophical comparison.

The resulting next connection is precise: **defend the practical force and scope of the actual whole relationship, then verify that proposed preservation through learning or revision retains that content**. The finite/infinite mathematics now supplies a checked test for a particular loss of content. It supplies neither the missing universal ethical justification nor a proof that such justification is impossible.
