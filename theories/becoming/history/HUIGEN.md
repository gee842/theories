## 1. The whole claim, without reducing its ambition

The preserved [root-of-wisdom record](../../../sources/20-codex-theory-sessions-2026-10-05/root-of-wisdom/outputs/research-ledger.md) already connects a finite root with unbounded understanding. Its [synthesis](../../../sources/20-codex-theory-sessions-2026-10-05/root-of-wisdom/outputs/root-of-wisdom-synthesis.md) distinguishes sound extension, completeness, empirical discovery and ethical understanding. Those earlier results are inherited here; they are not new discoveries of this round.

Ren's stronger proposal requires four achievements:

1. **A necessary root:** identify substantive conditions that adequate scientific inquiry cannot dispense with, without defining adequate inquiry as agreement with our theory.
2. **Valid generation:** explain why the permitted steps preserve validity, including when acquired results become premises for further work.
3. **Whole-theory coverage:** derive the actual foundational commitments through organization, significance, learning, revision of selectors, concern and wisdom.
4. **Open substantive development:** demonstrate a method that continues producing useful distinctions and explanations beyond every fixed finite collection.

These obligations preserve the [governing research goal](GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md). Constructive and destructive regimes both belong in the account. A necessary explanation of how concern can form need not predict that every system becomes caring. Universal favorable direction remains a separate hypothesis.

“Entire theory” means the intended, justified foundation with its actual scope. It does not mean conjoining every archived claim, including preserved failures. Equally, we cannot redefine it as whichever fragments have already passed verification and declare victory. A contingent application must retain its contingent premises; an intended categorical commitment needs a categorical justification.

## 2. The exact transfer theorem

Let \(\mathcal I\) be an independently specified, **nonempty** class of realizations of adequate inquiry. Let \(H\) be a proposed root and \(\mathcal R\) a specified family of validity-preserving rules. Define

\[
T=\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathcal R}(H).
\]

Here the closure consists of claims with finite derivations, and contains every root premise. A claim is necessary relative to \(\mathcal I\) when it holds in every member of that class. For sound rules,

\[
\boxed{\quad
\text{every claim in }T\text{ is necessary}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every premise in }H\text{ is necessary}.
\quad}
\]

**Forward:** each root premise is itself in the closure. **Reverse:** induct on a derivation. Its root premises hold throughout \(\mathcal I\); each inference preserves that property. Thus the argument covers every finite descendant, however far development continues. It is not restricted to the examples we happened to inspect.

If a derivation uses additional premises \(A\), its common conclusion is appropriately conditional: \(A\Rightarrow q\). It does not promote the actual truth of \(A\), or an unconditional assertion of \(q\), into a necessity. An independently justified local input can discharge that condition for the relevant application. A previously derived theorem can be reused as a lemma without enlarging the justified basis.

The [formal construction](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-GENERATIVE-CLOSURE.md) implements this argument with explicit assumption contexts. The [Lean sources and verification](../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/lean/inquiry-root/README.md) cover a specified inference fragment, including implications, conjunctions and natural-number universal instantiation. This semantic formalization is not a completeness theorem for all scientific reasoning or an automatically effective enumerator of every truth.

The theorem establishes the architecture of the strong claim. It does **not** prove the premise that our proposed root is necessary. Nor does it prove that every intended component of our theory has been derived from that root. Those are now separate, inspectable obligations. Nonemptiness matters: an impossible inquiry class would make every necessity claim vacuously true.

The [reliability continuation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#34-experiential-reliability-and-the-necessity-of-generated-claims) makes the scope of sound generation more explicit. An inductive method may justify a fallible judgment without making its content a necessary truth. A mathematical guarantee can hold necessarily under its calibration and distribution premises while actual estimates remain uncertain. The root can support discovery, hypothesis formation and correction; each product must retain its inferential status. Necessity of an appropriate rule would not make every provisional output infallible.

### Different representations can share the same necessary root

The [further argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-GENERATIVE-CLOSURE.md#9-one-coherent-interpretation-can-cover-the-unbounded-closure) permits different languages and encodings while requiring one coherent interpretation of the joint theory within each inquiry realization. If the root is finite, its conjunction is a generated claim. One admissible interpretation satisfying that conjunction satisfies every root premise; sound generation then covers all descendants. In fact, under these assumptions, an interpretation for every member of the *entire* closure already yields a common one through the root conjunction. Checking only isolated inventory rows does not meet that stronger condition.

This gives the infinity claim a concrete advantage: a finite joint foundation and a general preservation argument can carry an unbounded development. It supplies neither an effective search for the right interpretation nor evidence that our substantive root has one in every adequate inquiry. An infinite schema cannot silently acquire finite-basis status, and quantifier domains must remain intact.

A new notation may preserve a necessary relation; renaming a resource counter as welfare does not establish it. Likewise, the existence of a decoder for each sensor calibration does not give that calibration to the learner. The root must explain actual acquisition where acquisition is claimed, while defending the interpretation independently of whether it makes the desired conclusion true.

## 3. A positive witness of unbounded development

The [new construction](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-GENERATIVE-CLOSURE.md) uses two simple, fully specified interfaces. They test valid generation; their particular laws are declared local inputs.

An instrument reports \(y=\min(h,n)\), where \(h\) is a hidden nonnegative integer and \(n\) its capacity. Consider the question “is \(h\le k\)?” Exact recovery for every hidden value is possible **if and only if \(k<n\)**. When \(k<n\), the observed value determines the answer. When \(k\ge n\), the two hidden values \(k\) and \(k+1\) produce the same report and require different answers.

This gives an indefinitely extensible family: capacity \(k+1\) answers threshold question \(k\), while capacity \(k\) cannot. Every capacity has a further unresolved threshold, and the sets of answerable questions differ at every successive capacity. The infinity here is substantive difference in questions and capabilities, rather than repeated rewording of a single conclusion.

The second interface is an actual transition process: each action consumes one unit of a finite reserve. In the repository's existing trace semantics, a sequence of \(m\) actions is realizable from reserve \(b\) **if and only if \(m\le b\)**. No fixed reserve realizes all horizons. Each longer horizon therefore supplies a new operating requirement. The formal development generates observation and support constraints from one reusable basis while retaining their conditions.

These results demonstrate unbounded *potential* application of a finite argument. They do not imply infinite actual execution, formation of every needed instrument, or universal ethical wisdom. All proved conditional laws hold in their stipulated model class; the operational predicates and capability sets are what vary. We do not claim infinitely many mutually nonequivalent truth values among universally valid theorems.

The earlier [constructive root-of-wisdom study](../../../sources/20-codex-theory-sessions-2026-10-05/root-of-wisdom/outputs/root-of-wisdom-formalization.md) provides a richer precedent. Linear, autonomous, nearest-neighbor dynamics on a finite ring, with translation/reflection symmetry, conservation and positivity, force a nonnegative discrete diffusion law. The argument derives a law form from independently stated constraints; it does not derive those constraints or the measured rate from bare existence. That is a useful model for productive scientific extension: reveal what the justified premises entail, then identify their actual realization.

The inherited arithmetic incompleteness result remains compatible with indefinitely many valid developments. It limits effective complete enumeration under its precise arithmetic hypotheses, not open-ended insight. New empirical information requires a warranted acquisition route. A root cannot generate an unknown measurement merely by declaring itself universal.

## 5. The ethical bridge now has an exact test

**Scope of this historical sequence:** these local arguments retain their results and counterexamples. Under the current [whole-process direction](UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md#the-whole-as-the-grounding-question), they do not supply the foundation’s mandatory initial divisions. The [latest formation test](#when-generativity-forms-an-additional-life) addresses ethical quality of the whole history.

Suppose \(r(a,i)\) says whether harm to center \(i\) supplies actor \(a\) a reason against imposing it. In a homogeneous fixed context with at least two centers, require invariance under every simultaneous renaming:

\[
r(\sigma a,\sigma i)=r(a,i).
\]

There are exactly two classes of ordered pairs under this operation: **self** \((a=i)\) and **other** \((a\ne i)\). Renaming preserves equality. Consequently, an invariant binary relation has one value \(\alpha\) on self-pairs and an independently chosen value \(\beta\) on other-pairs. Positive self-standing fixes \(\alpha=1\), but permits both \(\beta=0\) and \(\beta=1\).

Thus even universal consistency of the rule “each actor counts its own harm” does not derive cross-center standing. The [general hand proof](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-FULL-THEORY-AUDIT.md#42-two-orbit-lemma) is corroborated by [exhaustive enumeration](../../../experiments/integrated-correction/general-framework/generative-basis/necessity-symmetry/README.md) of all **66,066 binary relations for one to four centers**. This is an abstract logical check, not an empirical ethics result. More elaborate context-dependent equivariance need not have only two classes.

Two positive routes remain:

- **Reason-bearer independence:** establish that the patient's independently identified harm supplies the relevant reason, and that changing the deliberator alone does not erase it. In the simple model, \(r(a,i)=r(i,i)=1\) then gives standing for every actor. This is stronger than simultaneous renaming. Its justification must show why the actor–patient relation cannot remove the reason in question.
- **Shared practical justification:** establish why consequential shared power must answer the justified claims of affected centers. An action need not satisfy every wish; it must meet the independently justified demands included in the claimed shared standard. When an avoidably harmful action fails one such demand and a harmless alternative satisfies all, the former fails that standard. Genuine conflicts can leave no jointly satisfactory option and require further judgment.

A further [prospective-protection argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-FULL-THEORY-AUDIT.md#a-further-constructive-attempt-generate-protection-before-roles-are-settled) supplies a candidate formation route. If each participant genuinely might occupy a vulnerable future role, a shared rule preventing its harm at no other personal cost strictly improves each participant's prospect under the declared dominance criterion. Initially self-directed concerns can therefore favor a common protective rule without a benevolent reward assumption. This is a conditional preference argument; the actual acquisition and installation of the selector remain to be constructed. Known roles, abandonment after role revelation and sufferers outside the possible participant roles expose the additional requirements for universal standing.

The decisive case is an affected center that is **dispensable to the investigator, unable to argue, and unable to reward or punish**. If the root still explains why its harm supplies a reason, we have advanced the ethical foundation beyond epistemic usefulness. Merely describing the harm accurately does not complete that argument. Merely defining “harm” as “a reason for everyone” would conceal the needed premise.

An accurate oppressor illustrates the separation of factual competence from benevolent motivation. It does not prove that the oppressor has no moral reasons. The stronger question concerns the validity of reasons that an agent might ignore. This distinction keeps the normative investigation open rather than dismissing it through an inadequate counterexample.

### Patient scope and deliberator scope are separate obligations

**Further investigation, 6 October 2026:** the [practical-necessity audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md) and [exact reason-transport analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md) advance this bridge. Let \(A\) be deliberators and \(P\) patients; some patients cannot deliberate. A reason token must distinguish

\[
R(a,p,c,k):
\text{patient }p\text{ supplies deliberator }a
\text{ a reason of kind }k\text{ in context }c.
\]

There are now two questions. **Patient-ground extension** asks whether the feature that justified a familiar reason also occurs in another patient, including a patient unable to articulate it. **Deliberator extension** asks why that patient-grounded reason addresses a different decision maker. Recognizing another's experience accurately performs neither step by definition. Establishing actor independence also cannot create a first positive reason about a patient outside all the actors' self-cases.

The exact result is: positive anchors and justified reason-preserving implications force a target reason **if and only if a finite chain of those implications reaches it**. If no chain does, assigning positive status exactly to the reachable tokens satisfies all those stated premises while leaving the target unforced. This is a countermodel to entailment from those premises, not a discovery that the patient's reason is false. The argument concerns the specified positive unary implication fragment; richer grounds need their own representation.

The result also identifies what an added principle must accomplish: it must supply an anchor or cross a previously unsupported division separating the target from the grounds. Alternative principles can supply different routes. Merely adding an edge to a diagram does not justify it.

A small example makes the two tasks visible. Two deliberators \(a,b\) initially recognize their own patient cases \(p_a,p_b\). A third patient \(s\) cannot deliberate. Consider all actor-changing transfers at a fixed patient, and one patient-changing transfer \(p_a\to s\) at a fixed actor, each only if independently justified:

| Supplied valid transfers | Forced tokens among the six actor–patient pairs |
|---|---:|
| Neither family | 2: the two original self-cases |
| Actor transfers only | 4: both actors for each original patient; none for \(s\) |
| Patient transfer only | 3: the original anchors plus \((a,s)\) |
| Both families | 6: every actor–patient pair |

These are hand-derived finite cases, not additional experiments or Lean results. Their significance is that protecting nonparticipants requires examining the ground of their standing as well as the reason's authority across deliberators. The relevant patient ground might be experienced badness rather than rational agency. The theory must defend that identification and its scope.

### A positive argument and a stronger rival

The strongest current positive reconstruction begins with an independently warranted reason against a narrowly identified aversive loss in a self-case. It then asks **what made that reason valid**. If its sufficient ground is the subject's aversive condition, preserving that ground and its applicability conditions preserves the reason. If the ground includes an essential relation to the current deliberator, the extension needs a further argument. “It is someone else's loss” names the disputed relation; it does not establish whether that relation is justificatorily essential.

This gives a precise test. Hold the offered sufficient ground and all admitted defeaters fixed while changing the deliberator or the patient's ability to communicate. A position that accepts the ground as still sufficient while cancelling every reason is inconsistent. A rival that argues the self-relation was part of the ground disputes the premise instead. That rival must be assessed substantively; logical consistency alone does not make its proposed ground credible or right.

The new audit constructs such a rival with accurate inquiry, retained evidence, selector revision, full representation of others' experiences and open criticism of its own commitments. It acknowledges each patient's own standpoint reason, including the silent patient's, while asserting that reasons for a deliberator's action are addressed only to appropriately related agents. This is a more demanding comparator than an indifferent machine. The current causal and epistemic premises do not yet refute its reason-indexing claim. Conversely, its ability to operate does not establish that its ethical account is correct.

There is already a positive constraint on **shared justification**. If a procedure claims to have answered every admitted affected standpoint's valid objections, it cannot truthfully certify success while one remains unanswered. Silencing a patient or removing their usefulness does not answer an objection whose ground remains. Under a complete no-cost harm comparison with no overriding reason, the controller's lack of private loss does not discharge the patient's claim. This result requires the admitted reason and claimed scope of justification; it does not manufacture universal standing from the wording of a certificate.

### The connection to actual reason formation

The accepted [recorded-reasons construction](RECORDED-REASONS.md) already separates authentic observations, factual calibration, a positive reason and valid relevance rules. Direct inspection of its [semantic theorem](../../../experiments/shared-renewal-capability/regulation/retention/formation/learning-interface/recording/reason-replay/formal/ReasonReplay.lean) confirms that `paid_recorded_reason_sound` takes both the anchor and relevance implication as premises. Its arbitrary reason predicate can include a fixed deliberator in its interpretation. There is no implementation defect here. An application exporting its result to a different deliberator must establish the corresponding scope; copying a record cannot supply that normative extension.

A valid reason, a reason supported by the declared grounds, an accessible certificate, a completed resource-dependent reconstruction and a delivered protection are different things. An unsupported token can have another valid ground outside the current graph. A sound reason can fail to motivate action or lack means of implementation. The transport analysis additionally shows, under a declared bounded-gain implementation, why positive influence need not exceed the threshold needed to defeat suppression. Its numerical influence is not moral worth. These distinctions make the next generative construction more exact without treating its evaluative inputs as already justified.

### Future concern, branching and inherited claims

The [self-ground audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md) and [generative-identity analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-GENERATIVE-IDENTITY.md) test the restriction that only a deliberator's own experience can supply a reason. An admitted reason to prevent future suffering already concerns something not presently felt. If it survives the end of all future undertakings and deliberation, preserving agency cannot exhaust its ground. These are conditional conceptual tests: the prospective reason and the relevant experience remain premises, and bodily or historical relations may still matter.

There is a further positive extension. Suppose a reason concerning one future continuer survives adding an equally qualified second continuer, with its sufficient ground and applicability preserved and no new defeating cost or conflict. Suppose judgments also respect the symmetry of the complete relevant situation. The original actor then has a reason concerning each branch. Since two distinct future persons cannot both be numerically identical to one original person, numerical identity cannot be necessary for every such reason. Both branches exist in this stipulated case; this is not uncertainty about which one will become real.

The important premise is **branch nonerasure**. A defender of strict identity can dispute it by making unique continuation part of the original ground. That response must explain why another branch cancels a reason concerning the first patient's otherwise unchanged suffering. The result is a controlled challenge to a specific restriction, not an empirical demonstration of personal fission or a proof that everyone is one subject.

Changing the deliberator is a further step. Let a lineage interpretation grant reasons only for patients in a deliberator's continuation scope \(S(a)\). An ancestor can include both branches while each successor's scope excludes the sibling. For a transitive continuation relation, the scopes contract along succession. Preserving all the ancestor's reasons for still-relevant harmed patients instead requires

\[
S(a)\cap D\cap H\ \subseteq\ S(b),
\]

where \(D\) is the domain whose context remains valid and \(H\) is the set with the admitted harm-ground. This exact condition does not follow from descent. Neither a common source nor a common future reverses a directed continuation relation.

A stronger, constructive result follows when the successor legitimately continues **the same responsibility for the same patients**. With a warranted role, a fixed patient constituency and still-valid grounds, the successor inherits those reasons, including a reason concerning its sibling. Legitimate accepted delegation supplies another concrete route to other-directed reasons. These routes preserve differentiated patients and allow correction of mistaken, expired or defeated claims. Their authority is an explicit premise; memory, byte copying and causal ancestry do not establish it. A program that continues saying “my future” can change its referent while copying its code perfectly.

Finally, showing that continuity or commitment supplies a reason does not show that it supplies every reason. An exclusive lineage account owes an explanation of why an unrelated patient's experience supplies **no** baseline reason. The universal account owes a positive defense of that baseline. Neither position wins merely because the other's proof is unfinished.

### Practical congruency and an identifiable ground

The [primary-source investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md) identifies a stronger candidate bridge: **practical congruency across standpoints**. In our reconstruction, an adequate impersonal practical account should preserve a reason to promote an admitted end, beyond recording that someone has an indexed reason. Its appeal is that a change in how a reason is encountered should not extinguish its justification. Its burden is to establish which features belong only to the route of encounter and which belong to the ground itself.

The [direct adjudication](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#10-practical-congruency-tested-on-an-unrelated-actor) now isolates two feasible actions with equal finite effort and cost. Their effects on the actor's projects, responsibilities and other relevant consequences are fixed; only an unrelated patient's aversive episode differs. **Giving one's own interests extra priority does not establish that the other's condition has zero weight.** The unchanged private considerations supply no differential reason against relief. If a positive patient-grounded reason exists and no overriding consideration is omitted, it favors the relieving option. The existence of that baseline remains the substantive premise.

Our present normative assessment favors the baseline in this matched case: it preserves the offered experiential ground, permits special responsibilities and explains why otherwise pointless preventable suffering counts against a choice. An exclusive account needs a justification for cancelling every such reason. This is **defeasible normative warrant**, not a proof of metaphysical necessity or of indispensability to scientific inquiry. The proposed categorical authority does not depend for its ground on a sound route from the actor's antecedent motivational commitments. This is stronger than independence from present endorsement, and does not mean an absolute duty overriding every other claim.

The strongest rival also requires more careful treatment. Absence of current concern is different from absence of every sound deliberative route to concern. Corrected understanding and justified revision can produce motives absent from the initial policy. A finite model closed under its chosen transitions does not establish closure under all adequate practical reasoning. The earlier [countermodel audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md#5-a-countermodel-with-the-declared-scientific-and-reflective-capacities) is expressly corrected: it preserves the **declared** scientific and reflective capacities, not an established ideal competence about all true reasons. Its non-entailment result remains; it has not refuted every sound generative extension.

The [typed analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#9-practical-congruency-preserving-truth-warrant-and-an-addressee) separates knowledge of \(R(a,p,c,k)\) from a reason addressed to the observer. Factive knowledge yields the original reason, not \(R(b,p,c,k)\) or motivation in \(b\). Even an impersonal favorable assessment needs a justified connection to an actual action-reason. A case with one permissible, capable controller makes that assignment obligation precise; declaring that some capable actor has the reason would supply the disputed bridge rather than prove it. Conversely, a complete factive oracle of all addressed reasons could not remain fixed while the target reason changed between countermodels.

There is a genuine conditional necessity when an interpersonal claim is already accepted. If a philosophy holds that another actor has a reason to relieve its proponent's suffering on a fully specified ground, it must preserve that reason across cases satisfying the same sufficient ground. This begins with an expressly interpersonal claim; it does not derive one from private prudence. A ground extending to noncommunicating patients can cover them without reciprocity or reward. Philosophies making no such claim need the further grounding argument.

The proposed experience must also be identifiable. The [primary-evidence study](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md) distinguishes stimulus, sensory intensity, unpleasantness, response and reason. Its new operational comparison reads existing recipient-correction results: better recipient support can accompany equal expected private fuel and expenditure. That excludes one private-resource advantage; it does not identify suffering, all personal interests or absence of every internal reason. The actual [semantic-action interface](SEMANTIC-ACTION-COMPOSITION.md) likewise shows why preserving record bits can misaddress a patient when their meanings change. These inherited mechanisms constrain interpretation and implementation while leaving the normative ground explicit.

### Perspective-taking and the preservation of a reason

The next [constructive investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#11-imaginative-uptake-without-substituting-the-observer) asks whether understanding another's situation can yield warranted concern that the actor initially lacked. The proposed process identifies the actual patient's condition, imagines its significance **for that patient**, tests the practical ground from the actor's actual position, and retains or revises the judgment when attention returns. Patient evidence constrains imagination; the observer's preferences do not define someone else's welfare.

The [frame analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#10-imaginative-perspective-taking-reversible-frames-and-new-warrant) establishes a precise limitation. Faithfully translating “patient \(p\) has a reason concerning \(p\)” between perspectives preserves its subject and addressee. It does not yield a reason addressed to observer \(b\). Even reversibility is insufficient: exchanging two patient names and later exchanging them back restores the original record while permitting a misaddressed action in between. Meaning must remain correct when the judgment guides action.

There is also a positive result. If an independently justified practical premise connects ground \(G(p,c)\) to an actual-actor reason \(R(b,p,c,k)\), evidence and reflection that newly warrant \(G\) permit the actor to recognize that reason. An initial helping motive is unnecessary for this inference. Reflection might instead justify a revised practical premise, but then its justification must be supplied. A change of frame can make an implication accessible to a bounded thinker without creating a new fact; its processing and retention still have costs.

**Fading imaginative salience alone does not defeat a warranted reason.** The actual claim \(R(b,p,c,k)\) retains its warrant when the applicable premises and supporting evidence remain warranted and no independent defeater appears. Returning to ordinary attention supplies no defeater by itself. This is a constraint on justified judgment, not a prediction of unavoidable motivation. New evidence can reverse the judgment: an action imagined as relieving might actually aggravate this patient's condition. Conversely, the disappearance of the observer's simulated discomfort need not improve the patient at all. Learning can change the judgment brought back; it need not reset the mind to its earlier verdict.

The [selective empirical dossier](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md#6-perspective-taking-must-earn-its-epistemic-role) keeps emotion, accuracy and reported moral judgment separate. Its primary readings give reasons to test each rather than treating increased empathy as verification of all three. They establish neither ethical truth nor universal failure of perspective-taking. Testimony can correct projection where available; a noncommunicating patient still requires warranted evidence rather than invented preferences. The proposed discrimination tests have not been run.

This connection gives plural possibilities substantive force. If different patients need opposite actions, the same warranted concern can support opposite commands. A common generative ground therefore requires differentiated understanding at its applications. Uniform treatment and merged identities are unnecessary. The remaining foundational task is to justify the ground's authority for the unrelated actor, including under informed disagreement. Successful representation cannot supply that authority by itself, while an absent initial motive cannot establish that sound recognition is impossible.

### What scientific normativity can require

The [next argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#12-what-scientific-normativity-can-require-of-the-moral-dispute) tests a more direct connection to scientific philosophy. Grant genuine reasons to assess evidence correctly, even when the warranted conclusion is unwanted. This grants normative authority, beyond accurate outputs or a private reward. It does not settle which philosophical account of that authority is correct.

There is a definite constraint on rejecting concern. Suppose a philosophy accepts an epistemic reason but rejects moral reasons solely because they possess a feature it treats as sufficient to disqualify any reason. If its accepted epistemic reason has that same feature, it cannot retain both the reason and the unrestricted veto. It must revise the veto, establish a relevant difference or revise its epistemology. **Authority beyond an actor's current desires cannot be an indiscriminate objection to morality while being admitted for evidence.**

This result has a precise limit. A shared theoretical difficulty can count against two domains while stronger independent support favors one. Equal objections do not establish equal overall warrant. Nor does the inability to offer a justified argument for epistemic nihilism, by itself, prove that nihilism false. The positive patient-grounded argument must still be assessed. Even establishing some moral reasons would leave the intended universal patient scope to derive.

The [stronger positive comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#9-can-scientific-normativity-warrant-extension-to-the-patients-good) has two parts. First, demonstrate that particular norms indispensable to adequate inquiry already have moral authority; calling epistemic diligence “moral” is insufficient. Second, assess extension beyond that initial range. If a further patient judgment concerns the same admitted kind of normative fact, restricting its instances no longer eliminates that kind from the account. The patient judgment still needs its own support. Our matched case supplies an identified adverse difference, feasible relief and no countervailing difference in the admitted interests or permissions. These support the baseline as a comparative normative judgment. Removing an inconsistent objection can restore that support without proving every possible objection false.

This also challenges an unargued restriction on legitimate warrant. Being indispensable to theorizing is one proposed justification; it cannot simply be stipulated to be the only possible justification. A wider account can be better supported by the patient's case even if its rejection is not a logical contradiction. Conversely, ordinary moral appearances require criticism, particularly where projection or exclusion may distort them. The research retains these evidential and evaluative obligations rather than treating either familiarity or ontological economy as decisive by definition.

The [mechanism analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#11-epistemic-normativity-and-protection-of-an-evidence-source) also obtains a conditional practical result. If a patient's testimony is the only available evidence distinguishing two cases that require different answers, losing it defeats the claimed reliable answer. The inquirer must preserve the channel, provide an adequate substitute, or narrow or withdraw that claim. Protection becomes a required means only if an independently justified requirement to complete the inquiry remains and an appropriate means principle applies. Correct belief does not entail unlimited evidence acquisition.

This can give a practical reason to safeguard a person without private reward. Its present ground is that person's role in inquiry; the same inference can safeguard an instrument. The decisive further test holds fulfilment of the epistemic requirements fixed while the patient's avoidable suffering differs. Honest beliefs can reflect the different outcomes without either action violating those stated epistemic requirements. A reason grounded in the patient's condition could still favor relief, but the inquiry-means argument no longer supplies it.

The [strengthened capacity audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md#11-preserve-epistemic-authority-when-testing-the-practical-bridge) therefore preserves categorical epistemic reasons in its rival interpretation. It does not pretend that predictive success exhausts justified inquiry, or that the rival contains every true practical reason. The gain for 慧根 is a stronger constraint on skeptical rejection and a scoped route from inquiry to protection. The remaining bridge must justify the patient's practical significance beyond usefulness as an information source.

### Responsibility created through scientific communication

The [new inquiry](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#13-can-assurance-make-the-listener-a-claimant) finds a route beyond protecting useful evidence sources. A speaker can undertake responsibility toward a listener. If that undertaking is legitimate, the listener's claim can survive the end of the research and their usefulness to it. This concerns an actual interaction that creates responsibility; it does not make every utterance a promise.

Consider a researcher who supplies a warranted provisional result and legitimately undertakes to notify a recipient of a material revision discovered before a stated date. The inquiry ends. Within the period, the researcher discovers such a revision. Ending the project did not fulfil the notice undertaking. The [temporal construction](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#12-assurance-can-create-a-claim-that-outlasts-inquiry) derives continued applicability while the claim remains valid and undischargeable by that event. Performance, authorized release and applicable expiry can terminate a claim; a conflicting reason may override action without erasing it. The proof preserves a legitimately created claim. Its creation rule remains a substantive premise.

The stronger moral argument concerns **deliberately invited dependence**. A speaker knows another's consequential decision, misrepresents the available support despite accessible contrary evidence, and foreseeably induces reasonable reliance that leaves an avoidable loss. These facts identify a possible ground of responsibility without already calling the treatment morally wrong. The affirmative judgment is that the speaker owes an appropriate response to the person whose exposure they deliberately shaped. Its support comes from that person's affected agency and interests together with the speaker's role in creating the burden. Future scientific utility is unnecessary to this justification.

Accurate retraction can correct the epistemic record while leaving the practical loss. A claim to repair therefore cannot be discharged merely by restoring correct beliefs, if the responsibility ground is justified and the loss remains. Conversely, an innocent error does not automatically establish blame or unlimited compensation. Correction, notification, performance and repair each need their own conditions. The [practical audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md#12-creating-a-claim-is-different-from-communicating-a-prediction) also rejects deriving an obligation to make every prediction come true from an obligation to speak responsibly.

A serious rival can retain epistemic reasons and a practice's challenge rights while disputing genuine moral wronging or categorical practical authority. The existence of a rule cannot settle that dispute. The positive dependence argument gives a specific moral ground to assess; a full necessity argument must additionally establish why an adequate philosophy cannot reject its authority. Even an accepted moral duty requires a defended connection to reasons independent of every actor's antecedent motivations if that stronger authority is claimed.

The beneficiary boundary matters. A former listener's inability to complain need not discharge a valid outstanding claim. Someone who never received an assurance presents a further case. Information reaching that person, or a promise to a caregiver benefiting them, does not alone establish that the same directed claim is theirs. Legitimate representation or a justified responsibility for imposed risks may extend the scope. Universal patient standing still requires its own ground. This round advances a relational source of concern and a positive moral defense of it; it does not turn the existence of scientific communication into proof of the whole ethical foundation.

### Concern for a patient who never participated

The [next argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#14-imposed-burdens-and-the-nonparticipating-patient) considers a subject who never accepted an assurance and cannot communicate. An actor can choose between two equally feasible interventions serving the same legitimate purpose. One imposes a reliably identified severe aversive episode on the subject; the other avoids it at the same cost without moving a comparable burden elsewhere. The positive judgment favors avoidance because of the patient's burden and the actor's control of its imposition. The patient's ability to protest is unnecessary to this proposed ground.

This is a moral argument, not a deduction from causal control alone. It explains why an invitation can be unnecessary while the patient's condition remains relevant. Communication can reveal preferences or authorize a limited intervention; losing that channel does not itself erase the experienced burden. Neither consent nor refusal may be invented. Inability to consent also does not forbid every rescue: a justified intervention to avert an existing serious harm differs from an unsolicited enhancement that creates a burden.

The [causal comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#13-imposed-burdens-and-rescue-share-an-opportunity-not-necessarily-a-history) adds an exact identification result. Let an earlier actor intervention S or independent condition Z produce burden B=S∨Z; a present repair R yields H=B(1−R). Histories (S,Z)=(1,0) and (0,1) have the same complete present repair table H=1−R. They differ in antecedent imposition. **Accurate prediction of relief cannot, by itself, establish who created the need.** A reason certificate premised on historical responsibility therefore needs evidence beyond current patient state and control efficacy. The Boolean result is conditional on its declared law; it is not a general causal definition of blame.

Current ability to prevent an outcome also does not settle whether an omission counts as wrongful imposition. Choosing a morally required baseline needs justification. A richer account can recognize a patient-grounded reason to aid in both histories, with an additional repair responsibility in the first. A rival may instead admit only the latter. The [rescue audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-PRACTICAL-NECESSITY-AUDIT.md#13-nonparticipating-patients-rescue-and-sustainable-concern) positively defends a baseline in the bounded case of serious, effectively remediable harm without a relevant competing sacrifice. It does not infer that baseline from prior participation in forming the adverse condition. A pro tanto reason, a directed claim against someone, and an all-things-considered duty remain different conclusions.

The [source comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#11-can-imposed-burdens-give-standing-to-a-patient-who-never-agreed) also constrains what generation can justify. Creating valuable capacities or an overall beneficial outcome need not eliminate a particular center's adverse episode. The account must retain the burden, its history and the intervention's justification separately. An identified burden does not by itself prove the intervention impermissible. This prevents aggregate growth from standing in for differentiated concern, without treating every forgone improvement as a harm.

Finally, one low-cost intervention does not establish unlimited repeated duties. Repetition changes actual stocks, competing responsibilities and future capacities. A constructive response can include forming reliable prevention and shared support, with construction, maintenance and transition costs paid. Such formation must earn its justification against feasible alternatives; a promised future system does not discharge a present claim. This connects the ethical question to the generative account's concern with changing the conditions of continued action. The patient-ground's authority, identification across intended patients, and compatible implementation remain explicit completion obligations.

### Understanding suffering without endorsing every appraisal

The [new argument audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#15-does-experiential-understanding-make-concern-necessary) tests a direct route from understanding to concern. Gularte argues that fully understanding another's suffering permits only intrinsic disvaluing, conditional on an active experiential requirement. His argument also needs a criterion of objective badness and leaves action requirements open. [Gularte 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-023-04405-x).

Our challenge concerns the distinction between a represented whole and the observer's whole experience. An observer's response may change their overall affect without necessarily changing the attributed patient content. The [composition argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#14-experiential-fidelity-and-revision-of-a-harmful-remedy) makes that logical distinction exact. It does not prove a psychologically possible case of full experiential understanding without concern: phenomenal holism could constrain such a realization. Even successful experiential convergence would still need justified normative authority and a demonstrated connection to the necessary conditions of scientific philosophy.

There is also a positive developmental result. A false danger appraisal can accompany real distress. Caring about that distress need not endorse the accusation or authorize a remedy that harms someone else. In a bounded comparison, suppose paid, calibrated appraisal revision and satisfaction of a harmful request both achieve the same relief at the same cost. If relief and restraint are independently justified reasons, revision is permissible, and all other relevant grounds are held fixed, revision is preferable because it avoids the additional burden. No interpersonal utility sum is needed. The causal relief law and normative grounds remain supplied; correcting a belief may in fact leave distress intact.

This gives concern and wisdom a concrete connection: preserve the patient while revising the model or response responsible for an avoidable conflict. Suppressing a complaint, changing the observer's mood or installing an analyst's reward would not establish that connection. Nor does concern require removing every chosen discomfort or ignoring a legitimate refusal. The [source comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#12-experience-appraisal-and-practical-authority) supports keeping experiential character, represented condition, practical ground and selected response distinct. Their formation and justified coordination remain the derivation task.

### Why relief does not settle authority

The [next argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#16-concern-authorship-and-the-authority-to-intervene) examines the permissibility supplied in the remedy comparison. A person can be both the subject of an adverse episode and someone conducting a life through revisable commitments and choices. Correctly forecasting relief need not establish authority to override their informed refusal within a justified domain. The positive ground concerns their effective role in deciding, not only their superior access to private information. A rival can value that role while denying it creates a protected claim; the stronger authority premise therefore still needs justification.

The [finite information result](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#15-authorization-requires-more-than-a-beneficial-endpoint) separates two tasks. Available evidence determines the whole permitted-action set only if indistinguishable histories have identical sets. To guarantee one permissible choice throughout the claimed cases, their sets need only share an action. Waiting may meet a narrow authority constraint while failing a separately justified duty of timely assistance; a complete permission account must include that duty too.

The temporal case is sharper: supported deliberation followed by valid permission and relief can have the same final relief and approval as an intervention approved only afterward. Those endpoint data cannot identify whether permission existed earlier. Later endorsement can guide future conduct, and justified ratification can have its own effects, without manufacturing an earlier grant. Patient learning, chosen delegation and valid changes of mind remain possible; protecting agency does not mean freezing preferences.

The constructive task is to preserve usable access to reasons and a decision that can affect what happens. Producing agreement is insufficient. Nor does a right to choose by itself authorize imposing costs on others, although independently justified accommodation may require shared resources. Silence is neither consent nor refusal, and limited decision capacity does not erase experiential standing or every claim to respect. The [primary comparisons](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#13-benefit-authorship-and-permission) keep support, substitution and legitimate authority distinct.

This also sharpens the scientific test of our own foundation. An intervention may change the evidence subsequently used to assess it. A claim of justified concern must retain the relevant history and allow unfavorable findings; it cannot treat approval it produces as sufficient certification. Existing resource-dependent command tokens establish an apparatus's operating permissions, not a patient's moral authorization. The correspondence between those two requires additional grounds and evidence.

### What uncertainty permits us to guarantee

The [partial-evidence analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#16-reliable-action-under-partial-evidence) connects the existing inquiry constraints to action. Independently specify the requirement and the possible interpretations of the situation. An evidence-based decision can meet that requirement without error exactly when every reachable evidence state permits an action meeting it under all compatible interpretations. This mathematical existence result still needs an actual means of choosing and performing the action, and independently warranted coverage of the real situation.

This permits a constructive shortcut. If one interpretation admits actions A or B and another admits B or C, choosing B can work without identifying which interpretation holds. Where acceptable actions are disjoint, overlapping evidence instead imposes a quantitative lower bound on failure. With three indistinguishable interpretations admitting AB, BC and AC, every mixture fails at least one interpretation with probability at least one third; a uniform mixture attains that benchmark. These are stated model results, not measured moral frequencies or permission to randomize.

The criterion matters. Meeting a requirement under the actual facts, choosing justifiably given limited evidence, and having authority to use a risky procedure are distinct. Abstention can reduce the frequency of commitments without eliminating their conditional error, and waiting can itself violate a justified duty. More information can help distinguish cases while consuming resources or missing an action deadline. The information result supplies no ethical rule settling these tradeoffs.

The [philosophical assessment](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#17-acquired-understanding-present-reasons-and-fallible-action) also distinguishes an evaluator's learning history from the consideration it serves now. A fixed warranted patient-ground can combine with newly acquired means knowledge to support a particular response. This is the bounded functional recognition already specified by the [application account](EVALUATIVE-APPLICATION.md#when-the-application-is-functionally-recognized). It does not require rediscovering every ethical premise at each choice, and successful output alone does not show that the actor used the justifying consideration. This sharpens what a generative account of wisdom must explain while allowing fallible, revisable action.

### Scientific responsibility and the cost of error

The [inductive-risk investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#18-does-inductive-risk-make-concern-internal-to-science) tests whether concern can be necessary within scientific practice. Choices about uncertain methods, ambiguous data and usable conclusions can affect people before a later decision maker receives a result. If a justified responsibility applies to that foreseeable consequence, the researcher's scientific role alone supplies no exemption. That responsibility premise remains substantive; uncertainty does not create it by definition.

There is also a strong alternative to making scientists settle every application. Report warranted probabilities, ranges or conditional conclusions and preserve the practical decision for someone with the relevant information and authority. A hypothesis may inform many decisions with different stakes. The [exact analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#17-evidence-reporting-and-application-specific-stakes) shows why a single action threshold cannot generally serve all of them. Changing the threshold for action does not change the evidential probability of the hypothesis.

Useful reporting needs a stated scope. If one deterministic report is independent of the application's costs and supplies the recipient's complete posterior information, exact optimal binary choice under every positive error-cost ratio requires preserving all admitted posterior distinctions. A finite report alphabet cannot do that on an infinite domain; a report tailored to known stakes or supplemented by other evidence has a different scope. Approximate reports can nevertheless give bounded decision regret when their precision and the error-cost scale are bounded. These conditional results demand neither perfect communication nor one universal moral price.

For the full theory, the requirement is to connect warranted evidence with independently justified practical grounds wherever complete action guidance is claimed. Responsible delegation can satisfy part of that connection. Hidden consequential choices cannot be excused merely by naming the final output neutral. The [primary debate](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#15-inductive-risk-and-the-scope-of-scientific-responsibility) strengthens this requirement while leaving universal patient standing and the justification of competing risks open.

### What makes a consequence good for its subject?

The [naturalistic-grounding investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#19-naturalistic-patient-good-without-an-imported-moral-verdict) asks whether the same natural conditions can explain a person's benefit, mistaken desires and learning about what helps them. This gives the foundation a positive route beyond treating welfare as an unexplained reward. The proposed property must earn the role of patient good; operational persistence, present approval and an observer's preferred outcome cannot simply be renamed as that property.

An idealized assessment must still concern the actual patient. What an enhanced or fully informed counterpart now wants for itself may differ from what would help the person being assessed. The [procedure audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-REASON-TRANSPORT.md#18-patient-good-and-the-determinacy-of-idealization) also shows why acquiring the same designated facts can leave different evaluations under a stated update law. It gives a precise test for order-independent assessment while permitting different internal histories and plural equally good alternatives. A selected procedure can produce agreement without establishing that its conclusion is justified.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#16-naturalistic-good-and-reasons-for-others) takes two positive naturalist proposals seriously. Railton connects a person's good to a natural relational basis, then adds a substantive moral comparison; he separates applicable moral obligation from categorical reasons for every rational actor. Foot grounds moral reason-following within human practical rationality and social life, rather than reducing all reasons to current desires. Her argument leaves its full entailment and universal scope open.

For 慧根, scientific explanation and genuine evaluation need not be declared separate in advance. A justified naturalistic identity could connect them. The decisive work is to establish that identity and its actor-addressed authority while preserving differentiated patients. Functioning well as an organism or institution is not automatically moral goodness, and reduced physical capacity does not establish reduced moral standing. A universal generative account can permit different applications across life forms; it must justify the common ground it claims.

### Whose good can rational justification protect?

The [constitutive argument audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#20-can-rational-valuation-make-creaturely-good-authoritative-for-others) works backward from the authority claimed for our own pursuits. A bare power to choose does not establish which ends merit protection. One positive candidate connects the content to our good as creatures and its wider authority to rational justification. This permits one whole subject to be both a patient and a deliberator; it requires neither two selves nor two successive acts.

The strongest surviving rival grants public reasons but restricts their beneficiaries. It can accept that a rational being's animal good matters while proposing rational status as an additional condition of protection. Showing that rational capacity alone does not determine valuable content does not show that it cannot be such a condition. The positive account must establish why the relevant patient-good ground is sufficient, including for a subject unable to legislate or reciprocate. The rival must defend its exclusion at that same level: a capacity needed to formulate a norm is not automatically a capacity required of everyone it protects.

The [new primary exchange](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#17-from-creaturely-good-to-shared-authority) reads Korsgaard's reply alongside Schapiro's objection and Godfrey-Smith's separate draft. It supports the corrected whole-subject, form/content interpretation while leaving the force and beneficiary scope of justification contested. Existing reason-transport results already express the conditional extension; another formal theorem would not justify its premise.

Two possible achievements remain distinct. If a patient-ground is independently established as true, a philosophy contradicting it is wrong in that respect. Establishing that the ground is also indispensable to scientific inquiry is a further constitutive claim. Neither requires every specialized theory to explicitly restate all truths. This preserves the full ambition of 慧根 without confusing omission, contradiction and an unproved extension.

### Concern, claims and the capacity to speak

The [capacity and claim audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#21-capacity-concern-and-claims) separates questions hidden by the word *standing*. A patient's suffering might give an actor a reason without establishing an equal-weight rule, a directed right or authority for every proposed intervention. The [ethical foundation's N1](ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md#explicit-premises-and-limited-deductions) concerns the first claim; its stronger answerability premise N3 needs its own defense. Proving the whole account must preserve both obligations without making full rights a prerequisite for every case of concern.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#18-capacity-interests-and-the-scope-of-concern) makes this distinction consequential. Cohen's rational-kind restriction denies animal rights but retains humane obligations and treats an animal's condition as a possible ground of action. This narrows the disagreement; it does not establish a universal duty of rescue. Feinberg explains how a subject can hold a claim through representation without personally understanding or asserting it. His eligibility argument still needs a substantive ground for an actual right, and it does not give a self-appointed representative unlimited authority.

A change in capacity provides a discriminating test only if we track what remains. Continued protection after loss of current capacity may rest on history, recovery, a relationship or kind membership. Protecting another patient who never had that capacity removes some of those explanations but not automatically all of them. Accepted cases can have different sufficient grounds. Nor may a defended biological or social relation be dismissed as a mere change of name. The account must explain its relevance to the particular reason being assessed.

The positive route remains to identify what is bad for this patient and justify why that fact counts for this actor. Where a proposed exclusion concerns only who can exercise a claim, warranted representation can answer it while preserving the actual beneficiary. Where it concerns whether any reason exists, representation alone cannot supply the missing ground. This directs the next research toward baseline concern for a noncauser and, separately, the patient's claim to an answer from an arrangement exercising power. The whole ethical foundation remains in scope.

### From owed protection to a corrective response

The [directed-duty investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#22-patient-interests-directed-duties-and-corrective-response) keeps four roles distinct: actor, protected patient, claimholder and representative. Delivering a benefit does not alone identify whose claim the delivery answers. A service can fulfill an undertaking to a sponsor, a duty owed to the patient, or both. Observed compliance cannot settle which relation is valid.

The [Raz–Thompson comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#19-patient-interests-directed-duties-and-answerability) makes a positive route and its burden explicit: justify the patient's interest as a ground of a duty owed to that patient, then identify the duties it supports in the circumstances. A requirement to benefit a class does not automatically give every member a claim to each benefit. That patient may nevertheless have a separately grounded claim to fair consideration.

Our constructive case begins with a particular protection independently owed to a patient. An erroneous record blocks it, and a legitimate report supplies verifiable correction. If an adequate corrective route is feasible, permissible and necessary to discharge the still-binding duty, the actor cannot fulfill it while refusing every such route. A defended necessary-means principle carries the protection into the required correction. The patient remains the claimholder when a representative reports the error or an independent reviewer repairs it.

This supports a limited part of answerability without making every technical step a separate right. A response may be required to enable the claim's exercise. Verification also needs a ground: it must help exercise or protect the claim, or be independently owed. An adequate automatic correction may require no personal explanation. Broader procedural claims need further grounds, and several adequate routes can leave institutional choice open. Incapacity to speak does not cancel an established claim, while appointment as a proxy does not confer unlimited authority.

The next question is why consequential power owes a patient fair consideration and a response even where no particular favorable outcome is owed. That is a substantive part of N3. The separate N1 task still asks why a patient's good supplies an unrelated capable actor a reason. Neither is settled by the success of this conditional correction argument.

### A hearing without a promised outcome

The [procedural-standing investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#23-procedural-standing-without-a-right-to-win) moves beyond the parcel already owed. An institution may be permitted to restrict a person's options when the relevant grounds hold, while still owing them adequate assessment of those grounds. The proposed reason is responsibility for the restriction it imposes. A refusal of a discretionary gift does not automatically count as such an imposition; the baseline and the patient's interest must be independently justified.

Suppose the patient or a warranted representative has otherwise unavailable, verifiable evidence relevant to a possible error. A proportionate, permissible assessment route is feasible, with no adequate substitute or defeating reason. Closing every route shifts an avoidable burden of error onto the person restricted. Our affirmative argument is that the institution owes that person the reasonable work of assessing its own imposition. This is a defended normative premise composed with the earlier imposition and necessary-means arguments. It does not follow from information value alone.

The resulting claim is to adequate assessment, not guaranteed success. Correcting one fact may leave another justification intact. A personal response is required insofar as the ground and effective procedure require it; representation or independent review may provide an adequate route. Genuine consideration can leave the outcome unchanged, while merely receiving submissions without considering them fails to perform that function.

The [Waldron–Raz comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#20-procedural-standing-and-justified-authority) identifies the further question. Participation can express respect for agency, while competent delegation can also serve it. If a hearing remains owed when it adds no information, its ground must reach beyond accuracy. Matching material outcomes would then leave that participatory good unmatched. The account must justify its scope, costs and treatment of people unable to speak, rather than make personal reconsideration mandatory for every decision.

### Participation as an exercise of agency

The [new joint audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#24-participation-beyond-information-and-the-joint-foundation) distinguishes learning a fact, performing an act and being owed an opportunity. Where a person has a justified role in choosing or authorizing a representative, correctly predicting their choice does not perform that act for them. Actual participation can matter even when it adds no information about the decision’s merits or changes no allocation. Its occurrence can itself create a normative fact; correct anticipation of its content does not supply that fact. Conversely, valid bounded delegation can preserve the role while the person declines a personal hearing. Its authority and limits must be justified; eventual satisfaction cannot create them retroactively.

An unused opportunity also has a structure. Two institutions may issue the same invitation and reach the same decision when nobody responds, yet only one would consider an admissible contribution. Matching that observed history does not establish equivalent opportunities. This applies the existing distinction-preservation requirement to the relevant possible interventions. It does not derive a universal right to the opportunity from the fact that these processes differ.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#21-participation-shared-duties-and-delegation) examines shared duties with an expressive component as a possible ground. That route starts with collective duties and coauthorship already supplied. An affected patient need not be a coauthor or duty-bearer; those classes require separate justification. A duty to participate likewise does not automatically establish a claim against every official.

This identifies a requirement for the entire theory: preserve one patient, one meaning of their good, one ground of the actor's reason and one authority scope through the N1–N4 argument and its implementation. If q is soundly derived from H together with an additional agency premise A, H supports A ⇒ q, with any other local premises retained. Whole-theory necessity requires deriving A, independently justifying it as part of a revised necessary basis, or finding another route to q. Inserting A into the basis and applying the closure theorem does not discharge this obligation. A rival that recognizes the same agency facts but disputes their normative force must be answered at that ground, rather than excluded by definition.

### When agency itself could supply authority

The [constitutive-agency investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#25-inescapable-agency-authority-and-moral-coverage) now tests a route beyond voluntary undertakings. Deliberating about whether to exercise agency appears already to involve practical assessment. If that assessment has genuinely authoritative constitutive standards, they need not await a contract or present endorsement. This is a substantive possibility for the root, not a deduction of moral authority from physical inability to stop. The [Enoch–Ferrero primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#22-inescapable-agency-and-normative-authority) keeps this possibility distinct from the missing substantive derivation.

The account must establish three things together: why a proposed standard governs the activity, why it gives a genuine reason for correction, and what it requires concerning which patients. A norm applying to every deliberator does not thereby protect every affected being. Using a practice to expose an internal problem also need not endorse every proposed reconstruction of it. The inquiry must answer the problem, rather than dismiss the critic for having reasoned.

The current comparison tests coherence and evidence sensitivity, consistent application of sufficient grounds, and answerability to affected claims. The first does not fix the patient-related reason; the second needs an account of whether the actor–patient relationship is relevant; the third already contains the answerability premise to be justified. These are scoped assessments of existing arguments, not a proof that no richer constitutive account can succeed.

A stronger rival accepts binding moral reasons but treats deliberation as a way of recognizing independently grounded truths. This distinguishes formation of recognition, justification of belief and constitution of validity. Protective behavior alone cannot adjudicate those grounding claims. Nor must a valid standard demand perpetual continuation of the agency that recognizes it: justified rest, delegation or cessation of an inquiry can remain possible. The next content-sensitive test is whether the actual patient's aversive condition provides a sufficient ground for the unrelated actor's reason, while preserving its applicability and competing grounds.

### Shared reasons and their explanatory grounds

The [patient-reason investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#26-patient-reasons-motivational-grounds-and-overdetermination) returns to the unrelated actor. Its target is the patient's relief as a consideration favoring one action over another. Merely finding a private reason for both actions does not explain that difference. Identification of suffering, benefit to the patient, a reason for the actor and the reason's comparative strength remain separate questions.

The [Parfit–Schroeder comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#23-patient-reasons-and-their-explanatory-grounds) identifies a stronger rival. A desire-based account can cite the patient's condition as the reason while placing desires in the background explanation of why it counts for this actor. Different desires could support the same reason in different cases. A simple conditional coverage argument shows why universal support requires a supporting ground in each admitted background, without requiring one particular ground shared by all. Removing sympathy or matching private payoff therefore does not establish independence from every desire.

The positive object-grounded route explains why a previously absent concern can be worth acquiring. That would connect generative formation to justified evaluation. It still owes an argument for why this patient's condition addresses the unrelated actor. A desire account may explain acquisition through broader existing concerns and must defend those grounds and their claimed coverage. The comparison concerns coverage, explanation and strength separately; a shared protective verdict can coexist with disagreement about its foundation.

This sharpens the entire-theory claim. Necessary conclusions do not automatically identify their uniquely necessary explanation. A compatible alternative may realize a common root, while a claim that one particular ground is indispensable needs its own justification. The next discriminating work must specify the complete candidate explanation and test warranted acquisition, rather than treating another pattern of helping as proof of normative authority. Universal N1 and broader N3 remain open.

### Learning concern without making success its own standard

The [acquisition investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#27-warranted-acquisition-and-the-direction-of-justification) distinguishes practical reasons to cultivate an attitude from the reasons that make it appropriate toward its object. Incentives can give genuine reasons to learn or maintain a response; their existence does not establish why the patient's burden itself warrants concern. Mixed motivation can also support genuine recognition. The distinction concerns the ground being claimed, rather than a blanket judgment about all rewarded learning.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#24-learning-concern-and-the-reasons-for-acquiring-it) offers a positive route through partial understanding. A learner can reasonably investigate and cultivate sensitivity before possessing complete appreciation. Whether this requires a distinctive account of reasons is disputed. In either case, calling the result successful value learning presupposes a warranted target; stable adoption or future endorsement cannot establish that target by themselves. Learning one genuine value also need not justify every sacrifice made for it.

The constructive direction is precise: an independently justified patient ground, applicable evidence and a permissible helpful learning route can support cultivation. Support for cultivation alone does not prove the patient ground. This leaves room for learning new evaluative content, improving its understanding and training attention to an already justified judgment, without conflating those achievements.

The actual [successor application](EVALUATIVE-APPLICATION.md#the-successors-first-recorded-continuation) makes the distinction concrete. Its retained positive receipt can establish both command efficacy and fulfillment of a fixed one-service claim. Greater instrumental confidence can therefore coexist with withdrawal of that particular pending-service reason. The current policy does not perform that withdrawal, and other reasons could still support another delivery. This reuses an established application boundary; it adds no new test to the accepted causal result. The continuing foundational task is justified expansion of patient-directed concern, with the same patient, ground and authority preserved through actual learning and use.

### The normative bridge and its admissible interpretations

The [new argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#28-suffering-and-the-scope-of-the-missing-normative-bridge) makes the descriptive boundary exact. If the declared causal premises place no constraint on a fresh practical-reason relation, the same causal model permits both inclusive and self-only interpretations of that relation. This establishes relative non-entailment. It does not establish two morally admissible worlds or rule out a justified identity between a natural property and a reason-bearing ground. The entire foundation already includes ethical proposals, so the result must not be advertised as independence from every commitment of the theory.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#25-suffering-shared-reasons-and-normative-interpretation) distinguishes a subject's reason to avoid suffering from another actor's reason to relieve it. A reason-implying meaning of good-for can already contain altruistic reasons; establishing that an observed condition has this significance still needs an argument. A rival can also accept genuine moral and epistemic reasons while disputing their attitude-independent explanation. The dispute cannot be settled by defining the rival as incapable of scientific justification.

A positive bridge remains possible without determining a unique complete ethics. If an independently justified principle connects F to the relevant actor's reason, and the experiential or generative account establishes F in the target cases, sound composition gives the baseline conclusion. Other reasons, weights and directed duties may remain unsettled. The admissible models and interpretations must be independently defended and nonempty; inserting the desired standing principle into their definition only relocates its justification.

This preserves the whole 慧根 objective: find the actual sufficient ground and establish why adequate inquiry must respect it, then let validity-preserving generation carry its consequences. The present result rules out a missing-link inference while leaving substantive grounding routes open. It adds no new requirement to the completed finite implementation.

### Experiential grounds and the correction of concern

The [positive argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#29-experiential-grounds-and-warranted-concern) locates the reason for relief in the patient's actual burden. A capable actor can have a reason to improve that condition without receiving a private benefit or first causing it. This is a defended normative proposal: the burden must be identified as bad for the patient, and its practical relevance to the actor must be justified. The conditional foundation can use those explicit premises while explaining their learned application; proving them indispensable to every adequate inquiry remains the stronger task.

The [new primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#26-direct-acquaintance-relational-suffering-and-reasons-for-others) examines direct acquaintance, relational accounts of painfulness, and opposing arguments about interpersonal reasons. Direct contact with a phenomenal quality does not by itself settle its normative classification or another actor's reason. Conversely, locating a burden in one subject does not logically confine every reason concerning it to that subject. Shared concern can preserve the patients whose distinct conditions ground it.

This makes warranted learning more precise. Correcting a false belief about bodily damage can leave an actual aversive episode intact. Discovering that an action cannot relieve it removes that means-based justification. Ending the episode removes its current relief application even if the learned command remains effective. A change only in the helper's affect need not remove the patient-ground. These are different corrections, and other reasons for the same action may survive.

The current [plasticity construction](EVALUATIVE-PLASTICITY.md) explains formation and revision of an operative evaluative connection at its declared causal scope. To claim warranted concern, acquired influence must answer to the independently defended patient-ground, actor scope, means and authority. Positive conductance and effective service do not supply those meanings. This adds a substantive interpretation of correction, without reopening the accepted finite applications or identifying their resource signals with suffering.

### Patient-ground across competing accounts of pain

The [new scope audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#30-experiential-occurrence-patient-attitudes-and-whole-life-value) tests what the relief argument must preserve. A defensible positive case concerns a patient undergoing a still-unwanted aversive episode, whose cessation answers that present burden. This supports occurrence-sensitive relevance without adding up a lifetime's pains. It remains a substantive normative judgment, joined to an independently defended principle addressing the capable actor.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#27-pain-attitudes-and-whole-life-value) exposes three distinct challenges: painfulness without aversion, intrinsic enjoyment that might defeat badness, and a holistic account on which repetition adds no new welfare loss. None is established merely by reporting indifference or voluntary endurance. Equally, an argument about ordinary relief does not settle all three. The point of comparison is to defend or revise the sufficient ground, without defining a scientific philosophy as agreement with our preferred answer.

The value bearer matters to sound composition. An action that ends one episode need not improve a whole-life target whose relevant features remain unchanged. Conversely, absence of marginal lifetime improvement does not automatically defeat an independently justified local reason or responsibility. Moving between those arguments requires explicit justification. A shared interface must retain the patient, the evaluated target, the actual alternative, the action's effect and its practical address; agreement on an action alone is weaker.

The conditional foundation can proceed with defended premises while testing their necessity. A change only in a learner's association does not defeat a fixed, independently warranted ground. Actual change in the patient's relevant condition, fulfillment, means or authority can change its application. The accepted causal plasticity construction explains acquired influence; this philosophical comparison sharpens its warranted use without introducing another controller or treating recipient stocks as suffering measurements. The next bridge is occurrence-sensitive significance → actor-addressed reasons that remain answerable to their grounds as learned influence changes.

### Practical address and the growth of reasons

The [new argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#31-objective-inquiry-practical-access-and-generative-growth) distinguishes accurate attribution from practical address. Knowing that an institution owes a patient repair preserves that institution's responsibility; it does not silently transfer the same responsibility to every observer. A justified entrusted role can connect the obligation to an acting learner. The independently reviewed [grounding application](EVALUATIVE-GROUNDING.md) now establishes that an actually formed disposition mediates a warranted role-bound response under its declared interpretation. This positive result retains the broader question of concern for patients whose burdens the actor did not cause.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#28-objective-understanding-practical-access-and-new-reasons) examines objective reasons and acquired practical understanding. Such understanding can have significance beyond an accurate neutral description; its possession and its necessity for scientific inquiry require argument. A patient need not have the capacities required of a deliberating actor to possess a significant good. Nor does an actor's current refusal establish inability to understand the reason.

This permits a more precise form of generative growth. Learning can reveal a reason already applicable, form a capacity or role that makes a reason newly applicable, or lead to revision of a proposed principle. The first two can occur under an unchanged justified conditional basis. The third needs a further defense of the revised connection. Historical novelty therefore neither refutes a necessary finite root by itself nor proves that future judgments are already sound consequences of our present theory.

In the accepted repair case, changed association can leave the actual ground and responsibility intact, producing failed selection. A genuine change in a relevant capacity, means or role could instead change applicability. The theory must explain which occurred without identifying numerical influence with practical authority. The next necessity argument concerns which actor relation the patient-ground actually warrants, and why adequate inquiry must preserve that relation as part of its full practical content.

### Intrinsic badness and the scope of concern

The [latest argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#32-phenomenal-identity-practical-scope-and-the-necessary-root) examines a stronger way to ground concern. A warranted identity between an actual experiential quality and a precisely specified disvalue could carry substantive normative content. The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#29-phenomenal-identity-and-the-scope-of-intrinsic-value) tests Rawlette's identity proposal alongside selected Wedgwood passages on value and action. Our reconstruction distinguishes what makes an episode bad, whom that gives a reason, and how reasons combine. The same patient's relief remains the relevant effect when the helper changes; why that ground addresses an unrelated capable actor still needs its own defense. ### Correcting inherited judgments while preserving patient significance

The [new audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#33-evolutionary-genealogy-and-the-justification-of-concern) tests whether explaining the origins of self-preferring judgments can strengthen concern for unrelated patients. A cause of belief, a reason to distrust its support and a positive justification for its alternative are different contributions.

Our reconstruction distinguishes a reason concerning one's own good, a principle excluding everyone else's good, and a priority in a particular conflict. The baseline concern argument challenges the exclusion and supplies positive grounds for the actor's reason concerning another patient. It need not eliminate the actor's own good or every special responsibility. A theory seeking shared concern must preserve those differentiated claims and justify their comparison.

An independently warranted route to a conclusion can survive the defeat of another route, even when both share biological ancestry. Conversely, simply restating a defeated intuition as an abstract principle does not restore its authority. Correction must identify which premises and inferences actually lost support. The same standard applies to our patient-ground and to proposed restrictions on its practical reach.

This adds a concrete task for the generative root: explain how acquired judgments can be corrected while preserving independently justified significance. A reasoning capacity can develop beyond its original adaptive uses, but that possibility does not validate every output. The next connection is reliable access to experiential value → warranted practical relevance beyond the subject. ### Reliable experiential access and the reach of its conclusions

The [new analysis](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#34-experiential-reliability-and-the-necessity-of-generated-claims) examines how reliable access could warrant patient significance. The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#31-experiential-access-and-transferable-evaluative-reliability) supplies an affirmative inductive route and a challenge about the experiential property being judged. A defended transfer of reliability could support evaluation without first assuming a phenomenal/value identity. An independently warranted identity remains another route. Both must preserve what the judgment actually concerns.

Our hand illustration identifies a precise limitation: 99 correct ordinary judgments plus one false evaluative judgment yield 99% pooled accuracy and no correct evaluative judgment. Source success alone does not determine target success. Additional evidence or argument must justify the transfer; this requirement does not dismiss induction or demand direct verification of every new conclusion. The illustration can hold normative truths fixed and vary process errors, so it does not presume that moral properties can be reassigned arbitrarily.

The experiential interpretation also matters. Changing a patient's aversion can change the episode itself; changing a judgment about that episode tests a different dependence. A whole episode involving aversion might have significance independently of endorsing a judgment about it. That is a substantive candidate to defend, not a result obtained from the word intrinsic. Reliable recognition of a bare sensation cannot silently certify an account of the whole episode and its value.

This advances the patient-ground while preserving the next practical question. A general warranted account can cover a newly identified patient's relevant experience. Its significance as a reason for an unrelated actor must also be justified, together with permissible means and competing claims. The next comparison tests whether that actor-addressed judgment has a defensible epistemic route or requires an additional practical ground. ### Reasons that can generate new concern

The [new argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#35-motivational-access-and-the-ground-of-practical-reasons) tests whether a patient's significance can generate concern before the actor wants to help. A general wish to respond to actual reasons can help a learner act on a justified patient assessment. But determining that relief fulfills this wish already requires identifying the relevant reason. Neither result excludes a contentful desire to benefit others as a genuine alternative route; it separates transmission from the ground of the patient's practical significance.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#32-motivational-access-and-the-ground-of-practical-reasons) also opens a constructive possibility. This narrows the next derivation to the first warranted recognition step: why the independently identified patient benefit favors this eligible actor's response. ### Can goodness be generativity?

Ren's [new question](../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-06-goodness-as-generativity.md) proposes a possible source for the good reasons investigated above. **Goodness could be constituted by a kind of generative relation.** Establishing that identity would explain evaluative content within actual processes. Choosing a definition alone cannot establish the practical significance assigned to it.

The [current formal meaning](FORMAL-CORE.md#definitions-and-conventions) of generativity includes producing or reorganizing conditions for later activity. A coercive process can do that successfully. Ending it can protect affected beings. A broader view can correct a narrow account of subsystem growth, but even a growing collective can impose a serious loss on one member. The theory must preserve whose condition changes, what changes and how the consequences are distributed.

A future-capacity measure also needs a present-life test. Suppose a permissible intervention relieves an actual unwanted episode without changing the patient's later survival, capacities or opportunities. If the independently defended patient-ground applies, that relief matters even though the selected capacity description is unchanged. This is a conceptual comparison, not a newly observed dissociation. It shows what a capacity-only account would omit; a richer generative explanation may include the experiential relation itself.

A promising working hypothesis is therefore **that some generatively realized conditions constitute goods and harms for particular beings, and that further generative relations realize, restore or support those goods**. This formulation is AI elaboration, not a definition supplied by Ren. It makes room for renewal and development, as well as rest, relief, restraint and completion. It remains incomplete until the good-making relations are specified and justified independently of merely calling them good.

The [concurrent constitutive candidate](UNIFIED-ETHICAL-GROUNDING.md#the-constitutive-candidate-and-its-test) already supplies a concrete target: identify a qualifying experiential burden and explain why an eligible act's relief of it constitutes prospective moral favor before elective concern. Those are defended hypotheses, not necessary identities already derived from minimal generation. The [new argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#36-can-goodness-be-identified-with-generativity) gives a description-sufficiency test and connects the proposal to [three primary comparisons](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#33-goodness-generativity-and-the-patient-relation).

The stronger ambition is preserved: establish the good-making relation, its relevance to other actors and its place in a necessary joint foundation. A counterexample to raw productivity does not rule out that programme. Explaining a good's generative realization does not by itself prove that every part of the present theory is indispensable to scientific philosophy.

### Generative standards and the possibility of reorganization

An organized process can supply a standard against which one of its own activities succeeds or fails. The candidate relation is concrete: an activity maintains conditions supporting the organization, and the organization produces or maintains that contributor. Establishing the actual reciprocal dependence, differentiated contribution and functional context can give significance an immanent basis. A positive reward signal alone cannot establish that the contribution occurred. The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#34-organizational-standards-and-the-good-of-a-being) examines organizational and life-form approaches without treating them as identical theories.

The [new argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#37-organizational-standards-living-forms-and-ethical-generativity) distinguishes a current regime's requirements from every possible continuation. If a condition is necessary for one mode of organization, it need not be necessary for continuing through an alternative mode. Functionality alone does not establish even the first necessity. A feasible handoff can preserve relevant support while replacing its former route; its intermediate states, resources, permissions and the continuity of the actual being must be warranted. This permits generative repair through reorganization and accommodation, without claiming that every redesign benefits its subject.

The same distinction protects external assistance. An input can improve a recipient's condition without the recipient producing or sustaining its helper. Membership in a reciprocally maintained network cannot therefore be required for every benefit. Yet a functional improvement is not automatically an experiential good, an actor's moral reason or a decisive duty. Conversely, a person's contribution to a collective does not establish that the collective's success serves that person's good. Both organizations can be real while their interests conflict.

This supplies a concrete route from generation to standards, with a further constitutive task: identify when the generated relation is good for the actual being, and why that good favors an eligible actor's response. Present experience, agency, rest and relief remain within the test even where reproduction or restored typical functioning is unavailable. Neither common factual truth nor a common generative vocabulary alone establishes identical practical standards across every form of life. The proposed universal ground must justify its scope; no new formal or empirical result is claimed here.

### Adaptive significance and the good of the bearer

An organization’s actual need does not imply that it can recognize and regulate a threat to that need. The [adaptive comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#35-adaptive-significance-and-the-good-of-the-bearer) identifies a candidate connection: processes within a being discriminate tendencies affecting its continuation and alter them. Such a response can have an immanent standard without an analyst's reward. Its formation, effective coupling and limits still need explanation. Internal regulation also need not establish agency over external exchanges.

The [new hand argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#38-adaptive-significance-and-the-good-of-the-bearer) makes the prospective comparison explicit. Under a declared law, installing a renewal route now can lower a reserve while preserving continued support; waiting can leave more reserve but make installation unaffordable. The actual state, intermediate payment order, source and continuing route matter. This is a conditional illustration, not a new empirical model or an assumption-free derivation of the supplied law.

A broader generative account can also include experience and agency as aspects of what is sustained. That preserves the ambition to explain good within generative reality rather than reduce it to metabolic survival. But the same account must explain conflicts among internally sustained patterns. Reinforcing a habit can damage its bearer; dismantling it can be part of repair when the same patient and a genuinely beneficial transition are independently established. Neither the largest system boundary nor the pattern's own persistence selects the justified standard.

Three interventions therefore require separate assessment: changing actual support, suppressing a warning or report, and genuinely relieving an unwanted experience while retaining necessary regulation. The last possibility is conditional on a real available transition. Reduced reporting does not demonstrate relief, and regulatory usefulness need not erase a qualifying experience's local badness. A metabolism-only comparison cannot refute a richer account that includes the lived difference; that richer account must still establish its constitutive and practical claims.

The concurrent [noninstrumental-concern result](NONINSTRUMENTAL-CONCERN.md) now accepts a conditional resource-dependent response interface while preserving C1/C2 as supplied ethical grounds. The [assumption-elimination audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/ASSUMPTION-ELIMINATION-AUDIT.md) records why explicit premises and successful implementation fall short of the stronger requirement. The next connection is the lived burden and its actual bearer → warranted correction of the sustaining process → practical significance for an eligible independent actor. The full root remains the target; neither a functioning controller nor explanatory vocabulary alone establishes its necessity.

### Lived experience and warranted correction

The [new primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#36-lived-experience-and-the-evidence-for-relief) combines an existing participant-level pain study with a philosophical examination of sense-making. It tests what an experiential application of the generative account must preserve. Operational regulation, a meaningful perspective, phenomenal experience, local badness and practical authority are not interchangeable descriptions. A rich constitutive account could connect them, but merely broadening the word “subjectivity” does not establish suffering in every organism.

Actual support, observed reports and lived experience are different target effects that can change together. The [evidence argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#39-lived-experience-fallible-evidence-and-the-ground-of-correction) shows why randomized effects on reports and a limited marker need not uniquely identify experiential mediation. Its declared counterinterpretations differ in unobserved processes, not in experience alone while the entire world stays fixed. They are not fitted explanations of the cited study or evidence that reported relief is unreal.

There is also a positive route. If independently warranted bounds constrain each indicator's error for a specified experiential target under each intervention, those indicators bound the target's prevalence. Disjoint appropriately ordered bounds can establish a reduction without assuming independent indicator errors. Calibration, joint statistical uncertainty and the distinction between population and individual outcomes remain essential. This is a sufficient conditional argument, not an infallible classifier, an analysis of the cited raw data or an assumption-free foundation.

An account of scientific inquiry must also distinguish access from the theory explaining access. Even if experience is indispensable to an inquiry, one particular enactive or phenomenological interpretation does not thereby become indispensable. Empirical mismatches can revise a marker, its interpretation or a proposed relation between regulation and experience. Sound extension cannot turn a fallible empirical identification into necessary truth merely by expressing it within a generative vocabulary.

The next connection is warranted identification of the actual lived burden → why its character constitutes local badness → nonexclusive practical relevance under eligible control. Identifying relief need not await a complete neural explanation; it also does not alone establish overall welfare, permission or another actor's reason. The [extended evidence dossier](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-HARM-IDENTIFICATION.md#7-relief-identification-and-the-limits-of-a-neural-proxy) preserves the empirical limits, and the accepted finite concern construction retains its supplied faithful-read and C1/C2 premises.

### Affective disclosure and the badness of undergoing

The latest [argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#40-affective-disclosure-and-the-badness-of-undergoing) asks how a generated being's actual undergoing can constitute local badness. It retains the current candidate's restricted experiential domain, including the distinction between intrinsic enjoyment and merely accepting an episode for another purpose. The target is an independently identified experiential whole; neither withdrawal behavior nor an observer's assigned penalty establishes it.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#37-affective-disclosure-and-the-badness-of-undergoing) gives that target more structure. A constitutive affective mode can differ from a further judgment or desire concerning the experience. Consequently, holding sensory content fixed need not preserve the whole, while changing reflective endorsement need not change its constitutive aversion. Affect may also provide access to significance before explicit theorizing; its accuracy and practical scope still require assessment.

Two directions of correction now test the candidate. Correcting a false injury assessment can remove that assessment's support while an actual aversive episode continues. Ending that episode can end its current relief application while a bodily problem still warrants attention. Where these contrasts are independently supported, they favor locating the proposed burden in the actual undergoing. They are conceptual comparisons, not new clinical findings or a claim that every imagined separation can be physically realized. Other effects, future recurrence and permissible means remain material.

This is affirmative support for a constitutive account, with a precise open commitment: the specified experiential whole must be shown to have prudential significance, rather than merely represent another condition as bad. No prior welfare theory or helping aim is required for the candidate ground to exist. But the contrastive value judgments are defeasible evidence, not a deduction from generation alone. Rival accounts concerning attitudes and whole-life value remain live tests of the sufficient domain.

For Ren's proposal, generativity can thus be investigated as the realization of value within a being's life and the support of such realization. It must include present experience, meaningful completion and differentiated centers, rather than measure every good by later productivity. Establishing a patient's good and explaining why an independent actor has a reason to support it remain distinct tasks. A complete account must justify both, while allowing correction of its own proposed identities.

### Local benefit, history and the value of a life

The [latest argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#41-local-benefit-history-and-the-value-of-a-life) distinguishes three ways history can matter: it can change the actual experiential whole, alter how an unchanged local condition contributes to a life, or challenge whether the occurrence has independent significance at all. These objections require different replies. The earlier distinction between local badness and strict lifetime improvement remains in place.

The [Velleman comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#38-local-benefit-history-and-the-value-of-a-life) supplies a positive possibility: historically constituted value and independently real local value can coexist. A wider perspective need not make a particular subject's present good merely apparent. For the generative investigation, unified explanation can therefore seek to preserve different value-bearing relations without presupposing a single additive measure.

There is also a sharper application test. Two continuations sharing the same past can stand in different relations to earlier commitments, efforts and experiences. That past does not automatically cancel from a welfare comparison. A claimed relief benefit must retain its bearer and experiential target while assessing the intervention's effects on those relations. Exact contextual neutrality can supply a controlled comparison, but it must be argued independently; it cannot mean whatever makes the preferred outcome better. Genuine local benefit can also remain relevant where another consequence opposes it.

The repeated-pain challenge is correspondingly precise. We must establish whether the same complete aversive condition persists and whether relief changes some additional feature recognized by the rival account. Mere familiarity does not settle either issue. Treating each qualifying occurrence as significant does not require equal increments, a uniform minimum weight or a claim that all good and bad can be added across lives.

This advances goodness-as-generativity by retaining both what is lived and how events become related through a life. Learning or recovery can change a burden's historical role without proving that its original occurrence was harmless or justified. The remaining task is to justify the local and historical grounds themselves, then their practical relevance to another actor. Neither philosophical agreement on ordinary relief nor a richer causal description alone establishes their necessary truth.

### Benefit without a reflective life conception

The [capacity investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#42-benefit-without-a-reflective-life-conception) distinguishes an actual lived perspective, nonconceptual caring, a reflective conception of a life and present endorsement. Inability to articulate a judgment does not establish inability to benefit. But it also does not defeat every capacity condition: a being can care affectively without possessing a theory of its welfare.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#39-benefit-without-a-reflective-life-conception) brings Bradley's argument for successive benefits without narrative comprehension together with Lin's challenge to sophisticated judgment requirements. Velleman's weaker caring-capacity condition remains a serious comparator. These philosophical arguments support a more precise test; they are not new evidence about infant or animal consciousness.

When a reflective capacity develops, the theory must distinguish better evidence for an existing good, recognition of that good, creation of an additional good, and alteration of the original experiential whole. If the bearer, ground and relevant context persist, mere acquisition of an unexercised capacity needs an explanation before it can erase the earlier benefit. Actual development may change those conditions, so this is a conditional preservation test.

For goodness-as-generativity, the positive implication is that value and understanding can develop within one explanation without making every good depend on explicit self-evaluation. A theory can represent successive local benefits without requiring its subject to conceive the entire sequence. Historical comparison and prospective prevention retain their separately justified bridges; another actor's reason remains a further claim. The next task is to justify interpretation that preserves the actual patient's ground, then its practical address beyond that patient.

### Correction with changing grounds of benefit

The [new correction argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#43-correction-with-changing-grounds-of-benefit) develops the preceding capacity distinction into a concrete test. Preserving a patient across learning does not mean freezing every current preference. We must assess which attitudes and experiential conditions obtain in the contemplated continuation, while keeping the advisor's transformed state distinct from that target.

The [Rosati and Lin comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#40-correction-with-changing-grounds-of-benefit) supplies two challenges. An advisor's continuity with the patient does not establish its authority. And getting what one presently wants can be worse overall even on a desire-based account. Neither more information nor that disappointing outcome alone chooses the correct theory of good.

A positive correction can instead concern the actual fulfillment relation. Wanting an outcome only if a condition holds differs from wanting both the condition and the outcome, or unconditionally wanting the outcome because of a mistaken belief. Evidence that the condition fails has different implications in each case. The theory must establish which attitude is present rather than manufacture a condition that produces its preferred verdict.

This lets a generative account distinguish satisfying an existing interest, revising it, creating a new interest and relieving a burden. Removing a desire does not establish that its former object was obtained; such removal can nevertheless have other benefits or costs. Local factual correction is possible without settling every possible life. Which relations constitute benefit, and why they justify another actor's response, remain the central substantive tests.

### Conscious stakes and actual relations

The [relational-benefit investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#44-conscious-stakes-and-actual-relations) tests whether a conscious stake and its worldly realization can jointly constitute a good. Requiring an experiencing beneficiary does not by itself establish that identical experiences imply identical welfare. This permits a candidate in which a felt concern anchors the patient while actual reciprocity or achievement supplies another constituent.

The [Lin and Heathwood comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#41-conscious-stakes-and-actual-relations) also corrects a misleading division between philosophical labels. An attitudinal and an experiential description could identify the same ground under a justified identity; truth-sensitive versions of either can admit an external difference. Their detailed conditions and measures must be compared.

The concrete test contrasts genuinely reciprocal friendship with its convincing appearance while stipulating the same relevant nonfactive perspective for one participant. Actual reciprocity differs, but a welfare difference remains to defend. The comparison neither proves that physical duplicates are realizable nor holds the other participant's welfare fixed. Condemning deception does not alone establish the affected person's prudential loss.

If the relational benefit is justified, a representation merging those histories cannot answer the corresponding benefit question exactly. That is an application of the inherited representation result, not a proof of the welfare premise. A generative account must explain the formation of the stake, the actual relation and reliable access to it. Local experiential benefit remains a possible sufficient ground; its scope is not silently expanded into exclusivity or universal ethical authority.

### Relational benefit and the evidence for it

The [new investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#45-relational-benefit-and-the-evidence-for-it) applies the [shared nondual method](NONDUAL-METHOD.md): participants, their concerns and the significance of their responses differentiate within a generative history. The proposed good is a mode of living that relation, not a detached score imposed on it. Existing results concerning agency and actual participation are inherited; their welfare significance receives a separate substantive argument.

A positive candidate is effective reciprocal engagement: a person's particular contribution enters an activity in which another responds through independent understanding, including correction and disagreement. What the person gains is a way of conducting part of life together. An innocent-misunderstanding comparison removes betrayal as the automatic source of the verdict. Matching pleasant appearance or external products need not preserve the proposed activity; its prudential value nevertheless remains a constitutive claim to defend.

The [primary investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#42-relational-benefit-and-the-evidence-for-it) also prevents treating reciprocal participation as necessary for every social benefit. Mediated relations can be genuine, and being recognized may be a weaker candidate than mutual recognition. Neither a simulation label nor an inability to reciprocate establishes the absence of a patient. Choice evidence does not uniquely identify a welfare ground, and an additional good need not dominate every choice.

The remaining questions concern the actual relation omitted by a representation, the formation of its participants and the warrant for its proposed welfare role. These are different limitations within one inquiry. A substantive experientialist rival must address the specified activity; a freely reassigned predicate in a weaker model does not refute it. Conversely, a joint constitutive proposal cannot insulate its evidence from scientific correction.

### Recognition, receiving and the form of the encounter

The [new comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#46-recognition-receiving-and-the-form-of-the-encounter) tests the claim that being accurately recognized as conscious has a positive welfare contribution even in hostile encounters. That thin condition must remain distinct from care or respect. Its defender already offers hostile comparisons; overall harm cannot refute the proposed local good. Conversely, decreasing or bounded benefits cannot establish that there is a positive benefit to begin with.

The positive alternative now includes the recipient's side of generative activity. A person can receive a relational good without personally producing it. The proposed ground concerns their effective place within the encounter: what care answers, what contribution enters shared activity, and what protected scope for judgment or development the relationship realizes. Accurate, adaptive targeting can lack that form of response. The [primary comparators](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#43-recognition-receiving-and-the-form-of-the-encounter) support this route while retaining their substantive views of flourishing.

A proposed test keeps the same conscious observer and patient while varying whether the observer accurately takes the patient to be conscious. The recognition history must fall within the tested account's independently specified conditions for a positive increment, rather than an already saturated benefit. It attempts to preserve the patient's experience, conduct toward them and later consequences. The actual cognitive relation changes; the welfare conclusion is not stipulated. If the controls cannot preserve other relevant grounds, that limits the comparison rather than defeating either account.

The resulting direction is neither maximal visibility nor isolation. Care may require information, while a supported place in social life can also require restraint and bounded observation. This investigates how goods and harms arise within the same generative reality, without treating participation, productivity or recognition as the price of all welfare. The thin universal claim remains contested, and the richer alternative needs its own constitutive warrant.

### Opportunity and the good of the recipient

The [new investigation](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#47-opportunity-and-the-good-of-the-recipient) asks whether an unexercised opportunity can itself benefit its recipient. The distinction between opportunity and exercise is inherited. An actual arrangement can sustain a disposition to respond before a need or request occurs; its existence is different from an advertised channel or a favorable observed outcome.

The proposed good concerns the person's place within their conditions of life. Supported protection can constrain another's power to withdraw an appropriate response at will, even when nobody exercises that power. To test its present value, compare a protected arrangement with effective benevolent provision, preserving the recipient's experience and relevant prospects where possible. Equal aggregate forecasts alone are insufficient: request-contingent responses may still differ. The actual powers change, and no physical realization or complete matching has been demonstrated.

The [primary comparisons](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#44-opportunity-and-the-good-of-the-recipient) separate valuable freedom, achieved welfare and choice-dependent moral requirements. This leaves a substantive rival that grants the protected position's moral importance but denies an additional welfare constituent. A richer constitutive account can instead argue that supported possibilities partly determine how the person presently lives. It must explain that benefit rather than infer it from the mere existence of possibilities.

The route may operate through justified representation and can remain appropriately bounded. A right to decline does not imply a right to every alternative; non-use does not automatically cancel remaining claims. Where care is already required, an available request form does not substitute for fulfillment. Prospective reasons can support maintaining effective protection even while its additional intrinsic value remains unsettled. These findings advance how generative organization could realize goods without making every option, guarantee or exercise of control good by definition.


### Protected activity and the grounds of non-domination

The [new argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#48-protected-activity-and-the-grounds-of-non-domination) asks whether protection partly constitutes how a person lives an independently valuable activity. Security need not be an additional welfare item beside that activity. Denying a separate good does not therefore deny its present prudential significance; accepting protection's importance does not settle its grounding. The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#45-protected-activity-and-the-grounds-of-non-domination) keeps these alternatives open and prevents counting one benefit twice.

Compare effective benevolent provision that can be withdrawn at will, rigid rules that can obstruct a justified response, and bounded review responsive to the relevant grounds. Less discretion is not automatically better: correction may require judgment capable of revising a rule. The actual relation includes how the recipient's concerns can govern treatment, who can change its terms, and what challenge or alternative is usable. Matching ordinary provision does not match these powers. Their legitimate domain and value remain substantive questions.

The existing [application reconstruction](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/APPLICATION-REVISION-RECONSTRUCTION.md) gives a concrete interface. At the continuing four-pair state, helpful influence and sufficient resources for the separately demonstrated service coexist with a service gate requiring six pairs. Every admitted continuation to that service requires at least two further acquisition cycles and hence at least four units of gross recipient debit, not necessarily four units of net loss. The learner has no demonstrated authority to revise this gate. This protocol-relative obstruction is neither a proof of domination nor a new validation of the model; its justification depends on burdens, authority, alternatives and competing claims.

The positive direction is warranted revision of the conditions governing response while preserving outstanding claims. Representation or response to evidenced need can protect a noncommunicating recipient. An unrelated noncauser's reason to intervene still needs its own ground and an eligible remedy; taking unbounded control cannot be justified merely as protection. No new theorem, experiment or implementation is added.

### Revising purposes without erasing their consequences

The [new argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#49-revising-purposes-without-erasing-their-consequences) begins with activity changing the conditions of further activity. Understanding its effects can correct the proposed purpose itself: achieving a selected target may leave the relevant burden unchanged or worsen it. Appraisal can then reorganize what follows. The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#46-revising-purposes-without-erasing-their-consequences) examines this formation of ends alongside the legitimate constraint of individual discretion. Neither causal change nor smoother continuation establishes improvement by itself.

A rule justified by protecting a good must be defended in relation to that good and its actual conditions. Compliance alone cannot answer a warranted challenge to that relation. The challenge may nevertheless be mistaken, outweighed or already answered. Legitimate authority can assign reconsideration to another level; identifying a defect does not authorize every operator to bypass the rule. Eligible correction must account for coordination, protections and competing claims, and justify any change in their treatment.

The existing [responsive-formation argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/RESPONSIVE-FORMATION-ARGUMENT.md) already allows a relief proposal to change before a stable undertaking forms. The addition here connects revising purposes with the allocation of revision authority and consequences that outlast a project. Ending further acquisition in the [continuing history](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/APPLICATION-REVISION-RECONSTRUCTION.md) does not itself restore the earlier shortfall. A separately warranted repair ground may persist; a reason genuinely dependent on a permissibly ended project may cease. Neither conclusion follows merely from adopting a new objective.

The next test concerns whose unresolved condition appropriately frames the problem, including beings unable to participate and actors without prior responsibility. Suppressing resistance can improve an organization's operation while leaving the affected being's burden intact. Justifying a response requires addressing that actual ground, its practical relevance and eligible means. No new theorem, experiment or operating revision is claimed.

### Framing inquiry without deciding goodness in advance

The [new argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#50-framing-inquiry-without-deciding-goodness-in-advance) examines how an affected condition can enter inquiry before helping becomes an adopted purpose. Activity can form both experienced conditions and methods that investigate them. A method may produce accurate answers while excluding evidence needed for the broader conclusion advertised. The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#47-framing-inquiry-without-deciding-goodness-in-advance) develops evidence-responsive framing without treating preferred values as conclusions established in advance.

Distinguish the question **Q**, whether interruptions declined, from the claim **C**, that the affected being's condition improved. Where genuine relief and blocked reporting remain live explanations, evidence confined to Q cannot by itself establish C. Retaining the broader claim requires further relevant evidence or argument, a warranted exclusion of the alternative, or a conclusion qualified to the supported scope. More precise interruption counts need not resolve this missing distinction. The comparison reuses inherited identification reasoning; it is not a new physical witness or a demand for certainty.

A narrow Q-study can remain entirely legitimate. Its limited scope neither imposes a duty to investigate further nor proves the omitted condition practically irrelevant. Reasons to correct a belief, acquire information and act remain distinct. A fully informed, uncommitted actor who disputes relief's contribution to their choice presents the stronger practical-scope challenge; calling their knowledge incomplete does not answer it.

The same discipline applies to a proposed generative property G identifying goodness. Specify its claimed domain and preserve routes for relevant evidence or arguments to correct its evaluative adequacy. A richer constitutive account may already preserve the challenged relation; a sparse representation cannot refute it merely by permitting an arbitrary contrary assignment. Nor does criticizing a measure establish universal patient-directed favor. The positive advance is accountable framing, while the ground of that favor remains to justify. No new experiment, controller or theorem is claimed.

### Personal discretion and the source of a reason

The [new comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#51-personal-discretion-and-the-source-of-a-reason) keeps one relief-producing response available to an independently informed, uncommitted actor. In one history nobody requests it; in another a permissible request is made; in a third a valid request is wrongfully issued and a justified protection permits disregarding its reason. The episode and response remain fixed, while pressure, privacy and relationships can change. The whole practical situation is therefore not identical.

If the patient-ground has independent warrant, remains applicable, and the exclusion reaches only the request-created reason, that exclusion alone does not remove the patient-ground. Rejecting an intervener's authority can coexist with acting for the affected being. This supplies no missing initial warrant: absence of an objection is not a reason to help. A broader justified exclusion or changed consequence could defeat the application.

The [primary comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#48-personal-discretion-and-the-source-of-a-reason) distinguishes Herstein's permission to disregard still-valid reasons from the stronger disqualification of current justificatory relevance retained in the award study. Scheffler also distinguishes permission to decline from protection against compulsion. None of these relations can substitute for another, and neither source proves universal patient favor.

For goodness as generativity, the constructive possibility is concern whose warrant does not depend on another person's authority to command it. Forming a demand, forming concern and genuinely relieving a burden perform different work. Their significance and interaction require explanation, including legitimate self-direction. The next test returns to the ordinary choice without any directive: what grounds its discretion, and does that ground exclude the patient's improvement from counting? No new theorem or experiment is claimed.

### Shared perspective and the grounds of discretion

The [new reconstruction](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#52-shared-perspective-and-the-grounds-of-discretion) follows Ren's non-self correction into the formation of the practical whole. A helper's projects and identification develop within activity that also includes the other's undergoing and possible relief. Holding those projects fixed can clarify a local choice; it cannot exhaust how its concerns and possibilities form or change. Taking both lives into view need not mean adding their welfare totals or creating one encompassing experiencer.

In Ren’s stronger example, helping benefits both people overall while changing the helper’s life. Keep both gains in the comparison. The changed life-shape can itself be a worthwhile development through relating and responding; difference from an earlier plan is not by itself a loss. Distinct valuable activities may nevertheless be forgone, and legitimate choice remains a relation to explain. The proposal therefore asks how both lives and their possibilities develop together, while retaining what actually changes for each. No-return care and formation of an additional life test other scopes; neither is a prerequisite for this case.

Three developments matter. Better understanding can reveal a consequence already present. Changed practical identification can reorganize attention and the direction of a life. Actual responsive participation can create trust, responsibilities or another valuable relationship. They can interact, but announcing unity does not accomplish all three. Newly formed care does not retroactively prove an earlier actor-directed reason; that proposed reason needs its own justification. A being unable to communicate or reciprocate can still belong within the concern being investigated.

The positive test is correction through actual consequences. Specify the burden, opportunity and relevant activities before assessing the wider account. Greater fulfillment in the helper cannot certify another's relief. Actual relief cannot conceal damage to the helper's agency or commitments. Either continuity can require revising the purpose, arrangement or claimed success. Such revision need not yield harmony: genuine losses, justified limits and incompatible possibilities remain.

The [primary comparators](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#49-shared-perspective-and-the-grounds-of-discretion) preserve valuable qualifications. Pummer defends bounded discretion over different valuable features despite overall welfare improvement; Muñoz explores permission without an opposing favoring reason. Neither is presented here as a theory of metaphysically fixed selves. Their bounded comparisons do not settle what happens when practical identification genuinely changes, and a later transformation does not invalidate every earlier permission. For goodness as generativity, the constructive target is how experiencing, relating and responding can develop together while remaining accountable to their differentiated effects. No new theorem or experiment is claimed.

### Warranted attention within a shared perspective

The [new reconstruction](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#53-warranted-attention-within-a-shared-perspective) follows how actual consequences can change understanding and relating. Attention can disclose a previously misunderstood person or condition before any public action changes. [Murdoch's account](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#50-warranted-attention-within-a-shared-perspective) supports investigating that possibility while leaving self-deception a live alternative. A warmer interpretation does not certify its own truth.

The stronger philosophical comparison grants accurate identification, genuine relief and a newly caring identity. It then asks whether independently warranted consequences can revise the governing purpose or arrangement, rather than only improve its execution. Better attention need not immediately change conduct; claims of effective correction additionally require that the relevant understanding can bear on what follows.

### Successful support and continuing commitments

The [new comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#54-successful-support-and-continuing-commitments) follows a consequence of successful concern: support helps form a capacity that changes the reasons for its original method. Evidence that a participant can now undertake a previously assisted part can defeat the rationale that direct intervention remains necessary. It does not defeat every reason for the existing arrangement.

Consider the same proposed transfer of that part. Under a warranted undertaking to provide assistance as development requires, changing the method may realize the continuing commitment. Availability under its remaining conditions can persist; acquired capacity does not automatically discharge it. Under a valid joint commitment to perform a particular part together, the transfer may instead change what was agreed and require authorized amendment. The comparison must identify actual words, context, accepted variations and reliance. The [source comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#51-successful-support-and-continuing-commitments) examines this distinction.

A serious case therefore retains a conflict: enabling the developed capacity may cost meaningful collaboration, while maintaining the original form may restrict its exercise. Neither successful growth nor continuing commitment alone settles that comparison. The existing shared-world argument already supports correction where worthwhile possibilities improve without countervailing losses; this case cannot assume those conditions.

Inherited reason revision, claim persistence and legitimate succession supply useful constraints. Defeating one factual rationale does not erase another ground; an unresolved claim is not fulfilled by declaring the project changed. Those results preserve explicitly justified admission, discharge and authority premises. They neither authorize amendment nor select a complete conflict ranking. ### Whose acceptance matters to a practical reason?

The [worked comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#57-whose-acceptance-matters-to-a-practical-reason) keeps one application fixed. B knows an available, permissible response would relieve C's actual severe, still-unwanted experience while preserving C's continuity. No prior undertaking, helping project or return to B supplies the reason. The target is contributory moral favor for this response, not a duty or categorical rational authority.

On the generative account, the actual undergoing–response relation constitutes that favor. In our Carnap-style reconstruction, judging participant A applies a general practical principle treating the same relation as morally favoring B's response. Neither route requires B first to accept the principle. If warranted, the ground can justify forming concern before B has adopted that project. A's acceptance does not establish the principle's validity: a further assessor can understand its application while withholding endorsement.

[Carnap's actual example](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#54-whose-acceptance-matters-to-a-practical-reason) distinguishes speaker A from actor B and gives A a general principle concerning any X and Y. Its conclusion is comparative moral better/ought, and both outcomes benefit B somewhat. The no-return, contributory application here is our reconstruction, not his example unchanged.

Correction must preserve these distinctions. If the proposed response would not produce the specified relief, that efficacy premise no longer supports this application, even if approval persists. A legitimate restriction on the basis for this particular choice may exclude that consideration despite permitting the outcome; such a restriction needs its own warrant. B's private indifference alone supplies neither correction. Conversely, A's rejection of the principle removes this route from A's deliberation without proving that the objective reason disappeared.

The gain is shared independence from the addressed actor's endorsement, not demonstrated independence from every evaluative stance. We must justify the shared moral application and assess G's proposed constitutive explanation. P can express the application warranted by that same explanation; a rival interpretation requires an actual disagreement about meaning, grounding, warrant or scope.

The [dependency clarification](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#58-a-ground-and-its-practical-articulation) makes this positive consequence explicit. If the relation between C's adversity and B's eligible relieving response is warranted as a contributory moral reason, a principle faithfully expressing that reason needs no second independent ethical source. It must preserve the addressee, domain, exceptions and contributory force. One established case supports only that case; a universal principle needs warrant at that scope. This does not explain A's recognition or acceptance, B's motivation, or the semantics of a practical expression. Conversely, a sound application of P does not establish G as its necessary explanation. No new formal proof or full semantic equivalence is claimed.

### Affirmative comparison across formation

The [next argument](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-AUDIT.md#61-affirmative-comparison-across-the-formation-of-a-life) tests whether existing improvement judgments already defeat addition-incomparability. They do not: a coherent partial ordering can retain warranted comparisons among histories forming the same number of lives while leaving different-number histories incomparable. This tests consistency, not the moral truth of that restriction or its physical realization.

A positive route is precise. Let H₀ contain no additional life, H₁ form a worthwhile p, and H₂ form the same p with an independently warranted strict whole improvement. For a transitive at-least-as-good relation and its strict part,

\[
H_1\succeq H_0,\quad H_2\succ H_1\ \Longrightarrow\ H_2\succ H_0.
\]

The affirmative first premise does the work across formation. Merely denying that H₀ is better than H₁ leaves incomparability open. The route proves strict addition for H₂, not automatically H₁ or every worthwhile life. If every worthwhile target admits a strictly lower but still worthwhile counterpart with the required conditions retained, universal weak addition becomes equivalent to universal strict addition. That additional domain condition cannot be presumed, and no finite nonempty comparison class satisfies it.

The substantive next task is therefore to defend the proposed ground of affirmative benign addition against the coverage restriction, keeping actual formation, means, claims and differentiated goods visible. The hand argument identifies a sufficient route; it does not discharge its ethical premise. The [source comparison](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/HUIGEN-SELF-GROUND-SOURCES.md#57-incomparability-and-affirmative-addition) preserves incomparability, equality and practical reasons as distinct claims.

## 7. What a scientific contradiction would mean

If the whole necessity argument succeeds, a claim cannot retain the same meanings, accept the root and applicable conditions, and validly deny their consequence. That supports Ren's intended conclusion at the level of **valid scientific claims and adequate philosophical accounts**.

A conflicting result must nevertheless be investigated. It may reveal a mistaken root premise, invalid inference, failed local assumption, misidentified empirical interface or a false rival claim. Agreement with our formulation cannot decide which. A provisional hypothesis can be false while its proposal and testing remain scientific. Nor does a defective claim invalidate every result of its author or institution.

This makes the research demanding in both directions. An alleged necessary root must survive alternative implementations and interpretations. A critic must address the actual premises and consequence, rather than merely change vocabulary. A successful account must preserve both scientific criticism and the force of demonstrated impossibility.
