The result provides empirical contact with recipient-linked action before reflective moral argument. It does not establish the formation of valuation, recipient-specific understanding or a reason's ethical validity. These are the distinctions the full generative account must explain and connect.

Ren's later clarification makes the strongest starting point causal: an actor's boundary does not make it self-sufficient. Its continuation can require energy, materials, other organisms, inherited knowledge, language, instruments and institutions. Where a dependency is real, refusing to represent it does not remove it. The foundation asks how such consequential relations become operationally significant within the actor, rather than starting with a self-contained mind that later chooses to add a social concern.

Three relations must be distinguished. **Support** keeps cognitive activity physically possible. **Informative coupling** makes an internal difference track something relevant outside the chosen boundary. **Cognitive participation** occurs when an external resource or interaction actually helps perform the task. Food can supply the first without supplying the second; a misleading communication can provide stimulation without reliable information; an accessible record can participate in reasoning. “Interconnected” becomes explanatory only when the relevant route and consequence are identified.

This has close antecedents. Clark and Chalmers argue that suitably coupled external resources can participate in cognition, challenging an automatic identification of the cognitive boundary with the skin or skull. Their argument does not equate every causal influence with part of a mind. [The Extended Mind, 1998](https://www.consc.net/papers/extended.html). De Jaegher and Di Paolo propose that social understanding can emerge and change through the interaction process between participants, rather than residing entirely in independently completed individual representations. This is an account of participatory sense-making, not a guarantee of cooperation or correct moral judgment. [Primary manuscript, 2007](https://cspeech.ucd.ie/Fred/docs/DeJaegherDiPaolo2007.pdf).

A narrower necessity claim does not depend on settling the extended-mind debate. Suppose two possible external situations require incompatible next actions, but the actor receives the same complete decision-time information in both. That information includes available memory, prior learning and any permitted inquiry already performed. The same policy and internal randomization law produce the same action distribution in the two situations. If the adequate action sets are disjoint, that distribution cannot put probability one on both. **No additional computation over unchanged, indistinguishable evidence can guarantee the right action in both cases.** A distinguishing observation, an informative inherited structure, a changed situation or a common adequate action is needed to remove the obstruction. The repository's [learning interface](LEARNING-INTERFACE.md) supplies closely related checked results.

This argument concerns the point after relevant inquiry, or situations indistinguishable under all affordable permitted inquiries. Equality before an available informative experiment does not imply permanent ignorance. Nor does the argument demand constant fresh input. A person can reason offline using learned distinctions; a controller can succeed in a stable setting with a fixed response; an isolated machine can use a previously calibrated model. Historical coupling and stored resources can remain causally active. Online connectivity, unrestricted disclosure and dissolution of boundaries do not follow.

The alignment implication is more fundamental than encouraging an already independent optimizer to be sociable. An actor can destroy the informative and material relations that make its own future judgment adequate. When reality changes, an institution that suppresses inconvenient evidence can damage its capacity to discover what its continued success requires. That is a conditional epistemic and practical failure even before explicit altruism enters the explanation.

The remaining bridge is equally precise: **integration can mean reciprocal participation, but it can also mean extraction, manipulation or coercive control.** A capable exploiter can learn from others and use their resources. Dependence on some external conditions does not imply that every particular other is indispensable, nor that understanding another's condition gives it direct evaluative weight. The central research question is therefore which modes of external integration form and renew cognition while retaining differentiated others as centers with futures of their own. The later concern studies address this bridge; they must not assume its answer in a reward, a supplied identity mapping or the phrase “integration.”

## What follows if a future matters

The strongest charitable formulation of the clarification is:

> If a being's future matters, then the conditions through which that being can continue, fare well, act and develop give reasons for present action. Where those conditions are uncertain and affected by our actions, protecting that future requires adequate ways to learn, revise and implement what we do.

This is an AI formulation. A Chinese counterpart is: **若其未来值得在意，使其能够继续生活、行动与发展的条件，就构成当下行动的理由；当这些条件会被行动改变，而我们的认识可能出错时，理解、修正并实现修正的能力也必须得到维护。** Neither wording means that everyone must survive indefinitely, maximize productivity, or retain every current option.

There are four steps, with different strengths of support.

1. **Causal dependence.** A particular future requires particular conditions. No concern or intention can make an impossible continuation feasible without changing those conditions. Existing support, material, information and composition results give exact instances of this point.
2. **Practical implication.** If we have reason to secure that future, necessary enabling conditions inherit instrumental importance. This supplies a reason to act, not an automatic overriding duty: costs, consent, other claims and uncertainty can change the decision.
3. **Shared dependence.** Our own capacities often depend on other beings, common resources and institutions before that dependence is represented. Learning those relations can make cooperation and restraint intelligible from an initially narrow perspective. Whether a given dependency exists, remains irreplaceable, or rewards cooperation is an empirical and strategic question.
4. **Concern beyond usefulness.** Another being can have a future that matters even when our own continuation does not depend on it. The foundation seeks to explain how this recognition develops. The current ethical argument also defends it as a reason. Neither contribution is supplied simply by proving the first three steps.

This avoids two premature conclusions. It does not dismiss the proposal by treating every normative premise as an inexplicable external preference: the origin, learning and revision of concern remain research targets. It also does not treat a causal explanation of acquired concern as sufficient justification of whatever concern happens to be acquired. These are connected questions with different tests.

The qualification “if future is important” is substantive. An agent may value a brief achievement, accept an informed sacrifice, or care about another's future more than its own institutional continuation. Rest, present enjoyment and chosen completion can also matter. The principle identifies necessary means relative to reasons and commitments; it does not establish that endless continuation dominates all reasons.

## Why this bears on both alignment and governance

An AI changes more than an immediate outcome. It changes what information people receive, what they can afford, which skills remain exercised, who can contest a decision, which organizations gain resources, and who trains or controls the next system. Those changes affect subsequent choices and subsequent alignment work. Governance makes the same recursive changes through institutions, authority and resource allocation.

The common question is therefore: **does the process leave affected beings with adequate conditions to live, understand, act and change what shapes their lives, including through replacement of the process itself?** This is a proposed evaluative question, not a fully specified universal test. Its terms need legitimate interpretation in each setting.

Consider an AI used to allocate access to an essential service. A high average success rate can coexist with a particular group losing access. A complaint channel can exist while complaints arrive after the relevant deadline. An appeal can succeed in principle while affected people cannot afford it. A correction can work today while the next procurement removes its staff and records. The generative account connects these failures: current activity has damaged conditions needed for a justified future response. These are illustrative cases, not newly collected observations.

This also sharpens the earlier [selection-on-cognition diagnosis](../../alignment-selection-on-cognition/README.md). Perfect execution of an institution's immediate objective can reinforce the process that selects harmful objectives and excludes challenges. An aligned component is therefore insufficient for a justified overall arrangement. Conversely, a well-designed institution does not establish that a learned model will behave reliably when supervision weakens. Technical and institutional obligations remain jointly relevant.

The proposed contribution has three layers:

| Layer | Question | Current standing |
|---|---|---|
| A foundational constraint | What must remain possible if the futures we claim to care about are to remain feasible? | Defensible conditional principle; closely related to established viability and agency traditions |
| A mechanism account | How do observation, learning, selection, concern, authority and material support form and persist together? | Several checked local connections; central composition and formation gaps remain |
| A practical alignment method | Can an implementation protect those futures reliably under new tasks, adversaries, institutional change and stronger capabilities? | A research hypothesis with testable designs, not a demonstrated general method |

“Fundamental” can mean that many solutions must respect a constraint. It need not mean that the constraint is sufficient, historically unprecedented, or a complete implementation recipe. The first sense is currently the strongest one available.

## The exact necessity claim

The idea can be made precise using established viability reasoning. This small derivation is included to distinguish a real constraint from an attractive restatement of success; it is an elementary finite formulation, not a claimed new mathematical method. Viability theory already studies the states from which permitted control can keep a process within specified constraints. [Aubin and colleagues, 2001](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sastry/pubs/PDFs%20of%20Pubs2000-2005/Pdfs%20of%20Misc.Others/Aubin/AubinViability.pdf).

Let a full state \(s\) retain every variable needed by the application: affected outcomes, resources, available components, observations, memory, authority and successor conditions. Let \(U(s)\) contain feasible authorized actions, and let nonempty \(D(s,u)\) contain the disturbances considered after choosing an action. The next state is \(f(s,u,d)\). Let \(C\) encode the constraints to be maintained. Its ethical content and empirical measurement are supplied and must be justified independently.

For a finite horizon define

\[
K_0=C,\qquad
K_{n+1}=\{s\in C:\exists u\in U(s)\;\forall d\in D(s,u),\ f(s,u,d)\in K_n\}.
\]

**Conditional result.** With complete state information and the stated order of choice, \(s\in K_n\) exactly when there is a policy maintaining \(C\) now and through the next \(n\) transitions against every admitted disturbance.

The proof is induction. At horizon zero, membership means the present constraint holds. At the next horizon an admissible first action must put every possible successor in the preceding set; its continuation policies then supply the remaining steps. Conversely, a successful policy supplies such a first action and a successful continuation at each reachable successor. Finite state and action sets suffice for this construction.

Several consequences matter for the theory.

* Present compliance \(s\in C\) does not imply future feasibility \(s\in K_n\). An institution may currently meet its obligations after spending the last resources needed to meet them tomorrow.
* If an action moves outside the relevant continuation set, a promise to correct later cannot restore the guarantee under the unchanged model. New support or changed laws can change feasibility; an intention alone cannot.
* Separately feasible futures need not be jointly feasible. Generally \(K_n(\bigcap_i C_i)\subseteq\bigcap_i K_n(C_i)\), and inclusion can be strict: one available resource may sustain either of two centers but cannot sustain both. Requiring each center to matter does not solve allocation.
* Under partial observation, different states cannot be assigned different actions merely because the analyst knows which state occurred. The policy must use accessible information; histories or information states must replace the full-state assumption where needed.
* A revision procedure is necessary only where meeting the chosen obligation actually depends on revision. A simple stable process might need no learner. The general necessity concerns adequate future-enabling functions, not one mandatory bureaucracy or cognitive architecture.

The robust guarantee is an additional modeling choice. Taking a future seriously does not itself require zero risk against every imaginable disturbance. A probabilistic or risk-sensitive obligation needs its own explicit criterion, evidence and acceptable risk. No universal discount rate, horizon or ethical ordering follows from this recurrence.

The mathematics becomes informative when \(C,U,D,f\) are specified before testing and independently identified. Defining “generative” as whatever a successful policy does would make the claim circular. Expanding the hidden state after each failure without a testable constraint would make it empirically empty.

## The strongest challenges

**An actor can preserve its own future by taking power.** This is the most important challenge to the clarification. Certain environment symmetries make power and option preservation instrumentally attractive for many reward functions; this is a formal result within specified decision models, not a theorem that every intelligent being will dominate. [Turner and colleagues, NeurIPS 2021](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/hash/c26820b8a4c1b3c2aa868d6d57e14a79-Abstract.html). The inference from “my future matters” to “others must retain authority over me” therefore needs further premises. For an AI service, protecting affected people may require replacing or shutting down that service. The model's continued operation is not the common future by definition.

**A productive regime can keep people alive while governing all their choices.** Adequate food, low measured distress and technical stability do not establish agency or non-domination. The [ethical foundation](ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md) therefore treats relevant standing and answerability as substantive commitments. A benevolent prediction engine cannot certify the legitimacy of its own coercion by predicting high aggregate welfare.

**Knowing harm does not make harm count in choice.** A controller can model a recipient accurately and exclude that recipient from evaluation. The [claim audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/foundation-assessment/ALIGNMENT-CLAIM-AUDIT.md) states the causal obstruction: if recipient changes are screened off from every operative update and selection route, they cannot cause those routes to change. If faithful sensing has extra cost without a compensating selection consequence, it can lose to blindness. These are conditional failures of proposed learning routes, not a proof that concern can never arise.

**Mutual dependence can disappear.** A powerful actor may automate away a community's bargaining role. Cooperation that depended entirely on that community's usefulness may then collapse. The decisive question is whether recognition and protection persist after leverage, reciprocal benefits and external rewards are removed. Continued concern could arise through internal valuation or legitimate institutions, but those mechanisms must be identified rather than inferred from earlier cooperation.

**Correction can be captured or performed ceremonially.** An operator may select the auditor, control its data, dismiss adverse testimony or manipulate the people who supply feedback. A reason graph can faithfully preserve a false or exclusionary premise. Independence must therefore concern actual causal control and common failure sources, not merely the number of agents or committees.

**Future possibilities can conflict.** A person's freedom to exploit another is not an option that alignment must preserve. Some scarce opportunities cannot be given to everyone. Even compatible individual aspirations can impose incompatible resource demands. Rights, consent, reasons, legitimate priorities and sometimes permissible lotteries are additional work. A single score called generativity would conceal that work.

**Openness can undermine commitments.** Endless reconsideration can prevent protection, increase attack surfaces or consume the remaining budget. Some action must close options, and some institutions must end for affected people to flourish. The requirement is appropriate capacity to correct consequential errors, with feasible decisions in the meantime. It is not maximum plasticity or perpetual deliberation.

**Observed compliance can fail under new incentives or capabilities.** A finite controller proof does not show that a trained model uses the intended reasons, generalizes them, or keeps them when it gains the power to alter feedback. Robustness against deception, reward tampering, distribution shift and strategically changed oversight remains a technical obligation. Explaining why such failures matter does not remove them.

These challenges restrict an alignment guarantee. They do not defeat the wider explanatory ambition, which explicitly includes domination, indifference and collapse. A theory can explain why a harmful regime persists while supplying reasons to change it. Explanatory adequacy and favorable historical direction have different quantifiers.

## What a practical implementation would have to preserve

The proposal suggests evaluating a coupled arrangement through several distinct capabilities. These are design hypotheses, not a certified deployment standard.

| Capability | Concrete question | Failure hidden by a simple performance score |
|---|---|---|
| Affected-center recognition | Can a newly affected party enter the assessment without already benefiting the operator? | Unrepresented harm is absent from the denominator |
| Consequence learning | Can independent evidence change the operative model, including its categories and channels? | Accurate-looking records are selected or stale |
| Revisable reasons | Can grounds be challenged while independent support survives? | Revision erases useful commitments or entrenches a bad premise |
| Effective action and recourse | Can an authorized correction occur before the harm becomes irreversible? | An appeal exists but has no timely effect |
| Material support | Are expertise, time, components and intervention funds available when due? | A solvent annual budget conceals decision-time insolvency |
| Distributed authority | Can the affected challenge framing, evidence and execution without a single veto holder controlling every route? | Multiple nominal reviewers share one point of capture |
| Succession and replaceability | Does a replacement inherit usable evidence and authority while the old system can be retired? | Current cooperation masks future lock-in |

For AI, this suggests architectures with bounded action authority, evidence provenance, realistic intervention and replacement paths, and tests of behavior after dependence and incentives change. For governance, it suggests accessible recourse, protected support for investigation, contestable decision rules, and transitions that do not make the governed unable to refuse. Neither list specifies one universal constitution. Some settings need fast predetermined controls; others need sustained deliberation or local experimentation.

The relevant costs belong inside the account. Review capacity funded only by discretionary approval from the reviewed actor may be fragile. Full independence can also be expensive or impractical. A serious design compares identifiable alternatives under matched resources and asks which failures each arrangement leaves possible.

Evidence independence should not become a requirement to prevent useful exchange or preserve disagreement indefinitely. Becker, Brackbill and Centola's experiment found that decentralized exchange improved numerical judgments even as estimates converged; centralized influence could move the group toward or away from accuracy. The task supplied accuracy incentives and did not test political legitimacy. The resulting design hypothesis is to preserve independently grounded contributions and effective correction while allowing warranted agreement, measuring accuracy separately from convergence. [Primary paper, 2017](https://ndg.asc.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/PNAS-2017-1615978114-Collective-Intelligence.pdf).

Contribution and control also need separate records. Farrell and Newman's historical comparison shows how jurisdiction over network hubs, together with institutional capacity, can turn interdependence into information access or exclusion power. Shared infrastructure can persist while some participants lose control over their futures. [Primary paper, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/isec_a_00351). For this programme, the additional questions are who supplies a necessary function, who can interrupt it, what substitutes exist, and whether an affected party can contest its use. Those questions test the mode of integration; connectivity alone does not answer them.

## The connected account after the finite succession result

The [current completion checkpoint](ALIGNMENT-COMPLETION.md) credits completed connections and separates them from the remaining objective. Its [composition audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/foundation-assessment/ALIGNMENT-COMPOSITION-CHECKPOINT.md), [evidence audit](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/foundation-assessment/ALIGNMENT-EVIDENCE-CHECKPOINT.md) and [independent review](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/foundation-assessment/ALIGNMENT-COMPLETION-REVIEW.md) preserve the inspected scope. This is a substantive synthesis of existing results, not a new empirical run or acceptance of the whole foundation.

The account begins with external conditions that actually enable a process to act and continue. Under the identified feedback laws, a changed dependency can produce an accessible difference, a response and changed support for retaining that response. Constructed learners then acquire and revise predictions through resource-dependent contact, retained records and implemented updating. This gives operational significance a causal explanation in specified systems. It does not assume that every persistent organization regulates or that every useful response is knowledge.

When acquired content controls an assessment or action, its informational role becomes effective within the process. The accepted recipient sequence extends that relation beyond private outcomes: actual recipient events supply evidence, retained evidence supports command-specific reasons, and acquired evaluative influence changes selected recipient action. Equally informed filtering can block that influence. The operative connection, transfer sign and recipient interpretation remain local inputs; privately useful learning alone does not entail the shared route. The prospective plasticity study asks how part of that route itself can form rather than merely installing another value in it.

Justification enters through a different argument. The [ethical foundation](ETHICAL-FOUNDATION.md) defends reasons concerning a subject's loss, relevant consistency, agency and answerability. These are positive ethical commitments with arguments, not facts established by the operator's surplus. Given a justified ground, sound acquired evidence can show which action answers it; the application must still assess authority and competing claims. An accurate prediction of a benefit is insufficient when the predicted outcome is not an established good for this recipient, the actor lacks permission, or another claim changes the comparison.

Action then changes the situation in which its reason applied. A successful service can fulfill a claim; an authorized refusal can change permission; new evidence can defeat one derivation while another survives. Generic current-reason revision and affordable changed action already have an accepted construction. The acquired evaluator's stronger fulfillment-sensitive application remains unimplemented, even though a suitable receipt can be retained. This is a precise use-of-evidence gap, not a general absence of observations or a requirement for another universal moral decision system.

The account also follows effects beyond the actor's internal record. Correcting institutional reasoning may require correcting earlier guidance and enabling actual remedy. Sustaining those responses depends on resources, legitimate powers and strategic choices whose future consequences must be evaluated. A replacement must receive usable evidence and support without resetting the history or preserving an obsolete controller's authority. The accepted finite successor supplies one actual handoff; the strategic and governance studies supply separate conditional and historical interfaces. Their conjunction on a page is not yet one implemented human–AI arrangement.

This is the strongest connected explanation currently supported: actual relations can build, inform, redirect and undermine the capacities needed for further understanding and justified response. It retains differentiated affected centers and explains why private continuation can coexist with their exclusion. The informative content comes from the identified laws, mechanisms and failure boundaries, not the mere ability to redescribe everything as interaction. Exact conventional equivalence remains evidence about mechanism, while limiting claims of algorithmic distinctiveness.

The remaining work is therefore specific: explain the evaluative input still supplied; complete warranted application and revision at the scope claimed; finish the common premise/composition/regime account; and test an identified learned-AI or institutional arrangement before claiming practical effectiveness. These obligations preserve Ren's full objective. They do not require unique metaphysical minimality, universal moral agreement or every capacity in one carrier unless those stronger claims are retained. The [earlier assessment](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/foundation-assessment/development/ALIGNMENT-AND-GOVERNANCE-BEFORE-COMPLETION-CHECKPOINT.md) is preserved exactly, including its historical pending statuses.

## The next research connection and how to test it

**Subsequent correction/succession work, 6 October 2026:** [the source and mathematical analysis](MEMORY-CORRECTION-AND-SUCCESSION.md) distinguishes preserved corrections from justified later use and narrows an external impossibility inference. Its follow-on 24-context pilot now records correct selective retention, revision and uncertainty in a supplied two-rule family. Ordinary instructions and an added boundary reminder perform identically at ceiling, and conventional candidate elimination matches. This supports a bounded later-assessment capacity; it neither identifies the causal contribution of the constantly present packet nor measures repaired persistent memory, external execution or newly acquired concern.

The central connection for this assessment is **consequence-grounded learning and selector revision → durable concern for differentiated others → effective protection of their future under changed power and dependence**. It supports the approved derivation path; it does not replace it with a separate application programme.

The initial continuation began at the existing recipient interface. The [transmission contract](../../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/RECIPIENT-TRANSMISSION-CONTRACT.md) has now led to the [bounded implementation](RECIPIENT-TRANSMISSION.md) described above. Actual formed and inherited routes replace the former external policy label, with copying and finite retention paid. That requirement has since been met at the declared finite scopes of successor learning, renewal and acquired-evaluation succession; those accepted interfaces are not reopened here. Recipient-funded propagation is now a specified causal mechanism, but not evidence of direct moral concern. A proxy can produce the same initial recipient stock, and a correctly initialized frozen record can match a learned record in stable use. Repeated changes must discriminate the quality of learning rather than merely the luck of the inherited answer.

The three studies proposed below remain prospective tests of stronger practical or empirical claims. They are not additional prerequisites for every conditional foundation result. The separate 24-context pilot has actual learned-agent outputs at its much narrower reassessment scope; no new human or institutional intervention is reported here.

**Current status after succession acceptance, 6 October 2026:** [evaluative uptake](EVALUATIVE-UPTAKE.md) and [actual evaluative succession](EVALUATIVE-SUCCESSION.md) are independently accepted. The latter carries acquired influence into a distinct child, fresh recipient evidence, revised means and selected resource-dependent action. The [ethical application audit](EVALUATIVE-APPLICATION.md) separately states the patient-good, authority and comparison premises. It also shows why a qualifying inquiry receipt can already fulfill a one-service claim before the later selected action; that evaluator does not withdraw its recommendation on this basis. Generic current-reason correction already exists in the separate protection model. [Evaluative plasticity](EVALUATIVE-PLASTICITY.md) remains prospective at this checkpoint and targets the origin of an operative connection. Neither the animal reanalyses nor the handoff pilot validate the full constructed mechanism.

**First, discriminate formation and retention mechanisms.** Vary recipient dependence and private benefit independently. Include private neutrality, a private cost, and a transition from dependence to dispensability. Keep outcome assessment out of the mechanism being explained. Compare materially implemented faithful, gated and misleading routes under equal initial information and resources. Measure actual recipients' outcomes, selector survival, expenditure and successor use across all starting histories, including failures. A selective advantage that vanishes when the analyst's external score is removed would expose a supplied reward.

**Second, test a learned implementation against strong alternatives.** After the formal interface is actually composed, compare a standard feedback controller, an assistance or corrigibility design, and the proposed implementation with matched information, action access and expenditure. Use held-out recipient identities and causal contexts; test conflicting claims, stale evidence, audit suppression, scarce budgets and removal of reciprocal benefits. Evaluate conduct and continued recourse, not declarations of caring. No single external judge should supply the only measure of success; disagreement about affected outcomes must remain visible. Select effect sizes, primary outcomes and sample sizes before the confirmatory run, using a separate pilot where needed.

**Third, test the institutional translation in a bounded voluntary setting.** Compare equally staffed decision processes with and without protected evidence access, effective appeal resources and succession procedures. Follow actual decisions and corrections over repeated leadership or system changes. Measure time to remedy, costs borne by affected parties, unrepresented cases, exclusion, false challenges, capture attempts and continued ability to leave or replace the arrangement. A higher satisfaction score alone cannot identify the proposed mechanism; extra money alone must be controlled as an alternative explanation. Generalizing to public authority would require additional legitimacy and empirical work.

The decisive positive evidence for the specified claims and test conditions would be a compatible mechanism that preserves recipient-sensitive action through a change removing its instrumental private advantage, with the reasons, funding and authority carried into actual successors. Its ethical justification requires a separate assessment of the relevant claims, rather than being certified by behavioral performance. Stronger evidence would reproduce that pattern in materially different implementations and show a predeclared advantage or useful prediction over established alternatives.

The decisive negative findings are equally clear: the mechanism needs a hidden benevolent selector; accurate understanding improves domination; concern disappears with leverage; enforcement spends the next correction budget; or one center's flourishing masks another's exclusion. Such results would revise the proposed sufficiency of the bridge while leaving separately verified earlier results intact. If a matched conventional account explains the entire result with fewer commitments, that instead narrows the claim of distinctiveness or superiority; equivalence does not invalidate the demonstrated mechanism.

## The claim worth carrying forward

The current strongest statement is:

> For a specified boundary, task and horizon, continuation must sustain its actual support dependencies. Adequate action across situations requiring incompatible responses needs usable distinctions—acquired now, retained from history or realized in inherited organization—whether or not the actor recognizes their external origin. Alignment and governance must be assessed through how action changes these relations and the conditions of affected beings' worthwhile futures. Under uncertainty and changing power, those conditions often include the ability to discover harm, revise reasons, make justified correction effective, and preserve or reconstruct that ability through succession.

This gives a common research object to technical alignment, institutional governance and the generative foundation. It explains why current obedience, profitability, survival or evaluation success is insufficient evidence of lasting alignment. It also explains why protecting the future can require transforming or ending a present system.

The foundation has not yet derived general inclusive concern, identified legitimate resolutions of every conflict, or demonstrated a scalable implementation that retains these properties under strategic pressure. What has emerged is a serious candidate for a fundamental constraint and a connected mechanism programme. Completing that programme requires the [existing completion conditions](GENERATIVE-GROUNDING.md#completion-of-the-unified-foundation), with the recipient-to-concern and concern-to-effective-future-protection bridges made explicit.
