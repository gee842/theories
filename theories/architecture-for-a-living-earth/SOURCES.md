# Sources and evidence boundaries

Initial bounded check, 2026-09-13. Compiled by the assistant. This is not an exhaustive review of future architecture, construction robotics, or ecological design. External sources support specific existing mechanisms or constraints; none validates the proposed settlement as a whole.

## Founding source

[Ren’s opening message](../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-09-13-architecture-for-a-living-earth.md), 2026-09-13. Establishes the intended vision: tree-like structures, elevated modular frameworks, living ground, moving shadows, cheap energy, recursive automation, recoverable materials, reusable cups, robotic finishing, optional flying transport, seasonal relocation, and ecosystem support. It supplies the direction, not empirical proof.

## Trees

[National Park Service — Mycorrhizal Relationships, Muir Woods](https://www.nps.gov/muwo/learn/nature/mycorrhizal-relationships.htm). Page opened. Describes shallow, widely spreading redwood roots and relationships with fungi. Supports distinguishing visible trunk area from underground extent. This example should not be generalized into one root geometry for all trees or a prescribed foundation shape for buildings.

## Foundations

[FHWA — Structural Foundations](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/engineering/geotech/foundations/) and [GEC No. 6 — Shallow Foundations](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/engineering/geotech/pubs/010943.pdf). Search-index excerpts checked; the manual PDF could not be opened in this session. The excerpts identify bearing capacity, settlement, and overturning considerations. These transportation references supply basic foundation context, not a design standard selected for an inhabited megastructure. No load calculations or code compliance assessment have been done.

## Light and ecology

[NREL — Initial Feasibility Assessment of Agrivoltaics](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy24osti/88816.pdf). Search-index excerpts checked; direct PDF opening failed. The available excerpt describes crop-specific shading, microclimate, and access questions. This motivates testing light over time. It provides no evidence that housing above a forest conserves forest biodiversity. No quantitative finding from this report is adopted here.

## Reusable structures

[World Steel Association — Reuse](https://worldsteel.org/wider-sustainability/circular-economy/all/reuse/). Page opened. Describes actual reuse examples and a building project using material passports and demountable connections. This is an industry source; the note uses the documented methods, not a universal claim about steel’s environmental superiority.

The existing local [material circulation evidence check](../../worldbuilding/research/2026-09-07-material-circulation.md) includes JRC guidance on assessing reclaimed structural components and UNEP evidence on reusable cups. That local note was read; its linked documents were not all reopened for this new exploration. It is a prior assistant evidence synthesis, not Ren-authored language.

## Robotic construction

[ETH Zurich — Robotic collaboration in timber construction](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2018/03/spatial-timber-assemblies.html), 2018. Page opened. Reports robotic fabrication and assembly of timber modules for DFAB HOUSE. Supports the existence of a bounded robotic construction process. It does not establish autonomous megastructure construction, complete robotic interior finishing, or factories capable of supplying their entire own reproduction chain.

## Material recovery

[Daehn, Serrenho, and Allwood — How Will Copper Contamination Constrain Future Global Steel Recycling?](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/93eecec5-af72-4cd4-9440-95f5eab378d5), 2017; [publisher record](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.7b00997). Search-index abstract excerpts checked. Supports treating steel composition and contamination as constraints on recycling. It is not a proof that future separation technologies cannot improve.
