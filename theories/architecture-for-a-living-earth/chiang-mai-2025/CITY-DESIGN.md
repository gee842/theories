# The supporting city

2026-09-13 · Assistant-authored design hypotheses for [Chiang Mai 2025](README.md). Fusion, autonomous construction, generous personal capability, and planetary shared provision are user-set premises. Exact infrastructure, geometry, and institutions remain proposals. No parcel has been selected, no surveys obtained, and no structure, tunnel, flood, or ecological model validated.

## Start with the valley and the inhabited city

Use Doi Suthep, the existing city, and the Ping River as orientation for a future regional drawing. Preserve connections to actual residents and inherited places. A conceptual west-to-east section can relate mountain-side habitat, the urban landscape, and river space; it must be labeled schematic, since a straight line does not represent the varied topography of the whole city.

The first site hypothesis is a previously developed neighborhood with retained mature trees, outside floodways established by a future site study. This lets the exploration test returning paved ground to life. It does not authorize building above intact forest or select a real parcel. Larger forests and working farmland remain their own landscape types.

The spatial proposal has several connected conditions rather than a uniform forest floor:

| Place | Proposed life and infrastructure |
|---|---|
| Existing cultural streets and courts | Retain useful buildings, familiar routes, craft and gathering places; adapt traffic and services where residents support it |
| Inhabited groves | Firm accessible paths, public facilities, shaded clearings, garden homes, and selected elevated clusters |
| Larger habitat areas | More ecological continuity, lower disturbance, and routes fitted to the habitat; fewer comfort interventions |
| River and drainage landscapes | Space for water, crossings with explicit flood behavior, and public access suited to changing conditions |
| Productive landscapes | Food, nurseries, material recovery, and logistics with their own land, water, and transport account |
| Industrial and energy sites | Fusion supply, manufacturing, computing, heat rejection, water treatment, and repair, situated through a regional study |

The mountain view, the ecology, and the human city are all part of the inherited place. The proposed design begins from them rather than treating the valley as empty land.

## Underground movement without an underground citywide slab

Ren’s underground-road idea can release surface corridors from heavy movement. A useful first hypothesis is a limited network of passenger transit, automated freight, and utility corridors linked to neighborhood service hubs. Some alignments could follow already disturbed transport land, subject to subsurface and heritage investigation. Much of the routine surface remains for walking, wheeling, play, and living ground.

Below-ground construction still occupies space. The section must show tunnels, ventilation, emergency exits, loading areas, drainage, and maintenance access. Boring, excavation, spoil transport, groundwater effects, and root protection belong in the construction account. Essential surface access remains available where required. Routes and safe access cannot disappear simply because the vehicles drive themselves.

Chiang Mai’s Ping flooding makes tunnel siting and portals central design questions. CMU’s flood record and mapping resources are starting points for a future site study. The October 2024 flood also documents the practical relevance of river overflow. These sources do not supply a design flood level for this proposal. [CMU flood resources](https://watercenter.cmu.ac.th/chiangmai), [October 2024 reporting](https://apnews.com/article/0d0391104d14db25c4d4c6cd73280f29)

For the fictional design, give below-ground networks separated compartments, routes that can be taken out of service, protected entries, drainage capacity, and independently usable alternatives. The objective is dependable life when one part is unavailable. Dimensions and performance require engineering before this can become a real plan.

This section is relational and not to scale. Foundations can extend much farther than the visible trunks. Trees need room below and above ground, including future growth. A drawing that shows a trunk standing in a tiny hole must not conceal a large underground support system.

Preserve meaningful sky openings between inhabited clusters. Study total daily and seasonal light at existing canopy and ground, including neighboring structures. Height, floor area, branch reach, stability, foundation disturbance, and light should be considered together. A low visible support count is not a sufficient objective.

The baseline comparison is a compact courtyard neighborhood supplying equivalent homes and shared spaces. Elevation earns its place when its whole system supports the landscape and residents better under the selected conditions.

## Comfort in a living landscape

**Paths.** Explore repairable surfaces with a fine texture, shade, drainage, and continuous accessible alignments. Test wet slip, surface temperature, root growth, cleaning, wheelchair movement, and bare-foot comfort before selecting a material. Natural appearance is a design intention, not evidence of permeability or ecological benefit. Sensitive roots may require a carefully supported crossing rather than pavement laid over them.

**Public facilities.** Place toilets, washing, drinking water, shelter, rest, and assistance along actual routes and at gathering places. Determine spacing through walking and accessibility studies, including children and people who cannot comfortably go far. Connect fixtures to managed water, wastewater, cleaning, and replacement systems. Streams and planted basins do not receive untreated waste because the setting looks organic.

**Insects and sleeping.** Comfortable public groves use local maintenance and protected refuges. Screens and mosquito-proof water storage have established roles in vector prevention; WHO also describes water, sanitation, and waste-management measures. This supports exploring maintained service areas and screened sleeping places. It does not establish a bite-free city or justify removing natural wetlands and insect populations. [WHO/UNICEF technical note](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240090644)

**Seasonal weather.** The TMD describes the broad hot, rainy, and cool seasons, with the rainy period generally running from around mid-May to mid-October. The design therefore has dry routes, shelter, drainage, ventilated shade, and private indoor choices alongside its open-air life. No exact comfort target has yet been calculated. [TMD seasons](https://www5.tmd.go.th/info/ฤดูกาลของประเทศไทย)

**Air.** Breathable outdoor life requires work beyond the city boundary. CMU’s study of 2019 samples identifies contributions from biomass burning, traffic, and transported pollution, with different patterns in and outside the sampled haze period. It is a bounded study, not a present citywide source inventory. [CMU research account](https://www.cmu.ac.th/web/article/89cb6260-ba01-4ed1-8f4a-9a9694d5a8dc)

Within the alternate history, the planetary provision network enables regional partners to support alternatives to avoidable burning, material collection, locally informed land management, and cleaner transport. Improved ambient air is an additional outcome the setting must explain and measure. Local filtered rooms provide a fallback. Fusion energy alone does not remove airborne smoke or settle land-management questions.

## A shared substrate that actually provides

Follow useful output through the whole network:

`energy + material + public knowledge → autonomous production → provision and repair + further capacity`

Food, housing, care, and creative resources need actual service capacity, logistics, and maintenance. Regional production and computing have land, cooling, and material requirements, even when their energy is cheap. The city includes its share of those effects in its ecological account.

The institutional proposal makes ordinary access durable across residence, project changes, and disagreement. Compatible local providers share resources and repair knowledge. Routine provision is available through ordinary physical access as well as personal agents. An individual can use meaningful compute and fabrication capacity without recruiting a profitable enterprise. Larger jobs can require scheduling or collective review of their physical effects. Human collaborators choose whether to participate.

Demanding voluntary work remains possible. Essentials continue if someone stops. Where human expertise is still wanted, the setting offers willing practitioners, training, and the ability to pause optional expansion; it does not conceal compulsory labor underneath the pleasant public life.

## Construction and materials in the background

A service hub receives inspected structural parts and keeps space for staging. Robots assemble a stable frame from an explicit sequence, install services and enclosure, and test them before people move in. The growing frame can provide robot access where engineered to do so. Fit-out, printing, inspection, and repair remain distinct operations.

When an association changes its rooms, the neighborhood first considers adaptation within the existing structure. If components move, the service route takes them to inspection, storage, and a next use. Unusable parts enter suitable material recovery. The foundation and the disturbed site have a separate restoration plan.

A cup from breakfast returns to a local collection point, travels to washing and inspection, and reappears in the available stock. Water, energy, cleaning inputs, losses, and eventual recovery remain visible to those maintaining the service. A person may also keep a beloved cup. These sequences develop the earlier [material foundation](../../../worldbuilding/foundations/regenerative-material-civilization.md).

## First architectural study

Produce a neighborhood plan and two comparable sections: elevated cluster and compact courtyard. Use an explicitly illustrative program before choosing dimensions. Include the same homes, public kitchen, learning court, toilets, service hub, protected sleeping place, garden, and usable routes.

Follow one continuous walk and one component delivery. Show normal operation, heavy rain, and a closed lift or service segment. Mark the real space taken by roots, foundations, machinery, storage, ventilation, drains, and emergency access. A later seasonal-light model can test the geometry.

The study should answer whether a person can enjoy the promised outdoor life, whether the ecosystem has the conditions to persist, and whether the common infrastructure can support both through ordinary change. A beautiful skyline alone cannot answer those questions.
