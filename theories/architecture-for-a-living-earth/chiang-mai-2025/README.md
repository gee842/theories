# Chiang Mai 2025

**A city whose shared abundance gives people room to live, and whose form gives life room to grow.**

Working world, opened 2026-09-13. Ren chose the name and an alternate 2025 in which fusion energy, autonomous construction armies, generous personal compute and automation, and a planetary infrastructure of shared provision already exist. The narrative and particular arrangements below are assistant-authored proposals based on [Ren’s direction](../../../sources/15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-09-13-chiang-mai-2025.md), integrated with selected earlier writing. This is an inhabited future under those premises, not a claim about historical 2025 or an engineering-approved plan.

Read the [supporting city design](CITY-DESIGN.md) for the physical and service systems, and [connections and evidence](CONNECTIONS-AND-EVIDENCE.md) for intellectual provenance, local research, and remaining uncertainties.

## A morning in the city

You come down from your room before breakfast. The lift opens beside a covered veranda, and the route ahead curves between trees. A child is crouching beside the path, watching ants carry something much larger than themselves. Her grandfather waits without asking her to hurry. Somewhere farther away, someone is practicing a string instrument, stopping at the same difficult passage.

The walking surface is cool underfoot. It has a fine, firm texture, with no sharp gravel in the route. At the edge, the maintained surface gives way to leaf litter and roots. A person in a wheelchair passes easily; the child draws her feet back with an exaggerated little bow. The trees have their own uneven ground. The path accommodates bodies that need something different.

A small pavilion stands at the next clearing. You can see drinking water, toilets, a washing place, and somewhere to sit through a shower. The doors are ordinary doors. You do not need to buy anything, join a community, or announce yourself to use them. A service robot is replacing a worn tap behind a partition; another basin remains open.

Breakfast is being set out under a broad roof. Some of the food arrives from the provision system. Some has been made by a neighbor who enjoys feeding people and is trying a different recipe this morning. She asks what you think. You can stay to cook with her, take a bowl to someone else, or continue along the path. She is free to stop cooking next week. Breakfast will still be here.

Through an opening in the canopy, you see the mountain. The city has kept long views toward Doi Suthep. The upper rooms of a neighboring cluster sit between patches of sky, supported from a few substantial trunks. They do not form a continuous ceiling. Some terraces are already busy; others have curtains drawn. One household has kept the same arrangement for years. A nearby association is planning a new studio.

You hear a low sound briefly from a service entrance, then its doors close. Goods and recovered components travel through the infrastructure below and beside the inhabited landscape. Its work is present in the dependable availability of things: water, breakfast, dry shelter, a lift that runs, a path that can be repaired. Most of the morning does not require thinking about it.

The scene’s mature trees belong to a landscape preserved or restored over an earlier history in this alternate world. Fast construction has not made an old forest overnight.

## What has changed underneath ordinary life

In this Chiang Mai, everyone has access to a good home, food, water, sanitation, care, mobility, communication, and the means to pursue interests. Personal compute and the ability to ask machines to help make something are ordinary resources. The provision is generous enough to support a life, including experiment, pleasure, guests, and changing one’s mind.

Ren’s image of a tree offering fruit captures the intended experience: the supporting world is fruitful, and a person can receive from it. To make that experience dependable at city scale, this proposal gives provision an actual material organization. Fusion supplies abundant energy. Automated agriculture, factories, logistics, repair, and construction turn energy, knowledge, and materials into useful services. Some productive output maintains and expands this capacity. Shared access makes the resulting capability available to people.

The infrastructure operates through connected local systems with compatible interfaces, public repair knowledge, and resources held across the wider network. This distributed arrangement is a design proposal for Ren’s planetary substrate. A neighborhood can keep essential services operating locally during a network interruption. A person who moves between communities retains access. Membership in a gardening circle or a game guild does not administer the right to a home.

People still make commitments. Someone can choose difficult work, demanding study, a competitive game, or an exacting discipline. They can keep a promise and be answerable for how their actions affect others. What changes is that a failed project or an ended affiliation does not push them back into material desperation. In this setting, demanding arrangements remain voluntary because a livable alternative is available.

That changes the question with which a person begins the morning. It becomes possible to ask where one wants to spend attention, whom one wants to be with, and what is worth doing together. There is also space for a morning without an answer.

## A landscape of different places

Chiang Mai 2025 develops around its mountain, river, existing communities, and inherited places. This is a transformation of a lived city. Its residents, languages, temples, craft traditions, gardens, memories, and ordinary attachments participate in what changes. The name 大同 expresses the orientation Ren brings to the exploration; it is not a new identity imposed on Chiang Mai or a claim that all local people share one philosophy.

The old city remains legible. Selected former traffic space becomes shaded walking ground, public rooms, water gardens, and places for play. Some beloved streets and houses stay recognizably themselves. Toward the river, the settlement opens into landscapes that can accommodate water. Farther out, gardens and inhabited groves meet farmland and larger habitat areas. Forest does not cover every square metre: the city also needs sunny clearings, productive fields, wet places, gathering grounds, and routes that remain usable in rain.

The [Living Garden](../../../worldbuilding/worlds/01-living-garden.md) becomes one of its possible neighborhood forms. Residents know particular trees and each other’s habits. Machines can maintain the essential systems, while people garden because they enjoy the practice and the relationships it makes. Some families prefer an elevated cluster. Others prefer a small dwelling around an existing clearing or a familiar courtyard. The architectural inquiry compares these forms by what they support, including the ecosystem around them.

An [Open University](../../../worldbuilding/worlds/03-open-university.md) occupies a connected set of public courts, libraries, instrument rooms, workshops, and quieter places. You can enter because you have a question. A long investigation can become your life for a while. Learning does not have to finish as a credential. People who enjoy music, athletics, spiritual practice, fabrication, or extended exploration find other institutions with equally substantial means.

Associations are easy to begin. A few neighbors can request a meeting place, design help, tools, and a small allocation of fabrication capacity. They might restore a stream, build an instrument, develop a theatrical world, study an insect, or make something simply because it delights them. A larger intervention involves the people and places it will affect. Access to productive means gives initiative substance; it does not make every desired site or another person’s attention automatically available.

The whole valley need not become one planned community. Towns, villages, institutions, and temporary gatherings share provision while retaining different rhythms. Someone can move toward a demanding research culture, spend a season in a playful town, and later settle in a quiet garden. Common means support those differences.

## When a question grows into a place

After breakfast, the child from the path asks why rain runs clear from one slope and brown from another. At the learning court, someone helps her put the question into words. An AI tutor offers possible explanations and shows what each would predict. A field researcher says they could go and look together.

A small group forms: children, two neighbors, a person who knows the local watercourses, and someone who likes making instruments. They borrow equipment. They keep observations through several rains. Nobody needs to turn the question into a business before being allowed to pursue it.

They discover that a heavily used shortcut near their own meeting place has exposed soil. Their first proposal is a hard path. A gardener points out where it would interfere with a tree’s roots; a wheelchair user explains where the alternative becomes too steep. With fabrication help, they try a short, removable crossing at another alignment. The next storms help them see what still needs changing.

The result is a better crossing, a record of the observations, and a set of construction details another community can adapt. The children turn the story into a rainy-season play. A visitor takes an interest in the instruments and starts a different inquiry. The people involved have also learned how their way of using a place changes it.

This is one concrete meaning of 生生不息 in the city. Something worthwhile leaves conditions from which further worthwhile things can arise: knowledge, an accessible path, an intact root zone, friendship, a story, a new question. A neighbor who watched the rain from a chair all afternoon owes no equivalent achievement.

Ren put the distinction directly in an earlier conversation:

> It’s not about being generative yourself, it’s about ensuring your environment is

He followed it with:

> 生生不息, this is the point

## A city people can play together

The [City of Shared Quests](../../../worldbuilding/worlds/06-city-of-shared-quests.md) supplies another part of the setting. In Chiang Mai, a game can begin as a neighborhood adventure and grow into a season of shared stories. Participants invent roles, hidden histories, puzzles, music, rival teams, and festivals. Some adventures lead into learning or repair. Others offer the pleasure of playing.

One association proposes a game about the watershed. Its first version awards points for planting seedlings. Participation grows quickly, and the planting days are joyful. Later, the group discovers that some planting sites were poorly chosen and that the point system has made patient care less visible than a new photograph.

They discuss what the game is teaching them. With local ecological advice, they change the next season: more observation, tending where it is useful, and stories that celebrate the place across time. Some areas are left alone. The closing feast includes a comic performance about their first overconfident plan. Their understanding has changed through the consequences of the world they made.

Recognition can take the form of friendship, a beautiful object, a remembered role in the story, a celebration, or the satisfaction of something living well. Competitive games can also award bounded prizes. Essential provision remains available to spectators, unsuccessful participants, and people who dislike games. A citywide invitation has quiet routes around it and ordinary ways to use the city.

This develops Ren’s earlier argument that winning should not keep purchasing the power to decide how everyone else must play. The practical question is how a shared activity shapes desire: whether success makes people more able to help others begin, or gives an established group indefinite control of the next beginning. ## 大同 as the character of daily relations

Here 大同 names a condition people are trying to sustain: no one is abandoned to material insecurity, and care can extend beyond the boundary of a household or faction. People find meaning in participating in a world that supports them and can be made better through their participation. This is an interpretation of Ren’s use of 大同, not a scholarly reconstruction of one ancient political program.

The culture develops through repeated experience. A newcomer is helped and later knows how to welcome someone else. A repaired object returns to use. An association shares an invention, and another association improves it. A disagreement leads to a changed arrangement rather than expulsion from the means of life. Children grow up seeing that asking, contributing, resting, and reconsidering all have a place.

Ren’s earlier 大同 conversation connects security with cooperative behavior while also emphasizing human drives and reinforcing feedback. That gives the city a cultural task alongside its infrastructure. The proposal expects material security and constructive experiences to make cooperation easier; it does not require that desire, pride, disagreement, or mistakes disappear. [Ren’s April 11 discussion](../../../sources/12-claude-ai-chats/2026-04-11_大同的技术可实现性_893f7a0e.md)

A gardener may want a place to remain unchanged. A young association may want to build beside it. Their disagreement belongs in the city’s life. They can draw alternatives, try temporary arrangements, and decide that a particular construction should happen elsewhere. The ability to manufacture quickly makes such choices more practical. Affection and ecological time still give reasons to keep things.

## Afternoon rain, evening shelter

The light changes while you are at the learning court. Rain reaches the roof before it reaches the inner tables. Some people move their chairs to watch. Others take a covered route home. Water from the building follows a visible, maintained drainage route into the neighborhood water system; the design has accounted for where overflow goes.

At one point, maintenance has closed a short route. The diversion remains accessible and sheltered. Later, a lift fault takes one cabin out of use. The cluster has an alternate route and places to wait comfortably while the system restores service. The city’s calm depends partly on making an interruption manageable.

After dinner you decide to sleep near the grove. A small screened sleeping pavilion has dry bedding, a raised sleeping surface, and a toilet within easy reach. You can hear the forest and open the screen when conditions suit you. Another person chooses a private room with controlled air. The wider habitat remains full of insects and other life; protected sleeping places provide comfort within it.

The city aims for low biting pressure along heavily used paths and gathering places through well-maintained water systems, local monitoring, and suitable protection. It does not claim that a biodiverse tropical forest can be made universally bite-free. The comfortable barefoot route is a maintained choice through the landscape, not a promise that every patch of ground is equally suited to bare feet. [Design and evidence boundaries](CITY-DESIGN.md#comfort-in-a-living-landscape)

Above the trees, a few occupied terraces glow softly. Many others are dark. The mountain is still there. Tomorrow you might return to the water group, visit a friend’s village, ask a machine to help make something, or spend another unhurried morning on the path.

## What to develop next

The first spatial study should follow this same journey: an inhabited cluster, its ground-level service pavilion, the woodland path, breakfast court, learning court, and a screened sleeping place. Show it in dry weather and heavy rain, then open a section through its foundations, circulation, robot routes, and water systems. Compare an elevated cluster with a compact courtyard version on the same illustrative site.

Chiang Mai 2025 is now the first situated case within [Architecture for a living Earth](../README.md). Its question is how a generous common foundation, a generative culture, and an inhabited ecosystem can reinforce one another in a place people would want to live.
