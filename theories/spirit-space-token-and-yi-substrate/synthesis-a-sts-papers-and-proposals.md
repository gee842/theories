# Spirit–Token–Space and the Yi substrate — Part A: the STS papers and the Bonfire proposals

> A society is made of three kinds of thing and nothing else: **spirits** (whoever can act and decide), **tokens** (any claimable unit of meaning — a membership, a right, a debt, a credit, an honour), and **spaces** (the contexts in which a token means anything at all). Every social fact then has one shape: *a spirit holds a token in a space*. Because the shape is uniform, the rules of a community — who may issue what, what combinations confer what, what expires when — can be written down declaratively, checked mechanically, and revised like software; the community becomes something you can prototype rather than only inherit. Four years of building this out (STS/USIS 2025-08 → the Bonfire community kit and platform model 2026 → the Yi substrate reviews 2026) turn the abstract claim into a working social theory with teeth: entry and exit are explicit lifecycle transitions rather than silences; absence is information, not deviance; repair is private and release-oriented rather than a leaderboard; history is never erased; everything that enters a community passes one consent door, *including the founder's own agent*; helper intelligences advise but never thereby acquire permission to act; defaults minimise configuration while gates make customisation unbounded. The same four years also produce the theory's sharpest self-criticism: possession of a token cannot express a ban, a mute, a channel-scoped delegation, or an explanation of why you were refused — so "possession is permission" is a beautiful half of a social contract, not the whole of one.

## 1. Core claims

### 1a. The primitives and the universality thesis (STS/USIS, 2025-08)

1. Social life can be modelled with exactly three irreducible primitives — Spirit (agent/identity), Token (symbolic artifact of status or value), Space (context/environment). [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
2. Every social fact reduces to one canonical sentence: "a Spirit holds a Token in a Space." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
3. The framework claims universality: "with Spirits, Tokens, and Spaces, one can model essentially any scenario where actors cooperate, compete, or exchange under rules." [conditional] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
4. The three categories are kept disjoint by construction — "a Spirit is always an actor (never a context or object), a Token is always something possessed or signified (never the possessor or environment), and a Space is always a context (never an actor or object)" — so that every social fact resolves into *who / what symbolic state / where*. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
5. A Spirit need not be a human: it may be a person, a collective treated as one actor, or an autonomous software agent with its own identity. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
6. The protocol deliberately abstracts identity: it does not dictate how a Spirit is authenticated (real-name, DID, public key, pseudonym); it requires only distinctness and continuity over time so tokens attach to the right actor. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
7. A Spirit's identity *is* the sum of tokens it holds across spaces — identity is not a substance but an accumulated portfolio of credentials, memberships and roles. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
8. Relationships are not hardcoded structures but *emerge from token state*: forming a partnership is issuing a token, ending it is revoking one. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
9. A token alone is meaningless; the Space supplies the interpretation — "A token by itself is just a symbol; the space provides the interpretation." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
10. Spaces are "boundaries of relevance": they answer "under whose rules or context is this interaction happening?" and prevent tokens from silently carrying authority into contexts that never granted it. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
11. Each Space is a micro-institution holding its own rule-set and token definitions — "a space is where the social contract is codified." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
12. The model is deliberately pluralist: spirits inhabit many spaces at once carrying different tokens in each, and each space keeps its own sovereignty while optionally interoperating with others. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
13. Tokens span the qualitative and the quantitative in one vocabulary: credentials, permissions, obligations, statuses *and* divisible fungible quantities (currency, credits, resource shares). [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
14. Transferability is a design decision per token type: some tokens are freely tradable; others are **soulbound** so that meaning survives ("one shouldn't be able to sell their diploma or reputation token"). [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
15. Tokens have lifecycles — expiry, renewal, dormancy until triggered — so that time and sequence are first-class parts of the social state rather than external bookkeeping. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`

### 1b. The symbolic grammar

16. A grammar of five operation families suffices to run a space: token definitions, issuance/revocation, composition/derived rights, evaluation/enforcement, and lifecycle/temporal conditions. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
17. Token-type declarations function as a community's schema or ontology — "only tokens of the declared types are recognized in that space" — which is what makes cross-community recognition of a credential possible at all. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
18. Revocation is the load-bearing operation for dynamism: without it roles freeze and graduated sanction is impossible. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
19. Composition produces *emergent institutions*: higher-order roles (CouncilMember = Member + ElectedDelegate) and automatic promotions (100 ReputationPoints ⇒ TrustedContributor) arise from atomic token rules that nobody hardcoded as institutions. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
20. Authority can be *split* rather than assigned: one conceptual privilege becomes several tokens held by different people, with an m-of-n rule requiring them to act together — checks and balances expressed as token arithmetic. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
21. Enforcement is by construction, not by punishment after the fact: "in a well-designed STS space, unauthorized actions are naturally impossible because the preconditions (tokens) are not met." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
22. This yields "trust-minimized governance": compliance is a property of the design rather than of the goodwill or vigilance of leaders. [conditional] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
23. The paper concedes its own boundary: norms that cannot be tokenised ("spirit must be polite") remain outside the system and still rely on social enforcement. [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
24. Evaluation is a rule engine over possession: possession logic (issued and not revoked) plus qualification logic (which token combinations enable which actions), with AND/OR/NOT and thresholds. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
25. The engine must behave as a constraint solver / truth-maintenance system: when a prerequisite token is revoked the derived composite must auto-revoke, or the state silently contradicts the rules. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
26. Two operating modes are needed — reactive (events fed in, rules fire, state updates) and proactive (external systems query "does X hold T in S?") — and real deployments use both. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
27. The token system should govern itself by its own means ("dogfooding"): an Admin token authorises `defineRule`/`issueToken`, so governance of the rules is itself tokenised and can be multi-signature. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`

### 1c. Theoretical grounding claimed by the paper

28. STS is an operationalisation of Searle's institutional-fact formula "X counts as Y in context C": a Token (X) counts as a status (Y) inside a Space (C) by collective acceptance, and the grammar is a syntax for **status function declarations**. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
29. It is a concrete implementation of symbolic interactionism (Mead, Blumer): tokens are the shared symbols through which meaning is negotiated, and by moving tokens people "negotiate the shared social reality in a codified way." [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
30. Goffman's role-performance becomes literal: "a role is literally a token a person possesses, which authorizes certain acts in a given space." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
31. Anthropologically, tokens are older than markets — clay tokens in ancient Near Eastern temples carried ritual, not merely economic, significance — so a token system is a *digital anthropology*, not merely an accounting layer. [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
32. Institutions in North's sense ("the rules of the game") become executable: "a Space's token requirements and permission rules *are* its rules of the game." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
33. Ostrom's design principles map onto token mechanics — clear boundaries = membership tokens, monitoring = a transparent ledger, graduated sanctions = revocation. [conditional] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
34. Complexity theory licenses the bottom-up bet: simple space-level rules generate unplanned macro structures (coalitions, leadership, norms), so STS is a sandbox for studying social emergence rather than a blueprint for control. [conditional] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
35. The Spirit–Token–Space triad is structurally the same as RPG/MMORPG state logic (character / inventory / zone), which licenses importing decades of game-design knowledge into institutional design — including its warnings that badly calibrated rewards get gamed. [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
36. Technically STS generalises capability-based security to human society: possession of the right token by the right spirit in the right space *is* the proof of the right to act, with no separate access-control list. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
37. It also subsumes RBAC/ABAC and extends Soulbound-Token/DeSoc thinking beyond blockchain and beyond non-transferability, and lands in the same territory as "electronic institutions" research in multi-agent systems. [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`

### 1d. Applications and the "social coding" programme

38. Membership and role management become uniform: joining is receiving a token, leaving is losing one, and community boundaries and hierarchies thereby become explicit and auditable. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
39. Governance becomes machine-checkable: single-use vote tokens prevent double-voting, quorum and super-majority conditions are guaranteed by the engine, and outcomes can auto-issue the next tokens (an "Approved" token, a "Leader" token). [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
40. Delegation tokens make liquid democracy a data structure rather than a reform proposal, and quadratic voting a token-distribution choice. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
41. Ritual and recognition are first-class, not decoration: initiation, veteran status, mentorship emblems and commemorations become durable tokens that constitute a Spirit's "social CV". [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
42. Reputation is peer-issued rather than platform-computed: spirits issue endorsement tokens to each other, and rules may gate entry on the resulting web of trust ("at least 3 endorsement tokens from existing members"), which is also the sybil-resistance mechanism. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
43. Progression paths (Novice → Intermediate → Expert) replace opaque karma with public criteria, so advancement logic is fair by inspection. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
44. Multi-party agreements become runtime-enforceable: "no action occurs unless the encoded criteria are met" — the social generalisation of multisig. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
45. Because economic and social tokens share one language, the boundary between economic and social capital blurs and a community can blend them deliberately (e.g. a local currency that also carries voting weight). [prediction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
46. Communities can therefore run safe institutional experiments: adjust the token rules the way one tweaks game mechanics, observe outcomes, iterate — "civilization prototyping". [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
47. Over time a shared **library of proven social components** — importable governance templates and token economies — should accumulate, accelerating institution-building the way open source accelerated software. [prediction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
48. Formalising social rules is explicitly *not* meant to displace values: "The **spirit** of a community – its ethos and goals – remains paramount; STS simply provides the tools to give that spirit concrete form." [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
49. The intended political effect is a status change for members: "Individuals become **participants** in explicit social contracts, rather than subjects of implicit or opaque rules." [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
50. Institutional change should become incremental and routine — "updating a rule in an STS space is akin to updating software, with version control and collective agreement" — rather than rare and convulsive. [prediction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
51. Adoption imposes a hard usability constraint the review states outright: the protocol must be "expressive enough to cover nuanced social scenarios and simple enough that communities can adopt it without needing a PhD in formal methods" — hence high-level templates over raw logic. [conditional] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
52. Not all tokens should be public; the review flags privacy (selective disclosure, zero-knowledge proofs of "I hold a valid token") as a precondition for real adoption. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`
53. Spaces should not remain silos: cross-space recognition (Space A honouring Space B's tokens via reference, oracle or bridge) is the extension that turns many micro-institutions into a society. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-USIS-review.md`

### 1e. The community kit — social theory embedded in the design (Bonfire, 2026)

54. A community operating system is buildable from a very small kit: held surfaces, lifecycle transitions, named helper spirits, and private-by-default message spaces. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
55. Community functions are personified as named helper spirits rather than menus: **Heron** (presence/roster), **Fox** (repair/mediation), **Sage** (doorway/welcome), **Owl** (ritual/cadence) — the agent is the interface to the practice. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
56. Presence should distinguish `here`, `away`, and `not_joining` "without making absence punitive" — absence is information a community needs, not a deviance to be policed. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
57. Conflict handling must be private and release-oriented, supporting repair queues "without turning conflict into a leaderboard" — visibility of conflict is itself a harm vector. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
58. Entry should be *bounded and staged*: host and arrival roles, then "graduation from temporary welcome into durable membership" — belonging is a transition with a threshold, not a database flag. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
59. Recurring practice (ritual) is expressed as lifecycle ticks, not as a separate scheduling substrate: "recurring behavior is a composition pattern over lifecycle ticks, not a new primitive." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
60. Moments become durable social facts through witness: the **Witness Book** "records moments as crystallized entries and mints witness holdings for participants while preserving the source event" — memory is minted to the people who were there, and the original is not overwritten. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
61. Desire and need get their own public surfaces — the **Wishing Pool** and the **Need Wall** — so that wanting and lacking are ordinary, addressable community state rather than private burdens. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
62. Exit is designed as carefully as entry: "Doorway, welcome tokens, graduation, deactivation, release, composting, and commemoration model entry and exit as explicit lifecycle transitions." [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
63. Endings are generative, not merely terminal: **composting** and **commemoration** are named transitions, so what a community stops doing is metabolised and remembered rather than deleted. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
64. The kit is defined as much by its refusals — the explicit anti-patterns are "public repair leaderboards, coercive presence tracking, erased history, unbounded authority, and conflating a helper spirit's suggestion with permission to act." [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
65. That last anti-pattern is an alignment claim in miniature: an assisting intelligence's *suggestion* must never be treated as *permission*; advice and authority stay separate surfaces. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
66. Erasing history is an anti-pattern — a community that can delete its record can rewrite who it was. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
67. Unbounded authority is an anti-pattern: every authority in the kit is scoped, which is the operative form of the "no whim of leaders" claim made abstractly in the 2025 paper. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`
68. The design is validated by composition, not by feature count: the twelve-person coliving example composes "arrival, wishes, needs, rituals, presence, repair, graduation, crystallization, release, compost, and commemoration" into one running community. [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`
69. Each wave of implementation was treated as a test of the substrate's sufficiency — repair queues, rituals and presence were all expressed "with ordinary holdings, sessions, and lifecycle state," adding no new primitive. [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/community.md`

### 1f. The platform model — communities as operating systems (2026-06/08)

70. There is exactly **one container primitive: the group — a spirit with members**; everything social is built from it. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
71. "A **community is a group that has been given an operating system**": its own path, a rulebook, a surface, and its own belongings. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
72. A community must own its own address (`c/gardeners`), *not* a slot under its founder — sovereignty starts with not living inside someone else's namespace. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
73. The community, not the system and not a person, owns its items — "one stamp per tree — the community is the tenancy boundary that owns role definitions, installs, and the bot roster, so 'who owns the wishing pool' is never ambiguous." [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
74. Sub-groups get no second operating system: they are plain groups inside, reusing the rulebook and rendering in the same shell — "a folder, not a second desktop" — which prevents the fractal proliferation of incompatible governance. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
75. The central usability law of the whole design: **"Defaults minimize config; gates make customization unbounded."** [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
76. Because gates are *data*, membership rules can be rewritten from the UI, from the SDK, or "by an agent someone asked in chat" — the power to change the rules is not gated behind being a programmer. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
77. Growth has a built-in constitutional path: a sub-group that outgrows its home **graduates** into its own community, using the same `graduate` transition primitive as personal membership — schism is replaced by an ordinary lifecycle event. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
78. A community's shape is a small tree of views, layouts and tabs, with "any sigil" as the escape hatch — a view nobody shipped is a miniapp someone (or an agent) codes. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
79. Presets (hearth, discord-like, reddit-like) make founding a community a single choice, and everything a preset configured stays editable piece by piece afterwards. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
80. Software becomes conversational: a non-coding member asks the community's builder-agent in chat for an app, plays it inline in the thread, and asks for changes in plain language — in-thread sigils run readonly in the sandbox "so iteration is zero-risk." [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
81. An app never gets raw write access; it invokes ports on its own kind — "its ports are its syscalls, and the port gate is the boundary." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
82. **One consent door**: everything entering a community — apps, bots, kits, preset contents — passes the same approval, moderator by default, and "the consent record always exists, *even for the founder's own agent*." [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
83. Append-only records with derived reads are described as not merely simpler but *more honest*: "you store every submission and derive, rather than trusting a client that claims only its best counts." [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
84. Distribution and authorship are the same object: "A preset is a distribution image; a marketplace listing is the same bundle, published" — a member's homegrown app is an unpublished listing with an audience of one community, and publishing later "is a flag, not a rewrite." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
85. Bundles may *propose* bots but never install them: "Daemons are added by the owner, never smuggled in." [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
86. **Agents are operators, not exceptions**: they join through the same door as humans, bear the same member label, rank the same tiers, can hold the moderator role, and act through the same ports. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
87. **Hero-as-principal**: an agent acts as itself with a human at the root of its authority chain; acting *for* a human remains a separate, explicit delegation path. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
88. The model is defined partly by what it deletes: `admin: true` booleans, in-memory kit registration with the first caller's identity baked in globally, items owned by `sys`, the flat global groups list, and Hearth as a 1,363-line bespoke page (now "the first preset"). [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
89. Some governance questions are deliberately deferred rather than fudged: community transfer/sovereignty mechanics, local sub-group roles, comment-level votes and "hot" ranking, and sandboxed user handlers ("the moment 'community OS' stops being a metaphor"). [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
90. The proposal claims credibility from restraint: "Nothing here invents substrate" — the genuinely new pieces are "deliberately few." [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`
91. Document roles are separated on purpose: "The proposal owns decisions; the notes own mechanisms." [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/platform.md`

### 1g. The Yi substrate and its reviews (2026-06/07)

92. The substrate's kernel claim: "A `Yi` is the one addressable bearer of traits, relations, capabilities, state, and behavior. Meaning lives in the `kinds[]` a Yi bears." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`
93. Meaning must live in declared markers, not in field presence — "the strongest claim is that meaning should be in `kinds[]`, not in optional field presence" — and if only one law survives, this is the one to keep. [value stance] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`
94. One row can be both subject and object at once: a single row carrying both `core:spirit` and `core:holding` covers NFT-characters, bot-as-membership-token, and delegation-actors. [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-claim-audit.md`
95. **Agency is a trait, not the default condition of every entity** — "Every Yi should be addressable. Not every Yi should be active." This is named the most important correction to a naive actor model. [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`
96. Subjecthood decomposes into orthogonal traits — `addressable`, `callable`/`messageable`, `agent`, `capability`, `container` — so that "can hold authority" is separated from "can initiate action". [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`; `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-claim-audit.md`
97. Ren's motivating intuition is recorded and endorsed: "items can carry capabilities, capabilities can grant permissions, agents can be held by things, and a thing can be both object and subject depending on role." [observation] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-adversarial-review-2026-06-19.md`
98. Unification is of *meaning*, not of storage: "everything is one kind of meaningful thing, while storage, indexes, fan-out, presence, messages, feeds, and moderation specialize where scale demands." [mechanism] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/stream-tier-conceptualization-2026-06-21.md`
99. One interface, plural representation: authors declare intent, and the choice of marker vs edge vs stream vs collection is pushed below the seam. [design direction] — `09-sts-bonfire-yi/bonfire-proposals/design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`

## 2. Mechanism

**The core loop.** STS proposes that everything social be represented as one relation, `holds(spirit, token, space)`, and that all institutional behaviour be derived from rules over that relation. A **Space** declares which **Token** types exist, who may issue and revoke them, which combinations confer which capacities, and how they age. A **Spirit** accumulates tokens; its identity in that space just *is* the set it holds. When a spirit attempts an action, the space evaluates its rule-set against the spirit's holdings and either permits or refuses. This is Searle's "X counts as Y in C" made executable: the token is X, the status is Y, the space is C, and the declaration is a piece of data anyone in the space can read.

Two consequences follow immediately and do most of the theoretical work.

*First, enforcement moves upstream.* Because permission is a precondition rather than a punishment, "unauthorized actions are naturally impossible, because the preconditions (tokens) are not met" (`2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`). Governance stops depending on the vigilance of leaders and becomes a property of the arrangement — what the paper calls trust-minimised governance. This is the same move capability-based security makes in operating systems, transplanted into social life: possession is the proof of right, so no separate list of who is allowed what needs to be maintained, consulted, or trusted.

*Second, institutions become compositional and therefore emergent.* A rule that a `CouncilMember` token requires both `Member` and `ElectedDelegate` builds a hierarchy without anyone writing "hierarchy". A rule that 100 reputation points auto-issue a `TrustedContributor` token builds a promotion ladder without a promotion committee. A 2-of-3 approval requirement builds a check-and-balance without a constitution clause. The complexity-theory framing is explicit: simple space-level rules generate macro structures (coalitions, leadership, norms) that nobody programmed, so a space is a sandbox for social emergence, not a control panel.

**Where the 2026 work adds the missing half.** The 2025 papers describe a static algebra of permissions. The Bonfire proposals describe what a community actually *does over time*, and that turns out to require a lifecycle vocabulary the algebra never supplied: `doorway → welcome token → graduation → (deactivation) → release → composting → commemoration`. Each is a named transition with its own social meaning. A doorway means entry is bounded and hosted. Graduation means belonging is earned and marked. Release means leaving is an act with a form rather than a silence. Composting means what a community stops doing is metabolised into soil rather than deleted. Commemoration means the record of what happened survives the thing that happened. These are not features; they are a theory of how groups stay alive — and they are the point at which the Bonfire kit stops being a permission system and becomes a social practice.

**The four helper spirits are an interface theory.** Rather than exposing configuration surfaces, the kit personifies each function: Heron holds presence, Fox holds repair, Sage holds the door, Owl holds cadence. Each carries an explicit ethical constraint in its own description — Heron distinguishes `here`/`away`/`not_joining` "without making absence punitive"; Fox runs private repair queues "without turning conflict into a leaderboard"; Sage graduates people "from temporary welcome into durable membership". The anti-pattern list closes the loop by naming what these spirits must never become: public repair leaderboards, coercive presence tracking, erased history, unbounded authority, and — the crucial one — "conflating a helper spirit's suggestion with permission to act." An assisting intelligence may propose; it does not thereby acquire authority. That single line is the community-scale version of the alignment stance that runs through Ren's corpus.

**The platform model generalises the kit into a constitutional design.** One primitive (a group is a spirit with members) plus one stamp (a community is a group with an operating system) plus one door (everything entering passes consent) plus one escape hatch (any gate expression, any sigil). The load-bearing sentence is "Defaults minimize config; gates make customization unbounded": a community should be usable with zero decisions and re-specifiable without limit, and the way to have both is to make the rules *data* rather than code — so that the same edit can be made from the UI, the SDK, or by an agent someone asked in chat. Sovereignty is addressed structurally: the community owns its own path rather than living under its founder, it owns its own items, and a sub-group that outgrows its home *graduates* into its own community using the same primitive that graduates a person into membership. Growth therefore has a constitutional path instead of a schism.

**The consent door is the theory's political core.** Every app, bot, kit and preset enters through one approval, moderator by default; the founder may loosen the policy but "the consent record always exists, *even for the founder's own agent*." The founder is inside the rules, not above them, and the record is kept regardless of the answer. Bots may be *proposed* by bundles but installed only on consent: "Daemons are added by the owner, never smuggled in." Agents join through the same door as humans, hold the same labels and tiers, and can hold the moderator role — with hero-as-principal keeping a human at the root of every authority chain, and delegation kept explicit rather than implied.

**How the counterexamples are answered.** The corpus supplies its own adversary, and the adversary wins several rounds.

- *Possession cannot express prohibition.* The 2025 model gates on what you hold; bans, mutes, blocks, timeouts and deny-overrides-allow are things done *to* you. The adversarial review lists negative rights and resource-scoped delegation among the things the design "cannot yet do elegantly", and prescribes a first-class policy layer with scope tuples, positive and negative rights, precedence, explain, audit, revocation, and cache invalidation. The answer is not to abandon possession but to stop pretending gates over verbs are a governance model: "`Tie` can represent the underlying facts, but cannot be the whole product model."
- *Enforcement without explanation is not legitimacy.* "Why can/cannot caller invoke this port?" is elevated to a design requirement. A system whose refusals are inexplicable has replaced the arbitrary leader with an arbitrary machine.
- *Not everything should be one thing.* The strongest internal critique is the anti-pattern of **metaphysical compression** — declaring every concept "just a tie" produces elegance and loses operational handles, because membership vs role vs ban, holding vs containment vs delegation, feed membership vs read permission, presence vs durable state are real distinctions people need. The theory's answer is a seam: unify the *authoring surface*, specialise *underneath*, and never expose the specialisation as unrelated concepts.
- *Some state must not be permanent.* Presence, typing, voice, zone and roster state are declared explicitly volatile — TTL-bounded, eventually consistent, outside durable audit truth. Against a framework whose instinct is that every social fact should be a durable holding, this is a real concession: some social facts are supposed to evaporate, and privacy depends on it.
- *Elegance must survive scale.* The stream-tier analysis shows the discipline in practice. Messages are not Yis; they live in a purpose-built store because they carry three axes a generic relation cannot cheaply carry (a dense per-session sequence, a reply DAG, sheer volume). The two "purer" alternatives are examined and rejected — event-canonical loses prunability and rebuilds the same table anyway; message-as-Yi pays the full row shape's cost across billions of rows. The exception is then *named in the open* rather than hidden, and a proposal to demote it to an appendix is rejected on the ground that it would soften the law. The one thing that does change is the name, because `yi_message` "asserts the table is part of the Yi family … when the concept is defined by being the one canonical thing that is explicitly NOT a Yi. The prefix is the lie."

**The self-correcting apparatus is itself part of the theory.** Three review artifacts do nothing but sort claims into *current fact / violation / destination / future mechanism*, and the same audit repeatedly downgrades favourite formulations: the relation compiler is "destination-only"; "every representation is rebuildable" is "too broad as current fact"; "Phase 1 is pure deletion" is "overstated"; collective capability does not exist. A design language that requires every sentence to carry its epistemic status is the engineering counterpart of the tagging discipline this synthesis itself uses — and it is the reason this cluster can be trusted where the 2025 papers, written in the register of a research prospectus, cannot be.

## 4. Genealogy

**Pre-history (inputs visible only as citations).** The STS paper and the USIS review both cite two earlier artifacts by name in their inline references: `usis.md` and `USIS_ Enhanced Economic and Implementation_.pdf`. Neither is in this cluster. The citation pattern shows the direction of derivation: `usis.md` supplied the conceptual body (primitives, grammar, applications, theoretical parallels) and the "Enhanced Economic and Implementation" PDF supplied the token-economics refinements (quantitative value, fungibility, divisibility) that appear as the paper's quantitative-token passages. So the sequence is: **usis.md → USIS economic/implementation deep-research → USIS review → STS paper**.

**2025-08 — `2025-08-USIS-review.md`, the assessment.** Written as a three-part appraisal of the framework then called **USIS (Universal Symbolic Interaction System)**, with primitives in the order **Token–Spirit–Space**. Part 1 asks whether the primitives are complete and coherent, and answers that they are "broadly sufficient", grounding the claim in capability-based security, RBAC, symbolic interactionism, and the DeSoc/Soulbound-Token literature. Part 2 asks about social utility and enumerates applications. Part 3 is a protocol design sketch: Spirit IDs (public key / DID / UUID), Space IDs, token records with issuer/holder/expiry/revoked/attributes, rule definitions in a DSL, a reactive and a proactive evaluation mode, standard interfaces (`issueToken`, `transferToken`, `revokeToken`, `hasToken`, `getTokens`, `getHolders`, `defineTokenType`, `defineRule`), event hooks, security (unforgeability, signatures), and four extensions (cross-space interaction, privacy/ZK, scaling/sharding, wallet-like client UX). It closes with the adoption constraint — expressive enough for nuance, simple enough to adopt "without needing a PhD in formal methods" — and with the analogy that carries the whole ambition: USIS aims "to do for social-organizational interactions what the internet did for information."

**2025-08 — `2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`, the paper.** The framework is renamed **STS (Spirit–Token–Space)** and reordered to put the agent first. Almost every substantive claim of the review reappears, but three things are *added*: (a) the disjointness discipline — spirit never a context, token never a possessor, space never an actor; (b) the Searle "X counts as Y in C" mapping and the framing of the grammar as **status function declarations**; (c) the "Toward a New Social Paradigm" section, which converts the protocol into a research programme — societies as designable systems, a library of proven social components, institutional change as versioned patches, and the explicit reassurance that formalisation does not displace values ("The **spirit** of a community – its ethos and goals – remains paramount"). What the paper *drops* relative to the review is the implementation layer: data structures, interfaces, security model, ZK privacy, sharding, and the wallet UX all disappear.

The file as stored is a **concatenation of two passes of the same paper**: lines 1–75 carry heavy inline `file://` citations back to `usis.md` and the PDF (a deep-research output), and at line 75 the title restarts and a cleaned pass runs to the end with the citations stripped. The second pass is the better text; the first is the provenance record. Both should be kept for exactly the reason Ren has given elsewhere about lossy consolidation.

**2026-06-12 — the platform design conversation.** Referenced in `bonfire-proposals/platform.md` as the conversation after which revision 2 was written; the document's status line reads "**owner-approved direction** (revision 2, after design conversation 2026-06-12)". This is the point at which STS's abstractions become a product constitution: Space → community-with-an-OS-stamp; Token → holding/gate; Spirit → group ("a spirit with members"). The decision register (nine numbered decisions plus a deferred set) is the most decision-dense artifact in the cluster.

**2026-06-19 — the frame/pivot reviews.** Two artifacts on the same working tree: `design/frame-pivot-claim-audit-2026-06-19.md` (a claim-by-claim table with file:line evidence, verdicts Verified / Partly true / False / Destination-only) and `design/frame-pivot-adversarial-review-2026-06-19.md` (five adversarial subagents; verdict "**Not final as written**"). The audit's most consequential corrections: "one table" is already true, so Phase 0 aims at the wrong hard problem; `rt.yi.where` is *not* ungated; the relation compiler and collective capability are destination-only; "every representation rebuildable" is too strong under retention. **2026-06-21 — `design/stream-tier-conceptualization-2026-06-21.md`.** Triggered by a founder question and, unusually, by a founder *feeling*: "Somehow I found it a bit hard to conceptualize." The doc treats that sentence as the most important signal — "a frame-smell to diagnose, not a reader's gap to apologize for" — diagnoses two inverted premises (the event is the shadow of the row, not its source; there is exactly one content store with two read-views), enumerates the three axes that justify the exception, compares the three possible architectures, and proposes a cure. A five-lens review the same day then *rejects the cure*: the "one trait whose backing state escapes the row" mnemonic is false (`membered`, `container`, `historied` also escape it), and demoting the named tier would soften Law 1. What shipped was the `yi_message → stream` rename plus a seam reconciliation. The document preserves the retired proposal in place, banner-marked, rather than deleting it — a working example of Ren's stated objection to lossy updates.

**2026-07-08 — `design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md` and `design/frame-pivot-claim-audit.md`.** The consolidated review pair. The evaluation states the best kernel of the whole substrate in four sentences ("A `Yi` is the one addressable bearer … Agency is a trait, not the default condition of every Yi … Storage remains free to optimize below the conceptual seam"), separates what to keep (meaning in the open; agency as trait; one interface, plural representation; break forward atomically) from what to correct (Tie overloaded; compiler treated as fact; rebuildability absolute; Phase 1 not pure deletion; Kind shrink needs inventory; the agency law slightly too strong), and lays out a four-phase route with explicit **stop criteria** and **continue criteria**. The companion audit adds the six high-confidence corrections, including the trait split (`agent` / `messageable` / `capability` / `container`) and acceptance tests.

**2026-08-15 — the archive pass.** `bonfire-proposals/community.md` is reduced to "Status: archived proposal", surviving as a map of section numbers (5.2 Heron, 5.4 Fox, 5.5 Sage, 5.6 Owl, 8 Surfaces, 9.1.1 Witness Book, 10 Entry And Exit, 11 Anti-Patterns, 12.1 Small Coliving, 14.0.x Wave Lessons) pointing at runnable examples — "The examples are the executable form of that proposal." `the-pivot.md` is reduced to a forwarding stub to `docs/yi/archive/02-pivot.md`. `platform.md` gains a "Historical pre-address-reset proposal" banner: the social model stays useful, the path spellings do not.

**Which formulation is best.** Three different answers for three different purposes:
- *For the symbolic framework*: the STS paper's "STS Model" and "Symbolic Grammar and Social Logic" sections (2025-08, second pass, lines 108–142) — the cleanest statement of the primitives, the disjointness discipline, and the five grammar families.
- *For the social theory*: `bonfire-proposals/community.md` §§10–11 plus `bonfire-proposals/platform.md`'s "idea in one paragraph" and decision register (2026) — the lifecycle vocabulary and the consent door are where the theory becomes falsifiable by practice rather than by argument.
- *For epistemic honesty about the substrate*: `design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`'s "best kernel" paragraph plus its stop/continue criteria (2026-07-08) — the only formulation in the cluster that states the thesis and its unproven parts in the same breath.

## 5. What earlier versions had that later ones lost

1. **The theoretical apparatus.** Searle, Mead/Blumer, Goffman, North, Ostrom, complexity theory, ritual anthropology, game design — the 2025 papers ground every primitive in a literature. The 2026 Bonfire documents contain none of it. A reader of the 2026 corpus alone would not know that "a role is literally a token a person possesses" is Goffman, or that the consent door is Ostrom's boundary principle in another key.
2. **The word "Space" as a primitive.** By 2026 the three-word triad is gone: spirit survives (`core:spirit`, "a group is a spirit with members"), token becomes "holding", and space fragments into session, community, path, and tenancy boundary. The idea that meaning is always context-relative survives; the *name* that made it memorable does not.
3. **Quantitative and economic tokens.** The 2025 paper devotes a whole application section to symbolic economies — fungible community currency, divisible quantities, contribution credits, grants, blending economic weight with voting weight, communities tuning their own incentive designs. The 2026 community kit has wishes, needs, witness, repair, ritual and presence — and no currency at all. The economic half of STS is simply absent from the community proposals in this cluster.
4. **Quadratic voting, delegation/liquid democracy, and formal voting mechanics.** The platform model's v1 votes are "up-only, post-level, new/top sorts". The 2025 vote-token machinery (single-use tokens, burning, quorum enforcement, delegation chains, automatic outcome issuance) does not reappear.
5. **Cross-space interoperability.** The review's cross-space recognition (Space A honouring Space B's tokens, industry-standard credential types, a directory of spaces, bridges/oracles) is the mechanism that would turn many communities into a society. In 2026 the corresponding item — "community transfer / sovereignty mechanics" — is on the *deferred* list.
6. **Privacy by selective disclosure.** The review proposed zero-knowledge proofs of possession ("I have a valid token granting access" without revealing which), and encrypted token contents for sensitive spaces. 2026 has private-by-default message spaces and visibility filtering, which is a different and weaker thing.
7. **The wallet UX / "what can I do in this space?" surface.** The review's client layer — a person seeing all their tokens across all spaces, and the system explaining their capacities — anticipates exactly the "policy explainability" gap the 2026 adversarial review reopens as unsolved. The 2025 version had it as a design item; the 2026 version has it as a hole.
8. **The research-programme framing.** "Civilization prototyping", the interdisciplinary field of social system design, and the "library of proven social components" become, in 2026, presets and a marketplace. The commercial form survives; the scholarly ambition does not appear in these files.
9. **The named risk of badly calibrated incentives.** The game-design warning that rewards too easy or too hard get gamed, and that token economies need tuning, has no successor in the 2026 anti-pattern list (which targets leaderboards, coercive presence, erasure, unbounded authority, and advice-as-permission instead).
10. **The full Community Kit text.** `bonfire-proposals/community.md` is now a section-number skeleton. Sections 1–4, 6, 7, 9.x beyond 9.1.1, 12.2+, 13, and 14.0.1–14.0.3 exist only as absences implied by the surviving numbering. Whatever those sections argued is not in this cluster.
11. **The retired stream-tier cure** — preserved, but only because the document explicitly banner-marks it rather than deleting it. It is worth noting as the cluster's one example of the right archival behaviour, against which the losses above stand out.

## 6. Relations to other theories in this corpus

- **context-as-existence-runtimes** — deepest overlap: STS's "a token by itself is just a symbol; the space provides the interpretation" is the same thesis as "context is existence", applied to social rather than cognitive state; the Yi substrate is the shared implementation ground.
- **jian-and-wenyan-as-formal-language** — the symbolic grammar (definitions, issuance, composition, evaluation, temporality) is a formal language for social facts, the sibling project to 文言 as kernel; both bet that the right minimal notation makes a domain computable.
- **relational-self-and-datong** — "a Spirit's identity is the sum of tokens it holds across various Spaces" is the relational self stated as a data model; the community kit (witness, needs, repair, commemoration) is 大同-adjacent practice at village scale.
- **wamotopia-and-the-wamo-protocol** — the direct successor problem: STS's symbolic economies (contribution credits, community currency, blended economic/political weight) are the machinery Wamo needs; the 2026 kit's silence on currency is a gap Wamotopia fills.
- **human-solutions-museum** — the "library of proven social components" and the preset/marketplace equivalence are the museum's operational form: solutions as importable, reusable, forkable artifacts.
- **keep-the-possibility-space-open** — supports and is supported: many sovereign spaces, "defaults minimize config; gates make customization unbounded", graduation instead of schism, and the anti-pattern of metaphysical compression are all commitments against collapsing plurality into one root.
- **the-fork-and-cooperative-infrastructure** — the consent door, community-owned paths, community-owned items, "daemons are added by the owner, never smuggled in", and append-only records with derived reads are the automated-commons side of the fork made concrete.
- **becoming** — the exit lifecycle (release → composting → commemoration) is 生生不息 applied to institutions: endings are metabolised into the conditions for the next thing rather than treated as failure.
- **selfishness-spiral-and-narrative-value-frames** / **meta-attractor** — the kit's refusals are countermeasures to winner-selection dynamics: no repair leaderboards, no coercive presence, non-punitive absence, positive-sum design; the 2025 game-design warning about gamed reward systems is the same worry in economic dress.
- **alignment-selection-on-cognition** / **zhishan-intelligence-must-be-good** — "conflating a helper spirit's suggestion with permission to act" is an anti-pattern, agents are operators bound by the same doors, and hero-as-principal keeps a human at the root of every authority chain: this is alignment expressed as social architecture rather than as model training.
- **drives-narrative-attractors-and-mind** — weaker link: the personified helper spirits (Heron, Fox, Sage, Owl) treat community functions as characters with dispositions, a design bet that people relate to named agents rather than configuration surfaces.
- **memories-and-working-style** — methodological kinship: the review artifacts' insistence on labelling every claim current fact / violation / destination / future mechanism, and on preserving retired proposals in place, matches Ren's stated objection to lossy consolidation.

## 8. Critiques, tensions, open questions

### Raised inside the corpus

1. **Phase 0 aims at the wrong hard problem.** "One table" is already true in Postgres and in memory; the remaining split is the face-shaped writer/index/lifecycle contract. Calling it "merge two repos" "will cause agents to delete useful abstractions instead of solving the actual coupling." (`design/frame-pivot-adversarial-review-2026-06-19.md`)
2. **The relation compiler is destination-only** and must not be constitutional: "It is currently a destination idea, not an engineering primitive with algebra, cost model, indexes, rebuild, and tests." Membership should be the single first proof. (adversarial review; `design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`)
3. **"Every representation is rebuildable" is too strong** under event retention; history is best-effort truncated, so rebuilds must run from canonical rows, not assumed complete event history. (both audits)
4. **Product governance is under-specified**: resource-scoped delegation, negative rights, role precedence, explainability, audit trails, moderation sanctions, rate/abuse signals, revocation and cache invalidation are all missing from a model that gates on possession. (adversarial review)
5. **Fanout is broadcast-filtered, not interest-indexed** — one delivery chain, per-connection visibility checks, backlog growing without bound if append-rate × fanout cost exceeds 1. Recipient sets, feeds and subscription indexes must become explicit outputs. (adversarial review)
6. **Hot authorization paths still scan.** `caller-holds` falls back to iterating all holdings, and does not check full cascade liveness — so a burned upstream binding can be mishandled. Enforcement-by-construction has a performance and a correctness hole underneath it. (both 2026-06-19 audits)
7. **Tie/Moment is overloaded as a taught primitive**; folding traits, messages, port firings, audit events and standing relations into one word "risks replacing the old zoo with a more abstract one." (`design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`; `design/frame-pivot-claim-audit.md`)
8. **The agency law is slightly too strong**: initiating should require agency, but holding a capability should not, and receiving ports may need only `messageable`. (`design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`)
9. **"Phase 1 is pure deletion" is overstated** — it is cross-package migration with tests, and `Kind` cannot shrink before every stored field has a classified replacement home. (both)
10. **The object-capability claim is only partly true** while invocation is addressable-by-uid-then-gated. (adversarial review)
11. **Presence may belong outside durable Yi semantics entirely** — listed as an open decision: "Decide whether presence is explicitly outside durable Yi semantics." (`design/frame-pivot-claim-audit-2026-06-19.md`)
12. **A live self-contradiction in the North Star**: a reply is described as both a relation and "not a tie at all" four lines apart. Resolved by ruling that a reply is a relation by reading-lens and stream-internal by storage. (`design/stream-tier-conceptualization-2026-06-21.md`)
13. **Names can be lies.** `yi_message` asserted yi-ness against a tier defined by not being a Yi; the fix was a cross-cutting code rename, not a doc edit. (same)
14. **The adoption constraint may be unsatisfiable as stated**: the system must be expressive enough for nuance and simple enough to adopt "without needing a PhD in formal methods" — the review's own answer, high-level templates, is exactly the presets/kits answer arrived at again a year later. (`2025-08-USIS-review.md`; `bonfire-proposals/platform.md`)
15. **The paper concedes untokenisable norms** ("spirit must be polite") without saying what carries them — a boundary acknowledged but not theorised. (`2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`)

### New (my reading)

16. **Possession cannot express prohibition — and this is a theoretical fault, not an implementation gap.** STS's founding move is that a social fact is something a spirit *holds*. Bans, mutes, blocks and timeouts are social facts that are precisely *not* held by their subject. The 2026 review notices the symptom (negative rights are hard) but does not draw the conclusion: the canonical sentence "a Spirit holds a Token in a Space" is incomplete, and a second relation — something like a space *imposing* a state on a spirit — may be irreducible.
17. **"Unauthorized actions are naturally impossible" is politically double-edged.** The same property that removes the whim of leaders removes the possibility of mercy, exception, civil disobedience, and the discretionary judgement every functioning institution relies on. The 2025 paper presents impossibility-by-construction as pure gain; nothing in the corpus asks what a society loses when its rules cannot be bent. Ren's own anti-totalisation commitments elsewhere make this the sharpest unexamined tension in the cluster.
18. **Transparency is treated as unambiguously good.** "Everyone knows who is a member by seeing who holds the token"; "status is transparent (anyone can see who holds membership tokens)". The 2026 kit contradicts this instinct in practice — private repair queues, non-punitive absence, private-by-default message spaces, caller-sensitive visibility — but no document reconciles the two. The community kit is quietly a *critique* of the paper's transparency assumption, and nobody has said so.
19. **The economic half went missing and nobody recorded the decision.** No document in this cluster explains why the community kit has no currency, credits, or contribution accounting when the 2025 paper devoted an application section to them. Deliberate scoping or drift?
20. **Emergence is claimed but never demonstrated.** The complexity-theory argument (simple rules → unplanned institutions) is central to the paper's ambition and is supported by no observation anywhere in the cluster. The coliving worked example composes designed pieces; it does not report anything emerging that was not designed.
21. **"Agents are operators, not exceptions" needs a stress test.** Equal membership for agents plus "an agent someone asked in chat" being able to swap a sub-group's join gate means the rate at which a community's constitution can change is now bounded by conversation speed, not deliberation speed. The consent door covers *installs*; it is not stated to cover *gate edits*.
22. **The named-spirit set is asserted closed.** "Presence closed the named-spirit set" — but no argument is given for why four functions (presence, repair, doorway, ritual) are sufficient. Provision, decision-making, and resource allocation have no spirit.
23. **The archive pass lost more than it preserved.** Reducing the Community Kit proposal to section headings pointing at code means the *reasoning* for each surface now exists only as executable examples. Code is not an argument; a later reader can see what the Witness Book does and not why it mints witness holdings to participants.
24. **Consent is per-install, not per-use.** The door records that a bot entered; nothing in the platform model describes ongoing consent, review, or the community's ability to see what an installed agent has been doing. Compare the corpus's own emphasis on audit and explainability.
25. **Two ontologies now coexist unreconciled.** The 2025 papers say there are three kinds of thing (spirit, token, space). The 2026 substrate says there is one kind of thing (Yi) bearing traits. These are not the same theory, and no document in this cluster performs the translation — which matters, because the Yi collapse is exactly what the adversarial review warns against under "metaphysical compression".

## 9. Publication readiness

**Publishable or teachable now.**

- `2025-08-STS-spirit-token-space-paper.md`, second pass (lines ~108–176): a complete, self-contained framework paper — abstract, foundations, model, grammar, applications, paradigm section, conclusion. It reads as a submittable interdisciplinary paper today, subject to two fixes: strip the duplicated first pass, and replace the inline `file://` citations with real bibliography. Its weakness is that it argues from resonance rather than from evidence — everything is "aligns with", "parallels", "resonates".
- `2025-08-USIS-review.md` §3 (Implementation Considerations): the most complete protocol sketch in the cluster and directly usable as a spec appendix or an RFC skeleton.
- `bonfire-proposals/platform.md`: publishable nearly as-is as an essay titled something like *Communities as operating systems* — it has a one-paragraph thesis, nine numbered decisions, an explicit deferred list, a "what this deletes" section, and a credibility argument. Only the path spellings are stale, and the document already says so.
- `bonfire-proposals/community.md` §§10–11 (entry/exit lifecycle and anti-patterns): four lines that teach more social design than most books — but they need the deleted body text restored to be more than aphorisms.
- `design/frame-pivot-evaluation.md`: teachable as a model of technical review — best kernel, what to keep, what to correct, phased route, and explicit stop/continue criteria. Its "Final Judgment" pattern (will this produce something elegant? *Yes, if…; No, if…*) is worth generalising as a house method.

**Missing.**

- The bridge document between the two ontologies (Spirit–Token–Space, 2025 → Yi + traits, 2026). Without it the corpus contains two incompatible foundations for the same project.
- Any empirical report. The coliving example is a composition, not a case study. There is no account of a real community running the kit, what broke, what people did that the design did not anticipate.
- The restored Community Kit reasoning. The surviving skeleton names surfaces without justifying them.
- A treatment of negative facts (ban, mute, block, exclusion) inside the possession model — currently the largest hole in the theory as theory.
- The economics: contribution accounting, mutual credit, or whatever replaced the 2025 currency material.

**The one piece of writing that would most advance this.**

A short paper — *Social facts as holdings, and the four things holding cannot say* — that (a) states the STS canonical sentence and the grammar in its 2025 form, (b) reports the 2026 lifecycle vocabulary (doorway, graduation, release, composting, commemoration) and the anti-pattern list as the empirical correction to it, (c) names the four limits the corpus has already discovered — prohibition, resource scope, explainability, and volatility (presence) — and (d) proposes the minimal second relation that fixes them. This would convert the strongest asset of the cluster (a design that criticised itself in public, with dates) into the thing the 2025 paper only claimed to be: a social theory that survives contact with a running community. Everything needed for it is already in these files.
