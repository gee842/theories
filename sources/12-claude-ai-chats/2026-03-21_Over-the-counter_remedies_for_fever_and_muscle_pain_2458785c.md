The user wants to help their friend who has a fever and muscle pain. They want to go to the pharmacy to get over-the-counter remedies. I can provide helpful advice about common OTC medications for these symptoms.Here's what you can pick up at the pharmacy to help your friend feel better:

**For fever and muscle pain**, the two main over-the-counter options are:

**Acetaminophen (Tylenol/paracetamol)** — Great for reducing fever and relieving pain. It's gentle on the stomach. Follow the dosage on the package and don't exceed the daily maximum (usually 3,000–4,000 mg/day for adults).

**Ibuprofen (Advil/Motrin)** — Also reduces fever and pain, plus it has anti-inflammatory properties that can help with muscle aches. Should be taken with food to avoid stomach irritation.

**Other helpful things to grab:**

- **Electrolyte drinks or oral rehydration salts** — Fever causes dehydration, so keeping fluids up is really important.
- **A thermometer** if you don't have one, so you can monitor the fever.
- **Throat lozenges or honey-lemon tea** if there's any sore throat involved.

**A few things to keep in mind:**

- Don't combine acetaminophen and ibuprofen without understanding the timing — they can be alternated (e.g., every 3–4 hours, switching between them), but your pharmacist can advise on this.
- If your friend has any allergies, liver/kidney issues, or is on other medications, mention that to the pharmacist so they can recommend the safest option.
- If the fever is very high (above 39.4°C / 103°F), persists more than 3 days, or is accompanied by stiff neck, confusion, difficulty breathing, or a rash, that warrants seeing a doctor rather than just managing at home.

The pharmacist at the store can also help you choose — don't hesitate to ask them directly!

- **Peppermint or lavender essential oil** — A drop on the pillow or diluted on the temples can feel refreshing and calming. Peppermint especially gives a cooling sensation.
- **Aloe vera gel** — Can feel soothing and cool when applied to the skin.
- **A cold compress or reusable ice pack** — Wrapped in a cloth and placed on the forehead or back of the neck, it's simple but really effective for comfort.

**Other small gestures that help psychologically:**

- **Her favorite drink or snack** — Even something small like a particular juice or fruit shows you care and can lift her spirits.
- **A light, breathable blanket or fresh change of clothes** — Feeling clean and comfortable goes a long way.
- **Something to keep her entertained** — A magazine, a playlist, or just your company so she doesn't feel alone.

Sometimes just having someone show up with a bag of supplies and some care is the best medicine for morale. Your friend is lucky to have you looking out for them!
