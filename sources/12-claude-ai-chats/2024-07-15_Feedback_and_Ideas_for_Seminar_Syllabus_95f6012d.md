**ASSISTANT** (2024-07-15T19:06):

 A manifesto is a public declaration of principles, beliefs, intentions, or motives, typically issued by a group, movement, or individual. The style of a manifesto generally has the following characteristics:

1. Assertive and declarative: Manifestos use strong, confident language to state beliefs and intentions.

2. Concise and direct: Ideas are often presented in short, punchy sentences or bullet points.

3. Passionate and emotive: The language is usually charged with emotion and conviction.

4. Call to action: Manifestos often urge readers to take specific actions or adopt certain viewpoints.

5. Future-oriented: They typically focus on changes the authors want to see in the world.

6. Ideological: Manifestos usually express a clear ideological position or worldview.

7. Rhetorical devices: They may use repetition, parallel structure, and other rhetorical techniques for emphasis.

8. Provocative: Manifestos often aim to challenge existing norms and spark debate.

9. Collective voice: Even if written by an individual, manifestos often use "we" to represent a group or movement.

10. Structured: They're often organized into sections or numbered points for clarity.

Famous examples like "The Communist Manifesto" by Marx and Engels, or the "Futurist Manifesto" by Marinetti, demonstrate these stylistic elements. The exact style can vary depending on the context, purpose, and author of the manifesto.
