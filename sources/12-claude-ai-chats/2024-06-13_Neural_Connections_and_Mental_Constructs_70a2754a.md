 You raise an interesting point about the relationship between neural connections, mental constructs, and intelligence. The way our brain's neurons are wired and interact with each other does seem to be the physiological basis for our mental representations and cognitive abilities. A few key points on this:

Mental constructs can be thought of as patterns or schemas that our brains use to process, organize and make sense of information. They are the building blocks of our mental models - the frameworks we use to reason, make judgments and predictions, and navigate the world. 

At the neural level, mental constructs likely arise from the strengthening of certain synaptic connections between neurons that fire together in response to particular stimuli or when engaged in certain cognitive tasks. As these connections are reinforced through repeated activation, they form stable patterns of interaction.

Different mental constructs are distributed across various regions of the brain that are functionally specialized for processing certain types of information (e.g. the visual cortex for visual mental constructs). However, intelligence seems to emerge from the coordinated activity and integration of mental constructs across brain regions.

Our mental models, as coherent networks of mental constructs, enable both automatic/intuitive and deliberate/rational thought. They fill in gaps, make inferences, and generate expectations and judgments almost instantaneously. But they can also be mentally simulated and manipulated, allowing us to reason through problems and think abstractly.

So in summary, I agree that the structure and dynamics of neural connections give rise to mental constructs and models that are the basis of intelligence. Of course, there is still a lot of uncertainty around the specifics of how this works. Mapping the exact neural correlates of different mental constructs and models is an active area of research in cognitive neuroscience.
