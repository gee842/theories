Here are some of the core concepts and recurring terms that sit at the center of CCP messaging:

**Legitimacy and national narrative:** The concept of 中华民族伟大复兴 ("great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation") is arguably the master narrative of Xi-era propaganda. It frames modern China as recovering from a "century of humiliation" (百年耻辱) at the hands of foreign powers, positioning the Party as the vehicle for restoring China's rightful place.

**Core recurring terms and frames:**

The Party's messaging gravitates around several clusters. *Stability and harmony* (稳定, 和谐) — the idea that social order is fragile and the Party is its guarantor, often invoked to justify restrictions on dissent. *Development* (发展) — economic growth as proof of the system's superiority. *Socialism with Chinese characteristics* (中国特色社会主义) — a flexible ideological container that allows the Party to claim fidelity to Marxism while pursuing market economics.

Under Xi specifically, several phrases have become central: 中国梦 ("Chinese Dream"), 人类命运共同体 ("community of shared future for mankind," used in foreign-facing messaging), and 新时代 ("new era").

**Structural ideas that do the heavy lifting:**

One is the **Party-nation conflation** — propaganda consistently blurs the line between loving China and supporting the CCP, making criticism of the Party equivalent to betraying the nation. Another is **whataboutism and comparative framing** — deflecting criticism by pointing to Western failures (racism, inequality, Iraq). A third is **developmental authoritarianism** — the argument that democracy is inefficient and that China's system "gets things done."

**Enemy construction** also plays a key role: "hostile foreign forces" (境外敌对势力) is a recurring frame used to delegitimize domestic dissent by linking it to foreign interference.

**What's distinctive** about Chinese propaganda compared to, say, Soviet-era messaging is its pragmatic flexibility. It's less rigidly ideological and more focused on performance legitimacy (we deliver results) and nationalist sentiment than on convincing people of Marxist theory. The propaganda system also increasingly operates through entertainment, social media influencers, and commercial platforms rather than just official channels — what scholars sometimes call "sharp power."

Worth noting that propaganda (宣传) doesn't carry the same purely negative connotation in Chinese political vocabulary as it does in English — it's an official, named function of the Party apparatus.
