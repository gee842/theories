 Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela were Chilean biologists and philosophers who developed the concept of autopoiesis and its relation to cognition. Autopoiesis refers to the self-maintaining and self-reproducing properties of living systems. The key points in the given statement are:

1. Structurally coupled autopoiesis: This refers to the idea that living systems are not only self-maintaining and self-reproducing (autopoietic) but also structurally coupled with their environment. Structural coupling means that the living system and its environment interact and influence each other's structure, leading to mutual adaptation over time.

2. Necessity for cognition: Maturana and Varela argued that this structurally coupled autopoiesis is a prerequisite for cognition to occur. In other words, they believed that cognition is not merely a process of information processing or computation but is deeply rooted in the living system's autopoietic organization and its structural coupling with the environment.

According to their theory, cognition emerges from the dynamic interactions between the living system and its environment, rather than being a pre-given or purely internal process. The living system's structure determines how it interacts with and makes sense of its environment, while the environment, in turn, triggers changes in the system's structure.

This perspective on cognition differs from traditional cognitive science, which often treats cognition as an abstract, disembodied process. Maturana and Varela's work emphasizes the embodied and embedded nature of cognition, highlighting the inseparable link between life and cognition.
