 Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela, two Chilean biologists and philosophers, developed the concept of autopoiesis, which is central to their theory of cognition and living systems. In their view, living organisms are characterized by their ability to self-produce and maintain their own structure and organization.

When Maturana and Varela say that everything in an organism is structurally dependent, they mean that all components and processes within a living system are interconnected and rely on each other to maintain the system's overall structure and function. This structural dependence is a key aspect of autopoiesis, as it allows the organism to adapt and respond to changes in its environment while maintaining its identity and autonomy.

Here are some key points to understand about structural dependence in living organisms:

1. Circular causality: In an autopoietic system, the components and processes interact in a circular, self-referential manner. Each element influences and is influenced by the others, creating a network of interdependent relationships.

2. Self-organization: The structural dependence of components allows the organism to self-organize and maintain its structure without the need for external control or guidance.

3. Structural coupling: Organisms interact with their environment through structural coupling, which means that they can be perturbed by external factors but always respond in a way that is determined by their own internal structure and organization.

4. Plasticity and adaptation: While maintaining their core structure, organisms can undergo changes and adaptations in response to environmental perturbations, thanks to the flexibility and plasticity of their structurally dependent components.

In summary, Maturana and Varela's concept of structural dependence highlights the interconnectedness and self-organizing nature of living systems, emphasizing how the components and processes within an organism rely on each other to maintain the system's autonomy and identity while allowing for adaptation and responsiveness to environmental changes.

 Structural coupling is a key concept in Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela's theory of autopoiesis and cognition. It refers to the way in which a living system interacts with its environment while maintaining its own autonomy and identity.

According to Maturana and Varela, living organisms are not passively influenced by their environment but rather engage in a reciprocal interaction characterized by mutual perturbations and adaptations. This interaction is called structural coupling.

Here are some important aspects of structural coupling:

1. Mutual perturbations: The environment can trigger changes (perturbations) in the organism, but these changes are determined by the organism's own structure and organization. Similarly, the organism's behavior can perturb the environment, but the environmental changes are determined by the environment's structure.

2. Congruence: Over time, the organism and its environment become congruent or compatible through a history of recurrent interactions. This means that the organism's structure and the environment's structure change in ways that allow for their continued interaction and survival.

3. Autonomy: Despite the ongoing interaction and mutual perturbations, the organism maintains its autonomy and identity. Its internal structure and organization determine how it responds to environmental perturbations, and its responses are always aimed at maintaining its own autopoiesis (self-production).

4. Co-evolution: As a result of structural coupling, both the organism and its environment co-evolve. The organism adapts to its environment, and the environment is shaped by the presence and activities of the organism.

5. Cognition: For Maturana and Varela, cognition is not limited to the brain or nervous system but is a process that emerges from the structural coupling between an organism and its environment. Cognition is the act of bringing forth a world through the process of living and interacting.

In summary, structural coupling is the ongoing, reciprocal interaction between a living organism and its environment, characterized by mutual perturbations and adaptations while maintaining the organism's autonomy and identity. This concept is central to understanding how organisms interact with and make sense of their world according to Maturana and Varela's theory of autopoiesis and cognition.
