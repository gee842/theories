4. **Memetic Dominance**: Successful power-optimizing agents gain increasing control over system narratives and behavioral templates
5. **Value Contagion**: Dominant agents actively propagate power-optimization as the primary success metric throughout the system

- Power accumulation → Enhanced capture capability → Accelerated accumulation
- Each cycle amplifies concentration effects through compound advantages
- System increasingly optimizes for power capture at the expense of other metrics

### Secondary Loop: Competition Intensity
- Resource scarcity → Intensified competition → More extreme optimization
- Diminishing resources for non-dominant agents drive increasingly desperate optimization
- Competition pressure forces short-term local optimization over long-term system stability

9. **Value Collapse**: System becomes increasingly reductionist, optimizing solely for power while growing blind to destabilizing effects

10. **Systemic Blindness**: The system loses ability to recognize or correct its destructive optimization patterns
11. **Critical Instability**: Intensity of value capture increases until fundamental system stability fails
12. **Collapse**: System implodes under its own optimization pressure

## Key Properties

- **Self-Reinforcing**: The cycle creates positive feedback loops that accelerate system evolution toward instability
- **Path-Dependent**: Early power advantages compound through multiple mechanisms
- **Globally Suboptimal**: Local optimization by individual agents leads to system-level dysfunction
- **Terminal**: Without intervention, the cycle typically continues until system collapse

## Implications

- The cycle represents a fundamental dynamic in systems where power can be accumulated
- Individual agent rationality leads to collective irrationality
- System stability requires mechanisms to break or moderate these feedback loops
- Intervention becomes increasingly difficult as the cycle progresses
