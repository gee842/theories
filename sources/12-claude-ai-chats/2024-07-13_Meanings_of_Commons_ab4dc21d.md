- Natural resources that are shared by all members of society, like air, water, or wildlife

2. In economics:
- Resources or goods that are open for use by all members of a society
- Often refers to the tragedy of the commons, where shared resources can be overexploited

3. British English:
- Short for House of Commons, the elected lower house of the UK Parliament
- Also used in some other countries with similar parliamentary systems

4. Universities:
- A shared dining hall in some colleges or universities
- Can also refer to common areas or shared spaces in educational institutions

5. Law:
- Common land: areas of private land where other people have certain traditional rights
- Common law: law developed through judicial decisions rather than legislative action

**ASSISTANT** (2024-07-13T10:37):

 Yes, you can say you're a commoner, but the meaning and appropriateness of the term depend on context. Here's what it generally means:

1. Historical/Traditional meaning:
- A person who is not of noble birth or royal blood
- Someone who doesn't hold a title of nobility

2. Modern usage:
- An ordinary person, as opposed to someone of high social or political status
- A member of the general public, not part of the elite or ruling class

3. British context:
- A person who is not a member of the nobility or the royal family

4. Informal/Colloquial:
- Sometimes used self-deprecatingly or humorously to emphasize one's ordinariness

It's important to note that "commoner" is not commonly used in everyday speech in most modern contexts. In many societies, especially those without a formal nobility system, the term can sound outdated or even slightly offensive if used to distinguish between social classes.

If you were to say "I am a commoner" today, it might be interpreted as:
- A joke or self-deprecating humor
- A statement about not having special privileges or status
- An archaic way of saying you're an ordinary person

In most modern, democratic societies, the distinction between commoners and nobility is largely ceremonial or historical, so the term isn't often used seriously.
