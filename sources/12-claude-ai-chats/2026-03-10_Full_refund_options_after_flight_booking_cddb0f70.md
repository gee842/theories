Let me summarize the key refund rules for the user.Here's a summary of your options for getting a full refund after booking a flight:

**24-Hour Free Cancellation (U.S. DOT Rule)**

If an airline requires payment with a reservation, it must allow the consumer to cancel and receive a full refund within 24 hours of booking. This applies to all ticket types, but you must have booked at least 2 days before departure. One important caveat: this 24-hour rule does not apply to tickets booked through online travel agencies or third-party platforms — only directly with airlines.

**Refundable Fare Classes**

Consumers who purchase fully refundable tickets are entitled to a refund when they do not use the purchased ticket. Every major airline sells refundable fares (usually at a higher price). These let you cancel anytime for a full cash refund.

**Airline-Caused Disruptions (Automatic Refunds)**

Under current DOT rules, you're entitled to automatic cash refunds when:

- Your flight is canceled and you choose not to rebook — airlines must issue an automatic refund to your original form of payment.
- The airline makes a significant schedule change (3+ hours domestic, 6+ hours international) and you decline the rebooking.
- An airline fails to provide extra services you paid for, such as Wi-Fi, seat selection, or in-flight entertainment.

By law, airlines must issue refunds within 7 business days for credit card payments, and within 20 days for cash or debit.

**Practical Advice**

Experts recommend booking directly through airline websites rather than third-party agencies, since the refund process is much smoother. If you want maximum flexibility, look for "Flexible" or "Refundable" fare classes when booking — Southwest is notable for offering free cancellation on all fares (refunded as travel credit for the cheapest tier, cash for refundable fares). Airlines like Delta, United, and American all offer refundable fare options at checkout.
