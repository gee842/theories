1. Ideology: This term is often used broadly to describe systems of ideas and ideals that form the basis of economic, political, or social theory and policy. It can encompass religious, economic, and political beliefs.

2. Worldview: This refers to the fundamental cognitive orientation of an individual or society, including their philosophy of life, values, emotions, and ethics. It can incorporate religious, economic, and political perspectives.

3. Hegemony: In Gramscian theory, this describes the dominance of one group over others, supported by legitimating norms and ideas. It can apply to religious, economic, and political power structures.

4. Nomos: Used in sociology, particularly by Peter L. Berger, this term refers to the meaningful order imposed upon experience through social institutions and norms. It could encompass religious, economic, and political orders.

5. Superstructure: In Marxist theory, this refers to the institutions and culture that emerge from the economic base of society, including religious, political, and legal systems.

6. Metanarrative: This philosophical term describes a comprehensive explanation of historical experience or knowledge. It can include overarching religious, economic, and political narratives.

If you were to coin a new term specifically for this concept, you might consider something like:

- Socio-spiritual-economic complex
- Politico-economic-religious system
- Ideological triad

These aren't established terms, but they attempt to capture the intertwined nature of religion, economics, and politics that you're describing.

The lack of a single, common term for this concept might itself be telling - it suggests that we often think of these as separate domains, even though they are deeply interconnected in practice. Your question highlights an interesting gap in our terminology for describing these overarching systems of belief and organization.
