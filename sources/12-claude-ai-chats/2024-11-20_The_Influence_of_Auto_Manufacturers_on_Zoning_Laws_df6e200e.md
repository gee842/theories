 Let me address this carefully:

The first part about mixed-use zoning being illegal in many American places through single-family zoning is largely accurate. Single-family zoning laws, which prohibit mixed-use development and multi-family housing, are indeed widespread across American cities and suburbs.

However, the claim about auto manufacturers being the primary drivers of these zoning laws is an oversimplification. While the auto industry certainly benefited from and likely supported car-dependent suburban development, single-family zoning emerged from multiple historical factors, including:

1. Early 20th century progressive era urban planning movements
2. Racial and economic segregation goals (particularly in the 1910s-1950s)
3. Post-WWII federal housing policies
4. Cultural preferences for suburban living
5. Property value preservation concerns among homeowners

The first single-family zoning law was enacted in Los Angeles in 1904, before cars were widespread. Berkeley followed in 1916, and many other cities adopted similar ordinances in the following decades.

While auto industry influence certainly played a role in shaping American urban development (particularly through lobbying against public transit and for highway construction), attributing single-family zoning primarily to auto manufacturer lobbying oversimplifies the complex historical and social factors that led to these laws.
