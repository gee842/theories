why abandoned the notion of ‘objective reality’ in second-order cybernetics

---
**ASSISTANT** (2024-05-02T11:14):

 In second-order cybernetics, the notion of an "objective reality" was abandoned for several reasons:

1. Observer-dependence: Second-order cybernetics emphasizes the role of the observer in any system. It argues that the observer's perception, beliefs, and actions are inseparable from the system being observed. This means that the observer's subjectivity is always present, making it impossible to achieve a purely objective view of reality.

2. Constructivism: This approach holds that individuals actively construct their own understanding of reality based on their experiences, beliefs, and interactions with the environment. Reality is not seen as a fixed, external entity but rather as a product of the observer's cognitive processes.

3. Self-referentiality: Second-order cybernetics recognizes that the observer is part of the system being observed. This self-referential nature of observation means that the act of observing influences the system itself, blurring the line between subject and object.

4. Circular causality: In second-order cybernetics, the relationship between the observer and the observed is characterized by circular causality, where the observer's actions and perceptions influence the system, and the system, in turn, influences the observer. This recursive interaction makes it difficult to maintain a strict separation between the observer and the observed.

5. Multiple realities: Given the observer-dependent nature of reality, second-order cybernetics acknowledges the possibility of multiple, equally valid realities based on different observers' perspectives and experiences.

By abandoning the notion of an objective reality, second-order cybernetics shifts the focus to the observer's role in constructing and interpreting reality, emphasizing the importance of subjectivity, self-reference, and circular causality in understanding complex systems.
