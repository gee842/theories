1. Reassess professional help: If you're not seeing a therapist or psychiatrist regularly, consider doing so. If you are, it might be time to discuss adjusting your treatment plan.

2. Consistency over intensity: Focus on maintaining small, consistent habits rather than pushing for big changes. Even tiny steps forward are valuable.

3. Track your mood: Keep a daily mood log. This can help you identify patterns and potential triggers, as well as recognize small improvements you might otherwise miss.

4. Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT): This approach focuses on accepting difficult thoughts and feelings while committing to actions aligned with your values. It might be helpful when other methods aren't working.

5. Explore underlying issues: Sometimes, persistent depression can be linked to unresolved trauma, relationship issues, or other underlying factors. Addressing these can be crucial for long-term improvement.

6. Consider lifestyle factors: Ensure you're addressing basics like sleep, nutrition, and exercise. These can have a significant impact on mood and motivation.

7. Peer support: Connecting with others who have similar experiences, perhaps through a support group, can provide validation and new perspectives.

8. Reassess your projects: It might be helpful to examine whether your current projects align with your values and interests. Sometimes, a shift in focus can reignite motivation.

Remember, recovery isn't always linear. Setbacks are normal and don't erase the progress you've made. Your struggle doesn't reflect on your abilities or worth. Depression is a serious illness that can impact anyone, regardless of their capabilities.

If you're feeling hopeless or having thoughts of self-harm, please reach out to a crisis helpline or emergency services immediately. Your well-being is important, and there are people ready to support you.
