**ASSISTANT** (2026-04-14T17:49):

The user is asking about categories of animal behavior - this is a well-established biology topic I can answer from knowledge.Animal behavior (ethology) is typically organized into several broad functional categories:

**Survival-oriented behaviors**
- Foraging and feeding — hunting, grazing, food caching, tool use
- Anti-predator — flight, freezing, alarm calls, crypsis, thanatosis (playing dead), mobbing
- Territorial — scent marking, patrolling, boundary displays, resource defense

**Reproductive behaviors**
- Mating/courtship — displays, dances, songs, nuptial gifts, mate guarding
- Parental care — nest building, brooding, feeding young, teaching
- Mate selection — intersexual choice, intrasexual competition, lek behavior

**Social behaviors**
- Agonistic — aggression, dominance hierarchies, submission displays, ritualized fighting
- Cooperative — cooperative hunting, alloparenting, mutualism, reciprocal altruism
- Communication — visual signals, vocalizations, chemical (pheromones), tactile, electrical
- Affiliative — grooming, play, bonding

**Movement and orientation**
- Migration — seasonal long-distance movement
- Homing and navigation — magnetic sense, sun compass, landmark use
- Dispersal — juveniles leaving natal territory

**Homeostatic/maintenance**
- Thermoregulation — basking, huddling, panting
- Grooming and hygiene — self-grooming, dust bathing, anting
- Sleep and rest cycles

**Learning and cognition**
- Habituation, sensitization
- Associative learning (classical and operant conditioning)
- Imprinting
- Social learning and cultural transmission
- Problem solving and insight

These overlap a lot in practice — a bird's song can be simultaneously communicative, territorial, and reproductive. The categories are more like analytical lenses than hard boundaries. Is there a particular category you're digging into?

The user wants a comprehensive taxonomy of animal behaviors organized by intent/motivation. Let me think about this systematically and give a thorough list.Here's a more exhaustive breakdown organized by the underlying intent or drive:

---

**1. SURVIVE (self-preservation)**
- Flee, freeze, fight (acute threat response)
- Crypsis (camouflage, mimicry)
- Thanatosis (feigning death)
- Aposematism (warning displays)
- Autotomy (shedding body parts to escape)
- Mobbing (group harassment of predator)
- Alarm calling
- Vigilance scanning
- Shelter seeking, burrowing, hiding

**2. EAT (energy acquisition)**
- Foraging, browsing, grazing
- Hunting (solitary and cooperative)
- Ambush predation
- Filter feeding
- Scavenging
- Food caching/hoarding
- Tool use for food extraction
- Kleptoparasitism (stealing others' food)
- Trap building (spider webs, antlion pits)
- Luring (anglerfish, snapping turtles)
- Farming (leafcutter ants, damselfish algae gardens)

**3. AVOID BEING EATEN (distinct from acute flight)**
- Deimatic displays (startle behavior)
- Müllerian/Batesian mimicry
- Ink/smoke screen ejection
- Spines, inflation, puffing up
- Bioluminescent counter-illumination
- Group dilution (selfish herd)
- Confusion effect (schooling, flocking, swarming)

**4. DEFEND TERRITORY / RESOURCES**
- Scent marking (urine, glands, feces)
- Vocalization (birdsong, howling, roaring)
- Boundary patrolling
- Ritualized displays (posturing, size exaggeration)
- Physical combat over territory
- Nest/den defense
- Resource monopolization

**5. FIND AND SELECT A MATE**
- Courtship displays (dances, plumage, colors)
- Song and vocal advertisement
- Nuptial gifts (food, silk, secretions)
- Lek aggregation
- Pheromone broadcasting
- Bioluminescent signaling (fireflies)
- Bower building
- Female choice assessment
- Sneaker male tactics (alternative reproductive strategies)
- Sperm competition (mate plugging, extended copulation)
- Mate guarding
- Sexual coercion
- Traumatic insemination

**6. REPRODUCE AND INVEST IN OFFSPRING**
- Nest/den construction
- Egg laying, live birth
- Incubation, brooding
- Feeding young (regurgitation, lactation, trophallaxis)
- Carrying young (marsupial pouch, mouth brooding, back carrying)
- Teaching (demonstrating prey capture, etc.)
- Brood parasitism (cuckoos, cowbirds)
- Infanticide (incoming male killing predecessor's offspring)
- Filial cannibalism (eating own young under stress)
- Offspring dispersal encouragement

**7. ESTABLISH AND MAINTAIN SOCIAL POSITION**
- Dominance displays
- Submission signals (rolling over, averting gaze, crouching)
- Ritualized fighting (antler locking, head butting)
- Coalition formation
- Rank-based resource access
- Winner/loser effects (confidence momentum)
- Badge-of-status signals (bib size in sparrows)

**8. COOPERATE / BUILD ALLIANCES**
- Cooperative hunting (wolves, orcas, chimps)
- Cooperative breeding and alloparenting
- Reciprocal altruism (vampire bat blood sharing)
- Sentinel behavior (meerkats)
- Interspecific mutualism (cleaner fish, oxpeckers)
- Coalition aggression
- Collective construction (beaver dams, termite mounds, coral)

**9. COMMUNICATE**
- Visual signals (posture, color change, gesture)
- Acoustic signals (calls, songs, stridulation, echolocation)
- Chemical signals (pheromones, allomones)
- Tactile signals (grooming, antennation, trunk touching)
- Vibrational signals (substrate drumming, web plucking)
- Electrical signals (weakly electric fish)
- Referential signaling (vervet monkey alarm types)
- Deceptive signaling (false alarm calls, broken-wing display)

**10. NAVIGATE / ORIENT / MOVE**
- Migration (seasonal, reproductive, altitudinal)
- Homing (salmon, pigeons)
- Dispersal from natal area
- Magnetoreception navigation
- Celestial navigation (sun, stars, polarized light)
- Landmark and cognitive map use
- Path integration (dead reckoning)
- Vertical migration (diel in oceans)
- Nomadism (following resources unpredictably)

**11. MAINTAIN BODY (homeostasis)**
- Thermoregulation (basking, huddling, panting, wallowing)
- Grooming, preening
- Dust/mud/ant bathing
- Sleep and torpor
- Estivation, hibernation, brumation
- Drinking, salt licking
- Sunning for vitamin synthesis or parasite control
- Self-medication (zoopharmacognosy — eating medicinal plants)

**12. EXPLORE / LEARN / PLAY**
- Exploratory behavior and neophilia/neophobia
- Object play, locomotor play, social play
- Habituation and sensitization
- Imprinting
- Observational learning and social transmission
- Innovation and problem solving
- Tool manufacture and refinement
- Cultural traditions (whale song dialects, chimp termite-fishing styles)

**13. MANAGE PARASITES / DISEASE**
- Allogrooming for parasite removal
- Anting, fur-rubbing with bioactive substances
- Behavioral fever (ectotherms seeking warmth)
- Social distancing from sick individuals
- Nest hygiene (ant corpse removal, bee hygienic behavior)
- Self-medication

**14. MANAGE TIME / SYNCHRONIZE**
- Circadian rhythms (sleep-wake cycles)
- Circannual rhythms (seasonal breeding, molting)
- Tidal rhythms (intertidal organisms)
- Synchronized spawning, hatching, emergence
- Dawn/dusk chorus timing
