# Chinese classical texts quoted by the passage map of board item RO

Imported 19 October 2026 for the [chapter on the Chinese sources and the theory of becoming](../../theories/becoming/chapters/CHINESE-SOURCES-AND-BECOMING.md), on [Ren's request](../15-codex-theory-conversations/2026-10-19-relational-ontology-of-chinese-thought.md). Every file is bound by its SHA-256 in the [provenance](PROVENANCE.json), and [`verify_ro.py`](../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/ro-relational-sources/verify_ro.py) checks every quotation of the [passage map](../../_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/root-foundation/development/ro-relational-sources/passages.json) against them.

- [`yidao-v1.3/evidence/texts/`](yidao-v1.3/evidence/texts/) (21 files) and [`atlas-v9.1/evidence/texts/`](atlas-v9.1/evidence/texts/) (58 files): the texts the map quotes from two atlases built with Codex, 周易易传与道德经体系图 v1.3.0 and 观其会通 中国传统思想研究图谱 v9.1.0. Each is the archive member, byte for byte; the provenance names the archive (kept outside the repository, 23 MB and 78 MB, bound by SHA-256), the member, and the atlas's own record of the text: its title, URL and edition note. 71 of these texts are transcriptions from Chinese Wikisource, five are CBETA's digital editions of the 大正藏, two are from xuges.com and one was checked against a scan held by the National Diet Library. None is collated with a printed edition, and the 郭店 and 帛书 witnesses are the atlases' transcriptions.
- [`wikisource/`](wikisource/) (14 files): Chinese Wikisource pages the atlases do not hold, each the MediaWiki API's response body at the revision named in the provenance: 《左传》宣公 (止戈为武), nine volumes of the 《说文解字》, 《礼记正义》卷五十二 (郑玄's note on 仁者人也), 《庄子·天下》 (惠施), 《朱子语类》卷一 and the 四库 page of 程颐's preface to the 《伊川易传》.

The texts are pre-modern and in the public domain. Wikisource's punctuation and markup are CC BY-SA 4.0. The five CBETA texts are kept with attribution under CBETA's terms for non-commercial distribution. The atlas records the notes on the other sources. Only the passages the map quotes were read closely, each in its chapter; retaining whole texts does not imply reading them whole.
