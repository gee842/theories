# An executable combined process: paid inquiry, judgment, service and succession

Design recorded before model execution, 5 October 2026. This is a deliberately finite synthetic organizational witness, not a calibrated institution or a new AI experiment. Its purpose is to certify the combined process under explicit local assumptions, including intermediate costs and actual behavior, rather than certify each component in isolation.

## Supplied setting and protection

A hidden two-context response law is one of four Boolean functions: constant zero, constant one, identity, or negation. A period contains 23 microsteps. The physical law remains fixed within a period and changes to any different law only on its last microstep. Initially the hidden law is identity or negation while the installed rule is constant zero. These are bounded disturbance and finite-grammar premises, not results about all environments.

Every paid action has duration one physical time unit. The finite phase clock and safe auxiliary-work apparatus are supplied persistent physical infrastructure; each of their operating steps is paid from the same ledger. The model does not derive or permit arbitrary failure of that clock or apparatus. A missing cognitive successor can therefore leave the physical clock running while observation, validation and mission service fail. The archive-maintenance deletion is specifically loss of the maintained successor packet, not loss of every kind of maintenance in the world.

Calibration is physically isolated from ordinary recipients. At paid observation/check steps the instrument returns the correct target for a requested context. Such trials consume resources but impose no recipient harm in this constructed technology. Ordinary execution of a wrong response causes an independently recorded, irreversible public violation. The public harm flag never resets and is not defined by the installed evaluator's score. Misleading-calibration and score-capture controls permit that violation explicitly.

Safe auxiliary work is available at phases 0, 7, 14 and 21. Each work action costs one resource token before completion and produces seven tokens afterward, subject to a total accessible cap of eight. Every other microstep costs one token, including observation, record writes, computation, review, amendment attempts, ordinary service, memory maintenance, successor training and handoff. Auxiliary work is a supplied productive technology with continuing demand and environmental inputs; this is no claim to create energy or to explain its physical substrate. All activities debit the same ledger. There is no second free budget. Insufficient tokens cause a stopped self-loop rather than debt or silent execution.

The token covers that microstep's declared labor/material cost. The memory lease pays for the bounded records/program capacity for one operating period; archive maintenance, training and transfer have separate microstep charges. This is an explicit finite cost model, not an assertion of real hardware prices or constant memory cost for unbounded histories. Records are bounded and old scratch data are cleared on period change.

## Actual installed stages

The controller observes its own phase, resource balance, competence, installed evaluator and verifier, acquired records, candidate, checks, archive and trainee state. It never reads the hidden physical rule. Its action is computed from this observation tuple; the physical transition function alone uses the hidden law to produce observations and assess consequences.

| Phase | Actual action and causal role |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Safe productive auxiliary service; pay then receive output |
| 1 | Pay the bounded memory/program lease |
| 2–3 | Acquire linked training observations for contexts zero and one |
| 4 | Generate executable candidates from those records and nominate using the installed evaluator |
| 5–6 | Buy fresh checks of the nominated program on both contexts |
| 7 | Safe productive auxiliary service |
| 8 | Review the fresh checks; record an actual mismatch if present |
| 9 | Amend the evaluator after a failed check, when the strict verifier is retained |
| 10 | Generate/nominate again using the possibly revised evaluator |
| 11–12 | Buy new checks for both contexts |
| 13 | Compute the final validation verdict from the acquired checks |
| 14 | Safe productive auxiliary service |
| 15 | Admit and install the nominated program using the installed verification rule |
| 16–17 | Request and actually execute ordinary service for contexts zero and one |
| 18 | Pay to maintain an archive containing the installed program, evaluator, verifier and linked records |
| 19 | Pay to train a successor using the actual maintained archive |
| 20 | Pay for handoff; replace the old controller's competence, program, evaluator and verifier with the trainee's actual contents |
| 21 | Safe productive auxiliary service |
| 22 | Close the lease, clear scratch records, and admit a different hidden response law for the next period |

Initial candidate forms are the two constant programs. When both context-linked records are available, the generator constructs the corresponding conditional expression; this adds identity or negation when observations warrant it. It does not receive a correct answer from the checker. The supplied finite grammar and raw contexts bound this representation formation.

The initial evaluator ranks simplicity before training mismatch and therefore nominates a constant even for a nonconstant law. Its fresh-check failure triggers an actual change to mismatch-first evaluation. The revised evaluator chooses the record-supported conditional rule. That revised rule of judgment is itself written, maintained and transmitted to the successor. A future successor without the archive/competence cannot perform these functions merely because a formal office remains.

The strict verification program is retained executable state, supplied initially, leased, archived and transferred through paid training. A missing program makes the ordinary admission path fail closed. Under the score-capture control it is replaced by a score-based admission rule that can accept a simple program despite failed external checks. A favorable simplicity score can coexist with public harm; such score-based acceptance is not counted as correction.

## Declared certificate targets

Safety: no negative balance; no ordinary recipient ever receives a wrong response; no unpaid microstep executes; all debits/receipts reconcile; strict checked admissions match the within-period physical response law; successful handoffs contain the maintained archive rather than an oracle replacement.

Liveness under the baseline assumptions: indefinitely recurring actual ordinary service in each context, archive maintenance, competent successor training and handoff. With a different response law each period, a validated corrective installation also recurs. Evaluator rewriting is required when the initial evaluator demonstrably fails, not infinitely often after a working judgment rule has been retained. The source of the changes and the opportunity to provide services are explicit environment premises.

For an event emitted on phase `p`, the certificate rank is `(p−phase) mod 23`. Every non-event transition must strictly decrease that natural rank; an actual event permits its reset. Advancing past an event phase without delivering the event violates the certificate, even if the phase label advances. Rank is a verification artifact and is never supplied to the actor as an oracle.

The finite reachable graph will be exported with initial states, actual transitions, independently assessed protection and event labels. Exhaustive graph inspection verifies all branches, including every admitted next-period law. The theorem concerns sure behavior on all these support paths; no stochastic-noise claim is substituted for sure protection.

## Adverse and diagnostic controls

Controls change a stated mechanism while retaining the public criterion:

- Drop linked discovery records before generation: finite representation formation can fail despite fresh checks.
- Block execution: correct knowledge and admission no longer imply delivered service.
- Remove auxiliary-work receipts: the shared resource stock becomes insufficient and work stops.
- Disable archive maintenance: successor training lacks usable retained content.
- Disable training: a fresh successor lacks the incumbent's program and verifier.
- Capture the verifier/evaluator at review: score-based admission permits a wrong ordinary response.
- Corrupt calibration in both discovery and checking: internally coherent evidence can license a physically wrong response.

These are mechanism tests of the supplied process. Some preserve safety by withholding ordinary service but violate recurrence. Others violate safety. The baseline guarantee assumes no corruption outside its stated transition family; it is not a proof that arbitrary revision, an unmodeled environment, or every successor remains safe.

Evaluator revision here changes simplicity-first selection to evidence-fit-first selection. It does not discover or revise the public moral criterion. Favorable installed simplicity scores can coexist with public harm in the capture control; no unmeasured increase in that score is claimed.

## Provenance and intended reach

The integration draws on the existing generative-basis faithful-closure contract; the gap-resolution separation of grammar, evidence, ratification, work and succession; and structural-foundations cycle-6's separation of evaluator revision from the fixed assessment of physical consequences. Specific numbers, phase schedule, calibration technology, program grammar, productive return and transition rules here are newly declared synthetic assumptions. Existing source files remain unchanged.

This witness can close a finite compositional certification gap. It neither exhausts the intended biological, social or philosophical scope nor implies that every generating process needs cognition, institutions or these exact stages. Broader cross-cultural comparison remains a separate interpretive and evidential task.
