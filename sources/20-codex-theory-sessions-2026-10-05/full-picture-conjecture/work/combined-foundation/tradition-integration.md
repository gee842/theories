# Integrating moral cultivation and shared generation across scales

5 October 2026. Source-based interpretation and a proposed mechanism mapping. This note reads the supplied texts as contributions to a combined theory of formation, action, feedback, and correction. It neither treats their differences as a reason to abandon integration nor treats similarity as experimental proof of one ontology.

## The integrated claim

The strongest shared functional proposal is that **action changes the conditions of later action**. What a person attends to, repeatedly desires, remembers, acknowledges, practises, or refuses can change their later perception and choice. Speech and conduct also change other people's conditions; institutional rules shape which experiences are heard and which responses can be enacted. Cultivation and correction therefore act on the process that forms decisions, not only on a final isolated choice.

This fits the revised `conscience-and-morality-core.md`: conscience is a fallible capacity to register others' situations and reconsider one's conduct; shared generation is the openly defended ethical direction; models examine whether its requirements can be jointly realized. The integration does not require agreement on the ultimate nature of soul or self, or proof that every coherent person must accept the ethical commitment.

The cross-traditional comparison is evidence that these problems and proposed interventions have been articulated in several settings. It gives the research programme a richer mechanism catalogue and opposing interpretations to test. The passages themselves do not supply controlled causal estimates, universal prevalence, or statistical independence. Shared human problems, transmission, translation, and selective comparison remain possible explanations of resemblance.

## A mechanism vocabulary that makes the comparison useful

The following roles are proposed analytical variables, not translations claimed to exhaust the original terms.

| Role | Within a person | Between persons or in institutions | A concrete intervention or failure question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | Which cues receive processing and repeated rehearsal | Which experiences reach discussion, records, media, or an agenda | Does a new hearing route change what can be recognized, or merely add repetition of the same evidence? |
| Desire and threat | What feels urgent, attractive, or dangerous | Incentives, fear of exclusion, rivalry, and status stakes | Does reducing a specified threat permit acknowledgment, or does exclusion continue because authority remains unchanged? |
| Memory | Retained experience, learned reasons, and available comparisons | Records, testimony, precedents, and institutional memory | Does a record preserve the link needed to evaluate a new action, or only an aggregate that erases it? |
| Recognition and judgment | Interpreting a situation and reconsidering a belief | Granting evidence and affected experience a place in a decision | Can a warranted finding actually change the operative rule? |
| Habit and policy | A learned propensity or deliberate procedure | Routines, professional practices, sanctions, and selection | Does changing a routine alter later decisions after the immediate incentive is removed? |
| Standing and authority | Whose interests an evaluator includes | Who may speak, refuse, authorize, appeal, and revise | Does an excluded person's claim enter the objective and have an executable response route? |
| Capacity and nourishment | Time, bodily support, skill, and recovery | Care, funding, access, maintenance, and succession | Is the correction route still available when it is needed, including its reconstruction cost? |
| Cultivation and correction | Practising attention, reflection, and response | Education, reciprocal criticism, protected inquiry, institutional review | Does the intervention increase warranted revision without merely increasing conformity to whoever teaches it? |

The roles can be causally coupled in one state:

\[
z_t=(\text{world, attention, memory, dispositions, relationships, rules, capacity})_t,
\qquad z_{t+1}=F(z_t,u_t,\xi_t).
\]

The participant observes only \(o_t=H(z_t)\), not every variable in the analyst's model. The action rule and its memory can themselves be components of \(z_t\), so practice, fatigue, social incentives, and rule revision can alter future choices. A common disturbance may affect several components at once. “Conscience” can be an identifiable capacity and process within this system without being stipulated to be infallible, detached from support, or metaphysically separate.

This equation is a modelling interface. Substantive content comes from declared dependencies and interventions: for example, whether a threat changes attention; whether a recognized harm can enter a decision; whether a new rule suppresses reports; whether renewed support makes correction executable. Replacing these questions with an undifferentiated “good state” would discard much of what the sources contribute.

## Transfer across scales: possible, but the map must preserve action

The existing relative-completeness framework applies to intrapersonal, interpersonal, and institutional systems when each is supplied with an adequate model of the declared class. It does not require that an institution literally be a person or that a person's mental components be independent agents.

For a claimed cross-scale equivalence, use a map \(q\) that preserves the protected outcomes, permitted observations, feasible interventions, and relevant successor consequences. In the finite stochastic case the exact test is equality of transition probability into every retained block for each declared intervention. In the sure-control case retain support-path lifting and admissible policy correspondences in both directions. Matching an attractive verbal pattern or one passive outcome is insufficient.

The cross-scale map can fail meaningfully. A personal reminder and an institutional appeal both return a neglected consideration to a decision, but an appeal may require another actor's permission, a public record, and protection from retaliation. A state that records only “reflection available” omits those dependencies. Restoring them makes the comparison more exact; it does not show that the traditions were secretly using identical mechanisms.

A weaker and often appropriate result is **one theory schema with several distinct mechanisms**, rather than an exact quotient between every scale. Each implementation can instantiate the general formation–feedback–correction structure while retaining its own variables and causal laws. The dependency-closure theorem can then identify which support conditions each implementation requires. The two-sided policy theorem transfers conclusions only where its correspondence is proved.

Normative assessment also needs the wider boundary. A routine that stabilizes its operator while damaging patients, employees, outsiders, or future persons can be stable and still fail the shared-generation commitments. Conversely, ending a harmful routine, permitting withdrawal, resting, or closing a dangerous institution can protect those commitments. The revised core already makes this distinction. No new requirement of endless production should be imposed.

## Negative cases and discriminating tests

These are explicit constructed countercases or proposed empirical contrasts, not claims that new experiments have been performed.

1. **Attention is not automatically beneficial.** A perfectly accurate private understanding with no permission, resources, or safe route to act changes no outcome. A model claiming that recognition alone guarantees correction is false in this case. Compare restored information with restored authority separately.
2. **Anger and desire are not uniform harm variables.** In a model where anger prompts a truthful complaint and a review channel repairs an injury, suppressing it worsens the protected outcome. In another, threat-driven rehearsal prevents a factual correction. The hypotheses must identify the mediation through attention, evidence, and action, rather than infer wrongness from a feeling's name.
3. **Cultivation can train compliance.** A practice can improve concentration and group coordination while making participants less willing to acknowledge an outsider's injury. This falsifies “better discipline implies greater shared protection” unless the practice and evaluation include those affected parties.
4. **Truth may be recognized without being enacted.** If the decision maker privately accepts an error but loses office or income by correcting it, acknowledgment and repair can separate. That case requires an institutional incentive/authority mechanism, not a claim that conscience was absent by definition.
5. **Habit need not be irreversible.** A model can permit one sufficiently credible encounter or a changed environment to reverse a disposition. The existence and size of a persistence effect must be tested under stated conditions; calling a process self-reinforcing does not prove an absorbing state or two universal attractors.
6. **Consensus can erase relevant difference.** Unanimity obtained by excluding a harmed group does not preserve its standing. Conversely, disagreement with protected speaking and appeal can retain correction. A consensus label alone fails to distinguish the cases.
7. **Parallel virtues need not compose.** Accurate individual judgments can still produce joint harm under correlated shocks, conflicting permissions, or resource competition. The joint mechanism must preserve those couplings; multiplying independent success rates without justification is invalid.
8. **No purchase of inquiry may be needed.** If one feasible action protects under all possible conditions, further discrimination is unnecessary for that objective. When hidden conditions demand incompatible actions, the same information channel can become indispensable. This reproduces an existing exact distinction rather than declaring contemplation universally mandatory.

A discriminating study would specify a limited target mechanism before outcomes are inspected, measure the proposed mediator independently, vary or compare a feasible intervention, retain harmful and null outcomes, and check whether the effect survives competing explanations. For example, a change in how testimony is heard should be distinguished from a simultaneous change in sanctions or resources. A source passage supplies the candidate relation; the intervention study assesses its causal force in the declared setting.

## What the integration establishes

The material supports a substantive combined research foundation: **human conduct and its evaluators are formed within processes that can nourish, distort, institutionalize, or correct their future operation**. Shared generation supplies the present theory's explicit public direction; conscience supplies one fallible route of sensitivity and reflection; cultivation and institutions modify the conditions of that route; exact modelling checks whether the declared protection and progress requirements are attainable.

This is a coherent integration across scales and traditions at the level of functional questions and testable mechanisms. Some finite mathematical interfaces are already complete under their declared contracts. The textual convergence strengthens the relevance of the questions and expands the mechanism catalogue. It does not itself establish a complete causal law for all people, all traditions, or all future situations—and integration need not wait for universal agreement before becoming useful or defensible.
