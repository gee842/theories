# Four primary-text probes of the full-picture conjecture

Research date: 5 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. Scope: a deliberately small comparison, not a survey of cultures or a historical genealogy. All proposed mappings and examples below are this research team's interpretations, not claims made by the historical authors. The source capsules are kept below 200 words per work; the later formal examples are original constructions.

## Why these four

The comparison samples different explanatory tasks: differential reproduction, a cosmological and practical account of change, the formation of an inquiry, and institutions that can change the rules governing their own activity. The selection follows this task's existing questions. It cannot establish independent cultural convergence, universality, representativeness, or novelty. Similar vocabulary is a lead for a mapping; it is not a successful mapping.

## Primary-source capsules

### 1. Darwin: selection under relations of dependence

**Text checked.** Charles Darwin, *On the Origin of Species*, first edition (1859), chapters III–IV, especially printed pp. 62–63 and 80–83; chapter VI, pp. 200–202. [Darwin Online, full primary text](https://darwin-online.org.uk/content/contentblock?basepage=1&itemID=F373&viewtype=text).

Darwin's struggle includes dependence and reproductive success, not only direct fighting or individual longevity. His mistletoe example involves hosts and bird dispersal; excessive parasites can kill a host. Selection concerns inherited advantageous variation under these relations. His discussion of injury across species and imperfect adaptation gives no warrant for equating selective success with universal benefit.

**Our interpretation.** Map organisms and their environment to configurations; reproduction, death, and dispersal to transitions; hosts and dispersers to identified enabling relations. This preserves a conditional causal question: which variation changes expected descendants in a specified setting? It drops most morphology and history unless they affect the selected query. It cannot transport biological inheritance into institutional imitation merely by renaming descendants “copies.” Nor does it supply a normative duty to preserve every lineage. A population-frequency projection needs a separate dynamical adequacy check, demonstrated below.

### 2. *Xici*: generation, symbols, and situated conduct

**Text checked.** *Yijing*, *Xici* I, chapters 1–5, 8–12, especially I.5. [Chinese primary text, stable Wikisource revision](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=易傳/繫辭上&oldid=2611140). Authorship and dating are not established here; the hosting site's attribution is not adopted.

I.5's 生生之謂易 may provisionally be rendered “generation upon generation is called change.” This is a working gloss, not a uniquely correct translation. The surrounding text concerns yin/yang, goodness, nature, virtue, qian/kun, and divination. I.2 and I.8 connect figures, explanations, judgments, and conduct; I.12 treats limits of writing and speech together with the expressive role of images.

**Our interpretation.** A partial reading can retain continuing generation and the practical use of representations within change. A stock-replenishment equation drops the cosmological, evaluative, and divinatory setting. Neither resource persistence nor indefinite biological reproduction is therefore a faithful equivalent of 生生. A six-bit encoding can represent line patterns but cannot establish the truth of their worldly interpretations. Reducing the text to neutral dynamics would erase commitments that require argument or rejection, not silently translate them away.

### 3. Dewey: inquiry forms its problem

**Text checked.** John Dewey, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (1938), chapter VI, “The Pattern of Inquiry,” printed pp. 101–111 in this scan (chapter continues through p. 119). [Primary chapter scan](https://thehangedman.com/teaching-files/pragmatism/dewey-logic2.pdf). These are the printed original pagination, not the different pagination of *Later Works*.

Dewey treats inquiry as conduct within an unsettled situation. A definite problem is an achievement of inquiry; its formulation regulates which observations and proposed solutions count as relevant. Ideas anticipate consequences and require embodied symbols. Resolution involves operations on conditions, not merely a change in a private mental state. He also treats methods of inquiry as open to inquiry through their consequences.

**Our interpretation.** Include representational vehicles, problem descriptions, tests, interventions, and changed circumstances in the process state. This preserves feedback between framing and observation; a posterior over a fixed hypothesis list captures only a restricted instance. It discards the lived qualitative unity of a situation unless a defensible representation is supplied. It cannot turn Dewey's account into a guarantee that every difficulty is algorithmically soluble. His standards for warranted inquiry also cannot be replaced by whatever representation spreads most widely.

### 4. Ostrom: participants can transform an action situation

**Text checked.** Elinor Ostrom, “Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems,” *American Economic Review* 100 (2010), 641–672, revised Nobel lecture. Relevant printed pages: 646–648, 651–653, 663–664. [Primary article](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.3.641).

Ostrom distinguishes a framework, a theory, and a model. Her framework relates action situations, contextual conditions, rules in use, outcomes, and evaluation. Participants may revise rules; whether they can do so is an empirical condition, not a universal capability. Her discussion of robust resource institutions includes monitoring and collective choice. She does not claim one explanatory model or one unambiguous theory of justice covers every setting.

**Our interpretation.** Represent authorized rule revision as an action with effects on later action availability, information, and incentives. This preserves an endogenous institutional mechanism if actual practices, authority, and enforcement are measured. A stock-only projection drops those mechanisms. Formal availability cannot stand for lawful permission, practical capability, and legitimacy simultaneously. A framework broad enough to host many models remains different from a theory that predicts which institutional changes will succeed.

## Original mapping tests

These constructions establish limited mathematical points. They are not historical evidence or empirical tests of the works above.

### A. A frequency map that fails to preserve selection dynamics

Let a microstate record two organisms' types and locations. There is one productive site, where an organism yields two descendants, and one barren site, where it yields none. The two states are

\[
x=(A\text{ at productive site},B\text{ at barren site}),\qquad
y=(B\text{ at productive site},A\text{ at barren site}).
\]

Let \(p\) retain only type frequencies. Then \(p(x)=p(y)=(1/2,1/2)\). After reproduction, \(p(Fx)=(1,0)\) and \(p(Fy)=(0,1)\). There is no deterministic macro transition \(\bar F\) such that \(pF=\bar Fp\) on both states.

The map preserves present type frequencies and discards type–site associations. Those associations carry the causal relation needed for the next-frequency query. Adding the joint distribution of types and sites repairs this particular example. It does not prove that the resulting state description suffices for every other query. In a stochastic version, exact aggregation requires equality of the pushed-forward next-state laws for microstates in each macrostate, under each intervention being transported.

This makes “population,” “resources,” and “selection” more than interchangeable labels: an actual map is accountable to the transition it purports to preserve.

### B. A policy is not a world model, and certainty is not repair

Consider a machine with a jammed valve. A physical diagnostic display states the cause correctly; the operator's world model predicts that opening the valve restores flow. A policy maps the available display and work order to either opening the valve or waiting. Both objects can be physically represented while playing different functional roles.

Let \(z=(v,m,\pi,b)\) contain valve state, encoded model, encoded policy, and repair budget. The observation map can return the same accurate diagnosis in two states with different budgets. A projection retaining only the probability assigned to “valve jammed” assigns both posterior probability one. Nevertheless one state can finance repair now and the other cannot. Correct belief is therefore insufficient for resolution of the material difficulty.

A second failure is equally precise: a controller whose fixed grammar contains only “adjust thermostat” cannot produce “replace valve” merely by becoming statistically confident within that grammar. Enlarging the grammar is an additional modeled transformation. An analyst may supply a finite menu of such enlargements, but that implements revision among supplied possibilities, not unrestricted formation of possibilities. The model must state which of these achievements it actually demonstrates.

### C. Stocks and flows cannot recover permission or legitimacy

Construct two institutions with identical reservoir levels, withdrawals, incomes, and maintenance payments over a year. In one, users authorize the extraction rule and can appeal; in the other, an authority imposes the same schedule and suppresses appeals. Let \(q\) retain only the numerical trajectory. Then \(q(x)=q(y)\), while the explicitly represented participation and appeal relations differ.

Consequently no function of \(q\) alone can reconstruct those relations. If an evaluation judges them morally relevant, that evaluation does not factor through \(q\). This is a failure of the projection, independent of whether the observer endorses either institution's outcomes.

Even action availability needs several types. “Pump water” may be physically feasible, legally prohibited, institutionally punishable, and materially unaffordable for different actors at the same time. One undifferentiated set \(T\) cannot answer all four questions. A usable account distinguishes physical feasibility, access, authorization, expected enforcement, and chosen action. These can interact causally without becoming synonyms.

### D. A symbolic encoding is not an empirical abstraction

Define a six-line symbol as \(s\in\{0,1\}^6\); a designated line-change operation toggles specified coordinates. This is an exact encoding of the chosen combinatorial operation. It preserves the number and position of lines and the stipulated toggle relation.

Now assign each symbol a verbal account of a social situation. Without an independently justified map from social histories to symbols and a rule connecting social interventions to toggles, the coding gives no prediction about social outcomes. Many incompatible worldly interpretations fit the same combinatorics. A syntactic preservation theorem does not supply semantic truth, causal adequacy, or ethical warrant.

This is a deliberately failed route to unification. The useful remaining question is whether a particular interpretive practice changes what users notice or do, and with which consequences. That would require primary contextual study and observations of practice; it cannot be settled by inspecting a binary representation.

## Distinctions and incompatibilities the synthesis must keep

The comparison does not establish a debate between these authors. Their different explanatory projects create demands on a proposed synthesis:

1. **Success needs a typed criterion.** Descendant number, resolution of a difficulty, resource provision, consent, and a cosmological judgment are different predicates. A model may study relations among them. Calling each “fitness” obscures the relation requiring proof.
2. **Transformation is too broad to predict on its own.** A common transition language can encode both repair and destruction. Predictive content comes from restrictions on transitions, observation, budgets, and intervention effects, justified independently of the observed outcome.
3. **The evaluator can be included without being certified.** A model can represent the researchers' notes, procedures, resources, and interventions as further events. This is representational inclusion. It is not evidence that the model correctly understands every such event or can validate its own adequacy from nowhere.
4. **The explanatory and normative layers need a declared bridge.** Dependence can explain who is affected; power and information constraints can explain what remedies are feasible. Neither alone decides whose interests ought to count. Supplying that commitment is a substantive part of the account.
5. **A common formal fragment does not imply complete translations.** Two accounts may share a resource-balance submodel while disagreeing about ontology, warrant, purpose, and moral standing. The shared fragment must be named at the level actually demonstrated.

## Rejected formulations

- “生生 is biological reproduction or conservation of a resource stock.” Rejected as an equivalence; a restricted interpretive use remains possible.
- “Selection and inquiry are the same algorithm.” Rejected without an explicit mapping that preserves both their transition rules and success predicates.
- “Institutions are policies.” Rejected as an identity: an institution may distribute policy, modeling, rule revision, authorization, and enforcement roles across different realizations.
- “Encoding every object in a state space establishes completeness.” Rejected: encodability provides neither an empirically adequate transition law nor access to the encoded distinctions.
- “Traditions resonate, therefore they validate the synthesis.” Rejected: the selection of traditions, translation choices, and interpretation are additional sources of flexibility.

## Concrete next comparison test

Use the same small reservoir simulator for two candidate descriptions. The first retains aggregate stock, extraction, and finance. The second additionally represents observation access and the authority to propose, admit, and enact changes. Before running the test, freeze both descriptions and the interventions each is meant to support.

Construct matched initial aggregate states and randomize (i) who receives a diagnostic signal and (ii) who can enact a validated repair rule, holding physical resources constant. Demand out-of-sample predictions of repair timing, stock-floor crossings, and the separately specified participation criterion. The aggregate projection should fail whenever the omitted relations change the relevant outcomes; the richer model must predict the sign and magnitude before observing them. Include a null condition where authority and information do not constrain repair. A richer model that only redescribes results afterward gains no support.

This would test an expressly limited mechanism and its projection. It would not validate all four primary accounts, all cultural interpretations, or a universally complete theory.
