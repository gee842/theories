# Audit of the prior selection–renewal–inquiry results

Research note, 5 October 2026. Written in the dedicated full-picture session. The source workspace was read only. This audit independently checks elementary results and their logical relationships; it does not revalidate the larger synthetic optimizations or their empirical applicability. Its independent arithmetic is in `prior_checks.py`, with output in `prior_checks.json` alongside this note.

## Judgment

The existing record establishes several conditional constraints on processes whose supports, observations, and interventions themselves require maintenance. It does **not** establish that selection, renewal, cognition, institutions, and ethics are projections of one discovered universal structure. Its strongest defensible connection is a conjunction:

1. A specified function requires physically and institutionally available resources at every relevant prefix of its trajectory.
2. A contingent response requires the distinctions that select it, or a common safe alternative, before the response deadline.
3. A feasible response must belong to the actual policy class, including available actions, authority, timing, and paid access.
4. An evaluation of these consequences needs explicit protected functions, affected parties, horizons, and burdens.

The first three have exact mathematical instances. The fourth supplies the specification to which those results apply; the equations do not choose that specification. Common notation can expose their interaction but is not evidence of a universal explanatory law. The record itself usually maintains this boundary carefully.

## Independent checks and a new insufficiency counterexample

**Selection and scale (U02).** In pure selection, `ṅᵢ=rᵢnᵢ`. For `ñᵢ=a(t)nᵢ`, `r̃ᵢ=rᵢ+ȧ/a`; therefore `x̃ᵢ=xᵢ` and `r̃ᵢ−r̃ⱼ=rᵢ−rⱼ`. For example, two equally frequent types may have total abundance `N=eᵗ` or `N=e⁻ᵗ`, with identical shares forever. This is an exact quotient/identification result. It does not preserve absolute resource demands or ecological boundary conditions. A projection to shares erases exactly the question of absolute scale.

**Historical compression (U20).** Histories `00010` and `01000` have endpoint zero and two switches in four transitions. Under the equal-prior slow-then-fast/fast-then-slow models (`.05`, `.45`), their predictive probabilities are **.44835161092568626** and **.05164838907431374**. At threshold `.3`, their actions differ. Independently enumerating all 16 switch words verifies identical total-count distributions across the two ordered models. Thus no downstream computation or signaling from the total count can recover the erased regime orientation. Early/late counts do preserve it for this particular expanded family; raw history is not uniquely necessary.

**Aging decision value (U18).** Direct subtraction of Bayes risks with `h=.2,L=10,c=4` gives historical-state values **1, .4, .04, 0** at ages one through four, agreeing with the formula. Its historical truth has not changed. Inference from that truth and the price of making it available are separate.

**Inquiry reserve (U24).** With cost one, cap two, and grant three with probability one half, the uncapped mean net grant is `+.5`. The exact lifetime equations `e₁=1+e₂/2` and `e₂=1+e₁/2+e₂/2` give **4** and **6**. Its living-state transition spectral radius is **.8090169944<1**. The result depends on inaccessible overflow and no restart. Its whole host institution may still be producing.

**Functional replacement (U10).** Reintegrating logistic adoption and lagged maturation at seed `.01`, productivity multiplier `2`, lag `2`, and adoption rate `1` gives the flow minimum at **6.2794900989** and required reserve **.7291694326**, agreeing with the recorded rounded value. Earlier positive flow is included; merely integrating the negative-flow interval would overstate the necessary initial reserve.

**Recovery bridge (U27).** If `ḃ=A−D e^(−δt)`, then cumulative cash change is `At−D(1−e^(−δt))/δ`. For `A=1,D=3,δ=1`, its minimum occurs at `log 3`, and the required initial cash is **.9013877113** despite the positive mature margin. This independently checks integration and the stationary point, not a discretized fitted trajectory.

**Necessary information is insufficient for a policy.** Take independent binary tests with sensitivity and specificity `.9`, no ordinary information, false-reopening bound `α=.05`, and required detection probability `.9`. U26 requires transcript divergence at least `kl(.9,.05)=2.3762054022`. Each test supplies `kl(.9,.1)=1.7577796619`, so the bound only requires **at least two tests**.

Yet exactly two tests cannot attain the target. Under the healthy world, two positive outcomes have probability `.01`; under the hazardous world they have probability `.81`. Mixed outcomes have probability `.18` under either world. The best randomized test spends its `.05` false-positive allowance on all double positives, then `.04` of the mixed outcomes, for maximum power **.85**. Remaining outcomes have a lower likelihood ratio and cannot improve it. Three tests with a two-positive threshold achieve size **.028** and power **.972**. Thus permitting two tests and enough cash for them can satisfy the KL lower bound while the exact desired correction remains unattainable. The conjunction of several necessary inequalities is still only necessary. An exact policy feasibility result requires a dynamic recursion or equally strong construction.

The randomized Neyman–Pearson calculation is explicit: the likelihood ratios for `++`, either mixed word, and `--` are respectively `81`, `1`, and `1/81`. Accept `++` certainly, accept a mixed outcome with probability `(.05−.01)/.18=2/9`, and never accept `--`. Size is `.01+(2/9)(.18)=.05`; power is `.81+(2/9)(.18)=.85`. Equivalently one can randomize one mixed word with probability `4/9` and reject the other; the independent script enumerates all four exact rational outcome laws and greedily fills the size allowance in likelihood-ratio order. This solves the finite linear decision problem, rather than assuming a deterministic threshold restriction.

Any strategy that adaptively stops after **at most two** of these fixed iid tests can be simulated from the full two-sample transcript and its private randomization: reveal only the prefix requested by the strategy and ignore unused draws. It therefore cannot exceed the full-transcript optimum `.85`. This argument does not cover actions that change the experiment's likelihoods, an expected-sample budget permitting more than two tests on some paths, or other information channels. The example is an application of classical testing theory, not a claim of mathematical novelty.

## What survives a transformation

There is no single notion of “invariant” covering this record. The following are different, checkable preservation claims.

| Transformation | What survives | What need not survive |
|---|---|---|
| Bijective relabeling of state, actions, observations, with kernels, costs, and protected sets transported consistently | Trajectory laws, feasible policies, safety probabilities, expected costs; exact theorems are conjugate statements. | An interpretation in another domain; empirical adequacy of the transported labels. |
| Compatible positive change of resource units, with thresholds/flows/conversions changed together | Balance and prefix-feasibility inequalities. | A common scalar for unlike resources, rights, or burdens. |
| Common positive abundance rescaling with corresponding rate shift, U02 | Shares and pairwise growth advantages. | Total scale, output, stock balance, absolute viability. |
| Deterministic observation compression | Any decision measurable with respect to the retained statistic; total variation and KL cannot increase. | Fine distinctions, safe action availability, posterior sufficiency under a new model, causal effects of omitted variables. |
| Decision-sufficient summary for a declared joint model and binary event | All threshold Bayes risks exactly iff full predictive probability is measurable from that summary, U20. | Unspecified future questions, costs of retaining/using the summary, revised feasible actions, expanded models. |
| Transition quotient preserving action availability, observation law, rewards/costs, safety predicates and next-block distribution for every allowed action | Quotient policy predictions and appropriate finite-horizon decision values. | Such preservation does not follow from shared vocabulary or ordinary observational fit. In controlled problems action/observation compatibility is essential. |
| Finer information or larger policy class under identical budgets/costs and unchanged objective | Optimized value weakly improves by the option to ignore it; attainable set weakly expands. | Improvement by the actual evolving policy, net value when information is priced, legitimacy, stable adoption. |
| Replacing one functional carrier with another while preserving all required service, timing, inputs and observation/authority interfaces | Specified functional tests can remain attainable. | Identity of carrier, all other functions, unmodeled effects; a beneficial mature successor does not prove a safe transition. |
| Restricting actions alone in an otherwise fixed model | Attainable outcome set weakly shrinks. | Effect of an actual institutional change that also changes information, production, incentives or preferences. |

The variable tuple `(E,C)` in U06 has an exact **balance cancellation**: for `V=E+C/η`, paid conversion cancels in `V̇=P(C)−δE−μC/η`. This is not conservation: production and losses remain. Nor does the scalar `V` generally give a closed state. Equal `V` with different `C` can produce different `P(C)` and future possibilities. Analogously, summing cash across legally inaccessible accounts does not preserve action feasibility.

An especially small counterexample is `(accessible cash, barred treasury)=(1,4)` versus `(0,5)`. Both project to total five. A necessary immediate inquiry costs one, transfers are disallowed, and no output arrives beforehand. The first state can act; the second cannot. An aggregate `R=5` lacks the access relation needed to answer the question. This is a reason to type resources and enabling relations separately or to retain an explicit availability predicate. Calling both “resources” does not repair the lost relation.

## Relation to a minimal formal core

For these existing results, a controlled transition system with state, action availability, observation interface, resource ledgers, and explicit evaluation specification is sufficient. It can include controller memory, maintained sensors, institutions, and policies as causal state or distributed components. That representation does not require a physically separate omniscient observer; however, its own correctness and coverage are analyst assumptions subject to test.

The provisional `S=(X,T,R,O)` is a useful signature only if its types are fixed. `X` could mean a state set or current configuration; `T` a set of transformations, action kernel, or temporal relation; `R` both quantities and rights; `O` a set of distinctions or an observation kernel. These are not interchangeable. For example, a law over observations is needed by U22/U26, a possible-successor relation by U05, differentiable flows by U01/U06, and an evaluation predicate by every safety result. One signature may organize these as typed specializations; it cannot silently identify their objects.

Model-change operations also need their own semantics: changing a state representation is not necessarily changing the underlying world; admitting a new hypothesis differs from new evidence; changing an institution can change both action availability and observation production. Encoding all such changes into a large fixed meta-state is formally possible in bounded examples, but shifts expressive choices to the meta-language. It does not demonstrate ontological completeness or unlimited concept formation.

## Audit qualifications and rejected shortcuts

No contradiction was found in the checked elementary derivations. This does not endorse every proof or every large computation. The claim register often suppresses assumptions supplied in linked memos; those need to be carried into any synthesis. In particular U18's `h<1/2`, U17's discounting, U24's absorption assumption, and U26's complete-transcript condition matter.

Several past corrections were explicitly retained and must remain visible: a nonhyperbolic invasion join required a separate argument (U09); an initially predicted frame escape failed (U12); an archive-loss reset would have omitted information in visible decisions (U19/S02); evidence produced by readiness can be signaling rather than future test capacity (S03); a grant benchmark matches nominal conditional mean, not total spending (S05); and the S06 selected path does not demonstrate retesting after a within-horizon negative. These strengthen the record by narrowing claims. They are not evidence of a final complete theory.

Rejected unifications:

- “Everything spends the same resource.” Stocks, liquid funds, authority, information, error probability, and burden have different units and algebra. Information can be copied; spending a likelihood budget is not paying a bill. A common cost can be imposed by an explicit evaluation, but it is then part of that evaluation.
- “Any positive feedback is renewal.” A loop can finance coercion, errors, or destructive extraction. Net flows, identified dependencies, and the protected criterion must be checked.
- “All constraints are conservation laws.” U01 is a balance; U02 an identification limit; U05 a policy-existence theorem; U20 a sufficiency result; U26 a statistical distinguishability limit; N01 a normative commitment.
- “A theorem covering the pieces proves the pieces are jointly attainable.” Shared budgets, sequential payments, incompatible action choices, and non-tight information bounds obstruct conjunction. The two-test example makes this exact.
- “All evidence and evaluators are endogenous now.” Some financial and information flows are endogenous in S06; model family, objectives, repair technology, authority, and free belief retention are still supplied. Each model has a different boundary.

## Concrete next test

Construct two finite controlled models with a specified projection that preserves ordinary observed output and total cash but erases access permissions, evidence quality, or retained temporal order. Hold primitive physical production and initial resources fixed. Compute the exact attainable **vector** of (productive failure probability, inquiry expense, unwanted-intervention burden, response deadline probability) in both models; use no common scalar unless explicitly justified.

First test whether the proposed compressed signature preserves those vectors under every available intervention. The example above predicts failure when cash access is omitted. Then add only the missing typed relation and test restoration of equivalence. A second manipulation removes the recording/recognition feedback at matched actual expected spending and comparable information, to distinguish an endogenous mechanism from a static resource restriction. A discovered discrepancy refutes the proposed abstraction, not merely a parameter estimate. Success over a finite intervention family supports that precise transport claim, not universal completeness.
