# How inquiry renews its capacity to continue

5 October 2026. Eighth continuation of Ren's research on selection and the conditions for continued becoming. Ren requested the “next frontier” after the finding that a useful question need not appear as an anomaly in existing records. Three AI agents reviewed mathematics, implementation, and primary literature. This record contains conditional proofs and exact exploratory calculations, with no empirical institutional or deployed-AI validation.

The new result separates **the value inquiry produces, the credit an institution gives it, and its ability to continue operating**. These can move in different directions. A procedure can generate useful proposals and earn positive average funding, yet lose its future ability to investigate. Conversely, protecting its funding can preserve a procedure whose ongoing inquiry has negative value.

This cycle makes proposals arise from participants' local observations and makes funding depend on independently observed evidence. The recognition and allocation rules remain supplied; their consequences are endogenous, while the evolution or legitimacy of those rules is not yet explained.

## A conditional constraint on continued inquiry

Let b_t be the resources accessible to an inquiry service, bounded above by B. Each active round costs c>0 before any new credit arrives. A performance-funded rule gives

\[
b_{t+1}=\min\{B,b_t-c+G_t\}.
\]

If b_t<c, the service cannot investigate. This becomes permanent shutdown only if it has no funding or restart route available without another investigation. The rest of the institution may continue producing normally.

Suppose that, conditional on every surviving history, there is probability at least epsilon>0 of a sufficiently long funding shortfall over the next L rounds. Then

\[
\Pr(T>nL)\le(1-\epsilon)^n,
\qquad \Pr(T<\infty)=1,
\qquad \mathbb E[T]\le L/\epsilon.
\]

The proof conditions on successive surviving blocks: each has probability at most 1-epsilon of surviving the next. Independence is unnecessary. One sufficient mechanism is a uniformly possible run of no credited returns long enough to exhaust B. The condition concerns entire histories, not merely an unconditional frequency of unsuccessful rounds.

Positive mean income does not remove this constraint. With cost one, reserve cap two, and a return of three with probability one-half, uncapped expected income after expense is +.5 per active round. Nevertheless, two consecutive zero returns stop inquiry, and the exact mean lifetime is six rounds. At the cap, favorable returns spill elsewhere while losses reduce the accessible reserve. This does not claim positive reserve drift in every state.

The result applies across implementations once its resource, access, timing, and shortfall assumptions hold. It fails as a universal extinction claim if reserves can grow without bound, inactivity is costless and appropriate, rescue remains available, inquiry is required only finitely often, or long shortfalls cease to be possible. It is an application of established ruin theory: [Dickson and Waters](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0515036100013878) study related bounded-surplus processes and financing after ruin.

## Questions arise from local encounters

The implemented institution encounters independent new projects. Each project has two observable contexts, A and B. In the useful-project world, one has need probability .65 and the other .15; the orientation is redrawn fairly each round. A participant in each context observes four baseline outcomes. The proposed intervention targets the context with the larger observed count, provided that count is at least two; ties are fair. Some encounters produce no proposal. The institution never receives the simulator's hidden probabilities or correct context as an instruction.

This is generation within a supplied two-rule grammar. It does not invent an unforeseen concept. Projects genuinely differ, so the calculation does not create recurrent ignorance by deleting knowledge of a previously solved project. General beliefs may remain available; the specified procedure nevertheless requires fresh project-specific confirmation. It is not claimed to be the optimal learner using every possible form of prior knowledge.

The proposed response costs .4 and is assumed to prevent a unit harm perfectly. Sixteen fresh baseline outcomes in the nominated context test whether its expected benefit exceeds that cost. The exact binomial gate accepts at eleven or more need events, with conditional false-adoption probability at most .05 for a nonbeneficial candidate. Response efficacy is a structural assumption; observing need would not identify an unknown causal treatment effect.

After acceptance, a separate monitoring cohort of eight baseline/control outcomes estimates benefit. A monitoring average above .4 earns a positive credit. It is independent of the selection and validation samples. A grant therefore depends on fresh observable evidence, not the hidden true effect or an unavailable same-person counterfactual. The positive credit is noisy and is not labeled realized profit.

The intervention then operates on forty fresh cases, half in each context in expectation. The evaluator calculates the resulting expected operating benefit. That evaluation is used to assess the procedure, not supplied to the funding authority as an oracle.

## Finance is paid and consolidated

Each active round pays one unit for the inquiry service: local observation, screening, validation and monitoring capacity, and interpretation. This is a fixed retainer with reserved capacity; unused slots remain paid. A further .05 social loss represents disruption or contact burden. It is not deducted again from the cash account. Baseline operation during the reserved measurement intervals is common to all compared procedures.

Ordinary production supplies six units per round, including when inquiry is inactive. A recognized positive credit earns a grant of five. The remaining output and any overflow above the reserve cap return to the operating treasury. The exact branchwise identity is

\[
(b_{t+1}-b_t)+\text{treasury retention}+\text{inquiry expense}
=\text{ordinary output}.
\]

Transfers are not counted again as social consumption. The social comparison includes inquiry expense, inquiry burden, and the intervention's operating gain or harm. The fixed production floor and initial reserves are supplied resources; this cycle does not explain their ecological regeneration. Response financing is represented in the operating-loss comparison separately from the inquiry cash ledger.

The reserve cap covers everything the service can access under the rule. Treasury balances exist, but the performance-only institution may not draw on them without another credit. Thus its failure is partly a rule about access, even when the wider institution retains resources. If an institution can instead apply for a grant while inactive or reuse an earlier claim, the absorbing-shutdown assumption must be changed.

## Positive value and positive income can coexist with shutdown

Exact enumeration of the participants' counts, fresh validation, and independent monitoring gives a recognized-credit probability of .37325246 per active round. The procedure's quantities are:

| Quantity per active round | Expected value |
|---|---:|
| Grant before applying the reserve cap | 1.866262 |
| Grant minus the one-unit retainer, before the cap | +.866262 |
| Gross operating improvement | 2.087754 |
| Improvement after retainer and inquiry burden | **+1.037754** |

With initial reserve and cap both eight, the mean number of active rounds before shutdown is **98.6084**. After 100 projects, the probability inquiry remains active is .360377; after 1,000 it is approximately .00002015, or .002015%. Exact finite-chain calculations and independent high-precision checks agree.

The practical timescale matters enormously. With cap 32, expected lifetime rises to approximately **1.112 million rounds**, and the chance of remaining active after 1,000 rounds is **99.914%**. Both finite caps still satisfy the eventual-shutdown theorem. Almost-sure eventual failure therefore cannot be used as shorthand for imminent failure or an empirically important threat.

These numbers are properties of a declared artificial procedure and resource process. They are not predictions of research-laboratory lifetimes.

## What receives credit changes future capacity

A second ledger recognizes the same fresh monitoring evidence only when the proposal concerns context A. Candidate generation, validation, deployment, and social value per active round remain identical. The credit probability halves to .18662623, and average uncapped funding after the retainer falls to −.066869. At cap eight, mean lifetime falls from **98.61 to 20.36 rounds**.

This comparison isolates recognition from proposal quality. The procedure produces the same expected value while it operates, but the narrower ledger replenishes less of the shared inquiry capacity. It shows how an accounting rule can reduce later opportunities for both contexts. It does not model competing laboratories, evolving evaluation standards, or a strategic explanation of why the narrow rule arises.

An additional control redirects the single investigation slot to A with a specified probability, overriding the local evidence. Fresh validation remains intact. Increasing this diversion reduces useful proposals and the subsequent renewal of inquiry funds in the supplied useful-project world. This is an imposed agenda-diversion mechanism, not a demonstration of strategic capture emerging from incentives. Neither honest proposal error nor a noisy false positive is called capture.

## Paid continuity and restart are different achievements

A recurrent-allocation rule sets

\[
g_t=f+(5-f)I_t,
\qquad b_{t+1}=\min\{B,b_t-a_t+g_t\},
\]

where a_t indicates active inquiry and I_t a recognized fresh credit. With f=0 this is the performance-only rule. With f=1, the service receives its one-unit operating floor even without a credit, paid from the same ordinary production. Once funded it can operate continuously; from zero it spends one round inactive and then restarts.

The guarantee follows directly from a floor covering the expense. Its substantive assumptions are continued productive output, the allocation commitment, and access even during inactivity. It is not evidence that a protected service automatically generates its own support. With a smaller floor, paid accumulation can allow intermittent inquiry: a cost-two/floor-one control starting empty runs 49 times in 100 rounds. This preserves recovery without uninterrupted operation.

A further benchmark pays a constant grant of 5r=1.866262 each round. In the main useful-project case it maintains inquiry while matching the performance-only rule's **nominal expected grant per active round**. This isolates what predictable access can accomplish under the supplied parameters. It does not match capped deposits or cumulative expenditure after the other rule shuts down, and uses the known stationary credit rate to set the allocation. It is a benchmark, not a learned policy or a universally available equal-spending intervention.

## Continued inquiry can also be wasteful or harmful

The absent-benefit control gives both contexts need probability .2, below the .4 response cost. Over 1,000 projects, the paid floor keeps inquiry operating and adds expected social loss **1,050.04**. The performance-only procedure shuts down after about eight rounds and adds **8.40**. No inquiry has zero incremental loss by definition. Maintaining a function is not sufficient evidence that its use is valuable.

The near-null control, with both probabilities .39, shows another limit. Every validation test retains its per-project error guarantee, yet 1,000 protected inquiries produce **11.77 expected false adoptions**, alongside inquiry costs. With infinitely many independent attempts and nonzero error probability, false adoptions recur indefinitely. A per-test .05 guarantee is not a lifetime safety guarantee.

Correctly rejecting an unhelpful proposal, finding no actionable candidate, missing a useful proposal, and accepting a harmful one have different epistemic meanings. The implemented success-credit rule can leave all of the first three financially unrewarded. That is a property of its ledger, not a claim that negative results lack value. The computation records no-proposal, acceptance, credit, and useful/false-adoption rates separately; it does not assign a general value to knowledge beyond the supplied decisions.

## A separate limit on paying only for improvements

There is also a deterministic accounting constraint. Suppose successful revisions monotonically improve a bounded score V_t<=V_max, and each improvement is paid at most once at rate gamma:

\[
0\le G_t\le\gamma(V_{t+1}-V_t).
\]

Then total credited resources cannot exceed gamma(V_max−V_0). If every required inquiry round costs at least c, the number M that can be financed satisfies

\[
Mc\le b_0+\gamma(V_{\max}-V_0).
\]

Successful improvement can exhaust this particular funding base. Recurring services may instead need a share of continuing benefits, productive income, or an independently justified option reserve. The bound does not prove that inquiry should continue after its task is finished. Monotonicity, bounded total improvement, one-time payment, and continuing cost are essential: repeated new problems can create unbounded positive improvements despite a bounded score.

This is a separate telescoping argument. The implemented experiment has independent new opportunities and does not instantiate the bounded-total-improvement premise.

## Relationship to the research program and prior work

The previous cycle separated evidence admission from evidence production. This cycle shows how the consequences of that process replenish—or fail to replenish—the means for another round. A useful research question becomes: **under what conditions can a system continue producing warranted challenges to its current activity, including after uncredited but competent inquiry?**

No single scalar settles this. Financial persistence, information quality, useful action, and legitimate standing remain distinct. Nor does a permanently active provider define 生生: a function can be maintained through standby, restart, or replacement when continuous operation is unnecessary. The obligation to consider other people's harms is a normative commitment, not a consequence of the reserve equation.

The building blocks have substantial prior art. Alongside ruin theory, [Manso's innovation model](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/manso/mi.pdf) studies contracts that support exploration through failure tolerance and longer horizons. [Smaldino, Turner, and Contreras Kallens](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731693/) model research expenses, grant allocation, publication, and cultural selection of methods. Their financially depleted labs can still seek grants, a material difference from our absorbing performance-only rule. The current calculation uses observable proposal and evidence processes under fixed rules; it does not replace those models or establish novelty for funding-shaped scientific selection.

## The next target

The remaining tension is concrete: inquiry must be able to become quiet when it is unhelpful while retaining a feasible route to resume when conditions change. A constant funding floor alone cannot resolve that, and endlessly repeating a fixed test accumulates errors.

Next, join evidence-guided suspension and restart to a productive state that correction helps maintain. Replace the guaranteed six-unit income with explicit resource renewal; allow warranted negative results to inform when inquiry is used; and state a cumulative harm or error criterion. The target is to preserve recoverable inquiry and standing to reopen a question without making continuous expenditure, uninterrupted activity, or the survival of one provider the definition of regeneration. New concept formation, evolving evaluative purposes, strategic capture, and empirical calibration remain open.
