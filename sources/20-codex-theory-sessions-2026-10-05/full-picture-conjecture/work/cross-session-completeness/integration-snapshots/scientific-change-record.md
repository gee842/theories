## Implementations and checks

The [single-study program](revise_standard.py) generates a bounded rule edit, screens it on observed task reports, checks it on fresh reports, and enacts the result on prospective tasks. The record contains ten seeded study conditions and five transfer profiles. Six noiseless interaction orientations are checked exhaustively. The [exact external evaluator](exact_transfer.py) independently enumerates policy values across six task families and does not send those values to the learner.

The [three-phase program](repeated_revision.py) toggles the same requirement in either direction and carries the resulting rule into the next phase. Each phase executes its installed rule on 32 further tasks. Its sample sizes and error allocation are fixed before execution. The study schedule, edit grammar, task families, resource budgets, and routine feedback channel remain supplied.

Independent review checked the information interface, trial chronology, acquisition counts, cost ledger, fresh-sample boundary, range bound, exact interaction arithmetic, stochastic record replay, and actual postdecision execution. All three repeated-study records replayed, and instrumented checks confirmed that an unfunded phase acquires no evidence. The primary programs were also rerun from the delivered output directory. Verification of code establishes its stated consequences; it is not evidence that human investigators use the same rules.

The main numerical distinctions are preserved. Cash expenditure differs from prediction loss. A funded study need not be worthwhile. Benefits can depend on whether trial predictions count as useful services or research overhead. A future gain measured in the objective is not automatically spendable revenue. Conservative reserves and confidence bounds are not minimum possible financing or sample requirements.

## Corrections found during review

An early pilot implementation acquired labels before checking its small pilot budget. The affordability check was moved ahead of acquisition; an instrumented zero-budget case now obtains no observations. The reported default result did not change.

An early enlarged mixed-task check reused the prefix of a smaller check that had failed. It was replaced with a separately fixed, newly seeded study. The record does not treat those attempts as one lifetime .05 procedure. The later three-phase extension instead allocates .05 explicitly across its three fixed checks.

The implementation changes an admission and search prerequisite. It does not generate a new ultimate aim, new semantics outside the supplied feature language, or the outer evidential rule. The independently scheduled protected trial is an input; a closed institution does not spontaneously invent its own escape route in this model.

## Remaining explanatory work

The record now contains repeated local rule revision, but not a community that discovers when to initiate a rule review, finances it from continuing activity, maintains its own checking capacity, changes its purposes, and produces new instruments or representational primitives. The earlier laboratory and joint community proposals remain unrun.

The next discriminating empirical study should compare fixed access, externally changing access, and access changed by current allocation decisions. It should measure capability before later performance and separate evidence availability, interpretation, admission, authority, and rewards. Direct variation in restoration access is needed to test the proposed mechanism rather than attributing every effect of incentives to capacity.

Earlier reports and their research bundle remain preserved. The new synthesis supersedes their statement that standards revision is wholly unimplemented only within the narrow admission-rule scope established here.
