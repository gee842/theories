# How we notice possibilities our current model leaves out

Synthesis for Ren, 5 October 2026. This answers the question left by our [active-inquiry comparison](active-inquiry-findings.md), using the saved research, implementations, and evidence from other sessions. Source snapshots, exact checks, and review records are preserved in the accompanying [evidence record](missing-possibilities-evidence.json).

**We notice important omissions by maintaining ways to encounter, express, and test distinctions that our current working model does not yet use.** The distinction can come from joining previously separated observations, recombining familiar features, changing an experiment, or hearing an experience that the existing account excludes. Its importance is then assessed through consequences and evidence. A current-model anomaly is one possible trigger, but cannot be the only admission rule.

My earlier description of this whole question as unresolved was too broad across the research programme. The other sessions already provide an implemented, bounded route from records to a previously absent policy, and exact results explaining why that route can be blocked. What remains unestablished is a method guaranteed to generate every important possibility, including those outside all currently available observations and representational resources. We can give a constructive answer without claiming that guarantee.

## How a possibility enters

The key distinction is between the **working model**, the **language available for constructing alternatives**, and the **experience available to the inquirer**. These need not have the same boundaries.

In the existing discovery experiment, the working policy initially treats every case alike. The records preserve six binary attributes and their links to delayed outcomes. A generator constructs 73 possible groups: all cases, each single-attribute value, and each pair of attribute values. It ranks differences in observed failure rates, then proposes using a different action in a selected group. The correct group is not handed to that nomination procedure.

This is a concrete answer to where a new possibility can come from: **a relation assembled from observations that the current decision rule has not organized**. Construction and exploratory ranking make it a candidate. They do not establish that it is true, causal, or worth acting on.

The program consequently freezes the candidate and conducts a fresh randomized comparison of the two actions within that group. Its acceptance rule accounts for the extra action cost and statistical uncertainty. Separate gates determine whether supported evidence receives institutional standing and whether the response is implemented. Formation, validation, hearing, and action are different achievements.

The distinction is consequential in the artificial world. Always using action A has expected loss .2375 per case. Always using the more protective action B has expected loss .30, including its cost. But using B only in the hazardous group gives .1125, an operating gain of .125 over A. An aggregate A-versus-B choice misses that improvement. These values exclude research expenditure; the inherited evaluation separately accounts for it.

The protected-inquiry condition found and validated the planted distinction in all 256 hazard seeds. This is a strong, deliberately simple synthetic signal, not a general discovery rate. Our review reran the four inherited mechanism tests, recounted all 7,168 stored trajectories, reproduced their summary, and exactly replayed 56 selected trajectories. The 7,168 records contain paired conditions within 256 seed clusters, not 7,168 independent replications. In the no-hazard condition, search still produced apparent candidates, but none passed confirmation in those 256 protected runs; the unsuccessful inquiries still cost resources.

A real research precedent also exists. King and colleagues' robot scientist generated and experimentally tested hypotheses about yeast gene functions. That establishes a domain-specific precedent for constructed hypotheses and physical checking; it does not establish unrestricted invention. [The Automation of Science](https://research.aber.ac.uk/en/publications/the-automation-of-science/).

## Why waiting for a contradiction is insufficient

Suppose every record an inquirer can inspect has the same probability law in two possible worlds. Any rule using only those records has the same distribution of outputs in the two worlds. Additional computation cannot tell it which world it occupies. A randomized rule can investigate both, but cannot selectively recognize the important case from information that does not distinguish them.

*Questions without anomalies* supplies an exact example. Even if routine records contain counts, an endpoint, and the later outcome, those records have the same law in worlds where a proposed conditional response is beneficial and where it is harmful. Historical order alone also fails to separate the relevant pair. What distinguishes them is **linking the detailed history to its own subsequent outcome**. The omission concerns a relationship between records, rather than simply an absent field.

We independently re-enumerated this example with exact fractions. The candidate's gain, after its ongoing recording expense, is approximately −.008738 per decision in one world and +.018215 in the other. The routine observation laws are exactly equal; the linked observation laws differ. Research costs remain additional. Separate equality results for unlinked samples must not be combined into a claim about records already linked case by case.

A separate [four-case exact illustration](missing-possibility-check.json) shows how inspecting each of two attributes individually can miss a useful relation between them. It stipulates that protection works and reports operating losses before inquiry costs; it is an explanatory construction, not another empirical experiment.

This is a standard identification limit. Related domain-adaptation results construct tasks that are indistinguishable from the learner's input but require different predictions. They support the importance of the observation boundary, not a universal impossibility of learning. [Ben-David and colleagues, 2010, section 5](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/david10a/david10a.pdf).

The practical consequence is that a limited inquiry sometimes needs permission **before** the current account can demonstrate why its answer will matter. This does not require accepting the candidate in advance. It requires distinguishing permission to seek evidence from permission to believe or deploy.

## The language of alternatives must also be revisable

There is a further obstruction. An inquirer may keep all six attributes yet lack a way to express the useful combination.

We checked this with a new exact counterexample using the inherited experiment's actions and costs. Change the hazardous context from two specified attributes to four. The correct four-attribute policy has positive gain, but every one of the original 73 candidate policies has negative gain. More observations can make the rejection of those candidates more reliable without creating the absent candidate.

| Candidate language | Best candidate gain over always A in the four-attribute world |
| --- | ---: |
| Original language through two attributes | −.015625 |
| Expanded through three attributes | +.015625 |
| Expanded through four attributes | +.03125 |

Always A remains an available baseline with gain zero. These are exact operating-value calculations, not observed learning success with noisy data. The four-attribute witness is a new diagnostic in this continuation, not part of the inherited evaluation.

There is an explicit way to remove this particular obstruction: enumerate deeper combinations on a bounded schedule. Six binary attributes give 160 three-attribute predicates and 240 four-attribute predicates. Adding them does not require knowing which combination is correct. A suitable procedure reserves some candidate slots and testing resources for the expanded language, so existing candidates cannot consume every opportunity.

Crucially, it must not discard all extensions of an unprofitable simpler candidate. In this example, useful more specific groups refine candidates that are individually unprofitable. A rule demanding improvement at every intermediate step blocks their construction.

This supplies a computable alternative to “just consider more possibilities.” It proves that the useful expressions enter the candidate set. It does not prove that finite noisy data will rank them correctly, that a rare group will supply enough trial participants, or that the inherited test size has adequate power. Continuing through all conjunction depths on these six bits still cannot introduce an unmeasured variable, a different kind of relation, a new action, or a new conception of what matters.

## An operational answer

The following is our synthesis of the verified mechanisms and their limits. It is a proposed practice, not another benchmarked claim that a checklist improves reasoning.

1. **Inspect what the present account makes indistinguishable.** Look behind an average, score, category, or summary. Ask whether time order, interactions, local circumstances, or unrepresented people's experiences were erased. An omission is a candidate for attention, not automatically a mistake.
2. **Make a bounded change to how possibilities are produced.** Join an outcome to its original case; restore ordering; compose features at a greater depth; compare a differently situated case; or seek a new observation channel. Some inquiry must be able to start without an old-model anomaly. Specify its expense, burden, and stopping point.
3. **State the proposed difference and why it could matter.** For example: “Cases with the same current score may need different responses because of this interaction.” Distinguish a missing prediction from a challenge to the purposes or people represented by the score.
4. **Seek evidence that could separate the relevant accounts.** Use fresh confirmation after exploratory selection. If the claim concerns the effect of changing an action, use a suitable causal design. A test must be capable of exposing the rival explanation; repeating a test that both accounts predict cannot decide between them.
5. **Revise only as far as the evidence and feasible response warrant.** Preserve the finding and its conditions. Separate justified belief from authority and ability to act. Record rejected and inconclusive candidates. Stop or pause within the budget while keeping explicit conditions for reopening.

A useful initiating question is: **Which different situations do we currently treat as the same, and what observation could show that the difference changes what we should do?** A second question checks the generator itself: **Which kinds of distinction can our current way of asking questions never propose?**

An observation route does not need to be wholly independent of the current model. Existing knowledge can guide which experiments are feasible and promising. The restriction is on making the current model's own success criterion the sole gate through which a challenge must first pass. The amount of exploratory support still needs justification in terms of resources, risks, coverage gaps, and purposes; no universal exploration percentage follows from these examples.

Independent confirmation also has limits. In their account of Adam, its developers reported a supported-looking gene-function conclusion for which an omitted regulatory mechanism could explain the observations. Their model did not represent that mechanism. This is a historical report of the system's limitation, not a claim here about the gene's current classification. It illustrates why confirming a prediction does not establish that the causal account is complete. [Sparkes and colleagues, 2010, Adam's results](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1759-4499-2-1).

## What this means for the root of wisdom

The constructive answer is a capacity for **renewing the means of understanding**: maintaining encounters, records, ways to form distinctions, and opportunities to test and use them. A finite inquirer can possess procedures that generate descriptions it has not yet considered, and can enter new evidential situations. It need not contain those future descriptions as completed knowledge.

“Important” adds a further demand. A discovered distinction can change a prediction without changing the justified action. It can improve an action under one objective while exposing an omitted harm or obligation under another. The affected people's standing and the defensibility of the purposes cannot be derived from prediction accuracy. Our examples demonstrate parts of epistemic correction; treating them as a complete account of wisdom would repeat the original scope error.

We therefore have a substantive answer at the level of mechanism: **keep the working account open to richer encounters and richer ways of describing them, support bounded attempts to turn those differences into testable candidates, and preserve the route from warranted evidence to a defensible response.** Every version has a boundary. Finding a useful omission warrants a particular correction; it cannot certify that no important possibility remains outside the revised account.

The remaining research task is narrower than before: identifying which expansions of observation, language, and inquiry support work under particular costs and circumstances, especially when the relevant features and purposes are themselves unsettled. The answer already available should be used; its boundaries should remain available for revision too.
