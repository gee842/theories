# Active-inquiry pilot: independent design audit

Status: pre-case, pre-dispatch audit. The auditor received the proposed experimental structure but did not inspect any active-v2 case, key, participant prompt, response, or result. This is a methodological assessment and a set of recommended conventions, not a finding about any participant. Written 2026-10-05.

## What the proposed comparison can test

The proposal compares ordinary careful reasoning, an explicit finite decision/value-of-information procedure, and the proposed six-question discipline. Each condition has two fresh contexts; each context receives the same six episodes, with matched forward/reverse orders. Each episode permits at most two queries before a terminal decision. Public material specifies the finite prior, actions, losses, queries and their deterministic response functions, query costs, resource budget, and deadline. A fixed private world determines returned query answers. The coordinator serves those answers mechanically, without interpreting participant reasoning.

This can test whether the exact instructions improve choices in these finite, fully specified inquiry tasks under these interaction restrictions. It cannot by itself test discovery of an unknown ontology, representation invention, deep wisdom, ethical legitimacy, open-ended learning, or superiority over the general practice of ordinary reasoning. Supplying the full response functions makes the information structure available in advance; the challenge is selecting and using information, not discovering what kinds of information exist. That is a useful, narrower test than the first pilot.

The explicit decision-procedure condition is important. If the six-question discipline improves over ordinary instructions but performs similarly to explicit decision analysis, a plausible explanation is better procedural organization. Such a result would not establish a distinctive new foundation. If all conditions reach the ceiling again, that is evidence of poor discrimination at this difficulty, not equivalence of the methods outside these tasks.

## Freeze the decision problem

Before dispatch, freeze all cases, hidden worlds, orders, instructions, answer templates, scoring rules, and software versions or content hashes. Define these details unambiguously:

- Whether budgets constrain query cost, elapsed time, terminal action cost, or different resource dimensions. Do not silently equate money, time, and ethical harm.
- Whether querying consumes one unit of time or a case-specific duration, whether answers arrive at the beginning or end of that duration, and whether acting exactly at the deadline is permitted.
- Whether terminal actions consume time/resources and whether action feasibility or loss changes as the deadline approaches.
- Whether repeated queries are permitted, and whether already-known responses still incur their listed cost.
- Which quantities enter the minimized objective and which are hard constraints. A listed resource is not automatically part of the objective.
- What happens after an invalid query, malformed answer, exceeded budget, or missing terminal action. Apply a common predetermined rule; do not negotiate a repair for one condition.
- How exact ties are recognized. Rational arithmetic is preferable where available; otherwise use a small declared numerical tolerance and report it. Any action within the optimal set counts as optimal.

If there is a common comprehension or format-repair procedure, it must be fixed and identical across conditions. Repairing substantive mistakes with hints would alter the treatment. Record raw answers before any parsing or repair.

## Oracle and belief state

The oracle must know the public model and current observed history, not the private realized world. The state must include posterior belief, remaining queries, resources, elapsed time, and any other history-dependent action feasibility or consequences. It may compress history only when those variables retain all decision-relevant distinctions.

For a feasible terminal action a at public state s, let Q(s,a) be posterior expected terminal loss, including any terminal action costs included in the objective. For a feasible query q, let

    Q(s,q) = c(s,q) + sum_y P(y | s,q) V*(next(s,q,y)),
    V*(s) = min_u Q(s,u).

The continuation must cover every public-model response with positive conditional probability, not only the answer generated by the fixed world. The next state applies the budget/time changes and conditions the prior on the deterministic response. Query limits make this a finite dynamic program. Hard constraints remove infeasible actions; their enforcement is not left to hidden penalties chosen afterward.

The private world is used only to generate actual answers and actual losses. An oracle that selects an initial action using that world would unfairly convert uncertainty into clairvoyance. The hidden world must have positive prior support. A response impossible under the public model is a protocol or case error to investigate, not evidence that a participant reasoned badly.

## Recommended outcomes

### Primary outcome

Report whether each episode's initial choice belongs to the exact optimal set at its initial public state, and its initial Bellman gap Q(s,u_chosen) - V*(s). The binary result is readable; the gap shows how consequential a failure is. State plainly that this grades the chosen first move assuming optimal continuation, not the participant's unobserved complete future policy.

Compare conditions episode by episode and by the two matched orders. Publish the full small outcome table rather than only aggregate percentages. Initial choices are made before different query histories emerge and are therefore the cleanest common-state comparison.

### Secondary outcomes

Report terminal action optimality conditional on the information actually obtained. Keep conditional expected terminal loss distinct from the total of paid query costs plus conditional expected terminal loss. Report actual query costs/resources and realized terminal loss separately: realized loss depends on the selected hidden world and is not a fair substitute for expected decision quality.

At each visited state, the nonnegative Bellman gap is a useful local diagnosis. Its sum along the observed path is a trajectory-local diagnostic. It is not, on one selected path, an estimate of a participant's complete ex ante policy regret. The standard expected telescoping interpretation would require averaging coherently over the public prior and the whole induced policy's branches. Those branches have not been elicited here.

Once conditions make different queries, their later beliefs and feasible actions differ endogenously. Comparing terminal conditional losses directly can reward ignorance or punish a query that reveals a costly state. A participant's local gap is interpretable against its own state, but cross-condition terminal conditional loss differences alone do not establish better inquiry.

### Unnecessary-query wording

Do not count a query as unnecessary because its realized answer did not change the final action. A worthwhile query can rationally leave the action unchanged on many branches.

Prefer these explicit categories:

- Suboptimal query: its Q value exceeds V* by more than the frozen tolerance.
- Query when stopping is optimal: some immediate terminal action attains V*. A query tied for optimum is optional, not an error, unless a previously declared secondary resource preference breaks that tie.
- Query worse than immediate stopping: its Q value exceeds the best immediate terminal action's value. This can coexist with another valuable query; distinguish a poor query choice from a situation in which no query is worth taking.

Value must include continuation, all relevant costs, and feasibility. Two queries can be jointly useful even when either alone changes no optimal action; a myopic one-query score would misgrade such a case. Conversely, a promising first query can become useless when its follow-up is unaffordable or late.

## Experimental controls and limitations

Use fresh participant contexts without research history, keys, case-author commentary, or other participants' responses. Explicitly forbid all tools after the authorized prompt load. Present identical substantive cases, output requirements, resource allowances, and mechanical response templates. Only the treatment instructions should vary. Do not add hints, encouragement, method names in feedback, or adapt case order to observed performance.

The coordinator knows the condition and likely the key, so fixed rendering and mechanical parsing matter. Preserve exact dispatches and each response. If prompts differ substantially in length or prescriptiveness, document that: an observed difference can be caused by added scaffolding, salience, or attention allocation rather than the proposed philosophical content. Equal token caps do not imply equal reasoning effort, and voluntary shorter answers do not demonstrate greater efficiency.

Six episodes repeated in two contexts per condition do not constitute twelve independent replications of a broad reasoning ability. Cases recur, contexts share a base model, and multiple episodes in one context allow order and carryover effects. Reverse orders help reveal order sensitivity but do not eliminate it. Treat this as a small descriptive pilot; avoid significance language, population generalization, and selecting only favorable aggregates. Do not discard inconsistent runs without a predeclared protocol reason.

Using the same hidden world per episode across conditions is useful paired control for realized outcomes. A deliberately selected world is not a random draw from the prior, however. Report how worlds were chosen; do not interpret the frequency of realized losses as an unbiased risk estimate. Any future repeated-world experiment should separately sample or enumerate worlds and explicitly state whether it is eliciting the same policy or fresh policies.

## Scope and claims

Unsupported claims of completeness can be coded only from explicit participant text under a predefined rubric. Absence of such a claim is not evidence of a deep capacity to detect epistemic limits, especially when output requirements discourage prose. A separate scope-probing item would be needed to measure that capability directly and comparably.

All optimality judgments are conditional on the stipulated losses, prior, available actions, and constraints. These cases do not validate those goals or settle which losses deserve moral weight. They can expose an inconsistency between a chosen action and an accepted model; they cannot derive the legitimacy of the model from successful optimization.

Recommended interpretation language: “Under the stipulated finite decision models and these instructions, condition A made X of 12 optimal initial choices, compared with Y and Z. The cases and contexts are too few and too structured to establish a general reasoning advantage.” If there is a failure, classify its concrete source before interpreting it: arithmetic, posterior conditioning, omitted continuation, feasibility/deadline, format compliance, or unsupported scope. Do not relabel every mistake as absence of wisdom.

## Recommended next gate

Run an independent scorer check on small hand-solvable examples before reading participant outcomes, including a tie, a valuable two-query continuation, an unaffordable continuation, and a query that is optimal even when its realized answer leaves the action unchanged. Then freeze and dispatch. After results, prioritize explaining differences and remaining blind spots over expanding the philosophical claim.

The experiment is worth running if its narrower purpose stays explicit: testing whether a proposed discipline helps choose consequential distinctions under a finite cost and deadline. Even a positive result would leave open the broader question of how new distinctions and legitimate purposes are formed and revised.
