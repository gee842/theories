# Audit: what continuity through change can establish

Prepared for the current investigation, 5 October 2026. This note supplies an independent conceptual audit; it does not revise preserved outputs. The claims below are arguments and definitions developed here, without attributing them to a philosophical school.

## The central correction

“It remains recognizably itself” bundles at least four questions:

1. **Material retention:** how much of its earlier material remains?
2. **Organizational persistence:** which relationships, responses, or capacities continue?
3. **Historical continuation:** does the later occurrence develop from this earlier occurrence by the relevant ongoing process?
4. **Reidentification:** what evidence permits an observer to track that continuation?

These can come apart. A maintained pattern can replace its material. A faithful replica can preserve organization without being the same historical occurrence. A continuing object can undergo a large change in function. An object can continue when an observer loses track of it. A tracking algorithm's uncertainty is not automatically an object's disappearance.

The earlier maintenance example establishes material turnover together with convergence toward a positive organizational regime. That result is valuable. It does not, by itself, establish the numerical identity of a persisting token: independent runs, restarts, and copies can converge to the same regime.

## Minimal counterexamples to overly strong criteria

| Proposed sufficient condition | Counterexample | What the condition actually establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Same instantaneous internal state | Two identical copies at different locations | Qualitative agreement in the represented variables |
| Same input-output behavior | Two separate devices implementing the same response map | Functional equivalence over the specified interventions |
| Same attractor or basin | A first device is destroyed and a second converges to the same stable regime | Persistence or recovery of a type of behavior |
| Continuous physical material | The same material is rearranged into a device with entirely different organization | A material lineage, not every proposed organizational identity |
| Common causal ancestry | A parent and its two descendants | A lineage relation that can branch |
| Continuous parameter change | Two regimes are joined along a continuous path passing through loss of the defining capacity | Continuity of the selected coordinates, not preservation of the selected capacity |

The interruption case is informative rather than merely troublesome. A whirlpool stops when its sustaining flow stops; a switched-off machine may retain the disposition to resume its operation. They have different persistence conditions because their structures and capacities are different. There is no need to settle both with one undifferentiated use of “process.” A model can represent a continuing disposition during quiescence, but must provide its physical support rather than identify absence of observations with absence of the entity.

## Two precise obstructions

### Copies and state-only tests

Let the represented properties of two simultaneous tokens be identical: \(f(A)=f(B)\). Every proposed test \(g\) based only on those properties gives \(g(f(A))=g(f(B))\). No such test can establish which token is the historical continuation of an earlier token. Adding location, a material label, or a causal record can resolve particular cases, but adds relevant information; it does not rescue the sufficiency of the earlier description.

The same point can be expressed without insisting that physically perfect copies are available. If a description admits a permutation of candidate continuations that preserves all the data used by the criterion, those data do not select a unique continuation. Symmetry of an incomplete description is not a proof that the world has no fact beyond that description.

### Branching and numerical identity

Suppose one earlier token \(A\) gives rise to two later tokens \(B\) and \(C\), which are distinct at the later time. If both were numerically identical to \(A\), symmetry and transitivity of identity would imply \(B=C\), contradicting that premise. Therefore a branching continuation relation cannot simply be identified with numerical identity while retaining the usual logic of identity and the distinctness of the branches.

Three coherent responses remain available: specify a physically or practically justified preferred successor; say that the original ceases and has two descendants; or use a many-successor lineage relation without calling every successor numerically identical. Dynamics alone need not choose among these formulations, although it can eliminate formulations that contradict the actual history. Fusion produces the corresponding many-predecessor case.

## What a dynamic model contributes, and what it does not

For a well-posed deterministic system, the law and full initial condition select a unique trajectory. This supplies historical structure that an instantaneous resemblance test lacks. However, it initially tracks the state of the supplied system. If the question concerns an entity whose material, boundary, or membership changes, it remains necessary to specify which features or events along the full trajectory instantiate that entity.

This is not an objection to modeling. The entity criterion can earn its place by explaining recurring observations, surviving interventions, and predicting when the entity will lose a capacity. The choice of representation is constrained by what works. It is not transformed into a uniquely forced metaphysical classification merely by being useful.

Three distinct successes should therefore be reported separately:

- The law predicts material and causal history.
- A proposed entity criterion detects an enduring organization, with stated tolerances.
- The evidence identifies a unique continuation under that criterion.

## Strongest positive operational proposal

Treat a persistence claim as a **supported tracking claim** with four explicit ingredients:

1. **Subject and scale.** Specify what kind of occurrence is tracked, what observations or capacities distinguish it, and the time range and tolerance involved. The criterion should be justified by the question, not chosen after the result to ensure persistence.
2. **Physical support.** Identify the structure and activity that carry those capacities. Show how they respond to perturbation, replacement, or interruption. Recovery requires resources and conditions; include them where they matter.
3. **Historical linkage.** Establish a connected succession through the relevant carrying process. Similar endpoints alone are insufficient. Represent copying, splitting, fusion, and restart explicitly instead of silently identifying them with uninterrupted continuation.
4. **Uniqueness or declared ambiguity.** Where several successors satisfy the available evidence, retain the ambiguity or use the appropriate lineage relation. Where a unique successor is established, make the tracking claim at that supported level.

This is a research protocol, not a new physical law. Its strength is that each ingredient can fail for a different reason and can demand a different investigation. It supports objective findings such as “the organization can recover after this perturbation,” “the original material has been replaced,” “this is a copy,” or “these measurements cannot distinguish the successors.” It does not collapse them into an unearned universal yes/no about identity.

## A small exact example: when reidentification is guaranteed

Suppose objects have measured features in a metric space. At time \(t\), their feature points are separated by more than \(2\varepsilon\). Suppose independent physical knowledge warrants that each persisting object's feature changes by at most \(\varepsilon\) before \(t+1\). Assume no births, losses, splits, fusion, or measurement error over that interval.

Then every later point has exactly one earlier candidate within distance \(\varepsilon\). Existence follows from the motion bound for its actual predecessor. If two earlier points were both candidates, the triangle inequality would place them within \(2\varepsilon\) of one another, contrary to their separation. Therefore the data determine a unique matching.

This is an elementary sufficient condition for tracking, not a metaphysical definition. If separation fails, tracking might still be possible using further trajectory or mechanism information. With measurement error, the bound must be enlarged before claiming uniqueness. The law gives a way to improve or qualify the evidence rather than making uncertainty disappear.

## Recommended wording for the next report

Lead with the distinction between **what continues** and **how we know it continued**. Then demonstrate a maintained capacity and the conditions of its loss. Do not label recovery of one attractor “identity established” without the independent historical criterion.

A careful, substantive conclusion is:

> Some occurrences persist because an ongoing physical organization carries particular capacities through changes in material and state. Which occurrence continued is additionally a question about its history; matching appearances or functions alone cannot answer it.

This advances the inquiry without treating all entities as the same kind, treating every boundary as arbitrary, or claiming that continuity has already established monism. The productive next question is how a proposed carrying organization arises and becomes robust enough to support tracking in the first place.
