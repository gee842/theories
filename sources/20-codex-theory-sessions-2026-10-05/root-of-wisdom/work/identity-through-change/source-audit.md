# Maintained patterns, turnover, and identity: primary-source audit

## Recommended empirical anchor

**Bin Liu, Juntian Wu, Marc Geerts, Omer Markovitch, Charalampos G. Pappas, Kai Liu, Sijbren Otto (2022), “Out-of-Equilibrium Self-Replication Allows Selection for Dynamic Kinetic Stability in a System of Competing Replicators,” Angewandte Chemie International Edition 61(18), e202117605.**

- [Primary publisher text](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/anie.202117605)
- [Primary version-of-record PDF, University of Groningen](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/212550361/Angew_Chem_Int_Ed_2022_Liu_Out_of_Equilibrium_Self_Replication_Allows_Selection_for_Dynamic_Kinetic_Stability_in_a.pdf)
- [Bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35179808/)

Two competing replicators behave differently in closed and flow conditions. The faster replicator dominates under continued supply and removal; stopping both flows allows the slower competitor to prevail. Changing flow changes composition. This is evidence that an observed population regime depends on continuing production and removal. It is not evidence of atom-level turnover in each tracked individual, numerical identity, or irreversible extinction. The chemistry allows spontaneous nucleation, unlike a model with an exactly absorbing zero-replicator state. The mass-action model supports qualitative interpretation rather than a quantitative fit.

## Theoretical comparison: replacement plus limited recovery

**Gabriel Piedrafita, Francisco Montero, Federico Morán, María Luz Cárdenas, Athel Cornish-Bowden (2010), “A Simple Self-Maintaining Metabolic System: Robustness, Autocatalysis, Bistability,” PLoS Computational Biology 6(8), e1000872.**

[Full primary article](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000872)

The authors model interlocking catalytic reactions whose catalysts are themselves network products, with irreversible degradation. Fixed external substrates are supplied. A positive attracting state can coexist with collapse. Simulated removal of all forms of one catalyst can be repaired while other intermediates remain; removing the material needed to cross the activation threshold instead permits collapse. This is a deterministic theoretical construction, not a wet-laboratory demonstration. Its contribution is an explicit mechanism for replacement and some recovery, conditional on rate choices, remaining intermediates, and external resources. It does not supply its own spatial boundary or demonstrate identity of an individual.

Review scope: article abstract, introduction, model, stationary-solution section, bistability/hysteresis, recovery experiment description and Figure 6 caption, and stoichiometric discussion were read in full primary text. Equations represented as image placeholders were not independently reconstructed; do not report independently verifying its complete numerical model. Useful comparator for the local model, not a source to copy as a newly derived result.

## Experimental contrast: recurrence of a kind is not continuation of an individual

**Marta Tena-Solsona et al. (2017), “Non-equilibrium dissipative supramolecular materials with a tunable lifetime,” Nature Communications 8, 15895.**

- [Primary full text in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5520059/)
- [Primary author/institution PDF](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1440336/document.pdf)
- [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms15895)

Chemical fuel converts charged precursors into assembling anhydrides; hydrolysis restores the precursors. Chemical measurements, microscopy, scattering, and rheology connect changing chemical states to appearance and disappearance of assemblies. Adding fuel again can recreate particles with similar radii and lifetimes, including repeated cycles. Some assemblies inhibit their own degradation; some tested materials become kinetically trapped. Thus neither “fuel is necessary for every assembly” nor “all assemblies vanish as soon as fuel is exhausted” follows. Crucially, reappearance after dissolution supports repeatable formation of a kind of structure. It does not establish that the same individual particle returned.

Review scope: full indexed primary article text returned by web search, including reaction characterization, ink and colloid experiments, hydrogel discussion, discussion, and methods. A later direct PMC open returned a browser check; the review relies on the earlier returned full article text. Supporting information and raw data were not independently inspected. No new experimental replication performed.

## Application of the newest local mechanism work

Read-only source: `/Users/ren/theories/_process/research/2026-10-05-generative-foundation-review/final-synthesis/MECHANISM-MATH.md`, “Mechanism-to-persistence closure for a class of endogenous renewal systems,” dated 5 October 2026. Entire memo read. This is user-owned theoretical work; no claim of external peer review or empirical confirmation.

The relevant advance already exists in that memo: capacities obtain support; their use of support produces replacement capacity; capacities disappear; practices are inherited and copied. Conditional exact thresholds select positive attraction or collapse. It explicitly distinguishes absolute surviving capacity from composition: a limiting mixture is not a surviving population when both absolute stocks vanish. This is directly relevant to identity language: a ratio, shape, or description can remain well-defined analytically while the physical carriers required to instantiate it disappear.

The memo also proves an almost-sure extinction result for bounded finite populations with bounded birth intensity, uniformly positive baseline death, and no immigration, protected seed, or restart. A positive deterministic attractor therefore cannot be described as guaranteed endless persistence of each finite realization. This is a useful limit for any new cohort calculation.

Its causal boundaries are explicit: external feedstock, technology, conversion/loss laws, practices, inheritance fidelity, and copying mechanism remain supplied. It does not show a system maintaining all conditions of its own existence. The report should use “renewal of specified carriers under supplied conditions,” not unrestricted self-sufficiency.

## Interpretive recommendation

Separate four questions before introducing a philosophical synthesis:

1. Does a measured quantity or pattern remain approximately constant?
2. Which physical events maintain it while carriers are removed or changed?
3. Does present activity causally depend on earlier activity through a specified chain of seeds, templates, or other retained conditions?
4. What criteria justify calling the later occurrence the same individual rather than a descendant, reconstruction, or another instance of the same kind?

The papers and local equations contribute strongly to questions 1–3. They do not settle question 4 by replacing its words with “pattern,” “flow,” or “organization.” A useful next mathematical example can track old carriers and inherited lineage separately. The first can approach zero while the second persists; neither alone proves philosophical numerical identity. If all relevant propagating material is removed and spontaneous creation is excluded by the model, recurrence requires an external seed. This distinguishes a continuing causal chain from a reconstructed similar state without claiming that all identity is continuity.

No source here establishes that unity is ontologically prior to parts. The positive inference is narrower: some stable macroscopic properties are continually realized by changing carriers, and experimentally changing the sustaining process can change or terminate those properties.
