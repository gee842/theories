# Renewal of inquiry from observed credit

5 October 2026. Exploratory finite calculation for the eighth research cycle, written before the main calculation. A small parameter-development calculation checked that the selected proposal and test sizes produce positive gross expected rewards and positive social value in the useful-project world; these choices are not preregistered or an empirical sample. The full grid, negative controls, and costs are retained.

## Question and scope

Can inquiry with positive average net return still disappear when a capped reserve is replenished only by occasional credited successes? Does a paid recurring allocation preserve inquiry or permit restart? Candidate generation, verification, credit, and finances are separate. This is a specified institutional procedure, not the optimal learning or budgeting policy. Its recognition rule and productive-output technology are supplied. Questions arise from observations within a two-candidate grammar; unrestricted concepts, strategic capture, and changing purposes are not implemented.

## Independent new projects and situated observations

Each round is a new independent project. In the distributed world, one of two observable contexts A/B has need probability .65 and the other .15; which is which is redrawn fairly and independently each round. Other controls have both probabilities .2 or .39. Contexts each make up half the deployment population. Old evidence and learned generalities are not erased: project identity changes and the specified procedure requires fresh project-specific evidence. It can retain aggregate beliefs freely; no evidence acquired in another project reveals this project's hidden orientation. More efficient use of those beliefs is outside this fixed workflow comparison.

An active inquiry pays a retainer c=1 upfront. That retainer covers the participants' observation collection, candidate screening, validation capacity, independent monitoring capacity, and their interpretation. Reserved capacity is paid even when not used; this is a fixed operating charge, not a per-observation pricing model. A separate .05 expected loss represents disruption/contact burden of inquiry; it is scored socially but is not reserve cash or deducted twice. These normalized prices are assumptions, not calibration.

Each context's participant observes four independent baseline need outcomes. The candidate is the context with the larger count if at least one count is >=2, with a fair tie. Otherwise no proposal is produced. The procedure receives the counts and context labels, never the simulator's probabilities or hidden orientation. An agenda-diversion control overrides this selection with candidate A with probability kappa; otherwise it uses the data-derived proposal. This is an imposed diversion of a scarce investigation slot, not a learned strategy or fabricated test evidence.

The candidate proposes responding to the selected context. A response costs .4 and is assumed to prevent the unit harm perfectly. Fresh baseline need samples therefore identify its expected benefit given that supplied efficacy assumption. They would not identify an unknown causal response effect.

## Validation and separate observable credit

Use 16 independent baseline need samples in the selected context. Deploy only if the exact one-sided Binomial(16,.4) tail is <=.05; the critical count is 11. Each proposal is fixed before its validation cohort, and the gate is evaluated once. No reuse of discovery counts, optional stopping, or hidden true-effect test occurs.

After adoption, collect an independent monitoring cohort of eight baseline/control need outcomes in the selected context. They are kept distinct from the intervention population and precede any funding credit. Under the known perfect response assumption, count/8 minus .4 is an observable estimate of per-treated-person benefit. The standard ledger credits an adopted proposal if this estimate is positive. The narrow ledger additionally requires the selected context to be A. No actual realized same-person counterfactual is given. A positive credit is noisy and is not a proof of realized profit; the grant below is an institutionally chosen award, not automatic appropriation of a hidden true benefit.

Deploy on 40 new cases, half in each context in expectation. The evaluator scores gross expected operating improvement 40*.5*(p_selected-.4) after adoption. Actual need is not shown to the procedure as a hidden-score oracle. Group labels are common observable operational data; there is no differential deployment-record fee in this cycle. The retainer pays the added inquiry/monitoring service. Raw evidence acquisition and all response costs are included in the stated accounting. The baseline harms during the reserved discovery, validation and control-monitoring intervals are common to all procedures and cancel; the comparison does not give immediate earlier deployment to inactive arms.

A .05 level is a conditional per-project guarantee. Continuing infinitely many independent inquiries can produce infinitely many false deployments when the false-positive probability is nonzero. Report false adoptions and social loss per round and over the horizon, never a lifetime .05 safety claim. The .39 control tests this limit near the decision boundary.

## Funding reserve and output ledger

Ordinary production continues irrespective of inquiry and supplies six units of distributable output each round. This positive lower bound is supplied, not a result about an ecology regenerating itself. There is no borrowing. A cap of eight limits the institution's total liquid resources available to inquiry. Any spill returns to the operating treasury, from which the performance-only rule cannot draw absent a new credit.

With opening reserve b_t, inquiry runs if a_t=1{b_t>=1}. An active round incurs the retainer and may produce a credit I_t through the observed procedure above. No inquiry means I_t=0. A funding floor f in {0,1} gives the end-of-round grant

    g_t = f + (5-f)*I_t,
    b_(t+1) = min(B, b_t - a_t + g_t).

The full grant comes from that round's ordinary output and never exceeds five. The remainder and any reserve spill stay in the treasury. The pathwise monetary conservation identity is

    (b_next - b) + treasury_retention + inquiry_expense = ordinary_output.

Transfers themselves are not additional social consumption. Total incremental social loss is inquiry retainer + inquiry burden - operating improvement. Future treasury consumption and terminal reserve uses are not given a separate utility bonus. Initial reserves are the same supplied stock for each comparison; output and reserves are distinguished from response loss accounting. Equal maximum grants and output technology do not mean equal realized funding or expenditure.

With f=0, insufficient funds cause absorbing inquiry shutdown because only inquiry can generate another credit. The wider institution keeps producing; this is loss of the investigation function, not collapse of all production. With f=1, an active account can always finance the next round, and a zero account receives enough to restart the following round. This conclusion requires available output and enforceable transfers. A recurrent grant below the cost can permit intermittent restart without uninterrupted inquiry; a two-half-unit control checks this distinction.

Two comparisons isolate mechanisms. A constant grant mu=5*r, where r is the exactly calculated credit probability per active round, matches the performance-only grant's uncapped conditional mean; it is paid every round from the same output. This benchmark uses the known stationary r to set funding, so it is not an implemented learning rule. It has no stochastic funding variance. A frozen no-inquiry arm measures the cost/benefit of asking, especially in the absent-benefit worlds. Realized cumulative expenditure differs after shutdown.

## Exact calculation and controls

Enumerate all local counts and integrate exact independent validation/monitoring probabilities. This produces per-active-round proposal, acceptance, false-adoption, credit, and social-value quantities. Credit events are iid across fresh projects while active. A finite Markov kernel on reserve 0..B then computes the full reserve distribution each round, active probability, cash flows, expected useful/false adoptions, and social loss. For f=0, invert I-Q on active states to obtain expected time to inquiry shutdown and inspect the residual. No stochastic sampling is needed.

Use caps 1,2,4,8,12,16,24,32, both floors, standard/narrow recognition, and kappa=0,.25,.5,.75,1, preserving all cases. Record results at horizons 10,100,1000. A fixed two-period toy with cap2, expense1, reward3 with probability1/2 verifies positive uncapped expected net income alongside mean lifetime6 and certain eventual shutdown. Boundary controls include no credit, certain credit, zero initial reserve, below-cost floor with restart, no proposals, and near-null false acceptance. Controls of the financing theorem distinguish zero upkeep, unbounded accessible reserves, contingent external rescue, and productive-output droughts; where not implemented these are analytic limits rather than simulated findings.

The theorem and the exact chain must not confuse positive uncapped expected income with positive drift at a full capped account. Prove eventual shutdown separately from practical finite-horizon risk. Preserve exact code/protocol/config/test/analysis snapshots and hashes. The main calculation refuses to overwrite results.
