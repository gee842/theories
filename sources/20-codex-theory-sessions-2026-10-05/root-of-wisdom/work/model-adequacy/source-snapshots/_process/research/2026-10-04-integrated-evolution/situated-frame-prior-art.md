# Situated frames, endogenous evidence, and the limits of learning: prior-art audit

Research memo, 5 October 2026. AI-generated audit for the next shared-purpose experiment. Primary papers were consulted directly where linked below; manuscript versions are identified. This is a focused comparison and a set of elementary benchmark deductions, not an exhaustive priority search or a new empirical study.

The audit reads the [situated correction framework](../../../experiments/evaluator-succession/SITUATED-CORRECTION.md) together with the [research hub](README.md) and [research log](RESEARCH-LOG.md). Their current target distinguishes beliefs within a supplied representation from the formation of observations, model classes, admissible evidence, conceivable responses, and purposes. The initial experiment holds evaluative commitments fixed to isolate epistemic mechanisms.

## Main finding

The following phenomena already have close formal precedents: correct predictions about repeatedly experienced outcomes alongside incorrect counterfactual beliefs; beliefs shaped by the actions they induce; rational cessation of experimentation; common-purpose communities ending informative inquiry; and discovery processes that stabilize in incomplete conceptions. Claiming any of these broad phenomena as newly established would overstate the contribution.

The strongest prospective extension is narrower: couple the formation and revision of frames to the **maintained, financed, and authorized production of consequential evidence and responses**, with finite opportunities for correction. Then test which intervention changes the causal chain. This is a proposed contribution direction, not a verified novelty claim.

## Closest mathematical precedents

### 1. Self-confirming equilibrium: correct along the observed path

[Fudenberg and Levine, *Self-Confirming Equilibrium* (1993)](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/sce.pdf) define strategies that are optimal given beliefs, while requiring beliefs to be correct along the path actually generated. Beliefs about unvisited contingencies may remain incorrect. Repeating the same behavior then need not expose a contradiction. This is the direct precedent for the situated framework's distinction between familiar prediction and counterfactual judgment.

[Fudenberg and Kreps, *Learning in Extensive-Form Games I: Self-Confirming Equilibria* (1995)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0899825605800168) connect stable points to learning from observed play under asymptotically myopic best responses. Unvisited parts of the game need not be learned.

**Usable boundary.** A self-confirming equilibrium is not automatically a dynamically attracting state. Existence, compatibility with observations, convergence under a particular learning rule, and resilience to experiments are separate claims. The game and its possible contingencies are still supplied by the analyst; this does not explain the generation of new concepts or purposes.

### 2. Berk–Nash: the evidence distribution is endogenous

[Esponda and Pouzo, *Berk–Nash Equilibrium* (2016; revised 2019 manuscript, v4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.1152v4) fit possibly misspecified models to consequences generated by strategies. Theorem 2 makes stable behavior under optimal myopic policies a Berk–Nash equilibrium; it does not guarantee stability. The forward-looking extension requires weak identification.

**Version correction, 5 October 2026.** This audit initially used the 2015 manuscript. The [v4 revision notice](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.1152v4) flags a corrected Theorem 3 (printed p. 26). For an equilibrium \(\sigma\) of a perturbed game weakly identified at \(\sigma\), fix supporting beliefs \(\bar\mu^i\). Priors must satisfy \(\mu_0^i(\cdot\mid\Theta^i(\sigma))=\bar\mu^i\), where \(\Theta^i(\sigma)\) is player \(i\)'s KL-minimizer set. For each \(a>0\), an asymptotically optimal policy then gives convergence probability greater than \(1-a\). This qualified existence result supplies neither a general convergence guarantee nor probability-one convergence for a single policy. Use the corrected statement, rather than broader summaries elsewhere in the manuscript.

A single-agent specialization makes the mechanism transparent:

\[
\Theta^*(\sigma)=\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}
\sum_a\sigma(a)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P^*(\cdot\mid a)\Vert P_\theta(\cdot\mid a)).
\tag{1}
\]

Equilibrium requires optimal action given a belief supported on \(\Theta^*(\sigma)\). An action assigned zero weight contributes no fit penalty. If truth lies outside \(\Theta\), even experimentation with every action need not eliminate error.

**Usable boundary.** Best fit among permitted models need not mean accurate fit to observations. Exact on-path calibration belongs to special cases, not every Berk–Nash equilibrium. A fixed model class formalizes misspecification, not endogenous representation formation.

### 3. Active learning: patience can help experimentation without guaranteeing convergence

[Fudenberg, Romanyuk, and Strack, *Active Learning with a Misspecified Prior* (2017)](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/Active%20learning%20with%20a%20misspecified%20prior.pdf) analyze Bayesian control with two subjectively possible states and noisy action-dependent payoffs. Proposition 1 supplies conditions under which sufficiently patient agents avoid uninformative actions. Proposition 2 shows that a strictly myopically optimal uninformative action remains optimal for sufficiently impatient agents. Their three-action example has convergent myopic beliefs but nonconvergent beliefs for sufficiently patient agents.

**Usable boundary.** More patience or experimentation is not a general theorem of convergence, truth, or better realized outcomes under misspecification. An action can be informative about the distinctions in the agent's model while those distinctions fail to represent the actual mechanism.

For genuinely stateful environments, [Esponda and Pouzo, *Equilibrium in Misspecified Markov Decision Processes* (2021)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/TE3843) further show why replacing active learning with a fixed equilibrium belief can fail. Their sufficient conditions include subjective staticness, identification, or full support over states in the limiting distribution. The full learning dynamics cannot always be suppressed into an equilibrium description.

### 4. Endogenous attention can be tested, but attention is not awareness

[Caplin and Dean, *Revealed Preference, Rational Inattention, and Costly Information Acquisition* (2015; author manuscript)](https://www.columbia.edu/~md3405/Pub_Paper_8.pdf) characterize when state-dependent choice data can be rationalized by optimal costly information acquisition. Given the specified prior and utility, their Theorem 1 uses two necessary and sufficient conditions: no improving action switches given acquired information, and no improving cycles that reassign information structures across decision problems.

**Usable result.** Costly attention is not an unrestricted explanation that can rationalize every pattern once the relevant primitives and data are specified. This offers a way to distinguish information-cost explanations from other failures.

**Boundary.** The state space, consequences, and relevant choice problems are represented. Economizing on observation within that space does not explain inability to conceive a category outside it. Choice data alone should not be relabeled evidence of coercive goals, cognitive failure, or unavailable authority without discriminating those mechanisms. A rationalization by some information cost also does not identify the actual institution producing that cost.

### 5. Unawareness and discovery: conceptions themselves can stabilize

[Schipper, *Discovery and Equilibrium in Games with Unawareness* (2021)](https://faculty.econ.ucdavis.edu/faculty/schipper/sceunaw.pdf) models discovery as transitions between extensive-form games with differing awareness. Proposition 2 establishes eventual self-confirming games for its discovery processes. Theorem 1 supplies a rationalizable discovery process leading to a self-confirming game with a corresponding rationalizable self-confirming equilibrium.

The mechanism has important restrictions: the analyst supplies a finite collection of possible representations, awareness expands monotonically, and stationary discovery strategies govern transitions. Absorption follows from this finite ordered structure. It need not imply full awareness. The underlying payoffs remain common across the successive game representations.

**Usable boundary.** This is an unusually close precedent for stable limits on revision, including changes in what agents know is possible. It does not establish that real conceptual development must terminate, nor explain transformations outside the analyst's catalogue, forgetting, changing purposes, or the material maintenance of a discovery channel. A stochastic escape route or unbounded representation space can invalidate the absorption argument.

### 6. Common-purpose collective learning can still stop producing useful evidence

[Bala and Goyal, *Learning from Neighbours* (1998; linked 1996 repository manuscript)](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/1362/eeb19960111120062.pdf) explicitly give agents common preferences. They learn from their own and neighbors' experienced outcomes. They do not infer additional information from neighbors' action choices as fully Bayesian observers of those decisions would.

Their Theorem 3.2 establishes equal limiting utilities in connected societies under the model's assumptions; equality need not mean optimality. Their countably infinite “royal family” example adds a small universally observed group to local neighborhoods. Unlucky public results can make everyone abandon a superior informative action, ending further learning. Removing those links restores complete learning in the compared example. Their sufficient conditions for complete learning use local independence and restrictions on beliefs or informativeness.

**Boundary.** Common interests, connectivity, and substantial available evidence do not individually imply correct collective learning. More communication can change experimentation itself. These are specified counterexamples and conditional theorems, not a recommendation to reduce communication generally.

[Zollman (2007)](https://www.jingyiwu.org/uploads/1/3/8/0/138010336/zollman_2007.pdf) develops the epistemic-community interpretation through simulations, including reliability–speed comparisons across networks. Those findings are simulation results for the stated learning rules, not universal graph laws or proofs that diversity is always beneficial.

## Two small benchmark results to keep the mechanisms separate

The following calculations are elementary special cases, supplied as usable checks for a new experiment. They are not presented as novel results from the cited papers.

### A. No conflict, no misspecification, but rational non-experimentation

Every decision maker has the same declared objective: maximize discounted rewards. A familiar action yields one every period and reveals nothing about the context. An alternative yields two in context \(H\) and zero in context \(L\). Its first use reveals the context perfectly. The common prior is \(p=\Pr(H)\in(0,1/2)\), so the true context is represented. The first trial has an additional one-time setup cost \(c\ge0\); subsequent operation has no setup cost. Discount future rewards by \(\beta\in[0,1)\).

Always choosing the familiar action has value

\[
V_S=\frac1{1-\beta}.
\]

Trial now followed by the known optimal action has value

\[
V_T=2p-c+\frac{\beta(1+p)}{1-\beta}.
\]

Because waiting yields no information and leaves this stationary decision unchanged, an optimal policy can be chosen to test immediately or never test. Testing is strictly preferred exactly when

\[
\boxed{\beta>\frac{1-2p+c}{1-p+c}.}
\tag{2}
\]

At equality the policies tie. With \(p=1/4\), the threshold is \(2/3\) when \(c=0\), and \(6/7\) when \(c=1\). Below the threshold, everyone can rationally select the familiar action forever. In true context \(H\), realized predictions about that chosen action are perfectly accurate while the superior alternative is never discovered through use.

This illustrates endogenous evidence without opposed private interests, a missing hypothesis, or a malicious evaluator. It also shows why calling every untested possibility a suppressed truth is wrong: in context \(L\), the same trial carries real cost without improving subsequent action. Additional access to free, reliable, consequential information and a forced costly experiment are different interventions.

### B. Exploration cannot repair an excluded model by itself

Let the familiar action's reward be Bernoulli with known mean \(.5\). The alternative's true mean is \(.8\), but the agent's subjective family restricts it to \(\theta\in[.1,.4]\). All outcomes have positive likelihood, so Bayesian updating remains defined. Without trials, the familiar action is locally calibrated and strictly preferred within every contemplated model.

If external trial opportunities generate a positive limiting share of alternative observations, the model fitting problem contains

\[
\min_{\theta\in[.1,.4]}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\operatorname{Bern}(.8)\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(\theta)).
\tag{3}
\]

Its unique minimizer is \(.4\), since the divergence decreases up to the true mean \(.8\). With a regular prior having support throughout the interval, posterior mass concentrates near that boundary. The best permitted forecast remains worse than the familiar action. More trials can expose an empirical misfit without creating a better representable explanation or altering the policy after externally required trials end.

This distinguishes a model-class intervention from an exploration intervention. It is still misspecification within an analyst-supplied catalogue. Merely adding \(.8\) by instruction would demonstrate the effect of model expansion, not explain how an institution invents or admits the needed distinction. A negative-control environment where the alternative truly has mean \(.2\) should also be included; trials then incur an opportunity cost of \(.3\) per use before any separate test cost.

## An exact sufficient-learning condition and what it leaves open

For a minimal well-specified benchmark, let actions \(A\), admitted observations \(Y\), and hypotheses \(\Theta\) be finite. The true hypothesis \(\theta^*\) is in \(\Theta\) and has positive prior mass. Conditional on the chosen action and the past, the next recorded outcome follows the stationary law \(P_{\theta^*}(\cdot\mid a)\). All model probabilities are bounded away from zero. Actions may depend on all past evidence. Suppose their empirical frequencies converge almost surely to \(v(a)\), and every wrong hypothesis satisfies

\[
K_v(\theta)=\sum_a v(a)
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\theta^*}(\cdot\mid a)\Vert P_\theta(\cdot\mid a))>0.
\tag{4}
\]

Then posterior consistency follows:

\[
\frac1T\log\frac{\mu_T(\theta)}{\mu_T(\theta^*)}
\longrightarrow -K_v(\theta)<0,
\qquad \mu_T(\theta^*)\longrightarrow1.
\tag{5}
\]

**Proof sketch.** The log posterior odds equal initial log odds plus the sum of log likelihood ratios. Conditional expected increments are minus the action-specific KL divergences. Boundedness makes the martingale-difference average vanish almost surely. Convergent action frequencies give (5); there are finitely many wrong hypotheses. Hypotheses with zero prior mass can simply be omitted from the odds comparison. ∎

This is a standard finite-model consistency argument, restated to make the experimental requirements explicit. Positive asymptotic sampling of every action is sufficient when those actions jointly identify all hypotheses, but it is stronger than necessary. The theorem does not say that every kind of experimentation is informative, affordable, or timely. It assumes representability, correct likelihoods, sufficiently discriminating admitted observations, and stable conditional laws.

In particular, let an evidence-admission rule \(C_E(y\mid z,a)\) map raw experiences \(z\) into recorded observations \(y\). The relevant model is

\[
P_\theta^E(y\mid a)=\sum_z C_E(y\mid z,a)P_\theta(z\mid a).
\tag{6}
\]

If that rule merges the consequential distinction, (4) may vanish even when raw trials distinguish the contexts. Repeating an experiment and admitting its diagnostic result are different mechanisms. If a model changes its likelihoods or evidence rules over time, the fixed-model proof must be replaced rather than reused without checking.

A positive trial frequency with a nonzero per-trial resource cost also requires indefinitely renewed resources over an infinite horizon. Finite reserves alone cannot finance that condition forever. Decaying exploration, reusable measurements, safe parallel trials, and learning from genuinely independent experience can change the resource requirement, but need their own guarantees. Asymptotic identification also does not imply correction before a finite irreversible deadline; the [observability and correction memo](observability-and-correction.md) supplies that separate constraint.

## What the next experiment should claim and discriminate

The shared-purpose experiment should avoid treating all cognitive restrictions as one noise parameter. Separate at least the following comparisons:

| Mechanism held back | Intervention that directly addresses it | Comparison that would not identify it |
|---|---|---|
| Needed action is known but not tried | Paid, permitted trial with recorded outcomes | More data from the familiar action |
| Relevant distinction is absent from the model | A mechanism allowing a new distinction or causal model to enter and be compared | More Bayesian updates inside the same restricted family |
| Diagnostic experience is not admitted | Change the stated evidence rule while holding raw exposure fixed | Additional raw reports that are filtered identically |
| A response cannot be conceived | A discovery/proposal mechanism with explicit inputs and costs | Random choice among the current menu |
| A supported response cannot be implemented | Effective authority and resources, independently varied | Better forecasts alone |

Track current-policy calibration separately from counterfactual prediction, admitted diagnostic information, proposal generation, correction probability before a deadline, trial burdens, and renewal of the inquiry capacity. Expose the initial priors and frame contents. Include cases where the alleged hazard or proposed improvement is absent, so protected inquiry is not beneficial by construction. A route for investigation should not automatically compel adoption of every dissenting proposal.

Changing appointment policy alone has an exact null when candidate frames, beliefs, purposes, and decision rules are identical. Distinct experiences or representations can break that null without introducing private rent seeking. That extension is useful for the benchmark but is not itself a new general theorem about institutions.

## Novelty boundaries and the north star

**Already occupied:** on-path confirmation with off-path error; endogenous sampling and pseudo-true model fit; optimal non-experimentation; common-interest collective lock-in; discovery leading to incomplete self-confirming conceptions; costs of acquiring information; nonconvergence under misspecification.

**Potentially useful contribution requiring further comparison:** a tractable model in which institutional choices jointly maintain or degrade investigation resources, evidence admission, model/proposal revision, and actual correction authority; a result showing when a viable route for revision exists but is not reproduced by the institution's own selection and learning processes; and experiments that distinguish those mechanisms at matched present performance.

**Not established here:** a complete account of how purposes arise, a scalar metric of openness, a universal benefit of dissent or experimentation, a general attractor theorem for society, or a guarantee that analyst-supplied categories include what future participants will need. The listed priors, utilities, categories, and hypotheses remain local modeling choices.

The orienting question retained from the situated framework is scientifically productive because it demands a causal route: which consequential experience can be generated, become an admissible reason, alter a representable proposal, and lead to an authorized response while correction remains possible? The literature shows that each link can fail without opposed purposes. Connecting those links to their own material and institutional renewal is the current research opportunity, rather than replacing them with an instruction to maximize information or openness.
