# Critical synthesis: noticing a consequential possibility absent from the working model

Independent audit, 5 October 2026. This memo reads the preserved results of other research sessions. It does not claim to have rerun their experiments or independently verified every numerical result. Its conclusions distinguish exact information constraints, finite constructed mechanisms, practical proposals, and normative commitments.

## Proposed answer, with priorities

**1. The strongest answer is a mechanism, not an instruction to think harder:** preserve a bounded route from encounters the current account does not organize, through candidate formation and new evidence, to warranted revision. That route must be allowed to begin before its specific result is known. The current model can inform its budget and risk limits; it cannot be the sole source of evidence that every candidate deserves investigation.

This corrects an important circularity. “Look for anomalies” assumes the recorded variables and comparisons can express the omitted distinction. “Consider alternatives” assumes a generator able to supply them. “Investigate when the expected value of information is positive” assumes a supplied hypothesis/experiment family and an adequate value model. Each is useful within its scope. None, alone, explains entry into that scope.

**2. A missing possibility becomes noticeable through a change in the encounter, the retained relation, or the question—not only through worse fit of the old model.** A local report can introduce a distinction absent from central records; linking delayed outcomes can make an interaction visible; reconsidering the order of events can make the same truthful history support a new explanation; a bounded intervention can expose outcomes routine action never produces. These are specified routes, not a guarantee that any one will find the important omission.

**3. “Important” must remain separate from “novel,” “predictive,” and “profitable.”** A distinction matters for a stated decision when plausible resolutions change what should be done under the relevant consequences and constraints. An affected person's previously unrepresented harm may instead challenge the consequences and constraints themselves. No fixed optimization automatically settles that normative revision. A more accurate exploiter remains a counterexample to identifying this capacity with wisdom.

**4. Continued noticing depends on material and institutional conditions.** Exposure, record linkage, candidate generation, independent checking, standing to contest, and timely enactment are different links. Their costs, timing, and authorization can be complementary. Protecting endless inquiry or a particular provider is not the target; preserving a feasible route to resume warranted correction is.

## Why an anomaly-only answer fails

The clearest existing result is QWA lines 11–22. Let D include the entire observation transcript actually available to an investigation gate and let g(D) be its admission probability. The difference in admission probabilities between two worlds cannot exceed the total variation distance between their laws for D. Equal laws force equal admission probabilities. Thus a gate that seldom investigates in a world where a candidate is useless must also seldom investigate in an observationally identical world where it is useful.

This is stronger than an example of poor attention or insufficient computation. No computation on that interface can identify the missing distinction. Its constructive implication is to alter the information channel or permit limited investigation without prior identification. It does not imply that every unfamiliar proposition deserves the same budget, nor that some particular alternative is true.

The four-world construction gives both directions of the counterexample. World U has no historical anomaly under the available archive yet supports a useful new conditional response. World T has a historical anomaly without useful response consequences. See QWA 26–43 and 81–92; claim register U22/S04, lines 183–205. This must be retained as a negative control against treating “surprise” as “importance.”

The boundary also applies to “keep all the data.” Full historical windows without their later linked outcomes do not identify future usefulness in the stated example. More storage of the same interface cannot add the missing relation. Conversely, if those links are already present, the claimed impossibility no longer applies. Actions, silence, inherited beliefs, testimony, and ordinary consequences cannot be omitted from the transcript merely to manufacture ignorance; RIE 11–15, 109–133 and RI 32–47 explicitly warn against that mistake.

## The smallest operational alternative

The following procedure is a proposed practical synthesis. Existing experiments implement parts of it, not the whole procedure or an unrestricted discovery algorithm.

1. **Name the working account and its boundary.** State the decision, measured variables, compressed summaries, sampled people/settings/actions, time window, and declared consequences. Name at least one way its records are produced by its own choices. This describes what the present evidence can certify.
2. **Make one bounded attempt to change the encounter or retained relation.** Inspect a raw case behind an aggregate; preserve a delayed outcome's identifier; hear a differently situated participant; sample an unvisited setting; or run a small, reversible intervention within acceptable burdens. Choose based on a concrete coverage gap, consequential exposure, or conflict of testimony where available. A small scheduled exploratory allowance remains possible when none produces a current anomaly.
3. **Express the candidate as a contrast with a consequence.** “If this omitted distinction matters, cases/actions X and Y should differ in observation Z, and that difference could change response A.” Keep the hypothesis provisional. If it is a claim about an unrepresented burden or right, identify whose claim it is and which evaluation rule it contests; do not disguise the disagreement as a missing coefficient.
4. **Check with evidence not already used to choose the candidate.** Specify the observation or trial that distinguishes the relevant alternatives, its costs and deadline, and what would count against it. Use an appropriate valid test or careful qualitative confrontation; independent confirmation is the implemented finite benchmark's protection against searching noise. A useful association alone does not establish that an intervention helps.
5. **Separate the resulting decisions.** Was the candidate generated? Did the evidence support it? Can the evidence be heard? Is a response authorized, affordable in sequence, and timely? Each answer can differ. Record a negative or inconclusive result and the conditions for reopening rather than automatically extending the inquiry.

This reduces a vicious regress without pretending to abolish all assumptions. The first probe need not prove the unknown answer; it needs a limited justification for changing an inadequate or unexamined coverage boundary. Budgets, acceptable investigation burdens, and rights to raise questions are explicit prior commitments. Its operational deliverable is one contestable candidate and a discriminating observation, not a declaration of universal openness.

## What the other sessions already demonstrate

**Candidate formation and fresh confirmation.** DBR 25–51 describes a generator that searches 73 predicates on linked discovery records, then freezes a proposed conditional policy and tests it in a fresh randomized trial. The planted conjunction is not supplied to the learner. This is a real implemented route from experience to a new model distinction; both raw attributes and the finite predicate grammar are supplied by the analyst. It therefore supports bounded discovery, not unanticipated concepts in an unrestricted sense. Noise generates apparent candidates even in the absent-hazard world; four unsuccessful confirmation attempts still cost 150.49 units per run (DBR 131–137). Evidence of generative capacity must include false candidates and inquiry costs.

**Record preservation is model-relative.** IEMR 11–50 constructs two truthful histories with the same endpoint and total changes but opposite implications after temporal models are admitted. Timed counts suffice in that family, so raw preservation is not uniquely required. IEMR 72–84 distinguishes free gross information value, costly preservation, and introduction of excluded model support. This suggests preserving provenance and selected potentially useful relations rather than assuming either current sufficient summaries or maximal retention are universally correct.

**Exposure and uptake can reinforce exclusion.** FRF 21–37 and 39–80 specify an activity/retained-understanding feedback with an attracting exclusion regime despite a beneficial alternative and common interests. Ordinary prediction can improve while that revision route vanishes (169–179). Proposal origination can destabilize exclusion only when the specified acquisition/uptake product beats loss (82–129). These are conditional dynamics with two analyst-chosen representations; they do not independently generate the content of the omitted concept. The useful takeaway is that the current account partly selects the experiences from which it might be corrected.

**Evidence is different from standing and enactment.** DBR 59–73 keeps validated evidence fixed while imposing separate standing and deployment vetoes. These are enforced controls, not empirical discoveries about politics. They expose an error in calling an institution “unable to learn” when it has learned but cannot act, and the reverse error of calling a deployed change warranted merely because it was permitted. PIC 100–116 adds that funding and authority can be complements: an isolated ineffective relaxation does not identify an irrelevant condition.

**Discovery has to be financed before it can demonstrate its own return.** DBR 75–105 gives dated lower bounds for observation and total inquiry funding. Eventually attractive results cannot pay an earlier mandatory bill. ROI 11–31 and 77–106 show that value, recognized credit, and continued accessible finance differ. Positive average returns can coexist with eventual shutdown under bounded reserves and no restart; the result is sensitive to actual horizons. A guaranteed floor also keeps harmful or fruitless inquiry running. These checks forbid both “fund only proven returns” as a universal discovery rule and “preserve inquiry forever” as a universal correction.

**Suspension can preserve, or destroy, future correction.** RIE 61–76 separates today's test value from the option value of retaining access. RI 5–28, 93–109 shows that ordinary evidence, rebuilding, and direct action can make permanent diagnosis unnecessary. A quiet interval is not proof of epistemic closure. The relevant audit is whether sufficiently informed correction can still occur before the response opportunity disappears, with payments feasible at every stage. SCOR 133–201 makes the analogous handoff point: adopting a superior successor can retire indispensable expertise before the successor functions; overlap or a feasible reserve can matter.

## Counterexamples that should accompany the answer

| Overclaim | Required counterweight |
|---|---|
| Important omissions necessarily create anomalies | QWA's U world: useful candidate, observationally identical historical record |
| An anomaly proves a useful change | QWA's T world: historical anomaly, no beneficial candidate response |
| More data automatically expand the model | IEMR's excluded finite-family member stays excluded under ordinary conditioning; support revision is a separate operation |
| More memory always helps overall | Timed statistics can suffice; storage costs can outweigh a given question's value; retained data can still lack the relevant link |
| More diverse hypotheses imply learning | Null discovery still generates apparent associations; independent confirmation rejects them and costs resources |
| A good candidate will earn the means to establish itself | Required pre-result expenditure, imposed standing veto, and unavailable enactment break different links |
| A more active inquiry system is better | Absent-benefit protected inquiry wastes resources; repeated valid tests can accumulate false adoptions |
| Closing an inquiry service proves loss of function | Affordable timely rebuilding, alternative providers, ordinary evidence, and direct response may preserve correction |
| A well-functioning corrective system is wise or legitimate | Stable coercion and inventive exploitation; omitted stakeholders; imposed common objective does not establish whose reasons count |

## Scope and recommended phrasing

The answer can be affirmative without being total: **We notice important omissions by preserving ways for the world and other people to introduce distinctions our present account does not yet reward, and by turning those encounters into candidates that can be tested and acted on.** This is a disciplined, finite, revisable process. Existing sessions establish specific information obstructions and demonstrate parts of that process. They do not establish a universal method guaranteed to invent every important possibility, a complete theory of human insight, or that any checklist enhances model performance. The earlier ceiling comparisons cannot be used as evidence for such effectiveness.

## Addendum: a constructive repair for the inherited grammar obstruction

The discovery audit supplies a particularly useful new counterexample. Keep the six recorded binary attributes and the inherited failure/action costs, but define the hidden hazard by four matching literals. A correctly targeted policy then has population gain `(1/16)(.75−.25)=1/32`. Every predicate in the inherited all-cases/one-literal/two-literal grammar has nonpositive gain; the best two-literal ancestor has `(1/4)(.75/4−.25)=−1/64`. Richer retained records alone do not fix that representational limit, since the needed feature conjunction is present in the records but absent from the generator.

An explicit extension, requiring no injected correct predicate, is:

1. Before seeing confirmation outcomes, declare a maximum conjunction depth, a generation budget, a nomination budget per depth, and fresh confirmation/error budgets. At scheduled checkpoints, increase the allowed depth even when the current policy remains well calibrated and earlier nominees fail. All actual generation, enrollment, feature collection, and trial costs count.
2. Generate all conjunctions at the next depth by adding one unused attribute, with each possible value, to prior conjunctions and deduplicating. Do **not** prune descendants because an ancestor is unprofitable: that would recreate this example's obstruction. Reserve some nomination slots for the newly generated depth so an incumbent ranking cannot indefinitely starve it.
3. Rank eligible candidates from linked discovery records, nominate within the declared budget, and freeze each nominated predicate before a fresh randomized confirmation trial. Evaluate net policy benefit, including ongoing use costs. If at most M confirmation attempts are allowed, an appropriate per-attempt error allocation summing to the declared familywise bound can control false acceptance under the inherited trial assumptions. Reusing exploratory records to rank is different from reusing confirmation outcomes without a valid adaptive procedure.
4. Record failures, costs, and exhausted coverage. Rest when the declared budget is spent. Reopening or expanding further needs a separately specified rule; an unsuccessful shallow search is not a certificate that deeper or differently structured candidates cannot help.

For six binary inputs this is a concrete finite generator: depth three adds `C(6,3)2^3=160` predicates; depth four adds `C(6,4)2^4=240`. Searching through all six depths produces `3^6=729` distinct conjunctions including the empty one, because each input is absent, fixed to zero, or fixed to one. A matching three-literal ancestor already permits a useful coarse policy in this four-literal example: `(1/8)(.75/2−.25)=1/64`. Depth four contains the exact planted policy. These calculations prove representability and conditional usefulness, not that a particular noisy ranking and finite trial budget will discover or accept them.

There are practical limits even in this small repair. Deeper groups are rarer, so acquiring enough qualifying fresh cases can dominate cost and time. A confirmation sample size adequate for the larger original effect need not have useful power for the coarser three-literal policy's smaller conditional net gain. Multiple testing, elapsed time, enrollment, and noisy nomination can still prevent success. The absent-hazard control must remain: expanded search creates more opportunities to mistake noise for a candidate and expends resources even when nothing helpful exists.

This counterexample plus systematic expansion is enough to answer **how this inherited task can get past its specific missing possibility**. It removes the circular “consider the correct alternative” instruction by spelling out a generator that reaches it without receiving its identity. It is not a universal discovery algorithm: the six inputs, action pair, conjunction language, consequence measure, and tests still come from the designer. An unmeasured factor requires another observation route; a dependence outside the conjunction family may require another grammar; an omitted person's claim may require revision of evaluative commitments. The proper claim is “a constructive repair to an identified representation obstruction,” not “the general mystery of insight is solved.”
