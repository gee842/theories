# Claim register for recursive self modeling

This register records the status of the 5 October 2026 investigation. “Proved” means an elementary argument under stated mathematical assumptions, checked within this research session. It does not mean formal proof-assistant certification, empirical validation, or a claim of novelty. Proofs and worked examples are in [the synthesis](research.md).

| Claim | Status and assumptions | What can defeat an overbroad reading |
|---|---|---|
| C01 Applicable constraints also govern implemented meta-processes. | Conditional logical consequence. The process must belong to the constraint's domain; the constraint must actually hold there. | Building a meta-process can leave a restricted class, such as a particular memory bound. The representational label does not establish closure. |
| C02 Processing a model alone cannot increase information about the same target. | Standard data-processing result for finite variables F→M1→M2. Proof supplied. | Extra internal evidence, training information, original data, or changed target time invalidates the assumed chain. |
| C03 More information cannot worsen the ideal optimum when it is free and may be ignored. | Fixed model, losses, actions, and unrestricted admissible decision rules. Every rule using a garbling can be reproduced from the original observation. | Costs, delay, limited computation, changed goals, or forced use alter the comparator. More confidence is not more information. |
| C04 Additive active costs bounded below give a finite simultaneous depth bound. | N≤floor(R/c_min), with separately attributable c_k≥c_min>0 and a hard resource budget. | Positive summable costs, resource sharing, sequential reuse, or a renewable budget need separate treatment. |
| C05 Expected expense bounds expected depth, not every run. | C≥c_min N pathwise and E[C]≤R imply E[N]≤R/c_min. | A small probability of very long runs is compatible with the bound. It does not supply a fixed deadline. |
| C06 Exact closed deterministic abstraction exists precisely when merged states have merged successors. | r(x)=r(x′) implies r(Tx)=r(Tx′); constructive proof supplied. | Different future states inside a merged class prevent closure. For control, all allowed interventions and decision-relevant outputs must also be preserved. |
| C07 A conserved quantity descends only if it is constant on merged state classes. | Factorization J=J_bar∘r; conservation transfers when dynamics commute. | A constant representation can respect trivial dynamics while losing a conserved bit. |
| C08 Approximation error has a conditional recurrence bound. | Uniform one-step error ε, G Lipschitz with constant L, a specified metric, and valid domain. | Loss of uniformity or changed interventions invalidates the bound; L>1 permits amplification. |
| C09 Invertible coordinate changes preserve decoded trajectories. | G=h∘T∘h^(-1), transformed output/action rules, bijection h. | Different clocks, accessible information, memory, or interventions are substantive changes rather than coordinate changes. |
| C10 The supplied bias meta-model converges under two stability conditions. | Constant bias, exact contemporaneous reference, update chronology, and abs(a−k)<1, abs(1−λ)<1. | Reference bias leaves residual error; drift or latency requires new analysis. Direct reference control is a stronger simple comparator when always available. |
| C11 Additional modeling can degrade control and calibration. | Explicit counterexamples: gain changes .3 to −1.2; repeated odds squaring raises Brier loss while correctness stays .6. | These refute universal improvement, not the usefulness of every reflection. |
| C12 Later model–target agreement need not demonstrate knowledge of an earlier state. | Independent M and F0, controller sets F1=M. Exact finite example. | Evaluate prediction time and intervention together; successful control is a different accomplishment. |
| C13 Finite self-reference needs no completed infinite execution. | Finite reference graphs and self-reproducing programs provide constructions. | This does not prove accurate total self-knowledge or a theory of consciousness. |
| C14 Fixed-point existence does not imply convergence or truth. | R(m)=1−m supplies nonconvergent trajectories despite a fixed point. A constant false estimate can be update-stable. | Contraction or other dynamical assumptions can establish convergence, but truth needs additional evidence. |
| C15 A one-step stopping rule can miss complementary computations. | Two individually uninformative fair-bit shares jointly identify XOR; two .1 costs buy .5 error reduction. | Finite multi-step optimization accounts for this in the specified model; solving that optimization also costs resources. |
| E01 Monitoring can dissociate from task performance. | Empirical example and computational accounts in the source map. | Does not imply all introspection is unreliable or that a higher-order consciousness account is uniquely supported. |
| E02 Labeling can affect emotional processing in context-dependent directions. | Specific face-labeling neural study and own-feeling distress experiment; tasks and measures differ. | Neither tests arbitrary nested reflection. No clinical or universal regulation recommendation follows. |
| H01 Reflection's net benefit depends on evidence, computation, dynamics, and price. | The first proposed test is now implemented; see S01–S04 below. Broader empirical validation remains open. | A result disappearing under matched access or an equivalent implementation narrows the architectural claim. |
| N01 Affected interests and contestability belong in evaluation. | Explicit normative commitment carried from Ren's research context. | Causal participation, prediction quality, viability, and stable control do not logically determine legitimate authority. |

## Direct links to verification

The 15 named checks in [verification.json](verification.json) cover exact feedback recurrences and limits at finite steps, reference bias, control multipliers, XOR side information, intervention-created agreement, calibration loss, one abstraction counterexample, resource arithmetic, fixed-point cycling, and complementary-computation arithmetic. These are not 15 independent experiments.

The information-structure premise for complementarity is established by enumerating fair bits in the proof; the numerical check verifies its net-value arithmetic. Two independent team derivations agreed on the feedback formulas. A second execution reproduced the checks before the final precision edits. The final script was then rerun. Agent agreement itself is not evidence of universal validity.

Some proofs in the synthesis, including the general abstraction theorem, coordinate conjugacy, and approximate error bound, are algebraic arguments rather than exhaustive numerical tests. The record deliberately distinguishes them from the 15 executable checks.

## Claims from the completed continuation

The [experiment report](experiment/report.md) records 80 cases with 1,000 paired seeds each, frozen after separate development. The following are conditional simulation results, not empirical psychology.

| Claim | Evidence and status | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| S01 Retained calibration improves selected crude baselines but loses all eight primary comparisons. | In the supplied model and prices, the ordinary-data joint estimator selected on development beats retained correction beyond the declared .02 margin in every primary world/stage. Adjusted paired intervals retained. | The joint estimator itself models bias. Neither “reflection is useless” nor a universal architectural ranking follows. |
| S02 An extra audit can miss changes between the checks admitted by its own uncertainty gate. | Adaptive audit loss is 7.182 for a step-30 bias change and 27.523 for the prespecified step-32 timing control. Acquisition schedules independently reconstructed. | Two change times, supplied gate and costs; this is not a universal reflection-depth effect. |
| S03 Better control can coexist with badly calibrated uncertainty. | In the biased-reference matched stage, the joint estimator improves control over its ordinary-only version while nominal 90% interval coverage falls from 89.93% to 53.24%. | Specific reference misspecification. State-estimation MSE difference is unresolved, and Brier scores assess a different aspect of forecasts. |
| S04 Nested and flat versions of one estimator are behaviorally identical. | Exact per-step equivalence across all 10,000 stage/world/seed comparisons. Four repeated estimate calculations preserve behavior and add .018 priced expense. | Establishes this implementation equivalence, not equivalence of every neural or software architecture. |
| C16 Known dynamics can make ordinary sensor bias observable. | For constant bias, the two-state matrix has determinant 1−a=.2; the noiseless inverse is explicit. | Finite noisy estimation, unknown dynamics/forcing, and prior assumptions must be treated separately. |
| C17 With unanchored forcing and biases, a shift preserves all observation/action histories while changing absolute state and loss. | [Proof](experiment/identifiability-proof.md): transform (x,b,d,g) to (x+c,b−c,d−c,g+(1−a)c), under an admissible shift-closed class. | Fixed known forcing, fixed informative prior, or an independent absolute reference can break this symmetry. It does not apply unchanged to the frozen simulation. |

The continuation adds 12 filter tests, 10 independent runner tests, and 2,184 independent assertions checking the raw result summaries and acquisition mechanisms. Numerical checks and general proofs remain distinct.

## Claims from the fresh correction-access study

The [second continuation](correction-access/report.md) contains 90,000 episodes from 2,000 paired evaluation seeds after new development. Its primary outcome prices control and sensing, with free computation. It is not directly comparable to the earlier work-priced loss.

| Claim | Evidence and status | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| S05 Surprise and periodic access improve the randomized-change mixture while worsening initially unknown constant-bias cases. | Six frozen primary contrasts: change differences −3.209 and −2.571 versus confidence, adjusted intervals below −.02; constant differences +2.718 and +3.044, intervals above +.02. Hybrid change benefit remains unresolved. | Supplied equal-weight forcing/reference-bias mixtures, finite candidate gates, shared scalar update. Separate no-bias control does not show the same harm. |
| S06 Fast mismatch monitoring can exhaust the future correction budget. | Surprise acquires within four steps whenever a post-change sample is obtained, but 16.39% of change-world episodes obtain none; all these misses exhausted six references before the change. | Empirical paired-world fraction, not a guarantee for arbitrary changes or triggers. |
| S07 Protected acquisition can succeed while interpretation fails. | Periodic/hybrid acquire after every tested change. The three-state periodic benchmark has roughly 90% coverage under correct zero forcing but only 2.5% under hidden forcing despite all six references. | Three-state versus scalar changes inference as well as uncertainty modeling. This is a particular misspecification result, not a universal impossibility claim. |
| C18 Finite protected sampling has an exact gap bound. | For N intervals, B post-boundary purchases including a terminal sample, maximum gap is at least ceil(N/B); balanced gaps attain it. Study schedules have integer arrival bounds 14 and 29. | Initial paid calibration is additional; terminal availability and change persistence required. The chosen schedules are not minimax over the restricted change-time support. |
| C19 Identical evidence imposes a squared estimation-error lower bound. | For two admissible observation-equivalent states separated by c, at least one expected squared error is ≥c²/4; cumulative T-step counterpart ≥Tc²/4. | Requires a common observation-based estimator and an admissible observationally equivalent pair. Fixed known priors/anchors can remove the pair. |
| C20 A false zero-forcing assumption can support a displaced control target. | Exact transform x'=x−r,b'=b+r,d'=d+r,r=g/(1−a) preserves observations and removes forcing. If perfect-coordinate tracking, no noise and unclipped stable control hold, actual x→r. | The finite Gaussian filter's convergence is not proved. Saved example supports the mechanism; initial priors are not preserved by the shift. |

The second continuation adds 18 filter/runner tests, an independent 5,355-assertion audit reconstructing 72,000 scalar episodes, and exact scheduling/coordinate checks. Access, discrimination and useful revision remain distinct claims.
