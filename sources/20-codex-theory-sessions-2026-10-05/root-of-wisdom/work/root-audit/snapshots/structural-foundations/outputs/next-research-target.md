# Recurrent correction with paid suspension and restart

Execution update: the [second cycle](cycle-2/synthesis.md) completed modular replacement and restricted recurrent paid repair. The [third cycle](cycle-3/synthesis.md) added cross-scale transport, paid repair infrastructure, and finite paid memory/succession. The [fourth cycle](cycle-4/synthesis.md) added actual infrastructure adoption, paid learning, and rule transmission. The [fifth cycle](cycle-5/synthesis.md) implemented population selection with funded births, paid evaluation, and joint copying. The [sixth cycle](cycle-6/synthesis.md) now implements paid revision of an evaluator within a known changed-contract family, an exact recovery window, noisy finite-memory revision, and a robust quota alternative. Revision beyond the supplied family, construction of new executable candidates, and empirical scale validation remain open. The next proposed formal test introduces an anomalous condition outside the represented family and prices the tests and bounded candidate-construction process, compared with a funded robust fallback or legitimate finite closure. The original proposal below is preserved as the historical scope record.

This is a proposed next study, not a completed experiment. The present session establishes a formal interface and finite correction example. The next useful result would characterize when a system can repeatedly restore a productive function while paying to preserve, suspend, and rebuild its ability to investigate and revise. The target is a conditional characterization, not a universal duty to keep investigating.

## Question and stopping rule

For a specified finite model, determine the states from which an admissible controller can keep a declared function within bounds and meet successive correction deadlines against a declared disturbance class. Give the least initial reserve when it exists, a witness controller when the requirement is feasible, and an explicit counterstrategy or resource certificate when it is not.

The immediate milestone is a smaller modular test: pair an operating process with an inspect-authorize-implement process. Compare a replacement that preserves their decision and action interfaces with one that matches current output but loses an interaction or retained distinction. Test whether a proposed compact capacity summary preserves the correction guarantee. The sufficient refinement criterion in `formal-foundations.md` supplies explicit proof obligations; a failed summary should produce a counterexample identifying the omitted dependency.

Only then extend the same mechanism to recurrent operation. Stop the first study after obtaining independently checked results for that model and its controls. Do not add evolving purposes, unrestricted language invention, strategic capture, and multiple generations before the basic chronology and resource closure are verified. These remain substantive research questions rather than omissions to conceal with a larger state tuple.

## Why this target follows from the current record

The parent research already models selection against maintenance, nonlinear renewal thresholds, proposal generation, evidence inheritance, and recurrent inquiry financed by observable credit. Its latest inquiry model supplies productive output even after inquiry shuts down. The next research decision in that record is to make productive renewal depend on correction while allowing evidence-guided suspension and restart.

This session adds a useful constraint on that integration: the existence of each corrective component separately does not establish one affordable, authorized, observation-based sequence. The successor model must preserve that distinction over repeated episodes. It should also permit replacement of a provider, so continuing a function is distinguishable from keeping an incumbent alive.

## Minimal model specification

Use a finite controlled transition system with the following state components, each with an operational interpretation established before simulation:

| Component | Role and required accounting |
|---|---|
| Productive condition | Determines actual output and whether the protected service is delivered. Correction changes this condition. |
| Accessible reserve | Receives explicitly recorded output or boundary inputs; pays operation, investigation, maintenance, rebuilding, and implementation. |
| Disturbance mode | A finite hidden state affecting which response works. Its law or adversarial constraints must be declared. |
| Investigation apparatus | May operate, wait in paid standby, or be absent; rebuilding takes specified time and resources. |
| Retained decision record | A finite register with explicit update and retention costs, including loss or corruption when applicable. |
| Institutional access | Separately records who can submit evidence, decide, and implement, and how those relationships are renewed or replaced. |
| Active interpretation and rule | A small supplied set of diagnostic models and response rules, with explicit transitions for admission and adoption. |
| Provider identity | Allows a handoff with establishment cost and time, without making preservation of the current provider part of the objective. |

A first implementation may hold the last two components constant to isolate recurrent finance. If it does, report that the study concerns correction within a supplied representation rather than formation of new purposes or concepts. Every added component must change a tested prediction; otherwise omit it.

The analyst may know the full simulated state. Controllers must use only the information available at their decision point. If memory is priced, a controller must not receive an unpriced transcript through a solver's default policy representation. Either include its finite register in the physical model and restrict policies to that register, or explicitly report perfect recall as a separate optimistic bound. A standard belief-state algorithm must not silently solve a different memory problem.

## Event chronology

Within each period, specify the order of disturbance, passive signal, output, controller action, payment, delayed completion, and register update. When no physical reason selects an order, compare the alternatives rather than hiding a favorable one. In particular, late output cannot pay an earlier fee unless credit is explicitly available.

Use an expanded microstep graph for simultaneous-looking operations. A rebuild that finishes after a correction deadline is different from an immediately usable resource. A sensor that reports after implementation cannot guide that implementation. A publicly visible authorization or silence can convey information and must be included in the controller's interface.

All productive output requires declared boundary conditions. A renewable external feedstock may be supplied, but it must be named and its disruption included or excluded explicitly. No model can establish indefinite physical continuation from finite irrecoverably consumed support merely by labelling a transition “renew.”

## Objective and uncertainty

Specify a protected service region and a finite set of recovery tests. Report feasibility, intervention burden, delay, and effects on affected parties separately. A scalar aggregate is unnecessary. Whose interests define these tests remains an explicit ethical and institutional choice.

Start with two alternative uncertainty interpretations rather than conflating them:

1. A robust game with a stated bound on disturbance frequency and severity. Compute a safety or repeated-response winning region only for this disturbance class.
2. A stochastic model with a supplied transition law. Report survival and recovery probabilities over a declared horizon, and expected cost. Positive expected income does not establish almost-sure indefinite continuation with capped reserves.

Allow evidence to justify no current investigation. An admissible controller should be able to choose inactivity while maintaining or rebuilding future capacity. Whether it does so successfully is a result, not a design requirement that inquiry always occurs.

Repeated inquiry requires repeated decision-relevant uncertainty. If one perfect diagnosis of a permanently fixed fault remains in a free persistent register, paying to maintain diagnostic access again may be redundant. Introduce new disturbances, stale evidence, or changed diagnostic relevance explicitly, and retain the fixed-fault perfect-recall case as a null control.

## Comparisons and controls

Compare reserve accumulation, apparatus standby, evidence retention, renewal of standing, and provider replacement as distinct interventions. Match initial productive condition and cash. Record every later transfer and expenditure; matching a nominal subsidy or expected budget alone does not establish pathwise resource equality.

Include these controls:

- No hazard is present and the service remains safe, so a policy that performs no inquiry is feasible and inquiry can impose needless cost. Keep this separate from an ineffective-correction case where a required recovery is impossible.
- Rebuilding is instantaneous and cheap, testing whether standby only helps through the proposed lag or lost prerequisites.
- All relevant information is supplied, isolating implementation and institutional access from discovery.
- Implementation access is supplied, isolating informational and physical bottlenecks.
- Apparatus or institutional access has an alternative provider, testing whether an alleged necessary incumbent is actually necessary.
- Informative records are lost before acquisition versus after acquisition, with the controller's retained memory treated consistently.
- A disturbance falls outside the promised class, making the limitation of the robust certificate visible.

Do not identify intervention effects by merely deleting all substitute routes. The finite example in this session deliberately retains more expensive substitutes for that reason.

## Verification and proposed claim

For small finite horizons, compare exact strategy enumeration with an independent dynamic program under precisely the same policy class. Preserve the state transition table, timing specification, policy witnesses, negative cases, and machine-readable outputs. Check accounting along every reachable transition, not merely average trajectories. If the model has partial observation and bounded memory, report the search scope and do not substitute full-information reachability.

Finite-horizon checks do not establish indefinite recurrent capacity. For an infinite claim, supply an invariant finite controller and a proof that every reachable closed behavior satisfies the specified recurring-response duty, or solve a precisely stated finite game with an appropriate recurrent acceptance condition. Report a least reserve only for the controller and disturbance class actually certified. Arbitrarily long simulations alone are insufficient.

Seek an explicit frontier relating initial reserve, rebuilding delay, and admitted disturbance spacing. Identify regions where paid standby, evidence, or standing improves feasibility relative to the best reserve-only or suspend-and-restart controller, and regions where it is redundant. Report the minimum-reserve and intervention-burden frontier under the declared criterion. The model does not determine how those burdens ought to be valued or whose interests should prevail.

A useful negative result would be equally informative: after correct resource matching and information chronology, the claimed advantage may disappear, be reproduced by a simpler reserve policy, or depend entirely on an imposed access rule. Such outcomes would narrow the proposed structural account.

## What would remain unresolved

Even a successful study would leave open empirical identification of the relevant stocks and relationships, emergence of alternative interpretations, contested standards of justified revision, strategic manipulation, and transfer to human or AI institutions. The mathematical result would concern the declared model. Cross-domain relevance would require a demonstrated correspondence of mechanisms and intervention effects, not the shared use of the word “renewal.”
