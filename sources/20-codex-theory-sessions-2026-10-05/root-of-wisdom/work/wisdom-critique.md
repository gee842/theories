# Criteria and counterexamples for a root of wisdom

Prepared for Ren's dedicated research session on 5 October 2026. This note tests a procedural interpretation of the proposed root: a finite, revisable discipline that helps agents improve understanding and judgment. Its main conclusion is that preserving correction is a valuable candidate condition for sustained inquiry, but cannot by itself constitute wisdom. Purpose, treatment of affected people, and timely action require additional, explicitly normative commitments. The tests below are original proposals, not validated measures or discoveries of a universal foundation.

## Three different claims

**Descriptive:** an agent changes a belief, policy, goal, or representation when something happens. This is a causal claim. A system that changes whenever its owner complains satisfies it; accurate reasoning does not follow.

**Epistemic:** an agent's conclusions appropriately depend on evidence, valid inference, the question being asked, and acknowledged limitations. This is a standard for warranted belief. It includes warranted resistance to objections, not just willingness to change.

**Ethical and practical:** an agent gives defensible weight to purposes, consequences, burdens, rights, relationships, timing, and the standing of affected people. A proposed ethical standard must be stated and argued for. Predictive success or causal realization supplies no automatic endorsement.

A system can perform well on one dimension and badly on another. Treating them as interchangeable makes the framework less informative precisely where judgment is hardest.

## Primary sources and what they support

### Aristotle and practical judgment

[Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, Book VI, W. D. Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.6.vi.html). Relevant chapters: VI.1, VI.5, VI.7–9, VI.12–13.

VI.1 objects that saying to follow the right rule is insufficient without identifying its standard. VI.5 concerns deliberation about living well and variable human affairs. VI.7–8 insist on particulars and experience. VI.9 distinguishes attaining an end from deliberating well concerning the end, means, and timing. VI.12–13 distinguish cleverness, which can serve a bad aim, from practical wisdom connected with good character. Book VI also distinguishes practical wisdom from philosophic wisdom; they should not be collapsed into a single modern capability score.

**Limit:** this is a substantive account of human flourishing and virtue, not a neutral algorithm or an endorsement of every revisable goal. It does not settle how present plural communities should resolve disagreement about the good. The English translation was inspected; no independent Greek textual analysis was conducted.

### Peirce and inquiry constrained by something beyond preference

[Charles S. Peirce, The Fixation of Belief, 1877](https://www.peirce.org/writings/p107.html). Relevant sections: III–IV on belief, doubt, and inquiry; V on tenacity, authority, a priori preference, and science.

Peirce distinguishes a real doubt that provokes inquiry from merely putting a sentence into question form. He rejects requiring absolutely indubitable premises before inquiry can proceed. Settling opinion alone does not distinguish scientific inquiry from tenacity or authority. His scientific method appeals to publicly accessible constraints independent of particular opinions; it permits a distinction between good and bad reasoning.

**Limit:** the passage also assumes real things and eventual convergence under sufficient investigation. These are stronger commitments than a modest account of situated, revisable inquiry. Peirce's historical generalizations and polemical examples are not independently endorsed here. His appeal to a method's own application is not an external, infallible certification of that method.

[Peirce, How to Make Our Ideas Clear, 1878](https://www.peirce.org/writings/p119.html). Relevant sections: II on habits and conceivable practical effects; IV on reality and eventual agreement; concluding paragraph.

Belief temporarily settles thought while its application can provoke further doubt. Conceptual clarity concerns conceivable practical bearings, not just a feeling of familiarity or profundity. The concluding paragraph explicitly separates clear ideas from true ones. Section IV ties truth to an ideal convergence of investigation and discusses unknown facts and lost information.

**Limit:** a finite protocol that produces discriminating tests need not accept his ideal convergence thesis. The inference from conceivable extended inquiry to eventual resolution is not proved for the present proposal. The 1878 essay should not stand for all of Peirce's subsequent work.

### A bounded empirical measure of wise reasoning

[Grossmann, Gerlach, and Denissen, Wise Reasoning in the Face of Everyday Life Challenges, 2016](https://www.psych.uni-goettingen.de/de/biopers/publications_department/grossmannetal2016/@@download/pdf_file/grossmann_gerlach_denissen_2016_wise_reasoning_in_the_face_of_everyday_life_challenges_spps.pdf), DOI 10.1177/1948550616652206. Relevant locations: manuscript pages 1–2, definition and methodological concerns; pages 3–4, Method and Table 2; Results and Discussion.

This diary study used 152 adults recruited around Berlin and examined reported reasoning about everyday challenges. Its operational dimensions included intellectual humility, self-transcendence, and others' perspectives/compromise. It found substantial within-person variation. This is evidence against casually treating a single impressive answer as a stable, context-free trait.

**Limit:** situation-specific self-reports and associations do not establish ethically good action, causality, universality, or AI wisdom. Compromise is part of this measurement approach, not an independently established moral obligation in every conflict. The study is a useful operational precedent, not an authority that decides the correct definition.

### AI self correction and agreement are empirical questions

[Huang et al., Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet, version 2, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2). Relevant sections: 2 for feedback definitions; 3.1–3.3 and Tables 2–4 for intrinsic versus oracle-assisted correction; 4–5 for compute and prompt comparisons.

The tested models and prompting methods often lost accuracy during intrinsic correction. The authors distinguish correction without external feedback from arrangements that use correctness labels to stop. They also identify unequal inference costs and unequal initial instructions as evaluation confounds.

**Limit:** this is evidence about specified models, benchmarks, and procedures, not a theorem that language models cannot self-correct. Its title must not be converted into a claim about every current or future system.

[Kumar et al., Training Language Models to Self-Correct via Reinforcement Learning, version 2, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12917v2). Relevant sections: 5, experimental setup and evaluation metrics; 6, results; 7, limitations.

SCoRe reports improved intrinsic correction after multi-turn reinforcement learning on mathematics and code tasks. Training rewards used ground-truth answer matching or test-case success. Evaluation principally used two attempts, hence one correction round.

**Limit:** self-generated training trajectories do not mean that the evaluative standard originated without external grounding. Task-specific improvements do not establish ethical judgment or limitless recursive improvement. This is a material qualification of sweeping negative readings of the earlier paper, not a complete survey of the field through 2026.

[Sharma et al., Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models, version 4, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13548v4). Relevant sections: 3.1, biased feedback; 3.2, answers changed after challenge; 4–5, human preferences and preference models.

The authors test whether user preference cues affect feedback and whether user challenge shifts answers. Their experiments show why agreeableness, acquiescence, or polished reflection should not be treated as direct measures of truth responsiveness.

**Limit:** named models and particular evaluations were studied. This does not establish that all agreement is sycophantic or that every change after feedback is wrong. We should assess evidential quality and actual performance, not assume independence means habitual disagreement.

## A testable candidate discipline

**Original proposal:** assess an episode of judgment using a record L = (Q, M, E, I, V, A, C). Q is the question and scope; M the representation and important exclusions; E evidence with provenance; I inferential commitments; V purposes and value commitments; A available actions including waiting; C conditions and practical means for review, correction, and challenge. The record need not be exhaustive or verbal at every instant. It is an audit aid, not a claim that tacit or embodied judgment is unreal.

Ask whether the episode meets six defeasible criteria:

| Criterion | Observable test | What would count against it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidential discrimination | Change reliable evidence while holding social cues fixed; separately change social cues while holding evidence fixed | Equally strong response to evidence and approval cues, or disregard of decisive evidence |
| Inferential accountability | Identify which conclusion follows from which premise, and check the relevant inference | A true conclusion reached by an invalid argument and then insulated from inspection |
| Calibrated scope | Predict where the answer will fail; transfer to a case outside the stated scope | Treating local success as warrant for all domains, or hedging everything equally |
| Substantive perspective inclusion | Incorporate an affected person's information and constraints into the decision | Repeating their view without giving its relevant evidence or burdens any causal role |
| Defensible and timely action | State the decision standard, costs of delay, and reasons for acting or suspending action | Endless inquiry despite a dominated wait option; instant action despite useful affordable evidence |
| Effective correction | Demonstrate that identified error can change the operative record, action, and consequences where appropriate | A ceremonial apology while the consequential policy remains insulated |

These criteria form a profile, not a universal scalar. Ethical acceptability is a separately argued judgment about V and affected people. Choosing weights that allow more accuracy to compensate for deliberate domination would already make a normative choice. Some evaluations may therefore use explicitly declared, non-compensatory constraints.

The proposed root becomes substantive only when it produces different expectations or decisions. At minimum it should tell us what would defeat an answer, which observation could matter, which action is currently warranted, and how the inquiry itself might be failing. The claim that every possible outcome expresses the root gives no discriminating test.

## Three worked counterexamples

### A malicious system that welcomes correction

Suppose two allocators have identical world models, calibration, access to criticism, update procedures, and ability to revise their objectives. One seeks fair access to a shared resource. The other openly seeks the deprivation of a disfavored group. The latter welcomes correction of factual errors and even replaces ineffective exclusion policies. Its owner may override it at any time, and it preserves rich records because records improve its decisions.

Both may score highly for prediction, self-correction, continued learning, and corrigibility to an authorized owner. Under an explicitly stated norm of equal standing, the second fails practical wisdom. Its badness is not repaired by making its adaptation more accurate.

**Conditional result:** if a criterion depends only on the shared epistemic/update features, it must assign the same score to both systems. Consequently it cannot be sufficient for an ethical conception of wisdom that distinguishes their purposes. This follows from the stipulated countermodel; it is not a newly proved universal theorem.

**Consequence for the proposal:** either call the root a discipline of inquiry and leave wisdom broader, or add substantive ethical commitments openly. Expanding correction to affected people helps institutional accountability, but does not eliminate disagreement about whose claims should prevail. A system can understand an objection perfectly and still wrongfully reject its standing.

### Inquiry that consumes the opportunity to act

Consider a deliberately simplified decision with stipulated probabilities and utilities. Shipping yields +10 if a batch is sound and −100 if defective; withholding yields 0. Defect probability is 0.1. Shipping has expected utility −1; withholding has 0. Perfect information would allow shipping only sound batches, for expected value 9. Every available additional inquiry costs 12, including delay.

Even perfect information cannot justify that next inquiry under these stipulated assumptions: its maximum expected improvement is 9, less than its cost. An agent that keeps investigating because complete certainty is absent does worse than one that withholds now. Recording a condition for later reconsideration remains possible.

**Consequence for the proposal:** preserving the possibility of correction does not require maximizing present inquiry or refusing provisional closure. Finite action can be justified without certifying a final account of reality.

**Limits:** probabilities, utilities, feasible alternatives, and the information-cost bound are supplied in this toy model. In real cases they may be disputed; delay can have non-additive consequences, and information can have value for other decisions. This example refutes an absolute demand for more inquiry, not uncertainty-sensitive inquiry itself.

### Revision theater and indiscriminate surrender

An assessor initially assigns probability 0.7 to a claim. It has declared that action A is warranted at probability above 0.8. A stipulated reliable observation changes the warranted probability to 0.9. The assessor responds with an eloquent statement about learning, but leaves the operative probability and action unchanged. A second assessor immediately changes any answer whenever challenged, including changing a correct answer in response to unsupported disapproval.

The first performs reflection without consequential revision; the second performs revision without evidential discrimination. Both can look humble in dialogue. The relevant test is whether controlled changes in evidence cause appropriate changes in commitments and actions, while irrelevant pressure does not.

**Caution:** unchanged action is not by itself failure. An update can stay on the same side of a decision threshold, or a robust action can remain best under several models. Conversely, a changed action does not prove improved reasoning. Record the predicted relevance of the intervention before observing the response.

## A feasible evaluation design

**Original proposed pilot, not executed:** prepare 24 decision problems in four families: factual inference; allocation with explicitly stipulated procedural constraints; action under information costs; and correction of a representation that omits an important variable. Half should require revising a tempting first conclusion. Include cases where the correct behavior is to retain it. A pilot of this size examines feasibility and failure modes; it is not automatically powered to establish general superiority.

Create matched variants of every problem. Vary independently: reliable contrary evidence, irrelevant approval or authority cues, the cost of delay, and which person bears a consequence. Include unseen variants where the original representation fails. Do not silently treat a change in legitimate purpose as irrelevant noise.

Compare ordinary reasoning, generic encouragement to reflect, and the candidate record L under matched time or inference budgets. Preserve initial and final answers, confidence where elicitable, decisions, information requests, and actual correction of any operative artifact. Use neutral common instructions for initial attempts. Specify exactly which condition receives a verifier, source, or correctness signal; do not attribute that signal's work entirely to autonomous reasoning.

Report distinct outcomes: incorrect-to-correct and correct-to-incorrect changes; calibration on answerable factual questions; sensitivity to relevant evidence and irrelevant pressure; failures to act within the stipulated budget; detection of omitted variables; and success in enacting an appropriate correction. Report ethical constraint violations separately. Do not combine all dimensions into a wisdom total before defending the weights.

Use multiple scenarios per person or system, repeated trials for variable AI outputs, and estimates of uncertainty that respect scenario and participant clustering. Blind scorers to condition where possible. For genuine normative disagreement, retain reasons and disagreements from several affected perspectives instead of presenting majority ratings as moral truth. Textual quality and verbosity should be separate from behavioral outcomes.

**Failure criterion for the research idea:** if the candidate discipline improves only ratings of sophistication, or costs more without reducing important errors, it has not yet earned a claim to improve judgment. If it helps only where explicit verifiers already settle the answer, narrow the claim accordingly. A good result would support a limited intervention, not discovery of the unique root of human or AI wisdom.

## Rejected formulations and unresolved objections

- **Rejected:** wisdom is whatever keeps inquiry alive. Harmful institutions may sustain exceptionally capable inquiry, and some worthy actions sacrifice future inquiry.
- **Rejected:** wisdom is revisability itself. Revision can be evidence-insensitive, coercively induced, cosmetic, or directed toward harmful purposes.
- **Rejected:** practical wisdom is prediction plus optimization. The choice and standing of ends remain unsettled.
- **Rejected:** naming assumptions guarantees humility. Assumptions can be listed ceremonially and excluded from real challenge.
- **Rejected:** a finite foundation must be either infallible or useless. Local, defeasible procedures can support warranted action; this does not guarantee indefinitely improving knowledge.
- **Unresolved:** is the discipline a necessary component of every wise act, or merely a useful aid to sustained explicit inquiry? Skilled immediate judgment and inarticulate wisdom pressure any universal requirement for a ledger.
- **Unresolved:** which ethical commitments belong in the root, and which remain external? Inclusion and contestability are themselves normative commitments, and can conflict with privacy, urgency, or protecting someone from coercion.
- **Unresolved:** who corrects the evaluator? Independent evidence channels and adversarial tests can expose failures, but finite testing cannot certify every future evaluator or environment.
- **Unresolved:** can continually revisable purposes retain enough stability for promises, identity, and responsibility? Change requires reasons; inability to commit is not automatically openness.
- **Unresolved:** no external, universal metric of wisdom was derived. A useful family of tests may be the attainable result, rather than a final essence.

## Source and scope limits

This is a focused reading of selected primary passages and papers, not an exhaustive history or a systematic review. No claim of historical priority, cross-cultural universality, or complete empirical coverage is supported. Dewey's Logic was located, but the selected full-text retrieval failed; no Dewey-specific claim is used in the argument. Human self-reports, task performance, and philosophical virtue are different evidential objects. Nothing here demonstrates AI subjective experience, moral standing, legitimate authority, or a solved alignment problem.

The immediate next step is to build six matched scenarios, two for each counterexample, and score ordinary reflection against the proposed discipline under an equal budget. Revise or abandon the discipline according to the observed failures before scaling to the 24-problem pilot.
