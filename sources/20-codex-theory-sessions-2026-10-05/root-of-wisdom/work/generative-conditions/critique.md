# Audit: change can produce conditions for further change

5 October 2026. Read-only audit of `outputs/generative-becoming.md` and `outputs/changing-the-interface.md`; neither output was edited. This note extends their interpretation rather than claiming another physical discovery.

## Strongest useful formulation

**Interactions produce changes that can become conditions of subsequent interactions. When those products enable further production, generation becomes recursive; when later production repairs or reproduces the enabling conditions, the process can maintain that generativity.**

This articulates the user's proposal positively while preserving three different achievements: an effect, an enabling effect, and renewed enabling capacity. Only the latter two explain productive generativity. The word “can” is substantive: the supplied dynamics must actually contain the relevant production and coupling, rather than these being deduced from change alone.

For the world considered as a totality, there is no assumed external environment. A subsystem's environment consists of other interacting parts of that world. Partitioning them helps explain pathways; it does not require separate substances or an outside agent. This compatibility does not itself prove monism.

## What adds content beyond “state evolves”

Writing `z[t+1] = F(z[t])` by itself explains no productive mechanism. A substantive example must identify:

1. A transformation that produces a physical change.
2. A causal route by which that product changes a later transformation: supplies substrate, enables transport, changes coupling, catalyzes conversion, retains information, or removes an obstruction.
3. A consequence different from matched physically admissible circumstances without that product or route.
4. The material, energy, time, and support conditions of both production and later operation.

A downstream operation need not become absolutely possible for the first time. The product might increase its rate or probability, make it executable by this organization, or bring it within a stated time and resource allowance. Those are distinguishable, testable contributions.

The interface investigation already supplies a precise example of changed access and executable response. It does not yet supply an unrestricted invention mechanism. The generative-becoming report already distinguishes production of capacity from its continuing renewal. These points should be inherited, not presented again as discoveries in this continuation.

## A useful theorem: local enablement does not enlarge global reachability

Take a fixed transition relation `→` on a complete state space, including physically consequential surroundings, resources, mechanisms, and memory. Define `Reach(z)` as all states reachable from `z` by a finite permitted path, including the zero-length path. If `z′ ∈ Reach(z)`, then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Reach}(z')\subseteq\operatorname{Reach}(z).}
\]

**Proof.** Concatenate a permitted path from `z` to `z′` with any permitted path from `z′` to a later state. The resulting path is permitted from `z`. ∎

This applies to deterministic, stochastic-support, and nondeterministic transition descriptions when “reachable” is defined by permitted finite paths. For continuous stochastic models, an arbitrary zero-probability singleton should not casually be equated with a positive-probability event; use a clearly declared transition relation or suitable support notion. No probabilities are needed for the graph theorem.

The controlled version remains true when permitted paths include all physically implementable actions and can be concatenated. The full state must retain controller limitations and consumed resources. An arbitrary family of preselected policies need not be closed under concatenation; the theorem does not grant the ability to combine unavailable programs, replenish budgets, or restart a deadline. For a state reached in `t` steps, the finite-horizon version is `Reach_H(z′) ⊆ Reach_(t+H)(z)`, not generally inclusion within the same `H`.

Consequently, actual evolution under a fixed complete law does not add a globally forbidden future to that law. It can activate a previously unavailable *local operation*. The smallest illustration is:

\[
z_0\xrightarrow{\text{build}}z_1\xrightarrow{\text{use}}z_2.
\]

The operation `use` is available at `z₁` and unavailable at `z₀`; nevertheless, `z₂` was constructibly reachable from `z₀`. **Constructible in principle and executable now are different properties.** A world can produce new operating capacities without changing its underlying laws or increasing its all-time set of reachable endpoints.

This gives the strongest correction to loose language about “creating possibilities”: specify which effective operation, resource allowance, scale, participant, or response probability changed. It preserves the user's insight while making its meaning sharper.

## Environmental change as a carrier of causal history

Suppose the joint state is `(x,e)`, where `x` is the tracked organization and `e` contains relevant surroundings. Two histories can return the organization to the same `x` while leaving different `e`. If later responses differ, the environment carries a consequential trace of those histories. The individual need not contain every effective memory of its activity.

This is a concrete extension of “change creates further change”: a product can alter the encounter through which later activity takes place, including activity of another organization. It also explains why a locally recurrent system can participate in a larger nonrecurrent process. Local repetition need not mean repetition of the joint situation.

Once all consequential traces are included in a complete Markov state, this is ordinary state dependence rather than an extra force of the past. Conversely, two genuinely merged joint states can still have different actual histories; future adequacy does not erase those historical truths. This preserves the earlier identity investigation's restriction.

## Counterexamples that locate the claim

| Process | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| A two-state alternator | Indefinite change | A new operation or expanding novelty |
| A finite domino chain | Effects become causes of later effects | Renewal of the capacity to keep producing |
| Fuel burning until exhaustion | Products alter surroundings | Continued productive capacity |
| Catalyst production that enables a reaction | A product changes an effective operation | Adequate supply or lasting renewal |
| A renewing organization at steady macroscopic state | Recurrent productive capacity | Increasing visible complexity or constant innovation |
| A self-amplifying process that destroys its substrate | Positive feedback | Sustainable generativity |

“Change changes its environment” also needs coupling: an isolated degree of freedom can change without affecting a chosen other part. Actual interacting systems provide many candidate mechanisms; bare change does not logically entail them.

## What closure and novelty still require

A causal loop is neither a sufficient resource supply nor an adequate production rate. Closure of support dependencies describes who renews which conditions; continuation additionally requires feasible rates, reserves, and timing. The two prior reports already supply examples of those separate requirements.

Novelty has at least four meanings here: a state not previously visited; an operation newly executable by a participant; a previously absent mechanism or organization; and a description absent from an observer's model. No equivalence follows among them. Neither finite recurrence nor unbounded state variation automatically proves open-ended production of new kinds of capability.

The most promising next target is therefore **a produced structure that changes a later interaction, whose altered products then either maintain, transform, or destroy the conditions of the next productive round**. That construction would unite causal inheritance, local enablement, environmental feedback, and continued operation within one explicit dynamics. Its result would be a mechanism for recursive generativity, with success and failure cases, rather than an assumed tendency of everything toward growth.

## Recommended compact phrasing for the user

“Yes. You are pointing to a recursive feature of interaction: what happens does not merely leave another arrangement; its products can change what subsequent interactions can do. A product becomes a resource, a constraint, a channel, or a catalyst. Further activity then occurs under conditions partly made by earlier activity. When that activity also renews those conditions, generativity can continue. Within a single world, its interacting parts can therefore build the circumstances of one another's next transformations.”

The central formal distinction to add immediately afterward is: this is the creation of **effective capacities**, rather than proof that underlying laws change or that novelty must grow without limit.

## Latest steering: generation generating generation

The user's “generation generating generation,” 生生不息, and 道法自然 invite an affirmative process interpretation: **what is generated can itself take part in generating what comes next**. Continuation need not preserve the original object or its exact form. It can occur through successors, transformed capacities, shared conditions, or a changed environment that enables another process.

Thus “becomes future more” can mean that productive effects are repeatedly carried into later production. It need not mean a count of branching futures, increased fitness, or indefinite survival of the same individual. A stronger claim that these effects become *more prevalent* requires a further mechanism of differential transmission or reproduction, with adequate resources and losses below replacement. Merely having a productive consequence does not supply those conditions.

Useful concise wording: “Generation continues by passing into its own consequences: what has been produced becomes part of how further production happens. Sometimes the capacity is renewed in the same organization; sometimes it continues through changed forms and other participants.” This is a proposed explanatory reading of the user's phrases, not a claim that the cited traditions all assert an identical formal theory.
