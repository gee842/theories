# Nonseparation, self, and unity: primary-source comparison

Research note, 5 October 2026. Prepared for the new branch while preserving the existing theories. Read `outputs/next-root-joint-organization.md` before this comparison. This note offers a sympathetic philosophical synthesis, not evidence that all traditions secretly assert an identical metaphysics.

## The positive advance

The user's direction can be made stronger by moving from the number of fundamental things to the status of their boundaries:

> **A distinction can be real and consequential without being self-sufficient. Its identity and powers can depend on the relations and conditions through which it exists. The distinction between self and other is subject to the same inquiry.**

This formulation joins the previous report's account of emergent parts to a first-person and ethical investigation. The observer who makes a distinction, the organism whose identity is maintained, and the social group that declares an outsider all belong inside the account. They do not receive an unexplained exemption from dependence simply because one of them is called “me” or “us.”

The deepest useful reading of nonseparation may therefore be **no independently constituted standpoint outside the relationships under examination**. This does not require every event to affect every other event, erase information barriers, or imply that different people have interchangeable experiences. It motivates locating every proposed self within its actual conditions of formation, maintenance, knowledge, and action.

Calling this a universal hypothesis is appropriate; declaring it established merely because we name the totality “one” is not. A unifying conception acquires content when it explains why particular boundaries arise, which dependencies they preserve, and when their claimed independence fails.

## What the primary texts actually contribute

### Early Buddhist not-self and dependent arising

SN 22.59 tests identification with bodily form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness. Their susceptibility to change and suffering, together with the inability to command them completely, undercuts treating them as an enduring sovereign self. Its argument concerns mistaken identification and release from clinging. It does not posit a universal consciousness into which individual selves merge. Nor does the passage establish that practical personhood or all capacities for control disappear. [SN 22.59, Thanissaro Bhikkhu translation](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN22_59.html).

SN 12.15 redirects an existence/nonexistence polarity toward the conditioned arising and cessation of suffering. This makes dependencies and their transformation central. It does not endorse either a cosmic Self or the claim that nothing happens. The translation's introductory essay and footnotes are the translator's interpretation, separate from the discourse. [SN 12.15](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN12_15.html).

A separate primary ethical text extends goodwill to beings regardless of size, strength, visibility, or distance, and rejects despising others or wishing their suffering. This supplies an explicit ethical commitment; it is not a deduction from the cardinality of reality. [Sutta Nipata 1.8](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/KN/StNp/StNp1_8.html).

### Madhyamaka: dependency without a final self-sufficient substance

MMK 24.18–19 links dependent arising, emptiness, and dependent designation. Verses 24.8–10 maintain a distinction between conventional and ultimate truth and the necessity of convention for teaching. The chapter answers an objection that emptiness would destroy action and consequence. This gives a substantive route to a universal claim about the absence of inherent existence while retaining functioning distinctions. It is not the claim that there is one inherently existent super-object. Different readings of the chapter's vocabulary remain possible; the page presents Stephen Batchelor's translation alongside clearly marked Tsongkhapa commentary. [Nagarjuna, MMK chapter 24](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/book/mulamadhyamaka-karika-english/d/doc79738.html).

### Upanishadic nonduality and Shankara's reading

Chandogya 6.2 begins with Being without a second. Shankara reads this as excluding another independent source and treats names and forms as differentiations of the same reality. His interpretation adds sentience to the origin of the universe; it is stronger than a claim that everything obeys a shared mathematical law. We should neither silently remove that claim nor treat his description of rival Buddhist positions as a neutral statement of Buddhism. [Chandogya 6.2 with Shankara's commentary, Ganganatha Jha translation](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-shankara-bhashya/d/doc1145305.html).

Chandogya 6.8.7 identifies the subtle essence, the Self of all, and Svetaketu. Shankara explicitly interprets the universe's Self as Being, its source and reality. This affirms ultimate Self rather than the Buddhist refusal to identify conditioned aggregates as self. His discussion traces bodily existence through sustaining conditions, but its ancient physiology is not modern scientific evidence. The metaphysical conclusion needs evaluation independently of those details. [Chandogya 6.8 with Shankara's commentary](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-shankara-bhashya/d/doc1145311.html).

For a modern traditional rendition of the verse and its Sanskrit, [Lokeswarananda's translation of 6.8.7](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-english/d/doc239302.html) was also checked. Its commentary identifies the changing names and forms with an unchanging essence. This is precisely why “all traditions mean the same dynamics” is too quick: in this reading the ultimate is not itself a changing dynamical organization.

### Zhuangzi: oneness includes a critique of saying “one”

Chapter 2 joins a statement of unity between the speaker and the myriad things to an immediate reflection on language: “One and Speech are two; two and one are three.” The same chapter explores the mutual dependence of this/that perspectives and the noises produced through differing apertures. It gives us a useful tension: shared reality can support diverse responses, while turning unity into one final conceptual possession can recreate the division it purports to overcome. That is an interpretation of the passage, not a consensus account of every Daoist school. [Zhuangzi chapter 2, James Legge translation](https://ratmachines.com/philosophy/chuang-tzu-legge/chapter-2).

The Chinese wording and the quoted English were corroborated in the [Chinese Text Project search result](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/adjustment-of-controversies/ens). Direct opens of that site failed. Legge's introductory and footnote interpretations are Victorian scholarship and should be distinguished from the translated chapter. The public-domain translation is useful primary evidence, not a claim that its English settles contested terminology.

## A comparison that preserves both convergence and disagreement

| Inquiry | Early Buddhist discourses / Madhyamaka | Advaita reading represented here | Zhuangzi chapter 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| What undermines ordinary exclusive identity? | Conditioned experience fails the proposed self's demands; inherent existence is challenged. | The individual identification mistakes limiting names and forms for ultimate identity. | Fixed perspective and linguistic opposition cannot exhaust the changing whole. |
| Does the inquiry affirm an ultimate Self? | These sources do not; Madhyamaka resists self-sufficient essence. | Yes, in the cited Shankara reading. | The cited passage does not supply the Advaita argument or terminology. |
| What is the useful shared question? | How does attachment to a bounded identity distort understanding and action? | How is apparent separateness related to what is more fundamental? | How do our distinctions and commitments arise, and when do they become obstructive? |

This table is a comparison of selected passages, not an exhaustive classification of Buddhism, Hindu thought, or Daoism. In particular, the Upanishads have several Vedanta interpretations, and Buddhist traditions vary in their treatment of consciousness, personhood, and the ultimate. The common question can be real without an identical answer.

## The bridge from “no self” to “no us versus them”

Three transformations must be distinguished:

1. **Ontological:** abandoning the assumption that a person or group exists entirely through itself.
2. **Epistemic:** recognizing that one's standpoint and interests do not exhaust what is real or relevant.
3. **Ethical:** declining to treat a group's identity alone as a sufficient reason to disregard outsiders' interests.

The first supports investigation of the second, but neither logically contains the third. An interconnected predator-prey system exhibits dependence without supplying equal concern. A single organized institution can coordinate exploitation. These are logical reminders of the missing ethical premise, not warnings against the user's direction.

The strongest ethical bridge states that premise openly: **if suffering and agency provide reasons for consideration, merely changing whose they are does not remove those reasons.** Obligations can still depend on causal involvement, promises, vulnerability, and capacity. Thus overcoming antagonistic identity need not eliminate particular responsibilities or erase another person's refusal.

Self/other can continue to mark where experience, knowledge, action, or accountability is located. What becomes contestable is the promotion of that location into absolute metaphysical independence or automatic moral privilege. On this interpretation, nonseparation increases attention to differentiated consequences: it does not make them disappear.

## Logical checks on the proposed root

**Universal scope is not the same as numerical unity.** A principle can apply to every member of a domain containing many entities. Conversely, positing one fundamental entity does not determine that entity's structure, laws, or what follows ethically. Monism can organize an explanation; mere counting cannot finish it.

**Sharing a source is not sharing all properties.** Even in a unified process, one local region may be hot and another cold. Deriving a part's behavior requires its position, organization, and boundary conditions. A universal foundation therefore needs a rule explaining differentiation, not an assertion that all distinctions are false.

**Absence of independent identity is not absence of functioning individuality.** The earlier four-site calculation demonstrates how dependence and relative autonomy coexist within an explicit model. It does not establish that all individuals in the universe have exactly that mechanism.

**Nonduality is not automatically one cosmic subject.** A change in the experience of self-boundaries, a metaphysical account of reality, and evidence of universal consciousness are different claims. The cited primary texts cannot substitute for a separate argument connecting them.

**Dependency is not always mutual all-to-all influence.** Causal dependence, constitution, statistical dependence, linguistic contrast, and ethical relevance require different tests. Calling all of them “connection” loses the mechanism the inquiry seeks.

## Strongest candidate for the new research branch

> **Whatever is individuated must be understood together with the conditions that make its difference possible. The same account must include the individual who knows and acts. Wisdom is participation that recognizes this dependence while answering to the distinct consequences it helps produce.**

This formulation leaves open whether dependence ultimately terminates in one fundamental substance, one generative process, a network of relations, or no independently grounded terminus. That openness is useful at this step: it allows the shared inquiry to become more precise before resolving the metaphysical disagreement.

The next positive question is: **which processes create, preserve, and revise a self/other boundary, and which interventions show when that boundary is a useful distinction versus an unjustified claim of independence or privilege?** This can join the previous dynamical analysis to inquiry about identity, institutions, and responsibility without reducing any tradition to a physics metaphor.
