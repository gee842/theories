The significant distinction is between (1) reproduction favored locally, (2) renewal of a support needed by the modeled population, and (3) ethical benefit to those affected. This model connects the first two. It does not identify either with the third.

## Supplied mechanism and mathematical scope

- Assertions that the unpenalized case converges to zero regenerative frequency require \(0<p_0<1\). A population initialized at \(p_0=1\) stays there without mutation; one initialized at \(p_0=0\) cannot acquire regeneration merely by changing \(s\).
- The shared term \(B(r)\) cancels from relative selection. Under the supplied payoffs, producing more common support does not by itself overcome the private regeneration cost. This is a feature of this payoff model, not a theorem that communal benefits can never favor regeneration.
- Positive \(s-c\) favors the supplied regenerative type, but its limiting frequency is not sufficient for uninterrupted viability. The support stock can cross its failure threshold before the frequency change repairs its decline.
- A viable limiting stock also requires an appropriate relationship between \(a/\delta\) and \(r_*\); equality and initial conditions need separate treatment.
- The equations are a pre-failure system. Continuing a formal solution after the declared failure does not establish that the original population recovers or persists. Absolute population size, reproduction, and extinction are not specified by a frequency equation alone.
- With \(s=c\), frequency is constant and stock tends to \(ap_0/\delta\). This boundary case is useful because it separates preserving an existing composition from restoring a depleted one.

## What the offset s does not explain

The constant \(s\) is a supplied type-specific payoff disadvantage. Calling it a penalty does not derive sensing, discrimination, enforcement, authority, or compliance. If a maintained institution implements it, its resources and continuing operation must be modeled. For example, a common material cost would change the stock equation to \(\dot r=ap-\delta r-\kappa(s,p,r)\); a private cost borne by regenerators could also change the selection differential. These are prospective extensions, not results of the present model.

Neither the best magnitude of a payoff disadvantage nor its ethical legitimacy follows from restoring \(r\). A coercive system can sustain itself. Social use also requires who bears burdens, who can contest classifications, how errors are corrected, and whose welfare or agency the maintained stock actually supports. These questions are not evidence against the mechanism; they delimit what it establishes.

## Why the two types are not good people and bad people

The type names identify supplied causal roles. Consumption without present regeneration need not be culpable or wrong: infants, disabled people, temporary recipients of care, and specialized members of a division of labor can depend on production by others. Conversely, something that replenishes one measured stock can harm beings or conditions omitted from the model. The stock and system boundary must therefore be justified independently of the desired ethical conclusion.

Cancer can illustrate divergence between reproduction at one level and viability at another. It is a disease process, not a morally responsible actor. The mathematical type labels do not classify illness or affected people as evil.

## Strongest warranted conclusion

The model can show that regenerative capacity may be selected against under a particular relation between private costs and shared benefits; modifying that relation can favor regeneration while still arriving too late to preserve its supports. A successful repair therefore requires both an adequate causal mechanism and a feasible transition.

For the broader theory, this makes the next research target concrete: identify arrangements that align local reproduction with renewal of shared supports, pay for maintaining those arrangements, and test their effects on independently specified welfare and agency. This is a route for investigating ethically favorable generativity, not a derivation of 善 or 恶 from evolutionary fitness.
