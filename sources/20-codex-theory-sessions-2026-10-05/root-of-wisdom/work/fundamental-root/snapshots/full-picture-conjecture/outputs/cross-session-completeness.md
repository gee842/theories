## Results recovered from the other work

The most consequential discovery is the existing **Toward a generative basis for alignment and coordination** study in the theories workspace. It already pursues this completeness question directly. Its proofs establish:

- An exact necessary-and-sufficient condition for a proposed finite state partition to preserve the specified intervention consequences.
- A terminating construction of the unique coarsest faithful partition under that strong state-level contract.
- A sufficient model boundary derived from the dependencies of supplied local mechanisms, rather than from a supplied full transition table.
- A restricted program language generating every finite rational stochastic kernel at declared execution boundaries.
- Conditions under which faithful modules remain faithful when composed, and counterexamples involving hidden guards, shared disturbances, and intermediate harms.

The third result is especially relevant to the root question. Begin with the outcomes, observations, and permissions the explanation must preserve. Include the state coordinates their mechanisms depend on, then repeat for the newly included coordinates. In a finite network this reaches a least dependency-closed set. Two initial states agreeing on that set, exposed to the same permitted inputs and joint noise, then have identical retained trajectories under corresponding information-respecting policies.

This can close a recursive dependency cycle without adding an endless hierarchy of governors. It establishes a sufficient representation of the supplied mechanisms. Whether those mechanisms maintain acceptable conditions is a further dynamical question, which the continuation results address.

The constructed example expands from **3 to 7 to 9 coordinates** and safely omits a decorative badge. When authority is changed to depend on that badge, the sufficient boundary expands to all ten. Under a specified three-step intervention, the two badge states then produce probabilities **3/8 versus 13/16** for an originally retained burden. Thus the omitted distinction changes an answer to the original question; this is more than an internal bookkeeping difference.

## What completeness can now mean precisely

Fix a finite controlled process with:

1. declared local dynamics, possible disturbances, and initial uncertainty;
2. each actor's actual observations, permissions, and admissible controls;
3. explicit resources, timing, termination, and intermediate events where consequential;
4. specified protection and recurring-progress objectives.

For the single-controller class covered by our finite partial-observation results, the recovered representation work and this session's decision theorems compose as follows:

\[
\boxed{\text{The concrete process admits a permitted successful policy}
\iff
\text{its faithful representation admits one}.}
\]

The representation must preserve the actor's information, not merely the analyst's state. Retaining nine coordinates in a mathematical model does not give an actor permission to observe all nine. Policies on either side use the same permitted observation histories.

For sure guarantees, every supported represented path must also lift to a concrete path. The faithful quotient supplies this: a positive transition into an abstract block means that every concrete representative has a positive transition to some state in that block. Repeating this construction lifts finite and infinite supported paths while preserving the specified labels. Equality of path probabilities alone would be insufficient for sure recurring progress, because exceptional infinite paths can have probability zero.

The fixed-point and finite-memory constructions already proved in this session then decide sure safety and safe recurring progress exactly for this class. If a suitable policy exists they supply a controller; otherwise the characterization rules it out within the specified class. Finite observation-compatible coding also supplies a direct representation when no useful compression exists.

For this effective finite theorem, boundary inputs are either treated as arbitrary allowed adversarial choices or generated by a specified finite process included in the state. An arbitrary unmodeled infinite input schedule is not silently converted into a finite decision problem. The theorem does not supply a general decentralized synthesis algorithm.

**This is a completed relative-completeness result.** Relative means the domain, questions, information, and controller standard are declared; it does not mean approximate within that domain. It covers infinitely many finite instances. It is a mathematical composition, not a claim that a new unified software implementation or Lean certification was completed in this pass.

Agreement with a conventional exact model under the same conditions is compatible with this completeness claim. Whether the formulation supplies original mathematics, a better explanation, or practical advantage is a separate question; those tests should not be substituted for the stated mathematical criterion.

The actual installed process is a separate object. Its dynamics may fail to select or preserve an available successful controller. The other continuation study supplies finite Markov-process criteria for that question, including probability-one safety and recurrent events. Sure guarantees, probability-one guarantees, finite-horizon risk, and endpoint competence remain distinct.

The [composition proof](completeness-bridge.md) states the two-way policy-transfer conditions. A forward transfer of guarantees alone is not enough: a conservative model can wrongly rule out a successful concrete policy. Nor does general state expressiveness alone establish a transfer preserving information, cost, or authority.

## How to extend the completed foundation

The next work should target specific unfinished interfaces. It need not restart the completed modules.

**First, certify one joint mechanism from the existing parts.** Include candidate formation, fresh checking, admission, execution, maintenance, succession, and rule revision wherever they affect the chosen outcome. Derive the dependency closure and check that each component's outputs satisfy the next component's premises. This is where common disturbances, reused evidence, lost records, insufficient reserves, and information privileges can break a seemingly valid composition. Existing studies close substantial subsets; the reviewed records do not certify their entire conjunction under one shared contract.

**Second, state which enlargement is being proved.** Examples include admitting a richer candidate grammar, another actor's protected outcome, a new intervention, an unknown change time, or an unbounded state space. The generative-basis theorem already proves that adding questions or interventions can require a finer sufficient representation. Changing a claim's domain therefore calls for a new coverage proof, not necessarily new fundamental principles. The earlier arbitrary-set safety characterization supplies one extension beyond finite states, but does not automatically give executable selectors or a complete decision algorithm there.

**Third, validate the interpretation through intervention predictions.** The remaining empirical task is more specific than accumulating examples described with the same words. Measure the proposed mechanisms independently, specify how interventions and outcomes correspond, freeze predictions, and test them against strong alternatives. Existing ecological and human-data analyses provide bounded, mixed evidence. They do not yet establish a common causal mechanism across domains. A measured finite-horizon error bound could justify approximate transfer; better average prediction alone does not establish such a bound.

**Fourth, keep the stronger philosophical claims distinct.** A conditional theory of protected continuation can state its normative commitments openly. A claim that those commitments are uniquely justified, that the proposed ontology exhausts reality, or that meaningful novelty is cosmically inexhaustible requires an additional argument. Those are not prerequisites for the finite operational completeness now established.

The strongest defensible update is therefore: **we already have more than disconnected examples—a composable, conditionally complete finite foundation, plus working models of several mechanisms that renew or revise its conditions.** Its extension to the programme's full intended scope remains an explicit research obligation. None of the inspected sessions claims to have completed that extension with a proof that survives its own qualifications.
