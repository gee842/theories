# Primary sources for the scientific principles investigation

Reading record, 5 October 2026. This focused map separates verified primary passages, our conditional reconstructions, and leads whose full texts were not available. It is not a comprehensive history of scientific method. The accompanying [investigation](scientific-principles-investigation.md) supplies the arguments and worked examples.

## Philosophy of scientific testing

**Karl Popper, Science Conjectures and Refutations.** Lecture delivered in 1953, first published in 1957, later chapter 1 of *Conjectures and Refutations* (1963). Read primary extracts on compilation pp. 183–185, especially p. 184. Relevant to risky prediction and the distinction between truth and scientific status; current measurement limitations are distinguished from logical testability. [Primary-text compilation](https://kpfu.ru/staff_files/F812164186/HISTORY_AND_PHILOSOPHY_OF_SCIENCE_COMMON_PROBLEMS.pdf)

**Popper, The Logic of Scientific Discovery.** English edition 1959, §§5–7, read on compilation pp. 208–212. Relevant to the logical asymmetry of universal statements and counterinstances, fallible observation, and methodological responses to evasive auxiliary changes. We do not attribute simple infallible one-observation refutation to Popper. The separate full-book download was incomplete and was not used. [Verified extracts](https://kpfu.ru/staff_files/F812164186/HISTORY_AND_PHILOSOPHY_OF_SCIENCE_COMMON_PROBLEMS.pdf)

**Pierre Duhem, La Théorie physique son objet et sa structure.** 1906, Part II, chapter VI §§I–III, original pp. 295–312; especially §II pp. 301–308. Read the French primary text with linked facsimile provenance. His analysis of connected hypotheses in physical testing motivates the measurement-package example. Its stated domain should not be silently expanded into a claim about every belief. [Chapter VI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Th%C3%A9orie_physique/SECONDE_PARTIE/Chapitre_VI)

**Duhem on physical measurement.** Same book, Part II, chapter IV §§I–II, pp. 233 onward. Read the instrument example separating visible readings from their theoretical interpretation. It supports attention to measurement premises, not the conclusion that evidence is arbitrary. [Chapter IV](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Th%C3%A9orie_physique/SECONDE_PARTIE/Chapitre_IV)

**Thomas S Kuhn, Logic of Discovery or Psychology of Research.** In *Criticism and the Growth of Knowledge* (1970), pp. 1–23. Read printed pp. 4–10 in the publisher-distributed preview, PDF pp. 12–18. Kuhn's unit of discussion is research practice and its development, especially puzzle solving and the preparation of severe tests. His position is not equivalent to our information inequality. Historical examples are his arguments, not newly verified history in this investigation. [Publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139926973_A24437007/preview-9781139926973_A24437007.pdf)

**Imre Lakatos, Science and Pseudoscience.** 1973 BBC/Open University talk, preserved LSE transcript with later additions visibly marked. Read transcript pp. 4–6. Relevant to research programmes, new predictions, and the timescale of appraisal. The source's polemical characterization of Kuhn is not adopted as a neutral description. Access was through a preserved copy whose initial pages identify the LSE provenance. [Preserved transcript](https://brutalminds.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Science-and-Pseudoscience-Overview-and-Transcript-_-Philosophy-Logic-and-Scientific-Method-1973-1.pdf)

These sources supply genuine competing methodological accounts. None establishes our finite-probability claims by historical authority. Those claims are proved separately. Likewise, our proofs do not settle these authors' disagreements about demarcation, discovery, or scientific change.

## Statistics and learning

**Steven Howard, Aaditya Ramdas, Jon McAuliffe and Jasjeet Sekhon, Time uniform Chernoff bounds via nonnegative supermartingales.** *Probability Surveys* 17 (2020), 257–317, DOI 10.1214/18-PS321. The 2020 author version v7 was inspected; a later arXiv version also exists. This is primary mathematical support for time-uniform boundary crossing methods. Our coin martingale proof is an elementary instance, not a reproduction of the paper's general results. [Inspected version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.03204v7)

**Aaditya Ramdas and colleagues, Game Theoretic Statistics and Safe Anytime Valid Inference.** *Statistical Science* (2023), DOI 10.1214/23-STS894. A primary modern treatment of e-processes and confidence sequences. Useful for the next extension to composite nulls and adaptive protocols. The report does not claim that fitting a null denominator automatically gives an e-process. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-STS894)

**Cynthia Dwork and colleagues, Generalization in Adaptive Data Analysis and Holdout Reuse.** 2015. Primary full text and abstract were accessed. It formalizes adaptive reuse and constructs controlled mechanisms; it supports neither unrestricted holdout reuse nor a universal requirement for wholly fresh data. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629)

**Dwork and colleagues, Preserving Statistical Validity in Adaptive Data Analysis.** STOC 2015; inspected §§1.1–1.2 and 5. A companion foundation for adaptive queries and generalization under explicit data and mechanism assumptions. The developing framework does not extend its guarantees to drift merely by naming causal participation. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.2664)

**Tilmann Gneiting and Adrian Raftery, Strictly Proper Scoring Rules Prediction and Estimation.** *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 102 (2007), DOI 10.1198/016214506000001437. Author-hosted primary text inspected. Supports the scoring-rule context of the elementary Brier identity. Truthful reporting incentives under a supplied belief are distinct from whether that belief is empirically calibrated. [Author copy](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)

## Causal inference and changing targets

**Judea Pearl, An Introduction to Causal Inference.** *International Journal of Biostatistics* 6(2), article 7 (2010), DOI 10.2202/1557-4679.1203. Full text accessed, especially §§2 and 4.3. Relevant to distinguishing a causal target, assumptions, identification, and estimation; models can agree observationally and disagree on interventions. Our binary structural example is a self-contained construction. We do not adopt every comparative claim in this survey about other causal traditions. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836213/)

**Juan Perdomo, Tijana Zrnic, Celestine Mendler Dünner and Moritz Hardt, Performative Prediction.** ICML 2020, PMLR 119, pp. 7599–7609. Inspected §2, Definitions 2.1 and 2.3, Example 2.2, and Theorem 3.5. Provides a formal example of predictions changing the distribution they face. This is a particular feedback model; its conditions are not consequences of the framework's general vocabulary. [Primary paper and metadata](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/perdomo20a.html)

## Replication and criticism

**National Academies of Sciences Engineering and Medicine, Reproducibility and Replicability in Science.** 2019, DOI 10.17226/25303. Read Summary pp. 1–6 and chapter 5, especially pp. 73–75 and 85–90. This is the primary committee report for the terminology and methodological judgments cited. It is a consensus report, not an experimental proof that replication always improves inquiry. [Summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25303/chapter/3), [chapter 5](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25303/chapter/8)

**Barbara Mellers, Ralph Hertwig and Daniel Kahneman, Do frequency representations eliminate conjunction effects.** *Psychological Science* 12 (2001), pp. 269–275. Inspected pp. 269–270 and Table 1 for an adversarial-collaboration protocol. It is an original example of jointly agreed tests and recorded expectations; disagreement remained. It does not establish the universal superiority of adversarial collaboration. [Author-hosted article](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/2001---do-frequency-representations-eliminate-conjunction-effects.pdf)

## Incomplete readings and follow up

**Larry Laudan, The Demise of the Demarcation Problem.** 1983, pp. 111–127. Publisher metadata and introduction were accessible; the complete chapter was not successfully retrieved. Detailed page-specific criticism is therefore not attributed as directly verified evidence here. [Publisher record](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-009-7055-7_6)

**Jerzy Neyman and Egon Pearson, On the Problem of the Most Efficient Tests of Statistical Hypotheses.** 1933, DOI 10.1098/rsta.1933.0009. The original scan was located but not fully retrieved. Retained as a primary historical lead; the report's elementary testing proof does not depend on an unverified passage. [Record](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1933.0009)

**David Blackwell, Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments.** 1953, DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177729032. Bibliographic record and abstract were accessible; a complete primary-text reading remains outstanding. The simple policy-inclusion proof in our notes stands on its stated assumptions. [Record](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032)

**A P Dawid, Statistical Theory The Prequential Approach.** 1984. Publisher summary and primary PDF were located, but this pass did not complete a page-by-page reading. It is a relevant next comparison for prediction-first objectives, not authority for additional claims in the report. [Publisher summary](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/2981683)

Priority follow-up is a complete primary severe-testing treatment, Laudan's full chapter and responses, and a careful comparison of Bayesian, likelihood, and error-statistical meanings of adverse evidence. None should be silently equated with the others.
