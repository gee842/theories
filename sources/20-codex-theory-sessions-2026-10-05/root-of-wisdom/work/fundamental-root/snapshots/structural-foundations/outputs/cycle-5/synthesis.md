# The same constraint, different outcomes: from repair to population renewal

Fifth research cycle, 5 October 2026.

We now have an explicit mechanism in which a decision rule spreads to every member of a population and the population nevertheless disappears. The copying institution rewards an early financial result, while the actual successor population must pay for the whole renewal process. A different use of the same available evidence selects a rule whose successors remain financed under the declared external support.

This advances the proposed cross-scale account: **continuation depends on whether the actual process preserves or reconstructs the conditions needed for its next required transition.** At the population level, those conditions include funded successors, inherited apparatus, usable evidence, and the joint reliability of copying. A growing share of a rule does not establish that these conditions are being met.

The results are conditional proofs and exact synthetic examples. They do not establish a universal empirical law or a new mathematical foundation. The contribution is a connected, auditable mechanism spanning paid repair, actual choice, evidence, inheritance, and population renewal.

## 1. What the common constraint now means

The earlier cycles distinguished a physically possible response from one that is affordable, authorized, informed, timely, and actually selected. The fourth cycle added decision rules that choose and learn. This cycle makes differential copying and the financing of actual descendants explicit.

For a declared duty and environment, three questions remain separate:

1. **Capacity:** does an admissible information-based policy exist that can meet the duty?
2. **Actual behavior:** does the implemented rule meet it from the current state?
3. **Renewal:** can the required apparatus, rule, records, and operating reserve reach a successor that can meet its next duty?

These are properties of a process relative to a duty, horizon, information interface, and environment. They are not intrinsic virtues attached to an isolated object. A finite task may end successfully; a population's disappearance is a failure here because continuing population renewal is the declared test.

A useful operational state is

\[
P=(\{z_i\}_{i=1}^{N},R,e),
\]

where each carrier state contains its plant, current rule, usable memory, accessible resources, and relevant rights; `R` is accessible shared support and `e` is the relevant environment. The implemented transition includes operation, observation, interpretation, allocation, construction, transmission, and retirement. Its kernel describes a mechanism only after those steps have been specified. Merely adding their names to a state tuple would explain nothing.

The common constraint is therefore a requirement on an implemented sequence: its necessary distinctions and access must reach the relevant decision; each physical and financial prefix must be feasible; and completion must leave a state satisfying the next obligation. The duty determines what must continue. The mechanism determines what actually happens.

## 2. An actual population selects a rule that cannot renew itself

The [funded-selection experiment](funded-selection.md) uses the previous eight-module repair process. Program S plans one repair cycle ahead; L plans two. Both maximize their own finite forecast of terminal cash. Each new organization starts with cash 240 and no diagnostic channels.

| Actual outcome | S | L |
|---|---:|---:|
| Installed diagnostic channels | 2 | 4 |
| Cash after cycle one | 140 | 128 |
| Cash after cycle two | 100 | 136 |

Every parent operates for **two cycles under both evaluation procedures**, then retires at the same physical time. Its cash returns to a common treasury; its apparatus has no salvage. A newborn costs 260: 240 transferred working cash, 16 for its basic plant, and four for program copying. Later channel purchases come from its working cash. Construction and copying have no additional modeled delay.

An external grant supplies 150 per active parent per epoch, and the treasury prepays two per parent for observation and record retention. Production also uses declared external inputs. The original two founders require a separately recorded founding endowment of 520. These assumptions make the system's funding boundary explicit.

For counts `N_S,N_L`, total `N`, and treasury `R`, the actual number of funded successors is

\[
M=\min\!\left(4,\left\lfloor
\frac{R+148N+100N_S+136N_L}{260}
\right\rfloor\right).
\]

Only after determining affordable births does the copying rule assign their programs. Treasury is capped at 259 after paying for births, and overflow is discarded. No grant arrives for an empty population; its remaining treasury cannot fund a 260-unit birth. There is no immigration. A maximum-cash rule copies S when it uses cycle-one cash and L when it uses cycle-two cash. The timing of births, grants, and retirement is identical in both treatments.

Starting with one carrier of each program:

| Copying criterion | First successor population | Later result |
|---|---|---|
| Raw early cash | Two S; S share reaches 100% | No successor can be funded at epoch 23 |
| Later cash | Two L | Nonempty indefinitely under the stated inputs; reaches four sites at epoch 11 |
| Predicted later cash from the early report | Two L | Identical funded transition law to later-cash evaluation |

The early-selected population ends with treasury 256, just below one birth packet. Its share is then undefined. Cash has not literally fallen to zero, and copying scores have never been treated as resources.

## 3. A renewal inequality explains the divergence

Let `s` denote discarded treasury overflow. The replacement-funding potential

\[
\Phi=260N+R
\]

obeys the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\Phi'=\Phi-12N_S+24N_L-s.}
\]

This potential values each current carrier at the funding required for its replacement; it is not literal cash inventory. The separate cash ledger treats a child's 240 as a transfer and charges only actual expenses as payments leaving the system.

Every nonempty all-S epoch decreases finite `Φ` by at least twelve, proving eventual extinction. In an all-L population with faithful copying, each parent contributes 284 to the pool, enough for its own 260-unit successor; nonemptiness is invariant. The base grant lies between the exact per-parent replacement thresholds: **126 for L, 162 for S**. Raising support to 162 makes S renewal sustainable; removing the grant makes both copying treatments fail to renew. The result depends on operating behavior together with the funding and retirement institutions.

More generally, a specified handoff with no interim income needs

\[
b\ge k_0+\sum_j(k_j+r_j)+r_{\mathrm{retained\ parent}},
\]

where setup and copying fees are `k`, usable successor reserves are `r`, and retained parents have their own reserve requirement. Under the stated construction, timing, access, and transmission conditions, this is a sufficient funding test and is necessary when every listed payment and reserve is mandatory. It is not a claim that money alone supplies materials, permissions, or information. The [population proof](population-and-scale.md) states the full hypotheses and a repeated-succession invariant.

This is the same prefix-financing constraint that appeared inside a repair, now applied to the creation of a carrier capable of performing the next repair. Its form recurs; its thresholds and outcomes change with the mechanism.

## 4. Better evidence must still leave an executable successor

The early financial record already distinguishes S from L in this example. A supplied correct predictor reads 128 as the investment signature of the better two-cycle return. Its entire population kernel matches later evaluation on all 3,900 states checked. The population experiment therefore does **not** establish a need to wait for later evidence; it establishes a consequence of the supplied interpretation rule.

The separate [paid-evaluation experiment](paid-evaluation.md) prices waiting, observation, retention of the selected source address, and transfer to one successor. Its accounting boundary differs: the two trial plants and the successor's basic body are separately endowed. The thresholds below cannot be substituted directly into the population birth bill.

| Protocol | Least evaluator/successor reserve guaranteeing continuation |
|---|---:|
| Correct early interpretation, copy program only | 223 |
| Later cash ranking, copy program only | 233 |
| Correct early interpretation, move donor's channels too | 106 |
| Later cash ranking, move donor's channels too | 116 |

At reserve 230, early calibrated evaluation leaves 215 for a cold successor and succeeds; later evaluation leaves 205 and fails despite identifying the same L program. At reserve 120, later program-only copying fails, while transferring the donor's four channels leaves 92 and succeeds. The donor retires and loses those channels: the assets are moved, never cloned.

If four channels are already inherited, even S can maintain the plant. In that state extra evaluation can consume reserve without improving the required behavior. Removing future faults likewise removes the need for diagnostic infrastructure. Conversely, when the observation interface reports only whether the first repair passed, both programs look identical; no evaluator using that report alone can reliably identify L under both hidden address assignments.

These controls separate four issues: information availability, its interpretation, its physical retention, and the resources needed to act on it. A correct judgment does not automatically cross a handoff or pay for its own implementation.

## 5. Population reliability needs a joint law and a stated horizon

Under weighted rather than winner copying, each funded child independently samples a parent's program in proportion to the observed score. Exact rational calculations, combined with persistent and doomed classes, bound eventual nonextinction:

| Faithful copying | Eventual nonextinction probability |
|---|---:|
| Raw early cash | Between 0.4553133970 and 0.4553135483 |
| Later cash, or calibrated early prediction | Between 0.6490735099 and 0.6490735989 |

These are certified eventual-survival bounds, not Monte Carlo estimates or merely survival through the simulated horizon. The interval width is the unresolved probability of still being mixed after 24 epochs. Even the better criterion can lose all L carriers through finite-population sampling.

Recurring independent variation introduces a further distinction. At copying error `μ=0.01`, extinction by epoch 30 is lower than with faithful copying because variation can restore L after its loss. Nevertheless, for every fixed `0<μ<1`, eventual extinction is almost sure in this bounded, unrescued model. A sufficiently long all-S sequence has uniformly positive conditional probability and exhausts the bounded potential. The proof supplies no practical estimate of extinction time; a finite-horizon improvement and this asymptotic result are compatible.

Marginal error rates alone do not settle that conclusion. The companion proof gives two equally funded copying schemes with the same expected valid offspring count of one. One guarantees exactly one valid child; the other creates two independently valid candidates with probability one-half each. The first persists, while the second is a classical nondegenerate critical branching process and dies out almost surely. Dependence among copies changes the outcome even when their individual validity rates and expected counts agree.

## 6. What can be carried across scale

The [population correspondence theorem](population-and-scale.md) extends the earlier actual-process result. For finite or countable state and event spaces, a state map `φ` and event map `χ` give a sufficient condition through equality of the **whole projected joint kernel**:

\[
\sum_{\chi(e)=\bar e,\;\phi(P')=\bar P'}
K_{\mathrm{fine}}(P;e,P')
=K_{\mathrm{coarse}}(\phi(P);\bar e,\bar P').
\]

With corresponding initial distributions and tests, this preserves the translated path law. Extinction transfer additionally requires that a mapped population is empty exactly when the original is empty. Prefix safety and timely completion inside each census transition need their own certificates.

The correspondence must preserve relevant funding, retirement, rounding, caps, observation timing, and joint transmission. Replicating a physical system with independent copies generally changes its extinction probabilities; synchronized packet replication can admit an exact map. Matching type shares or mean offspring is insufficient.

This gives a precise version of the proposed universality: the same feasibility and correspondence requirements can be posed at several scales, while changed support, information, timing, and dependence predict different outcomes. Exact equality across scales is a stronger claim requiring an exhibited mapping. Neither structural resemblance nor shared terminology provides that mapping.

## 7. What is established, and the next discriminating test

The connected result is now stronger than “systems need resources.” Supplied objectives generate different infrastructure; paid observations alter actual selection; inheritance changes what the same rule can execute; funded population dynamics can amplify a rule while eliminating its carriers; and joint copying determines the strength of continuation guarantees. Each connection has an explicit mechanism, a proof or finite certificate, and a control that can reverse the result.

The general ingredients have substantial prior art. The [source audit](population-audit.md) connects specified payoff-to-reproduction dynamics, resource-dependent branching, and classical extinction theory to their original assumptions. No novelty or empirical universality follows from combining them here.

The present outer evaluator, funding rule, and retirement institution remain supplied. A useful next formal target is **paid revision of the evaluator when its calibration becomes wrong**: introduce a declared regime change, give the evaluator only funded observations and retained records, allow a priced switch of criterion, and test whether revision completes before successor finance is lost. The unchanged-evaluator control and the cost of acquiring evidence should be fixed before comparing outcomes. This would extend the current mechanism without attributing foresight to an unexplained outer selector.

For an empirical bridge, first compare the same repair-and-renewal function at two physical sizes. Measure payment order, downtime, evaluation and inheritance costs, resource inputs, and copying dependence independently of the outcome. Predict both absolute continuation and composition under a specified intervention. A failed prediction should identify an incorrect mechanism or scale mapping; it should not be absorbed by declaring whatever happened “the constraint.” Cross-domain claims require additional evidence after that test.
