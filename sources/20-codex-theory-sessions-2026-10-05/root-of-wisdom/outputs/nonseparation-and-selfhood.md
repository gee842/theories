# Nonseparation and the conditions of selfhood

New branch for Ren, 5 October 2026. All previous theories are preserved. This investigation takes the proposal of one common reality seriously and asks what it would explain about universal truth, selfhood, and the distinction between us and others.

The strongest working hypothesis is:

> **Selves, observers, and groups are differentiations within the reality they inhabit. An account of their identity and action must include the conditions and relations that make them possible. Drawing a boundary cannot by itself remove those dependencies or their consequences.**

This advances the [previous branch](next-root-joint-organization.md), which explained when relatively autonomous parts can be identified. The new step applies the same inquiry to the participant who draws the boundary. The proposed foundation includes knowing and acting within its scope.

The hypothesis is compatible with a form of monism, but universal scope still needs substantive justification. The mathematical results below establish particular forms of nonseparation and maintained identity. The traditional texts contribute different philosophical arguments and practices. Their combination is an investigation, not a proof that every tradition or every phenomenon has already been reduced to one law.

## What universality would mean

We can give the user's proposal a precise requirement: the principle must apply consistently to a part, to its environment, to the larger whole, and to the observer studying them. It must explain why their situations differ without giving any of them an unexplained exemption.

That is stronger than merely collecting everything under one name. A conserved whole can contain changing parts. A common reality can contain different experiences, temperatures, capacities, and needs. Universal applicability therefore requires a rule relating those differences, rather than attributing every property of the whole to each part.

There is a useful exact example. Suppose a finite process has quantities \(x_i\), signed currents \(j_{ij}=-j_{ji}\) from site \(j\) to site \(i\), and specified local production-minus-loss terms \(s_i\):

\[
\dot x_i=s_i+\sum_jj_{ij}.
\]

For **every** region \(S\), define \(X_S=\sum_{i\in S}x_i\). Then

\[
\boxed{\frac{dX_S}{dt}
=\sum_{i\in S}s_i+
\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\j\notin S}}j_{ij}.}
\tag{1}
\]

The proof is cancellation: every internal exchange appears twice with opposite signs. The same law applies to each site, any group of sites, and the whole. For the whole, the boundary term is empty; for a part, it generally is not. Conversely, the singleton cases recover the original local equations.

Grouping regions and then grouping them again gives exactly the same balance as grouping the original sites directly. An exchange changes from external to internal when the boundary expands, and its two contributions then cancel. **Changing the accounting boundary cannot make the exchange disappear from the complete account.**

This is a precise form of universality with differentiation. The law has no privileged boundary. It does not claim that every physical quantity is conserved: the quantity, transfer mechanism, and source terms must have an independently justified interpretation. Nor does a balance equation guarantee that a region's total is enough to predict its future currents.

The same point applies to an observer's intervention. An input can be external to the subsystem being studied while originating in another part of the larger process. Including the controller, its resources, and its consequences changes the scope of the account. It does not require a controller outside reality.

## A precise sense in which a part is not self sufficient

In symmetric exchange, let

\[
j_{ij}=w_{ij}(x_j-x_i),\qquad w_{ij}=w_{ji}\ge0.
\tag{2}
\]

With no source terms, a nonempty proper region \(S\) has an exact autonomous law \(\dot x_S=f_S(x_S)\) valid for all nonnegative preparations **if and only if every exchange weight across its boundary is zero**.

If no weight crosses the boundary, the internal equations close. If a positive weight does cross, hold the whole internal state fixed and change the exterior endpoint. The derivative of an internal quantity changes. The same internal state would therefore require two different derivatives, contradicting an autonomous law.

Consequently, in a connected positive-weight exchange network, no nonempty proper region is exactly dynamically self-sufficient across all preparations. This preserves distinct regions and allows excellent approximate autonomy at appropriate scales, as the earlier four-site example showed. Vanishing current at one equilibrium does not establish independence under changed conditions.

This theorem provides one exact meaning for nonseparation. It does not establish that every pair of actual things influences each other, or that dynamical dependence proves the absence of a metaphysical self. Those claims require further premises.

It also exposes a limit of the balance principle: many different current mechanisms can produce the same local derivatives. A circulation around a cycle can even leave every site's instantaneous total unchanged. The common balance law therefore needs measured mechanisms and rates to explain the actual world. The retained theory's law-selection results remain necessary.

## Identity can persist through replacement

A second construction investigates how something can remain recognizable without retaining its original material.

Let \(x,y\) be abundances of two cooperating components. Each enables production of the other from supplied material; components also disappear, and crowding increases the loss of \(x\). With positive rates and a held resource condition \(f\ge0\), take

\[
\dot x=afy-\delta x-cx^2,\qquad
\dot y=bx-\mu y.
\tag{3}
\]

The rates and resource provision are premises, not a derivation of an organism. A strictly positive equilibrium exists exactly when

\[
abf>\delta\mu,
\]

and has

\[
x^*=\frac{abf/\mu-\delta}{c},\qquad
y^*=\frac b\mu x^*.
\tag{4}
\]

There is an all-positive-initial-state convergence proof. Set \(R=abf/\mu\), \(C=cx^*>0\), \(u=x/x^*\), \(v=y/y^*\), and

\[
W=u-1-\log u+\frac R\mu(v-1-\log v).
\]

Along the dynamics,

\[
\boxed{\dot W=-R\frac{(u-v)^2}{uv}-C(u-1)^2\le0.}
\tag{5}
\]

The function diverges as either positive coordinate approaches zero or infinity, and its derivative vanishes only at \(u=v=1\). Its compact sublevel sets and the displayed strict decrease establish convergence to the positive equilibrium.

Now label the material initially present. Assume new components use fresh supplied material, with no recycling of the original label into them, and that losses remove labeled material at the same per-unit rates as other material. Then

\[
\dot x_{\rm old}=-(\delta+cx)x_{\rm old},\qquad
\dot y_{\rm old}=-\mu y_{\rm old}.
\tag{6}
\]

Both original labeled amounts tend to zero while the maintained abundances tend to a positive regime. Their fractions therefore also tend to zero. In this continuous model the original label generally remains positive at every finite time; no instantaneous or finite-time total replacement is asserted.

The exact lesson is that a persistent organization need not consist of permanently retained original material. Functional roles and production relations still matter. Calling this organization the same continuing system uses an explicitly organizational criterion of identity; the equations do not settle personal identity, consciousness, or a religious doctrine of no-self.

The held resource condition marks an open-system boundary. A larger account must include whatever maintains it. If the supply changes, the regime may change or fail. This is precisely where the first result joins the second: maintained identity has conditions that a narrow description can conceal.

## What no self can mean in this investigation

We should distinguish the experienced standpoint, the capacity to act, the continuing organization, and the proposed independent owner of them. Questioning the last does not by itself settle the first three.

Our strongest research interpretation is that a self may be a situated organization of perception, memory, action, and bodily maintenance whose capacities depend on conditions it does not completely command. That hypothesis calls for mechanisms and evidence appropriate to living and experiencing beings. The exchange and replacement models clarify its structure; they do not establish its complete application to people.

The observer belongs within this inquiry. Their classifications have a history, their information arrives through channels, and their actions alter what they study. An adequate account includes those relations where they affect the conclusion. Universal scope does not confer a universal viewpoint on any particular observer.

This also returns us to the original question of noticing a missing possibility. If the same represented internal state can lead to different next observations when an omitted external condition changes, that condition supplies a consequential distinction the model lacks. The exchange theorem gives an exact witness. It suggests a search method: examine the conditions hidden by the chosen boundary, including the observer's measurement and intervention, and vary those that could change the conclusion. This can reveal specific omissions; it cannot guarantee discovery of every unknown possibility.

This also clarifies a further challenge for monism: the proposed whole must explain differentiated standpoints. Merely replacing many independent selves with a single unexplained owner would leave the mechanism of knowing and acting unresolved. Different monist and nondualist traditions answer that challenge differently.

## Comparing traditions by their arguments

The user's suggestion of convergence is worth investigating through what each tradition questions, what it affirms, and what change in understanding or conduct it seeks. Selected primary passages show both contact and disagreement:

| Source | Contribution to this inquiry | Distinction to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Early Buddhist not-self, SN 22.59 | Examines the changing, vulnerable, incompletely controllable aggregates and challenges identification with them as a sovereign self. | The discourse does not replace individual selves with one cosmic Self. [Primary translation](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN22_59.html). |
| Nagarjuna, MMK 24.8–10 and 18–19 | Connects dependent arising, emptiness, and dependent designation while retaining conventional discourse and functioning consequences. | The argument does not install one independently existing super-object beneath everything. [Chapter 24](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/book/mulamadhyamaka-karika-english/d/doc79738.html). |
| Chandogya 6.8.7 in Shankara's interpretation | Identifies the Self of all with the ultimate reality and the person being addressed. | This positively affirms ultimate Self. It is a substantive difference from the Buddhist arguments, not merely alternate vocabulary. [Text and commentary](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-shankara-bhashya/d/doc1145311.html). |
| Zhuangzi, chapter 2 | Places the speaker and the myriad things together in unity, then questions the distinctions introduced by speaking of them as one. | The passage investigates perspective and language; it is not a statement of the Advaita or Buddhist argument. [Legge translation](https://ratmachines.com/philosophy/chuang-tzu-legge/chapter-2). |

These are selected texts and interpretations, not summaries of entire cultures. In particular, an account of an unchanging ultimate reality is not automatically equivalent to a theory of universal becoming. A shared question can survive that disagreement: **what happens when an ordinary bounded standpoint mistakes itself for something independently complete?**

Zhuangzi's treatment is especially useful for our method. Calling reality one is itself an act of distinction within the reality being discussed. Our interpretation is that a foundation should account for that act as well as for its subject. The proposition of unity cannot gain authority simply by pretending to occupy a position outside all perspectives.

## From self and other to responsibility

No absolute separation can mean that another's situation cannot be excluded from a causal account merely because it lies outside our chosen boundary. The balance theorem gives an exact illustration of that requirement. Ethical concern adds a further premise:

> **If suffering, agency, or vulnerability provide reasons for consideration, changing whose they are does not by itself remove those reasons.**

This is a proposed ethical commitment, not a theorem derived from conservation or monism. Particular responsibilities can still depend on promises, relationships, causal involvement, and capacity. Distinct experiences and the ability to refuse remain relevant precisely because consequences occur at particular standpoints.

The other-session corpus already develops a compatible interpretation. Its discussion of nonduality emphasizes answering to another's reality without absorbing it into one's own objective. Later material preserves multiple centers of knowledge and refusal within a common world. This gives “no us versus others” a practical direction: investigate and loosen the assumption that group membership alone settles whose consequences count.

Under this reading, nonseparation deepens attention to differences. A gain to a whole does not tell us who bears a loss, and a shared dependency does not make that loss disappear. Those are questions the larger account must retain.

## The next root and its remaining burden

The next candidate is **participation within a common reality, with no privileged exemption for the self that knows or acts**. It connects the retained results as follows: interactions generate consequences; joint organization supports distinctions and relative autonomy; maintained organization supports continuity; and observers participate in the same conditions they investigate.

To develop this into a universal foundation, we must justify the extension beyond the proved model classes, specify the relation between physical organization and experienced selfhood, and compare the different accounts of ultimate unity. A proposition may be universally applicable without every part sharing the whole's properties. Numerical oneness alone does not choose the correct law or settle those philosophical questions.

The strongest concise formulation currently warranted as a research hypothesis is:

> **Understand each apparent self through the conditions of its differentiation, continuity, knowledge, and action. Apply that understanding to oneself as well. Let recognition of shared conditions inform responsibility for the distinct consequences one helps produce.**

The immediate next investigation is to make self/other boundaries change within a model: identify what creates and maintains them, how observations and actions depend on them, and which changes expose a useful boundary as an unjustified claim of independence. This would give the proposed connection between monism, no-self, and wisdom further explanatory content.
