# What continues when the material changes

Research continuation for Ren, 5 October 2026. Earlier theories are preserved. This investigation follows the correction that a useful mathematical pattern should not immediately be called a deeper metaphysical root. It examines one concrete case and keeps the meanings of continuity separate.

**The advance is a distinction between maintaining a pattern and continuing a particular history.** We can explain how a stable population is maintained while its original molecules leave. That does not, by itself, tell us whether a later population continued from it or was independently reconstructed. We also find that current activity can stop while a capacity to resume remains.

## Four questions hidden inside the word same

When we say something remains the same, we may be asking different questions:

| Question | Evidence it requires |
|---|---|
| Is the original material still present? | Tracking the original constituents. |
| Is a particular organization or capacity maintained? | Measuring its structure, activity, or response under specified conditions. |
| Did this later occurrence develop from that earlier one? | A history of the relevant carrying or reproductive process. |
| Can we identify which occurrence continued? | Observations that distinguish the candidate histories. |

These questions can have different answers. An observer can lose track of something that continues. A copy can reproduce a pattern without sharing the original's uninterrupted history. A temporarily inactive device can retain the physical capacity to operate again. The choice of question determines what evidence is sufficient; it does not make the answer arbitrary.

## One maintained chemical population

Consider an idealized, well-mixed vessel. Fresh precursor molecules, called A, enter. Liquid flows out at the same volume rate. Molecules called X help convert A into additional X:

\[
A+X\longrightarrow X+X.
\]

The original catalyst survives each conversion, while one new X is made from A. Assume the inflow contains no X, that X cannot arise spontaneously, and that material leaving the vessel does not return. These are premises of the model. The vessel and feed apparatus are supplied; this example does not explain the formation of a self-contained organism.

Let \(a\) and \(x\) be the two concentrations, \(a_{\rm in}\) the incoming precursor concentration, \(D\) the fraction of the vessel volume replaced per unit time, and \(k\) the reaction coefficient. Then

\[
\dot a=D(a_{\rm in}-a)-kax,
\qquad
\dot x=kax-Dx.
\tag{1}
\]

The first equation accounts for precursor supply, removal, and conversion. The second accounts for X production and removal. No separate organizing agent is hidden in the equations.

For \(k,D,a_{\rm in}>0\), every initial condition containing some X approaches a positive maintained concentration precisely when \(ka_{\rm in}>D\):

\[
a_* = D/k,
\qquad
x_* = a_{\rm in}-D/k>0.
\tag{2}
\]

At that state, production exactly balances removal. There is ongoing reaction despite constant concentrations. The [mathematical companion](identity-through-change-mathematics.md) proves the result, including the threshold and unseeded cases.

Now mark only the X molecules present initially, using a hypothetical neutral mark that is not passed to new molecules. At the maintained state, the fraction of original X remaining in the vessel is

\[
\boxed{\text{original fraction}=e^{-Dt}.}
\tag{3}
\]

After time \(5/D\), approximately 0.67 percent remains, while the total X concentration is unchanged. In this continuous model the original fraction approaches zero without reaching it at a finite time. This calculation predicts turnover under the specified assumptions; no labeling experiment was performed here.

An ancestry label answers a different question. Assign each newly made X to the ancestry of the catalyst that helped produce it. In the model, these ancestry classes propagate even as original molecules leave. Without outside X being introduced, descendants of an initial seed can maintain the population. An ancestry label is a record of production history, not a claim that an original atom or molecule has been preserved.

The earlier [maintenance investigation](nonseparation-and-selfhood.md) already established that organized activity can persist through material replacement. This example adds explicit material balance, different laws for original material and ancestry, and sharper interruption cases.

## Stopping activity does not have one meaning

The model distinguishes interventions that ordinary language might all call “stopping the process.”

| Change | What follows in this model |
|---|---|
| Remove some X but leave a positive seed | Under the sustaining conditions, the concentration recovers. |
| Remove every X from the vessel | Precursor alone cannot restart production, because spontaneous formation was excluded. |
| Replace the nutrient feed with blank solvent while outflow continues | Both A and X eventually wash out. |
| Stop both inflow and outflow | With X still present, remaining A is converted into X. Reaction activity approaches zero as A is exhausted, but X remains and can respond to renewed precursor. |
| Inhibit the reaction while outflow continues | X declines because production no longer replaces removal. |

Thus current activity, retained capacity, and uninterrupted population presence are distinguishable. A loss of activity does not automatically establish a loss of capacity. Conversely, retaining the vessel does not guarantee that the population inside it continues.

The exact zero case depends on the supplied reaction scheme. Real chemistry with spontaneous nucleation, an overlooked reservoir, or return flow can behave differently. Likewise, a positive concentration in a deterministic model is not a guarantee that a finite molecular population will never lose its last catalyst. The companion states that distinction explicitly.

## An identical present can conceal a different past

Compare two histories over a specified interval:

1. The X population remains present throughout, maintained by production and removal.
2. X is completely removed, and later an external preparation restores exactly the same precursor and X concentrations.

At the comparison time, the modeled reactor states agree. With the same subsequent inputs, their future concentration responses agree as well. Nevertheless, the statement “X remained present throughout that earlier interval” is true in the first history and false in the second.

No function of those current concentrations can answer the historical question: it receives identical inputs in both cases and would have to return different answers. The same limitation applies to future tests whose behavior depends only on that state. Surviving ancestry markers, records, or observations of the intervention would add information and could distinguish the histories.

This does not claim that complete states of the universe have merged. We are comparing a restricted reactor description; the external preparations and environmental records can differ. It also does not assume that uninterrupted X presence settles every philosophical question of identity. It establishes a definite historical difference before asking how to interpret it.

The resulting correction to our earlier emphasis on responses is important:

> **A description can be sufficient for every allowed future response and still be insufficient for a fact about how the present came about.**

Response equivalence remains useful for the questions it preserves. It is not, by itself, an account of all truth. The insufficiency belongs to the description; it does not make the historical fact relative to an observer.

## Contact with actual experiments

Liu and colleagues studied two chemical self-replicators under closed and flow conditions. Continued supply and removal favored a different replicator from the one favored after both flows stopped. This supports the dependence of a maintained population regime on production and removal. Their chemistry includes spontaneous emergence, and their model was qualitative; it is not an experimental verification of our absorbing zero-X assumption or our ancestry calculation. [Liu et al., 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/anie.202117605).

Tena-Solsona and colleagues measured fuel-driven assembly, dissolution, and renewed assembly in synthetic materials. Re-forming particles with similar characteristics after dissolution supports recurrence of a kind of organization. Our inference is that this evidence alone does not establish that an individual particle returned. Different materials in that study also showed different persistence behavior, so fuel exhaustion cannot be equated with immediate disappearance in every case. [Tena-Solsona et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms15895).

These are published experiments. The work here consists of an idealized model, proofs, exact checks, and conceptual analysis; no new physical experiment or reanalysis of the published raw data was conducted.

## What this lets us say about continuity

A continuing ancestry can branch. If one earlier occurrence has two distinct descendants, both can continue aspects of its organization or history. Calling both literally identical to the original would, under ordinary identity logic, imply they are identical to each other. A lineage relation therefore carries information that a simple “same individual” label cannot always express.

The useful finding is consequently specific:

> **Some patterns persist because existing activity produces replacements for what is lost. The original material can diminish while that causal succession continues. Determining which occurrence continued additionally requires a history.**

This does not require every thing to be an active process, every boundary to be conventional, or reality to contain only one fundamental entity. It gives us a mechanism by which change can sustain continuity, and a reason not to confuse renewed resemblance with an uninterrupted history.

For the wisdom inquiry, the practical consequence is to ask what exactly has been preserved after an apparent recovery: the measured output, the capacity to produce it, the earlier relationships and commitments, or merely a similar appearance. Those are substantive questions, and they can require different evidence.

The next unresolved formation question remains concrete: how can the structures that carry a persistent pattern, including its boundary and repair capacities, themselves arise and remain viable? The present model supplies its vessel and reaction law. It clarifies continuity within those conditions before claiming to explain their origin.
