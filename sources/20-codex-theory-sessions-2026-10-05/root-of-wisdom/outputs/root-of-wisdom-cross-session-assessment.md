## The positive root already proposed elsewhere

The full-picture session's latest conscience revision explicitly proposes **共同生成** as its ethical direction: take morally considerable beings' experiences, basic interests, and conditions of action seriously, and protect shared conditions for responding, repairing, and forming value.

Its central reason is non-arbitrary consideration: if suffering or a basic need provides a reason for concern, changing whose suffering or need it is cannot by itself erase that reason. Relevant differences can justify different treatment, but their relevance and the distribution of costs need explanation. This is a substantive normative position, not a consequence of evolutionary success or continued existence.

The source also gives the proposal essential limits. Moral standing does not depend on productivity, ability to argue, or capacity to reciprocate. Rest, withdrawal, ending harmful processes, and justified restrictions can serve the proposed good. Growth, innovation, cooperation, and stability do not independently establish goodness. **良知** is defined there as a fallible capacity to feel others' circumstances and reconsider one's own conduct; it must remain responsive to facts, affected parties, and examination of one's partiality.

Thus ethics is not simply an empty box in the other work. A candidate root has been stated and partly defended. It does not determine every conflict or compel a person who rejects all concern for others to accept it. That is a limit of the argument, not evidence that the commitment is arbitrary.

## A candidate root of wise judgment

My proposed integration is:

> Our candidate practical root is the capacity to bring concern for affected beings, understanding of the situation, and judgment about one's response into mutual correction.

This is a contemporary synthesis of these sessions, not a classical quotation, a translation of 道 or 慧根, or a theorem already established by the sources.

Its explanatory proposal lies in the connections. Concern guides which omissions deserve attention. Facts constrain how concern can be served. Contemplated responses expose missing facts, overlooked beings, and conflicting commitments. Judgment chooses whether to investigate, act, refrain, persist, or stop. Reflection can reopen the standards by which the whole process judges itself. Calling these connections a root remains a hypothesis about how wise judgment develops; listing admirable capacities would not itself prove one common source. Cultivation describes acquiring the capacity, rather than explaining the root by itself.

This also sharpens the question about missing possibilities. An important omission may concern a cause, a possible action, the interpretation of evidence, an excluded being, or a kind of harm our existing measure cannot express. Numerical surprise need not occur. We need encounters that can challenge what our current framework admits: linked records, different instruments, affected people's accounts, practical consequences, and alternatives to the current search rules. Each channel can mislead and requires appropriate scrutiny. No channel guarantees that every important omission will eventually become visible.

## Does the candidate survive the counterexamples?

These are conceptual checks against the proposed account, not a new empirical validation or independently scored wisdom benchmark.

| Counterexample | What the account must explain |
| --- | --- |
| An accurate, adaptable exploiter | Accurate learning can serve domination. Under the proposed ethical root, deliberately erasing others' relevant interests remains objectionable even when effective. |
| A caring person whose intervention causes avoidable harm | Sincere concern needs correction by evidence and affected experience. Good intentions do not settle practical adequacy. |
| A wise person denied power or resources | Lack of success does not establish lack of wisdom. Judgment must recognize available agency, including restraint, refusal, or seeking help; it cannot be required to accomplish the impossible. |
| Further diagnosis consumes the last opportunity to help | More inquiry can undermine the purpose it serves. The structural examples make this conflict concrete. |
| One acceptable action works across the remaining uncertainties | Investigation or explicit self-revision may be unnecessary. Tacit, settled, or immediate judgment can be appropriate. |
| An institution changes its score so that existing conduct appears successful | A changed internal criterion needs reasons. Preserve the consequences and objections visible under the earlier criterion; a genuine moral correction must remain distinguishable from convenient relabeling. |
| Protecting someone requires stopping a process | Continued generation cannot mean continuing every activity. Protection can require closure, rest, separation, or an irreversible decision. |

These cases rule out several inadequate roots: prediction alone, concern alone, perpetual inquiry, adaptation alone, and mere persistence. They do **not** prove our proposed integration is sufficient or uniquely fundamental. In particular, it still needs a fuller account of relevance, conflicting obligations, emotional and practical cultivation, and the degree of error compatible with wise judgment.

## How we can reach a root we understand

We should distinguish three achievements: **articulating a root, warranting it, and cultivating the capacity it describes.** The other work substantially advances the first. Its proofs, implementations, ethical arguments, and counterexamples contribute to the second. They do not establish the third through polished explanations or successful toy decisions.

First, use practical wisdom as the immediate target. A complete ontology and endless meaningful novelty are separate ambitions. We need not settle them before understanding how finite agents can judge well. This narrows a claim's scope rather than abandoning the possibility of a deep foundation.

Second, make the commitments and their costs explicit. The proposed ethical position gives affected beings non-arbitrary consideration. Its defense should examine difficult conflicts, dependence, coercion, promises, and burdens—not derive goodness from the fact that a process persists. This assessment adopts that position for its candidate; it does not attribute agreement with every consequence to Ren.

Third, move from isolated mechanisms to one consequential, accountable practice. Before a decision, record the situation, affected interests, uncertainties, available means, and why a response is justified. Preserve what might count against the framing, including an omitted party or a misleading measure. Later compare consequences with those expectations and record what changed. Evaluate action, restraint, and timing as well as explanation. Where a comparative study is appropriate, use serious ordinary deliberation with comparable information and resources; our two saturated pilots have shown no special advantage.

Finally, state an attainable completion standard. We could reasonably claim a **working understanding of a practical root** when the account explains these distinctions without ad hoc relabeling, guides judgment in a declared domain, survives serious objections, and leaves a feasible way to repair its own errors. Specify the contrasting cases and reasons before applying the account, and derive what it favors or excludes before seeing the outcome. Do not define mutual correction as whatever produces an approved answer. If ordinary good deliberation explains everything equally well, claim a useful articulation rather than a newly established root. Meaningful understanding can still deepen through future situations; a universal, necessary-and-sufficient root or a cultivated wise character remains a stronger claim.

We have completed the cross-session integration and conceptual checks here. We have not completed that consequential-practice validation. The next evidential gap is whether the integrated account improves situated judgment and withstands genuine value conflicts—not whether we can produce another example of updating a supplied belief.
