# Evidence from feedback and successor experiments

5 October 2026. Research conducted for Ren following the review of work after v7.3.

The live AI experiment supports a specific causal mechanism: timely evidence of errors changed founders' maintenance decisions, and access to their corrective handoffs improved the performance of fresh successors. All three predeclared tests passed. A separate software transfer experiment established an exact limit on a simplified prediction: financing and permission cannot always be represented by independent success factors when diagnostic and repair work share a queue.

These are meaningful results about actual AI responses in a constructed task and about an executable simulator. They do not establish a universal foundation, a human institutional mechanism, or a predictive advantage over established learning and control methods. The original human experiment remains unrun.

## The handoff result

Thirty two founder lineages completed an initial routing task. Twenty four faced an exception to their inherited rule; eight had an adequate inherited rule. After every founder submitted its baseline answers, feedback timing was randomized within fixed blocks. Founders then chose between completing four ordinary cases or spending one of those work slots on a handoff of at most 120 words while completing three cases. Correct current and successor answers were worth the same number of team points.

Only after every maintenance decision was sealed was packet access assigned to one of two fresh successor contexts per lineage. The other successor received the same general orientation without the lineage's packet. The resulting 64 successor contexts each answered 16 cases absent from the verified baseline report. Four of these cases had been shown to the founder as ordinary work during maintenance, without returning their correct labels. The experimental unit is the founder lineage; the 1,024 successor answers are not 1,024 independent organizations.

The following results concern the 24 changed-rule lineages, with 12 lineages in each feedback condition and paired access conditions within each lineage.

| Feedback before maintenance | Successor packet access | Successor accuracy |
|---|---|---:|
| Delayed | Blocked | 75% |
| Delayed | Enabled | 75% |
| Timely | Blocked | 75% |
| Timely | Enabled | 100% |

All 12 founders receiving timely evidence of errors produced valid corrective packets. None of the 12 with delayed feedback did. Each successor receiving one of those 12 packets answered all 16 cases correctly; its paired successor without access answered 12 correctly. A nominally open transmission route accomplished nothing when no packet had been produced.

The eight adequate-rule controls all declined maintenance and their 16 successors scored 100%. This supports a bounded response to relevant errors in this task, rather than an indiscriminate preference for more maintenance. The study did not test whether these controls could rebuild corrective capacity after a later change.

The packets consumed the declared work slot. Timely-feedback challenge founders attempted three ordinary cases and correctly routed all three. Delayed-feedback challenge founders attempted four and averaged 2.75 correct. Current output, sacrificed service opportunities and successor accuracy are retained separately. A work slot is a task opportunity cost; actual model computation and monetary expenditure were not matched or measured.

## The registered tests

The protocol fixed the sample, hypotheses, task family, randomization, scoring and decision rule before main responses. The analysis implementation was separately frozen before maintenance or successor responses. The three positive predictions had to pass two-sided exact randomization tests at a Bonferroni threshold of 0.05 divided by three.

| Prediction | Observed effect | Two-sided randomization p | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Timely feedback increases delivered maintenance | +100 percentage points | 0.00004287 | Pass |
| Packet access improves successors after timely feedback | +25 percentage points | 0.00048828 | Pass |
| The access gain is larger after timely feedback | +25 percentage points | 0.00004287 | Pass |

The first and third calculations enumerate all 46,656 permitted feedback assignments across the six challenge blocks. The second enumerates all 4,096 access-seat swaps in the 12 timely-feedback challenge lineages. A separate full enumeration checked the analysis method. These are finite-task randomization results under the declared isolation and sharp-null assumptions, not estimates of a universal success probability. In particular, the interaction test addresses a sharp null of no feedback effect on each lineage's paired access gain; it does not automatically test every null of zero average interaction with heterogeneous effects. The three tests are dependent and are not three independent replications. The factorial interaction does not by itself identify a natural indirect effect through the scalar maintenance indicator.

## What was learned and supplied

The founders and successors were fresh AI contexts using the same inherited model settings. Founder maintenance used that founder's existing context; successors received none of its history beyond their assigned materials. Decisions and answers came from actual model responses. No policy in the experiment driver prescribed whether a founder should maintain or what a successor should answer.

The task was deliberately bounded. Participants knew the family of possible rules: an inherited binary rule, either unchanged or inverted under one two-field exception. Eight verified founder cases uniquely identify every admitted rule. The successor cases use previously unscored joint field combinations. This tests identifying and transmitting a rule within a supplied family, not discovering an unconceived representation or adapting to a second change in the true rule.

A conventional finite-hypothesis learner also identifies the correct rule from the full verified founder reports and scores 16 out of 16 on every successor case set. The unchanged-rule default scores 12 out of 16 on each challenge set and 16 out of 16 on controls. This comparator was specified before successor results. It compares inference given the report, not a complete autonomous maintenance process under matched costs. Its performance shows that the observed correction needs no new theory of inference. No comparative claim about matched computational efficiency or model superiority is supported.

The study therefore supplies evidence for the operational route from outcome information to a costly delivered artifact to a successor's response. It rejects the restricted fixed-maintenance account in these conditions. It does not show that a real institution will establish the reporting system, maintain its funding and authority, survive repeated succession, or justify its goals.

Participants were instructed to read only their own prompt and write only their own response. Context separation used fresh starts, but filesystem access restrictions were instructions, not an operating-system sandbox. The evidence package contains assigned inputs, recorded outputs, hashes and an orchestrator record of dispatched task identities. That identity record was reconstructed from this conversation's dispatches; it is not an independently authenticated platform log. The audit can check the saved inputs and outputs, but does not independently certify every context boundary or complete participant tool trace. No human participants were recruited.

## The transfer test

The second experiment implemented a separate service with queued application, diagnostic and deployment jobs, resource-release times, permission gates and deadlines. The simple transported prediction was recovery equals a finance-dependent diagnostic probability multiplied by a permission-dependent enactment probability. Those probabilities were estimated in separate local calibration trials. Predictions were sealed before evaluating 1,024 paired target seed clusters in each of three regimes.

A serious conventional comparator independently computed FIFO departure times from calibrated component distributions and the stated scheduling rules. It did not call the target execution function. All interventions within a target seed shared the same primitive shocks.

| Regime | Maximum simple-model recovery error | Maximum queue-model recovery error | Registered practical decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Separate maintenance worker | 1.50 percentage points | 1.63 percentage points | Both pass |
| Shared worker | 2.48 percentage points | 1.34 percentage points | Both pass |
| Shared worker with tighter deadline | 2.82 percentage points | 0.49 percentage points | Both pass |

All three simple-model comparisons passed the predeclared absolute tolerance of 7.5 percentage points with simultaneous nominal 95% residual bands. That tolerance is generous relative to some low recovery rates. It must not be replaced after seeing the results, and these practical passes must not be relabeled failures.

The prediction of the interaction was less successful. In the tighter-deadline regime, the simple model predicted a 2.66-point interaction while the observed interaction was 9.77 points. Its discrepancy interval was 4.64 to 9.58 points. The separate-worker control also produced a small nominal interaction rejection even though its exact factorization is correct. This false rejection is retained and is a reason to distinguish sampling results from the exact calculation.

The predeclared finite-law audit and a subsequent exact algebraic deduction resolve the structural issue. For the tighter-deadline service, exact recovery probabilities are:

| Finance timing | Permission restricted | Permission enabled |
|---|---:|---:|
| Late | 4.0826% | 4.0826% |
| Early | 5.6128% | 15.7759% |

Permission has no effect in the late-finance row and a positive effect in the early-finance row. Both rows have positive recovery probability. No common permission-only multiplier can reproduce them. More generally, the two-by-two determinant must be zero for every product of a finance-only factor and a permission-only factor. Exact determinants are nonzero in both shared-worker regimes and zero in the separate-worker control.

This rules out every such scalar factorization as an exact description of the two constructed shared-worker services, not merely the particular fitted factor values. The determinant argument was a post-run deduction, not a newly registered prediction. It leaves the original practical decision unchanged. It also does not refute the source probability identity under its assumptions: finance changes diagnosis timing and the backlog encountered during enactment, violating the required invariance.

The independent service engines agreed on 24,192 finite paths. A further calculation through the target object engine reproduced all exact recovery fractions and determinants across 6,912 weighted paths. Frozen source hashes remained unchanged. The experiment establishes a concrete boundary on transport into these constructed services; it provides no observations of physical infrastructure or human institutions.

## What the combined evidence warrants

The positive result is that consequential feedback can induce maintenance whose usable transmission improves a successor's response, under specified incentives and information conditions. The negative result is that separately adequate component summaries can lose the dependence and timing needed to predict the combined process.

Together they strengthen the programme's focus on actual mechanisms carrying correction across change and replacement. They do not establish a uniquely sufficient root. Conventional rule learning explains the handoff task, and a conventional queue model explains the software service. No advantage in explanatory compression has been demonstrated: the simple factor model also required calibrated proposal-time distributions and supplied scheduling assumptions, so counting only its four displayed probabilities would understate its inputs.

The remaining broad evidential gap is repeated, independently observed renewal in human institutions or another real implementation, together with predictions that distinguish this integration from serious conventional alternatives. The preserved human-study design remains a concrete next experiment; it has not been substituted with fictional human observations.

## Evidence and reproduction

The accompanying experiment-evidence.zip contains the live protocol and all 128 participant submissions, all assigned prompts and hidden scoring rules, randomization records, analysis code, audit records, the separate service implementation, calibration and target data, frozen predictions, exact certificates, and snapshots of the prior designs. Machine-readable combined results are also saved as experiment-results.json.

A separate auditor implemented checks without importing the live generator or primary analysis. All 1,848 checks passed, covering the 128 submissions, response schemas, packet contents, assigned inputs, frozen hashes, randomization and scores. The auditor independently enumerated the primary randomization distributions and reproduced their p-values. This is an independent implementation within the same AI-assisted team, subject to the provenance limits above.

To recompute the live results from recorded observations, extract the archive and run `python live/analyze.py`. This uses the standard Python library. Regenerating model responses would be a new run and need not reproduce the same responses. To rerun the service experiment, follow transport/RESULTS.md in a fresh copy; it requires Python and NumPy. Mathematical and implementation audits here were conducted within the AI-assisted team and are not independent human scientific review.
