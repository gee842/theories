Independent experiment-method review
Research date: 5 October 2026
Status: recommendations made before held-out evaluation; not results.

Protocol update received from the coordinating researcher before development
results: control outcome is x_next; .05 action price, .02 sensing price, and
.0001 weighted model-block price are fixed. Compute-price sensitivity is
0,.0001,.001,.01 on frozen policies. The primary simple comparator is selected
globally across four equally weighted development worlds from raw, direct, and
joint_y_only, separately for each of two access stages. Eight primary comparisons
(four worlds times two stages) use Bonferroni intervals: 99.375% two-sided each,
using quantile 1-.05/16. Audit versus retained is exploratory. A bias switch at
step32 is a descriptive timing sensitivity alongside the main step30 switch.
The shared gain is k=.5. The adaptive gate buys when k² times modeled current
state-variance reduction exceeds .02 plus .0001 times the weighted reference
update cost; it never reads the reference first. Forced access buys at 12 offered
times; adaptive access has a cap of six purchases. The cap is a sensing cap;
unless separately restricted, computation is priced rather than hard-budgeted.
Retained bias variance inflates by .0025 per step; retained alpha is selected
from [.2,.5,.8], audit slow alpha from [.1,.2], fast alpha from [.8,1], audit
threshold from [2,3], and joint bias process variance from [0,.0025,.04]. The
gate is a myopic approximation that ignores future persistence value and cannot
assume its uncertainty model is correct. Its own evaluation overhead is charged.
Where the recommendations below mention four-comparison intervals or alternative
budget/margin designs, they are conditional suggestions, not the adopted protocol.

The central estimand is the effect of preserving and updating a bias estimate,
under declared information access, compared with frozen simpler methods. A
second estimand is the net value of a policy that chooses when to buy evidence.
Neither estimand identifies the value of semantic meta-depth in isolation.

1. Freeze and leakage controls

Use development seeds 0–99 to choose controller gains, prior covariance, bias
process variance or forgetting rate, audit threshold, acquisition thresholds,
simple comparator, and any combined-loss prices. Specify the exact grid and
selection rule, including tie breaking. Freeze a human-readable manifest and
code hashes before evaluation on 1000–1999. The 1000 evaluation seeds are paired
across policies. After evaluating, changing any policy creates a new exploratory
analysis; preserve original results. New confirmatory assessment needs new seeds.

For the primary comparison, do not choose the best simpler comparator using
evaluation performance. If selecting one simpler method per world on development
data, state that these are scenario-specific comparators, not one deployment
policy that inferred the world. Prefer one pooled-development policy setting
across worlds for the principal deployable-policy comparison. No policy should
receive true bias, reference error d, forthcoming switch time, future disturbances,
or the world label as an input unless an explicitly named oracle comparator.
Scoring may use hidden truth; estimation and acquisition may not.

Pre-generate x0, b0, process noise, ordinary-sensor noise, and possible calibration
noise for each seed independently of the policy. Draw all potential reference
noise whether or not purchased. Otherwise conditional draws can desynchronize
the paired random streams. Disturbances and timestamps match, but observations
need not have identical numeric values across control policies: their actions
produce different x trajectories. Identical access means identical channels and
times, not access to another policy's counterfactual measurements.

2. Comparison hierarchy

A. Fixed access. Every eligible policy is offered/billed the same prespecified
calibration schedule, independent of residuals, observations, or future truth.
Its use or non-use of received observations is part of its computation. Compare
persistent correction with immediate reference control and ordinary filtering.
Also report an ordinary-only no-purchase benchmark as a different access case;
do not call that comparison matched information. Log the actual access vectors.

B. Adaptive access. Give every policy the same hard sensing/compute resource cap
and opportunities. An acquisition policy sees current ordinary observation and
its own past before choosing to buy current reference. It must decide before
the reference value is exposed. Charge acquisition-policy execution, including
checks that decide not to purchase. Common ceiling is not common expenditure.
Report spending and savings alongside outcome, and call this a policy comparison.

C. Resource frontier. For any predeclared budget grid, re-evaluate already-frozen
policies at each allowed cap without retuning using evaluation outcomes. A fair
cost comparison need not force a frugal policy to waste its unused allowance.
Instead compare attained loss versus actual expense, and identify dominated
policies. Matching purchases after observing outcomes can create selection bias.

D. Nested-versus-flat negative control. Define the same sufficient state, same
update order, observation access, control rule, and charged operations in two
representations. Assert per-step equality of posterior means, covariance, bias
state, actions, access, forecasts, and costs to a specified floating-point
tolerance on all seeds. Matching aggregate mean loss is not sufficient. This
control refutes inference from a semantic label alone. It cannot prove every
cognitive hierarchy is flattenable without losing relevant resource properties.

3. Outcome definitions and indexing

For t=0,...,59, observe, optionally purchase, estimate, choose u_t, forecast,
then transition to x_(t+1). A clean intervention-responsive state loss is
  S_i = sum_{t=0}^{59} x_(t+1)^2.
Report x0 separately. If the existing script scores x0,...,x59, then include
x60 as a terminal cost or disclose that final action's state consequence is
unscored. This choice can otherwise reward an unintended terminal action.

Per-seed metrics, summarized across seeds:
  S_i: cumulative squared state after each action.
  U_i = sum u_t^2: action effort.
  E_i = (1/60) sum (xhat_t - x_t)^2: filtered state error before action.
  B_i = (1/60) sum (bhat_t - b_t)^2: bias error, for policies defining bhat.
  M_i = max_{0<=t<=60}|x_t|: maximum excursion.
  N_i: number of calibration purchases and denied purchase requests.
  C_i: operation/block count under a documented accounting convention.
  Memory_i: peak live numeric state count (not process RSS labeled algorithm memory).
  Calibration metrics: Brier loss, interval coverage and width, normalized error.
  Recovery: predeclared threshold and right-censor status after the bias switch.

Keep state accuracy distinct from prediction accuracy. The latter is a forecast
of x_(t+1), which includes process noise, rather than an estimate of x_t after
seeing y_t. For a model posterior N(xhat_t,Pxx_t), chosen action u_t, and independent
w_t with variance .0025, the declared Gaussian predictive approximation is
  mu_t = .8*xhat_t + u_t;
  V_t = .64*Pxx_t + .0025.
For the event x_(t+1) outside [-.5,.5], record before transition
  p_t = Phi((-.5-mu_t)/sqrt(V_t)) + 1-Phi((.5-mu_t)/sqrt(V_t)).
Then score (p_t - 1{|x_(t+1)|>.5})^2. Normalized prediction error is
  (x_(t+1)-mu_t)^2/V_t;
nominal 95% interval coverage is the fraction inside mu_t±1.96 sqrt(V_t).
Report interval width as well, since wide intervals can achieve coverage cheaply.
Variance floors should be numerical safeguards declared beforehand, not tuned to
improve evaluation scores. A policy's Gaussian forecast can be misspecified,
especially after bias shifts or with a biased reference; that is tested, not
assumed away. A Brier difference reflects overall forecast quality, not solely
calibration. Calibration and accuracy must not be used as synonyms.

Because actions influence the event rate, Brier loss across control policies
also reflects task difficulty induced by those policies. State-estimation error
before action and within-policy reliability remain useful complementary views.
Do not interpret a controller making states remain near zero as independent
evidence that its model understood an earlier state correctly. Optional fixed
replay estimation runs can compare identical state/observation sequences, but
their estimand differs from on-policy control and should be named separately.

Define recovery before seeing evaluation. One option: earliest post-switch k
with |xhat_t-x_t|<.25 for five consecutive steps. Report recovery probability by
the 30-step post-switch horizon and a censored duration statistic; never average
only recovered runs. If using a clipped duration of 30, explicitly call it a
capped duration and separately display censor fraction. Five steps and .25 are
research choices, not natural constants. Exclude b0=0 from the actual-switch
recovery subset and state its sample size. State recovery and bias-estimate
recovery answer different questions, so identify which is being reported.

4. Prices, budget, and uncertainty

With the proposed prices, use
  L_i = S_i + .05 U_i + .02 N_i + .0001 C_i.
These prices express this experiment's supplied preferences. They are not human
utility measurements or empirically measured compute cost. Document exactly what
constitutes one compute block. A block price cannot establish machine time,
energy, or cognitive cost. If real elapsed time is measured, report it separately
with machine/runtime description; do not confuse interpreter overhead with
physical deliberation latency. Actual control delay is not modeled here.

Charge each active policy's own control/estimation/acquisition/audit operations.
Scoring and experiment logging are common external infrastructure, not policy
computation, unless a policy uses them. Mandatory baseline work must have budget
reserved or the cap must specify fallback behavior; do not overspend a hard cap.
If the cap covers only optional sensing/computation, call it exactly that rather
than a total-resource cap. Separate action penalty from sensing/compute cap unless
the latter explicitly includes physical actuation expense.

For two fixed policies A,B, the mean loss difference at new prices is
  Delta(p_s,p_c) = Delta(S+.05U)+p_s Delta(N)+p_c Delta(C).
This gives a transparent break-even line. Repricing frozen trajectories is a
sensitivity analysis, not evidence of optimal behavior under the new prices,
because rational acquisition may change with price. If both count differences
are zero, the price cannot change the paired ranking. Display a small predeclared
price grid or the break-even line instead of choosing favorable prices after
the fact. A second price analysis should retain separate performance components.

The independent inference unit is the entire seed trajectory. For each world,
form D_i=L_i(persistent)-L_i(frozen simpler) for i=1000,...,1999, and report mean
D, standard deviation of D, Monte Carlo standard error sd(D)/sqrt(1000), and a
paired confidence interval. Positive means persistent is worse. Do not use
60,000 time steps as independent samples. Bootstrap resampling, if used, resamples
seeds together across policies and worlds, retaining each seed's whole trajectory.
These intervals quantify Monte Carlo uncertainty under the supplied simulator,
not uncertainty that this model describes people or consciousness.

For four prespecified within-world claims in one family, two-sided 98.75%
intervals give Bonferroni family coverage at least 95% (approximately z=2.498,
or the corresponding t quantile). Unadjusted 95% intervals are acceptable as
explicitly descriptive intervals without claiming family-wide error control.
Secondary metrics should remain exploratory unless their multiplicity is also
addressed. If more primary access regimes/comparisons are included, update the
family size; four only covers four designated comparisons, not every table row.

Freeze a practical margin m before evaluation. A possible convention is m=.02
cumulative loss, equal to one calibration fee. It is defensible as a declared
resource-equivalent choice, not an empirical threshold of meaningful cognition.
For D=persistent-minus-simple, a benefit claim requires upper CI < -m. Harm beyond
margin requires lower CI > m. An interval fully inside [-m,m] supports practical
equivalence at that declared margin. Every other case is unresolved with respect
to margin, including small directionally significant effects. Failure to show
benefit is not proof of equality. Negative-control equality is instead checked
algorithmically and by its construction.

5. Focused verified primary-source map

Friedland, Bernard (1969), Treatment of Bias in Recursive Filtering. IEEE
Transactions on Automatic Control AC-14(4):359–367.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1969.1099223
Author-institution research record and abstract inspected:
https://researchwith.njit.edu/en/publications/treatment-of-bias-in-recursive-filtering/
Scope verified: constant unknown bias in a linear process, affecting dynamics
and/or observations; separates optimal state correction from bias estimation
under the article's assumptions. Supports established engineering precedent for
state/bias decomposition. It does not validate our adaptive-rate heuristic,
non-Gaussian prior, bias-switch treatment, or theory of introspection. Full text
was not inspected in this review, so no exact theorem is attributed beyond the
institutional abstract's scope.

Hay, Nicholas; Russell, Stuart; Tolpin, David; Shimony, Solomon Eyal (2012),
Selecting Computations: Theory and Applications. UAI 2012.
Author-hosted paper inspected, especially pp. 2–4, Definitions 1–3 and Theorems 5–7:
https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/uai12-meta.pdf
Scope: metalevel Bayesian selection compares expected decision improvement with
computation costs; a myopic stopping policy may stop before useful complementary
computations. Their bounded-utility, positive-cost setting bounds expected
computation and implies almost-sure stopping, not a universal fixed runtime.
This motivates cost-aware audit decisions; it does not prove our threshold
policy optimal or establish bounds for every cognitive recursive process.

Tzoumas, Vasileios; Carlone, Luca; Pappas, George J.; Jadbabaie, Ali (2020),
LQG Control and Sensing Co-Design. IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control.
Author preprint inspected, especially Section II and Theorem 1:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08376
Scope: chooses a fixed sensor subset from a finite set for a horizon, with
sensor-specific costs and linear Gaussian dynamics. It distinguishes minimizing
control loss subject to sensing budget from minimizing sensing expense subject
to a performance bound. Supports evaluating sensing through control objectives,
not covariance reduction alone. Its separation and approximation guarantees do
not transfer to our observation-triggered, online purchases, control clipping,
reference misspecification, or model-change heuristic.

Gneiting, Tilmann; Raftery, Adrian E. (2007), Strictly Proper Scoring Rules,
Prediction, and Estimation. JASA 102(477):359–378.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437
Author-hosted paper inspected, introduction and scoring/interval material:
https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf
Scope: proper scoring rules assess probabilistic forecasts against observed
outcomes and distinguish forecast concentration from statistical calibration.
Supports scoring declared probabilities and reporting interval width with
coverage. Propriety does not establish calibration on finite data or protect
against a controller changing the outcome-generating policy. The explicit
action-conditioned formulas above are derived for our simulator.

Source-selection decision: Joshi and Boyd (2009), Sensor Selection via Convex
Optimization, was also inspected at the authors' Stanford page and PDF. It is a
static parameter/sensor subset selection problem under an estimation criterion.
Tzoumas et al. is the closer focused citation for this controlled dynamic study;
neither alone validates adaptive sensing. No source is treated as evidence that
recursive self-modeling has a unique or universal cognitive architecture.

6. Limits to preserve in the report

The strongest result available from this experiment is conditional functional
performance in a small, specified dynamical model. An auditor that wins after a
switch may simply implement a useful change-detection mechanism. A persistent
bias state is information retained through time; calling it a meta-model is a
semantic interpretation grounded in its target, not a new mathematical species.
A biased reference tests shared dependence on a calibration channel. A useful
algorithm can still be overconfident or have a wrong model. Constant-bias and
switching worlds should remain separate results, including no-bias harms.
Delay, competing goals, endogenous world changes, strategic feedback, human
experience, and ethical legitimacy remain outside this simulator.
