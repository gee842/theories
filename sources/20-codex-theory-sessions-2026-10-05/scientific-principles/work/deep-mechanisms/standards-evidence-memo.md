# Two mechanistic cases for a theory of scientific inquiry

Research memo, 5 October 2026. Primary evidence, explicit comparison, and a proposal for discriminating mechanisms. These cases support components of an architecture; they do not validate a universal theory. The medical example concerns historical research and is not present treatment guidance.

## Finding

A useful science-specific account needs more than an agent that updates hypotheses. Its minimal explanatory unit can be a **distributed arrangement that produces, interprets, preserves, contests, and acts on evidence**. A laboratory's instruments, records, trained roles, channels of criticism, budgets, and authorization rules can determine whether an error is even available for correction. The arrangement can make its own previously stable instruments or classifications into research objects.

Two contrasting cases make that claim concrete. Hubble's optical failure shows an informative discrepancy failing to alter a protected measurement process. The H. pylori research sequence shows an initially suggestive association becoming a target of more discriminating interventions and subsequently informing bounded clinical recommendations. Neither case licenses a simple equation between social acceptance and truth, or between dissent and discovery.

Crucial scope distinctions: Hubble is primarily engineering and scientific-instrument quality assurance, not a comprehensive case of theory replacement. The 1994 ulcer recommendations are clinical practice standards, not universal epistemic standards. Their relevance lies in inspectable mechanisms that a wider theory must explain, rather than in taking either as a miniature of all science.

## Case 1: Hubble — trusted measurement as both controller and blind spot

### Primary evidence

The 1990 investigation found that the reflective null corrector used to shape and verify the primary mirror was misassembled. The retained device allowed remeasurement; the approximately 1.3 mm lens displacement accounted for the observed optical error. Other test results had signaled the defect but were dismissed. The report distinguishes nominal precision from ability to detect a large error: supposedly “uncertified” alternative instruments could supply a useful independent check even though they did not meet the desired final precision. The certification labels themselves lacked adequate definition and documentation. [Allen board report, executive summary, PDF pp. 4–6; lessons VII.C, PDF pp. 59–60](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19910003124/downloads/19910003124.pdf).

A separate NASA quality-assurance investigation documents missing specialized training, restricted access to critical metrology, insufficient independent escalation, and inadequate retained records. It distinguishes what records show, what witnesses said, and investigators' proposed explanations. Cost and schedule pressures were implicated, but the report also discusses expertise, technical constraints, proprietary arrangements, and conflicting rationales. The record therefore supports a failure of implemented checking more directly than a single-motive story about management. [Rodney, *Hubble Space Telescope: SRM&QA Observations and Lessons Learned*, especially pp. 11, 15–18, 23–25](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19920008654/downloads/19920008654.pdf).

Recovery has a subsequent performance check. Jedrzejewski and colleagues reported in 1994 that, with COSTAR, the Faint Object Camera concentrated 85% of total light within 0.1 arcsecond radius at 486 nm, compared with 18% for the aberrated image. They also reported reduced throughput and field of view. Thus correction changed physical capability with measurable tradeoffs; improvement was not just a revised label. [Primary paper abstract in NASA's archive, *Astrophysical Journal Letters* 435, L7, DOI 10.1086/187581](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19950040548). The full journal paper was not accessed in this pass.

### Mechanistic reconstruction — our inference

The relevant process contains at least two possible errors: the mirror can be wrong, and the test apparatus used to judge it can be wrong. Using one device both to guide production and to certify its output creates a feedback loop. Successful convergence of the production loop can coexist with failure against the physical target if the judging device is biased. More repetitions can stabilize the wrong result.

The proposed causal chain is:

`trust in primary test → admission of discrepant checks → diagnostic investigation → instrument correction → changed optical performance`.

This is not a claim that every arrow is statistically identified by the historical record. It is a mechanism that connects an experimentally investigable physical fault with documented decisions about checking. The physical diagnosis is much more tightly constrained than the relative causal weights of budget pressure, expertise, hierarchy, and confidence.

A science model that includes only sensor readings and a rational Bayesian updater misses an important variable: which readings enter the updater and which actors can demand an additional test. A model that includes only hierarchy misses the physical error signature and the prospective constraints on a corrective device. The two levels can be modeled together without making optics depend on consensus.

### Discriminating rivals

- **Insufficient precision:** an independent check can be too coarse to certify final performance while still discriminating a large systematic error. These are different tasks and therefore different information requirements.
- **No informative evidence existed:** distinguish absence of evidence, failure to retain it, failure to transmit it, and failure to act on it. A traceable discrepancy can separate these explanations.
- **Any checking procedure would suffice:** a check that shares the same calibration error may be uninformative. Diversity of causal error channels matters more than the number of nominal reviews.
- **The target was simply redefined:** assess a physical intervention against a held-constant optical outcome, then separately account for tradeoffs and new capabilities.
- **One social incentive explains everything:** test competing access, expertise, and reward mechanisms rather than inferring motives from the eventual failure.

### What this case does not establish

It does not show that independent checking is always worth its cost, that every ignored anomaly is a suppressed discovery, or that management rather than local technical judgment is always the relevant bottleneck. Nor does it establish the particular probability of failure under alternative historical arrangements. Those are counterfactual claims requiring additional evidence or explicit assumptions.

## Case 2: H. pylori — association, a discriminating intervention, and bounded uptake

### Primary evidence

Marshall and Warren's 1984 paper describes biopsies from 100 consecutive consenting gastroscopy patients. Curved or spiral bacilli were found in specimens from 58, and cultured from 11. The authors proposed an etiological possibility from their association with disease; the abstract did not equate association with demonstrated causation. [Primary study, *Lancet* 1(8390), 1311–1315, PMID 6145023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6145023/). Only the primary abstract was reliably accessible; the university-hosted full-text link returned 404 locally. No claim about the exact culture-discovery chronology is based on this access.

A subsequent trial addressed an important alternative explanation: bismuth might protect tissue directly rather than help through bacterial elimination. Hentschel and colleagues randomly assigned 104 infected patients with recurrent duodenal ulcer to antibiotics or identical-appearing placebo, with both groups also receiving ranitidine. Endoscopic follow-up measured recurrence. The report gives one-year recurrence as 8% in the antibiotic group and 86% in the placebo group. This is evidence for a treatment-package effect under the trial's conditions, with microbiological findings informing the proposed pathway. It is not a logical proof that every ulcer has one cause. [Primary study, *NEJM* 328, 308–312, DOI 10.1056/NEJM199302043280503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8419816/); [publisher's indexed full-text methods and results](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199302043280503). Direct publisher access later returned 403; indexed primary text and the abstract were read.

The 1994 NIH consensus conference explicitly asked separate causal, diagnostic, treatment, malignancy, and future-research questions. Its recommendations favored antimicrobial treatment for infected ulcer patients while leaving the value for infected non-ulcer dyspepsia unresolved and calling for further cancer research. The recommendations therefore preserved distinctions among questions rather than treating one success as validation of the entire research program. [Conference statement, February 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7920747/); [primary JAMA publication, 272, 65–69](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/375955?resultClick=1).

A later primary observational study makes another distinction: dissemination of a practice recommendation is not equivalent to implementation. A five-state quality-improvement project studied 4,292 hospitalized Medicare patients in 1995 and 1997, separately measuring care processes and outcomes. Its design can document practice and outcome associations but cannot, by itself, assign all changes to the guideline or intervention. [Brock and colleagues, *JAMA* 286, 1985–1993 (2001), methods and abstract](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/194313). Screening and treatment increased, but treatment was not associated with lower rehospitalization or mortality in the Colorado outcome cohort. This observational result concerns a different population and implementation setting from the earlier trial; it does not erase that trial. It is a scope check on automatic translation from recommendation to improved real-world outcomes.

### Mechanistic reconstruction — our inference

The architecture contains distinct transitions:

`detect a repeatable pattern → represent a candidate organism and causal role → admit targeted research → design an intervention discriminating an alternative pathway → assess outcomes → deliberate about scoped use → implement or fail to implement`.

The transition from observed association to intervention is crucial. A perfectly replicated correlation can remain compatible with several causal arrangements. A treatment comparison changes which causal hypotheses are under pressure, provided assignment, follow-up, measurement, and relevant background conditions are sufficiently controlled. Evaluating mediation through the organism remains more demanding than estimating a randomized treatment effect.

Concept development here is not merely renaming. A candidate biological entity becomes a target of culture, detection, manipulation, and causal questions. Nevertheless, these selected documents are not a complete history of how that candidate first became conceivable. They establish stages of evidential development; they do not isolate a general psychological mechanism of invention.

A change from treating immediate symptoms to investigating recurrence can be a change in operational target and method while the broad goal of patient benefit remains. A model should not count every new endpoint as a new ultimate value. Conversely, the relative importance of benefits, harms, costs, and whose experience matters cannot be supplied by a bacteriology result alone.

### Discriminating rivals

- **The organism is only a correlate:** prospective interventions offer evidence unavailable from repeated cross-sectional association; off-target intervention effects remain an alternative to examine.
- **Early apparent benefit is entirely a second treatment action:** choose a design that removes or independently varies the suspected alternative pathway. This is a mechanism-level improvement in experimental design.
- **Acceptance was only a reward or prestige cascade:** ask whether changes in judgments track externally informative outcomes conditional on social exposure. Trial outcomes and acceptance histories are different data sets.
- **Evidence necessarily causes uptake:** compare exposure to findings with adoption, technical access, training, resources, and local authorization. A published recommendation is an input to that process, not its completion.
- **The new view replaces every older cause:** retain heterogeneous causes and explicitly bounded populations; do not infer universality from an effect in a selected group.

These sources do not identify a causal effect of scientific prestige, commercial interests, or suppression on adoption. Claims of a lone hero overcoming a uniformly irrational establishment would outrun the evidence inspected here. Archival reviews, correspondence, funding histories, and comparative adoption data would be needed.

## Architecture these cases can constrain

Use a graph with typed nodes instead of a single undifferentiated “belief state”:

| Node type | State to record | Proposed intervention for causal discrimination |
|---|---|---|
| Physical target and environment | Hidden physical state; changes caused by observation or treatment | Change one physical mechanism while holding the evaluation protocol fixed |
| Instrument and preparation | Calibration, resolution, systematic errors, specimen handling | Add a separately calibrated channel, blind reference, or alternative intervention |
| Records | Raw traces, transformations, provenance, exclusions | Preserve or reveal a selected trace with provenance; distinguish availability from use |
| Concepts and questions | Candidate entities, variables, causal targets, admitted comparisons | Supply a new distinction or candidate without supplying its truth |
| Skills and roles | Competence, time, task allocation, ability to reproduce a procedure | Training or role reassignment, separately from new evidence |
| Authority and checking | Who can inspect, object, escalate, stop, or redirect work | Change review access or response obligation while holding evidence fixed |
| Rewards and resources | Credit, deadlines, continuing support, maintenance | Change returns to checking, availability of instruments, or renewal policy separately |
| Standards and purposes | Error criteria, acceptability thresholds, declared beneficiaries and uses | Freeze one evaluative dimension while revising another; record what changed |

This table is a proposed model decomposition. It is not an empirical claim that every science has a laboratory, identical hierarchy, or clinical endpoint. Historical sciences may depend on surviving traces; theoretical sciences on demonstrations and calculational checks. Roles and relations can be instantiated differently.

A reliable explanatory claim must say which intervention changes which observable outcome, under which preserved conditions. For example, retaining a discrepancy will not cause correction if no capable actor can access it; granting access will not help if the supposed check shares the error; giving authority will not supply an unavailable test. These are separable failure modes, not synonyms for a general lack of openness.

## Close prior art: strongest overlap and genuine differences

Rheinberger's primary 2011 discussion is especially close. Sections 2.1–2.4, pp. 311–315, relate instruments, skills, representations, and local research arrangements. A technical condition can itself become an object of investigation, and a resolved object can become a tool. Research arrangements reproduce while generating differences and connecting to wider cultures. This anticipates much of the proposed materially embedded architecture. His account is historical epistemology, not a supplied stochastic transition model or error-control theorem. A formal reconstruction can be useful without claiming to supersede it. [Full primary paper](https://www.scielo.br/j/man/a/nkLPLcFWdXRWyXNQqbvzp6y/?format=pdf&lang=en).

His 1994 paper also cautions against assuming one globally coordinated logic of scientific change; it follows local experimental systems and technical transformations. We should preserve that challenge to a universal developmental law. [Primary author-uploaded text, *Science in Context* 7, 65–81, especially pp. 67–70](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/27275531_Experimental_Systems_Historiality_Narration_and_Deconstruction). The publication is 1994, despite a later upload date on the host.

Laudan's *Science and Values* is a direct predecessor for interdependent theories, methods, and aims. The publisher's table of contents identifies the reticulated model and goal evaluation; the root investigator is separately examining the author text. This memo does not claim a full primary reading of the book. [Publisher record](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/science-and-values/pdf).

Longino's contextual account and the cultural-selection models already read in the preceding memo likewise prevent a novelty claim for social criticism or reward-based propagation. Hull's *Science as a Process* is an important further comparison, but its full primary text was not examined here. Do not attribute detailed conclusions to it based only on the title or secondary summaries. [Bibliographic record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL2527484M).

The possible contribution is a set of **explicitly testable couplings and conditional guarantees**: when do access, interpretation, checking, authority, and material renewal jointly preserve the ability to detect and correct specified errors? Neither these cases nor the formal vocabulary establish novelty of that contribution.

## A discriminating next empirical design

Build a laboratory-team experiment with an imperfect primary measuring device and a task that admits at least two causal explanations. Independently randomize: availability of an informative secondary test; competence to interpret it; whether discrepant raw traces are retained and visible; authority to request correction; and reward for current output versus diagnostically useful checking. Keep the physical target and final external performance measure constant. Separately vary whether the secondary test shares the main device's fault.

Measure the full mediation chain: detected discrepancy, proposal, admission, test execution, correct interpretation, accepted revision, and externally improved performance. The architecture predicts interactions: access alone should not work without informative evidence and usable interpretation; institutional reform should not improve diagnosis in a strictly uninformative condition; more checking can harm net performance when it consumes resources without discriminating the relevant error.

This goes beyond showing that an adaptive agent can choose a better action from a programmer's menu. It tests whether a socially implemented route for generating and acting on questions explains correction. It still does not explain unrestricted concept invention or justify ethical goals. A prospective historical follow-up could code dated documents against the same stages, with blinded coding and explicitly competing explanations; that would be triangulation, not random assignment of past institutions.
