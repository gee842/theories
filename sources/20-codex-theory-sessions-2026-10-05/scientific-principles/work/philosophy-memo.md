# Falsifiability and corrigible inquiry: philosophy audit

Research memo, 5 October 2026. Prepared for Ren's scientific-principles investigation. This is an interpretive and analytical memo, not an established universal theory. The source workspace was read only. No external messages or publication occurred.

## Finding

Conditional requirements for learning can be derived. A complete scientific method cannot be deduced from causal embeddedness alone. Once an aim, accessible evidence, model class, and reliability criterion are supplied, informative discrimination requires some accessible difference between the competing possibilities. If the procedure must be able to correct an initially favored but false claim, its response cannot favor that claim regardless of every possible observation. These requirements explain a role for adverse evidence, without requiring that every scientific model logically prohibit a finite observation.

This is weaker than a necessary-and-sufficient definition of science, and more explicit about its premises. Calling it a conditional account of corrigible inquiry is defensible. Calling it a derivation of Popper's demarcation criterion, all accepted scientific practices, or the historical inevitability of science is not.

## Distinctions needed before any derivation

1. **Logical refutability:** relative to a language and auxiliary premises, a claim entails the absence of some describable observation. A true incompatible observation yields a deductive contradiction of that package.
2. **Empirical testability:** an observation or intervention can bear on a specified empirical claim under a measurement and sampling model. Evidence may be graded and fallible.
3. **Identifiability:** distinct targets imply distinct accessible observation laws under a declared experiment class. This can hold at the distribution level while finite data remain weak.
4. **Statistical distinguishability:** a finite procedure reaches stipulated error or power requirements for named alternatives. Identifiability alone does not guarantee useful discrimination within a budget.
5. **Likelihood discrimination:** evidence changes the relative support of two supplied predictive models. This does not automatically provide a calibrated decision rule, posterior model truth, or exhaustive model comparison.
6. **Practical feasibility:** the investigator can execute the required observation, computation, or intervention with available resources, permissions, and time. Logical possibility, identifiable parameters, and practical feasibility are separate.

These are stipulated working definitions for this investigation. They deliberately avoid treating all historical uses of “testability” as identical. An accepted stochastic model may assign positive probability to every finite sequence and still make sharply discriminating predictions. For continuous data, moreover, an exact point can have probability zero under every model; a zero-probability singleton is not by itself a useful empirical falsifier. Measurement resolution and events must be specified.

## A minimal conditional reconstruction

Let a feasible protocol e produce a finite report Y under either model H0 or H1, with laws p0 and p1. Include the sampling rule, stopping rule, instrument model, and observation selection in e. Suppose the epistemic task is to select the correct model with equal prior weights and symmetric unit error loss.

If p0=p1, every decision function has exactly the same output law under either model. Its average correctness cannot exceed one half. Physical implementation or recursive self-modeling does not create a distinction absent from the complete report. For unequal priors, the correct benchmark is the accuracy of always choosing the more probable model, not necessarily one half. For different losses, use the corresponding no-information decision.

If p0 differs from p1, some report favors H0 and another favors H1, because both distributions sum to one. With common positive support, the likelihood ratio p1(y)/p0(y) is greater than one somewhere and less than one elsewhere. Positive prior odds then move in opposite directions on those reports. Thus discrimination supplies a precise role for possible adverse evidence. It does **not** require a report assigned zero probability, a guarantee of decisive finite evidence, or rejection at every unfavorable update.

A stronger aim adds a stronger requirement: if a method must sometimes correct initial acceptance of H0 when H1 holds, then its conclusion must be capable of changing on a report possible under H1. An always-H0 rule cannot do so. This is a narrow procedural necessity, not a derivation of a whole philosophy of science. Reliable correction additionally requires enough separation, correct error accounting, access to the report, and a usable alternative or suspension option. A rule that randomly changes its answer is revisable but unreliable.

The resource bound enters through the feasible class E(B). If every e in E(B) yields identical laws, reliable discrimination is impossible under that budget even if a more expensive protocol would succeed. If laws merely differ minutely, formal identification may coexist with poor achievable power. A sensible test recommendation then needs an explicit tradeoff between expected information or decision benefit and costs. Causal embeddedness tells us to include those costs and effects; it does not select the loss function or require unlimited testing.

## Four worked conceptual cases

**Non-discriminating addition.** H0 gives a complete observable law. H1 adds an undetectable cause but stipulates exactly the same law under every accessible protocol. Their observable likelihood ratio is always one. This particular empirical comparison cannot favor either. That result does not prove H1 meaningless, false, immoral, or devoid of heuristic use; it specifies a limit of the declared evidence.

**Deterministic refutable package.** A device, initialized to state 0, is claimed to output 0 after one operation, and the observation channel is assumed faithful. Output 1 contradicts this conjunction. If initial state or readout fidelity is uncertain, the bare core claim has not been uniquely identified as the false premise. Stating “the specified device/protocol package failed this test” is stronger logically than silently choosing the culprit.

**Testable stochastic alternative.** Compare independent Bernoulli(.5) and Bernoulli(.75) trials. Every finite binary sequence has positive probability under both. For n trials with k ones, the likelihood ratio favoring .75 is 1.5^k·0.5^(n−k). At n=4, k=4 gives 5.0625, while k=0 gives .0625. These observations support opposite models without a deductive refutation. A frequentist rejection threshold requires a separate error criterion, and failure to reject at that threshold does not establish fairness. The independence and stability assumptions also remain testable components of the package.

**Auxiliary-dependent apparent refutation.** Let the observed reading be Y=theta+b, where theta is the target and b is an unknown instrument offset. A reading of 1 conflicts with theta=0 only if b=0 is retained. The models (theta,b)=(0,1) and (1,0) are observationally equivalent under that single measurement. A separate calibration observation Z=b can identify the components in this idealized setting. With noise, the distinction becomes statistical. Repeating Y indefinitely with the same unknown offset does not remove the ambiguity. An auxiliary diagnosis should therefore be supported by discriminating evidence, not merely invented after failure.

## Consequences for the developing framework

Transparency can preserve the information required to reconstruct which package was tested. It does not make premises true. Independent checking can reveal mistakes if checks access relevant evidence and do not share all the original failure modes. Calling two teams independent does not establish statistical independence. The calibration example shows why repeated agreement can be uninformative about shared systematic error.

Revisability must concern more than changing a probability within a fixed family. The source workspace's inherited-evidence study shows how a summary sufficient under one family loses distinctions needed by an expanded one. Its questions-without-anomalies study shows why permission to investigate an alternative cannot universally require an anomaly in an uninformative incumbent interface. Neither entails that every proposed alternative deserves adoption. Admission, investigation, validation, and implementation remain distinct decisions with distinct costs and authority.

Theory-dependent measurement warrants explicit alternative instrument models and cross-checks. It does not eliminate observational constraints. When an experiment changes the target, the object compared across trials must be a specified process including intervention history, rather than an imagined unchanged system. A model of how testing changes behavior may itself be assessed; absent such modeling, an initial success cannot automatically transport to later deployment.

Finite resources also permit rational periods of routine work or suspension. A requirement to challenge every foundational assumption simultaneously would destroy the stable tools needed to investigate anything. The analytical question is when retained assumptions prevent relevant correction, when revision becomes worth its expense, and how the capacity to resume inquiry survives. These are prospective design questions, distinct from claims about what historically caused scientific progress.

## Counterexamples and rejected derivations

- **Falsifiable therefore reliable:** false. A precise but false prediction is falsifiable; selective reporting, flawed instruments, or inadequate samples can still make its appraisal unreliable.
- **Not deductively refutable by one finite sample therefore unscientific:** false for the stipulated stochastic models. Their probability laws are discriminable without impossible finite outcomes.
- **Any auxiliary revision is evasion:** rejected. Correcting a faulty instrument or independently detecting an omitted cause may improve predictions. Unconstrained rescue without fresh consequences is a different case.
- **Empirical adequacy fixes ontology:** rejected. Accessible equivalence leaves some representational differences unidentified.
- **Failed prediction identifies the mistaken premise:** rejected by the theta/b example unless additional assumptions or observations localize the error.
- **All traditions express the same information principle:** rejected. Formal identifiability, Popperian criticism, Kuhnian research practice, and Lakatosian programme appraisal have different subjects and standards.
- **Causal survival fixes legitimate science:** rejected. A durable institution can systematically suppress evidence or serve coercive ends. Error reliability cannot determine whose interests count or legitimize the interventions used to obtain evidence.

## Next discriminating research test

Build a small finite measurement problem with a target parameter, shared calibration offset, costed calibration, and an optional independent instrument. Compare repeated same-instrument testing, disclosed-method reanalysis, calibration, and genuinely different measurement channels under equal total budgets. Include worlds where the original target claim is wrong, where only the instrument is wrong, and where there is no fault. Preserve all outcomes and protocol choices.

Predeclare two endpoints: correct localization of the faulty component and total decision loss including test cost. The expected result is conditional: cheap repeated measurements reduce random noise but cannot resolve exact target/offset equivalence; an informative calibration can. The negative control is an allegedly independent instrument with the same hidden offset. If that control produces a gain without another information difference, the analysis has probably introduced unacknowledged evidence or independence. This test directly operationalizes the auxiliary problem while retaining the framework's costs, causal participation, and fallibility.
