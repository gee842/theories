# Adversarial audit: when a framework becomes a theory of science

Research memo for the continuing inquiry, 5 October 2026. This memo distinguishes proposed mechanisms, proved restrictions, empirical conjectures, and evaluative commitments. It audits the earlier investigation rather than treating that investigation as validation.

## Strongest defensible thesis

**Scientific change can be modeled as feedback among the distinctions investigators can make, the questions and representations they use, their methods and standards of appraisal, and the material and social arrangements that sustain those capacities. These feedbacks can generate predictable failures of correction even while an incumbent performance measure improves.**

This is a promising substantive thesis once particular feedback mechanisms are specified. The broad vocabulary alone does not establish it. Its most vulnerable content concerns *which conditions produce which transitions*, how often the mechanism operates, and whether modeling it improves prediction or intervention beyond established alternatives.

The distinctive ambition should be explanatory: account for how scientific practices become available, selected, stabilized, and revised. “Methods are causally realized” is a starting constraint. “This funding and retention rule causes this otherwise invisible loss of diagnostic capacity, which prolongs this particular mistaken interpretation” is a causal explanation.

## What is missing between a schema and an explanatory theory?

A state description such as S=(X,T,R,O) can represent indefinitely many histories. Merely adding questions Q, concepts C, methods M, and standards V does not restrict them. Every conceivable historical change can be redescribed as a change in these variables. A theory must additionally supply:

1. **Operational distinctions.** What counts as a new question rather than a new answer? A new concept rather than a renamed category? A changed standard rather than changed performance under the same standard? These boundaries can be task-relative, but must be declared before classifying outcomes.
2. **Transition mechanisms.** For example, a new sensor makes a previously unresolved distinction observable; a problem-solving failure redirects search; a funding rule removes a calibration capability; apprenticeship spreads a representation; a political constraint prevents a proposal from receiving a hearing.
3. **Constraints and competing causes.** Some mechanisms enable a transition without selecting it. Affordable access to an instrument may be necessary for a test while training, prestige, curiosity, or a practical crisis explains why that test is actually chosen.
4. **Counterfactual commitments.** Which transition becomes more or less likely if access, incentives, records, or criticism channels are changed while specified other conditions remain fixed?
5. **Scope and comparison.** Which populations and timescales are covered, and what simpler explanation would make different predictions?

An existence proof that a loss of inquiry capacity is possible does not establish that it explains an observed episode. A retrospective fit does not show a unique mechanism. A useful intermediate product is therefore a family of explicit models with shared architecture and separately testable mechanisms—not a universal deterministic history of science.

## Four things to explain, with four distinct traps

**Questions.** A question arises when an investigator can frame alternatives whose resolution matters for some purpose. That can follow an anomaly, but also a new instrument, analogy, imported technique, practical demand, aesthetic interest, or unexplored possibility. An anomaly-only theory is too narrow. Distinguish proposing a question, obtaining institutional attention, receiving resources, and establishing an answer. The framework is strongest when it explains separations between these stages.

**Concepts.** A concept changes the distinctions a system can express or use. A model can represent conceptual change as splitting categories, composing relations, or revising a measurement interpretation. But if its grammar lists every permitted new concept, it explains search within that grammar. It does not thereby explain unrestricted discovery. Conversely, “new concepts emerge” without a generation and selection process explains little. Finite representational experiments are legitimate local tests; they must not be sold as the solution to conceptual creativity.

**Methods.** A method is an executable arrangement for producing and interpreting evidence, including instruments, skills, sampling, computation, and checking. Its spread need not track reliability. A cheap, teachable, prestigious, or administratively convenient method can outcompete a better one. A theory restricted to normatively optimal method choice will fail as a descriptive theory unless it models this gap.

**Standards.** Standards regulate what counts as a good question, satisfactory evidence, adequate explanation, or acceptable risk. Some changes follow discoveries about a method's error behavior; others reflect changed purposes or power. Learning that an assay produces false positives can justify a procedural change under continuing aims. Deciding that a previously ignored population deserves protection changes an evaluative commitment as well. These should not be collapsed into a single “adaptive improvement.”

## Self-application without a vicious regress

If methods for evaluating methods require evaluation, must inquiry wait for an infinite chain of certification? No—unless one demands an antecedent, infallible certificate for every step. A finite investigator can compare a proposed change against accessible evidence while provisionally holding some commitments fixed. Those commitments can later become objects of scrutiny. Sequential dependence is not infinite execution.

For example, a laboratory compares two calibration procedures on separately prepared references, records both procedures' predictions, and changes procedure after detecting a systematic discrepancy. It need not first solve all epistemology. But its references, independence assumptions, and error criteria remain fallible. Local checking supplies conditional warrant; it does not certify the whole system from outside itself.

There are nevertheless two real hazards. First, the same corrupted measurement can validate both the original method and its supposed auditor. Second, changing the target after every failure can manufacture apparent improvement. Versioned standards, retained predictions, cross-checks that expose relevant shared errors, and explicit accounts of target changes address particular hazards. None is a universal self-validation device.

The framework itself receives no exemption: identifying an unmodeled causal route, an unpriced cost, or an alternative explanation should narrow or revise its claims. An account in which every success confirms adaptability and every failure confirms embedded limitations would have no discriminating content.

## Evaluating revision without a permanent universal scalar

A fixed scalar objective is sufficient for some comparisons but not necessary for every warranted revision. Let a procedure have an assessed vector of error, delay, expense, and specified harms. Under declared constraints, one procedure may weakly improve all relevant dimensions and strictly improve at least one. That is a local dominance argument. Its force is limited by the chosen dimensions, uncertainty, and the population over which they are assessed.

When dimensions conflict, the vector deliberately does not select a unique winner. Constraints, negotiated priorities, or explicit choices are needed. Calling a tradeoff “adaptation” does not settle it. Likewise, adding a neglected dimension can expose a limitation in an earlier assessment without proving one universally best aggregation rule.

For a proposed standard revision, assess both old and new procedures under both standards. This cross-evaluation can distinguish better performance on a continuing target from a change of target. Also examine consequences that both parties accept as relevant, costs of transition, previously uncounted failures, and whether the new standard can be challenged. Agreement on some comparisons need not require agreement on everything.

If the parties share no evaluative commitments and reject every common test, this procedure may not adjudicate their conflict. That is an honest limit, not evidence that every choice is equally warranted. The framework can model how the disagreement persists without claiming to resolve its legitimacy.

This is not an unprecedented problem. ## Strong counterexamples and their consequences

**Successful closure.** A finite question can be correctly answered, documented, and its laboratory closed. No universal duty to preserve that laboratory follows. The relevant condition is capacity for *specified future inquiry*, possibly supplied elsewhere, not perpetual survival of each institution.

**Productive commitment.** Refusing to reopen foundational assumptions during every calculation can enable difficult work. A theory that predicts maximum revision or criticism at every step is false even in simple finite-budget models. Scope, scheduling, and costs matter.

**Open but uninformative inquiry.** A community may protect disagreement and fully disclose methods while all available instruments share an unresolved ambiguity. Procedural openness cannot manufacture an identifying channel.

**Reliable but closed episodes.** A narrowly defined process can deliver accurate results without broad local revisability because an external designer supplied a correct model and a stable environment. This defeats the claim that local revisability is necessary for every instance of knowledge; it does not establish reliable correction after unforeseen change.

**Strategic adaptation.** Investigators can learn how to pass audits while concealing errors, and administrators can alter definitions of success. Adaptability without an accountable target can improve appearance rather than knowledge.

**Genericity.** Courts, engineering teams, journalists, and everyday problem solvers also allocate attention, revise categories, and check evidence. The framework's generality may be an advantage, but these features do not demarcate science. A theory of science additionally needs accounts of particular institutions, accumulated instruments, training, public records, and disciplinary practices. It need not identify a timeless necessary-and-sufficient boundary.

**Equivalent rival descriptions.** A suitably specified Bayesian decision process, control model, or institutional model may encode exactly the same transitions. Then the proposed framework supplies a synthesis or useful modeling discipline, not an empirically distinct rival. Relabeling equivalent dynamics cannot establish explanatory superiority.

## A discriminating study worth conducting

Use a simulated laboratory where an incumbent instrument supports successful prediction on current cases but cannot separate two causes that matter after a later shift. Records can retain or discard a second feature. A calibration capability has a real maintenance cost and can disappear. A resource-allocation rule rewards current predictive performance. Agents may propose questions, modify categories, buy tests, and revise evaluation procedures within a declared finite grammar.

Compare three **models of the same system**: (A) fixed-access learning, (B) learning with exogenous resource decline, and (C) endogenous loss or renewal of diagnostic capacity through current allocation choices. Independently manipulate whether rewards affect future capacity, whether stored features remain available, and whether the later shift creates a genuine diagnostic need. Match total resource caps and account for actual costs. Include stable worlds where preserving capacity is wasteful.

Freeze rival predictions before results. The substantive prediction is an interaction: a reward rule favoring present performance should prolong mistaken diagnosis specifically when it removes a capability later required to discriminate causes; decoupling that capability's maintenance from the reward rule should reduce this delay when its price is justified. Measure capacity loss *before* the shift, admitted questions, representational changes, diagnostic error, false revision, time, and cost. This chronology tests the proposed mediation rather than merely correlating openness with success.

The mechanism loses support if the interaction is absent where its conditions hold, if access changes cannot explain the outcome, or if simpler rival models predict intervention effects equally well. A strategy can also fail on net after its preservation costs are charged. Even success establishes only a local mechanism. To support a theory of science, extend to human laboratory tasks and prospectively coded institutional cases, comparing selection explanations rather than treating an engineered simulation as historical evidence.

**Decision:** pursue this as a conditional, mechanistic theory of scientific change. The strongest next contribution is a restricted transition model with a consequential prediction. Do not yet claim that S derives science, that correction capacity is a universal scientific essence, or that the approach uniquely resolves conceptual creation or evaluative disagreement.
