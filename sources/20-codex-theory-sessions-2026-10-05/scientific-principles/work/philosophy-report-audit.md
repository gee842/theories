# Philosophy audit of the integrated investigation

5 October 2026. Read-only audit of `outputs/scientific-principles-investigation.md`, focusing on its definitions, conditional derivation claim, and “What the philosophy sources add.” No new broad literature research was performed.

## Verdict

The historical characterization is supported by the verified primary passages. No material overstatement of a necessary-and-sufficient demarcation criterion was found. The report consistently separates mathematical requirements for declared aims from an account of the entire scientific enterprise, and distinguishes testability from truth, reliability, practical feasibility, and ethical legitimacy.

The discussion correctly avoids caricaturing Popper as requiring abandonment after one anomalous reading. Duhem's package-level problem is preserved without inferring that criticism is impossible. Kuhn's normal-research account is treated as a distinct position about scientific practice, rather than a denial of statistical distinguishability. Lakatos's programme-level appraisal is stated at the level warranted by the short primary lecture; it does not import his polemical characterization of Kuhn as settled fact. Laudan's unfinished reading is accurately disclosed.

## Two small clarity improvements

1. **Probability-law distinction in the dependency table.** The row “Compare two distinct, fixed probability models using likelihoods” could be sharpened to “Compare two fixed models inducing distinct probability laws for the accessible evidence.” Distinct causal or latent models can have identical observable laws, as the report itself demonstrates. The later finite-mass proof is already correctly specified. This is a wording clarification, not a flaw in that proof.

2. **Definition of probabilistic prediction.** “Assigns probabilities before the relevant observations are used to assess them” describes a prospective protocol rather than the whole concept. It may inadvertently exclude properly accounted-for posterior predictive checking or in-sample model criticism. Suggested replacement: “Probabilistic prediction specifies probability laws for observations. Prospective predictions are fixed before their assessment outcomes; fitting or updating on assessment data requires the evaluation procedure to account for that reuse.” This preserves the report's anti-overfitting point without turning fresh-data evaluation into an unconditional definition of scientific testing.

## Scope of the audit

No new mathematical or computational verification was attempted. The package-refutation argument, distinction between continuous probability zero and impossibility, and finite full-support stochastic example are conceptually sound. The statement that bare falsifiability does not suffice for epistemic reliability is appropriately weaker than claiming to have refuted every possible demarcation proposal. The statement about mathematical and exploratory components of science also concerns their role within inquiry, not a blanket exemption from evidential accountability.

The conditional-derivation framing should remain prominent in the final answer. The most defensible user-facing conclusion is that constraints on discriminating, checking, and correcting claims can be derived from specified aims and interfaces; historically developed scientific practices remain contingent ways of meeting those constraints.
