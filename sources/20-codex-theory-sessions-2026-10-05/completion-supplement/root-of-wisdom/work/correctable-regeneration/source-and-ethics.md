## Two directly relevant primary predecessors

1. [Sigmund, De Silva, Traulsen, and Hauert (2010), *Social learning promotes institutions for governing the commons*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09203). The three-page [author-hosted article](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~hauert/publications/reprints/sigmund_nature10.pdf) was read. Its model compares paid peer and pool punishment, including contributors who do not support sanctioning. Which regime spreads depends on second-order punishment and the permitted participation choices. It does not fully specify implementation of the institution. This is close prior work for costly maintenance of a rule, not a warrant to infer legitimacy from its persistence.

2. [Hilbe, Traulsen, Röhl, and Milinski (2014), *Democratic decisions establish stable authorities that overcome the paradox of second-order punishment*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1315273111). The five-page [institutional repository article](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_1909829/component/file_1909830/content) was read. It combines a model with a public-goods experiment comparing repeated choice between institutions with a binding majority choice. Institutional selection and payoffs differed between the treatments. This establishes a specific relevance of governance choice; it is not a general proof that majority rule protects every participant.

No empirical result from either paper was reproduced here. The new construction must be assessed under its own production, reporting, delay, payment, and enforcement assumptions.

## The concrete proposed addition

The mechanism agent proposes two modes with the same productive output and continuing shared reserve. One mode privileges an incumbent while depleting a capacity belonging to another participant, whose capacity is not required for that aggregate production. A participant-report and binding-response channel has its own maintained stock, consumes resources when used, and acts after a delay. The alternative mode restores the participant capacity. Current-return selection can restore the incumbent-favoring mode, making correction a recurring requirement.

The discriminating result is not merely that correction costs something. It is that **aggregate regeneration can coexist with participant depletion, while participant protection requires a separate, recurrently funded causal route**. An alarm triggered only by the shared stock misses the damage by construction. Explicit response delay, participant reserve, and recurrent funding can then be tested together. The particular cycle and thresholds require independent mathematical and executable verification; this source note does not certify them.

This compares two distinct propositions:

- The productive arrangement renews what it needs to continue.
- The arrangement protects the declared capacity of every included participant.

The first does not imply the second when a participant is dispensable to aggregate production. That gap is exactly why moving to a larger system boundary does not, by itself, transform persistence into goodness.

## Concrete evaluation criteria

The finished construction should report separate outcomes, under identical production and initial-resource assumptions:

1. Shared support stays above its declared floor on every modeled step.
2. Each protected participant capacity stays above its own declared floor, including during filing and response delay.
3. The correction mechanism remains capable of its next required operation; its successor capacity and every due payment are actually funded.
4. Participant access is usable at the time harm must be reported. An unaffordable, too-late, or powerless formal permission does not satisfy this operational criterion.

If filing or funding is prescribed by the supplied protocol, say so. If voluntary participation is claimed, compare the participant's consequences under filing and the specified no-filing/refusal option at the actual information history, using the existing participation framework. A physically affordable contribution is not automatically willingly supplied. If the mechanism can reduce the refusal option itself, apparent acceptance requires an additional audit rather than being counted as evidence of freedom.

The binding force of the gate also has to be named as a supplied law or strategy. Materially maintaining a gate can explain when it operates; it does not establish why powerful actors comply, how its authority first appeared, or whether its decisions are legitimate. If strategic compliance is not modeled, describe operational recurrence rather than a self-enforcing institution.

## Ethical interpretation of a successful result

The normative premise is that the protected participant matters independently of whether the productive whole needs that participant. The model makes that premise effective through a specified floor and corrective rule; it does not derive the premise from selection.

A successful test would show how one specified form of participant protection can be renewed alongside material production despite recurrent capture pressure. It would not prove complete welfare, universal justice, or that all participants endorse the regime. Participant capacity is an explicit operational measure, not a synonym for a person's entire good.

For the user's proposed relation between generativity and 善, the advance is consequently precise: ethically favorable generation requires inspecting whose future capacity is renewed, including people whose deprivation would not interrupt the system. The system's ability to correct such deprivation is itself a capacity that must be kept alive. This yields a concrete investigation of good generativity without declaring that every persistent or expanding organization is good.
