# How regeneration can sustain effective correction

Research continuation for Ren, 5 October 2026. This completes the proposed finite mechanism study connecting shared renewal, participant consequences, and materially supported revision of an allocation rule. Earlier theories remain available. The result is a conditional mathematical construction, not an empirical theory of every institution.

**A system can renew its shared productive support while depleting a participant's protected capacity. Under the specified replacement rule, protecting that participant requires a causal route responsive to that harm, and maintaining that route requires funding its repeated use as well as its existence.** The construction below demonstrates both outcomes under the same production technology and initial resources.

This sharpens the proposed connection between generativity and 善. The ethical premise is that an affected participant matters independently of whether the productive system needs that participant to survive. The model explains how one specified protection can be made operational; it does not derive that premise from reproduction or persistence.

## A shared system and an affected participant

The system produces ten resource units per round while its shared productive support satisfies \(E\ge2\); the main comparisons start at \(E=3\). Two units replenish the two units of shared wear. Both allocation rules therefore preserve the shared support stock \(E\). Production presumes continuing access to supplied energy or feedstock; this is not a closed physical universe. The affected participant is a recipient whose protected capacity is not required for this production; this deliberate separation permits testing whether aggregate persistence implies participant protection.

Two supplied rules allocate the remaining output differently. A funding amount \(f\) is reserved for a correction mechanism in either rule:

| Recipient | Restorative rule F | Extractive rule X |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shared productive support | 2 | 2 |
| Correction mechanism | \(f\) | \(f\) |
| Affected participant | 3 | 1 |
| Incumbent operator | \(5-f\) | \(7-f\) |
| Total | 10 | 10 |

The comparison uses nonnegative allocations. At the main threshold \(f=11/9\), both incumbent allocations are positive. X gives the incumbent operator two more units than F. The specified replacement rule installs the higher-current-return rule whenever no effective corrective mandate constrains it. This is a supplied deterministic rule for selection among operating practices, not a complete model of human choice or biological evolution.

The participant has reserve \(B\), capped at six, and needs two units per round to support a specified function. F replenishes one net unit; X removes one. The protected requirement is \(B\ge1\) at the end of every round. At zero, the participant has lost the modeled capacity, including effective access to filing. This is not a claim that the participant literally dies or that this one stock measures their entire well-being.

Under controller-only revision, X keeps being selected. Starting at six, the participant's reserve reaches zero after six rounds, although shared production continues. Thereafter unmet participant needs can accumulate while the aggregate system keeps operating. Monitoring only shared support gives no alarm.

## A correction channel that has to remain usable

The corrective mechanism has its own reserve \(Q\), capacity \(K\), observation channel, and binding allocation gate. It must pay one unit of upkeep at the start of each round. Initiating a claim costs it two additional units, while the participant pays one unit to file. Both bills are due before that round's production arrives. Thus filing requires at least three units already accessible in \(Q\).

If upkeep cannot be paid, the material carrier fails and its gate becomes ineffective. Future allocations do not automatically reconstruct a destroyed carrier. A separate installation mechanism would be needed for that. The stock is a means of operating the specified channel, not a substitute for having the channel or authority in the first place.

The participant protocol files when X is active and \(B\le4\), provided filing is affordable and the corrective carrier operates. Correction has a two-round delay, including the filing round: X remains in effect during those rounds. The gate then installs F until the participant reaches six, when the temporary mandate expires. The incumbent operator's greater private return under X again makes X the selected rule.

This specifies who initiates correction, which observation triggers it, how its costs are paid, what changes the allocation, and when the mandate ends. Automatic filing, the reserved funding stream, and the binding gate are supplied features of this institution. Its strategic acceptance, historical origin, and legitimacy are not inferred from affordability.

## One full round of depletion and restoration

Start with the participant at six and an operational carrier. The exact participant cycle is:

| Round | Effective action | Participant reserve afterward |
|---|---|---:|
| Start | Full participant reserve | 6 |
| 1 | X allocation | 5 |
| 2 | X allocation | 4 |
| 3 | Pay one to file; first delayed X allocation | 2 |
| 4 | Second delayed X allocation | 1 |
| 5 | Binding F allocation | 2 |
| 6 | F allocation | 3 |
| 7 | F allocation | 4 |
| 8 | F allocation | 5 |
| 9 | F allocation; release mandate afterward | 6 |

The restored reserve is the actual starting resource for the next cycle. Over nine rounds, participant receipts total nineteen: four X receipts and five F receipts. Eighteen units support the ordinary function and one pays filing. Nothing is replenished by resetting the state between trials.

The model permits the participant to use the current round's allocation toward that round's ordinary needs. Filing and carrier expenses, in contrast, are prefunded. The end-of-round protection criterion must not be confused with a stricter requirement to prepay every ordinary need while retaining an intraround reserve.

## Exact funding and timing boundaries

Each nine-round cycle requires nine units of carrier upkeep and two units for a claim. With no outside credit or additional subsidy, recurring operation therefore requires

\[
\boxed{f\ge1+\frac29=\frac{11}{9}.}
\]

Below this rate, every successful cycle spends more than it receives. A finite initial reserve can finance only finitely many such cycles. Maintenance-only funding \(f=1\) can keep an idle channel present while failing to finance its continuing use.

The average condition has a separate liquidity requirement:

\[
\boxed{K\ge3.}
\]

Even abundant future funding cannot pay a three-unit prefunded bill through a smaller store. At \(f=11/9\), capacity three and initial carrier reserve three are sufficient. The first two ordinary rounds keep the already full store at three; filing leaves \(11/9\) after current funding arrives; the next eight ordinary rounds restore it to three before the next claim. The resulting cycle can repeat indefinitely. Any initial overflow is recorded rather than reused.

More exactly, at this funding rate the least sufficient initial reserve is \(23/9\) when the participant starts at six, because two ordinary rounds add \(4/9\) before filing. This initialization belongs to the stated phase of the cycle; another initial stage needs its own prefix check.

The participant has a different constraint. If filing occurs at reserve \(h\), filing costs \(c\), and \(d\ge1\) rounds of X follow before restoration, its minimum reserve is \(h-c-d\). Hence

\[
\boxed{h\ge1+c+d.}
\]

For \(h=4,c=1,d=2\), the minimum is exactly one. Waiting one round longer, or adding a third delayed X round, breaches the protection requirement before the favorable rule can act. More money in the carrier cannot replace the participant's missing time or reserve.

These thresholds are sharp for the specified protocol, cost accounting, release rule, and initialization. They do not establish a universal minimum cost of justice or an optimum over all possible institutions. A permanently effective alternative rule could avoid these recurring claims; its implementation would require its own account.

Earlier action also changes recurring cost. Filing at five instead of four keeps the participant's minimum at two, but shortens the cycle to seven rounds. Required recurring funding rises to \(1+2/7=9/7\). The safer timing margin has a specific additional cost under this release rule; an unchanged budget cannot be assumed to pay for it indefinitely.

## What the comparisons establish

An externally fixed F rule is a reference case: it protects the participant without corrective claims, while stipulating that replacement cannot overturn it. It is not evidence that permanent protection is free or naturally maintained.

The meaningful matched comparison holds production, initial stocks, allocated correction funding, and payment rules fixed between controller-only revision and participant-effective revision. The intervention changes which participant signal can activate the binding route. Under the former, the shared system persists while the participant loses capacity. Under the latter, the paid cycle protects both.

The checks separately reduce funding, delay execution, disable effective claim access, and withdraw binding authority. The access switch disables the claim route jointly; it does not isolate sensing from every later stage of communication and execution. A statement of rights, a funded reserve, and a working route to rule change are distinct conditions. The corrected result depends on their conjunction; it is not caused by an undisclosed increase in production.

The exact comparisons include these failures:

| Intervention | Consequence under the specified initial conditions |
|---|---|
| Controller-only revision, same resources | Participant reserve reaches zero in round 6; shared production persists. |
| Carrier funded only for upkeep | One recovery succeeds; participant reserve reaches zero in round 15. |
| A third delayed X round | Participant protection fails in round 5, before restoration takes effect. |
| Funding just below the recurring threshold, with a larger initial reserve | Failure is postponed, not eliminated; the independently checked example fails in round 194. |
| Withdraw funding surplus, effective claim access, or binding authority after the first recovery | Each withdrawal breaks participant protection six rounds later. |

The last interventions locate a remaining dependency: this institution renews correction while its stipulated funding and execution arrangements hold. It does not prove immunity to arbitrary capture of those arrangements. No rescue after a breached floor is counted as uninterrupted protection.

## The connection to generativity and 善

The completed model separates three propositions: the system renews its productive support; the participant retains a protected capacity; and the participant's route to future correction remains usable. The first can hold while the other two fail.

That is the precise limit of judging goodness by the survival of a larger whole. Some affected beings are dispensable to the processes that affect them. Ethical standing asks that their consequences count anyway. Making the declared protection effective in the model requires an explicit protection criterion and a working path through which that criterion can affect outcomes.

Our candidate formulation is therefore:

> **Ethically favorable generativity includes renewing affected beings' worthwhile capacities and their effective means to correct how those capacities are treated.**

This is a proposed ethical commitment joined to a causal mechanism. It does not identify every form of degeneration with moral evil, classify participants by their productivity, or settle all conflicts of welfare, agency, and obligation. The protected stock here measures one capacity, and the supplied protocol supports one kind of complaint.

The bounded plan is completed by the mechanism, comparisons, recurrence proof, and explicit failure boundaries. The broader origin and legitimacy of corrective institutions, unrestricted new threats, and a universal derivation of 善 remain outside what this construction establishes.
