# Paid evaluator revision before renewal capacity is lost

The evaluator now changes its own forecasting rule after a hidden change in the settlement contract. Observation, preservation of its report, replacement of the forecast table, and the next physical birth are all financed. The result is an exact window for this revision route: learning the correct contract can occur while too little remains to create a usable successor.

The experiment extends the [previous funded population](../cycle-5/funded-selection.md). The outer revision program, two possible contracts, and two available operating programs remain supplied. This is actual updating within a known family, not invention of a new model class or an explanation of every outer institution.

## The operating plant and the changed contract

Programs S and L still operate the same eight-module plant for two fault cycles. Each newborn has cash 240 and no auxiliary channels. The one-cycle planner S installs two channels and finishes with cash 100; the two-cycle planner L installs four and finishes with cash 136. Their early cash reports remain 140 and 128 respectively. The [checker](verification/check_evaluator_revision.py) imports the actual planners and slot-level accounts from the preserved earlier model.

The changed environment is a declared **retirement settlement contract**, not a change secretly inserted into the plant's repair law. Its rate is either `d=0` or `d=24`. Each retiring parent pays `d` for each installed auxiliary channel, then receives a basic-plant allowance `11d/4`. Thus the changed contract charges 24 per channel and supplies an external allowance of 66 per parent. These are separate outgoing and incoming payments; the allowance is not generated by a score.

| Quantity | S | L |
|---|---:|---:|
| Actual cash before settlement | 100 | 136 |
| Auxiliary channels | 2 | 4 |
| Remittance under old contract | 100 | 136 |
| Charge under changed contract | 48 | 96 |
| External allowance under changed contract | 66 | 66 |
| Remittance under changed contract | 118 | 106 |

Both programs can pay the charge **before** the allowance arrives. The new settlement reverses their ranking. Their internal planners still optimize the supplied operating cash objective before retirement settlement; a change in that objective would be a different control.

The old evaluator maps early reports to the old remittances and selects L. The revised evaluator maps them to the changed remittances and selects S. The numerical early reports themselves do not change. Counterfactual forecasts for both known programs are stored, so a candidate need not still have a live parent in order for its known profile to be evaluated.

There is at most one irreversible change from the old to the new contract; its onset is not supplied to the evaluator and it may never occur. After the changed contract is confirmed, no further monitoring is needed under this declared family. Arbitrary repeated or previously unrepresented changes are outside the result.

## What is retained and what reaches the evaluator

A paid archive retains the actual S and L program payloads, the two supplied profile tables, the installed evaluator bit, and its finite scheduling state. Custody costs two per population epoch. A source address is not executable program code: copying needs the archived payload or an accessible current donor. A control deletes the S template before the experiment, while all current parents are L.

The evaluator's routine observation interface contains the paid early reports. It does not receive the funder's treasury or aggregate late remittances. A separate inspector can provide the settlement-contract bit at the end of an epoch. A physical report register must carry that bit across the inspector's retirement to the rewrite controller. Without retention, that register resets to empty.

The actual monitoring program uses only its phase counter, installed model bit, and retained report. Financial services enforce affordability; an unpayable requested transaction cannot execute. No analyst history is passed as controller memory. The fixed custody price covers the finite registers and supplied tables used here; it is not a general memory-price law.

This interface restriction is consequential. Access to ordinary late returns or settlement bills could provide a cheaper signal, and payment refusals or balances can themselves convey information in a different interface. We make **no claim that the paid inspector is the cheapest possible route**, or that no other admissible controller could infer the contract. The experiment checks this actual observation-and-revision mechanism.

## Chronology and the complete account

A population epoch remains two repair cycles, sixteen physical slots. All births use the same endpoint. The constant grant is 150 per active parent, in either contract. A newborn packet is still 260: cash 240 transferred to the child, basic-plant construction 16, and copying four. The maximum population is four; treasury is capped at 259 **after** births, with overflow discarded.

The initial state is two L parents, zero treasury, old calibration, and both program templates available. Founding finance 520 paid their plants, copied programs, and cash endowments; the initial evaluator and archive apparatus are separately endowed. Their subsequent custody is charged. This is a specified inherited state, not an unrecorded free production of two organizations.

Within each nonempty epoch:

1. The per-parent grant arrives. Prepay the ordinary early audit at two per parent and archive/controller custody at two total.
2. If scheduled, prepay the contract query at twelve and report retention at two.
3. Parents complete their unchanged two-cycle operations. They pay the settlement charge, receive its allowance, retire without salvage, and return their remaining cash.
4. The query result arrives. After the inspector retires, the retained register is read. A changed-contract report requests replacement of the old forecast table, costing 24.
5. The updated evaluator selects a program. Fund whole newborn packets using the remaining treasury, then cap the remainder.

Revision affects these newborns; it cannot change the already completed parent operations. The 24-unit rewrite is a supplied implementation bill, not a price inferred from the size of a mathematical bit. Query and handoff have no additional delay beyond the specified epoch boundary; the noisy companion models a separate explicit revision-time deadline.

With population `N_S,N_L`, treasury `R`, query/retention bill `Q`, rewrite bill `W`, and actual remittances `v_S,v_L`, the pool after revision is

\[
F=R+148(N_S+N_L)+v_SN_S+v_LN_L-2-Q-W.
\]

An earlier guard separately checks all prepaid costs against treasury plus the arriving grant. A nonnegative final pool cannot finance an earlier deficit. Birth count is `min(4,floor(F/260))` when the selected source exists. If its source is missing, this supplied controller produces no child and has no fallback; unused funds are capped and spilled normally.

The checker balances original parent cash, treasury, grants, operating receipts, and settlement allowances against operating expenses, settlement charges, prepaid fees, rewriting, construction/copy fees, child cash, remaining treasury, and spill. The newborn's 240 is a transfer. The separate replacement potential `Φ=260N+R` changes, before extra query/rewrite bills and spill, by

\[
\Delta\Phi_{\rm old}=-12N_S+24N_L-2,
\qquad
\Delta\Phi_{\rm changed}=6N_S-6N_L-2.
\]

Thus fixed all-S depletes under the old contract, and fixed all-L depletes under the changed contract. Old-contract all-L remains funded even with a query every epoch: its increment is `24N−16>0`. Changed-contract all-S, after monitoring stops, contributes `6N−2>0` and can finance at least its existing count at every epoch.

## An exact deadline for this revision route

First suppose the change has occurred by epoch one, but the first query is delayed to epoch `j`. The initial stale epoch maps two L parents and treasury zero to one L parent and treasury 246. Thereafter, while the old evaluator continues, one parent loses eight units of treasury per epoch. Immediately before epoch `j≥2`,

\[
R_j=262-8j.
\]

A query, retained report, and rewrite cost `12+2+24=38`. That epoch's L return and grant, after ordinary costs, supply `R_j+252`. To pay for one new S requires

\[
R_j+252-38\ge260
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_j\ge46
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad j\le27.
\]

| Revision time or control | State after the decisive epoch |
|---|---|
| Paid query and rewrite at epoch 27 | One S, treasury zero; funded renewal continues |
| Same procedure at epoch 28 | Correct new evaluator installed, treasury 252, no child |
| No revision | No successor at epoch 32 |
| Free signal, but rewrite still costs 24 | Latest successful epoch 28 |
| All query, retention, and rewrite costs waived | Latest successful epoch 31 |

At epoch 27, the newborn S receives the full operating endowment of 240. Treasury zero does not mean the child is unfunded. Its next renewal adds four to treasury at population one, establishing a closed continuation region.

At epoch 28, the evaluator has learned and updated correctly, but learning has arrived through a route that consumes the remaining birth finance. No active parent remains to receive the next grant. Empty states are absorbing even if archived code remains: treasury below 260 cannot construct a carrier and no external restart source exists.

**Epoch 27 is a protocol-specific recovery deadline, not an all-policy impossibility frontier.** A free signal, cheaper installation, funded credit, a permitted blind switch, or an alternative population allocator changes it. A blind switch at epoch 28 pays the same 24 as the free-signal control and can succeed if the contract really changed. Without a change, however, committing to all-S eventually depletes. The evaluator is not told which experiment it is in.

The missing-template control also matters: a correct new model cannot copy an absent S program from an all-L population. Under the supplied no-fallback execution rule, that revision yields no birth. Correcting a ranking and retaining an available alternative are distinct obligations.

## Monitoring a change whose date is unknown

The implemented periodic monitor queries every `q` epochs while its model remains old. A retained changed-contract bit triggers the paid rewrite, changes the selected program, and stops further queries. An old-contract report leaves the model unchanged. The clock is a finite phase register included in custody.

From the declared initial state, this program sustains nonempty renewal for **every possible single change onset, including no change, exactly when `1≤q≤27`**.

This is not an extrapolation from a finite simulation horizon. Before a change, all-L populations cannot shrink. From an initial count two, even querying at every step gives `Φ′≥min(Φ+32,1299)`, including the final capped step. After at most 25 old-contract epochs, the capped state of four L and treasury 259 is reached under every combination of query/no-query decisions. It remains fixed under both decisions. Later onset possibilities therefore reduce to the monitor's `q` clock phases.

The checker exhausts onset epochs `1,…,25+q` for each period from one to sixty, covering early transients and every recurring phase. Every period through 27 passes. For any period at least 28, onset at epoch one already defeats the mechanism: either the first query is beyond its paid recovery window or extinction precedes it. The no-change invariant separately covers never observing a change.

The 25-epoch bound is conservative. It bounds convergence of the pre-change financed state, not detection delay. The result relies on the single irreversible change and the decision to stop monitoring after confirmation; it does not certify continued fees for an indefinitely changing environment.

## A different allocator can avoid diagnosis

Neither reversal of the two fixed-program rankings nor the failure of late revision establishes that all persistence requires identifying the environment.

A separate control begins with **three S, one L, and treasury 16**, requiring outside founding finance 1,056 plus endowed control/archive apparatus. It uses a supplied alternating quota allocator: the next population is two S/two L; the next is three S/one L; repeat. It pays the same custody and physical birth bills, but never queries or rewrites. This is a different admitted controller and initial endowment, not a policy discovered by the original evaluator or a proved route from two L with treasury zero.

| Current cohort | Old-contract potential change | Changed-contract potential change |
|---|---:|---:|
| Three S and one L | −14 | +10 |
| Two S and two L | +22 | −2 |

Each fixed-contract pair of epochs gains eight before spill. The worst one-time crossing instead loses sixteen: old-contract first row followed by changed-contract second row. Initial treasury sixteen pays that sequence. A fourteen-unit reserve would cover a fixed old-contract pair but fails at this hidden crossing, showing why block averages alone do not prove the required guarantee.

The checker verifies a closed invariant with four phase/regime guards: before change, treasury is at least sixteen in the three-S phase and two in the two-S phase; after change, the respective bounds are zero and two. Every admissible stay/change transition preserves the next guard, including the treasury cap. All four carriers remain funded. This uses 1,020 invariant states and 1,522 certified edges.

Here a maintained mix of operating rules and enough shared support substitutes for diagnosis. The earlier monitor is still a valid adaptive mechanism; it is not the only conceivable response to uncertainty. Scale affects the available response routes through integer cohort composition, reserve sharing, and fixed institutional expenses—not merely through proportional enlargement.

## Verification and boundaries

The [results](verification/evaluator-revision-results.json) retain exact payment ledgers, detection controls, all tested periodic monitors, and the quota certificate. The [reserve and size frontier](verification/revision-window.csv) covers every initial population from one through four and treasury from zero through 259. The checker verifies 46,800 ledger/interface cases, 2,080 renewal-invariant states, 3,330 representative onset/period cases, and 1,040 initial-state frontiers, in addition to the quota edges.

The old plant and previous population implementation are read-only dependencies. Infinite statements use closed invariants, decreasing potential, or the finite phase reduction; finite traces alone do not establish them. The tariff, grants, candidate library, exact inspector, custody price, and update rule are supplied assumptions. The examples establish no empirical claim about real institutional fees, unavoidable observation costs, or the general superiority of adaptation over diversification.
