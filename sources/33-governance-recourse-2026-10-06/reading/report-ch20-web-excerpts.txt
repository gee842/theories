L21144@P596: decisions about social security debts. Effective merits review is an essential part of the legal framework

L21178@P597: where the initial decision is not affirmed, or, if it is affirmed, where it is requested to do so.15 It is not clear
L21179@P597: why AAT1 reasons for decision are not published and if there is a cogent legal or policy justification for

L21181@P597: against publication.18 (The justification for holding AAT1 hearings in private is that the AAT1 requirements

L21438@P604: Rather than appeal AAT1 decisions rejecting the use of income averaging as a basis for raising debts,

L21450@P604: More importantly, the Social Security (Administration) Act requires the secretary of DSS, whose powers
L21451@P604: DHS officers exercised pursuant to delegation, in “administering the social security law” to have regard
L21452@P604: to “the need to apply government policy in accordance with the law and with due regard to relevant
L21453@P604: decisions of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal”.84 There is no question that the 8 March 2017 decision

L21455@P604: reasoning about the lawfulness of income averaging. Consequently, the secretary and DHS officers
L21456@P604: exercising the secretary’s powers were obliged by statute to have regard to them.

L21458@P604: decision of the AAT1 is wrong, so that having regard to it will create problems in administering the law,
L21459@P604: DSS should appeal that decision to AAT2.85 If it is not willing to appeal, DHS and DSS should accept the

L21461@P604: inconsistencies in AAT decision-making, it should recommend that DSS undertake an appeal of the
L21462@P604: inconsistent decision in order to clarify the issues.

L21492@P605: The secretary has a broad power in s 126 of the Social Security (Administration) Act to review the “decision
L21493@P605: of an officer,” even if an application to the AAT is on foot “if the Secretary is satisfied that there is sufficient

L21495@P605: allows for a proceeding to resolve in advance of a full hearing or, if the matter does proceed to hearing, to
L21496@P605: clarify the issues in dispute.95 It is in keeping with the objective that the AAT is informal, cost effective and

L21498@P605: of the AAT’s decisions;97 it is plain on its face and in context that it does not contemplate the exercise
L21499@P605: of the review power once the decision has been made. Apart from the express reference to the power
L21500@P605: being exercisable even if an application to the AAT has been made (as opposed to determined), it appears
L21501@P605: in Part 4 of the Social Security (Administration) Act under “Internal Review”, an indication that it has no
L21502@P605: application to decisions made externally.
L21503@P605: The secretary is required to give notice of the review decision to the Registrar of the AAT, another
L21504@P605: indication that it can be made only while the AAT1 proceedings are live.98
L21505@P605: S 182, the second provision on which Mr Sparkes relied, does not create any power of review but concerns
L21506@P605: the situation where an officer varies or substitutes a decision after an application for AAT1 review or after
L21507@P605: an application for AAT2 review. The first situation would arise with the exercise of the s 126 power; in that
L21508@P605: instance the application (to AAT1) is taken to be for review of the new or varied decision. In the second
L21509@P605: situation (application to AAT2) the varied or substituted decision is treated as having been made by AAT1,
L21510@P605: and the application is taken to be for review of that decision. Such a situation might arise where a decision
L21511@P605: is upheld by AAT1 or a decision is set aside and the matter is remitted for a new decision to be made, or an
L21512@P605: application for AAT2 review is made and an officer then varies the decision upheld by AAT1 or substitutes
L21513@P605: another decision in accordance with the AAT’s directions.

L21519@P606: 5 Conclusion
L21520@P606: 5.1	 The disregard of Tribunal decisions
L21521@P606: The Scheme was launched in circumstances where the DSS legal advice was that income averaging, as
L21522@P606: it was used in the Scheme, was not in accordance with the legislation,99 and DHS had not obtained any
L21523@P606: relevant legal advice. The AAT1 decisions confirming that averaged PAYG data alone did not constitute
L21524@P606: evidence of fortnightly income earned, derived or received, as the Social Security Act required, should
L21525@P606: have reinforced that original advice and caused DHS and DSS to reconsider the legality of the Scheme.
L21526@P606: Instead, DHS chose not to recommend any challenge to those decisions, explicitly or tacitly accepting them
L21527@P606: as legally correct, but implementing them only as far as was convenient and disregarding their effect for
L21528@P606: the purposes of the Scheme as a whole.
L21529@P606: DSS took no active role, apart from discontinuing an appeal in the 4 May 2018 decision, which, as the AFAR
L21530@P606: astutely noted, would have brought the illegality issue into the public arena and undermined the “whole
L21531@P606: approach” of the OCI phase of the Scheme.
L21532@P606: Because the adverse decisions were not published, they were not publicly accessible. DHS was able to take
L21533@P606: advantage of that situation; in the narrower sense, by not bringing them to the attention of applicants or
L21534@P606: AAT members, and in the wider sense by continuing with a Scheme based on unlawful debt raising. DSS
L21535@P606: was shielded from the adverse publicity which would certainly have followed a public understanding of
L21536@P606: what these decisions were saying and how many of them there were.
L21537@P606: Clearly, to avoid repetition of that situation, publication of significant decisions in an accessible form is
L21538@P606: desirable. Professor Carney suggests that AAT1 be empowered, of its own volition, to publish de-identified
L21539@P606: rulings on key issues as it has done in the child support context.100 Economic Justice Australia supports a
L21540@P606: recommendation to that effect, as does the Commonwealth.101
L21541@P606: There was no systematic means of identifying AAT1 decisions significant to DHS’s application of law or
L21542@P606: policy, which was a serious failing; however, there is reason to doubt that, had one existed, it would have
L21543@P606: changed DHS’s view that it was entitled to disregard the AAT1 decisions. The SOS should have, in theory,
L21544@P606: caused referral of all the decisions which identified the legal issue in relation to income averaging to
L21545@P606: DSS, but they too seem to have been disregarded. Again, there is reason to doubt that even if they had
L21546@P606: been observed, DSS would have responded appropriately, given its conduct in relation to the 4 May 2018
L21547@P606: decision.
L21548@P606: As noted earlier in this chapter, it is the obligation of the secretary of DSS under s 8 of the Social
L21549@P606: Security (Administration) Act to pay due regard to relevant decisions of the AAT. Presumably, the referral
L21550@P606: requirements in the SOS are designed to bring relevant decisions to the attention of the secretary. Indeed,
L21551@P606: Ms Musolino’s evidence was that the AFAR process was about meeting those requirements.102 That did not
L21552@P606: happen; in fact, there is no evidence that the secretary was ever made aware of the effect of any relevant
L21553@P606: AAT decision by those who were responsible for informing the secretary about those matters. To the
L21554@P606: contrary, Finn Pratt AO (DSS Secretary) said that he did not recall any AAT cases about the illegitimacy of
L21555@P606: debt-raising on the basis of averaging.103
L21556@P606: It is impossible to see how the secretary could have met their obligations under section 8 without an
L21557@P606: effective system in place for referring relevant decisions. It is clear that the system that was in place was
L21558@P606: not effective; DHS was not referring relevant decisions to DSS, and DSS was doing nothing to ensure that it
