L1872@P44: Use of ‘averaged’ ATO income data
L1873@P44: 2.31 Under the Social Security Act, a fortnightly income test is applied to determine
L1874@P44: a daily rate of payment, generally paid in fortnightly instalments. A person’s
L1875@P44: entitlement in any given fortnight will therefore be assessed on the income they
L1876@P44: earned, derived or received that fortnight. This is different to the tax system (including
L1877@P44: family payments) which is concerned with assessing annual income. ATO data
L1878@P44: normally provides an aggregate annual employment income figure and does not
L1879@P44: provide the detail required to accurately assess fortnightly social security
L1880@P44: entitlements.
L1881@P44: 2.32 DHS has always had a process for averaging ATO data to calculate debts.101
L1882@P44: The process is set out in its operational guidelines102 however it was limited to last
L1883@P44: resort situations: ‘If every possible means of obtaining the actual income information
L1884@P44: has been attempted, it is possible to use any evidence available to raise a debt
L1885@P44: including an annual figure.’ The guideline also identifies the risks involved in
L1886@P44: averaging income data and shows DHS was aware that averaging ATO earnings
L1887@P44: may result in incorrect debts:
L1888@P44: ‘Some of the difficulties:
L1889@P44: - If a customer reports fortnightly and has under declared, there may be no evidence to
L1890@P44: support a finding of false or misleading information for any given fortnight
L1891@P44: - If employment is for a part of a year only, averaging over 12 months will not result in a
L1892@P44: correct result if the customer should have received a full rate at other times of the year
L1893@P44: - If income varied greatly during the year, the result may be incorrect
L1894@P44: Actual period(s) worked should be obtained so that averaging only occurs for periods
L1895@P44: worked.
L1896@P44: The raising and recovery of debts must satisfy legislative requirements. Evidence is
L1897@P44: required to support the claim that a legally recoverable debt exists’. 103
