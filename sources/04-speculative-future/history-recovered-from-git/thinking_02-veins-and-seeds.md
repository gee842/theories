# 02 · Veins and Seeds

A working map of the thought and practice we can build on. Two claims organize it:

1. **The future is already here, unevenly distributed.** Most of what a better future needs
   already exists — as theory, prototype, or working example — but scattered, unintegrated,
   and therefore not *perceivable as a whole*. Part of this project's job is integration:
   assembling the scattered seeds into something you can see at once.
2. **Every vein has an objective face and a perceptual face** (per `01`). Mechanisms change
   the payoff landscape; reframings change what we attend to and expect. We need both.

This is a living index, not a syllabus. Names are coordinates under ideas, not a reading list
to revere.

---

## A. Seeing differently (the perceptual escape)

- **Mode of attention** — Iain McGilchrist (*The Master and His Emissary*, *The Matter With
  Things*): the metacrisis as a crisis of *attention* — a civilization captured by a
  manipulative, decontextualizing mode at the expense of a relational, living one. The escape
  is partly a shift in *how we attend*.
- **Story of separation → story of interbeing** — Charles Eisenstein; contemplative
  traditions; the dissolution of the separate, competing self that the market frame presupposes.
- **Resonance vs. acceleration** — Hartmut Rosa: the good life as *resonant* relationship to
  the world, not controlled accumulation of resources. An alternative to alienation that isn't
  mere slowness.
- **Re-enchantment** — without a sense of the intrinsically valuable / sacred, everything
  collapses into instrumentality and the market (pure means) totalizes. Disenchantment (Weber)
  is part of why the future feels flat.
- **Slowing down** — Bayo Akomolafe: "the times are urgent; let us slow down." Post-activism,
  making sanctuary.

## B. Competition is not the deep law of nature (undoing the naturalization)

- **Major evolutionary transitions** (Maynard Smith & Szathmáry): life's great innovations are
  *formerly-competing units becoming cooperative wholes* — genes→chromosomes, cells→eukaryotes,
  organisms→multicellular, individuals→societies. Cooperation at a higher level is evolution's engine.
- **Symbiogenesis** (Lynn Margulis): the eukaryotic cell as a merger, not a conquest.
- **Mutual aid** (Kropotkin); **multilevel selection / prosociality** (David Sloan Wilson).
  Cooperation is at least as deep in nature as competition; social Darwinism is a choice of
  emphasis, not a finding.

## C. Moloch is escapable — the evidence

- **The commons is not doomed** — Elinor Ostrom (Nobel): communities *do* solve commons
  problems without privatization or top-down control, via polycentric, nested, self-governed
  institutions. Empirical refutation of inevitability.
- **The arrow toward non-zero-sum** — Robert Wright (*Nonzero*): a real, punctuated directional
  tendency toward larger-scale cooperation.
- **Anti-rivalrous goods** — value that *increases* the more others have it: language,
  knowledge, open source, trust, culture. Digital + AI tilt the economy toward these. A
  civilization organized around anti-rivalrous goods has fundamentally different dynamics.

## D. Coordination technology (the objective escape, AI-relevant)

- **Digital democracy that bridges** — Audrey Tang & Glen Weyl, *Plurality*; vTaiwan;
  **Polis** (consensus-finding by clustering agreement across divides). Real, deployed,
  anti-zero-sum politics.
- **Bridging-based ranking** — reward content/agents that bridge divides rather than maximize
  engagement/outrage (Ovadya). AI could power bridging at scale.
- **New economic primitives** — quadratic voting & funding, Harberger taxes, data dignity
  (Weyl & Posner, *Radical Markets*); assurance contracts for public goods; retroactive
  public-goods funding.
- **Cooperative AI** — AI that improves civilization's *cooperative* intelligence: credible
  commitment, trust-building, mediation, translation across worldviews (Dafoe et al.).

## E. Alternative economic frames

- **Doughnut economics** — Kate Raworth: thrive in the safe-and-just space between social
  foundation and ecological ceiling. Being operationalized by real cities (Amsterdam).
- **Post-scarcity / abundance** — artificial scarcity (housing, energy) manufactures zero-sum
  fights; cheap clean energy is a master key (solar cost curves). Earn the optimism with the
  engineering.
- **Commons / P2P** — Bauwens et al.: production organized around shared resources rather than
  enclosure.

## F. Whole-civilization visions (to engage *and* critique)

- **Game B** (Jordan Hall, Jim Rutt, Schmachtenberger): an explicit attempt to name a
  post-rivalrous civilization. Strong as a sensibility, thin on concrete texture — a gap this
  project could help fill.
- **The third attractor** (Schmachtenberger): neither catastrophe (first) nor dystopian
  control-to-prevent-catastrophe (second), but a genuinely new mode — wisdom commensurate with power.
- **Metamodernism** (Vermeulen & van den Akker; Hanzi Freinacht): the structure of feeling
  *after* postmodern irony — sincere, hopeful, constructive, but not naive. Possibly the
  meta-frame for the whole project.
- **Cosmotechnics** (Yuk Hui): there is not one universal Technology on a single trajectory but
  *many* technics, each embedded in a cosmology. Re-opens "what kind of technological future, in
  what kind of world." Decolonizes the future.

## G. Imagination as method

- **SF as social dreaming** — Ursula K. Le Guin: "We live in capitalism. Its power seems
  inescapable — so did the divine right of kings." Writers as "realists of a larger reality."
- **Emergent strategy** — adrienne maree brown (after Octavia Butler): "all organizing is
  science fiction"; shaping the future through fractal, adaptive, emergent practice.
- **Experiential futures / design fiction** — Stuart Candy et al.: make the future *felt*, not
  just argued. Directly relevant to the eventual artifact.

---

### Open questions this map raises

- Which vein is the **spine** of the presentation, and which are supporting?
- Is the through-line an *argument* (escape is possible, here's how) or an *experience* (feel a
  different future, then explain it)?
- How concrete do we go — gesture at a sensibility, or specify actual mechanisms and a
  plausible path?
