# 04 · Theory of Change

*Revised after correction. The earlier draft borrowed Friedman's "change needs a crisis." That is
rejected — both as defeatist, self-fulfilling reasoning and as the architecture of the disease. What
follows is reasoned from system dynamics, not from legitimated theory.*

> **Telos:** deform the attractor landscape *now* — make the alternative visible, build living
> instances of it, connect the people who already disbelieve the race — so the system can slide
> toward a better basin *before and without* catastrophe. Crisis-readiness is a hedge, not the plan.

---

## I. The present: a coordination failure held up by belief

- **Belief creates the trap.** "Others will defect; not racing means losing; we can't lose; so keep
  going." Acting on it makes it real — worship of Moloch so certain it feels like rationality. A
  cult dynamic; a form of extremism. It need not be so: we are here through *lack of wisdom*, not
  necessity.
- **Not only profit.** Even without profit-optimization, political realism alone turns the world
  into a dark forest. More than one engine drives the race.
- **The selection effect.** Most people do not want this and do not believe the delusion — but the
  system *selects* the ones who do into the driver's seat. Mass disbelief and universal racing
  coexist. (Havel, *The Power of the Powerless*: a system held up not by belief but by everyone
  *acting as if*; it looks eternal until private disbelief becomes *common knowledge* — then it
  falls fast.) The gap between what people want and what the system does is enormous **potential
  energy** — a latent majority, unorganized, disbelieving its own power.
- **Goals are leverage.** Neoliberalism installed a *system-goal* — "the corporation exists to
  maximize shareholder profit" — into the most powerful subsystem on Earth. The goal of a system is
  one of the highest leverage points there is; change it and behavior reorganizes. This is the real
  lesson of Friedman: not a strategy to borrow, but a case study in how a single goal-parameter
  drives a whole civilization's dynamics.

## II. The theory of change — deform the landscape, don't wait for the kick

A system in an attractor leaves it two ways:

1. **Perturbation** — a shock large enough to kick the state over a ridge. A *crisis.* But it is
   uncontrolled: you do not choose which basin you land in, and it may be a worse one (the
   authoritarian basin). Relying on it is passive *and* dangerous.
2. **Landscape deformation** — change the parameters and feedback structure so the current basin
   *shoals* and a new one *deepens*, until the system rolls over without a violent kick.
   Transformation *without* catastrophe. You choose the destination. This is what "act before the
   crisis" means.

"Only a crisis produces change" is therefore both a defeatist, self-fulfilling map *and* bad systems
thinking — it mistakes *change* for *perturbation* and ignores deformation entirely.

**Where to push (leverage, low → high):** parameters < feedback loops < information flows <
rules/goals < **paradigm** < the power to transcend paradigm. The project aims at the top — the
shared mental model ("competition is rational," "the market is the only coordinator") and who can
*see* what. That is the highest-leverage place to intervene, and it is available *now, pre-crisis.*
This is the dynamics-grounded form of "the substrate is how we see and choose."

**"I can't find the path"** is not a dead end — it is the signature of working at the top of the
leverage hierarchy, where causation is diffuse and slow (you can't flip a paradigm like a switch),
not absent. The path:
- make the alternative **visible** (information flow);
- build **living instances** of a different system-goal, so people see a real thing not organized
  around winning (material proof);
- **connect the latent majority to itself** (manufacture common knowledge).
All pre-crisis. All high-leverage.

**Map becomes territory.** Over time, belief concretizes — into institutions, money, property,
identities, infrastructure — until the constructed *is* the territory and the seam disappears
(hyperreality). You cannot disbelieve your way out of concrete. So the perceptual unlock is
necessary but not sufficient: once seeing shifts, you must **pour new territory** — build real
alternatives — or the old hyperreal stays standing on its material base. *"Living proofs, not just
ideas" is structural, not tactical.*

**Crisis-readiness — a hedge, not the plan.** If a rupture comes anyway, have the alternative ready.
But a crisis is a **fork, not a dawn**: the fear-and-control answers will also be lying around,
better funded, with an easier pitch ("give me power, I'll keep you safe"). So the alternative must
**out-compete fear in the moment** — vivid, felt, immediately usable. In a crisis, **induce beats
argue.** Do not depend on the rupture; do not need it.

## III. Means vs. ends (a standing discipline)

Centralized / decentralized / polycentric are **means, not ends** — the West is confused here, and
so is the canon. The ends: **non-domination, an open possibility space, no point capturing-and-
totalizing the whole, flourishing.** Structure is chosen by which serves those ends in a given
dynamic. (Defeating a multipolar trap may require strong, binding, *central-looking* coordination;
reflexive "decentralization = freedom" can sabotage the very coordination we need.) The invariant is
*functional*, not structural.

## IV. What this means for the artifact

- Work in a real mind — frightened or ordinary: **induce** a different seeing, then hand over a
  concrete foothold.
- Be **timing-robust** (valuable whether change comes by deformation now or rupture later), and
  paired with at least one **living proof.**
- Deepest function: **common-knowledge manufacture** — let the latent majority see that it exists.

## Method (standing)

Reason from **system dynamics** — feedback loops, attractors, leverage points, selection pressures —
not from legitimated theories, however high their status; those are *maps* to test against the
territory, not the territory. Keep **means and ends** distinct. Watch for *map-as-territory* in our
own cognition (the Friedman misstep was exactly that).

## Context (the strategist's stance)

The user prioritizes **acting before the crisis**, and names the honest frontier: *"I can't find the
path."* Crisis-prep is a reluctant hedge, not a belief that crisis is required. They want to **end**
the suffering, not exploit it. A rupture may come (~1–2 yrs in their read) — held loosely; the
strategy is built to be right regardless of timing.
