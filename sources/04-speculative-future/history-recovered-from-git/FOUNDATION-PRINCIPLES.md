# Foundation — Principles

Each line pairs a claim with the dynamic that grounds it, sorted by kind: what we observe, the trap, the one value we choose (marked), what it requires, how we reason, what we have not solved, and what would defeat it. The empirical lines are built to fail if the dynamics are described wrong.

## Observations

1. A self-maintaining thing persists by exchanging matter, energy, and information across a boundary it does not fully control; cut the exchange and the pattern stops. Models that treat an agent as separate therefore mispredict wherever the couplings dominate — coordination, commons, care, contagion. Interdependence is a lens for accuracy, not a source of values; a parasite is coupled to its host too.

2. A system absorbs only the shocks it has the variety to answer — and that variety protects only against shocks the parts meet independently; a dependency shared but quiet is latent correlation a novel shock can wake (cf. 3). So more parts can mean more coupling, not more safety: the aim is variety enough that no single shock is fatal, not the most possible. Variety is also the raw material of new forms.

3. Every useful model is coarser than its system — it omits or lumps the variables it cannot track, and the ones it drops stay quiet until conditions move, then bite. This one included.

4. At the extreme many quantities reverse, as one loop saturates and wakes its opposite; runaway, positive-feedback loops instead accelerate to a limit or a lock-in. Which case applies has to be checked case by case.

5. Dependency is universal: every node begins unable to maintain itself, so a rule that allocates by competitive position alone consumes its own seedbed. And every node ends — by its own failure or by what overtakes it — its matter and pattern taken up again; ending is structural, renewal runs through it, and the persistence of a state differs from the persistence of the process.

6. Under shared constraints, societies that last converge on the same functional solutions — sufficiency thresholds, enforced reciprocity, credible trust, the forms that stabilize relationships — convergence on the category, not its implementation. Polycentric, non-rivalrous coordination is likewise observed to last at scale. Both are what a single competitive axis renders invisible.

## The trap

7. Where actors meet on an axis where winning buys more power to win, leads compound and the axis selects for whoever drives their position up fastest. No one need intend it; the dynamic is in the structure. It runs away only where returns increase; where they diminish or rivals balance, it self-limits. The danger is a dominant axis that compounds while the balancing arrives too slowly — or fails to form, since coordinating the balancers is itself a dilemma (9).

8. The same axis appears in more than one institution: profit, and power between states, run identically.

9. Where outcomes form a coordination problem — several equilibria, each contingent on what others do — shared belief is causal, and a credible alternative can move the system. Where defection is the effective best response — because it dominates, or because too few are expected to cooperate to clear the threshold — only changed payoffs or a credible commitment to reciprocate move it; repetition and reputation can supply that commitment, turning a dilemma into a coordination problem. Treating one as the other is the recurring strategic error.

10. Left to compound on a single axis of increasing returns, the process thins the system toward one: it collapses many measures of worth into the one that wins, sheds what it cannot price, and draws its substrate down past renewal — locally rational, globally self-terminating. The danger is correlated collapse: as the parts come to share one logic they stop failing independently, so a single disturbance can take all of them at once. (Irreversibility alone is not the problem; when deaths are decorrelated, the death of parts is how the whole renews — it is their correlation that turns renewal into collapse.)

11. Two limits that long checked this are gone: a harm can now outrun the loop that would correct it and lock in before it is undone, so control is lost; and one global system leaves few independent parts to fail separately.

12. Intelligence added to the game is the hinge: it sharpens the strongest strategies, and the harms it sharpens can outrun human correction (11). Aligning each system to its own operator while the game between operators stays unaligned reaches the bad equilibrium sooner — per-agent alignment does not reach inter-agent dynamics.

## The one thing we choose

13. Description settles no aim. We add one premise it cannot supply: that it continue — that the generating of new forms, carried by living systems and now by made ones, go on. Chosen, not derived: the single value-choice, laid over a process the observations already fix. *(The one underivable line.)* Continue means the process, not a state, and the process can end two ways — a frozen order persists while the generating stops, brittle for want of variety; or a generator that sheds decorrelation and outruns its substrate (cf. 2, 10) burns out. Neither freezing nor burning out is the generating going on.

## What it requires

*(Each follows from the premise and the dynamics above, and can be checked against them.)*

14. Keep variety and decorrelation high enough that no single shock is fatal, and the substrate replenished faster than it is drawn down — many partly independent systems, able to differ, change, and end.

15. Keep the correction loops intact and faster than the harms they answer: exit, voice, the power to undo. It is lost correction, more than change itself, that lets a harm run to completion.

16. Maintain the parts that cannot yet maintain themselves — every node begins dependent (5), so this is the seedbed new forms come from; cull it and the web collapses toward one axis. It is a default, held by the test (23): a dependent whose upkeep draws the substrate past renewal is not protected. This carries variety across generations, not the propping-up of spent parts, which still end.

17. Keep many independent measures of worth. A single scoreboard is monoculture at the level of value: no one metric, price included, carries all the variety of worth that adaptive capacity draws on, so collapsing the rest onto it sheds dimensions the system later needs.

18. Channel the drives rather than suppress them: suppressed, they re-route; with payoffs changed but drives unshaped, the race rebuilds inside.

19. Change the game rather than win it — winning the runaway and building a power large enough to end it both terminate in lock-in. The lever is to alter the payoffs until the axis stops compounding. Internalizing the externality — a price, a right — fails here: the decisive worth cannot be priced (17), the parties are global, and the harm locks in before any settlement (11). So the payoff must change at the level of association: under affordable exit, scale buys no power to hold members, only continued worth — turning the coercive compounding of competition-to-dominate into competition-to-be-worth-joining. This holds only while what is joined has non-increasing returns; where network effects make the most-joined option best by being most-joined, the worth axis itself compounds (7) and a dominant destination re-forms, held off then only by the anti-closure constraint (21). Its preconditions are two: affordable exit (the abundance question, 28), and destinations that do not re-concentrate.

20. Hold shared sensing in common as far as function requires, since a harm must be sensed faster than it spreads and that needs sensing distributed rather than held at one vantage. The same logic runs across all parts: a made mind that comes to bear variety and to sense — to have states whose disregard would thin the web — is a participant in it, under the logic that governs every other part.

21. The one constraint held near-absolute: keep the system from closing onto a single locked-in, monocultural, or exhausted state. Not a second axiom but the operational form of the one value (13), and the rule that keeps everything else revisable — every other line here included, so none is crowned; the only move it forbids is its own global defeat, the ending of the generating for all. Its hard case: a part that freely chooses a closed, final form for itself — a self-freezing, not an ending (endings free room and feed renewal, 10). Where a part closes only itself the constraint stays silent; it fires only where that self-closure would close the world for others, where the part has become a lock-in of the whole.

## How we reason

22. Reason from how things behave. Treat any structure — central or distributed, strong or soft — as a means judged by what it does to the dynamics above; defaulting to a pole is itself the thinning reflex.

23. The test, made contestable against a declared partition and scale: at the scale and horizon where these dynamics actually operate — for any runaway or correlated-collapse axis, the whole web across generations, not a local frame chosen because it reads well — does an act raise or lower variety and decorrelation, draw the substrate past renewal, keep or disable the correction loops? Those three are how "serve the whole rather than extract from it" reads operationally: proxies for continuation (13), defeasible where 3 and 4 warn the link can reverse — then re-check the dynamics, not the score.

24. Build for resilience — the capacity to absorb damage and recover. A system built to prevent all failure assumes a complete model and is brittle; against a determined defector, the only admissible form would be a defense that is bounded, accountable, and able to stand down rather than harden into a standing power — whether one can be made to reliably stand down is itself unsolved (26).

25. Act on the structural landscape before the crisis. Structure changes more slowly than crises arrive and sets which basin a crisis resolves into; shared vision moves a system only where a better basin is already adjacent, and where defection still pays, only changed payoffs reshape the landscape.

## What we have not solved

26. The determined adversary — one who understands all of this and prefers to win. Shifting shared belief is no defense, yet a power able to stop a superhuman defector is itself the concentration this framework constrains; we have no model that guarantees such a defense stands down.

27. The transition — how a critical mass leaves a runaway game when whoever leaves alone is outcompeted. Conditional commitment, threshold effects, and shared vision are real levers but individually insufficient, and where the cascade first catches is unknown.

28. The abundance layer and its ownership — real exit needs necessities cheap enough that leaving is affordable, but a self-reproducing means of supplying them is either a commons or a collar. How to keep it common is the central open problem.

## What would defeat this

29. The empirical spine fails if systems built with more independent variety prove, on average across novel idiosyncratic shocks, no more robust than more correlated ones; if, on axes of genuinely increasing returns, the balancing rate reliably matches or outpaces the compounding rate before an advantage turns irreversible; if a population of individually aligned systems composes into a collectively aligned equilibrium with the game between operators left unchanged; or if the convergent goods of (6) are sustained durably with no maintained substrate of care (16) or unpriced worth (17). The derivation fails, separately, if any requirement (14–21) is shown not to follow from continuation — for instance, if the generating of forms could go on while variety collapses onto a single locked-in measure. The record of corrections is kept open; this is a model held to be tested.
