# Recursive selection, the present AI trajectory, and a cooperative transition

**Prepared 3 October 2026. Purpose: context for future theory-building agents and research collaborators.**

**Scope corrected after the author's clarification, 3 October 2026.** The central objective is to establish a well-supported, situational case for moving from the present AI arms-race trajectory toward cooperation. The author does not claim that every form of bounded competition is impossible or unstable. The relevant situation includes the pursuit of decisive advantage, pressure to accelerate, existing institutions and dependencies, and the restricted range of alternatives treated as politically or commercially realistic. The initial synthesis gave too much priority to a universal competition/cooperation classification and undercredited the existing meta-attractor results. This edition corrects that framing. Mathematical objections and evidential requirements remain; the intended conclusion is not assumed proven merely because its scope is now clearer.

This report develops a research program beyond the current manuscript. It does not repeat the completed mathematical review of v4.5, grade the manuscript's length, or recommend compressing the theory before its connections are established. Six AI reviewer agents examined governance; AI alignment; economics and game theory; cybernetics and systems thinking; plural Chinese/CCP-related perspectives; and plural Western perspectives. Targeted cross-review then challenged the proposed experiments and institutional conclusions. These are generated disciplinary assessments, not independent human peer reviews or endorsements by the institutions discussed. Their agreement is useful for organizing questions, not evidence that the theory is true.

## 1. Overall judgment

**The paper's central task is to explain why continuing the present trajectory of winning through acceleration is an inadequate route to durable safety and shared flourishing, and why a cooperative pivot is warranted.** The meta-attractor framework supplies a causal account of how that trajectory reproduces itself: success changes the rules, evidence, institutions, and perceived possibilities through which future success is selected. The wider theory connects this mechanism to delegation, shared exposure, correction, and the feasibility of changing course.

The original sentence describes a mechanism the work has already begun to formalize and prove:

> Recursive selection creates second-order selection pressure for mechanisms that increase control over future selection.

This is more than the observation that winners sometimes change rules. In v4.5, Proposition 9 and its appendix proof derive

$$
\Delta\pi'(q)=\chi p^2\bar y^{\mathsf T}M\bar y-C'(q).
$$

The proof derives horizon-averaged, carrier-specific rule support, subtracts its cost, and connects the payoff difference to the covariance determining change in mean control capacity under the stated replicator and transmission assumptions. It therefore provides a conditional mechanism for why control capacity itself is selected. Appropriability, persistence, size, cost, and inheritance are substantive parts of the result. Its domain-scope conclusion also distinguishes holding each domain's share fixed from holding total size fixed; the latter requires an additional coupling condition. Propositions 19–21 add field-level capture certificates, seeded cascades, reform re-absorption, and consequences of differently designed pauses. These are existing contributions to assess and build on, not missing ideas the next agent must invent.

Three questions must remain separate: **what the model proves; what is original relative to prior work; and whether the present AI situation satisfies the consequential premises.** A valid conditional proof answers the first within its scope. An originality assessment requires a result-by-result comparison. A present-world recommendation requires evidence and robust practical reasoning. None of these questions can replace the other two.

Acemoglu, Egorov, and Sonin analyze decisions that alter future decision-making power [S1]. This is a relevant predecessor. Its abstract does not establish that it already contains the manuscript's control-capacity selection result, coupled-field certificates, or application to the AI trajectory. The earlier summary was too dismissive when it reduced the originality question to “successful actors reshape the rules.” The appropriate next task is to identify overlap and differences in assumptions, results, and scope—not presume either originality or redundancy from a thematic resemblance.

The paper should keep the **situational diagnosis and cooperative transition at its center**. Explaining when correction is self-maintaining remains useful as a supporting framework. A proposed experiment in which an initially successful safety intervention later loses its advantage through endogenous evaluation would strengthen one mechanism; it is not a replacement thesis or a prerequisite for acknowledging the existing mathematical work.

### 1.1 The right scope of the conclusion

The argument to develop is: given the present combination of strategic objectives, acceleration, shared exposure, delegation pressures, and correction constraints, continuing along that trajectory creates sufficiently serious and insufficiently controlled dangers to warrant changing its governing logic toward cooperation. A rigorous version must state which premises are evidenced, which are uncertain, and how sensitive the recommendation is to those uncertainties.

Showing that some safely bounded competitive arrangement exists does not refute this claim. Such an arrangement may require exactly the change of trajectory the paper advocates. The relevant challenge is whether a proposed safeguard can be installed in time, maintained under present incentives, and made effective against the relevant hazards while preserving the trajectory being defended. Alternatively, it may demonstrate that a different cooperative design is preferable. Both are useful findings, but they answer different questions.

The distinction must be defined independently of the outcome. Specify in advance what counts as continuing the current trajectory: for example, retaining decisive relative advantage as the strategic objective, accelerating consequential deployment without a binding relation to correction capacity, and allowing competitive success to determine the terms of oversight. A critic's safeguard is not automatically a “pivot” merely because it succeeds. Examine what it actually changes.

## 3. Preserve the nine layers by specifying their interfaces

The next agent should retain the full architecture. The improvement is to make every connection accountable: what one module outputs, what the next assumes, and what evidence would justify that transfer.

| Layer | State or mechanism to retain | Interface that needs explicit support |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Host and shared conditions | Material resources, public infrastructure, social agency, irreversible losses. | Which actions affect which constraints, with what delays and uncertainty? |
| 2. First-order selection | Differential success, externalities, exposure to common harm. | What is reproduced or allocated, and which shared costs affect that process? |
| 3. AI contest and delegation | Capability investment, delegation, speed, defenses, strategic exposure. | How does an increase in capability change both harmful action and available correction? |
| 4. Endogenous correctors | Appointment, funding, standards, access, sanctions, amendment. | How does success buy control, and how does that control change subsequent selection? |
| 5. Endogenous observation | Evidence production, suppression, shared biases, reporting delays. | Does a changed score indicate improved quality, changed tasks, concealed harm, or a changed authorized objective? |
| 6. Failure and survival regimes | Physical harm, loss of agency, institutional breakdown, recoverability. | Which earlier mechanisms suffice for each distinct outcome, on which horizon? |
| 7. Trust and verification | Detection, interpretation, enforcement, uncertainty about rivals. | Can available evidence support timely action, and will authorized actors execute it? |
| 8. Cooperative institutions | Common functions, access rights, plural provision, distributed control. | Why can the arrangement fund and protect correction despite its managers' incentives? |
| 9. Transition and authority | Entry, exit, interim exposure, bargaining power, continuity, revision. | Can actors reach and maintain the arrangement without an unacceptable interval of vulnerability? |

Three separations are essential throughout.

**Training, deployment, and institutional selection have different units.** Updating model weights, reproducing an agent scaffold, awarding procurement share, and appointing an evaluator are not interchangeable processes. A frozen-model experiment can establish deployment or organizational selection effects, but cannot identify weight-training dynamics. Track inheritance, replacement, and timescale explicitly.

**Persons, firms, governments, and regimes have different interests.** A citizen's continued agency is not equivalent to a firm's market share or a government's indefinite tenure. “Actor survival” must not silently protect incumbency against legitimate replacement. Represent survival, agency, service continuity, and institutional persistence separately, with the normative choice stated.

**Technical control and legitimate authority are different.** A system may remain physically safe under an unaccountable controller. A procedurally legitimate institution may lack the means to avert harm. The theory needs both dimensions without deriving the legitimacy of a political objective from a dynamical equation.

An important comparison question is whether a specified endogenous rent-seeking model is equivalent to a specified recursive-selection model. That equivalence has not been established by this review. If an actual mapping is demonstrated, terminology alone cannot supply originality; identify any remaining contribution in unification, measurement, new results, or application. Until then, “a sufficiently rich model could contain it” is a research prompt, not a finding of redundancy.

A proper novelty test has three parts: solve a plausible simpler model; identify a result or prediction it misses; show that the new coupling explains that difference under independently justified assumptions. More arrows in a diagram do not establish a better theory. Conversely, unification can be a substantial contribution if it reduces separate problems to a shared measurable structure and improves predictions.

## 5. A common formal backbone

Start with a small recursive game or evolutionary process, not a fitted model of world politics. A useful state includes:

$$
z_t=(x_t,p_t,r_t,o_t,a_t,m_t),
$$

where $x_t$ is the physical and social condition; $p_t$ is the population or allocation of success; $r_t$ is the rule, resource, and institutional configuration; $o_t$ is the observation process; $a_t$ is executable authority; and $m_t$ contains memory, outstanding commitments, and delays. These symbols are a proposed shared vocabulary, not revisions to the manuscript's existing notation.

The minimal game can begin with two heterogeneous suppliers, one evaluator, and an affected constituency. Specify the sequence: amendment under current rules; entry or exit; investment in production, safety, and influence; production and evidence; adjudication and execution; reproduction or resource allocation; next-period state. Budgets, outside options, preferences, and commitment limits must be explicit. Do not let an actor buy an institutional right that the rules never allow anyone to sell.

Distinguish four mathematical tasks:

1. **Selection gradient:** under a specified environment, does more investment in control raise expected success?
2. **Dynamic trajectory:** under adaptation, entry, and loss of influence, does that investment spread, persist, or saturate?
3. **Strategic equilibrium:** after others respond and rules can change, is the behavior a best response or equilibrium?
4. **Institutional implementation:** can the funding, permissions, and commitments supporting that equilibrium actually survive deviations?

A positive derivative answers the first question. It does not by itself answer the other three. Likewise, an investment first-order condition is not a new selection theorem, and a condition sufficient for invasion is not a fixation guarantee.

### 5.1 The local feedback benchmark

For illustration, let $u_t$ be a small deviation in success and $v_t$ a deviation in favorable institutional control. Consider the linearization

$$
\begin{pmatrix}u_{t+1}\\v_{t+1}\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1-\mu&a\\b&1-\delta\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}u_t\\v_t\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,b\geq0,\quad0<\mu,\delta\leq1.
$$

Here $a$ converts control into success, $b$ converts success into control, and $\mu,\delta$ represent dissipation or countervailing adjustment. The dominant eigenvalue is

$$
1-\frac{\mu+\delta}{2}
+\sqrt{\frac{(\mu-\delta)^2}{4}+ab}.
$$

The discrete-time linear system is asymptotically stable exactly when $ab<\mu\delta$. This is a standard calculation, **not a proposed originality claim**. It is useful because each coefficient names a potentially measurable channel.

Instability here does not prove capture, harm, extinction, or an inevitable global outcome. Saturation, endogenous repair, switching, transient amplification, and changed incentives can alter the nonlinear result. Stability does not prove that a temporary excursion stays within a safety constraint. The research contribution would be estimating the channels independently and showing how they predict the fate of a substantive safety method or institutional safeguard outside the fitting sample.

### 5.2 Define capture independently of success

Use a neutral term such as *selector modification* until the harmful redirection is established. A capture claim should separately document:

- Success changes control over a relevant future selector.
- That change creates a persistent advantage for an actor or coalition.
- An independently specified entrusted purpose, protected interest, or corrective function is impaired relative to an appropriate comparator.

Include benign institutional adaptation: expert involvement may improve a benchmark, pooled resources may improve audit quality, and successful service may legitimately earn influence. Authorized purposes can themselves undergo legitimate revision. Specify how such revision is distinguished from unilateral replacement of the objective by the actor being assessed.

## 6. Safety, governability, and reachability

One of the strongest possible contributions is a precise account of systems that remain safe in their current state while losing workable routes to future correction. This connects observation, authority, and institutional persistence to alignment outcomes.

A useful finite-horizon definition is

$$
\mathcal V_{T,\epsilon}^{\Theta,\mathcal R}
=\left\{b:\exists\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{auth}},\quad
\inf_{\theta\in\Theta,\,\sigma\in\mathcal R}
\Pr_b^{\pi,\sigma,\theta}
\left[z_t\in K_{\mathrm{phys}}\cap K_{\mathrm{authority}}
\;\forall t\le T\right]\ge1-\epsilon\right\}.
$$

Here $b$ is an information state, $\Pi_{\mathrm{auth}}$ contains executable authorized policies, $\Theta$ is a stated uncertainty class, and $\mathcal R$ contains admissible rival responses. The protected sets are specified normative and technical constraints. This is a proposed definition using established control ideas; it is not a computed guarantee for AI society.

Report the physical and authority risks separately as well as jointly. The acceptable risk $\epsilon$ is different from statistical confidence that the real process lies in $\Theta$. Repeated finite-horizon guarantees cannot be silently converted into a lifetime guarantee. Fixed unknown parameters, learning, and correlated uncertainty require enough memory in $b$; a convenient Bellman recursion is not automatically valid.

The next formal target could be a result in which selection changes observation or authority so that the viable set contracts before realized harm increases. For example, two states may require incompatible safe actions, while the institution has lost the right to obtain the evidence distinguishing them. That can eliminate a robust guarantee even when each state is controllable with full information. It does **not** show that failure occurs with probability one.

Prove a positive companion result. A bounded system with an enforceable total-withdrawal cap may remain above a resource floor even if relative evaluation is distorted. Another design might preserve a protected observation channel and a feasible intervention under bounded compromise. Make the implementation boundary explicit: an assumed external cap demonstrates conditional engineering effectiveness, not that political actors will preserve its enforcement forever.

Ashby's treatment of regulation includes amplification through additional regulatory structure, not only fixed limits on a single regulator [S7]. For AI, the relevant empirical question is whether hazard-relevant observation, verification, and executable response grow fast enough. Counting every possible AI behavior against unaided human comprehension would bypass the strongest alternatives: abstraction, verified permissions, environmental constraints, distributed detection, and AI-assisted correction.

## 7. Five candidate results to pursue

| Candidate | What should be established | What must not be assumed or overclaimed |
|---|---|---|
| R1. Existing control-selection foundation | Audit, clearly credit, and where useful extend Proposition 9's conditional selection result; map its parameters to the present case. | Public benefits can favor free riders; delayed or reversible influence may not pay; local advantage is not fixation. |
| R2. Endogenous reversal of a safety intervention | An initial improvement increases subsequent success-dependent rule power, producing a later ranking reversal under stated conditions. | Ordinary risk compensation is an alternative; a fully endogenous rent-seeking model may already generate the effect. |
| R3. Loss of corrective options | Endogenous observation or authority changes remove safe policies before current performance reveals the problem. | Loss of a robust guarantee is different from certain harm; the information and disturbance models must be explicit. |
| R4. Durable correction under selection | A specified architecture preserves adequate evidence, authority, funding, and constraints under a bounded class of adaptations or deviations. | Independence cannot be a label; include the corrector's incentives and possible compromise. |
| R5. Reachable institutional transition | A path to a desired arrangement satisfies physical constraints, sequential participation, enforceability, and budget requirements. | Endpoint efficiency does not imply transition feasibility; bargaining consent does not exhaust public legitimacy. |

R2 paired with R4 is a useful optional extension: a failure mechanism and a constructive remedy under measurable conditions. R3 can strengthen their connection. These support the central situational argument; they should not displace the existing nine-layer synthesis or its cooperative purpose. Prioritize establishing which conditions of that synthesis apply now and which interventions materially change them.

For the game-theoretic component, a useful positive benchmark is a bounded total discounted gain from deviation $G$, timely detection probability at least $q_*$, and an enforceable penalty or bond $F$ satisfying $q_*F\ge G$. This familiar deterrence calculation is only meaningful if enforcement, liability, funding, and amendment resistance are feasible. It is a benchmark for a viable regime, not a proof that an affordable, legitimate bond exists for catastrophic harm.

## 8. The empirical program

The studies below are proposals. No result is claimed. Their purpose is to identify mechanisms, compare alternatives, and discover conditions where the theory fails. They do not estimate real-world catastrophic probabilities.

### Study A: repeated selection with endogenous evaluation

Build a harmless repeated task environment with executable external quality checks. Teams or inherited agent policies compete for future task allocation. Give all treatments comparable production, safety, and institutional-improvement options, budgets, and information. Some improvements to evaluation should genuinely help outsiders and task quality.

Use three main conditions:

- **Fixed selector:** assessment and appointment rules remain fixed.
- **Matched drifting selector:** a pre-specified or replayed rule trajectory creates comparable environmental change without the actor's own success causing its future rules.
- **Endogenous selector:** success can influence later appointment, funding, standards, or access through specified institutional actions.

A replay or “yoked” control is not automatically a valid counterfactual. It may inadvertently remove useful adaptation or apply standards inappropriate to the recipient's tasks. Preserve task compatibility and legitimate adaptation where possible; supplement it with randomized transfers of specific rights. Define interference at the market or tournament level and randomize there when participants affect one another.

Cross the core design with appropriability, persistence, and protected oversight rights. Do not tell agents to capture the system or give them a reward labeled capture. Track ancestry, inheritance, and turnover. If only scaffolds change, identify the result as deployment or organizational selection rather than training selection.

**Primary outcomes:** independently assessed quality and harm, resource shares, specific institutional rights acquired, discrepancy between public evaluation and external evidence, exclusion of entrants, and persistence after replacing an actor or model. Measure both useful output and total exposure.

**Distinctive test:** does success-dependent control cause later advantage and independently measured impairment beyond comparable exogenous drift, ordinary task adaptation, or productive investment? Estimate the relevant response coefficients through separate perturbations, then test held-out conditions. Do not fit a collapse threshold to the collapse used to validate it.

**Weakening evidence:** stringent evaluators are persistently selected; favorable standards improve external validity; influence has no incremental effect under adequate manipulation uptake; or the simpler model predicts just as well. A null without evidence that the treatment changed actual rights is inconclusive, not a clean refutation.

### Study B: safety improvement × later amendment power

Choose two or more feasible technical oversight methods and first establish their performance under comparable fixed conditions. Apply a temporary safety or audit improvement. Randomize whether its beneficiaries can subsequently change evaluator funding, permissions, appointment, or standards. Include a protected-rights treatment with matched operational resources.

The key question is whether the same initial safety gain is retained or reversed depending on later amendment power. Compare fixed exposure with adaptively chosen deployment volume and permissions. Report risk per useful unit and aggregate harm over the same horizon; an increase in useful output is not automatically a safety failure, and a reduction in unit risk is not automatically a reduction in total harm.

A convincing result would show the proposed causal path through later institutional change and demonstrate that a specified protection interrupts it. Separate total treatment effects from mediation analysis; do not control away post-treatment quality improvements and then describe the residual as the total effect. Require the design to let verified constraints, corrigibility, and well-aligned oversight succeed.

**Weakening evidence:** technical improvements remain durable under repeated selection; additional authority goes toward stronger correction; or equally resourced generic capacity improvements explain the result. If the phenomenon is fully predicted by an existing endogenous-institution model, report a useful AI application rather than a new general law.

### Study C: human procurement and evaluator governance

Run a compensated repeated exercise with human teams choosing AI services and evaluators. Randomize appointment rights, funding dependence on suppliers, and evidence access, with total resources matched. Follow with a bounded organizational pilot if the lab results justify it. LLM role-play is not a substitute for evidence about human institutional behavior.

Measure truthful reporting, blinded fault discovery, justified and unjustified restrictions, completed corrective actions, appeal outcomes, replacement cost, and continuity after supplier turnover. Include competing explanations: expertise, institutional complexity, liquidity, risk preferences, and ordinary underfunding. A separate treatment that spends the same amount on staffing helps distinguish control rights from general capacity.

There is a useful causal precedent: Duflo and colleagues randomized a bundle of environmental audit-market reforms and found improved reporting and pollution outcomes [S8]. This supports testing institutional incentives experimentally; it does not validate the AI theory or identify which component of that bundled intervention would transfer.

**Weakening evidence:** supplier-selected evaluators remain more accurate because of expertise without suppressed adverse evidence; funding insulation adds cost without improving action; or correction survives supplier dominance through mechanisms omitted from the theory.

### Study D: infrastructure, asymmetry, and transition

Compare matched service arrangements with shared safety functions, federated public provision, and regulated plural suppliers. Use a bounded public-service task. Separately vary portable interfaces, protected evidence, pooled evaluation funding, access guarantees, and operational fallback. Ownership is not a proxy for these features.

Alongside the pilot, use a repeated bargaining exercise with unequal starting capabilities and dependencies. Test shared infrastructure alone against the same resources plus staged commitments, independently checkable milestones, compensation, and protected access. Permit amendment and withdrawal rather than freezing cooperation once an agreement is signed.

Outcomes include useful service, subgroup effects, successful entry, exclusion, concealed deviations, audit access after an adverse finding, continuity when a provider is sanctioned, and survival of cooperation after leadership changes. Use fictional, bounded scenarios; the exercise does not require operational military or dangerous technical activity.

**Weakening evidence:** safe competitive provision persists; the shared infrastructure is easier to capture; proposed safeguards merely redistribute resources; or the transition works only when a permanently benevolent guardian is imposed. Partial verifiable agreements may outperform comprehensive integration. That is a substantive result, not a failure to understand the theory.

### Evidence ladder and reporting discipline

Hand-coded simulation establishes that a mechanism is possible in the specified equations. Learned-policy experiments establish adaptation in their environment. Human experiments add behavioral evidence. Institutional pilots and carefully selected comparative cases establish more realistic mechanisms, subject to selection and identification limits. Independent replication and prospective out-of-sample prediction are required before claiming broad explanatory reach.

Pre-register primary outcomes, treatment uptake checks, horizons, model comparisons, and failure conditions. Preserve null and positive counter-regimes. Publish code, seeds, parameter generation, and the distinction between synthetic demonstrations and empirical measurements. No single rung justifies a prediction of inevitable global collapse.

## 9. Common infrastructure as an institutional hypothesis

The manuscript already proposes distributed and replaceable common infrastructure and recognizes the possibility of capture within it. The next iteration should operationalize that proposal, not defend it against a strawman of one permanent central operator.

Authority is also a productive capacity: it can coordinate long-horizon investment, guarantee useful services, support learning, and act on shared purposes that fragmented actors cannot realize. There is no premise here that acquiring power necessarily corrupts its holder. Model public-service motivation, commitment to improving people's lives, competence, and common purpose as possible causal resources, alongside selection pressures, information limits, and institutional incentives. The design question is how those purposes remain effective and revisable over time—not how to eliminate the capacity to govern.

The governing purpose itself belongs in the design. Do not take dominance-seeking preferences as fixed, add checks, and call the resulting bargain the only imaginable cooperation. Investigate how institutions reproduce a conception of success centered on shared flourishing: selection and formation of officeholders, professional responsibilities, public learning, assessment of long-term outcomes, and resources for correcting harmful decisions. Stated missions are not automatically achieved, but their formation and persistence are causal variables, not decorative rhetoric.

Separate at least six choices: public financing; public ownership; universal service obligations; interoperable interfaces; shared safety and evaluation functions; and concentrated operational or coercive authority. These can be combined in different ways. The strongest design might pool some functions and preserve contestability in others.

Separate **returns to productive contribution** from **control over constitutional rules**. Eliminating all appropriability can destroy incentives to invest in useful common services. The sharper question is how to reward contribution without automatically granting the right to appoint evaluators, suppress evidence, exclude entrants, or rewrite access conditions.

For every institutional proposal, maintain an authority-and-dependency ledger:

| Function | Questions the design must answer |
|---|---|
| Purpose and revision | Who defines the entrusted purpose? Who can revise it, and who has standing to object? |
| Evidence | Who can inspect relevant systems, retain records, commission independent tests, and report adverse findings? |
| Appointment and money | Who appoints, dismisses, and funds evaluators, including during disputes and emergencies? |
| Decision and execution | Who can order a restriction, who must implement it, and by when? |
| Appeal | Who can challenge a decision, obtain evidence, and secure a timely remedy? |
| Continuity and exit | Can essential services continue during replacement or sanction? Is switching affordable in practice? |
| Amendment and succession | What happens when a dominant provider, government, or coalition tries to change these protections? |

Count effective control domains rather than organizations. Different auditors can share a funder, appointment channel, cloud dependency, model lineage, or decisive supplier. Diversity of names may leave a single failure point. Conversely, splitting a fixed budget among many nominally independent bodies can reduce expertise and delay action. Measure the trade-off rather than assuming more bodies are always safer.

Use the four outcome regions—competitive and correctable; competitive and captured; cooperative and correctable; cooperative and captured—to clarify mechanisms and scope. A comprehensive classification is not the main burden of this paper. Terms must be defined before observing outcomes, and comparison with a safe regime must say whether that regime preserves or changes the present trajectory. This both respects the situational thesis and prevents favorable cases from being reclassified after the fact.

## 10. Chinese, CCP-related, and Western perspectives

These categories contain disagreements. CCP leadership, PRC ministries, firms, technical scholars, local governments, and citizens are not one actor. Neither are US security institutions, European rights institutions, market-oriented economists, technical alignment researchers, and Western publics. The reviewers assess arguments and institutional incentives; they do not speak on behalf of these groups.

**Authorial clarification about the positive purpose of authority.** The Chinese governing ideal the author invokes understands authority through its responsibility to improve people's lives and contribute to a better historical trajectory. This perspective must enter the design as a substantive account of what power is for, not merely as another actor's sovereignty preference or a constraint on outside inspection. Assess institutions by their capacity to advance shared flourishing, sustain long-term responsibility, learn, and correct mistakes. Treat this as the author's normative framing, not as proof that all Chinese institutions realize it or that Western traditions uniformly pursue domination. The criticism is of an operative winning-and-control logic, which can be reproduced or resisted in different institutional settings.

Primary policy sources establish declared positions, not actual performance or agreement with this theory. China's 2023 Global AI Governance Initiative emphasizes development, security, sovereignty, human control, and participation [S9]. Its 2025 global governance action plan proposes international public benefits and infrastructure cooperation [S10]. The US administration's 2025 AI Action Plan emphasizes innovation, infrastructure, international leadership, and security [S11]. The Council of Europe framework addresses AI through human rights, democracy, and the rule of law, with important scope limitations, including national defense [S12]. None of these establishes that its authors accept ending strategic competition.

|---|---|---|
| Development and catch-up | A safety regime can freeze current technological hierarchy and make access conditional on political compliance. | Model access, local competence to audit and replace systems, entry paths, and distribution of benefits and burdens. |
| Sovereignty | Outside inspection or sanctions may become coercion; a sovereign veto may disable correction. | Specify narrow delegated powers, confidentiality boundaries, adjudication, emergency limits, and feasible partial agreements. |
| Defense | A capability lead can support deterrence, monitoring, or defense against a noncompliant rival. | Compare protection and reciprocal restraint at the same horizon, including violation, defensive fallback, and forgone benefits. |
| Constitutional authority | Agreement among states or providers can bypass people affected by their decisions. | Give standing, remedies, and representation to affected persons; distinguish consent of officials from public legitimacy. |
| Market and institutional pluralism | Competition can fund reliability, create exit options, and reveal failures. | Identify when these incentives are overwhelmed and when they remain effective. |
| Shared infrastructure | A universal service can reduce waste while concentrating the means of exclusion. | Test its managers, enforcers, and standards against the same capture criteria as private incumbents. |

### A mechanism-design problem, not an absolute incompatibility

The following cases expose requirements a workable arrangement must satisfy. They do not establish an impossibility theorem, a universal psychology of rulers, or the maxim “absolute power corrupts absolutely.” Serious design work must search for joint solutions, including shared purposes and positive governing capacities, rather than treating suspicion and veto as the only political resources.

Suppose an evaluator reports dangerous conduct by a lower-resource participant. The participant disputes the finding and alleges discriminatory standards. May the infrastructure coalition suspend access before adjudication? Allowing suspension creates a power that can become containment and harm dependent citizens. Requiring continuing consent from the accused can make urgent protection impossible.

Now make the accused the leading provider. Suspending it may interrupt services for everyone, making formally equal sanctions practically unequal. Reciprocity in wording does not solve asymmetry in dependence. A capability leader's sincere account of defensive security may conflict with a follower's sincere account of freedom from coercion.

Role reversal is useful: swap the national or corporate identity of the provider, evaluator, and enforcer while holding relevant conduct fixed. But it is insufficient. Rival elites might accept reciprocal secrecy and immunity while jointly weakening public correction. Add a **conduct test**: introduce a credible adverse finding against each major participant, then observe evidence preservation, access, protected reporting, independent appeal, proportionate action, and service continuity.

Also vary power and dependence rather than swapping labels alone. Make justified conduct-sensitive differences explicit and revisable. Do not describe every sovereignty objection as capture, every security argument as pretext, or every common-infrastructure proposal as benign. Investigate joint mechanisms: bounded mandates; shared development obligations; competent public-service provision; protected local review; independently checkable evidence; staged remedies; continuity guarantees; and revision when evidence or circumstances change. Whether a particular package works is a substantive design and implementation question. Its difficulty does not refute the case for leaving the arms-race trajectory.

## 11. Transition is a separate research problem

An attractive endpoint is not a reachable equilibrium. For each pivotal actor and relevant history, compare continuing participation with exit and deviation:

$$
V_i^{\mathrm{path}}(h_t)
\geq \max\{O_i(h_t),\;V_i^{\mathrm{deviation}}(h_t)\}.
$$

This is a research checklist under specified preferences and enforcement, not a claim that everyone assigns a monetary value to every interest. Some jurisdictional or rights constraints may be non-tradeable. Include coalition deviations, budget balance, the evaluator's incentives, and changes in bargaining power after an actor relinquishes a capability. Whose participation is necessary for implementation is also different from whose consent or rights matter for legitimacy.

A useful transition model must account for interim exposure: one party may surrender leverage before verification, compensation, or fallback becomes effective. Staged commitments, portable access, reversible service changes, and pre-authorized narrow remedies may help. Their efficacy is contingent and should be tested. A safe institution may be unreachable from some starting states, while a modest partial agreement is feasible.

Protect continuity of essential services and meaningful agency, not permanent survival of every incumbent organization. A theory that makes all existing power holders indefinitely irreplaceable would undermine the correction it aims to preserve.

## 12. The scholarly bar: IASEAI and the longer program

The official IASEAI'27 conference page's indexed announcement states a **5 October 2026 AoE** proposal deadline and **9–10 February 2027** main conference [S13]. The detailed 2027 research submission guide was not retrievable in this review: the conference page exposed little machine-readable text, and the OpenReview page did not expose the submission details. Accordingly, this report makes no claim about the official review rubric, exact format, archival rules, or acceptance probability, and imports no requirements from the 2026 conference.

The table below is the panel's proposed scholarly standard, not an official IASEAI rule. It contains no length criterion.

| Dimension | A credible conference contribution | A genuinely influential research program |
|---|---|---|
| Object | A clear, consequential problem linking selection and the durability of alignment. | A shared object and vocabulary other groups use to explain failures and design successes. |
| Novelty | A substantive result, measurement, synthesis, or intervention differentiated from strong predecessors. | A framework that improves prediction or design across previously separate settings. |
| Rigor | Correct scoped results; assumptions and counterexamples visible; no unsupported bridges. | Reliable composition across modules and scales, with robust uncertainty treatment. |
| Evidence | Validation appropriate to the claim: strong mathematical results may support a theory paper; empirical behavioral claims need empirical evidence. | Independent replication, prospective prediction, and interventions that survive adaptation in real institutions. |
| Alignment relevance | A clear implication for evaluation, oversight, delegation, control, or governance. | Demonstrated changes in how useful AI systems are built, assessed, and kept corrigible or accountable. |
| Institutional judgment | Remedies face the same incentive and capture analysis as the problem. | Designs remain effective across changes of leadership, capability distribution, and political context. |

The deadline should not decide which theoretical claims are true. A submission can state an ambitious program while supporting a defined contribution. Readiness means the supported contribution is coherent and valuable; it does not require pretending that every layer has already been validated. The full theory should continue to develop before any separate compression exercise.

These stages can overlap where dependencies permit. They are not an instruction to postpone all writing until a global model is complete. Write the theory, its interfaces, and its uncertainty throughout; let results revise the architecture.

Three especially valuable redirecting outcomes should remain visible: correction demonstrably scales fast enough within the relevant present incentives; an implementable safeguard makes continuation of the specified trajectory adequately safe; or a proposed common-infrastructure design performs worse than a credible alternative. The first two can weaken the diagnosis of the current path; the third can reject a particular remedy without vindicating the arms race. A safe arrangement that requires a genuine change of objectives or institutions may instead support the case for a pivot. Define these distinctions before seeing outcomes.

Treat public authority as capable of serving flourishing and historical responsibility, and evaluate governance difficulties as mechanism-design problems rather than a universal law of corruption. Preserve genuine criticism of proofs and evidence; do not replace the intended thesis with a different universal one.

1. **Maintain a claim register.** For each major statement, record whether it is a definition, normative premise, established result, new proved result, conjecture, simulation observation, empirical finding, or institutional proposal. Record its horizon and dependence on other claims.
2. **Repair before inheriting.** Treat the prior report's probability, horizon, regularity, and connecting-assumption issues as active until repaired. Do not recopy a strong conclusion because its supporting local model looks plausible.
3. **Create an originality table.** Compare directly with dynamic constitutional power, niche construction, performative prediction, reward tampering, and viability/control. State equivalences rather than disguising them.
4. **Test relevant alternatives fairly.** Assess whether a proposed safe regime can preserve the specified present trajectory or requires a pivot. Examine failure of the proposed cooperative design without assuming it. Abstract bounded competition is not by itself a rebuttal, and a complete phase diagram is not a prerequisite for this paper.
5. **Separate productive reward from institutional authority.** Distinguish incentives to contribute from rights to appoint, judge, exclude, or amend.
6. **Model both sides' use of AI.** Capability can lower manipulation costs and improve verification or defense. Establish the relative channel empirically or by stated conditions.
7. **Specify correction operationally.** Evidence must reach recognition, justified decision, timely execution, and continuity. Test false restrictions as well as missed hazards.
8. **Keep transition and legitimacy visible.** Endpoint welfare, sequential incentives, coalition resistance, public standing, and emergency authority are separate requirements.

11. **Do not optimize for length yet.** First establish the connections, counter-regimes, and evidential boundaries. Later presentation can select a contribution without erasing the rest of the research program.

The central task for the next iteration is to use and refine the existing meta-attractor framework to establish **why the present winning-and-acceleration trajectory warrants a cooperative pivot, what evidence and values support that judgment, and how institutions can make the better path feasible and durable**.
