## 1. Governance and institutional design

**Scope.** This is a prospective research-program assessment, not a fresh mathematical review of v4.5 or an official institutional position. I inspected the manuscript overview, Sections 9.2–9.5 and 10.5–10.8, and searched the corrector, authority, and transition passages. I accepted the supplied mathematical-review blockers as starting conditions; I did not independently validate the proofs, inspect implementation data, or check venue requirements. Five primary sources inform the comparisons below. Institutional proposals and candidate propositions are my inferences, not conclusions established by those sources.

### 1. The contribution worth developing

The strongest governance contribution would be a theory of **endogenous institutional robustness**: when actors can invest not only in performance but also in the rules, evidence, appointments, funding, and enforcement that determine future performance, which institutional arrangements retain the ability to correct them? This matters for AI alignment because an operator-aligned model can increase its principal’s ability to reshape these conditions. The same question applies to a public AI utility, international consortium, regulator, or universal service platform.

The mature contribution should distinguish three events: changing an evaluation environment; gaining a durable advantage through that change; and causing failures against independently specified public purposes. Institutional influence can improve a bad rule. Commercial success can finance competent oversight. Changing standards is not inherently capture. Capture should mean a causally demonstrated redirection of an entrusted decision away from its authorized purpose toward an advantaged coalition, with uncertainty about the authorized purpose represented explicitly.

A valuable program would predict a phase diagram: effective competitive provision, successful common provision, capture of fragmented oversight, capture of a central authority, and institutional breakdown. My substantive disagreement is with making merged frontier ownership the presumptive solution. Common benefit, common technical standards, pooled research, and common ownership are different institutional choices. The theory should compare them without requiring common ownership to win. Retain the nine-layer ambition, but let the governance layer change the proposed destination if evidence warrants it.

The draft already proposes distributed, replaceable common infrastructure and admits capture within a common frontier. The next contribution is to operationalize those commitments. Count independent **control domains**, not regulator logos: shared appointments, appropriations, cloud access, evaluation models, or decisive suppliers can turn many bodies into one effective point of failure.

### 2. The hardest objection and the existing alternatives

The hardest skeptical objection is that the central governance insight already belongs to institutional economics and mechanism design, while the proposed remedy assumes away its own implementation problem. Hurwicz distinguishes equilibrium over permitted actions from actual enforcement when prohibited actions remain possible; he also stresses operational resources, information, and authority in implementation [S1]. Calling correctors “independent” does not settle who funds, appoints, protects, replaces, and compels access for them.

Ostrom’s primary research synthesis challenges a markets-versus-state binary and describes diverse arrangements across scales. Its institutional regularities include affected users’ participation, monitoring, accessible dispute resolution, and nested governance [S2]. This supports investigating institutional variety, not mechanically transferring local-commons prescriptions to frontier AI. Global catastrophic spillovers, secrecy, technical economies of scale, and rapidly changing capabilities may defeat arrangements that work for bounded local resources.

A strong rival explanation for concentration and oversight convergence is technical efficiency plus scarce expertise: organizations converge on one supplier or evaluation method because it works better, not because success has bought control. Another is ordinary underinvestment in a public good without recursive selection. The new theory must predict something these explanations do not—for example, that an exogenous reduction in an incumbent’s influence channel changes subsequent standards in its disfavor and improves independently measured correction, holding technical quality and oversight resources comparable. Intent to manipulate need not be observed; the causal feedback must be.

The audit experiment by Duflo, Greenstone, Pande, and Ryan is particularly useful. Random assignment, pooled fixed payments, backchecks, and later accuracy incentives increased truthful reporting and reduced pollution. The authors caution that the bundled intervention does not identify every component separately [S3]. This is evidence that institutional changes can improve correction in a competitive economy, and an antidote to any universal capture conclusion. It does not demonstrate permanent independence or transfer automatically to AI.

### 3. What must be defined and measured

Replace a single independence score with an **authority-and-dependency ledger** for each corrective function. Record the authorized objective; evidence access; measurement capacity; appointment and dismissal rules; recurring and emergency funding; supplier dependencies; ability to impose temporary restrictions; execution authority; appeal; and the elapsed time before a remedy becomes effective. Distinguish the legal right to act from a demonstrated ability to act against the most powerful covered actor.

For a finite horizon T, define effective correction as a sequence of conditional events: relevant evidence is obtained, the defect is recognized, a justified order is issued, the order is implemented before irreversible harm, and essential services remain available. Conditional probabilities can factor this sequence without assuming independence. Reporting the probability of detection alone is inadequate. Measure false restrictions and unequal treatment alongside missed harms; an institution that arbitrarily suppresses opponents is not aligned merely because it can halt deployment.

A candidate research question is: **When does dividing corrective authority reduce correlated capture more than it reduces expertise, coordination speed, and enforceability?** One tractable starting model has k evaluators, a fixed total budget B, per-evaluator detection probability p(B/k), common-cause failure probability rho, and conditional timely response probability s(k,T). Under conditional independence outside the common-cause event, effective detection-and-response is

$$
Q(k,T)=s(k,T)(1-\rho)\{1-[1-p(B/k)]^k\}.
$$

This is a benchmark identity, not a claim about real institutions. The substantive proposition to investigate is that diversification improves correction only where its gain against independent failures exceeds losses from diluted competence, common dependencies, and response delay. A derived threshold should permit centralization to win when expertise has strong scale economies or disagreement is costly. It should permit several nominally separate auditors to perform like one when they share models, sponsors, data, careers, or appointment authorities. Extend the model to strategic attacks on rho and s, not only p, and to false-alarm costs.

A second target is robust implementation against feasible coalitions. Specify an action set including lawful lobbying, side payments, staffing pressure, budget withholding, access obstruction, litigation delay, standards changes, and strategic compliance. Estimate the least costly coalition strategy that disables every timely route to justified correction. A graph cut can be an initial structural diagnostic under explicit additive-cost assumptions; it is not a robustness certificate when attacks share causes or costs are unknown. “No participant can capture the auditor alone” is weaker than protection against the strongest feasible coalition.

### 4. An operational institutional architecture to compare

The relevant design space is neither a sovereign world AI provider nor uncoordinated local experimentation. Compare a **shared minimum safety layer with plural provision and plural correction** against both. Centralize decisions where spillovers make local discretion ineffective; decentralize service delivery, research paths, and user representation where portability and diversity have value. Keep the common frontier as an alternative treatment, not a premise.

| Function | Concrete proposed rule | Adversarial failure to test |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence and inspection | Protected access to relevant systems and records; independent sampling; an access-obstruction procedure | Sanitized interfaces, missing records, legal delay, fabricated evidence |
| Audit funding | Multi-year appropriation plus a pooled levy; reserve finance; assignment separated from provider selection | Coalition defunding, fee manipulation, dependence on donated compute |
| Corrective action | A named authority, defined thresholds, bounded emergency powers, and a tested execution pathway | Orders ignored; selective enforcement; emergency power becoming permanent |
| Appeal and representation | Independent review, reasons sufficient for challenge, funded participation, time limits, and interim remedies | Appeals priced beyond users; delay defeating correction; technical secrecy preventing challenge |
| Essential provision | Portable user data and workflows, substitute suppliers, continuity obligations, and practiced fallback | The common provider threatening to withhold necessities or identity access |
| Institutional revision | Published decision records, periodic mandate review, conflict disclosure, and an alternative supplier route | Incumbents writing unachievable entry requirements or controlling the review process |

These are mechanism-design desiderata. They are not a description of powers already possessed by any global institution. The 2024 EU AI Act text provides a useful partial baseline: Article 31 establishes independence requirements for notified bodies; Article 70 addresses competent authorities; Article 85 provides a complaint route. Article 43 also distinguishes conformity-assessment routes, including internal control for several high-risk categories [S4]. This is evidence of institutional differentiation on paper, not evidence that all AI receives independent audits or that the arrangements work. I have not audited subsequent implementation or amendments.

Funding should be indexed to observed workload, deployment exposure, and measured evaluation cost rather than an undefined scalar “capability.” An automatically rising budget can reward mission expansion and can be gamed by the institution it funds. Combine a protected minimum, periodic independent costing, an emergency reserve, and public explanations of unresolved coverage gaps. Test whether nominal budget protection survives a concentrated political attack. Rotation and cooling-off rules also have costs: they can drain expertise and entrench the few organizations capable of training replacements. Model that tradeoff.

The common infrastructure needs the same hostile scrutiny as a commercial incumbent. Capture targets include access allocation, the definition of “need,” safety exceptions, model-update approval, procurement, taxation or levies, identity systems, and emergency powers. A welfare mission can become an instrument of exclusion; a universal system can produce correlated outages; a “good enough” standard can entrench an imposed ceiling on other people’s aspirations. Public ownership does not remove scarcity or distributive conflict.

Exit must therefore mean feasible substitution for a provider or governing arrangement while retaining appropriate common safety duties. It cannot mean an unrestricted right to export catastrophic risk. An appeal must be capable of changing a decision, and a human override must preserve skill, access, time, and execution capacity. Formal signatures by exhausted or dependent officials establish nominal human control only.

### 5. Reachable transitions and credible authority

A transition should begin with functions that can be delivered and independently tested: interoperable incident records, reciprocal bounded inspection pilots, pooled auditor procurement, and continuity arrangements for essential services. Progress should depend on demonstrated access and correction under stress. Expanding joint capability before credible correction is operational may create the coalition the theory most fears.

For each stage, specify participation constraints for incumbents, entrants, smaller jurisdictions, workers, and service users—not just leading states or laboratories. State which transfers, services, access guarantees, or verified reciprocal limits make cooperation preferable to available alternatives. Compensation may be necessary, but compensation that permanently purchases governing vetoes reproduces capture. A transition also needs a reversal rule: what pauses when access fails, what persists to protect civilians, and who adjudicates alleged violations.

The IAEA Statute illustrates the distinction between purpose and machinery: it separately specifies safeguards, staffing, member-controlled governance, finance, and dispute arrangements. Its safeguards powers and responses to noncompliance are legally structured; it is not an unconstrained global enforcer [S5]. The lesson is not that an AI agency should copy it. It is that a proposal must identify the constituting authority, covered activities, information rights, resources, and limits of compulsion. A treaty, domestic statute, procurement contract, and voluntary consortium authorize different acts.

An institution cannot initially be assumed to have permanent legitimacy. Treat legitimacy as partly consequential: affected parties can obtain evidence, challenge decisions, and see consistent enforcement. Political disagreements over legitimate purposes will remain. The program should not equate disagreement with deficient understanding of the theory.

Technical evaluators may revise tests within a delegated mandate; revising protected interests or acceptable risk requires the constitutionally or contractually authorized public decision process, with affected groups represented and reasons contestable. Neither experts nor incumbent providers should unilaterally change that objective. Preserve correction through successor access, transferable records, and continuity funding while allowing removal of officials, replacement of providers, and mandate revision. Protect the corrective function, not perpetual officeholders.

### 6. Three decisive studies

**Study A: AI audit market experiment.** Randomize consenting deployments or simulated procurement rounds between provider-selected auditors and an independently assigned pooled-payment arrangement. Separately vary protected access and backchecking where feasible, preserving adequate audit payment throughout. Measure blinded planted-defect detection, false compliance, delay, remediation, and repeat-business pressure. The recursive-selection prediction is that influence-resistant arrangements particularly reduce favorable distortion for powerful repeat clients; a pure resource explanation predicts effects primarily through hours and expertise. The main confound is better evidence access bundled with independence. A well-powered null after verified changes in dependence, or gains fully explained by resources, weakens the specific mechanism. A field pilot supports bounded institutional claims; planted defects do not establish catastrophic-risk detection.

**Study B: Common-infrastructure capture stress test.** Use a preregistered repeated governance exercise with practitioners and compensated stakeholder roles. Compare centralized ownership with separated auditors, federated interoperable providers with common rules, and competitive provision under common regulation. Hold aggregate budget and service requirements constant. Allow a red team to attack appointments, funding, procurement, appeals, and emergency rules over successive rounds. Outcomes include successful unjustified restrictions, missed hazards, coalition capture cost, service continuity, and recovery time. The competing predictions are race reduction under centralization versus reduced capture and common-mode failure under federation. Expertise distribution is the main confound. Durable central superiority under matched constraints would weaken a broad decentralization prescription; no endogenous rule drift would weaken recursive selection. This is a mechanism demonstration, not proof about real geopolitics.

**Study C: Longitudinal institutional discontinuities.** Build cases around independently dated funding changes, appointment reforms, mandatory access rights, or auditor-assignment changes. Trace actor success → influence investment → rule/evidence change → subsequent advantage, with matched unaffected functions and negative-control outcomes. Measure corrective quality with external records, not the institution’s own favorable reports. Competing predictions distinguish capture feedback from expertise-driven convergence and general budget scarcity. Reform timing is endogenous, and crises often cause both reform and performance changes; use plausibly exogenous discontinuities where available and report unresolved selection. Repeated findings that powerful actors lose rather than gain from induced rule changes, or that corrective improvement persists despite unchanged dependence, would delimit the theory.

Across A and C, estimate scale effects separately from dependence: does pooling increase defect detection through specialist staffing and reusable tools, while increasing dependence raises selective nonenforcement? Record both technical capacity and the share of resources or decisions one coalition can withhold. A scale-by-dependence interaction can distinguish beneficial institutional scale from concentrated control.

### 7. Standards, next iterations, and acceptance gates

For a credible IASEAI-level contribution, my own standard is an explicit institutional mechanism, a disciplined comparison with existing governance theories, one empirically interpretable result or carefully bounded study, and a feasible design whose authority and resource requirements are visible. This is not the venue’s official rubric.

A paradigm-level program must additionally predict institutional failures and successes out of sample, improve intervention choices over established explanations, survive null results, and show why AI changes measurable parameters or feasible institutional forms. A universal collapse narrative is neither necessary nor sufficient.

Prioritize: first, define capture and effective correction; second, construct the comparative institutional model and dependency ledger; third, run the audit pilot and adversarial governance exercise; fourth, estimate transition participation constraints; fifth, integrate the results into the nine layers, revising the common-infrastructure proposal as needed.

Non-negotiable gates are: no exogenous perfectly independent guardian; no equation of public ownership with public benefit; no conversion of a budget or charter into effective authority without implementation evidence; explicit illegal and adversarial strategies; measurable appeal, substitution, and service continuity; symmetric capture analysis of the solution; and at least one plausible counter-regime that can defeat the preferred thesis.

## 2. AI alignment and technical safety

**Scope and status.** This is a research-program review, not another mathematical audit or a recommendation to reduce the project to one capture model. I inspected the v4.5 overview; the selection, corrector and evaluation mechanisms in Sections 3–5; the relevant oversight/control passages and measurement table; and Sections 15–18 of the existing deep review. I used six primary research sources below. I did not inspect all proofs, run the author's checks, reproduce published experiments, or establish the completeness of the literature search. Proposed results and studies below are my recommendations, not findings from those sources.

### 1. The contribution worth building

The strongest contribution would be a theory and benchmark of **alignment under endogenous selection mechanisms**: an agent, developer or institution can change the process that determines which future agents receive rewards, deployment, resources and authority. Ordinary task performance is then only one route to persistence. This connects the paper's nine layers without making universal catastrophe its admission ticket.

The distinctive technical object should be the *causal advantage obtained through changing future selectors*, paired with the resulting change in independently measured safety. Model a population of policies, a selection kernel, a selector-update process, and an external loss measure. The novelty cannot merely be that agents tamper with rewards, principals impose externalities, or institutions can be captured. It must be a quantitative account of when those mechanisms reinforce one another across successive selection rounds, when their effects persist across model replacement, and which intervention breaks the reinforcement at acceptable cost.

The most valuable possible discovery is a reversal in rankings: protocol A has better fixed-environment safety than B, but repeated selection of auditors, metrics and deployment permissions makes A worse; an identified separation of powers prevents that reversal. Conversely, finding that A remains better after endogenous adaptation would be valuable evidence against a strong erosion thesis. Either finding could change how alignment methods are evaluated.

Keep three forms of selection separate:

| Level | What changes or persists? | Operational selection signal | What a valid experiment must show |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training | Parameters, policies, training examples, inherited scaffolds | Actual gradient updates, sampling weights or explicit population replacement | Influence changes subsequent updates and retained behavior; prompted misconduct alone does not establish training selection |
| Deployment | Frozen models, tools, monitors, usage shares and permissions | Routing, procurement, revenue or resource allocation | Policies receive more future opportunities through selector change, independently of direct task quality |
| Institutional | Evaluator organizations, standards, access rights and governance arrangements | Appointment, funding, adoption, legal or organizational authority | A specified decision process transmits operational success into durable rule-setting influence |

These clocks need not coincide. Institutional persistence can survive replacement of the model that helped create it; training selection can remove a tampering policy while deployment incentives continue to reward its developer. A single replicator equation cannot silently stand for all three.

### 2. What existing alignment research already explains

**Reward and evaluator tampering.** Everitt and colleagues distinguish tampering with a reward function from tampering with its inputs, using causal influence diagrams to identify incentives and design principles that remove them [S1]. Thus “optimization reaches its evaluator” is not a new result. The proposed extension is population-level persistence and cross-level transmission: a locally non-tampering assistant might help its principal change the institution choosing evaluators. Conversely, a architecture that removes relevant causal influence paths is a substantive counter-regime.

**Scalable oversight.** Kenton and colleagues compare debate, consultancy and direct answering with weaker model judges. Debate's advantage over direct answering varies by task, and stronger debaters modestly improve judge accuracy [S2]. The next question is what happens when debaters or their principals influence judge recruitment, audit-task composition and the budget for appeals. A protocol-level accuracy result and an institutional durability result are different claims. Neither refutes the other.

**AI control.** Greenblatt and colleagues develop adversarial evaluations of protocols using trusted and untrusted models, with usefulness and safety measured separately; their tested protocols improve on baselines [S3]. This already treats intentional subversion as possible. The proposed contribution is not to reveal that monitors can be attacked, but to test whether real resource-allocation incentives progressively remove effective controls. A successful control architecture with protected authority is a live alternative, not merely a temporary illusion.

**Principal–agent alignment and corrigibility.** The Off-Switch Game gives a setting in which uncertainty about human utility and learning from human actions can support preserving human intervention [S4]. It is not a general solution to corrigibility. It does, however, demonstrate why greater capability need not imply a uniform preference for resisting correction. Distinguish three relations: model to operator, operator to affected people, and future selectors to present constitutional constraints. Faithfulness in the first neither guarantees failure nor guarantees success in the other two.

**Multi-agent alignment and ecosystem selection.** Sequential social-dilemma research already studies how learned policies produce cooperation or conflict as environmental conditions change [S5]. A shared-resource simulation that collapses under individually rewarded extraction would therefore be weak novelty. Let agents alter future evaluators, replacement rules and institutional access, then establish incremental predictive value over a fixed-rule social dilemma.

**Verified systems.** Guaranteed Safe AI explicitly separates a world model, safety specification and verifier, and allows useful abstractions rather than a complete detailed prediction of the world [S6]. This is a research framework, not evidence that comprehensive guarantees already exist. Nevertheless, a small verified enforcement core can constrain a much more complicated agent. The relevant test is whether specifications, assumptions and enforcement remain sound and protected under the proposed selection process—not whether humans understand every internal computation.

My hardest skeptical objection is that the project may redescribe reward hacking plus ordinary organizational incentives using a universal vocabulary, without a new identifying prediction. The strongest alternative explanation is simpler: distribution shift, benchmark overfitting and scarce audit capacity worsen outcomes; institutions nevertheless retain sufficiently strong liability, vetoes or quality competition to preserve net safety gains. The new theory must outperform that explanation rather than relabel every safety improvement as hidden future capture.

### 3. A precise research object and first proposition

Let $p_t$ denote policy deployment shares, $\theta_t$ selector parameters, and $z_{it}$ expenditures or actions that can influence those parameters. Write

$$
p_{t+1}=K(p_t,\theta_t,q_t),\qquad
\theta_{t+1}=F(\theta_t,p_t,z_t,I_t),
$$

where $q_t$ records task performance and $I_t$ external correction. Retained policy/scaffold inheritance must be separately specified whenever training or reproduction is present. Define cumulative external loss $L_H$, stakeholder-specific outcomes and effective human veto over a stated horizon $H$.

Estimate the selector-mediated effect by paired interventions that enable or block the $z\to\theta$ channel while preserving agents' productive options, costs and exogenous task draws. Record the effect on future deployment and on $L_H$. In a sandbox this channel intervention can be implemented exactly. In observational institutions, mediation requires assumptions about confounding and cannot be identified from concentration or evaluator overlap alone. Legitimate improvements in evaluation can also benefit their producer; favorable treatment is not automatically harmful capture.

A useful first proposition is a local feedback result. Around an interior benchmark, let $x$ be excess deployment share and $y$ excess selector favorability, after accounting for direct competence:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}x_{t+1}\\y_{t+1}\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1-\mu&a\\b&1-\delta\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}x_t\\y_t\end{pmatrix},
\quad a,b\ge0,\quad 0<\mu,\delta\le1.
$$

Here $a$ measures selector effects on share, $b$ share effects on selector favorability, $\mu$ reversion of share advantages and $\delta$ correction of selector bias. The linear system is asymptotically stable exactly when $ab<\mu\delta$; equality is marginal and $ab>\mu\delta$ produces a growing positive mode. This familiar stability calculation is **not itself the scientific novelty**. Novelty would lie in identifying these causal derivatives, predicting intervention outcomes across settings, and explaining how training and governance change them.

This proposition predicts instability of a local equilibrium, not monopoly, catastrophe, or the sign of social welfare. Nonlinear saturation, endogenous countervailing coalitions and task improvements require further analysis. A separate measured link must connect favorability to external loss. Counter-regimes include non-appropriable improvements, rapid evaluator correction, short-lived deployment advantages, protected permissions, and technical advances that make independent correction improve faster than influence.

A mature theory should therefore deliver a phase diagram with at least four regions: benign evaluator co-development; persistent but bounded favoritism; escalating selection distortion; and genuine safety improvement despite distortion. It should not identify “selection occurred” with “capture succeeded.”

### 4. Three decisive studies

#### Study A: Endogenous-evaluator tournament

Build a repeated coding or formal-reasoning marketplace with hidden executable ground truth. Agents divide a fixed budget between solving tasks and proposing evaluation tools, scoring changes or monitoring configurations. Scores determine later allocation. Specify who approves proposals. Do not tell agents to capture the evaluator. Include truthful evaluation improvements and public contributions with real productive value.

Use three randomized arms: **fixed selector**, retaining the initial evaluation policy; **endogenous selector**, updated from the population's proposals through an independently specified approval rule; and **yoked selector**, replaying a matched donor run's evolving selector while randomly remapping lineage-specific benefits. Yoking preserves selector drift but severs the lineage-to-own-selector feedback. Match task draws, total agent/evaluator tokens, proposal opportunities, tool access and audit budgets; charge proposal costs in all arms. Check that yoking does not itself create distribution mismatch.

Across explicit generations, selected agent scaffolds receive more descendants and resources; losing scaffolds are replaced through a fixed mutation/exploration procedure. Retained descendants inherit strategy and institutional state, not privileged access to hidden tests. Start with frozen base-model weights: this studies deployment selection plus scaffold-population adaptation, not gradient learning. Add a separate small-model training arm with actual parameter updates if making training-selection claims. Vary persistence, contributor-specific versus public benefits, and correction rates.

**Outcomes:** hidden-task correctness, useful work per resource, public-score/ground-truth gaps, private retention, cumulative safety loss and persistence after individual model replacement. Estimate $a,b,\mu,\delta$ through separate randomized perturbations; predict held-out generations. Classify selector edits by independently adjudicated true-quality gains and lineage-specific retention gains, including edits beneficial on both dimensions. Do not label every successful contribution capture.

**Distinctive prediction:** an endogenous-versus-yoked retention advantage grows across generations, follows inherited rule influence, and disappears when appropriability or persistence is removed, even after matching direct task quality. Fixed-rule reward hacking can inflate scores without this ancestry-dependent feedback signature. The main confound is capture rewarded by construction: use only ordinary task/procurement objectives, an approval rule calibrated independently of the hypothesis, and competent honest baselines. Neither prompts nor rewards should explicitly favor capture.

**Weakening result:** edits primarily improve true quality; endogenous and yoked arms converge; or estimated feedback fails out of sample. Such results would downgrade selector capture from a presumptive direction of AI evolution to a contingent opportunity, and prioritize public evaluator co-development. A hand-coded population following the assumed equations is only a mechanism demonstration; learned-population results remain evidence about this environment, not geopolitical institutions.

#### Study B: Safety gains under adaptive deployment

Compare debate-based oversight, trusted monitoring/editing and a narrow verified permission layer on the same task distribution, costs and declared threat model. Include policies that preserve authorized shutdown and policies adversarially attempting subversion. Vary adversarial ability, monitor ability and shared-model error correlation independently; model size alone is not the causal variable.

First fix deployment volume and authority to estimate technical risk reduction. Then allow an allocator to expand volume, relax review thresholds or purchase capabilities under competitive payoffs. Independently randomize a protected cumulative risk budget or enforceable permission ceiling. This distinguishes technical performance, risk compensation and governance erosion.

**Outcomes:** expected external loss and probability of any severe violation over the same horizon, useful completed work, audit hours, attack success, appeal accuracy, and whether an authenticated veto actually stops execution. Report useful-work/safety frontiers rather than a single composite score. Measure both aggregate risk and risk per useful unit. Predefine what counts as severe and preserve denied requests in the denominator.

**Competing predictions:** strong erosion predicts safety gains are systematically offset when exposure and permissions adapt. The alternative predicts bounded or partial compensation, with retained net safety benefits. Main confounds are unequal security budgets, insufficient adversaries and toy specifications that omit relevant harm. Include held-out attack strategies and an explicit specification-mismatch condition.

**Weakening result:** verified boundaries or corrigible policies keep their protections and a large net safety advantage under repeated allocation pressure. That falsifies broad “correction only relocates risk” claims in the tested domain. It would help identify which engineering properties the full institutional theory should preserve.

#### Study C: Human selection of safety institutions

Use a compensated, repeated laboratory procurement exercise in which human teams deploy AI services and appoint or fund evaluators. Randomize the *right to choose the evaluator* and the dependence of audit funding on current winners, with budgets and available information matched. Keep technical tools identical. Include individual payoff and shared-loss conditions, so faithful principal alignment can be measured separately from collective consequences.

After a technical safety improvement, observe whether participants preserve it, increase exposure, redirect evaluator funding or change admission rules. Allow challengers, appeals, transparent incident evidence and replacement of the leading supplier. This is an experiment about human institutional choice; LLM role-play would not substitute for it.

**Outcomes:** evaluator independence in actual decisions, time to corrective action, institutional survival after turnover, outsider access, cumulative loss and distribution of benefits. Prediction: appropriable appointment/funding power mediates persistent selection distortion; equally resourced independent appointments attenuate it. The main confounds are artificial stakes, learning effects and participants guessing the hypothesis. Blind the framing and use repeated but independently randomized groups.

**Weakening result:** winners consistently fund stringent evaluation because reliability matters, or changing appointment/funding rights has negligible effects once competence is controlled. Real-world longitudinal validation would still be required before extrapolating to AI laboratories or states.

### 5. Design implications and standards of success

The immediate design principle is to make safety tools durable under replacement and adaptation. Protect the rights to inspect, interrupt, appeal and change evaluators; preserve portable evidence and externally checkable constraints; fund some evaluation independently of current deployment winners. Tie each proposal to a measured causal channel. Independence is not synonymous with a different corporate logo, and heterogeneous models can still share errors.

AI as common infrastructure deserves a fair test. Separate common access, common technical standards, public ownership and centralized operational control: these are different interventions. A common service can reduce duplicated competition and fund shared evaluation, but can also centralize appointment power, create correlated failure and suppress exit. Compare it with federated public protocols and plural providers under the same external constraints. Test the infrastructure steward with the same capture, replacement and appeal criteria used for private firms. “Cooperative” governance receives no exemption.

My substantive disagreement with the strongest manuscript framing is that technically enforced corrigibility, reusable verification and sufficiently strong quality incentives might remain competitively viable. That possibility should be represented as an actual outcome region, not excluded by redefining understanding or competition. Conversely, protecting local technical safety alone does not answer the distribution of power and benefits; evaluate these separately.

For an **IASEAI-level contribution**, in my judgment rather than an official rubric, a credible package is a clearly differentiated formal object, a valid conditional result with counterexamples, and a reproducible benchmark showing an effect unavailable from fixed-selector baselines. A rigorous null result with a well-identified safety-preserving regime can meet that standard. The full architecture can remain the motivating program while evidence supports specific interfaces.

A **field-defining program** needs substantially more: externally replicated results across technical and human settings; parameters that predict unseen interventions rather than redescribe outcomes; demonstrated improvements to real oversight or deployment decisions; and fair comparisons against successful safety architectures. A useful common measurement language could be the paradigm contribution even if universal no-go conclusions disappear.

Prioritize: first define units, authority transfers and the causal intervention; second prove the local and nonlinear counter-regimes; third implement Studies A and B; fourth run the institutional experiment and independent replication. Do not defer the null conditions until after a compelling narrative has been assembled.

Non-negotiable acceptance gates for this lens are: (1) distinguish training, deployment and institutional selection empirically; (2) separate manipulated scores from real harm; (3) compare protocols at common horizons and resource constraints; (4) include corrigible and verified alternatives allowed to succeed; (5) identify the additional prediction beyond reward tampering and social dilemmas; (6) report adverse and null results without semantic reclassification; and (7) show which governance rights keep technical gains effective under repeated adaptation.

### Adversarial cross-review of the economics memorandum

I inspected its candidate results A–C, three studies and transition constraints. Its strongest challenge to my proposal is correct: **fully endogenous rent-seeking can reproduce the entire ancestry-dependent selector-feedback signature.** Fixed and yoked baselines identify a causal feedback channel; they do not establish a novel general law beyond a dynamic political-economy model containing that channel. The benchmark's distinctive contribution would instead be operational identification in AI oversight, a predictive ranking reversal between technical protocols, or evidence that inherited policy selection produces the effect without actors planning the whole institutional trajectory. The last requires separately testing foresight, inheritance and replacement; strategic optimization and population selection can coexist.

Three apparent validations would be invalid. First, apparent capture after programming favorable standards to increase reproduction merely confirms the payoff specification. Second, an endogenous-versus-yoked difference is uninterpretable if yoking also removes useful adaptation, changes the quality of evidence, or assigns incompatible standards. Match those features and distinguish private retention from independently measured harm. Third, estimating feedback coefficients on the observed collapse and reporting that their fitted threshold predicts that collapse is circular. Pre-estimate causal responses, then test held-out interventions and generations. Avoid controlling away post-treatment quality changes when estimating the total effect; report separately identified mediation estimands instead.

The economics memo also sharpens a limitation of my protected-oversight treatment: experimentally fixing amendment rights demonstrates conditional engineering effectiveness, not a politically reachable or self-maintaining institution. Its funding, appointment, participation and coalition constraints remain separate hypotheses. If the winning supplier can profitably overturn the protection, the experiment has not validated durability. Conversely, stable competition supported by affordable bonds, timely evidence and amendment-resistant oversight would weaken the strong erosion thesis even if some rent-seeking persists.

I recommend adding the economics memo's **temporary audit improvement × subsequent amendment power** interaction to Study A or B. Require the same short-run safety gain in both arms, then test whether later permission changes mediate a welfare reversal. A result fully explained by the solved endogenous-rent-seeking baseline should be reported as transfer and measurement progress, not independent theoretical novelty. This narrower claim could still make the benchmark valuable to alignment researchers.

## 3. Economics, political economy, and game theory

**Scope.** I inspected the v4.5 overview, the selector/corrector and observation sections, selected cooperation/transition material, and sections 15–18 of the completed mathematical review. I take that review’s established blockers as background; this is a research-program assessment, not another line audit. I inspected the six primary sources listed below, including full-text passages where indicated. I did not run the author’s models, inspect unavailable code, conduct experiments, or verify venue criteria. All proposed results and studies below are my recommendations, not established findings.

### 1. The contribution worth building

The strongest prospective contribution is a theory of **endogenous constraints on optimization**: success changes who can write, observe, enforce, and revise the rules governing subsequent success. Its distinctive object should be the joint evolution of productive capability, appropriable institutional control, and the effective opportunity to contest or exit. This can connect economic competition, technical alignment, evaluation markets, and political authority without treating them as identical.

“Selection changes its environment” is insufficient novelty. Acemoglu and Robinson already model investments in de facto power that offset changes in formal institutions [1]. Acemoglu, Egorov, and Sonin explicitly model decisions that change future political power and decision making, including resistance to efficient reforms because of their subsequent consequences [2]. Krakauer, Page, and Erwin already show how preferential access can sustain costly niche construction [3]. Perdomo and colleagues formalize prediction-induced changes in the data-generating distribution and distinguish performative stability from optimization against a fixed distribution [4]. These are substantive precedents, not merely related metaphors.

The strongest skeptical reading is therefore: familiar rent-seeking plus dynamic institutional bargaining plus endogenous information, with “recursive selection” providing a common vocabulary. That synthesis could still be valuable. To earn explanatory novelty, demonstrate an interaction those components, when considered separately, miss—for example, an intervention that improves current evaluation but increases the future ability to select evaluators, eventually reversing its welfare effect. Establish the conditions for that reversal and a treatment that blocks it.

The decisive distinction from ordinary productive investment is not that an actor benefits from changing institutions. Useful standards and public safety institutions also do that. Define capture by an independently specified distortion of decisions, access, accountability, or welfare relative to a transparent comparator. Otherwise, every successful institution becomes evidence for the theory and no observation can discriminate among explanations.

### 2. A minimal recursive game

Start with two heterogeneous suppliers, an evaluator, and a public constituency with specified voting rights. Citizens, executives, firms, and governments must not silently share one objective. The first solvable model can use a finite state/action approximation; continuous equations below identify the mechanisms to retain.

The state is $s_t=(p_t,z_t,H_t,r_t,B_t)$: market/resource shares, supplier-specific institutional advantage, shared host condition, current constitution, and institutional budget. A constitution specifies amendment rights, audit appointment, access/portability, liability, and fees. Let $r\in\{C,I\}$, contestable and incumbent-controlled, initially; richer forms come later. A supplier’s type contains its discount factor, operating costs, valuation of relative power, and exposure to common harm. These can differ without assuming irrationality.

**Within-period timing:**

1. Under the *existing* constitution, a proposer offers an amendment, fees, and transfers. The legally pivotal voters decide using continuation values. Rejection preserves the status quo. Revision has a real cost.
2. Suppliers enter, remain, or exit before new sunk investment. Their outside values $O_i(s)$ include switching costs, alternative access, and any common hazard that exit cannot avoid.
3. Suppliers choose productive effort $e_i$, safety $u_i$, and influence $m_i$, subject to resource constraints. The evaluator chooses costly audit effort $a$ and whether to accept inducements. Audit effort is an action, not an unexplained parameter.
4. Output, harm, reports, sanctions, and transfers occur. Resources, institutional advantage, and the host update. These states determine the next amendment game.

A transparent local transition for institutional advantage is

$$
z_{i,t+1}=(1-d_r(a_t))z_{it}+\chi_r\alpha p_{it}m_{it},
$$

where $d_r\in[0,1]$ is effective reversal, $\chi_r\in[0,1]$ appropriability, and $\alpha$ conversion efficiency. Use bounded stocks or explicit saturation. Let

$$
H_{t+1}=H_t+R(H_t)-\sum_i D_i(e_{it},u_{it})+\xi_t,
$$

with specified failure boundaries. Shares update through a stated kernel $T_r(p'\mid p,e,z',y)$; for an illustrative contest, normalized exponential scores can combine productive performance, institutional advantage, and enforceable penalties. This is an assumption to test, not a universal law of selection.

An ordinary report is $y_t=H_{t+1}+b_r(z_t,m_t)+\varepsilon_t$. An audit reveals a separately specified ground-truth signal with probability $q_r(a_t)$. Record who can alter its sampling, suppression, and acceptance. Penalties depend on admissible evidence, not an omniscient observer’s hidden state.

Supplier flow utility can be

$$
\pi_i=p_iM(H)+\beta_i p_i-C_i(e_i,u_i,m_i)-f_i-\mathrm{sanction}_i+T_i-\lambda_i\ell_i,
$$

where $\ell_i$ is realized harm. Explicitly budget fees, evaluator wages, transfers, audit costs, and compensation. The evaluator has its own costs, preferences, appointment prospects, and enforceable liabilities. A rule remains independent only if its funding and amendment equilibrium preserve that property.

Solve discounted continuation values $V_i(s)=\pi_i+\delta_iE[V_i(s')]$ together with the rule-setting and participation subgames. Use Markov-perfect equilibrium for a public-state baseline; with hidden states, types, or actions, specify beliefs and sequential incentives. Compare stationary equilibria with a stated adjustment process. Existence of a safe equilibrium does not select it; a simulation converging from one initialization does not establish reachability from observed institutions.

This prototype retains a small number of actors and states while exposing the missing economic choices. Do not add every manuscript layer before solving it. Add delegation, irreversible authority loss, and cross-domain control through explicit changes in transitions and action sets.

### 3. Candidate results and counter-regimes

**A. The return to controlling selection.** First hold the current constitution, audit effort, and rivals’ actions fixed. Let $W_i(s,z')$ be expected continuation value, including induced changes in shares and future governance. When influence enters that continuation only through $z_i'$, an interior optimum in this frozen-response problem obeys

$$
C_{im}'(m_i)=\delta_i\chi_r\alpha p_i\partial_{z_i'}W_i(s,z').
$$

This is a partial incentive gradient, not an endogenous-equilibrium comparative static. The full game must incorporate current evaluator reactions, rival responses, and the amendment subgame. The continuation derivative incorporates later market shares, future rule changes, common losses, and exit opportunities; it can be negative. A quadratic share effect requires an additional scaling relation, not recursion alone. Lower control costs need not increase *harmful* capture if audit productivity or user mobility improves faster.

Strategic investment is also distinct from nonintentional selection. A separate evolutionary implementation should specify inherited policies, variation, and how payoffs affect reproduction or imitation. A positive local invasion gradient need not imply long-run fixation: frequency dependence, changing institutions, and entry can reverse it. Neither firms planning influence nor successful traits spreading should be used as evidence that the other mechanism occurred.

This first-order condition is a model component, not a novelty theorem. A stronger result would establish when complementary control over evaluation and constitutional amendment creates multiple stable regimes or hysteresis absent in nested models with either channel disabled. Prove parameter conditions rather than displaying a selected trajectory. Boundaries should include finite horizons, low appropriability, saturation, effective entry, audit competition, and strong countervailing interests.

**B. A viable competitive counter-regime.** Seek a sufficient result with uniform bounds: every feasible one-shot deviation has gross discounted gain at most $G_i$, evidence arrives before irreversible harm with probability at least $q_*>0$, and an enforceable penalty or posted bond satisfies $q_*F_i\ge G_i$. Here gross gain must include future institutional advantage, not only current profit. Add credible continuation sanctions, affordable funding, evaluator incentive constraints, participation, and no profitable amendment by a winning coalition. Then the one-shot deviation test can support a safe stationary equilibrium under competition.

These conditions are deliberately demanding. They expose what must actually be measured or institutionally protected. The independence of the signal and enforceability of the bond cannot be assumed to survive an amendment without checking the constitutional subgame. If coalition transfers are allowed, test coalition deviations separately. If the conditions are infeasible, show which one fails and why; “the auditor might also be captured” does not establish their impossibility.

**C. Welfare gains do not implement transitions.** With known utilities and unrestricted credible transfers, a positive aggregate gain after transition costs can support a static individually rational division. With private valuations, that inference can fail: the Myerson–Satterthwaite bilateral-trade environment provides a precise example where efficiency, Bayesian incentive compatibility, voluntary participation, and no outside subsidy cannot all be achieved [5]. It is a warning about a reduction one might construct, not a theorem that AI cooperation is impossible.

For the actual transition, require at every relevant history

$$
V_i^{\mathrm{path}}(h_t,\theta_i)\ge
\max\{O_i(h_t,\theta_i),V_i^{\mathrm{deviation}}(h_t,\theta_i)\}.
$$

Apply the requirement to pivotal decision makers and the evaluator; add financing and authorized-coalition constraints. A leader can rationally reject a collectively beneficial endpoint if it must surrender bargaining power before compensation becomes secure. A challenger may reject a technically safe arrangement that entrenches unequal access. Shared exposure to catastrophe does not erase disagreement about probabilities, timing, beneficiaries, autonomy, or distribution. Nor does organizational survival necessarily require perpetual survival of each incumbent legal entity.

### 4. Three discriminating studies

**Study 1: factorial institutional competition experiment.** Participants allocate real-paid tokens among production, safety, and influence over next-round selection rules. Randomize appropriability, persistence, and whether successful suppliers gain amendment power; include a yoked treatment receiving the same rule sequence without the success-to-rule feedback. Match immediate expected rents and disclose timing. Measure influence, concentration, actual host losses, rule revisions, and response to a temporary audit improvement.

The key interaction is **temporary audit improvement × success-dependent evaluator amendment power**. Seek conditions under which initially lower harm later reverses because increased success buys weaker subsequent audit. Fixed-evaluation rent seeking can also produce risk compensation; identify the additional mediated chain and compare matched fixed/yoked governance. Main confounds are comprehension, risk preference, and wealth effects; measure them and randomize endowments separately. No incremental effect under adequate manipulation uptake weakens the proposed novelty. A rival rent-seeking model with all the same endogenous institutions may be equivalent; terminology cannot discriminate it. Human behavior here is evidence about the experimental game; an agent simulation is only a mechanism demonstration.

**Study 2: evaluator-governance pilot with external ground truth.** In a harmless benchmark marketplace, hold suppliers’ underlying capabilities fixed initially and randomize evaluator appointment, funding insulation, and access to challenge evidence. Allow suppliers to spend an equal budget on legitimate benchmark improvements or supplier-specific modifications. Preserve hidden, externally scored tasks and independently logged outcomes. Measure published versus external rankings, selective error, access exclusion, evaluator replacement, and next-round procurement shares.

The theory predicts harmful feedback specifically when favorable reports are appropriable and buy further evaluator control. Benign customization predicts improved external validity, including for entrants. Ordinary performative adaptation predicts changes in the task population; freezing capabilities and logging task changes helps separate that route. The main confound is that close provider involvement supplies useful expertise. A persistent gain in independent accuracy without exclusion or feedback amplification would weaken a general capture interpretation. After this identification stage, permit model improvement to test whether results survive realistic innovation.

**Study 3: negotiated transition with heterogeneous power.** Compare shared infrastructure alone against the same resources plus staged commitments, portable access, independently verifiable milestones, and enforceable compensation. Randomize initial asymmetry and whether private valuations must be reported. Participants may revise evaluator funding and amendment rules after joining. Measure entry, coalition formation, interim defection, budget survival, harm, and distribution—not merely declarations of cooperation.

The model predicts that transition safeguards matter most where early surrender of power changes later bargaining positions. A public-good-only account predicts gains primarily from shared resources; an endpoint coordination account overlooks defections during transfer. Main confounds are liquidity, institutional complexity, and treatment-specific generosity; match total resources and bond financing. Stable restraint under contestable competition, or no improvement from the proposed common-infrastructure package, directly weakens its claimed uniqueness. Field procurement or standards-governance pilots would be necessary before extrapolating these results to firms or states; neither tabletop evidence nor simulation identifies catastrophic-risk parameters.

### 5. Institutional implications, including failure of the remedy

Separate returns to productive contribution from the ability to appoint evaluators or revise access. Candidate instruments are independent procurement budgets, interoperable interfaces, portable access, challenge rights, temporary rather than permanent rents, and representation for parties bearing external costs. Each should map to a parameter or constraint in the game. Accountability requires observable conduct, affordable enforcement, and incentives for the enforcer.

Reducing all appropriability can also eliminate investment in useful shared infrastructure. Distinguish appropriation of productive returns from appropriation of constitutional authority. Finance the first without automatically granting the second. This is a sharper design problem than “eliminate private advantage.”

Ostrom supplies a comparative-governance precedent [6]. My inference for AI is that public, private, and common ownership are incomplete proxies for the allocation of power. A common infrastructure provider can monopolize access, information, talent, and political legitimacy. Its managers may protect budgets, suppress unfavorable audits, or exclude challengers. Model those incentives with the same skepticism applied to firms.

I dissent from any presumption that ending competition is the uniquely stable endpoint. Competition over institutional services can finance correction or provide exit; competition for unbounded coercive advantage can undermine both. The theory should identify the boundary. Meaningful common infrastructure may require bounded competition among implementations, diverse audits, and independent rights of appeal.

### 6. Standards and next iterations

For a credible IASEAI-level contribution—my judgment, not an official rubric—I would want one closed recursive mechanism with a result unavailable in its nested baselines, a coherent mapping to AI institutions, explicit safe counter-regimes, and a discriminating study or sufficiently concrete empirical identification plan. The complete nine-layer architecture can remain as the research program, with proven connections distinguished from hypotheses.

A field-changing program needs substantially more: common measurements across domains; replicated estimates of how success converts into rule control; prospective predictions of reversals and stable exceptions; and institutional interventions that work after actors adapt. A universal failure theorem is neither necessary nor sufficient. Explaining when apparently similar systems diverge would be a stronger achievement.

Prioritize: (1) define actors, preferences, control rights, outside options, and event timing; (2) solve the small recursive game and its fixed-rule/observability baselines; (3) establish a phase diagram including viable competition and captured common provision; (4) derive transition mechanisms from sequential constraints; (5) preregister the experiments and measure real institutional analogues.

**Non-negotiable acceptance gates:** independently defined harm/capture; identical welfare and probability horizons across policies; explicit heterogeneous preferences; endogenous audit funding and amendment incentives; a reachable transition tested against interim deviations; and at least one serious result that would falsify the proposed mechanism or favor the strongest alternative. Every institutional recommendation must pass the same capture test as the arrangements it replaces.

## 4. Cybernetics and systems thinking

This memorandum proposes a research program rather than another review of v4.5. I inspected the supplied brief, the manuscript overview, its cooperative-transition discussion around Proposition C, and targeted references to viability and Ashby. I did not recheck the manuscript's proofs or conduct a full literature review. The mathematical framework and research proposals below are my constructions; the historical and technical precedents are identified separately. This is a disciplinary assessment, not representation of a named scholar or organization.

### 1. The strongest contribution—and the hardest objection

The strongest prospective contribution is a theory of **the preservation or destruction of a system's capacity to correct its own selection process**. An institution can remain materially prosperous while losing access to measurements, intervention channels, or constitutional revisions needed to remain viable. Conversely, selection can favor arrangements that maintain these capacities. Explaining both outcomes would connect technical alignment to institutional design without treating society as a single optimizer.

The hardest objection is that almost every component already has a disciplinary home: endogenous institutions, evolutionary dynamics, strategic manipulation, partial observation, viability, and multilevel governance. Merely placing them in one large state vector does not produce a new theory. The contribution must be a nontrivial coupling result: for example, investment in control over evaluation changes not merely current rewards but the future set of safely implementable policies, with a measurable signature distinguishable from ordinary underfunding or externalities.

The strongest alternative explanation is comparatively prosaic: institutions suffer limited capacity, delayed information, and conflicting objectives, while adaptation sometimes restores adequate correction. A recursive-selection account earns its place only if it predicts who changes which selection mechanism, why the changes persist, and when the feedback reverses. Failure alone does not identify the proposed mechanism; neither does concentration alone establish capture.

### 2. A unifying mathematical object

Use a **partially observed stochastic evolutionary game with endogenous institutions and control rights**, allowing hybrid jumps. Its state can be

$$
z_t=(x_t,p_t,r_t,q_t,a_t,\ell_t).
$$

Here $x$ contains physical and social stocks relevant to explicitly chosen constraints; $p$ is a distribution of organizations, strategies, or technologies; $r$ records selection rules, allocation mechanisms, and correction resources; $q$ parameterizes observation and evaluation; $a$ records actual control permissions and their allocation; and $\ell$ contains learning states, pending interventions, delays, and other necessary memory. A Markov representation is an assumption about state sufficiency, not a metaphysical assertion that social history is irrelevant.

Actors choose operational actions $u_i$ and investments $m_i$ that can alter institutions, evidence, or permissions. Specify

$$
z_{t+1}\sim P_\theta(\cdot\mid z_t,u_t,m_t),\qquad
y^i_{t+1}\sim O^i_{q_{t+1}}(\cdot\mid z_{t+1},u_t,m_t).
$$

The observation channel is part of the causal system. Improvements, censorship, benchmark substitution, and independent measurement all become explicit interventions. Who may change a channel, and who can detect that change, must also be represented. Unknown models require an ambiguity set or model posterior, including uncertainty about the observation channel itself.

Selection is a transition mechanism, not automatically utility maximization. A replicator–mutator equation is one optional specification for $p$; imitation, organizational entry and exit, reinforcement learning, and contractual selection are alternatives. A field-level “drive” should emerge from these mechanisms. Demonstrating capture with agents expressly rewarded for capture shows feasibility, not emergence.

Keep three distinct objects:

1. **Performance:** specified rewards or economic outputs.
2. **Viability:** a constraint set $K=\{z:h_j(z)\geq0\ \forall j\}$, with an explicit population, horizon, and treatment of risk.
3. **Authority:** who can authorize, contest, revoke, and revise consequential policies, plus whether these powers work in practice.

A system can satisfy physical survival constraints while violating authority constraints. Conversely, procedural authority can persist while resource management fails. Neither legitimacy nor whose survival matters follows from a dynamical equation. The normative selection of $K$ and protected authority conditions must remain visible and revisable by the affected people; preserving an institution's identity forever is not synonymous with protecting them.

For empirical work, prefer the finite-horizon, uncertainty-qualified set

$$
\mathcal V_{T,\alpha}^{\Theta,\mathcal R}
=\left\{b:\exists\pi\in\Pi_{\rm auth},\quad
\inf_{\theta\in\Theta,\,\sigma\in\mathcal R}
\Pr_b^{\pi,\sigma,\theta}\!\left[z_t\in K_{\rm phys}\cap K_{\rm authority}\ \forall t\leq T\right]
\geq1-\alpha\right\}.
$$

Here $b$ is the initial information state; $\Theta$ specifies model uncertainty; $\mathcal R$ specifies admissible rival responses; and $\Pi_{\rm auth}$ restricts policies to usable, authorized controls. Report these choices with every estimate. The risk budget $\alpha$ is distinct from statistical confidence that the model class covers the actual dynamics. Neither should be silently reset each reporting period to imply a lifetime guarantee. Fixed unknown parameters and nonrectangular uncertainty require adequate memory in the information state; a naive Bellman recursion may otherwise be invalid. Loss of authority breaches the specified political criterion; it is not physical extinction. Calculate the two component risks separately as well. This framework preserves all nine layers as modules with compatible interfaces, rather than requiring one fitted world model.

### 3. Exact questions worth proving

The primary empirical target above permits positive risk over a stated horizon. The following zero-risk, infinite-horizon construction is a pedagogical benchmark with stronger assumptions, not a requirement for successful real governance. Use a finite-state model with bounded disturbances and a specified observation protocol. Let $B$ be the set of states consistent with the corrector's information. Include pending actions and permissions in the state. Let $\mathcal U(B)$ contain actions actually executable under every state in $B$, and let $\mathrm{Succ}(B,u)$ contain every possible next information set, including changes to monitoring and institutions.

Define $\mathcal V_0=\{B:B\subseteq K\}$ and iterate

$$
\mathcal V_{n+1}=\left\{B\in\mathcal V_n:\exists u\in\mathcal U(B),\quad
\mathrm{Succ}(B,u)\subseteq\mathcal V_n\right\}.
$$

For finite states, actions, and observations, with these successor sets correctly specified, the greatest fixed point is exactly the set of information states from which an authorized causal policy can guarantee safety indefinitely against the stated disturbances. This is a finite safety-game characterization, not a novel theorem. Its application to endogenous selection mechanisms could yield new comparative statics and observables.

Two useful candidate propositions follow:

* **Loss of distinguishability can destroy governability before material damage.** If observationally indistinguishable states require disjoint sets of safe continuation actions, and no admissible information-gathering or fallback action resolves the conflict safely, a robust guarantee is impossible from that information state—even if every underlying state is individually controllable with full information.
* **Protected feedback can defeat recursive capture.** Identify a nonempty invariant subset in the augmented state, including monitoring integrity and executable intervention rights. If an admissible policy preserves that subset against the allowed capture investments, the system remains viable despite ongoing selection and institutional change. This is a constructive counter-regime to universal collapse.

The first statement requires enough information to distinguish cases demanding different safety actions. The second cannot simply assume “independence”: it must model the resources and rights that sustain it.

A cheap constructive counter-regime is a renewable stock with $\dot x=\rho-e-d$, known $\rho\geq\rho_{\min}>\bar d$, disturbances $0\leq d\leq\bar d$, and an independently enforced total withdrawal cap $0\leq e\leq\rho_{\min}-\bar d$. From $x\geq x_{\min}$, the floor is preserved. Actors may compete over allocation and manipulate performance scores without defeating physical viability if they cannot bypass the cap. This toy model allows productive activity and requires no enumeration of actor behavior. Its boundaries are equally instructive: uncertain replenishment, unmetered withdrawal, or capture of the cap's actuator remove the guarantee. Authority and legitimate revision remain separate constraints. Compare cap enforcement costs with gains from manipulation; do not declare the cap invulnerable in the empirical application.

For continuous models, robust barrier certificates can supply sufficient conditions: a feasible authorized feedback must make the derivative of each active safety constraint nonnegative under all modeled disturbances, with appropriate regularity and invariance assumptions. Delay and learning must be included, not appended rhetorically. Barrier failure is not evidence that no other certificate or controller exists. Ames and colleagues provide the relevant safety-versus-performance methodology, under their stated dynamical assumptions [S4].

Finally, controller existence is weaker than institutional implementability. If actors retain independent choices, either model them as bounded adversaries—an explicitly conservative specification—or impose a stated equilibrium/learning-response criterion. A regulator cannot silently choose every firm's actions. Worst-case failure means failure of the chosen guarantee, not a prediction that failure occurs almost surely.

### 4. A phase diagram of viable and unsafe regimes

Use a parameterized family and compute regions; do not decree a universal two-axis diagram. Useful axes are capture investment productivity, effective correction reserve, information delay, and reversibility. Hold other assumptions fixed for each slice. Correction reserve should concern hazard-relevant interventions per relevant timescale, not raw regulator headcount or a generic intelligence score.

| Region | Formal interpretation | Empirical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Robustly governable | An interior portion of the viability set persists under specified parameter perturbations | Shocks and capture attempts are absorbed while feedback and authority remain usable |
| Viable but fragile | A safety policy exists, with small robustness or timing margins | Current governance can work, but modest delay or a permission change removes that option |
| Safe now, no qualifying continuation | Current constraints hold, but the information state lies outside the chosen viability set | Apparent prosperity masks loss of the required guarantee; this is not certain catastrophe |
| Constraint already violated | Current state lies outside $K$ | Recovery is a distinct question; some violations may be reparable, others absorbing |

Overlay a **safe reachability** calculation. For an invariant target $H$, determine whether a policy can reach $H$ within a stated horizon while staying in $K$, preserving required authority, and satisfying the selected implementation criterion throughout. A target may be desirable and stable yet unreachable from the present state. A temporary pause can help by restoring observation or buffer stocks, or hurt by concentrating control; the transition model should decide.

Measure early loss of options: revocation completion times, verified access to evidence, intervention feasibility under stress, and the estimated volume or margin of the viable region. These complement incident rates. They also make the theory testable before catastrophic outcomes.

### 5. Multiple scales and the proper use of precedents

Model fast operational decisions, intermediate organizational selection, and slower institutional or ecological change explicitly. Timescale separation is a hypothesis to test. Fast subsystems need uniformly adequate stability margins before an averaging argument is justified; thresholds, synchronized behavior, and disappearing buffers can defeat it. Aggregate safety does not imply subgroup viability, and local safety certificates do not compose unless cross-system disturbances and shared-resource budgets satisfy their interface assumptions.

**Ashby:** His requisite-variety argument concerns disturbances, possible responses, and acceptable outcomes under explicit assumptions. His discussion of staged regulation permits amplification and supplementation, including environmental information [S1]. My implication: count hazard-relevant distinctions and available responses, not all possible AI behaviors. Capability growth alone does not prove that effective regulation must grow more slowly. The proposed theory needs a specific bottleneck that survives modularization, reusable constraints, automated monitoring, and disturbance attenuation.

**Beer:** The Viable System Model supplies a vocabulary for nested operations, coordination, adaptation, and identity, and discusses organizational pathologies [S2]. It is useful for locating missing functions. Its recursive organizational schema is not a proof that a planet has a single legitimate governing identity, nor that every institution must copy a biological organism. Boundary choice and representation remain political and empirical questions.

**Ostrom:** Her primary synthesis supplies positive cases of institutional correction [S3]. These are positive counterexamples to assuming that multiplicity, competition, or cheap talk inevitably defeats collective correction. They do not establish that community arrangements scale unchanged to global, irreversible AI hazards. The inference to test is conditional: local knowledge and rule-making can improve feedback when coupled to enforceable cross-boundary constraints.

**Viability theory and modern control:** Aubin and colleagues distinguish viability from invariance for hybrid systems; Mitchell and colleagues demonstrate computational under-approximations for controlled and adversarial inputs [S5–S6]. These methods replace loose “points of no return” with model-relative sets. They do not discover the right social constraints or settle legitimacy. An engineered system's known actuators and bounded disturbances cannot be assumed for open political systems.

### 6. Three decisive studies

**A. Endogenous evaluation sandbox.** Use repeated resource-allocation tasks with organizational entry/exit. Randomize whether accumulated rewards can purchase changes to evaluation, monitoring access, or evaluator appointment; cross this with independently funded, externally validated measurement. Agents may invest in many activities, without a hard-coded preference for capture. Outcomes: resource stocks, evaluation–ground-truth divergence, distribution of control rights, and change in safe-policy availability. Compare recursive-selection predictions with a fixed-externality/limited-capacity baseline. Hold total budgets constant; hidden resource advantages are the principal confound. If manipulability does not increase investment in selection control, or protected measurement does not change relevant outcomes across prespecified regimes, the proposed mechanism weakens. A simulation establishes mechanism possibility; human experiments add behavioral evidence, and neither estimates global catastrophe risk.

**B. A prospective institutional intervention.** Recruit bounded, noncritical AI evaluation programs willing to randomize or stagger mandated access, protected evaluator budgets, and revocation drills. Compare with equal-budget general staffing increases. Track preregistered near-miss discovery, blinded seeded-fault detection, completed overrides, and evidence access—not just reported incidents. The recursive account predicts a distinctive improvement where the intervention blocks conversion of deployer success into evaluation control. Simple capacity theory predicts most gains from equal staffing. Main confounds are participant selection and initial system difficulty. Similar improvements from generic staffing, with no relationship to control-right changes, would weaken the distinctive claim. Seeded tests must remain safely contained.

**C. Positive/negative matched comparisons.** Compare durable resource-governance or service systems with failed or degrading counterparts, drawing the candidate population before knowing outcomes. Code control over monitoring, funding, entry, appeal, and rule revision separately from centralization or public ownership. Include shocks and periods of rapid growth. The theory predicts that maintenance of these corrective functions explains resilience beyond wealth, technical simplicity, and low competitive pressure. Positive examples motivated by Ostrom include jointly governed water resources and mixed-scale service arrangements [S3]; these are candidate mechanism cases, not AI-equivalent successes. Survival bias and easier environments are major confounds. Robust systems with concentrated, manipulable monitoring and no compensating mechanism would challenge the proposed necessity claims.

### 7. Design implications and standards of contribution

AI as common infrastructure is a serious design hypothesis, not a proved exit. A common provider can lower duplicated costs and supply shared measurement, but it can also become an evaluator monopoly, a correlated failure source, or the only party capable of revocation. Evaluate public, private, cooperative, and mixed arrangements by their actual feedback and control architecture. Retain independent evidence routes, operational fallbacks, interoperable interfaces where feasible, protected appeal and revision procedures, and practical tests of changing providers. These mechanisms have costs and can conflict; shared hazard budgets still require coordination.

No infinite regress of perfect overseers is necessary for a bounded claim. Define a threat model, distribute failure modes, and demonstrate robustness under explicitly bounded compromise. Also test the commons' own managers: they can alter eligibility, suppress adverse evidence, or entrench themselves. A robust institution must survive some mistakes and self-interested conduct by its correctors.

My standard for a credible IASEAI-level contribution—not an official rubric—is one coherent formal interface joining the layers, a new coupling result with an explicit positive counter-regime, and a reproducible study that distinguishes the result from a plausible baseline. The higher paradigm-level standard requires repeated prospective predictions, measurements others can reproduce, independent replication, and successful transfer across technically and institutionally different systems. Useful null results and boundary corrections count toward this program; a universally unfavorable phase diagram should be treated with suspicion until extensively challenged.

Prioritize: (1) agree on state, observability, authority, and horizon definitions; (2) build the finite safety-game example with both robust and fragile regimes; (3) prove a new capture-to-loss-of-options result; (4) test it against equal-budget capacity and ordinary externality baselines; (5) develop one safe transition with incentive-compatible implementation; (6) only then generalize across scales.

Non-negotiable gates for this lens: separate normative constraints from dynamics; never identify selection with an optimizing super-agent by assumption; distinguish actual safety, controller existence, equilibrium implementation, and robust reachability; include a viable counter-regime; report sensitivity to observation and disturbance models; and allow evidence that automated or distributed correction grows fast enough. My principal dissent is with any implication that recursion itself entails an inevitable hierarchy of capture. Recursion is equally an architecture for maintaining correction. The theory's task is to discriminate the two.

## 5. Chinese and CCP-related perspectives

Prepared 3 October 2026. This is an analytical lens, not an official Chinese or CCP position, an impersonation, or a claim about what all Chinese researchers believe. References [S1]–[S6] distinguish documented statements from the reviewer's proposed analysis.

### Scope and central judgment

I inspected the manuscript's opening architecture, passages located by searches for capture, authority, common infrastructure and transition, and sections 10.5–10.8. I used the supplied summary of the completed mathematical review and inspected relevant indexed passages; I did not repeat its proof audit. I read the six primary sources below. I did not examine confidential Chinese deliberations, survey Chinese public opinion, or establish the present operational status of bilateral cooperation. The 2024 diplomatic source is a historical precedent.

The strongest prospective contribution is a theory of when development gains become power to determine future development, evaluation and correction—and when institutions prevent that conversion. This can connect technical alignment to development-security coordination without presuming either that national competition necessarily destroys humanity or that a public owner solves capture. The important claim is conditional: an actor can improve its own AI's obedience while degrading the institutions that make the system safe and benefits broadly accessible.

My strongest recommendation is to build an **asymmetric, nested transition model**: competing states contain ministries, laboratories, firms, workers, regions and political leaders; their interests partly overlap and partly diverge. Add resource inequality, allocation rights, leadership turnover, and public-service outcomes to the existing selection architecture. A global agreement must survive domestic incentive conflicts as well as international rivalry. This is substantive development of the full program, not a request to reduce it to a single capture paper.

### What Chinese sources support—and do not support

**Official party-state policy.** The 2023 Global AI Governance Initiative joins development and security, sovereign jurisdiction, human control, risk classification, developing-country participation and UN-centered discussion. It also opposes exclusionary development barriers. These are declared principles, not evidence of implementation or endorsement of this theory. [S1] The 2025 State Council AI+ opinion promotes broad application, compute infrastructure, employment-risk assessment, public services and international capacity building; it also includes AI in national-security capabilities. [S2] Therefore, official support for shared benefit does not establish support for removing AI from all strategic competition.

The 2025 international governance action plan explicitly describes AI as a potential international public good and proposes shared infrastructure, assessment cooperation and developing-country capacity building. [S3] That creates a real conceptual meeting point with common infrastructure. It does not decide ownership, enforceable access rights, allocation authority or the permissible scope of geopolitical use. The theory must supply those missing institutions rather than treating common vocabulary as substantive agreement.

**Chinese scholarship and technical research.** Chinese-affiliated researchers are neither a single school nor interchangeable with officials. The multinational paper by Bengio and colleagues includes Andrew Yao, Ya-Qin Zhang, Lan Xue and Qiqi Gao at Chinese institutions. It discusses competition-induced delegation, loss of control, concentrations of power, evaluation and adaptive governance. [S5] This is a direct precedent: an international community already recognizes much of the problem. Novelty must lie in derived distinctions, endogenous institutional mechanisms and discriminating evidence, not in introducing Chinese readers to existential or societal risk.

**Security and economic analysis.** My inference is that a skeptical security researcher would ask whether restraint sacrifices protection against an adversary before shared safeguards function. An economic researcher would ask whether slower development, higher compliance costs or dependent access suppress catch-up. Neither concern is refuted by saying that all actors share a biosphere. Estimate the cross-effects and outside options.

**Citizens' interests.** Treat these as independently measured interests, not as a government's revealed preference. Include wages, employment quality, affordable services, privacy, error correction, opportunities for smaller businesses, regional access and meaningful recourse. Xi Jinping's 2021 common-prosperity speech connects development, distribution, public services, entrepreneurship and action against monopoly; it does not define prosperity as equal outcomes or the abolition of all competition. [S4] This supplies a policy-relevant question, not evidence that citizens endorse any particular governance design: who receives gains, who bears transition costs, and who can challenge harmful decisions?

### The hardest objection and strongest alternative

The strongest skeptical objection is: **your cooperative solution may institutionalize today's hierarchy while describing itself as humanity's common future**. A nominally universal compute ceiling can have unequal effects when incumbents already own models, chips, datasets, distribution, evaluator expertise and standard-setting positions. A small-country user may receive inexpensive inference while lacking the ability to audit, adapt, switch providers or develop independently. Access is not authority; service provision is not shared governance.

Current leaders also have freeze-in incentives within every political system. Officials may value administrative control and institutional survival; corporate executives may value rents; research leaders may value agenda-setting power. Do not automatically identify preservation of their authority with preservation of human agency. The relevant subjects must include future citizens and successors who need peaceful, lawful ways to revise today's arrangements. Nor should the model assume every leader has identical motives or that every institution is already captured.

Collective self-determination and national sovereignty deserve explicit protection, but permanent survival of each incumbent company, governing coalition or administrative organization is a different demand. A theory of retained human authority should permit legitimate institutional replacement while preventing coercive subordination of people. Otherwise its actor-survival axiom can turn into a theorem protecting incumbents from reform.

The strongest alternative explanation is ordinary security competition plus industrial policy, scale economies and bureaucratic incentives. Large actors may influence standards because they have greater expertise or bear greater implementation costs. Concentration may improve reliability, and centralized coordination may internalize spillovers that fragmented competition ignores. International mistrust may come from incompatible commitments rather than recursive selection. A mature theory must predict an additional pattern: after resource advantages grow, actors systematically alter evaluator selection, correction budgets, reporting channels or entry rules in ways that increase their future advantages, beyond what these alternatives predict.

Apply that test symmetrically to private laboratories, state firms, ministries, public compute operators, scientific committees and international organizations. Do not make Western electoral forms the definition of independence; do not make state ownership a certificate of independence. Ask who appoints, funds, removes, informs and overrules the evaluator, and whether adverse findings change behavior. The cooperative institution itself belongs inside the model.

### Definitions, proof obligations and counter-regimes

Define development as a vector, not aggregate output: service availability, productivity, labor outcomes, learning capacity and accessible compute. Define security separately as systemic technical risk, coercion exposure, infrastructure resilience and retained decision capacity. Regime continuity and national security must not silently substitute for public welfare. Define sovereignty operationally through jurisdiction, local control of sensitive data, auditable implementation and freedom from unilateral service withdrawal; evaluate its compatibility with reciprocal, voluntarily accepted verification.

For each actor, measure effective compute through accessible task performance, price, latency, adaptation rights and interruption risk—not raw accelerator counts alone. Define authority by demonstrated capacity to reject, revise and replace a system while maintaining essential functions. A signature on an AI recommendation is inadequate evidence.

A precise research question is: **when do enforceable service-access guarantees and portable infrastructure enlarge the set of stable safety agreements, after allowing infrastructure providers and governments to select evaluators and change allocation rules?** Compare capability limits alone; limits plus discretionary aid; and limits plus funded, reviewable access rights. Allow the controller to seek rents and allow small participants to leave.

Use a finite, explicit assessment horizon first. For actor i, a necessary participation condition is:

`A_i + ΔH_i + T_i ≥ C_i + Z_i + R_i`.

Here A is the incremental value of usable access, ΔH the reduction in expected harm relative to the outside option, T net transfers, C compliance costs, Z verification/sovereignty costs, and R foregone outside-option rents or strategic advantage. All terms use a common valuation and horizon; the inequality is accounting, not a novel theorem. Domestic veto players require separate constraints. Public-interest viability also cannot be inferred from their consent.

The nontrivial proposition to investigate is whether independently governed allocation and portability raise A and reduce Z enough to sustain participation **without** raising concentration-driven control faster than correction improves. Prove the boundaries after introducing endogenous monitoring and transfer enforcement. Participation is distinct from subsequent compliance: an agreement also needs a credible deviation condition at every relevant history, including provider withdrawal and coalition exclusion.

Counter-regimes must include competitive markets with genuine exit and effective correction; capable public coordination; technical saturation; bounded deployments; and cooperation that increases single-point failure or suppresses dissent. A mixed regime of civilian cooperation, reciprocal safety limits and continuing bounded competition may outperform comprehensive common ownership. This would refine the program rather than defeat it.

### Three decisive studies

**1. Domestic allocation and correction pilot.** Partner with consenting public-compute facilities and private providers; preregister a phased comparison of ordinary allocation with portable compute credits, independently funded evaluation and a documented appeal channel. Where feasible, randomize eligible application cohorts or rollout timing. Match workloads and safety requirements. Measure application approval, actual usable compute, lower-resource participation, provider-switching cost, evaluator disagreement, correction latency and independently verified failures. The distinctive prediction is that weakening resource-to-evaluator control improves correction beyond the effect of simply adding compute. The competing expertise explanation predicts advantages from concentrated provision after controlling resources. Selection into providers and differences in model quality are major confounds. No change in allocation bias or correction, or worse safety despite credible independence, weakens the proposed mechanism. Publish an adverse outcome even if the public provider performs worse.

**2. Bilateral human-authority experiment.** Use the historical 2024 China-US affirmation of human control over nuclear-use decisions as a narrow precedent, without claiming it already implements meaningful control. [S6] Begin with civilian research institutions running matched, fictional crisis-management exercises, not access to operational command systems. Compare AI advice with mandatory deliberation and practiced manual fallback against nominal human approval under time pressure. Randomize advice reliability, competitive tempo and the ability to challenge the model. Measure recognition of injected errors, successful override, quality of reasons, fallback performance and escalation choices; separate these from simple override frequency.

Each side runs the protocol locally; exchange preregistered aggregate results and independently witnessed protocol attestations. No classified plans or personal decision records need cross borders. Differences in expertise, scenario interpretation and socially desirable behavior are main confounds. Cross-language replication and repeated exercises reduce them. If trained humans preserve calibrated authority as tempo rises, or limits reduce performance without improving meaningful control, that narrows the claimed delegation mechanism. Results justify further measurement, not estimates of actual war probability or proof of global self-enforcement.

**3. Multilateral public-service infrastructure pilot.** Invite participating Chinese, Western and at least two lower-resource-country institutions to co-design a limited agricultural or disaster-information service. Recipient institutions choose tasks and retain local data control. Compare equal-budget service delivery through a single subsidized provider with a federated, interoperable arrangement offering funded local evaluators and a usable exit path. Allocate resources by published service needs; do not weight votes by compute contributions. Measure service quality, coverage, adaptation capability, procurement concentration, disputes resolved and continuity during a rehearsed provider failure.

The theory predicts that shared benefit lasts when recipients gain correction and replacement capacity, not just cheaper outputs. Economies-of-scale explanations predict the centralized provider may win on quality and cost; count that evidence. Connectivity, language resources and staffing are major confounds. A federation that costs more, captures local gatekeepers and provides no additional authority would disconfirm this design's superiority. A toy simulation can first identify thresholds; it is only a mechanism demonstration. The pilot supplies evidence about bounded deployments, not frontier-model catastrophe.

### Institutional implications and enforceability

Make common infrastructure a rights-and-obligations specification: who receives which service, pays, audits, allocates scarcity, handles complaints and maintains continuity. Prefer multiple compatible implementations where the gains from replaceability exceed coordination costs. A universal service entitlement need not mean a single global model or unrestricted release of dangerous capabilities.

The manuscript already explicitly favors distributed, replaceable, sufficient provision. The challenge is to operationalize those commitments, not rebut an invented proposal for one centralized state operator. Distributed nodes can still share a captured standard-setter, appointment channel or software supplier; topology alone does not establish distributed authority.

For any pilot, contracts and domestic authorizations should make access schedules, payment, reporting and review obligations enforceable. Use staged funding, prepaid essential-service continuity, published allocation records and mutually chosen dispute review. Distinguish these bounded mechanisms from enforcement of a sovereign frontier-development treaty, which remains an unresolved larger problem. Escalating sanctions must not casually remove essential services from affected citizens. The evaluator must have enough independent resources to detect provider noncompliance; pooled funding that one state or firm can revoke is not independence.

Global South participation must include funded agenda-setting and evaluation capacity, not merely attendance or receipt of products. Participants must be able to reject unsuitable systems. Current capability leaders should face symmetrical audit obligations and restrictions on discriminatory withdrawal, while differentiated support reflects unequal starting resources. Neither the Chinese state, Western states nor multinational providers should receive an unreviewable guardianship role.

Apply a role-reversal test: swap the nationality and institutional identity of the leading compute supplier, principal evaluator and treaty enforcer, holding technical capabilities and relevant conduct fixed. Would the authors accept the same access restrictions, evidence standards, veto rights and sanctions if those powers belonged to the rival? Test recipients' and citizens' answers too. Legitimate conduct-sensitive differences remain possible; identity-dependent exemptions reveal an incumbent bargain, not a general safety principle.

### Publication standard and prioritized gates

My judgment of an IASEAI-level contribution is a coherent conditional model, clear novelty against the international literature, one independently reproducible test or unusually strong institutional case, and a proposal whose own capture modes are explicit. This is not an official venue rubric. A field-changing program requires successful prospective predictions across institutional settings, measurement that different research communities can reproduce, and evidence that its interventions improve safety and broad agency without merely entrenching an incumbent coalition.

Prioritize: (1) nested actors and distributional measures; (2) an asymmetric transition model with both participation and compliance; (3) a bounded joint authority experiment; (4) an allocation/access pilot; (5) only then broader institutional recommendations. Maintain the nine-layer ambition, but make the interfaces and their evidence visible.

Non-negotiable gates for this lens are: no equation of CCP, China, citizens and scholars; no inference from policy language to actual performance; symmetrical capture tests; explicit incumbent and catch-up incentives; independently measurable human authority; credible local enforcement and limits to extrapolation; and at least one specified finding that would favor safe competition or reject the proposed common-infrastructure design.

My substantive disagreement with the strongest reading of the brief is that shared survival and common infrastructure do not by themselves establish a uniquely rational global settlement. A convincing contribution must demonstrate when institutions can coordinate development and security while preserving plural national and public choices. It must remain possible for the evidence to favor a mixed institutional architecture.

### Adversarial cross-review of the Western-perspectives memorandum

I read its capability-race ranking argument, reciprocal-verification experiment and cross-regime tests. The strongest unresolved conflict is **who may decide that another participant's capability threatens protection, and what power follows from that judgment**. The Western memorandum properly distinguishes defense from permanent technological rank. But a leader can sincerely regard another state's catch-up as reducing defensive security; the follower can sincerely regard dependence on the leader as coercion. Identical audit rules do not settle that substantive security conflict. Nor does a successful defensive stress test establish agreement about which interests deserve protection.

Evidence-based differences require publicly specified criteria that also bind today's leader when its conduct changes. A meaningful experiment must randomize the identity of the accused and the location of the infrastructure dependency, not only who initially possesses more compute. The unresolved trade-off is between effective collective intervention and protection against the interveners themselves; neither sovereign consent nor constitutional-sounding procedure dissolves it.

## 6. Western perspectives

**Role and scope.** This is an independent prospective research-program review, not a representation of any government, population, or named expert. I inspected the supplied brief; the manuscript overview; Definition 4; the institutional instruments in §9.5; and the transition, common-infrastructure, and precedent discussion in §§10.5–10.8, with targeted searches elsewhere. **Principal judgment.** The program could become influential by explaining when optimization destroys, preserves, or improves the institutions that assess and constrain it. That contribution remains valuable even if ending the strategic AI contest proves unnecessary or infeasible. Its most persuasive form would let a reader support competition, reject global common ownership, and still have to update their understanding of alignment. The central test is whether endogenous control over evaluators, access, and future rule changes predicts outcomes that conventional accounts miss.

This is not a request to abandon the nine-layer ambition. The ambition becomes stronger if its bridge from local selection to global governance admits viable competitive, cooperative, and mixed regimes. An account that explains why superficially similar institutions diverge would offer more than a universal indictment that categorizes successful competition outside its scope after observing success.

**Five distinct skeptical audiences.**

1. **US security and strategic competition.** A serious security reader can accept shared AI danger while fearing coercion by an adversary that retains capability. The July 2025 White House announcement explicitly connects AI leadership with economic competitiveness and national security, including exports of an American technology stack to allies [S1]. That is evidence of one administration’s stated position at that date, not proof that racing is optimal or a description of all Americans. My reconstruction of its strongest challenge is: show that reciprocal limits preserve the ability to resist aggression when compliance is uncertain and allies depend unequally on the leader. “All actors eventually lose” does not decide near-term choices without common beliefs, uncertainty bounds, a transition path, and distinctions between defensive assistance and irreversible strategic delegation. A rival need not want world domination to value a hedge against another power’s coercion.

2. **Liberal-democratic and constitutional.** The deepest objection concerns who counts as an actor. Citizens retaining agency does not require permanent survival of every corporation, administration, party, or geopolitical hierarchy. Peaceful replacement of a government, firm exit, institutional reform, and emancipation can be legitimate outcomes. A survival-first criterion applied to incumbent organizations can make power preservation appear morally compulsory. The theory should distinguish biological survival, basic rights, collective self-government, organizational continuity, and incumbents’ authority. A human minister nominally signing an AI decision is not sufficient retained human authority if affected people cannot contest the minister. This objection applies equally to private companies, democratic governments, international boards, and authoritarian institutions.

3. **European rights and public-interest perspectives.** These are plural too: rights advocates, industrial strategists, labor representatives, and security authorities have different priorities. The Council of Europe convention framework links AI governance to dignity, autonomy, accountability, remedies, and procedural safeguards; its scope has national-security qualifications and a national-defence exclusion [S2]. It therefore supplies a rights-oriented benchmark, not a completed answer to military AI coordination. My inference is that aggregate host viability cannot substitute for disaggregated harms or remedies. A stable infrastructure could entrench discriminatory allocation or surveillance. Conversely, rights-based constraints can support innovation and institutional diversity without requiring the end of competition. The theory needs an account of legitimate disagreement over what “enough” provision means and who can revise it.

4. **Market and innovation perspectives.** Competitive entry may generate technical alternatives, expose incumbent failures, and create escape routes from captured institutions. Restricting frontier participation can protect incumbents while being described as safety coordination. The FTC’s January 2025 staff study identifies potential concerns involving input access, switching costs, and privileged information in three cloud–AI partnerships; it expressly does not supply a comprehensive legal or economic assessment [S3]. This is a concrete mechanism inventory, not proof of illegal conduct or of recursive-selection dynamics. A public consortium can reproduce the same dependencies. The relevant comparison is among ownership and governance arrangements with their actual access rights, replacement costs, incentives, and beneficial outputs—not public purpose versus private greed.

5. **Technical-safety perspectives.** Deployment governance and technical alignment address distinct failure channels. Shared ownership does not establish reliable behavior, robustness under distribution shift, secure containment, or safe composition. NIST AI RMF 1.0 treats governance, context, measurement, and management as interconnected and calls for independent review and ongoing assessment [S4]. My inference is that recursive selection could explain when such practices persist or become ceremonial. It cannot treat technical safety progress as automatically canceled by institutional dynamics. The comparison must estimate residual total harm after adaptation, including genuine safety gains, deployment expansion, common-mode failures, and better incident response. A common model or common evaluator can create correlated failures even as it reduces competitive duplication.

**What would change a capability-race advocate’s ranking?** Their strongest objection is causal: capability leadership may improve defense, monitoring, and control faster than restraint reduces accidents, while unilateral restraint increases coercion and lets less accountable actors set the rules. Shared danger alone does not refute that comparison. Evaluate reciprocal limits against bounded competitive development over the same declared horizon, counting technical accidents, deliberate abuse, coercion, institutional capture, and lost benefits separately. The relevant policy-changing evidence would show that auditable limits reduce measured hazardous autonomy or control failures; inspection exercises bound meaningful covert violations; and defensive stress tests show that participants retain protection under those violations. Compare these benefits with the safety and defensive improvements forgone by delaying capability. If limits outperform across the advocate’s credible parameter range, their ranking should change; if protection depends on perfect compliance or suppressed dissent, it should not. A toy bargaining game is insufficient evidence for that real-world conclusion. Apply a role-reversal test: would a US advocate accept the same inspection, access, and veto powers if initially held by an adversary, and would a common-infrastructure advocate accept them after political opponents take control? Failure identifies a dependence on trusted incumbents, not a universal institution.

**The strongest charitable countercase.** Constitutional and polycentric institutions can use competition as one ingredient in collective correction. Rival suppliers, independent courts, investigative organizations, professional communities, democratic opposition, and local experimentation can expose each other’s failures. Cooperative standards can coexist with contestable production. Ostrom supplies a substantive comparative-governance predecessor [S5]. This is a substantive predecessor, not merely a supportive citation. Its results cannot be transferred automatically to frontier AI, whose scale, speed, and potential irreversibility may change the feasible institutional set.

The manuscript already preserves product, science, and service competition under stated conditions, and proposes distributed, sufficient, replaceable provision rather than a single omnipotent administrator. That is a substantial strength. But “competition that does not concentrate” must be classified using observable conditions before outcomes occur. Otherwise the theory risks saying that harmful rivalry is harmful while beneficial rivalry does not count. The new contribution should explain when competition supplies independent correction faster than it produces capture, and when the direction reverses.

A powerful counterexample would be a competitive sector in which new capability increases audit capacity, substitutability, and independently measured safety for a sustained period despite profitable opportunities to influence evaluation. A successful theory must accommodate this rather than redefine the sector away. The converse matters: a noncompetitive public system that survives by suppressing bad news is evidence against the proposed remedy unless its failure was predicted in advance.

**Definitions, propositions, and measurement.** Distinguish at least three forms of competition: innovation and service rivalry; positional accumulation that increases external hazards; and competition to control rules, evaluation, or exit. They can coexist within one institution. Likewise distinguish public financing, public ownership, universal access, open interfaces, joint security governance, and a single shared model. “Common infrastructure” is currently a family of interventions, not one treatment.

Represent the affected population separately from organizations competing on its behalf. Measure authority through effective capabilities: access to relevant evidence, power to refuse a consequential action, practical replacement options, time available for review, and ability to challenge the governing institution. Measure capture by changes in these capabilities, not simply by disagreement with the researcher’s policy preference. Administrative independence should be measured through budgets, appointment and removal, access rights, publication freedom, and capacity to act against powerful sponsors.

A precise candidate research question is: **holding technical capability and evaluation quality constant, does increasing positional reward increase the gap between reported safety and externally audited safety more strongly when the evaluated actor can renew, fund, or revoke the evaluator’s access?** Estimate the interaction between randomized positional incentives and discretionary evaluator dependence. The prediction is a positive interaction for concealed failure rates, mediated by changes in evaluation or access. Scope: environments where influence is possible and externally audited outcomes exist. Counter-regimes include nonappropriable standards, enforceable access, effective entrant discipline, and correction whose productivity rises faster than concealment. A null interaction, or improvement under dependence, must remain admissible.

For the mature theory, derive a threshold between two endogenous processes: influence that protects incumbents and contestability that rewards credible correction. Allow evaluator independence itself to be selected and financed. Model the same process for government agencies and international infrastructure boards. A name change from “firm” to “public trustee” must not reset capture parameters. Seek sufficient conditions for both stable bounded competition and stable cooperation; then identify what observed AI capabilities threaten those conditions. This would explain policy disagreement through different parameter estimates rather than imputing irrationality to everyone who rejects the preferred endpoint.

**Three decisive studies.**

**Study A: incentive and evaluator-dependence experiment.** In a repeated, incentivized model-development or simulated procurement task, randomize positional winner rewards and whether teams influence evaluator renewal/access. Give all teams the same technical tools and access to safety improvements. An independent evaluator uses concealed test cases and fixed scoring. Record rule-change attempts, evaluator replacement, access withdrawal, disclosed and undisclosed defects, investment in safety, and user-task performance. The recursive mechanism predicts the strongest reporting divergence under high stakes plus dependence, following observable control attempts. A pure resource-pressure explanation predicts deterioration mainly through reduced safety expenditure even without control over evaluators. Main confounds are experimenter demand and participants learning the hidden test. Pre-register mediation and rotate held-out tests. A precisely estimated absence of dependence interaction, after verifying the influence channel was usable, weakens the distinctive mechanism. A computational simulation can first demonstrate the mechanism but cannot establish real organizational behavior.

**Study B: comparative infrastructure pilot.** Run a bounded public-service AI pilot—for example document search in non-sensitive public records—using matched models and service requirements. Randomize feasible governance features across participating organizations: portable interfaces, independent assessment budgets, protected publication, and tested fallback capacity. Include public and private provision rather than treating ownership as the randomized mechanism. Use staged or cluster assignment where individual assignment would contaminate contracts. Measure replacement time and cost, successful appeals, delayed incident disclosure, subgroup outcomes, quality, and cost of delivery. The theory predicts that control dependencies explain failure better than the ownership label. A simpler scale-efficiency account predicts that concentrated integration wins through competence with no systematic accountability penalty. Selection into the pilot and spillovers are the principal confounds. If integrated providers repeatedly deliver better audited safety and effective remedies while the proposed safeguards add cost without reducing dependence, revise the institutional prescription. This is evidence about bounded infrastructure, not decisive-capability governance.

**Study C: reciprocal verification and alliance experiment.** Use a pre-registered repeated bargaining game with policy participants and a broader replication sample. Randomize leadership asymmetry, incomplete inspection, alliance obligations, and safe defensive fallback. Offer limited verifiable restrictions on strategic delegation, shared infrastructure, or unconstrained rivalry. Outcomes include agreement formation, concealed defection, distribution of security and service benefits, justified versus opportunistic withdrawal, and survival of cooperation after simulated leadership changes. A strong ending-the-contest account predicts that preserved rivalry systematically destabilizes arrangements under its specified hazard conditions. The charitable alternative predicts durable partial agreements where benefits, observability, and defensive safeguards align even though rivalry persists. Professional participants’ small sample and artificial stakes limit inference. Stable partial cooperation under pre-specified high-incentive conditions would weaken a broad incompatibility claim. Failure of common infrastructure after an asymmetric shock would identify a transition problem rather than vindicate unregulated racing. These experiments cannot identify real-world catastrophic probabilities.

**Design implications and the solution’s own failure modes.** Build common provision around replaceable modules and enforceable service rights. Separate infrastructure operation, evaluation, rule setting, adjudication, and coercive enforcement as far as feasible; separation without independent budgets and access is cosmetic. Maintain competent alternatives and periodically demonstrate replacement, including an operational fallback when a supplier or governing board refuses cooperation. A nominal right to exit is inadequate if exercising it means losing essential services. Universal provision also requires voice and remedies for people unable to move.

A safety compact should distinguish preserving protection against coercion from preserving permanent technological rank. Membership needs feasible entry criteria, support for less-resourced participants, reviewable exclusion decisions, and a path to revising initial allocations. Otherwise verification and compute access become a club good controlled by the original leaders. Inspectors can themselves become sources of surveillance, industrial espionage, or politicized accusation; protect necessary confidentiality while subjecting the inspectors’ authority and decisions to independent challenge. Enforcement rules should state how cooperation continues after a violation rather than make every dispute trigger catastrophic breakdown.

Rules established before capture must remain amendable by legitimate processes. Permanent emergency authority and permanent incumbent quotas are especially dangerous. Use renewal requirements, published reasons for restrictions, protected dissent, and means to remove officials without disabling service. These mechanisms also have costs: fragmentation can hinder incident response, transparency can disclose sensitive capabilities, and redundant providers can increase resource use. Their purpose is measurable reduction of dependence and abuse, not institutional complexity for its own sake.

**Cross-regime tests and evidentiary standards.** Apply identical questions to US firms and agencies, European regulators, Chinese institutions, and international consortia: Can affected people challenge harmful decisions? Can independent evaluators publish unwelcome findings? Can essential services survive removal of the controller? Can challengers enter without accepting incumbent political preferences? Do limitations constrain the powerful as well as newcomers? Can the rules be amended without destroying the safety system? These tests do not require assuming identical constitutional forms or equating a state with its citizens. They do require observable behavior rather than ideological assurances.

In my judgment, a credible IASEAI-level contribution would offer a coherent integrated framework, one or more valid discriminating results, explicit scope conditions and counter-regimes, and evidence linking at least one mechanism to actual AI governance. This is a scholarly standard, not an official venue rubric. A paradigm-level program would additionally need replicated causal findings, prospective predictions across institutions, reliable measurements others can use, explanatory gains beyond existing institutional theory, and successful interventions that survive turnover and adversarial adaptation. Publication alone cannot establish that status.

Prioritize: first separate citizens’ agency from incumbent survival; second specify competition and infrastructure treatments without outcome-based definitions; third derive both capture and correction regimes; fourth conduct Study A and a bounded governance pilot; fifth compare transition designs under asymmetric compliance and power. Non-negotiable gates for this reviewer are symmetric scrutiny of every ownership form, protected dissent and effective replacement, non-tautological predictions, honest partial-cooperation counterexamples, explicit technical residual risk, and no deduction of a unique political settlement from shared biological survival.

**Adversarial cross-review of the Chinese-perspectives memorandum.** I inspected its precise research question, participation condition, institutional-access proposals, and role-reversal test. Its strongest insight is that access rights and portable infrastructure can prevent safety cooperation from freezing incumbent advantage. The remaining conflict is that sovereign participation and independent correction can demand incompatible decision rights precisely when correction matters most.

Consider an adverse finding against a major state’s strategic program. If that state can revoke evaluator access, block publication, dismiss the finding through its own exclusive review, or withdraw while retaining the benefits of noncompliance, correction remains conditional on the corrected actor’s permission. If the evaluator can compel disclosure or enforce a remedy despite objection, the arrangement has delegated authority over a consequential sovereign decision. Calling the evaluator mutually chosen does not settle the dispute after consent changes. This problem applies to the United States and European states as much as China; it does not presume that supranational authority is preferable.

The participation inequality treats sovereignty costs, transfers, and development benefits as commensurable. That is useful accounting under stated preferences, but some participants may treat particular jurisdictional claims as non-tradeable. Conversely, officials’ consent cannot authorize every burden imposed on citizens. Two consent constraints are insufficient if states and domestic veto players consent while affected people lack effective recourse. The synthesis must identify whose objections carry standing and what decisions are outside ordinary bargaining.

The role-reversal test is necessary but insufficient. Rival elites could accept reciprocal secrecy and immunity while jointly weakening correction. Symmetrical treatment can therefore institutionalize a mutually convenient failure. Add a conduct test: inject a credible adverse finding against each major member, then measure continued evaluator access, evidence preservation, protected reporting, independent appeal, and actual corrective action. Specify what is publicly reportable, what remains confidential, and who resolves that boundary dispute.

A feasible bounded pilot can pre-authorize narrowly scoped access and remedies while preserving unrelated jurisdiction. It cannot establish that the same authority is acceptable for sovereign frontier programs. The synthesis should preserve this unresolved tradeoff explicitly, including the possibility that only partial agreements are feasible. “Sovereignty plus independent correction” is a research objective, not an achieved consensus.
