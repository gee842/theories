# Direction for v5.0: changing the present AI trajectory

**Authorial brief, research priorities, and writing-agent handoff — 3 October 2026**

This document consolidates the author's clarifications after the v4.5 mathematical review and prospective disciplinary panel. It supplies the direction for v5.0; it is not a new manuscript, another proof audit, or a claim that proposed experiments have been run. Read it before the other reviews in this package. The authorial scope below takes precedence over earlier review framing about the purpose of the paper. It does not nullify a valid mathematical objection or substitute a preferred conclusion for evidence.

## 1. The central direction

**v5.0 should develop the situational case for a cooperative pivot from the present AI arms-race trajectory, using the existing meta-attractor results as a central explanatory mechanism.**

The author is concerned with the conjunction of the present winning-and-acceleration mindset, material incentives, institutional arrangements, technological development, shared exposure, correction limits, and the range of alternatives currently treated as realistic. The central question is whether continuing along that path can credibly deliver safety and a desirable future, and what change of course is warranted.

The author explicitly clarified:

> “I am not saying that no bounded competition can exist or [be] stable.”

> “My conclusion is not pursuing the proof that we can't compete, but for where we are now, if we follow the current trajectory…”

The intended destination is a path pursuing cooperation rather than the arms race. This needs to organize the argument, rather than appear as one optional policy implication at the end. The task is to make the chain of reasoning sufficiently transparent and well supported that readers can examine the premises, understand the mechanisms, and assess the case for changing course.

**Suggested thesis paragraph for development:**

> This paper examines the present trajectory of AI development: the pursuit of decisive advantage through acceleration, in institutions whose conditions of evaluation and correction can themselves be reshaped by competitive success. The meta-attractor framework explains how this trajectory reproduces its incentives and can absorb local safety reforms. We connect these mechanisms to delegation, shared exposure, and the capacity to detect and correct consequential errors. The central argument is that the present trajectory warrants a cooperative change of course, and that making this change requires institutions able to sustain shared flourishing, effective correction, and a feasible transition. This is a situated argument about the path we are on, not a claim that every form of bounded competition is impossible.

This is a proposed statement of the paper's task. Its substantive claims still require the supporting proofs, evidence, and comparisons identified below.

**Further authorial clarification, incorporated before packaging:** even perfectly aligned AI does not resolve the instability generated by human decision-makers who continue to organize their activity around fighting and winning. AI must be approached as a planetary technology, developed for shared benefit rather than strategic struggle. A pause in training can buy time, but resuming with the same governing orientation does not by itself constitute readiness. Sections 3A and 9 below make this a central analytical and constructive task.

## 3. What the meta-attractor work already contributes

The organizing mechanism is:

> Recursive selection creates second-order selection pressure for mechanisms that increase control over future selection.

In the supplied v4.5 reading copy, Proposition 9 and its appendix derive a conditional selection gradient:

$$
\Delta\pi'(q)=\chi p^2\bar y^{\mathsf T}M\bar y-C'(q).
$$

Here $q$ is the carrier's conversion capacity, $\chi$ is the appropriable fraction of generated support, $p\bar y$ is its success profile, $M$ incorporates conversion and horizon-weighted support, and $C$ is the cost. The proof derives the support, aggregates its advantage, subtracts cost, and connects payoff differences to selection under the stated transmission assumptions. This supplies a mechanism for control capacity itself to be selected, rather than merely observing that powerful actors sometimes change rules.

The coupled-field results in Propositions 19–21 add capture conditions and certificates, seeded cascades, re-absorption of reforms, and different consequences of pauses that preserve or insulate rule-making. These should be part of v5.0's contribution inventory. The review also identifies limits to how some results are connected or generalized; acknowledging existing substance does not certify every later inference.

Keep three questions separate:

1. **Validity:** what follows within a specified model and under its assumptions?
2. **Originality:** which results, combinations, measurements, or applications differ from particular predecessors?
3. **Applicability:** what supports using the mechanism to diagnose the present AI trajectory?

The previous review cited dynamic political-economy work showing that present decisions can change future decision-making power. That establishes a serious comparison obligation. It does not, by thematic resemblance or an abstract alone, show that the manuscript's selection-gradient and coupled-field results are redundant. Conversely, a new name or a valid derivative does not establish originality. Compare actual models and propositions, identify overlap honestly, and state the incremental contribution precisely.

**Concrete output:** a contribution table with one row per retained result: statement; assumptions; predecessor; exact overlap; exact difference; proof status; present-world use. Do not reopen broad originality debate without that comparison.

## 3A. Perfectly aligned AI, human rivalry, and a pause that leaves the game intact

This is an explicit priority added by the author. It should appear in the main argument, not only in a limitation or conclusion.

### A. Specify what “perfectly aligned” means

For this argument, grant perfect alignment to the relevant human principal: the AI is truthful, understands and faithfully follows that principal's objectives and constraints, and does not pursue an independent goal. Humans retain the consequential decisions. This deliberately removes model disobedience as the source of the problem.

It does not follow that the principals' objectives are mutually compatible, that they account adequately for shared consequences, or that their decision process rewards restraint. If “perfect alignment” is instead defined to include a legitimate cooperative objective for humanity, the desired change in governing purpose is already included in that stronger definition. Keep these meanings separate.

The central claim to develop is **alignment within each principal–AI relationship does not, by itself, align the game among principals with planetary flourishing**. Better execution can increase the effectiveness of the game humans have chosen. Whether that improves or degrades collective outcomes depends on the game, its constraints, and what is selected as success.

### B. A transparent conditional demonstration

The following elementary example is provided as a proposed explanatory module. Its short proof is given here; it is a standard externality-and-positional-incentive construction, not an originality claim or an empirical model of actual states.

There are $n\ge2$ human principals. Each chooses $a_i\in\{0,1\}$, restraint or an increment of racing. Their one-period objectives are

$$
u_i=b\left(a_i-\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\right)
-c a_i-d\sum_j a_j,
\qquad c,d>0.
$$

The first term values relative standing, the second is private effort cost, and the third is shared damage experienced by each principal. If $b>c+d$, increasing $a_i$ from zero to one raises $u_i$ by $b-c-d>0$, whatever the others do. Racing is therefore strictly dominant and all-racing is the unique Nash equilibrium.

At all-restraint, every principal receives zero. At all-racing, every principal receives $-c-nd<0$. Summing the objectives gives

$$
\sum_i u_i=-(c+nd)\sum_i a_i,
$$

because the relative-standing terms cancel. Faithful AI assistance can implement each principal's individually preferred decision perfectly while implementing a collectively inferior outcome. Humans can make every final decision themselves and the result is unchanged. No AI rebellion, deception, or independent agency is needed for this mechanism.

The example establishes insufficiency of principal alignment under these incentives. It does not establish actual values of $b,c,d$, rule out repeated-game cooperation, or prove that all competition is harmful. Its connection to the meta-attractor framework is the next question: when does success preserve or intensify the winning objective and the institutions that make restraint costly, rather than enabling a change in the game?

### C. The additional premise needed for a long-run instability claim

A stage-game inefficiency alone does not prove collapse. For a precise conditional long-run result, separately assume that after any finite pause the relevant process resumes for infinitely many consequential rounds. Let $S_k$ be survival without the specified irreversible loss through round $k$. Suppose that, on every surviving history, the true residual probability of that loss in the next round—after the safety measures being evaluated—is at least $h_k$, where $0\le h_k<1$ and

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h_k=\infty.
$$

Then the chain rule gives

$$
\Pr(S_N)\le\prod_{k=1}^{N}(1-h_k)
\le\exp\!\left(-\sum_{k=1}^{N}h_k\right)\longrightarrow0.
$$

Independence between rounds is unnecessary because the bound is conditional on surviving histories. A finite pause that merely delays the same subsequent sequence does not change this limit. Perfect principal alignment also does not change it if the assumed residual hazard and continued decision process remain. This is a standard conditional probability result. The substantive work is to establish the causal connection between the present human rivalry and the nonsummable residual danger; **the winning mindset alone does not prove that premise**.

State exactly which irreversible loss is modeled. Physical extinction, loss of human decision authority, and failure of a particular institution are not interchangeable events. Report finite-horizon consequences as well: an eventual-zero result is insufficient for comparing the urgency or relative merits of available interventions. Cooperative alternatives may also carry risk and must be evaluated on the same basis.

### D. What a pause must accomplish

A pause can be valuable because it creates time to change the causal conditions: improve verification, reduce relevant residual hazards, maintain human capacity, establish enforceable limits, develop shared provision, and revise objectives and institutions. The claim is that **a pause with no relevant change is not a certificate of readiness to resume**. An indefinite stop, a lasting cap, or a pause that produces a different decision process falls outside the “mere delay” premise.

The existing field-model pause result, Proposition 21, supplies a narrower complementary mechanism: holding shares fixed while ordinary rule support continues to accumulate has different consequences from insulating rule-making during the pause. Preserve its actual assumptions. It does not by itself prove a civilization-wide statement about psychological readiness.

The strongest defensible formulation is about the **effective governing logic**: if the same pursuit of winning continues to generate the relevant dangerous choices and prevent adequate correction, technical alignment or the passage of time alone does not make resumption safe. Models can test objectives, incentives, permissions, and behavior. They cannot infer that every individual must undergo a particular inner conversion. Binding institutions could change the effective game even while private competitive preferences persist; whether they can remain effective is itself a meta-attractor and governance question.

### E. The planetary direction and readiness criterion

Treat “planetary technology” as both a scope claim to substantiate and an authorial governing commitment: relevant effects and dependencies cross the boundaries of the actors making decisions, so those actors' victory is an inadequate sole criterion of success. Document the actual pathways and limits of that exposure; do not use the label as a substitute for evidence.

The constructive direction is **AI for people collectively, rather than AI as the instrument by which one actor wins the common future**. This can support distributed infrastructure, plural local purposes, and bounded competition within shared conditions. Planetary purpose does not require uniform culture or a single permanent operator.

For v5.0, propose a readiness assessment that asks what has changed in technical reliability, shared objectives, strategic incentives, effective correction, deployment constraints, and the maintenance of human agency. These are dimensions to operationalize, not an already proved sufficient checklist. Progress in model alignment should count fully, while being assessed alongside the human decisions that determine its use.

**Suggested paragraph for the main paper:**

> Even perfectly faithful AI can intensify a harmful human game. If people continue to define success through winning an escalating contest, better alignment of each AI to its principal does not resolve the conflict among those principals or protect their shared conditions. A pause in training can create an opportunity to change this situation, but time alone does not do so. Readiness to resume must include a change in the effective purposes, institutions, and constraints governing the technology. For a technology with planetary consequences, the direction is shared flourishing and cooperation, rather than the use of greater intelligence to prevail over others.

The conditional mechanisms above support developing this argument. Its present-world conclusion still depends on the evidence and decision standards in the rest of the paper.

## 4. The necessary change from v4.5 to v5.0

v4.5 often allocates its strongest local results to proving that every relevant route through a race eventually fails. The author's clarification permits a more accurate organization: **use scoped mechanisms and present evidence to establish the practical case for changing this trajectory.**

This does not mean simply substituting “today” for “always” in an unsupported theorem. It changes the burden of argument. Abstract exceptions to a universal claim can become irrelevant to the situated diagnosis, but evidence about the actual situation becomes more important. Any mathematical conclusion retained must still be valid under its stated premises.

Separate three levels of claim throughout the manuscript:

| Level | Suitable form | Required support |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Under conditions $C$, a specified selection, capture, delay, or incentive result follows. | Correct derivation, scope, and counterexamples. |
| Diagnosis | Available evidence indicates that consequential parts of $C$ characterize the present trajectory. | Dated, relevant evidence; uncertainty; variation among actors and hazard classes. |
| Practical judgment | Given that diagnosis, the consequences, feasible alternatives, and stated values, a cooperative pivot is warranted. | A consistent decision standard, comparison of credible paths, transition costs, and sensitivity to disputed premises. |

A practical judgment does not need a proof of certain extinction. It also cannot follow merely from identifying a possible danger. Explain why the danger is sufficiently consequential, inadequately controlled, and better addressed by the proposed change of course than by credible continuation policies.

Maintain a common horizon and risk convention in comparisons. Distinguish physical survival, retained human agency, service continuity, and continued existence of particular organizations. If ambiguity aversion or a precautionary rule is used, name it and apply it symmetrically. Do not move between expected probabilities and worst-case reasoning depending on which path is being assessed.

## 5. Define the present trajectory and a meaningful pivot

Avoid treating “mindset” as an unexplained cause. Connect it to choices and institutions that can be observed. A candidate description of the trajectory combines:

- Strategic success defined substantially by relative or decisive advantage.
- Pressure to accelerate capability, deployment, or consequential delegation when rivals advance.
- Dependence of oversight on the actors, resources, tools, or schedules it evaluates.
- Reinvestment of success in both productive capacity and influence over future evaluation or rules.
- Shared harms and dependencies that are not adequately reflected in individual decisions.
- A restricted policy imagination in which collective restraint or shared provision is treated as unrealistic, while further acceleration remains the default response.

These are propositions to substantiate, not facts established by listing them. Actors may differ, institutions may resist, and particular technical or governance interventions may improve the trajectory.

**Continuation** should mean retaining a specified set of these governing dynamics. **Pivot** should mean a specified material change in objectives, decision rights, constraints, or shared provision. Bounded competition may continue inside an arrangement whose overriding purposes and constraints have changed.

Define this distinction before evaluating the outcomes. Do not rename every successful safeguard cooperation after the fact. Equally, do not treat a hypothetical safe competitive regime as a rebuttal without asking whether its conditions can be established on the path being defended.

For each proposed alternative, ask:

1. Does it address the relevant hazards and preserve useful benefits?
2. Can current actors establish it before the relevant losses or lock-in?
3. Can they fund, execute, and maintain it as capabilities and incentives change?
4. What does it change about winning, acceleration, authority, or correction?
5. Does it support a safer continuation, a cooperative pivot, or neither?

Evidence of adequate, durable correction under the pertinent present incentives would weaken the diagnosis. Evidence that a safe arrangement requires changing those incentives can support the pivot. Evidence against one proposed commons can redirect the institutional design without validating the arms race.

## 8. Make the current-case evidence the next substantive priority

The manuscript already has a conditions-and-measurement section. Develop that into a claim-evidence register. This document does not add new empirical findings or reverify the prior literature.

Each row should include: premise; operational indicator; population and hazard class; source date and scope; supporting and contrary evidence; uncertainty; causal alternative; sensitivity of the recommendation; and next measurement.

Prioritize six questions:

1. **What actually drives consequential decisions?** Distinguish race rhetoric from resource allocation, release decisions, delegation, and observed trade-offs between safety and strategic rank.
2. **How does success affect oversight?** Identify appointment, funding, access, benchmark, standards, or permission channels and plausible alternative explanations such as expertise.
3. **Which correction gaps matter?** Measure relevant hazard coverage, detection delay, executable response, and change over time. General capability growth is not itself a measurement of uncorrected danger.
4. **What is shared and difficult to reverse?** Distinguish harm to a provider, wider exposure, loss of human authority, dependence, and physical catastrophe.
5. **Which alternatives are treated as feasible?** Investigate the political and organizational selection of proposals, including cooperative initiatives that contradict a uniform arms-race description.
6. **How do proposed remedies compare after adaptation?** Include usefulness, distribution, residual risk, and durability at common horizons and resource constraints.

Do not invent estimates for unmeasured parameters. If the decision remains robust across a justified range, explain why. If one uncertain parameter determines the conclusion, make it a central crux rather than hiding it in a general statement about the present era.

The strongest new empirical target from the panel remains a candidate, not a prerequisite for acknowledging the existing contribution: an initial safety improvement changes later success and evaluator-control rights, and its long-run effect depends on whether those rights are protected. The corrected research roadmap provides fixed, matched-drift, and endogenous-selector comparisons. A simulator that directly rewards capture establishes only its programmed mechanism; independent outcome measures and meaningful alternative explanations are essential.

## 9. Give cooperation positive institutional content

The cooperative path should explain what people can build and achieve together, alongside what dangers it constrains. Shared flourishing, useful services, long-term responsibility, scientific progress, human agency, and the capacity to learn from failure are substantive governing purposes.

The author's further clarification strengthens this direction: AI should be developed and used for people collectively as a planetary technology. Technical alignment and a training pause are important possible components, but neither substitutes for examining what human decision-makers are trying to accomplish. The cooperative design must therefore address the reproduction of winning-oriented purposes as well as the fidelity of AI systems to those purposes. Section 3A supplies the conditional demonstration and the limits of what it proves.

The author's correction is explicit:

> “this is a mechanism design problem.”

Do not introduce “absolute power corrupts absolutely” as an axiom. Authority can supply coordination, investment, development, and correction that fragmented incentives fail to provide. Its design must address both competence and purpose, as well as misuse and error.

The governing objective itself belongs in the mechanism. Study how institutions select and form officeholders, reward service, sustain professional obligations, hear unwelcome evidence, evaluate long-term outcomes, and revise harmful practices. Do not assume that dominance-oriented preferences are immutable and that cooperation consists only of restraining them externally. Equally, a stated public mission is not proof of successful implementation.

Translate “AI as common infrastructure” into distinct design choices: financing, ownership, service obligations, access, technical interfaces, evaluation, rule-making, operational authority, and enforcement. The current manuscript already allows distributed and replaceable provision; preserve that rather than reducing it to a single monopolistic operator.

For each cooperative design, specify:

- What public purpose it serves and who participates in revising that purpose.
- How people obtain useful access and develop the competence to assess or replace systems.
- How evaluators obtain evidence, resources, and executable authority.
- How adverse findings lead to proportionate action, learning, and continued essential services.
- How leadership changes, mistakes, conflicts, and changes in capability are handled.
- Which forms of bounded competition, experimentation, or plural provision remain compatible with the shared constraints.

The Chinese–Western cross-review cases identify requirements, not an established incompatibility. A design must contend with both ineffective vetoes and abusive intervention, with unequal dependence, and with the leading provider as a possible subject of correction. Search for joint mechanisms rather than treating those tensions as a reason to abandon the cooperative task.

## 10. Make transition an argument, not an epilogue

A desirable arrangement must be reachable from the actual distribution of capabilities, dependencies, expectations, and bargaining power. The next version should develop at least one sufficiently concrete transition pathway to expose its assumptions.

Identify the initial actors and institutions, the first feasible commitments, what each party gives up or gains, and what happens before the entire arrangement exists. Include actors who cannot simply exit shared danger and people whose essential services depend on infrastructure controlled by others.

A candidate sequence for analysis is: shared diagnosis and measurement; narrow verifiable commitments; resources for independent correction and useful shared services; staged changes in strategic delegation and access; broader cooperation as implementation becomes credible. This is an illustrative sequence to evaluate, not a claim that all settings should follow it.

At every stage, analyze continued participation, deviation, budgets, coalition changes, and interim exposure. Explain how public purpose and preference change can affect these incentives without assuming every actor immediately adopts them. The best pathway may differ across civilian services, frontier development, and strategic decision-making.

Keep two distinctions visible: a cooperative destination need not eliminate every contest, and identifying a need to pivot does not prove that one particular institutional blueprint is uniquely feasible.
