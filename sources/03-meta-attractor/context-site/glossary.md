# Glossary (Canonical Terms)

- Meta‑attractor: A cross‑scale, self‑reinforcing pattern that reshapes environments to reproduce itself and progressively eliminates alternatives across minds, institutions, culture, and infrastructure.
- Basin (of attraction): The region of initial conditions and rules from which dynamics reliably flow into an attractor; depth indicates lock‑in strength.
- Metric collapse (Goodhart): When pushing a proxy hard decouples it from true welfare; optimizing the metric begins to harm the goal.
- Instrumental convergence: Tendency of capable optimizers to seek resources, reduce interruptions, and preserve objectives—unless constrained.
- Capture (regulatory/cognitive): Winners reshape rules, narratives, and even common sense to entrench their advantage; alternatives lose language and shelter.
- Proxy/KPI: A measurable surrogate for complex ends; useful until it becomes the goal.
- Slack: Buffers of time, capacity, attention, and redundancy that absorb shocks and enable course correction.
- Hysteresis: Path‑dependence where reversing inputs does not immediately restore prior state; deep basins require over‑correction to exit.
- Tripwire: Automatic slow/stop condition tied to safety/uncertainty thresholds (e.g., deployment gates, kill‑switches, compute caps).
- Buffer: Resource or structural reserve that dampens volatility (e.g., granaries, rate limits, rolling reserves).
- Polycentric oversight: Overlapping, semi‑independent governance centers that reduce capture risk and improve legitimacy.
- Sortition: Random selection (citizen juries) for oversight roles to dilute patronage and bias.
- Knowledge map: A structured concept/relationship graph (Mermaid) used to derive information architecture.
- Audience layers: Public (broad clarity), Practitioner (checklists, diagnostics), Scholar (formalism, proofs, references).
- Sequence (escape): Recognize → Slow/Cap → Align → Coordinate → Transform; order matters to avoid locking in the wrong basin.
- Alignment (here): Designing objectives, incentives, and constraints so optimization preserves plural human ends and commons.

Additional terms
- Ontological capture: Only what is billable/countable/rankable “exists” in decision‑making; non‑legible goods drop out.
- Legibility bias: Overweighting easily measured signals relative to complex, qualitative goods.
- Name–reality drift (正名): Titles/metrics diverge from real performance; ritual or audits needed to realign.
- Multi‑objective gates: Irreducible decision criteria that must each be satisfied (e.g., value created, harm avoided, uncertainty reduced).
- Provenance/credentials: Cryptographic or process guarantees of origin and integrity for high‑impact content.
- Compute gates: Scaling thresholds tied to verified safety evidence; capability grows only with demonstrated safety.
