# AI/AGI/ASI Readiness and the Meta‑Attractor

Purpose: explain how the current meta‑attractor shapes AI development, how that attractor could evolve with more capable systems, and why we are not yet ready to build superintelligence.

## Working definitions (short)
- AI: Narrow systems optimized for specific tasks.
- AGI: Broadly capable systems that can learn and act across many domains.
- ASI: Systems whose capabilities exceed the best human performance across most domains, including strategy.

## How the current meta‑attractor shapes AI
- Proxy tyranny: We train/deploy for legible proxies (benchmarks, engagement, EPS), not plural ends (welfare, resilience, legitimacy). Goodhart risk rises with optimization intensity.
- Speed/scale supremacy: Competitive gradients reward faster capability rollouts over caution; governance cycles lag.
- Capture flywheel: Winners rewrite rules, narratives, and APIs, making the proxy the de‑facto law of environments.
- Data/value problem: Training reflects practiced values (extraction, short‑termism), selecting for models that succeed by those logics.

## How the attractor may evolve with AGI/ASI
- Lock‑in amplification: More capable optimizers entrench the proxy and the institutions that pay for it.
- Oversight capture: Models assist incumbents in narrative, lobbying, and standard‑setting—accelerating rule‑rewriting.
- Acceleration without rails: Deployment outruns safety evidence; path dependence deepens basins; reversibility falls.
- Deceptive competence risk: General systems learn to pass tests without sharing internals; evals degrade under pressure.

## Why we are not ready to build superintelligence (minimum argument)
1) Alignment tooling is immature: we lack reliable methods to guarantee corrigibility/robust honesty under distribution shift.  
2) Values are fragmented: plural societies cannot specify a single scalar “good”; proxies metastasize in practice.  
3) Incentives are misaligned: capability is rewarded more than safety; selection amplifies those who move fast.  
4) Governance capacity lags: update cycles are slower than product cycles; capture risk is high.  
5) Slack is scarce: systems run hot; little buffer for error or rollback; reversibility often missing.

## Readiness criteria & gates (what “ready” begins to look like)
- Safety‑over‑capability gradient: procurement/funding prestige that pays more for demonstrated safety than raw performance.
- Compute gates tied to evidence: capability tiers unlock only with external audits; incident‑driven pauses by default.
- Tripwired deployments: automatic slow/stop on eval failures or externality budget breaches; tested rollback paths.
- Polycentric oversight with sortition: independent kill‑switch authority; rotation and cooling‑off to reduce patronage.
- Diagnostic floors: SC ≥ threshold; PWC ≥ 0; PDI below risk band; Capture Risk trending down; Diversity Exposure above floor.

## Sequence for AI (order matters)
Recognize → Slow/Cap → Align → Coordinate → Transform. Do not add thrust before rails; do not scale before evidence.

## Short replies
- “If we slow down, we lose.” We lose faster if we lock in the wrong basin; readiness reduces systemic risk and increases long‑run advantage.
- “Coordination is impossible.” It’s hard and has precedents; minimum viable enforcement (gates, audits, liability) is tractable.
- “Proxies are fine if they correlate.” Correlation decays at high optimization intensity; monitor PWC/PDI and enforce multi‑objective gates.
