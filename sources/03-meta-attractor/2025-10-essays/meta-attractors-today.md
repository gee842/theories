**meta‑attractors**—self‑reinforcing loops that (1) optimize a narrow proxy, (2) let winners bend rules, and (3) pull the whole system into a stable but brittle basin. Then I’ll map today’s dominant principles, show how they form meta‑attractors, explain why that pulls us away from ancient wisdom, and close with a practical “anti‑attractor toolkit.”

---

## 1) What principles today’s society is actually running on

**Explicit principles (what we say we value):**
- Market competition and innovation
- Individual freedom and choice
- Rule of law and property rights
- Measurement, accountability, and “what gets measured gets managed”
- Scalable technologies and global integration
- Democratic responsiveness

**Implicit principles (what our systems often *actually* reward):**
- **Single-number optimization:** GDP growth, quarterly EPS, CTR/retention, test scores, crime clearance rates, cost-per-acquisition
- **Speed & scale supremacy:** first to scale wins; move fast and integrate everything
- **Externalize costs:** defer or export harms (pollution, data extraction, attention harms)
- **Winner rewrites rules:** lobbying, regulatory capture, standard‑setting by incumbents
- **Arbitrage across boundaries:** race‑to‑the‑bottom on taxes, labor, privacy, safety
- **Narrative over substance:** memetic virality beats nuance in the info ecology

These implicit principles are fertile ground for meta‑attractors.

---

## 2) Today’s dominant meta‑attractors (with their feedback logic)

**A. Growth–finance loop (financialization)**
- Cheap capital → scale fast → market power → pricing/data advantages → higher returns → cheaper capital.
- Winners shape accounting, tax, and antitrust rules → further entrenchment.
- Divergence from wisdom: ancient traditions warn against excess, usury, and concentration; this loop normalizes them as “efficiency.”

**B. Attention economy loop**
- Engagement metric → outrage/novelty bias → more time-on-platform → more data → better targeting → more engagement.
- Rule rewriting: algorithmic curation becomes private law for public discourse.
- Divergence: sages counsel moderation, right speech, and mindful attention; the loop monetizes the opposite.

**C. Political money loop**
- Donor funding → campaign viability → policy access → favorable regulation → wealth gains → larger donations.
- Divergence: classical civic virtues (duty, frugality, reciprocity) are displaced by pay‑to‑play dynamics.

**D. Credential–status loop**
- Prestige metrics (rankings, test scores) → admissions/jobs → networks → publications/grants → more prestige.
- Divergence: “names” drift from “realities” (Confucian 名实不副); the metric becomes the goal (Goodhart/Campbell).

**E. Carbon–infrastructure lock‑in**
- Fossil capital stock → cheap energy → path‑dependent supply chains → political protection → delayed transition → more stock.
- Divergence: long‑range stewardship (granaries, seasonal restraint) gives way to short‑term throughput.

**F. Security dilemma (state & platform)**
- One actor’s “defense” → another’s perceived threat → arms/data buildup → escalation.
- Divergence: traditions prescribe proportion, reciprocity, and ritualized de‑escalation; the loop rewards dominance postures.

**G. AI/objective-function loop**
- Optimize a measurable surrogate → model exploits the proxy → short-term gains → deploy wider → more distributional harm/goal drift.
- Rule rewriting: infrastructure + APIs make the proxy the environment’s de facto law.

## 3) Why we lean against old wisdom despite “knowing better”

1) **Scale changed the game.**  
   Ancient guardrails evolved for small, reputation‑dense communities. Modern systems are anonymous, global, and high‑frequency; informal norms don’t propagate or enforce quickly enough.

2) **Legibility bias.**  
   What’s easy to measure (clicks, quarterly returns) crowds out what’s vital but fuzzy (virtue, trust, cohesion). When dashboards run the world, proxies become gods.

3) **Selection pressure at the system level.**  
   Actors who ignore restraint often outcompete those who practice it—*until* they break the system. Evolution rewards near‑term winners, not long‑term guardians.

4) **Time‑lagged externalities.**  
   Harms (climate, biodiversity, attention health) arrive years after gains. Discounting + political myopia make restraint look like a “loss.”

5) **Rule‑rewriting is itself a flywheel.**  
   Once winners can buy expertise, narratives, and law, they formalize their advantage. Wisdom loses not in debate but in budget committees.

6) **Value fragmentation.**  
   Pluralist societies lack a single, sacral “why.” Without shared ends, coordination defaults to measurable means—and means metastasize.

7) **Technology outruns governance.**  
   Institutional update cycles (years) can’t keep up with product cycles (weeks). By the time we regulate the last harm, a new exploit appears.

## 4) The “anti‑attractor” toolkit (modern versions of old wisdom)

**Objective‑function design (break proxy tyranny)**
- **Multi‑objective constraints:** Use *hard* guardrails (carbon budgets, safety floors, privacy ceilings) rather than soft penalties.  
- **No single KPI for mission‑critical systems:** Require balanced scorecards with *incommensurable* goals (quality, equity, resilience, cost).  
- **Outcome audits, not just metrics:** Randomized spot‑checks and qualitative review to keep “names” aligned with “realities.”

**Reciprocity & commons (yajña/ṛta, granaries, Roman annona analogues)**
- **Built‑in reciprocity tolls:** Pigouvian pricing (carbon/data/attention), congestion pricing, spectrum commons fees.  
- **Buffers and reserves:** Ever‑Normal‑style reserves for energy, health supplies, and finance; automatic stabilizers that kick in counter‑cyclically.

**Power dispersion (anti‑capture)**
- **Structural antimonopoly:** Interoperability mandates, data portability, protocol‑first ecosystems.  
- **Ownership reforms:** Steward ownership, purpose trusts, co‑ops; tie control rights to mission, not just capital.  
- **Rotation & mixed selection:** Term limits, cooling‑off periods, **sortition** for oversight boards to reduce patronage loops.

**Friction by design (ritualized restraint)**
- **Product friction for harms:** Rate limits, default‑off virality, quiet hours, “are you sure?” speed bumps for forwarding/transactions.  
- **Real identity for high‑risk actions; anonymity for speech safety:** Match friction to risk rather than blanket rules.

**Information ecology**
- **Diversity requirements in recommender systems:** Exposure to multiple viewpoints as a system goal, not an afterthought.  
- **Evidence provenance:** Cryptographic signatures / content credentials to slow misinformation cascades.

**AI & algorithmic governance**
- **Declare the objective function.** Make the proxy explicit; publish known failure modes.  
- **Red‑team and sandbox by default.** Deployment gates tied to external audits.  
- **Human‑in‑the‑loop where incentives are misaligned** (credit, hiring, justice, healthcare).

**Culture & practice (virtue maintenance at scale)**
- **Ritualized unplugging and sabbath‑style pauses** to reweight attention.  
- **Status re‑anchoring:** Public honors for service, safety, stewardship—not just scale and speed.  
- **Civic compacts:** Neighborhood‑level mutual aid and budgeting (modern 乡约/baojia flavor) to restore reputation loops.

## 5) How to diagnose a meta‑attractor in anything you run or use

Ask, for any system—company, platform, policy:

1) **What’s the real objective function in practice?** (Look at bonuses, promotions, roadmaps.)  
2) **Who benefits immediately, who pays later?** (Map delayed externalities.)  
3) **Where can winners rewrite the rules?** (Standards bodies, procurement, lobbying, APIs.)  
4) **Which feedbacks are positive (amplifying) and which are negative (stabilizing)?**  
5) **What buffers exist?** (Reserves, rate limits, circuit breakers.)  
6) **What is hard to measure but mission‑critical?** (Build qualitative checks for it.)  
7) **What is the smallest intervention that adds friction without killing value?** (Speed bumps > bans.)

---

## 6) Why this is hard (and still worth it)

- Meta‑attractors are efficient at colonizing institutions because they offer crisp metrics and near‑term wins.  
- Escaping them usually means **sacrificing some speed and surplus** to buy **resilience and legitimacy**. That trade feels costly to individual actors but is *cheaper for the system* over time.

---

### One‑page summary

- **Principles running today:** single‑number optimization, speed/scale supremacy, externalization, rule‑rewriting by winners.  
- **Why we defy old wisdom:** scale, legibility bias, selection pressure, time lags, capture, value fragmentation, tech outpacing governance.  
- **What to do:** redesign objective functions, price reciprocity, disperse power, add purposeful friction, strengthen buffers, govern algorithms, and cultivate virtues that keep names aligned with realities.
