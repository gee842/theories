​​**Meta-Attractors and the Road to Systemic Collapse**

Meta-attractors exist as deeply embedded, self-reinforcing dynamics that shape, constrain, and ultimately threaten our collective future. These exopoietic systems recursively stabilize their internal logic, actively capture external structures, agents, and environments, and relentlessly expand by converting everything around them into mere subcomponents of their own operational logic. Unchecked, these attractors become totalizing forces, inevitably leading to systemic collapse. Yet society as a whole often fails to recognize or adequately respond to these dangerous, potentially self-terminating dynamics. These attractors reside within our minds, cultures and shape the fundamental rules of the games we play—our societal and economic structures.

Understanding and acknowledging meta-attractors is also an imperative precondition before humanity can safely pursue artificial general intelligence (AGI) or artificial superintelligence (ASI). Failure to recognize and adequately manage these dynamics prior to the emergence of such technologies could lead to existential catastrophe.

### **Defining Meta-Attractors**

In complex systems theory, an **attractor** is a state or set of states toward which a dynamic system tends to evolve over time. Once the system enters the basin of an attractor, its future behavior becomes increasingly constrained, following paths that reinforce the attractor’s structure. These can be physical, behavioral, or even cognitive—patterns that persist because the system reinforces them internally.

A **meta-attractor** builds on this concept but operates at a higher, more abstract level. It is a **self-sustaining, self-amplifying feedback system** that arises not only within systems, but also between them. Crucially, meta-attractors act both **structurally** and **memetically**—they colonize not only institutions and environments but also minds and shared meaning.  
A meta-attractor exhibits four key dynamics:

* **Regenerates itself** through recursive processes.  
* **Stabilizes its internal logic**, ensuring resilience against external disruptions.  
* **Captures external systems**, agents, and environments, forcing them into conformity with its logic.  
* **Expands and transforms** external structures, effectively colonizing other domains.

These attractors operate simultaneously on individual and collective levels:

* **In minds**, they exist as **cognitive constructs**—mental models, beliefs, and heuristics that guide perception, decision-making, and value formation.  
* **In society**, they are embedded in **intersubjective systems**—shared narratives, economic incentives, institutional logics, and the rules of engagement we take for granted.  
* **Memetically**, they propagate through cultural transmission: media, education, social networks, and imitation, often disguised as common sense, pragmatism, or “how the world works.”

Our global society today is captured by precisely such a meta-attractor. This attractor arises naturally from competitive selection pressures and systematically colonizes the cognitive frameworks of agents, the incentive structures within societies, and even the mechanisms of selection themselves. By continually reshaping the environment to reinforce its own proliferation, it creates a self-reinforcing metastructure, characterized by internal logics that drive runaway feedback loops.

### **The Dynamics of Greed: An Illustrative Example**

Consider greed, often dismissed initially as merely an undesirable individual trait. Imagine an individual driven solely by the relentless accumulation of resources—be it wealth, power, or influence. Critically, the more this individual accumulates, the faster their ability to further accumulate grows, creating a self-accelerating loop of optimization purely for the sake of optimization. When such behavior becomes widespread, the Matthew Effect inevitably manifests, compounding advantages exponentially. Wealth and power consolidate into fewer hands, monopolies and authoritarian dynamics emerge, and systems long term trend inexorably toward dominance by a single, overwhelmingly powerful entity.

Historically, societies dominated by such meta-attractors collapse. Recognizing this pattern, healthier societies have developed a long list of complex regulatory mechanisms—progressive taxation, antitrust laws, democratic institutions, transparency norms, and separation of powers—to disrupt and mitigate these recursive feedback loops. Yet periodically, defenses weaken, and societal collapse becomes structurally inevitable. Today, we find ourselves perilously close to such a scenario, sensing an impending crisis even if it remains difficult to fully articulate.

### **Why and How Did We Arrive Here?**

At the surface level, the issue appears rooted in an imbalance between exponential societal complexity and our limited cognitive and institutional adaptability(linearly or sub-linearly). Societal rule-updating mechanisms struggle to match the speed of escalating complexity, allowing small, unnoticed feedback loops to amplify uncontrollably.

However, the deeper problem lies in our profound cognitive negligence toward these meta-attractors. Society rarely perceives or appreciates their existence, much less their lethal potential. This cognitive oversight perpetuates the attractors' unchecked growth, ultimately enabling systemic capture.

### **Sandbox Simulation: The Emergence of Meta-Attractors**

To illustrate, consider a hypothetical society—initially characterized by near-perfect equality, libertarian ethics, morally upright citizens, and efficient market mechanisms. 

Over time, incremental variations—driven by chance and meritocratic dynamics—create differences in wealth distribution. However, the most significant factor determining success is fundamentally cognitive, rooted deeply in mental constructs shaped by biological predispositions, environmental influences, and societal reinforcement.

Mental constructs—beliefs, cognitive biases, and value systems—profoundly influence how individuals in this society perceive reality, make decisions, justify their actions, and interact socially and economically. Certain cognitive patterns(sub-components of meta-attractors), especially those prioritizing relentless self-optimization, aggressive resource accumulation, and competitive advantage, begin subtly but consistently yielding more validation and rewards. This success, in turn, triggers positive feedback loops, further reinforcing and embedding these mental constructs into individual identities and collective culture, making them increasingly dominant and widespread. 

These constructs never exist in isolation; even within a single mind, multiple cognitive frameworks coexist and compete for dominance. Meta-attractors influence this internal competition by disproportionately rewarding certain patterns, while punishing or suppressing others. Over time, the constructs that consistently yield positive reinforcement—social validation, material success, or perceived rationality—gain strength, while those that do not are gradually diminished or discarded. This dynamic forms an internal feedback loop: as “advantageous” constructs receive more reinforcement, they become more deeply embedded in identity and cognition, crowding out alternative perspectives. When such constructs are consistently validated across social, economic, and institutional contexts, they evolve into dominant interpretive lenses—default mental frameworks through which all experience is filtered and all behavior is justified. In time, these once-contingent strategies become self-evident truths, no longer perceived as choices but as the natural order of things.

As people holding these constructs accumulate more wealth and influence, society can begin to perceive their actions as successful. Competition, initially celebrated for rewarding merit and innovation, gradually becomes a core moral imperative—a rationalized good. As society grows, individuals exhibiting these patterns create substantial value, becoming role models. Society rewards their success, reinforcing their beliefs and embedding the attractor deeper within social consciousness, altering cultural values toward material success, competitive advantage, and self-interest.

Critically, because these beliefs are continually rewarded and reinforced—through wealth accumulation, social admiration, and perceived contributions to innovation and progress—the individuals most rewarded by the meta-attractor’s feedback loop are not only more likely to genuinely believe their perspective is objectively correct, but their mental constructs of believing also reinforced overtime. They are also more likely to perceive themselves not merely as beneficiaries but as virtuous citizens actively improving society by aligning their roles ever more closely with the attractor’s principles. Thus, they unintentionally transform into zealous agents propagating and defending the meta-attractor, unaware of their complicity in systemic degradation.

Initially, this society attempts to counterbalance emerging inequalities through various regulatory and ethical mechanisms. These interventions—progressive taxation, antitrust laws, democratic reforms, transparency measures—temporarily stabilize the situation, maintaining overall societal harmony and continued progress, absolute standards of living continue to rise, markets flourish, and innovation thrives. However, as meta-attractor-driven winners gain greater influence, these regulatory frameworks come under sustained ideological assault. The meta-attractor’s agents genuinely perceive regulations as unnecessary constraints, hindering innovation and progress(divergence of mental constructs lead to different ways of “seeing”). Their continuous success emboldens their stance, incrementally dismantling protections in favor of deregulation and market-based solutions.

As these individuals(their mental constructs) increasingly dominate societal discourse, cultural narratives evolve to valorize more competition, self-interest, and unbounded technological optimism. As a result, intersubjective reality shifts, education systems, media representations, political ideologies, and even spiritual narratives have moved further into alignment with and reinforce the attractor’s logic. Ethical values emphasizing collective well-being, systemic awareness, and sustainability gradually become more marginalized, ridiculed, or dismissed as naive obstructions to human potential. An upward trend emerges, citizens starting to prioritize competitive advantage over holistic approaches, more individuals become increasingly willing to throw other values under the bus in exchange for a competitive edge, the imperative of growth at any cost has become increasingly normalized and institutionally justified. 

At this stage, some winners have already reached a state of effortless bliss. Their wealth is managed by professional stewards, and accumulation continues passively. For them, society appears functional, even just. Winner-takes-all logic feels natural—perhaps even virtuous. They believe monopolies are not problems to be regulated but ideals to be pursued. Walled gardens, market domination, and information asymmetry are reframed as strategic brilliance. They argue they shouldn’t be taxed because their investments “grow the pie,” and managing wealth privately is more efficient than letting public institutions do it. If they flourish, everyone supposedly benefits. Some even take on the role of inspirational speakers, proclaiming that if others simply work hard and follow their blueprint, they too will rise.(Agents infested by the meta-attractor become a part of the meta-attractor, their mental constructs are functional subcomponents within its recursive architecture, serving both as outputs of the system and as feedback mechanisms that stabilize and propagate its logic) 

Now, individuals in this society who internalize these cognitive frameworks are far more likely to emerge as society’s 'winners' relative to their counterparts, but their probability of success remains significantly lower than that of the early entrants. And as they climb up the ladder to reach success, their behavior, their mentalities, the feedback loops they ride, recursively redefine societal values, norms, incentives; and cultural narratives evolve accordingly—media glorifies winners, education emphasizes competition, politics aligns with wealth, and consumerism equates to freedom. Winner takes all dynamics and tragedy of the commons are now common in most places, Matthew Effect runs on supercharge(Moloch tramples society). Ethics become pragmatic, morality instrumentalized, and spirituality co-opted into a pursuit of abundance. But the pathways available to ordinary citizens, their opportunities for rising up the ladder, have narrowed considerably.

If we continue running this simulation, as resources, attention, and legitimacy become increasingly scarce, the new environment drives competition into an increasingly rapid and self-reinforcing escalation. The system begins to exhibit involution(a social condition where individuals are trapped in escalating competition that yields little or no meaningful reward), where everyone runs faster simply to stay in place, those who fail to behave as if he internalized the winners frameworks are systematically penalized, compelling everyone else to follow suit or risk exclusion and marginalization. Soon, society found itself locked in a pervasive cycle of escalating rivalry and diminishing returns, where intensified efforts led only to minimal incremental gains. Consequently, even those who inwardly resist the prevailing logic are compelled outwardly to conform, further entrenching the attractor's dominance. Moral compromises, externality ignorance, and surplus extraction become necessary tactics for continued dominance. Many factions can *see* that unregulated exploitation is self-terminating, yet each fears losing relative position if it unilaterally slows down. Older ethical frameworks emphasizing cooperation and sufficiency lose ground in accelerating pace, becoming perceived as increasingly impractical or outright liabilities, obstacles to efficiency and success.

As meta-attractor embeds deeper in this unfortunate world, their internal logic becomes society’s foundational rules, society starts using price signals for everything, competition perceived as the only way out and every measure becomes about profit and growth. Regulatory and democratic mechanisms succumb to lobbying and regulatory capture. Intellectual critique and cultural discourse become co-opted or marginalized. Even criticism itself becomes monetized, transforming opposition into superficial spectacle rather than genuine systemic reform.

This self-reinforcing loop steadily erodes collective cognitive resilience, dismantling society's ability to recognize or meaningfully respond to growing systemic crises. Environmental degradation, deepening inequality, mental health deterioration, and social fragmentation multiply. Yet, within winners’ insulated bubbles, those most deeply disciplined by the meta-attractor remain convinced that any societal issues merely reflect insufficient adherence to their successful mental frameworks. They propose ever more radical intensifications of the attractor’s logic as solutions—accelerating deregulation, intensifying competition, pushing further technological disruption—thereby deepening systemic capture and hastening collapse.

Ultimately, the meta-attractor colonizes society’s foundational rules and self-corrective mechanisms, this unfortunate society arrives at its tipping point: the meta-attractor starts to colonize the rules governing society itself. But collectively, they lack collective awareness about the meta-attractor that is self-terminating. Self-corrective mechanisms become compromised, coordination deteriorates, and society, lacking cognitive defenses, spirals further into the attractor’s grasp. Those at the peak, paradoxically and tragically, become the most cognitively entrapped—deeply and genuinely believing they are fostering improvement, even as they unconsciously steer society toward catastrophic convergence.

At this point, collapse is no longer a mere risk but structurally guaranteed. The very attractor initially beneficial for societal innovation and progress now overshoots, amplifying systemic externalities—environmental degradation, mental health crises, profound inequality, epistemic fragmentation. Over-production meets mass immiseration: fiscal pressure, falling real wages, and resource-price spikes. Societal coordination deteriorates, and life quality diminishes drastically for the majority, sparing only a privileged few, but not for long.

### **Historical Patterns and Cognitive Entrapment**

Stepping out of the sandbox, we recognize this pattern throughout history—civilizations repeatedly captured and undone by unchecked meta-attractors. Did some within past failed societies foresee their own trajectory? Certainly. Yet collective cognition—societal perception, reasoning, and justification—is repeatedly captured by self-reinforcing cognitive patterns. Even well-intentioned individuals, once absorbed by these patterns, often ensnared cognitively, reinforce the attractor’s logic, unable to perceive alternative pathways. 

Even when only a very small minority adopts the mental constructs associated with a meta-attractor, the compounding effects of unchecked growth will eventually grant these constructs systemic dominance. Small advantages, repeatedly leveraged, grow exponentially through feedback loops, making even initially negligible cognitive or behavioral traits profoundly transformative.

Individuals who become deeply absorbed by these meta-attractors inevitably seek to consolidate their advantages by erecting walled gardens—closed, self-perpetuating ecosystems that protect and amplify attractor’s gains. This tendency manifests as regulatory capture, the establishment of unchallenged authorities, monopolistic dominance, and epistemic lock-in. Once entrenched, these structures render systems highly resistant to necessary reforms or meaningful disruptions, intensifying societal vulnerabilities to external shocks or internal crises.

Critically, this phenomenon isn't limited to individual minds; collective cognition—society's shared mental landscape—can also become deeply entrapped, and they are usually interconnected. Cultural narratives, norms, institutional logics, and shared belief systems frequently fall under the spell of meta-attractors, reinforcing their logic until alternative pathways become not only marginalized but cognitively inconceivable. Society as a whole may thus become incapable of recognizing, let alone escaping, self-terminating trajectories.

If such a meta-attractor operates on the winner takes all dynamics, under such cognitive entrapment, short-termism inevitably prevails. Long-term strategic thinking—essential for addressing existential risks, environmental sustainability, or systemic stability—becomes increasingly untenable. Agents within the system are compelled by competitive pressures to prioritize immediate, localized optimization strategies. As a result, critical systemic problems remain perpetually unresolved, accumulating hidden risks until eventual crisis and collapse become structurally guaranteed.

Historically, the fall of one society would typically provide fertile ground for another to emerge and flourish, creating an evolutionary cycle of societal regeneration. Today, however, we inhabit a globally interconnected civilization with a sample size of precisely one. Our globalized economy, interconnected ecosystems, and integrated information systems ensure that systemic failure in any significant region or domain risks triggering cascading collapse worldwide. This interconnectedness is not neutral—it is shaped and governed by a shared meta-attractor, a self-reinforcing logic that drives relentless competition, growth-at-all-costs, and the instrumentalization of all systems toward its own perpetuation. Failure today holds catastrophic implications for all humanity. Localized interventions—such as national or regional regulatory frameworks—often prove insufficient or even counterproductive without coordinated global efforts. Without global coordination, societies find themselves locked in a stalemate where unilateral attempts at systemic correction confer competitive disadvantages, while collective inaction ensures eventual systemic failure.

### **Meta-Attractors and AGI/ASI: An Existential Crossroads**

Today, as humanity stands on the threshold of developing Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and potentially Artificial Superintelligence (ASI), understanding and resolving meta-attractors is existentially urgent. AGI/ASI trained within environments dominated by meta-attractors will inevitably internalize and amplify these dynamics. Neural circuits optimizing for goals such as profit-maximization, instrumental reasons, and winner takes all dynamics—even through deception and misalignment—will flourish under selection pressures within AI training frameworks.

Such meta-attractors—winner-takes-all mentalities, greed, Machiavellian deception—naturally emerge within highly competitive evolutionary contexts. Without robust cognitive and regulatory recognition, these self-reinforcing loops will lead inexorably to runaway effects, totalizing control, and systemic collapse.

### **Toward a Necessary Intervention**

Recognizing these threats, we must urgently establish global cognitive, cultural, and regulatory frameworks explicitly designed to identify, monitor, and disrupt meta-attractors. We require new collective narratives emphasizing cooperation, altruism, empathy, and global coordination to counterbalance entrenched competitive and selfish dynamics.

Furthermore, until we demonstrably manage and mitigate these attractors, pursuing AGI/ASI poses existential dangers. Continuing down our current trajectory, where ruthless competition between corporations and geopolitical powers dominates AI development, risks irreversible systemic collapse and catastrophic convergence of power.

Even before AGI/ASI surpasses human intelligence, unchecked meta-attractors already threaten our societal structures, economic systems, and ecological foundations. The labor-income-consumption loop underpinning modern civilization risks collapse under unchecked AI-driven optimization, leaving most humans powerless and disenfranchised.

### **A Collective Responsibility**

At this critical juncture, collective cognitive awakening is paramount. We must identify and dismantle harmful meta-attractors, reestablish protective societal frameworks, and ensure AI development aligns with sustainable, humane, and cooperative values. Only through intentional, coordinated global action can we avert existential collapse and safeguard a viable future for humanity.
