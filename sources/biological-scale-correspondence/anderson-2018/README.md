# Anderson and McMullan: tissue, avoidance and subsequent pathogen burden

Original sources for Anderson and McMullan (2018), *Neuronal and non-neuronal signals regulate Caernorhabditis elegans avoidance of contaminated food*, [Philosophical Transactions B 373:20170255](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2017.0255). Title spelling follows the source. Article and supporting data: CC BY 4.0.

`rstb20170255_si_001.xlsx` is the unchanged [supporting-data download](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/11095832), described by [Figshare record 6144314](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.6144314). Its 84,163 bytes and MD5 `8e5ced018a215e691cff5785475bf2ce` match the publisher's metadata. Original API metadata and Europe PMC article XML are preserved. [Manifest](manifest.json) records SHA-256 values, provenance and licenses.

The selected `Syto13 measurements` sheet has 33 standard-lawn and 37 full-lawn fluorescence values at the rectal opening. Full lawns restrict avoidance. These are individual values without retained plate or date membership, not matched pairs. A fluorescence difference is not a CFU count, a measurement of whole-community biomass, or an isolated movement-only intervention: lawn geometry and available exposure change together.

The separate avoidance sheets report percentages for strains on pathogenic CBX102 and nonpathogenic UV336 mixtures. Column header spellings are preserved, including `pr694`; the article calls the allele `p694`. Blank cells are missing, not zero. Row positions are not used to infer pairing between strains, preparations or fluorescence measurements. The secondary descriptive extraction includes wild type, the TAX-2 mutant, BUS-1 mutant, epithelial-promoter BUS-1 rescue and ASE-only TAX-2 rescue; the last fails to restore avoidance in the published interpretation. The `egl-5` promoter also expresses in three posterior body-wall muscles, so this is not an exclusively epithelial intervention.
