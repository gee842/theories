# Replication and assay correspondence

Original CC BY 4.0 primary articles and the Akinosho et al. raw workbook. [Manifest](manifest.json) preserves exact URLs, byte counts and hashes.

- Gainey, Shubin and Hunter (2025), [*Reported transgenerational responses to Pseudomonas aeruginosa in Caenorhabditis elegans are not robust*](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.100254). Read results on behavior, reporter instability, single-copy reporter and RNA-pathway mutants, and the corresponding methods. Their failure to obtain a reliable F2 response is retained; reporter and behavioral effects can dissociate.
- Kaletsky et al. (2025), [*Molecular requirements for C. elegans transgenerational epigenetic inheritance of pathogen avoidance*](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.105673). Read the azide/no-azide experiments and assay distinctions, condition-dependent RNA expression and molecular requirements. An initial-choice assay and an hour of unrestricted motion measure different outcomes. This explanatory response does not erase the other laboratory's observations.
- Akinosho, Alexander, Floyd and Vidal-Gadea (2025), [*Independent validation of transgenerational inheritance of learned pathogen avoidance in Caenorhabditis elegans*](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.107034). Read the full main article and inspect all workbook sheets; reanalyze Table B in full. The authors report an independent F2 replication under their specified assay; it is neither a Pv1 replication nor an F4 or ecological-feedback test.

No exclusion mask is supplied. Table B rows 32–41 and 64–73 repeat an entire ten-assay count sequence with bacterial count columns exchanged across different trial/generation labels. The reason is unresolved. Preserve the exact source and flag its interpretation; no allegation about intent or silent source correction is justified.
