## Reading scope and bounded synopsis

Read main Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion: especially “The Current Study”/Fig.1, Methods → “Public goods game”/“Procedure,” and Results → the Node-1/Node-2 intervention analyses/Fig.2. No independent dataset, Appendix A or cited-study audit; no replication.

The four-node chain has computer-controlled endpoints and two human intermediaries, each encountering different partners. An earlier human contribution is replayed onward without simultaneous participation. The second partner’s contribution is predetermined as ten minus the first partner’s contribution.

Generous versus selfish seeds affect the first intermediary’s onward contribution. Generosity does not significantly raise that person’s baseline; the second-hop cascade is not significant. Initial giving is offset in subsequent programmed receipts. Neither increased net benefit nor an actual initiating donor’s sacrifice is established.

## Our use

This is a comparison for forward causal transmission with no backward response in the experimental chain. Contribution behavior and cash earnings require further interpretation before being called ethical good. Repeating an action does not establish adaptive concern for a new participant’s condition. The source neither derives moral necessity nor validates the full generative foundation.
