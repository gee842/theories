**Bounded source synopsis:** Frick grounds welfare-related concern in persons' moral status across possible histories, not only currently existing people. A wide-scope conditional reason can constrain creation without favoring creation itself. His strict asymmetry denies even an unconditional welfare reason to create happy people. His detachment rules require feasibility and relevant unalterability by the actor. Threshold protection and same-person improvement do not alone yield different-person preference: §9 adds the Principle of Standard Selection. Promising and hiring analogies motivate that addition; they do not independently establish every uninvolved helper's standing or duty. His ethical grounds and inference rules are substantive proposals, not consequences of conditional logic alone.

**Our use and limits:** this is one contemporary philosophical comparator, not a characterization of Western ethics, individualism or any culture. Bearer-regarding concern does not by itself assert a metaphysically independent self. Our account retains real goods in newly formed lives and leaves the creation/noncreation comparison open. Denying that a creation duty follows is weaker than Frick's no-reason claim; his strict asymmetry, maximization and selection principles are not imported as established axioms. The prospective helper application must defend its actor scope and cross-history comparison; the source's promissory analogy cannot silently supply either. In particular, that some future bearer will exist does not establish that a particular bearer will exist independently of the helper's identity-changing intervention. No empirical evidence, formal verification or completed foundational derivation is claimed.
