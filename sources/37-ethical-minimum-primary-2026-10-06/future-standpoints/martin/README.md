**Bounded source synopsis:** Martin distinguishes reasons attributed to abstract types from type-based reasons attributed to particular people. She argues that generic descriptions must preserve the individual's relevant position. Her comparison separates uncertainty about which treatment could help one person from alternatives that form different people. A type's better prospects cannot automatically become a particular person's own comparative complaint. Section 4 expressly brackets noncomparative objections, and the conclusion leaves that route open. The critique targets a proposed contractualist solution; it does not establish that no ethical reason concerns identity-changing future protection, or that Kumar's complete noncomparative proposal is refuted.

**Use in this study:** this is a primary critical argument, not primary access to Kumar's earlier publications. It constrains our use of “whoever forms”: that phrase supplies neither a new experiencing subject nor a transfer of another possible person's benefit. The generative account's positive protective argument remains to defend at its own scope.
