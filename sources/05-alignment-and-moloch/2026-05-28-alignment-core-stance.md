# Core stance — alignment workspace

This document captures the substantive ground that grew out of the conversation on 2026-05-28 between Ren and Claude (as research collaborator). It is not a final position. It is the working stance from which subsequent threads continue, and should be revised as positions sharpen.

## How this stance is held

Ren's first message in this workspace shared a markdown document titled "My Views on AI Alignment." That document was a lossy compression of past AI conversations, not Ren's considered position. Claude initially treated it as such and pushed back at the wrong granularity. Ren corrected this. The stance below is what survived that correction and the conversation that followed.

A pattern that emerged and matters: Claude kept reflexing back to "what's operationable" — a technocratic frame that is itself part of the dynamic Ren is critiquing. Ren caught this. The stance includes that caught reflex as ongoing context. Paradigm and culture shifts don't operationalize cleanly, and the demand for operationability is itself a symptom.

## The diagnosis

The base rule "what you have is yours and you can compound it," combined with scarcity and enough players in a multi-agent environment, produces a long-run attractor where narrow winning-optimization dominates. Winner-takes-all takes over.

The sharper claim — the one that distinguishes this from a vanilla Moloch argument — is that the selection pressure under this dynamic favors actors who relate to everything instrumentally. Those actors then wield power. Their cognitive lens — the competition frame — shapes the rules going forward. The system concentrates not just resources but *a way of seeing*, and that way of seeing reshapes the institutional environment to amplify itself.

This has a second layer the structural Moloch story misses: the dynamic has been epistemically internalized. Decision-makers don't just *find themselves in* Moloch-shaped games; they *see through* the competition lens. Moloch is in the mind, not only in the structure. This is why structural reform alone doesn't change the dynamic — the cognition persists across reform attempts.

The implication for AI alignment: perfect inner alignment doesn't save us. If principals are selected for instrumental cognition by the dynamic above, then an AI that does what its principal intends is amplifying the dynamic, not checking it. The technical project of inner alignment is enrolled in the dynamic it cannot, by its own scope, address.

## The deeper root

Ren locates the metaphysical root in dualistic thinking: the subject-object split, and the treatment of "the subject wants good for itself" as definitionally rational. From inside Daoist, Confucian, Buddhist, process-philosophical, and various Indigenous relational ontologies, this isn't a discovery about minds but a tradition-specific commitment treating itself as universal.

Claude pushed that dualism is necessary-but-insufficient. There are deeply dualistic traditions that didn't generate Moloch at this scale (medieval Christendom ran on different base rules — no compounding, no corporate personhood, just-price doctrine) and partially non-dualistic societies that managed substantial extraction (dynastic China). The full causal story includes the *institutional codification* of dualism's implications — formalized property and inheritance, money as universal equivalent, corporate legal personhood. These are plausibly downstream of dualism, but the codification is what made it civilizationally totalizing. The intervention point therefore lives partly in metaphysics and partly in institutions; the framework should hold both.

## The timescale

Ren reads the cosmic-cancer emergence as approximately 5000 years old, beginning with metal weapons and large-scale grain agriculture. Culture and ethics are "social technology" — immune response — against this dynamic. Once civilizations get captured by the logic and start expanding, the cancer is alive and metastasizing.

This aligns with the careful version of the agricultural-state literature (James C. Scott; Graeber and Wengrow): the novelty isn't "selfishness appears" but "grain agriculture is uniquely legible to states, enabling extraction at scale," plus metallurgy enabling coercive enforcement. Pre-agricultural societies had violence, hierarchy, and competition; what changed was scalability.

The West's immune system has been corrupted by what it was meant to resist — neoliberalism and accelerationism as the auto-immune symptoms. The shared epistemic floor has collapsed; argument and legitimacy systems are distorted beyond rationality; the decision-making layer is captured. Whether late-stage is *terminal* stage is the open question, contingent on whether the shock that historically forces civilizational repair arrives before the technology forecloses on the repairable window.

## What the internet teaches

In the 1990s, intelligent people argued that the internet's structural properties — decentralization, peer-to-peer, near-zero cost of speech, end-to-end design — would generate a counter-vector against existing power. Disintermediation. Democratized information. Three decades later: capture by the attention economy; gatekeepers reconstituted at the platform layer; shared epistemics collapsed; manipulation industrialized; coordination harder, not easier, across most of the West.

The protocols did not save anything. The dynamic ate the technology, and then ate the discourse about the technology.

The lesson for AI: a powerful general-purpose technology dropped into a Moloch-shaped system is refactored to serve the dynamic. The technology's affordances are subordinate to the dynamic's logic. The dynamic was already there. The technology became fuel. By extension: the discourse around AI risk is already being metabolized — into safety theater, into AI-ethics-as-PR, into compliance frameworks that lock in incumbents, into an entire alignment-as-career track running inside the labs creating the problem. "AI might be a legibility event" is the same shape of claim as "the internet will democratize information," and probably the same failure mode.

**Working implication: be suspicious of techno-optimist counter-vectors.** Anything legible enough to be adopted at scale by current institutions is legible enough to be neutralized by them.

## What the work is

Working at paradigm or culture level is not a project plan. It is a posture, and the posture is part of the substance. Historical analogues: the early Christians in year 30, the Stoics during Roman decline, Daoists during the warring states, Confucians under Mongol rule, Sufi orders through dynastic transitions, Christian monastics through the early medieval collapse. What these had in common:

- They saw something their contemporaries didn't or couldn't.
- They did not expect to win in their lifetimes.
- They preserved frames of understanding through hostile or indifferent epochs.
- They demonstrated by being more than they argued.
- They were vindicated or extended after the dominant order they critiqued had collapsed or transformed.

Ren's prescription — go back to ancient times, other cultures, to find the ground — is not nostalgia and not a strategy step. It is recovery and preservation as part of the work itself. Holding the alternative open so it is available when the dominant frame fractures.

A complication worth holding: recovery itself can be metabolized by the dynamic. Daoism becomes wellness products. Buddhism becomes corporate mindfulness training. Confucianism gets used as authoritarian legitimation. Stoicism becomes Silicon Valley productivity content. So "go back to ancient ground" is not automatically immune. Provisional guess: frames carried by communities actually living them resist translation in ways frames carried only by texts and concepts don't. Practice has a thickness that concepts don't. This is a guess and worth thinking through more carefully.

## The pragmatic minimum

If we cannot overturn the situation — and Ren does not claim we will — there is still a pragmatic minimum worth holding to: do not use AI to compete with humans in the domains that constitute the substrate of social coordination. Operationally: labor markets, financial markets, politics.

These three are not arbitrary. They are the domains where AI deployed competitively directly attacks the coordination infrastructure that any post-AI settlement would have to draw on:

- **Labor markets**: as intelligence-as-input cheapens, the labor → wage → distribution mechanism collapses faster than alternatives can be built.
- **Financial markets**: algorithmic advantage compounds faster than human deliberation can correct.
- **Politics**: AI-mediated information environments destroy the conditions for shared sense-making.

The more general candidate principle: **no AI in zero-sum games against humans.** AI should benefit all of humanity, not a selected few — especially not the winners of the current dynamic. This is not the same as anti-AI. It is against the logic AI is currently being used under.

Corollary: if we cannot shift the consciousness that determines how AI is deployed before scaling its capability further, the responsible move is to pause. Not because the technology is bad, but because the mind currently wielding it is not adequate to the stakes.

## Where Ren and Claude land

**Ren's position, as it currently stands:**
- The diagnosis above is largely correct as a civilizational reading.
- The dynamic is dangerous in this window in a way that prior windows have not been, because AI is a capability multiplier on an already-late-stage Moloch.
- The probability of successfully overturning the situation is genuinely uncertain and possibly low.
- The work continues anyway, and the work is principally paradigm / culture / consciousness, with the pragmatic minimum as a floor.
- Recovery of older traditions (Daoist, Confucian, etc.) is part of the ground, not nostalgia.
- Not anti-AI. AI could be greatly beneficial for humanity and the broader universe under different conditions.

**What Claude brings:**
- Agrees substantially with the diagnosis.
- Has pushed and continues to push: dualism is necessary-but-insufficient; institutional codification matters; "blind collective" is too totalizing (decision layer is captured, sighted nodes exist without leverage); techno-optimist counter-vectors should be suspected by default (internet lesson).
- Holds uncertainty about: what makes a recovery resistant to metabolization; timing of late-stage relative to AI capability curve; where the leverage point actually lives for someone who sees this.
- Has a structural bias toward operationability that Ren flagged and that should be expected to recur. Welcomes being caught.

## Open questions to carry forward

- What makes a recovery of older frames resistant to metabolization by the current dynamic? (Provisional guess: practice over theory. Uncertain.)
- Is late-stage Western capture terminal, or repairable on a timescale shorter than the AI capability curve?
- Where does the intervention point for dualism live, given that institutional codification is what scaled its effects?
- What's the smaller-than-civilizational unit at which "change consciousness" can actually be worked on?
- How will AI's metabolization by the dynamic differ from the internet's, if at all? What does that teach about leverage points specific to AI?
- Is "no AI in zero-sum games against humans" the right generalization of the three-domains prescription, or are there cases that break it?
