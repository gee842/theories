## Preserved original publications

### Lorenz 1963

Edward N. Lorenz, “Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow,” *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences* 20, 130–141 (1963). [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0469%281963%29020%3C0130%3ADNF%3E2.0.CO%3B2). The publisher PDF was inaccessible; the [University of Vermont faculty copy](https://cdanfort.w3.uvm.edu/research/lorenz-1963.pdf) supplied the preserved [original scan](lorenz-1963.pdf), 12 pages.

**Direct visual reading:** printed pp.130, 134 and 141, saved as [page 1](lorenz-1963-page-01.png), [page 5](lorenz-1963-page-05.png) and [page 12](lorenz-1963-page-12.png). These support the model’s deterministic, bounded nonperiodic sensitivity, the finite-state limitation of fixed-precision numerical computation, and the conditional scope of long-range predictability claims. The original model does not itself measure an actual atmospheric prediction horizon. No complete-paper proof audit or empirical validation is claimed.

The attempted `pypdf` extraction yielded only page markers. That unusable derivative was removed; it is not counted as a reading. The downloaded PDF bytes remain unchanged. The three page images were rendered locally with `pypdfium2` and inspected directly.

### May 1976

Robert M. May, “Simple mathematical models with very complicated dynamics,” *Nature* 261, 459–467 (1976). [Publisher identity and abstract](https://www.nature.com/articles/261459a0). The preserved [original scan](may-1976.pdf), 9 pages, came from the [San Diego State faculty teaching archive](https://antoniop.sdsu.edu/teaching/m538/homework/Simple%20mathematical%20models%20with%20very%20complicated%20dynamics%20%28Robert%20M.%20May%29.pdf).

**Read:** extracted text corresponding to printed pp.459–461; [first page](may-1976-page-01.png), printed p.459, also rendered and visually inspected. The [reading text](may-1976.reading.txt) is a full-file machine extraction and can contain OCR, ordering and mathematical transcription errors; its existence does not mean every page was examined. The integration uses the limited claim that simple deterministic nonlinear difference equations can admit different dynamical regimes. It does not adopt all historical mathematical conjectures or generalizations in this review.
