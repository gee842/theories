## Valkey: continuity, reorganization and actual rule revision

The [Linux Foundation launch statement](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-launches-open-source-valkey-community), preserved as [HTML](valkey-launch.html), dates the new project to 28 March 2024 and identifies Redis 7.2.4 as its code starting point. Existing contributors formed the new technical leadership and development arrangement. This supports continuity through a changed organizational relation; it does not identify the effect of governance separately from personnel, sponsorship or inherited code. The announcement is an interested participant's account, not an independent comparative evaluation.

Read the entire [before](valkey-governance-before.md) and [after](valkey-governance-after.md) governance documents, pinned respectively to commits `4f61034934cf165163ef272e5795bccadc288b09` and `cd6faaa726791447f7adc196b6c77cc20658112d`. The latter introduced a two-support fallback for technical major decisions after two weeks without opposition, and distinguished governance decisions. **The earlier document already required two-thirds approval for amendment and TSC membership.** It would be incorrect to describe this as newly raising all governance decisions from simple majority.

The [pull request](https://github.com/valkey-io/valkey/pull/2927) has [metadata](valkey-pr2927.json), [review records](valkey-pr2927-reviews.json) and [discussion](valkey-pr2927-comments.json). All returned entries were inspected for adoption evidence and limitations. The proposal was revised in discussion and merged on 12 December 2025. A participant explicitly requested four votes to meet the existing two-thirds requirement. The title/body are not authoritative substitutes for the final merged text; the initial body retained wording about nontechnical simple majority that the merged document does not implement.

The public PR spans 11–12 December, while the governance text specifies a two-week voting opportunity. This packet does **not** establish whether an earlier process, notice, interpretation or exception accounts for the timing. It proves a documented proposal/revision/merge history, not complete procedural compliance. Review clicks also are not automatically an independently reconstructed membership-qualified vote count. No later technical PR using the fallback is audited here.

The Lean model fixes the majority denominator to the full recorded roster. The text's phrase about voting members does not by itself settle every operational denominator question. Consensus, notice, valid opposition by an absent officeholder, employment caps, private membership deliberations, software quality, actual access and enforcement need additional correspondence. The model's illustrative IDs are not the project's real participants, and its handoff is not an observed second Valkey fork.

## Strong theoretical comparators

[Pentland and Feldman (2008), *Designing routines*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infoandorg.2008.08.001), [author-hosted original PDF](pentland-feldman2008.pdf): read pp. 235–248, including the implementation case, artifacts/routines distinction, narrative networks and design discussion. Their account already treats routines as generative patterns of interdependent activity, with endogenous variation. It expressly rejects inferring enacted coordination from designed artifacts alone. This is a strong antecedent, not an inert routine theory defeated by our example. The formal history below isolates one institutional mechanism and leaves embodied interpretation and improvisation largely supplied.

[Winter and Szulanski (2001), *Replication as Strategy*](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.12.6.730.10084): publisher abstract read through the web tool; direct download returned HTTP 403, so no local full-text original is claimed. Its abstract distinguishes developing/refining an organizational model from its later replication and emphasizes broad knowledge transfer. Full-paper hypotheses and empirical material were not audited here. This bounded reading supplies comparative credit, not a reproduction of their results.

No interviews, full repository mining, productivity estimates or causal identification were conducted.
