## Developmental formation and inheritance

Ratcliff, Fankhauser, Rogers, Greig and Travisano (2015), *Origins of multicellular evolvability in snowflake yeast*, Nature Communications 6:6102. [Publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms7102), [preserved PMC HTML](ratcliff2015.html), DOI 10.1038/ncomms7102.

Read the introduction's developmental account, genetic-basis results, relevant figure caption and associated discussion. The knockout and complementation intervention supports a causal developmental mechanism. ACE2 coding changes were found in five of ten evolved lines; the study does not justify assigning that identical mutation to every line. Its genealogical bottleneck argument is not a claim that every physical propagule has exactly one cell. We did not rerun the genomic analysis, estimate heritability again or reproduce the source's developmental model. Common founding ancestry also does not prohibit subsequent mutation or establish universal absence of conflict.

## Stability after changed scaffolding

Doulcier, Takacs, Hammerschmidt and Bourrat (2024), *Stability of ecologically scaffolded traits during evolutionary transitions in individuality*, Nature Communications 15:6566. [Publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-50625-1), [preserved full-text XML](doulcier2024.xml), DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-50625-1.

Read the introduction, stochastic metapopulation model, formal conditions for trait endogenisation, endogenised-traits section and discussion. The model already supplies a conditional explanation of evolutionary stability after altered scaffolding, including failure and reversal. Its specified resources, replenishment, sensing, colonization and bottlenecks remain modeling commitments. Changing the focal scaffold parameter does not remove all organizing conditions. We did not reproduce the simulations, check the supplementary proofs in Lean or download the code archive. This is a strong theoretical comparator, not empirical confirmation of our reconstruction model.
