**Herron, Zamani-Dahaj and Ratcliff 2018**, *Trait heritability in major transitions*, BMC Biology 16:145, DOI [10.1186/s12915-018-0612-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12915-018-0612-6). [Preserved publisher HTML](herron2018.html). Read the abstract, analytical model, simulation results and discussion. The paper derives collective/particle heritability relations for clonal populations and explores nonlinear mappings and variable group size. Its qualified result refutes treating collective heredity as necessarily unavailable. The study's q is a developmental routing fraction, not this paper's variance-based heritability. Supplementary simulations and proofs were not rerun.

**Pichugin, Peña, Rainey and Traulsen 2017**, *Fragmentation modes and the evolution of life cycles*, PLOS Computational Biology 13:e1005860, DOI [10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005860](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005860). [Preserved publisher XML](pichugin2017.xml). Read the abstract, group formation/fragmentation model and growth/dominance analysis. Division generates groups; fragmentation patterns determine life cycles; a matrix describes their population growth. This is a direct conventional precedent for selection on reproductive organization. Our discrete two-state model is not a replication of its continuous group-size equations.

**Pichugin and Traulsen 2020**, *Evolution of multicellular life cycles under costly fragmentation*, PLOS Computational Biology 16:e1008406, DOI [10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008406](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008406). [Preserved publisher XML](pichugin2020.xml). Read the abstract, development/death model, cost definitions and selected result/discussion passages. Costs include delay, risk and loss, with different consequences for life-cycle selection. Our f=F-k is a declared narrow cost model, not an equivalence to all three. We did not rerun its numerical search or verify its supplementary results in Lean.
