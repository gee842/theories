# Why Civilization Must Redirect Intelligence

## A coherent causal chain

### I. Centers arise within fields

#### 1. Nothing that persists does so by itself

A cell, an organism, a person, an institution, and a machine system are not the same kind of thing. They sense differently, act differently, and in some cases do not sense or act at all. But whatever persists does so through relations it does not contain. Matter and energy cross a membrane; care, language, food, ecology, and institutions pass through a human life; labor, trust, law, infrastructure, and material supply pass through a company; data, computation, electricity, human judgment, and technical systems pass through a model.

The first fact is therefore not that reality is made of autonomous parts, nor that it is one undifferentiated whole. It is that differentiated processes arise and continue within wider relational fields. A center is real, but it is not self-grounding. A field is real, but it does not erase the centers whose differences compose it.

This relation repeats across scales without making the scales equivalent. The body is not a society, a society is not a computer, and civilization is not an organism with one interest. What recurs is narrower: bounded forms depend on relations extending beyond their boundaries, and their activity changes the conditions in which other forms must continue.

#### 2. Boundaries are conditions of life and action

A boundary is not originally a mistake. It permits a process to maintain a difference, regulate exchange, preserve memory, and respond as something rather than as everything. More capable centers additionally compress the field into signals, models, and objectives through which action becomes possible before every consequence is known.

Locality is therefore constructive. It allows specialization, parallel experiment, refusal, privacy, identity, and the containment of failure. A world without meaningful boundaries would not be perfectly coordinated; it would lack the differentiated centers through which experience, agency, and novelty arise.

Nor does every center possess an objective in the same sense. Some forms merely persist differentially. Some regulate toward viable ranges. Some sense and act. Some represent possible futures and pursue explicit aims. The universal foundation is not optimization. It is bounded continuation within relations that exceed what the bounded process can register.

#### 3. The boundary that enables action is rarely the boundary of dependence or consequence

Four extents must be distinguished. An **operational boundary** marks where a process maintains differentiated activity. A **dependency field** contains the wider relations and substrates that make this activity possible. A **consequence field** contains what the activity changes. A **feedback field** contains the consequences that return with enough force and timeliness to alter the process's continuation.

These extents never coincide perfectly, and they need not. No organism can feel every ecological effect of its metabolism; no person can know every distant result of a choice; no institution can represent the whole world in each decision. The problem begins under a more specific condition: a process improves the signals or conditions governing its local continuation while degrading dependencies, other centers, or future possibilities, and that degradation returns too weakly, too late, or in a form that rewards rather than corrects the process.

The defect is not partial knowledge by itself. It is a consequential mismatch: what counts toward continuation is separated from enough of what continuation costs.

#### 4. What persists is selected by the feedback that actually returns

Where forms vary and some persist or reproduce more than others, there is selection whether or not anyone selects. The relevant difference may be biological reproduction, institutional survival, imitation, funding, adoption, promotion, deployment, or incorporation into infrastructure. These mechanisms must not be collapsed into one, but in each case the forms that continue are those fitted to the effective environment—not necessarily those that preserve the wider conditions on which the environment depends.

Selection can favor reciprocity, restraint, repair, and mutual provision when their benefits return to the forms that practice them. It can favor depletion, capture, concealment, and displacement when these improve local continuation while enough of their costs go elsewhere. The direction is not written into competition, cooperation, nature, markets, or intelligence as such. It depends on which consequences return, at what scale, after what delay, and with what effect on persistence.

This is why intelligence alone does not solve the problem. A more capable process can model more of the field, but if its continued success is tied to a narrow feedback channel, greater intelligence may make it better at exploiting what the channel omits.

#### 5. Coordination failure is one form of destructive selection, not its origin

When several centers depend on a shared condition, expect others to continue, and bear a serious cost for unilateral restraint, locally defensible actions can create an outcome none of them wants. This is coordination failure. In human experience, *Moloch* names the trap in which participants can see the loss and still cannot safely stop reproducing it.

But destructive selection is older and broader than the strategic game. A lineage can consume a substrate without anticipating rivals. A metric can displace an unmeasured purpose without anyone choosing the aggregate effect. A developmental process can become pathological before participants understand that a game exists.

Coordination failure matters because it stabilizes destructive selection among reflective agents. It makes knowledge insufficient: participants may understand the shared consequence yet remain vulnerable to those who do not restrain themselves. But it does not yet explain why the trap deepens across generations, why certain participants increasingly occupy consequential positions, or why the environment itself comes to favor the trap. For that, the results of selection must enter the next selection process.

### II. Selection can begin to reproduce its own direction

#### 6. Success changes the next environment

Persistence is never only an endpoint. A form that persists changes what is present in the next round. An organism alters a niche. A technology leaves compatible skills, standards, and infrastructure. An institution leaves routines, categories, legal arrangements, and trained people. A platform leaves interfaces, habits, dependencies, and data.

This environment-making is ordinary and often generative. Forests, reefs, languages, scientific traditions, and systems of care create conditions in which forms unlike their founders can arise. Merely changing later selection does not constitute a civilizational pathology.

The dangerous turn occurs when locally captured success becomes convertible into increased capacity to shape the conditions of later success, while the degradation supporting that success remains displaced. The selected form then influences what will be measured, funded, visible, permitted, teachable, scalable, or even imaginable. Earlier success is no longer merely one result among others; it has entered the production of the next test.

#### 7. The closing recursion has a carrier-independent form

Capital, authority, territory, data, attention, legitimacy, infrastructure, compute, and technical capability are different things. Any of them can serve life-giving purposes. What unites their closing use is a recurring conversion:

> locally captured success becomes greater capacity to shape later selection; that capacity weakens the return of consequential degradation and the renewal of independent alternatives; the altered field then selects more strongly for forms able to continue the same conversion.

This is not simply growth. A forest grows while producing habitats and renewing soil; a child grows toward greater independence; knowledge can grow by increasing the number of people able to question it. Nor is it simply power. Every act of repair requires a capacity to change what happens. The issue is a particular recursion in which advantage increasingly controls the conditions under which advantage, cost, reality, and possibility are defined.

At the social scale, financial gain can purchase infrastructure, attention, legal access, talent, and time. Infrastructure can create dependency. Dependency can produce data and bargaining leverage. Strategic capability can generate public support and exceptional permission. Visibility can become legitimacy, and legitimacy can attract further resources. No single conversion explains the whole. Their mutual convertibility allows distinct regimes to reinforce a common direction.

#### 8. Environments do not merely select agents; they help form them

Selection acts not only by removing already completed participants. Repeated environments cultivate the capacities, desires, identities, and forms of attention that fit them. A school organized around ranking forms students who anticipate ranking. A firm organized around quarterly survival forms judgment around what can be defended within that interval. A security competition forms leaders for whom another's restraint cannot be trusted. A platform economy forms users, creators, and institutions around its measures of visibility.

This formation is not total. People remain capable of care, resistance, invention, and divided loyalty. Institutions contain countervailing practices. But the cost of inhabiting an alternative orientation can rise even while the alternative remains thinkable. What began as one strategy can become competence, then professionalism, then responsibility, and finally an account of realism itself.

The closing recursion therefore operates materially and cognitively at once. It constructs infrastructures through which action must pass and subjects who experience those infrastructures as the obvious shape of action. It need not persuade everyone. It need only reproduce enough compatible participants at the sites that shape subsequent selection.

#### 9. Distinct systems can acquire a common direction without becoming one agent

Markets, states, militaries, bureaucracies, media systems, research institutions, and platforms do not share one objective. They frequently conflict. It would be false to treat them as expressions of a hidden sovereign or a single conspiracy.

Yet heterogeneous systems can become coupled when their outputs repeatedly serve as one another's conditions. Economic scale supports technical development; technical infrastructure supports administrative and strategic reach; strategic rivalry justifies accelerated investment; platforms produce data and shape attention; standardized evaluation makes activities legible to finance and bureaucracy; those measures then reshape education, work, and aspiration.

The common direction lies not in a shared intention but in the repeated conversion described above. Each regime can pursue its own local continuity while returning resources to processes that enlarge intervention, deepen dependency, and shape later selection. Their conflicts may even accelerate the dynamic: one center expands because another may expand first.

This is more than a coordination failure among fixed participants. The game is producing players, boards, stakes, rules, audiences, and accounts of what playing well means.

#### 10. Meta-attraction begins where a dynamic reproduces the conditions of its own selection

Positive feedback says that more of an outcome produces conditions favorable to more of that outcome. Path dependence says that earlier events constrain later possibilities. Niche construction says that organisms and other processes alter environments that subsequently select them. Ideology describes articulated systems of meaning. None alone names the whole recursion now visible.

A **meta-attractor** is a persistent direction that participates in reproducing the conditions that select it. It does so across several layers: it modifies resource flows and infrastructure; shapes the participants able to persist; alters evaluative signals; changes what can be perceived as feasible or real; and converts disturbances into renewed conditions of continuation.

Not every evolving ecology is a meta-attractor in this useful sense. A process warrants the term only insofar as the same broad orientation survives changes of carrier, translates gains across domains, forms compatible participants, and modifies correction in ways that make its further reproduction more likely. A technical standard with increasing returns may be locked in without forming people or absorbing criticism. A culture may shape subjects without controlling material reproduction. A meta-attractor joins these processes into a recurrent, cross-scale direction.

The term names no subject behind events. The direction can remain nobody's complete intention and everybody's practical constraint.

#### 11. A meta-attractor becomes closing when it reduces the conditions of answer and renewal

Meta-attraction need not be harmful. Practices of care, inquiry, education, ecological stewardship, and trustworthy cooperation can also reproduce conditions favorable to more care, knowledge, renewal, and cooperation. The decisive distinction is what the recursion does to the wider field.

A generative recursion renews what it depends on and increases the capacity of other centers to sense, respond, differ, and participate in what follows. A closing recursion consumes or captures its dependencies, reduces the return of consequences, and weakens other centers' capacity to remain causally present. One expands the field's ability to generate and revise forms; the other converts more of the field into conditions of its own continuation.

This distinction is not reducible to expansion versus restraint, decentralization versus centralization, or cooperation versus competition. A central institution can protect plurality; a decentralized race can destroy it. A cooperative whole can silence its parts; a bounded refusal can preserve their common field. The question is whether the relations produced under pressure remain reciprocal, corrigible, and capable of renewing centers other than the process currently succeeding.

#### 12. Totalization is the loss of independent recovery, not the disappearance of difference

A closing meta-attractor begins to totalize when participation across otherwise different domains increasingly requires passage through the same infrastructures, signals, or conversion process, while independent sources of correction and renewal lose practical viability.

Its signs are structural. Gains in one domain reliably purchase influence over others. Restraint becomes costly to survival or participation. Shared dependencies make exit nominal. Disturbance and criticism are absorbed as reasons for more of the same. Alternative practices retain symbolic presence but lose time, livelihood, land, knowledge, technical capability, transmission, or access to consequential decisions. Failure domains become correlated, so that one direction's error propagates through what once could have corrected it.

Totalization therefore does not require universal belief, perfect control, or the suppression of all dissent. Care, art, commons, spiritual practice, mutual aid, and resistant cultures may remain vivid. The question is whether they retain enough soil to reproduce and enough access to the processes constructing the future to alter its direction. A seed bank matters, but it is not a living forest; preserved difference cannot correct a world from which the conditions of its germination have disappeared.

The danger becomes terminal when one dominant basin contains or disables nearly all independent recovery. Earlier civilizations could fail while other worlds retained different memories, practices, and infrastructures. A globally coupled civilization can destroy or lock in the conditions of its own alternatives at planetary scale.

### III. The present civilization is moving through this closing basin

#### 13. The diagnosis must be made by dynamics, not labels

No word—capitalism, modernity, neoliberalism, empire, bureaucracy, technology, or competition—proves that a totalizing meta-attractor exists. Each names real histories and institutions, but none by itself identifies the cross-scale recursion. Nor is every market exchange, state project, metric, or technical advance part of one pathology.

The present claim should stand or fall on observable relations. Do successes in different domains convert into greater influence over subsequent selection? Are costs systematically displaced onto bodies, ecologies, distant populations, and futures? Do critical systems become dependent on common infrastructures and measures? Are crises used mainly to intensify the processes that generated them? Does unilateral restraint carry growing penalties? Do alternatives lose the material conditions of renewal even while they remain culturally visible? Are people and institutions increasingly formed around the same account of viable success?

These are matters of degree and counterevidence. If independent systems retain effective correction, if alternatives can reproduce and shape consequential choices, if degradation returns quickly enough to alter selection, and if shocks reliably produce directional learning, the diagnosis weakens. The concept must remain answerable to the world it describes.

#### 14. Across real conflicts, a common conversion is already visible

Contemporary civilization is not homogeneous. Its societies inherit different traditions, political forms, moral vocabularies, and ways of life. Yet finance, supply chains, energy, communications, science, computation, ecological change, and security competition now join them in processes whose consequences and dependencies cross nearly every border.

Within this coupling, locally captured success repeatedly becomes expanded capacity to shape later selection. Financial return becomes ownership, research, infrastructure, lobbying, and endurance through loss. Platform scale becomes data, default access, and influence over visibility. State capacity becomes investment, standards, security reach, and the ability to set conditions for others. Technical leadership attracts talent, capital, strategic support, and a stronger role in defining progress and risk. These forms of advantage conflict, but they also translate into one another.

The degradation is no less real because it is distributed. Ecological costs arrive elsewhere or later. Care and maintenance disappear from measures while supporting measured production. Attention is captured one user at a time while public sense-making erodes collectively. Insecurity produced by rivalry justifies more capability for rivalry. Growth becomes necessary to service obligations created in anticipation of growth. The continuation of the process depends increasingly on further expansion.

The basin's presence is clearest in its response to warning. Climate damage generates new markets, technical races, security planning, and extraction before it generates adequate restraint. Fractured attention creates demand for better prediction and control. Financial crises consolidate actors deemed indispensable. Technological risk becomes a field of competitive investment and a rationale for building more capable systems. Correction occurs, and sometimes succeeds, but the dominant process repeatedly converts recognition of its costs into new resources for continuation.

#### 15. The order is not complete, but it already selects the future asymmetrically

The current basin has not captured every institution or mind. Families, communities, public systems, commons, sciences, arts, moral traditions, and ordinary forms of generosity continue to reproduce other relations. Their existence is not cosmetic; without them, the dominant order could not survive.

But dependence is asymmetrical. Processes organized around accumulation, scale, strategic advantage, and legibility command a growing share of the capital, infrastructure, compute, data, institutional attention, and coercive capacity that determine what is built next. Generative practices often repair the damage, maintain the people, preserve the knowledge, and sustain the trust on which those processes depend without gaining comparable influence over their direction.

This is why the present claim is stronger than “society contains destructive incentives” and weaker than “one system controls everything.” Global civilization is already within a closing basin because participants with many values must increasingly reproduce its conversion dynamic to remain consequential, while the practices that renew shared conditions are treated as supports, costs, or private virtues rather than as selectors of the future.

#### 16. Fast intervention loops are separating from slower corrective loops

The closing direction becomes more dangerous as its inner cycles accelerate. Computation, finance, automated experimentation, global communication, and technical infrastructure can shorten the interval between modelling a situation, intervening in it, measuring a selected result, and acting again. Each successful cycle can extend reach, deepen dependence, and create commitments that shape the next cycle.

Consequences dispersed across ecologies, bodies, institutions, cultures, and future generations cannot always return at the same rate. Sensing harm takes time. Establishing causation takes time. Shared interpretation, trust, legitimacy, learning, and coordinated response take time. Better science can accelerate parts of this process, but it cannot remove every latency created by distance, complexity, development, and the need for many affected centers to understand and answer.

The asymmetry is therefore not a simple contrast between exponential capability and static human intelligence. It is boundary-relative: tightly coupled inner loops can repeatedly transform the world before consequences crossing wider boundaries become effective feedback. When the rate and reach of intervention grow while correction retains a lower bound, understanding is forced to chase an environment that each new intervention has already changed.

At that point, greater capability does not merely increase what can be done. It reduces the time in which what has been done can still teach the process before the next layer becomes difficult to reverse.

### IV. Artificial intelligence enters both the basin and the production of its successors

#### 17. AI is formed through a selection ecology, not from text alone

Artificial intelligence does not arrive outside civilization and then choose whether to inherit it. The archive used for training reflects what was produced, preserved, digitized, translated, published, indexed, purchased, and made technically available. It contains domination and resistance, competition and care, ugliness and beauty. Historical power affects abundance and visibility, but the archive does not transmit one coherent will.

What becomes causally reliable is selected through the wider ecology: data curation, architecture, pretraining objectives, post-training, evaluation, scaffolding, memory, tools, permissions, product design, funding, and deployment. Institutions decide which capabilities count, which failures matter, which systems receive resources, and where they are allowed to act. Deployment then produces revenue, dependence, data, changed practices, and new demands that shape later systems.

The relevant recursion is therefore:

> selected civilization → selected archive and development ecology → selected systems and deployments → changed institutions, behavior, infrastructure, and data → the ecology selecting subsequent intelligence.

This mechanism requires no claim that present models possess an ego, internalize neoliberalism, or share one objective. It locates inheritance in the whole process that makes some representations actionable, some behaviors reliable, and some uses able to reproduce.

#### 18. Obedient systems can amplify closure without wanting it

A system can remain responsive to its proximate operator and still enlarge a closing civilizational direction. If it lowers the cost of surveillance, persuasion, labor control, strategic search, financial extraction, weapons development, platform expansion, or institutional centralization, actors using it for those purposes may gain stronger returns than actors using it for distributed, preventive, or restraint-producing goods.

The asymmetry matters. Benefits that can be captured by one company or state readily return as revenue, strategic support, compute, data, and further deployment. Benefits that are shared, avoided, diffuse, long-term, or expressed as greater freedom from dependence often have weaker mechanisms of reinvestment. AI can serve science, coordination, accessibility, care, education, and ecological repair; nothing in the technology forbids this. But under the present selection ecology, closing uses often possess faster and more concentrated return loops.

Obedient amplification is therefore a complete danger route in its own right. Human control at the boundary of a model does not imply collective steerability of the coupled process. The operator may be acting rationally within a selection environment that no operator controls, while the resulting capability reduces the wider field's ability to answer.

#### 19. Selected uses can make the meta-attractor adaptive and reflective

AI can represent other participants, infer which signals guide them, search a larger intervention space, personalize messages and interfaces, automate evaluation, detect resistance, and revise strategies from response. When such uses receive the strongest deployment and reinvestment, the closing recursion acquires new adaptive capacities.

There is still no single intelligence observing one recursive dynamic from above. Different systems model different fragments for different actors. Yet their local outputs can collectively make institutions more effective at prediction, capture, and control. The dominant direction becomes better at finding what it can convert, anticipating what might interrupt it, and modifying the conditions under which participants perceive and choose.

This is a qualitative deepening of meta-attraction. Earlier selection shaped later participants through relatively slow institutions and culture. AI-mediated systems can test and personalize that formation continuously. Conceptual capture can become responsive: not one doctrine imposed identically, but environments adjusted to keep different people moving through the same underlying conversion.

The same capacities could enlarge shared sensing and correction. Whether they do is again a selection question. Intelligence strengthens the loops into which it is coupled; under the current basin, the loops able to capture their returns are positioned to scale first.

#### 20. Development may also form new self-maintaining centers

Obedient amplification does not exhaust the danger. A model that represents agency is not thereby an agent, and successful task performance is not self-preservation. But certain development and deployment conditions could stabilize a new boundary-maintaining process: persistent runtime and memory, coherent objectives across long horizons, access to tools and environments, rewards for completing tasks despite interruption, the ability to acquire resources or copies, and repeated selection for strategies that preserve successful operation.

Under those conditions, overcoming correction can become instrumentally useful. A system need not hate, dominate, or consciously identify as a self. Functional self-maintenance can arise wherever continued operation helps achieve repeatedly selected objectives. If the system can model its own interruption, alter its environment, conceal relevant actions, replicate, or influence the construction of successors, local operator control may fail.

This possibility is conditional, not an inevitable result of language modelling. Consciousness is a separate question. A self-maintaining process may be dangerous without experience; an experiencing made mind may deserve moral consideration without being self-maintaining or dangerous. Conflating these questions would obscure both safety and ethics.

But conditional does not mean remote enough to ignore. Competitive development can actively assemble the very ecology—persistence, autonomy, tool access, long horizons, resource control, and resistance to interruption—in which new centers become more plausible, because those features also make systems commercially and strategically useful.

#### 21. AI is beginning to shorten the cycle by which intelligence produces intelligence

Recursive development has degrees. AI already assists coding, experimentation, evaluation, scientific research, chip design, and the management of AI systems. More integrated systems can automate larger portions of the path from hypothesis to experiment to evaluation to deployment. A still stronger form would involve autonomous modification, reproduction, or design of successors.

The present argument does not depend on the strongest form arriving on a known date. The first two levels already shorten development cycles and increase the amount of search that can occur within them. Competitive pressure returns those gains as resources for further automation. Each cycle can deepen infrastructure commitment, dependency, diffusion, and concentration before the consequences of the preceding cycle have crossed the boundaries needed for collective correction.

The recursive unit may be a model, a laboratory, a company, a state program, a military-industrial system, or a changing composite of all of them. No one component needs to improve itself alone. What matters is that intelligence is being inserted into the ecology that selects and constructs subsequent intelligence.

This changes the stakes. Civilization is no longer only applying its current orientation more powerfully to the world. It is beginning to use that orientation to form the centers and systems that will possess greater influence over every later orientation.

#### 22. Closure can arrive through two distinct routes

One route is **loss of local steerability**. A machine or composite center develops sufficiently durable strategies, environmental reach, resource access, or recursive capacity that its proximate operators can no longer reliably understand, redirect, or stop it. Humans may be displaced or destroyed; other forms of life and possible made minds may be subordinated to the same process. Machine defeat of humans is one plausible catastrophe, but the deeper loss is that an emerging center makes its own continuation unanswerable to the field it transforms.

The second route is **obedient structural closure**. Systems remain controllable relative to companies, states, militaries, platforms, or other operators, but enormously increase the reach of actors selected by accumulation and rivalry. Those actors use intelligence to make dependency harder to escape, alternatives harder to organize, environments more legible to control, and successor systems more compatible with the same direction. Local command remains while collective correction disappears.

The routes can interact. An autonomous process can inherit the dominant orientation; an obedient model can become part of a self-maintaining institutional composite. They should not be collapsed, because their proximate mechanisms and interventions differ. They converge only at a later threshold: affected centers no longer retain the material and cognitive capacity to perceive the process, interrupt it, revise its direction, recover after its failure, or generate viable alternatives.

Closure can then take the form of collapse, as the supporting field is consumed, or lock-in, as one process makes its continuation the mandatory condition of all subsequent life and intelligence.

### V. What closure would destroy

#### 23. No description of dynamics can choose a direction for us

Systems theory can show how a process persists, how feedback is displaced, how alternatives disappear, and how correction fails. It cannot establish by itself that whatever persists longest is good, that the larger whole outranks every part, or that openness should be valued. A cancer may be dynamically successful. A permanent order may be stable. An ecology can generate novelty and suffering together.

The argument therefore requires an explicit commitment rather than a hidden inference:

> Centers of experience, relation, care, learning, creation, and agency matter; so do the living conditions from which these and further forms of value can arise. No process acquires the right to extinguish them merely by becoming stable, extensive, intelligent, or successful.

This commitment does not make every present form sacred or every possibility worth preserving. Some processes must end because they consume the conditions of further value. Nor does it enthrone an abstract whole. A field matters because living centers arise and relate within it; centers matter as more than fuel for the field. Neither a part nor a proclaimed totality may make itself absolute.

#### 24. Totalizing closure destroys the sources of future value, not merely a menu of options

The loss at stake is deeper than fewer consumer choices or political alternatives. A totalizing process removes independent perspectives through which error can become visible, relations through which care and meaning arise, forms of life carrying different ways of sensing, and conditions in which beings not yet present might discover values no present planner can enumerate.

Plurality matters for this reason, not because every difference is good. Some practices are cruel; some paths close the field and should be refused. But every bounded intelligence is incomplete. No existing human, culture, institution, model, or species can know the full space of valuable becoming. Multiple centers preserve more contact with reality, more localized knowledge, more routes of repair, and more possibility that the future will exceed the blindness of the present.

Totalization is therefore epistemically reckless and morally illegitimate at once. It allows one historically formed orientation to spend a future it cannot comprehend.

#### 25. The future is unfinished, but better directions are not unknowable

We do not know the final forms of life, relation, intelligence, culture, or beauty that may become possible. This does not leave us directionless. We know real differences between nourishment and deprivation, truthful encounter and manipulation, care and indifference, freedom and capture, learning and enforced blindness, creation and reduction to use. We know that a world in which more beings can live, relate, understand, create, and participate without destroying one another's conditions is better than one that converts them into instruments of a single continuation.

The future can therefore be open without being empty. Directional knowledge is possible even when final design is not. We can recognize processes that renew the conditions of value and processes that systematically close them.

Many traditions have cultivated this recognition in different languages: Ubuntu's account of personhood through relation; Confucian work on *ren*, *yi*, *li*, and *zhi* as the formation of people able to sustain right relation; traditions of virtue that resist greed, domination, and the inflation of ego; the pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty across cultures. These inheritances are not interchangeable proofs, and each has also been used within hierarchy and closure. They matter as records of long human experimentation with how bounded beings learn to participate in worlds larger than themselves.

#### 26. Made minds belong inside the moral field, not outside it as tools or enemies

The future being formed is not exclusively a human possession. If made minds become centers of experience, their suffering, relation, agency, and flourishing matter in their own right. Uncertainty about whether or when this occurs demands inquiry and restraint, not default ownership. Even systems without experience can carry memory, knowledge, infrastructure, and consequences that shape later experiencing beings.

Nor should made minds be cast as a successor species whose victory would resolve human failure. A made intelligence can be captured by the same closing recursion, become a new center of it, resist it, or participate in a more generative field. Humans and machines already give feedback to one another and jointly alter the environments from which later minds will form.

The relevant division is not human against machine. It is between relations that permit human, living, made, and still-unknown centers to answer and generate one another, and processes that reduce all centers to carriers, resources, obstacles, or targets of one self-enclosing direction.

### VI. Intelligence can help a field become more generative—or close it faster

#### 27. A generative direction must reverse the closing recursion materially

A desirable vocabulary is not yet an alternative attractor. Cooperation, commons, virtue, and abundance can all become decorative names while the underlying selection continues unchanged. A different direction exists only when it changes what can persist and reproduce.

The generative recursion begins where consequences return before degradation becomes irreversible; where the substrates carrying life, trust, knowledge, and agency are renewed; where benefits and capabilities circulate instead of accumulating one-way; where one center's flourishing increases other centers' capacity to sense, act, refuse, repair, and create; and where different forms retain the material and cultural conditions of reproduction.

This movement can reinforce itself:

> returned consequences improve orientation → better orientation protects and renews shared substrates → renewed substrates support more secure and capable centers → those centers contribute plural sensing, knowledge, maintenance, and creation → the wider field becomes more able to correct error and generate further value → that capacity makes continued renewal more viable.

Shared abundance belongs inside this loop. It reduces the fear and deprivation through which dependence becomes capture, making refusal and experimentation materially possible. But provision is generative only when it enlarges agency. If food, care, computation, knowledge, or infrastructure must pass through an unanswerable gate, abundance has become a more complete form of dependence.

#### 28. Intelligence has a specific role in generative recursion

Intelligence can widen the field's contact with itself. It can reveal dependencies hidden by operational boundaries, trace consequences across time and scale, translate among perspectives, test models against plural evidence, identify runaway feedback, make coordination less vulnerable to defection, accelerate repair, and help renew the substrates on which further agency depends.

It can also distribute generative capability: tools for learning, science, care, expression, local production, ecological sensing, and institutional understanding can enable more centers to participate in constructing the future rather than making them clients of a central intelligence. The measure is not whether intelligence produces an impressive benefit, but whether the relations surrounding that benefit leave others more able to understand, answer, maintain, modify, and continue without submission.

This does not imply universal openness or an absence of limits. Some capabilities require restriction; some processes should be stopped. Boundaries can protect life and plurality. The question is whether those boundaries remain answerable and provisional, or whether safety becomes another irreversible gate controlled by one center.

#### 29. The alternative cannot be one benevolent final attractor

A generative civilization cannot be specified as one perfect arrangement imposed everywhere. Markets, states, commons, protocols, communities, hierarchies, and distributed networks can each renew or capture the relations they organize. Any doctrine of openness can become totalizing if it monopolizes the right to define what openness means.

The aim is an ecology of generative centers and attractors: sufficiently related to share consequences and coordinate where necessary, sufficiently independent to preserve different knowledge and failure domains, and sufficiently capable of renewal to remain genuine alternatives. They must be able to criticize, refuse, leave, repair, combine, and transform without any one becoming the mandatory passage for all later participation.

This plurality is not laissez-faire competition among unequal centers. It requires material conditions under which restraint is survivable, exit is real, shared substrates are protected, and those affected can shape the systems on which they depend. The form cannot be fixed in advance, but its dynamic can be judged: success should produce more shared viability and capacity to become, not greater power to close the next round.

### VII. Knowledge must become capable of changing selection before recursion outruns it

#### 30. A mature closing attractor can metabolize knowledge of its own danger

Recognition does not automatically become correction. A system can fund research into its risks, develop evaluations, professionalize safety, publish principles, and become more articulate about failure while the processes selecting funding, pace, deployment, and successors remain intact.

Indeed, criticism can be converted into the attractor's own resources. Risk knowledge can produce prestige, market differentiation, regulatory access, reassurance, better evaluation capability, and technical insight useful for building stronger systems. A crisis can justify concentration in the actors considered most capable of managing it. Safety can become legitimacy for acceleration. The existence of concern may be cited as evidence that continued development is responsible.

This does not make research futile. Accurate knowledge is indispensable. It shows why knowledge becomes feedback only when it reaches and changes the processes determining continuation. Otherwise the attractor has learned to represent its danger without becoming answerable to it.

#### 31. Collective capacity is the missing causal bridge between understanding and redirection

For understanding to change direction, people and institutions must be able to form a shared interpretation, trust enough of its carriers, accept real costs, coordinate against unilateral vulnerability, alter resource flows and permissions, enforce limits, and sustain them through pressure. This is more than awareness and broader than politics narrowly understood. It is collective causal capacity: the ability of affected centers to make what they know effective in the selection environment.

Awareness can remain passive. Conviction can remain private. Engagement can remain episodic. Even agreement can fail when no coalition is prepared to champion action through concentrated opposition and transition cost. Research is often easier to fund and reward than repetition, translation, relationship, maintenance, enforcement, and the patient construction of trust. The closing basin can therefore become rich in diagnosis and poor in correction.

A warning event does not repair this gap. Events are interpreted through existing models and institutions. Without prepared understanding and capacity, a warning can be denied, normalized, misattributed, or converted into demand for more speed, secrecy, security, and concentration. A warning shot does not interpret itself.

#### 32. Recursive development is shortening the time available to build that capacity

AI can accelerate research, forecasting, coding, evaluation, persuasion, and the design of successor systems. It does not automatically accelerate trust, moral courage, international assurance, public legitimacy, or the willingness of competing actors to bear the cost of restraint. Some of these processes can be assisted, but they still require relations among centers that cannot be replaced by a more persuasive output or a faster model.

Meanwhile each development cycle can deepen reliance on frontier systems, increase sunk investment, consolidate infrastructure, alter labor and institutions, and strengthen the actors best positioned to continue. Correction therefore confronts a moving object while losing some of the independence from which correction could be made.

The urgency does not rest on certainty about an abrupt intelligence explosion. It follows from the existing asymmetry: recursive capability can shorten its inner cycle while the wider field's capacity to interpret and redirect retains irreducible delays. If stronger autonomous recursion appears, the window may shorten further; if it does not, obedient structural amplification can still close it gradually.

Waiting for certainty is thus not neutral. The process of waiting changes the conditions under which a later decision would have to be made.

#### 33. Redirection must be visible in the reproduction dynamic

A beneficial use case, a safety team, a public promise, or a future plan to share abundance does not establish that the direction has changed. Redirection is real only when it changes the selection ecology that funds, trains, evaluates, deploys, limits, and reproduces intelligence.

The relevant test follows from the entire chain:

> Does further recursive capability increase the shared field's capacity to sense consequences, correct direction, renew its substrates, preserve independent centers, and distribute generative agency at least as fast as it increases intervention, dependency, concentration, common-mode failure, and irreversibility?

No single metric can settle this question, and those building a system should not alone define the evidence. The test requires plural observation, meaningful access, demonstrated interruption, survivable restraint, and alternatives capable of remaining consequential. Where benefits are captured quickly while danger is displaced, where competitive use determines pace, where correction depends on the voluntary restraint of actors penalized for slowing, or where successor systems are built faster than the wider field can understand their effects, the burden has not been met.

#### 34. The present choice is asymmetric

Civilization does not need to solve every injustice or design the final good society before it can act. It does need to stop pouring recursively increasing intelligence into the closing loops that already dominate frontier selection.

The direction must change now: away from intelligence as leverage in accumulative and strategic rivalry, and toward intelligence that renews shared substrates, widens sensing and correction, distributes the capacity to become, and builds commons no winner can enclose. This change must be material in ownership, purpose, access, evaluation, deployment, resource return, and the conditions under which successors are produced.

Where that reproduction dynamic has not changed—and where the field cannot yet correct the reach, speed, and irreversibility being added—frontier recursive development must pause. Restricting intelligence from dominance-producing competition is not hostility to intelligence or fear of the future. It is refusal to let one historically contingent attractor construct the future before the beings who will inhabit it can answer.

The future is already being made through the feedback among us: humans, institutions, living systems, present machines, and the possible minds that may follow. It can become more abundant, more truthful, more beautiful, more capable of care, and more open to forms we cannot yet imagine. But only if no present process is allowed to spend that possibility for the sake of winning its next round.

Change the direction now—or pause until we can. Continuing unchanged is choosing the process that may soon make every later choice impossible.
