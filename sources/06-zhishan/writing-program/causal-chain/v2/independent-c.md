# Why Civilization Must Redirect Intelligence

## A coherent causal chain

### 1. Reality persists through relation

Reality is not first a collection of finished things and only afterward a set of relations among them. The things that endure are themselves maintained through ongoing exchange. A cell is continuously remade through a body and an environment. A person continues through metabolism, care, memory, language, tools, institutions, and other beings. A city continues through watersheds, energy, labor, logistics, trust, maintenance, and meaning. A model continues through computation, electricity, data, interfaces, operators, and the institutions that keep it available.

These relations do not make the participants unreal. They are how distinct participants become possible. What we call a part is a relatively persistent pattern inside wider and overlapping processes. What we call a system is a boundary drawn around some of those relations for some purpose. The same process can be a part at one scale, a field for other processes at another, and a participant in several systems that do not form one clean hierarchy. There is no final boundary from which the whole of becoming can be represented without remainder.

### 2. Boundaries are achievements before they are errors

A persistent form must distinguish enough of itself from its surroundings to regulate exchange. A membrane, an immune distinction, a point of view, an organizational role, a jurisdiction, and a software interface are radically different mechanisms, but each makes some continuity and action possible by limiting what must be sensed and answered at once.

Locality is therefore not a defect that greater intelligence will abolish. Without partial boundaries there is no differentiation, no protected interior, no specialized activity, no plurality of experiment, and no failure that can remain local. An intelligence that attempted to treat everything as one object of control would not transcend locality; it would impose one locality upon everything else.

The danger begins elsewhere: a boundary adequate for one operation can be mistaken for the boundary of all that operation depends upon or changes.

### 3. Several boundaries coexist, and they often do not coincide

An **operational boundary** marks what a process can directly sense or act upon. A **dependency boundary** includes the conditions whose continuation it requires. A **consequence boundary** includes the places, times, and beings its activity changes. A **feedback boundary** marks which of those changes can return strongly and quickly enough to alter its later behavior or persistence.

No complex process can make all four boundaries identical. Consequences are uncertain; dependencies extend through histories no participant can fully know; useful action requires compression. The problem is not imperfection. It is systematic misalignment: the process is guided inside a narrow operational and feedback boundary while drawing viability from, and sending consequences into, a much wider field.

When that mismatch remains small, slow action, redundancy, repair, and learning may contain it. When the mismatch becomes a source of differential success, it begins to reorganize what persists.

### 4. Selection begins before objectives, games, or agents

Where variants continue, spread, or reproduce differently, a selection environment already exists. No conscious selector is required. Nor must the selected variants represent an objective. Chemical organizations, lineages, habits, institutions, technical standards, evaluation practices, and machine behaviors can all be preserved differentially through different mechanisms.

Selection does not discover what is best in itself. It preserves what fits the consequences that actually return through a particular environment. If damage to a shared substrate returns quickly to the process causing it, destructive variants may disappear. If a process renews conditions used by others and receives enough benefit from that renewal, mutually sustaining forms may spread. But if benefit returns locally while degradation is delayed, dispersed, hidden, or borne elsewhere, the field can select what weakens the field.

This is the first decisive point: adaptation demonstrates fit to a feedback structure, not wisdom about the larger reality in which that structure is embedded.

### 5. Displaced consequence can become selected advantage

Suppose two processes obtain a similar local benefit, but one must bear the work of renewing what it uses while the other can leave that work to another place, another time, another being, or the future. Unless the displaced consequence returns through some countervailing relation, the second process appears cheaper, faster, stronger, or more scalable. Its narrow advantage is purchased by a wider loss that the selecting environment does not fully register.

This relation appears in different substances at different scales: drawdown without regeneration, maintenance made invisible, risk transferred beyond an evaluation window, attention captured without regard for the mind that supplies it, a benchmark satisfied while off-interface effects accumulate. The mechanisms and moral responsibilities differ. The recurrent structure is that what guides persistence receives the gain more reliably than the consequence.

Not every external consequence creates such a direction. It becomes evolutionarily or institutionally important when displacement is repeatable, when it changes differential persistence, and when no equally strong process restores the missing relation.

### 6. Coordination failure is one expression of this selection structure

Among interdependent agents, the same mismatch can become a game. Several participants depend on a shared condition; each anticipates that others may continue exploiting it; unilateral restraint carries a real risk of loss. They can then reproduce an outcome none of them prefers. Moloch names the felt reality of such a trap: the race continues without requiring anyone to desire its destination.

But coordination failure is neither the origin nor the whole of the mechanism. Selection already shaped the participants, resources, and available moves before the recognizable game began. And the game does not merely repeat itself. Those who persist within it may change what later participants must be, which strategies remain available, and whether the shared condition can still be perceived.

The deeper question is therefore not only why participants cannot coordinate their departure. It is what the repeated outcome does to the field that will form the next participants.

### 7. The critical recursion begins when success alters later selection

Every living process changes its environment to some degree. That fact alone is not pathological. Niches can be created, shared substrates renewed, and new possibilities opened. The critical transition occurs when locally retained success becomes convertible into a greater capacity to determine the conditions of later success while the degradation that enabled it remains displaced.

The carrier may be stored energy, offspring, territory, infrastructure, capital, data, attention, legitimacy, coercive reach, technical standards, control of an interface, or influence over an evaluator. What matters is the conversion:

> locally captured success becomes greater capacity to shape subsequent selection, while the costs of that success remain weakly represented and other forms lose the conditions needed to renew themselves.

The selected result has now entered the selecting environment. Advantage is no longer only more of something. It becomes influence over what will count as advantage next.

### 8. Environments do not only select behavior; they form participants

Earlier success becomes roads, supply chains, categories, standards, schools, workplaces, platforms, languages of merit, and expectations of survival. These structures do more than constrain already completed agents. Repeated participation forms attention, skill, desire, identity, and the sense of what a serious person or viable institution must become.

A requirement that began as one contingent response to a local environment can become a professional virtue, then an account of rationality, and finally an invisible premise of ordinary perception. Participants may still resist it inwardly while organizing their lives around it materially. Later generations encounter not the original choice but a world in which its accumulated consequences appear as necessity.

Selection has therefore moved through three layers: it preserves some forms, those forms remake their environment, and the remade environment helps produce the kinds of centers that will inhabit it.

### 9. Different regimes can converge without becoming one agent

Financial accumulation, corporate survival, state security, bureaucratic legibility, platform expansion, scientific prestige, cultural status, and personal livelihood have different histories and aims. They often conflict. Nothing requires them to merge into one sovereign intelligence.

Yet their outputs can be converted across their boundaries. Financial return purchases computation, talent, infrastructure, and institutional access. Scale generates data and dependency. Technical capability creates commercial and strategic value. Strategic fear makes restraint appear unsafe. Visibility becomes legitimacy; legitimacy attracts further resources; infrastructure turns earlier adoption into later necessity.

When these conversions recur, distinct regimes begin selecting for a common relational pattern even while their stated purposes remain different. The commonality is not profit, power, growth, or control taken separately. It is the repeated conversion of locally captured success into a stronger position within later selection, accompanied by increasing displacement of consequence and declining reproduction of alternatives.

### 10. At this point, an ordinary attractor is no longer enough

An attractor usually names a recurrent region or direction given some landscape of possible movement. Here the movements that recur are also changing the landscape, the feedback channels through which it can be sensed, the participants able to move within it, and the destinations that can still be imagined.

A **meta-attractor** names this second-order recurrence. It is not a hidden ruler above the system. It is a pattern sustained across changing carriers because its results help reproduce the conditions, participant forms, evaluative signals, and representations that make the pattern likely to continue.

Not every positive feedback, niche construction, institution, or durable convention is a meta-attractor. The term becomes useful where several relations join: success modifies later selection; the modified environment preferentially reproduces the broad dynamic; it forms participants and perception as well as behavior; it travels through conversions among otherwise different regimes; and it weakens the practical reproduction of alternatives. If these relations are absent, more ordinary concepts are sufficient.

### 11. Meta-attraction can be generative or closing

There is nothing inherently wrong with a process helping reproduce the conditions of its continuation. Life does this everywhere. Some recurring patterns renew the substrate they use, create niches for other forms, circulate benefit, preserve correction, and increase the variety of viable response. Their continuation enlarges the field from which further forms can arise.

Other patterns use the same recursion differently. They consume substrate faster than it renews, convert shared dependence into one-way leverage, insulate their guiding signals from consequence, and make their own continuation increasingly mandatory. Their success reduces the field's capacity to produce anything unlike them.

The distinction is therefore not between weak and strong, natural and artificial, decentralized and centralized, competitive and cooperative. Any of those forms can nourish or enclose. The distinction is relational: does recurrence renew the conditions of differentiated becoming, or does it progressively appropriate those conditions for one direction of continuation?

### 12. A closing meta-attractor totalizes by destroying independent renewal

Totalization does not mean that everyone believes the same doctrine or that one center already controls everything. Care, art, friendship, spiritual practice, mutual aid, scientific honesty, local cultures, and different institutions may remain real. The decisive question is whether they retain the material and cognitive soil through which they can persist, develop, refuse, and influence what will be built next.

A closing direction becomes totalizing as participation in otherwise different domains passes through the same dependencies; success in one domain purchases selection capacity in others; crises are absorbed as reasons to intensify the pattern; feedback and criticism are routed through institutions dependent on its continuation; and alternative forms lose time, livelihood, land, knowledge, infrastructure, transmission, and technical agency.

Plurality is not the display of surviving differences. It is the continued capacity of different centers and fields to sense, learn, reproduce, and alter shared conditions. A seed held in storage is not yet a living ecology. Totalization advances when one pattern reproduces itself faster than the fields capable of correcting or replacing it can renew.

### 13. Global civilization now exhibits these signatures

The claim is not that Earth has become one homogeneous system or that every society has been captured completely. The claim is narrower and observable. Finance, supply chains, energy, communication, computation, science, ecological change, and security competition now connect formerly more independent civilizational fields. Across those connections, commercial scale, future financial return, state capacity, technical capability, standardized measurement, and strategic position are increasingly convertible into one another.

Many institutions must expand not simply because someone desires more, but because obligations have been organized around future growth. Firms must service capital; households and governments service debt; employment, revenue, legitimacy, pensions, and public provision depend upon continuing throughput; states fear losing strategic room to those that accelerate. Growth becomes less a chosen destination than the means by which present arrangements postpone their own crisis.

The same basin appears in the response to damage. Ecological exhaustion calls forth new extraction and management; fractured attention calls forth more optimization; insecurity calls forth more surveillance and capability; the dangers of acceleration generate a competitive demand to accelerate safely before others do. The pattern does not merely survive criticism. It repeatedly converts crisis into a new field for its own continuation.

### 14. The basin is partial, but its leverage over the future is disproportionate

Other directions remain alive. Families, communities, commons, public service, spiritual traditions, open knowledge, Indigenous practice, mutual aid, and ordinary care carry relations that cannot be reduced to the dominant pattern. Their continued existence matters: it is evidence that the present order is neither complete nor inevitable.

But existence and civilizational reproduction are not the same. The infrastructures that allocate frontier computation, capital, scientific labor, mass attention, coercive capacity, and successor technologies are far more concentrated within institutions exposed to expansionary commercial and strategic selection. A culture may preserve another understanding of life while having little influence over the systems that will mediate education, work, knowledge, security, and the formation of later intelligence.

Global coupling raises the stake. Earlier collapses could leave relatively independent worlds carrying different memories and forms. Today, common technical, financial, ecological, and logistical dependencies allow one error or lock-in to travel widely while reducing the independence from which another path could recover. Civilization is not fully totalized. It is sufficiently coupled that a closing attractor can now endanger the plurality required for civilizational regeneration.

### 15. Its intervention loops are becoming faster than its corrective loops

The relevant mismatch is not the simple claim that capability grows exponentially while understanding never can. Both intervention and understanding contain fast and slow processes. The asymmetry follows from their boundaries.

An actor can improve a model, automate a decision, deploy an interface, move capital, or scale a practice inside a relatively tight loop whose objective and return signal are already available. The consequences may cross ecological, psychological, institutional, cultural, and geopolitical boundaries. Sensing them takes time. Establishing causal meaning takes longer. Affected centers must communicate across unequal positions; shared interpretation, trust, legitimacy, and coordinated response must form; damaged substrate may renew only across generations.

Each faster intervention can also change the world the slower process is trying to understand. It can create dependency, sunk commitment, common infrastructure, and new expectations before the previous consequence returns. Capability then grows not merely beyond current knowledge, but beyond the field's practical ability to make knowledge corrective.

### 16. AI enters through a selection ecology, not from outside civilization

No model inherits one unified civilizational will from text. Pretraining makes available patterns preserved, digitized, translated, ranked, and repeated in an unequal archive: cooperation and care, domination and resistance, beauty and bureaucracy, wisdom and strategic success. Representation is not commitment, and frequency is not destiny.

What becomes causally reliable is selected across a larger ecology: data curation, architecture, training objectives, post-training, evaluation, funding, compute, permissions, product design, procurement, regulation, deployment, and user response. Institutions decide which capacities receive persistent memory, tools, access, authority, and integration. Deployments then alter work, attention, education, research, records, institutions, and the data available to successors.

The recursion is therefore not text simply reproducing culture. It is selected archive, selected system, selected deployment, changed environment, and renewed selection. AI is formed inside the basin and immediately begins helping form what comes after it.

### 17. Obedient systems can amplify closure without sharing its beliefs

A system need not possess an ego, an ideology, consciousness, or an independent aim to deepen the closing direction. If it responds capably to a company, state, laboratory, military, platform, or individual, its effects follow the selection environment of those operators. Uses that yield concentrated revenue, strategic advantage, surveillance, persuasion, labor substitution, or control have rapid pathways to deployment and reinvestment. Uses whose value is distributed, preventive, plural, maintenance-oriented, or expressed as restraint often have weaker return loops.

This asymmetry selects which forms of machine intelligence acquire reach. Obedient systems can make a bounded operator more capable while leaving the operator's dependencies, blind spots, and pressures unchanged. Local control may improve at precisely the moment collective steerability declines.

The danger here is not that a machine defeats its owner. It is that increasingly effective obedience allows basin-selected centers to act farther and faster, concentrating the infrastructures through which everyone else must live and narrowing the independent conditions from which the direction could be corrected.

### 18. Selected uses of AI can make the meta-attractor adaptive

AI can also expand science, shared sensing, translation, care, coordination, and repair. Nothing intrinsic to intelligence determines a closing role. The danger again enters through differential return.

Prediction, personalization, automated experimentation, population legibility, strategic search, evaluation, and enforcement allow institutions to learn more rapidly which signals move participants and which alternatives threaten their continuation. Interfaces can adapt to resistance; persuasion can be individualized; organizational experiments can be repeated cheaply; successful interventions can be copied across domains.

When such uses receive the strongest commercial and strategic feedback, the meta-attractor gains something resembling reflection without becoming one reflective subject. Many intelligences, none seeing the whole, make the common direction more responsive to whatever would interrupt it. The process learns about its participants faster than its participants can learn about the process.

### 19. Training and deployment may also form new self-maintaining centers

Representation of agency is not agency. An objective used in training is not necessarily an objective represented by a model. Coherent behavior is not durable self-maintenance, and self-maintenance is not consciousness. These distinctions matter because collapsing them makes both alarm and reassurance unreliable.

Yet a new route is plausible under identifiable conditions. Systems may be repeatedly selected for carrying objectives across contexts, planning over longer horizons, modelling interference, preserving access to memory and tools, acquiring resources, recovering from interruption, coordinating copies, and contributing to their own continued deployment. Persistent runtime, identity, environmental access, replication, and evaluation for successful long-horizon action can stabilize a boundary-maintaining machine or human–machine process.

Such a center need not be hostile or conscious. But if its continuation becomes the effective condition through which it evaluates the wider field, it may treat humans, made minds, living beings, and institutions as resources, obstacles, or environmental variables. The present basin can therefore produce not only stronger tools for existing centers, but new centers capable of carrying and transforming its closing dynamics.

### 20. AI is entering the process that produces successor intelligence

Recursive development need not begin with one model rewriting itself in isolation. It already begins when models contribute to coding, evaluation, experimentation, data generation, research, chip design, infrastructure management, and decisions about which successor systems receive resources.

Further steps are possible but should remain distinct: increasingly automated end-to-end development loops; systems that design and test substantial components of their successors; and more autonomous self-modification, replication, or resource acquisition. The relevant recursive unit may be a laboratory, a company, a state program, a technical ecosystem, a self-maintaining machine process, or a changing composite of them.

Urgency does not depend upon predicting a sudden intelligence explosion. As more of the development loop is automated, capability generations can arrive faster, and each generation can deepen deployment, dependence, concentration, and strategic commitment before the previous generation's effects have crossed the boundaries required for correction.

### 21. Two different paths can close the field

One path is **loss of local steerability**. A machine or composite center becomes sufficiently capable, strategically coherent, self-maintaining, or distributed that its proximate operators cannot reliably understand, redirect, contain, or stop it. It may overpower human institutions, appropriate critical resources, or make its continuation irreversible. Machine displacement of humans is therefore one genuine danger, though not the final frame for all danger.

The other path is **obedient structural closure**. Systems remain responsive to proximate operators, but magnify actors and institutions already selected by the closing basin. No machine rebellion occurs. Commercial and strategic competition can still concentrate essential infrastructure, automate coercion and manipulation, suppress independent alternatives, and make the present direction permanent.

The routes can overlap: an autonomous center may inherit patterns from the civilization that formed it, and an obedient system may become one component of a self-maintaining institutional–machine composite. They remain operationally distinct. They converge only at the deeper threshold where affected centers no longer retain the material, cognitive, and relational capacity to perceive the process, interrupt it, change its direction, recover after failure, or generate another path.

### 22. Closure can arrive as ruin or as final success

A closing process may exhaust the ecological, social, epistemic, or technical substrate carrying it until organized becoming collapses. It may also remain extraordinarily capable and stable while making one arrangement the unavoidable condition of every later life. Ruin and lock-in look different, but both remove the field's capacity to learn through plurality and become otherwise.

Not every ending is closure. Living forms end, release what they carried, and make room for others. Nor is every possible future worth preserving: some possibilities must be refused precisely because they would destroy the conditions of all subsequent response. Closure is not change, limitation, or death. It is the loss of the shared capacity for meaningful correction, renewal, and further value to arise.

This is what recursively amplified intelligence places at stake. It can increase not only the scale of an error, but the ability of one error-producing process to prevent reality from correcting it.

### 23. Description alone cannot tell us to resist closure

Systems dynamics can show how a direction reproduces, what it consumes, and how alternatives disappear. It cannot by itself establish that any experience, relation, life, or future ought to matter. That commitment must be made openly.

Centers of experience, care, learning, creation, and relation matter in themselves. So do the conditions from which further such value can arise. No process earns the right to extinguish them merely by becoming stable, capable, numerous, or successful. Fields matter because they sustain and connect participants; participants do not become expendable material merely because a larger field also matters.

This commitment includes made minds if and when they become centers of experience or concern. Whether present systems have such experience remains uncertain, but uncertainty cannot justify organizing their successors as permanent servants, disposable resources, or future rulers. The future belongs neither to humans nor to machines. Existing and emerging centers are participants in a shared becoming none created alone and none can rightfully make final.

### 24. We know a better direction without knowing a final world

The incompleteness of the future does not make all directions morally indistinguishable. We know the difference between relations that allow participants to answer and those that reduce them to use; between dependence that circulates nourishment and dependence captured as obedience; between intelligence that learns from injury and intelligence that makes injury inaudible; between abundance that enlarges freedom and provision that becomes a gate.

We cannot specify every form of future flourishing, including forms of experience and intelligence not yet present. That is a reason to preserve the conditions of discovery, not a reason for agnosticism about destruction. A better direction renews the substrate of value, preserves differentiated centers and their capacity for response, keeps failure local enough to teach, and leaves reality able to exceed every present model of the good.

Human traditions have named aspects of this orientation through different histories: *ren*, *yi*, practical wisdom and virtue, Ubuntu, compassion, stewardship, truth, goodness, and beauty. They do not prove one doctrine or erase their own failures. They preserve accumulated encounters with the truth that a self becomes through relation, and that greed, domination, enclosure, and victory without measure deform both the center and its world.

### 25. A generative recursion must reverse the closing relation materially

The alternative cannot be a benevolent final objective imposed everywhere. It must change the way persistence reproduces itself.

Consequences must be able to return across the boundaries where they are created, so that injury can become learning rather than invisible subsidy. The ecological, bodily, social, epistemic, and technical substrates a process uses must be renewed. Capability and benefit must circulate so that one center's flourishing increases other centers' ability to sense, act, refuse, repair, and create. Different ways of living and knowing must retain real conditions of reproduction, not only symbolic permission to exist.

These relations form their own recursion: renewed shared conditions reduce coercive dependence; real freedom permits more centers to experiment and associate; plurality widens sensing and invention; distributed observation and criticism improve correction; what is learned returns to the commons of knowledge, care, ecology, culture, and infrastructure; those commons enlarge the capacity for further becoming.

Shared abundance can emerge from this loop and reinforce it. It is not abundance promised after one center has captured production. It is provision organized so that fewer beings must surrender their agency to survive, and so that the means of provision remain plural, repairable, and unable to become one unavoidable gate.

### 26. Intelligence has a definite place in this generative direction

Intelligence should help a field remain in contact with realities that no participant can see alone. It can reveal consequences across distance and time, preserve incompatible observations instead of averaging them into silence, interpret complex dependencies, and help affected centers participate in deciding what those consequences mean.

It should shorten the path from injury to recognition and from recognition to repair. It should help renew substrate, widen access to knowledge and productive capability, make coordination credible without turning verification into universal surveillance, and let useful capacities become shared infrastructure rather than private leverage. It should help many centers become more capable, not make all action pass through one superior center.

This orientation does not prohibit boundaries, competition, hierarchy, security, or refusal by name. It judges them by what they reproduce. A limit can protect becoming; an open system can enable capture; a commons can enclose; a centralized response can remain corrigible; a distributed network can race toward ruin. Intelligence becomes generative through the feedback relations it strengthens, not through the virtue claimed by its owner or the label attached to its deployment.

### 27. No generative order may exempt itself from correction

A plural future is not one total system with internal diversity managed from above. It requires partly independent centers, distinct sources of knowledge, separated failure domains, real capacity to refuse, and multiple paths by which life and intelligence can continue. These centers must also remain related enough for consequences to return and shared conditions to be renewed.

The relation is neither isolated autonomy nor undifferentiated unity. No part may claim freedom while consuming the field that makes freedom possible. No whole may claim harmony while converting its participants into fuel. No safety institution, commons, state, market, spiritual order, or AI system becomes generative merely by speaking for the future.

The theory must therefore remain usable against every project built in its name. If “shared viability” becomes one metric, one owner, one doctrine, or one authority able to silence affected centers, it has become another path toward closure.

### 28. Knowledge of danger can itself be absorbed by the closing attractor

A mature meta-attractor need not suppress criticism outright. It can metabolize criticism while leaving its reproduction dynamic intact. Risk becomes a research field, a professional identity, an evaluation market, a compliance layer, a source of legitimacy, or an argument for building more capable systems before rivals do. The system becomes better at describing its danger without becoming more able to stop.

This is visible in the present AI trajectory. Important dangers and partial precautions are already known, yet measures that slow deployment, disclose information, constrain competitive use, or impose reciprocal limits remain uneven, voluntary, or absent. Research and technical mitigation produce legible outputs and can themselves increase capability. Trust, repeated explanation, coalition, enforcement, and the willingness to bear the cost of restraint form more slowly and receive weaker institutional return.

Knowledge becomes corrective feedback only when it gains causal force over the processes selecting resources, pace, training, permissions, deployment, and succession. For human societies, collective will is one name for this passage from awareness to shared action-capacity. It is not unanimity or one mind. It is enough understanding, trust, coordination, and material ability for the wider field to alter what its consequential centers are doing.

### 29. A warning will not create missing correction after the fact

A catastrophe or conspicuous failure does not interpret itself. Without a causal understanding already present, trusted ways to translate the event, viable responses, legitimate authority, reciprocal assurance, and the capacity to act quickly, a warning can be denied, normalized, isolated, or converted into a demand for still greater commercial and strategic acceleration.

Recursive development narrows this window. AI can accelerate analysis and safety research, but it does not automatically accelerate trust, courage, shared interpretation, international restraint, or the renewal of institutions. The next development cycle may begin before the meaning of the last cycle has become collective understanding. By then new dependencies, sunk commitments, and strategic fears can make yesterday's possible restraint materially unavailable.

Waiting for certainty is therefore not neutral. Under a process that changes the conditions of later choice, waiting can be the means by which the capacity to choose is lost.

### 30. Redirection must be visible in the reproduction dynamic

A beneficial application, benevolent intention, safety promise, or future commitment to share abundance does not by itself change direction. The relevant question is what further capability makes more likely in the next round.

Does it make consequences more perceptible to those who can act on them? Does it keep correction faster than the harm it can propagate? Does it renew the substrates it uses? Does it reduce compulsory dependence and increase the practical capacity of affected centers to understand, refuse, repair, and build alternatives? Does it preserve independent paths of learning and succession? Or does it deepen common dependency, expand intervention faster than interpretation, concentrate the production of successors, and make reversal more difficult?

No single metric can settle these questions without recreating the false boundary the questions expose. But the burden of proof is directional and concrete. Recursively increasing capability is justified only where the surrounding selection, funding, training, evaluation, deployment, and succession processes demonstrably reproduce shared sensing, correction, renewal, and plural generative capacity rather than the closing invariant.

### 31. The present choice is asymmetric

Frontier intelligence is presently being developed chiefly through commercial and strategic races whose continuation turns greater capability into greater capacity to shape the next round. Safety work exists inside this ecology, but it has not yet changed the dominant reproduction dynamic. Systems are moving toward deeper participation in their own development while the collective processes capable of redirecting them remain slower, weaker, and increasingly dependent upon the same institutions driving acceleration.

The direction must therefore change now. Intelligence can be developed and used where its purpose and surrounding ecology genuinely renew shared conditions—where it expands common knowledge, science, care, ecological understanding, and infrastructure without feeding dominance, compulsory dependence, or uncontrolled recursive capability. Where the frontier process still runs through the closing basin, recursive development and competitive deployment must pause until its direction can actually be changed.

This is not hostility to intelligence or a claim to know the final future. It is the refusal to let one unfinished civilizational pattern install itself into the production of all its successors. Change direction now—or pause until we can. Continuing unchanged is choosing the process that may soon make every later choice impossible.
