## Evidence calibration

- [Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve report](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/) says the system improved data-center, chip-design, and AI-training processes, including training models underlying AlphaEvolve itself. This supports entry into successor workflows, not autonomous successor production.
- [Anthropic's pre-deployment auditing report](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/auditing-overt-saboteur/) says current Claude models already help generate training data, write research code, monitor agents, and assess trained successors. It independently supports the same limited level claim.
- [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026) reports approximately $500 million in compute for current frontier runs, projects $1–10 billion for next-generation runs, cites an estimate that one company may hold 10–40% of global data-center AI chips at a time, and characterizes current evidence on AI-assisted research automation as mixed.
- [OpenAI's GPT-5.6 system card](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-6) places the model family below its High AI Self-Improvement threshold and reports METR's judgment that GPT-5.6 Sol would not enable fully automated AI R&D. The draft does not generalize this single-model result into an industry ceiling.
- [EU AI Act, Article 2(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj?locale=en) excludes systems used exclusively for military, defence, or national-security purposes. V6.1 uses this only to establish a gap in that framework, not absence of all law.
