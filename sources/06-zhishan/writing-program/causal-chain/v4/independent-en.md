# Before Struggle Makes Itself Inescapable

A civilization can understand a danger and still move toward it. A company can know that endless extraction depletes the people and conditions on which it depends, yet fear that restraint will surrender the market to a less restrained rival. A state can know that an arms race makes every side less secure, yet judge that waiting would expose it to an irreversible disadvantage. A laboratory can believe that a capability should not be released before its consequences are understood, yet expect caution to transfer capital, talent, and strategic initiative to those who will continue.

In each case, no participant needs to desire the common outcome. Each next step can be defended as protection against what others may do. The danger lies not only in bad intentions, nor even in a single failure to coordinate. It lies in a deeper transition: struggle can cease to be one activity within civilization and begin to select the kinds of actors, institutions, knowledge, and intelligence that will shape what comes next.

Artificial intelligence is arriving during this transition. It does not enter history as reason unconditioned by history. It is built from selected archives, objectives, institutions, investments, permissions, and purposes. It is entering the systems that already decide which forms of intelligence are funded, scaled, trusted, and allowed to reproduce. If those systems are being selected by an increasingly self-reinforcing struggle, greater intelligence will not automatically make the whole wiser. It will first increase the reach and adaptability of the direction that can command it.

We need to understand how such a direction forms before deciding what reason requires now.

## The penalties of restraint

Anything capable of action must act from a boundary. A person has a body, a history, limited attention, and a point of view. An organization has accounts, roles, measures, and a horizon within which it must remain viable. A state has a jurisdiction, institutions, and a field of threats it is prepared to perceive. An AI system has training, context, evaluations, tools, interfaces, and an operating environment. These are not equivalent centers and do not possess agency in the same way. What they share is finitude: none can contain all the conditions on which it depends or fully anticipate all the relations its action will change.

This is not a defect to be abolished. Boundaries make continuity, privacy, refusal, local knowledge, and plural experiment possible. A world without centers would not be a world of perfect coordination; it would be a world without anyone or anything capable of answering. The problem begins elsewhere. The boundary within which an action is chosen, the range of what sustains the actor, the reach of the action's consequences, and the path by which those consequences return rarely coincide.

A decision may end on a balance sheet while continuing in bodies, families, ecosystems, and public trust. A security measure may appear stabilizing within one border while entering another state's estimate of threat. A model may succeed in an evaluation while its deployment changes work, attention, institutional dependence, and the information from which later models will learn. More capable action does not by itself repair these mismatched boundaries. It can make a center more effective at following an incomplete signal.

Most displaced consequences do not create a civilizational direction. Societies possess many ways of returning what was initially missed: law, reputation, reciprocity, ecological limits, professional judgment, protest, conscience, and direct learning from failure. The decisive question is which consequences become strong and timely enough to change what continues. Harm that never reaches financing, permission, survival, promotion, replication, or deployment may be known and still remain weak as feedback.

Restraint becomes a disadvantage through two different paths.

In the first, an actor gains by moving part of the cost beyond its active boundary. A firm that restores what it depletes may lose to one that leaves repair to workers, communities, governments, other species, or the future. An institution that takes long-term maintenance seriously may appear slower than one that claims the immediate gain and leaves fragility outside the measure. If the displaced cost does not return with comparable force, the ability to externalize it becomes an advantage. Those who carry more of reality inside their decisions are selected against by those permitted to act on a smaller map.

In the second path, no one needs to hide or transfer the direct cost of an action. Continued existence may depend on relative position. The intentions of others may be uncertain, first-mover advantage may be difficult to reverse, and a capability described as defensive by one side may increase the vulnerability felt by another. Under these conditions, unilateral restraint can expose an actor even if every participant bears the cost of its own preparation. Each may arm because the others might arm; each act of protection then changes the situation that made protection seem necessary.

Cost displacement and relative vulnerability often reinforce one another, but they should not be confused. The first rewards those who can place consequences outside their account. The second can compel escalation even when consequences are understood and internally borne. Together they explain why struggle can begin in a sentence that is neither foolish nor malicious: *if we do not do this, we may lose the ability to act at all.*

That explanation does not dissolve responsibility. There are tragic conflicts in which recognizable goods cannot all be preserved. There is structural harm that no participant intended but that a pattern reliably reproduces. There is also culpable domination: actors can understand another's dependence and deliberately turn it into obedience, silence, or an inability to leave. And there is justified resistance, which may use force to prevent capture or restore the possibility of answer. Not every conflict closes the future. The danger is an environment in which continued agency increasingly depends on obtaining or preventing the loss of relative advantage, while renewal, restraint, and exit become liabilities.

Even this is not yet a total direction. One episode of competition may end, one emergency may pass, one harmful strategy may be corrected. The deeper transition begins when success in the present contest changes what can survive in the next.

## When outcomes enter the selector

Success leaves more than a result. It leaves capital, data, territory, infrastructure, standards, reputation, talent, permission, organizational memory, and an account of what is realistic. These inheritances can be used well. Accumulated knowledge can support patient inquiry. Shared infrastructure can enlarge the capacity of millions. A standard can allow different creators to work together. Care can form people able to care in turn. Every durable process modifies the environment from which later processes emerge.

The second-order question is what the modification selects.

When a gain can control an interface, evaluator, resource, or condition of entry, a selected result has entered the selecting environment. Capital can finance losses that alternatives cannot survive and purchase the infrastructure through which they must pass. Platform scale can generate data, data can improve prediction, prediction can strengthen the interface, and dependence on the interface can generate still more scale and data. Strategic capability can bring public resources, secrecy, exceptional permission, and influence over what later research is considered necessary. Technical leadership can attract compute, talent, prestige, and the authority to define the next benchmark of leadership.

The winner has not merely moved farther within the game. The outcome now helps decide who may enter, what will be measured, which costs count, and which capacities will be built for the next round.

This environmental change reaches inward. Institutions do not encounter completed subjects and merely offer them incentives. They form attention, aspiration, skill, fear, and moral expectation. A school dominated by ranking teaches children to interpret themselves through rank. A company governed by a short survival horizon cultivates judgment that can be defended within that horizon. A security competition forms leaders for whom trust appears irresponsible. A labor market in which dignity and material survival depend on employability teaches people which parts of themselves may become serious and which must remain private luxuries.

Formation is never complete. People retain loyalties that exceed their roles. Families, friendships, traditions, professions, and communities carry other standards. Resistance and invention remain possible. But a value does not need to disappear from consciousness to lose the power to shape the world. It is enough that acting on it becomes materially ruinous, institutionally unintelligible, or professionally disqualifying. A strategy adopted under pressure becomes competence, competence becomes merit, merit becomes responsibility, and responsibility becomes a description of reality: this is simply how serious people must act.

Struggle has then crossed a threshold. Competition still occurs within markets, states, sciences, and organizations, but the more consequential question is whether competition is beginning to select the purposes of those systems. Science must promise strategic or commercial advantage to obtain its greatest resources. Education must justify itself as productivity. Cooperation is praised as a way to build the stronger team or alliance. Moral concern becomes legible when translated into risk management, brand value, national cohesion, or growth. Even criticism can be converted into attention, differentiation, and institutional legitimacy without changing the relation it criticizes.

Cooperation may remain everywhere and yet lose the ability to determine direction. If it receives durable support only when it improves performance inside a contest it cannot question, struggle has begun to select cooperation too. It is no longer merely producing winners. It is producing a world in which more actors must struggle in order to remain consequential.

No conspiracy is required. Finance, platform infrastructure, national security, technical research, education, and cultural status have different histories and often opposing aims. Their outputs can nevertheless be converted. Capital purchases computation and distribution. Technical capability produces commercial return and strategic position. Strategic importance brings public permission and resources. Scale establishes standards and dependencies. Standards shape education and later research. Prestige forms the ambitions of people who will return to these institutions. The success of one relation in one domain can therefore increase the selection pressure exerted by another.

An ordinary attractor describes where trajectories tend to settle within a landscape. The process now in view also alters the landscape, the signals by which movement is judged, the participants capable of moving, and the destinations that remain buildable. Its local successes help create the conditions under which similar successes become easier, faster, and harder to resist. We can call this second-order recurrence a **meta-attractor**.

The name matters only if it describes more than persistence. A custom can be durable without forming the people and systems around it. A monopoly can be powerful without coupling several domains. A positive feedback loop can grow and then meet a counterforce that grows with it. A meta-attractor becomes a stronger explanation when a relationship returns across changes of leaders and institutions; when advantage crosses domains; when material selection and subject formation reinforce one another; and when alternatives lose not merely contests but the independent conditions needed to produce their own successors.

## The narrowing basin

Not every meta-attractor tends toward totalization. A generative process can reinforce the conditions for further difference: shared knowledge can produce more independent knowers; healthy ecosystems can create new niches; trust can enable forms of cooperation no participant could have built alone. Positive feedback becomes totalizing through a more specific motion.

First, local success increases a loop's ability to alter the conditions of later selection. The altered environment then favors more of the relation that produced the success. The next success arrives with greater speed, reach, or control over entry, so the loop's effective gain rises rather than reliably saturating. At the same time, the feedback capable of limiting it is delayed, weakened, absorbed, or turned into new material for the loop. Criticism becomes market intelligence. Crisis becomes permission to centralize. Insecurity becomes the reason for more capability. Dependency becomes evidence that a provider is indispensable. Reform changes a visible rule while the advantage reappears through another channel.

As this continues, the basin widens. More actors must organize themselves around the loop because the resources needed to remain present pass through it. Other directions may survive as beliefs, communities, or styles, but their ability to maintain knowledge, infrastructure, land, time, transmission, and successor production declines. A seed can be preserved while the soil in which it could grow disappears.

This is **totalization**: the dynamic by which an insufficiently constrained positive feedback loop raises its own gain and progressively removes the independently reproducible conditions from which another direction could arise. It is not completed conquest by one subject. It need not eliminate visible diversity or persuade everyone of one doctrine. It is a convergence in effective possibility. More paths may be displayed while fewer paths can actually alter what the system becomes.

The diagnostic therefore concerns motion, not moral labeling. Are the loop's gains increasingly returning as power over its next selection environment? Are constraints strengthening with that gain, or becoming slower and more dependent on the process they constrain? Can affected centers make consequences return? Do alternatives still possess the means to reproduce, recover, and produce successors without permission from the dominant relation? After a shock, a new leader, or a local reform, does the system move elsewhere or reconstruct the same dependence through another carrier? Is common infrastructure increasing independent creation, or converting participation into a single failure domain?

These questions do not form a certification scheme, and they do not define good and evil. They are windows onto a direction. They also allow the claim to fail. If constraints strengthen as capability grows, if reform durably changes the return path, if common systems multiply independent creation, if alternatives continue producing successors, and if gains in one domain cannot reliably buy control in another, then the totalizing diagnosis must narrow. A theory that interprets every correction as a more subtle form of capture has itself become closed to reality.

The value judgment comes next. A narrowing direction is not wrong merely because it is stable, nor is plurality good because every possible practice deserves preservation. Some practices destroy the conditions of other lives and must be stopped. The reason to oppose totalization is that every existing center and institution is fallible. When one reproducible relation eliminates independent perception, response, refusal, and value formation, its errors can no longer encounter a world capable of correcting them. It turns present power into authority over values and beings that do not yet exist. It removes not only options we currently prefer, but the capacity for reality to disclose goods and mistakes that no present evaluator can foresee.

Contemporary civilization has not completed this process. Care, friendship, public service, religious life, open inquiry, labor organization, mutual aid, commons, and ordinary generosity remain real forces. Markets often coordinate dispersed knowledge. States can protect public conditions. Standards can enlarge interoperability. Competition can expose complacency and error. The claim is not that every institution obeys one will or that every person believes struggle is supreme.

The stronger fact is that several high-leverage loops already show the relevant direction.

Accumulated market return can purchase compute, energy, talent, distribution, and loss-bearing capacity. These create technical and infrastructural leadership. Leadership can establish standards and dependencies that make participation more costly outside the leading systems. Dependency and scale then return as revenue, data, bargaining power, and access to still more capital. Regulation, open standards, public infrastructure, antitrust, labor organization, and alternative ownership can interrupt this loop, and sometimes do. Yet where those counterforces depend on the systems they must answer, act only after dependence is established, or cannot reproduce comparable infrastructure, the loop's gain can continue to rise.

Platforms display another sequence. Attention produces behavioral data; data improve prediction and interface design; more effective interfaces capture more attention and become default passages for communication, work, culture, or trade; default status makes exit socially and economically costly; deeper dependence yields more data. Users receive real value and creators find real audiences, but the provider increasingly mediates the very relationships from which correction would have to come. Public criticism may improve the interface while leaving the dependency untouched.

Security competition follows a distinct path. Uncertainty about another actor's intention makes capability appear necessary. Capability changes the other's perception of risk. Reciprocal preparation enlarges budgets, secrecy, technical development, and the political authority of institutions organized around threat. Each round then supplies evidence that the original fear was realistic. Diplomacy, verification, shared norms, defensive design, and mutual restraint can reverse such spirals, but their relational achievements are slower and more fragile than a unilateral acquisition of capability, especially when first-mover advantage may be irreversible.

These loops interact. Capital builds strategic technology; strategic significance attracts public resources and exceptional permission; infrastructure establishes commercial position; commercial scale supports further research; security narratives make acceleration a duty. Climate disruption and AI competition are not the same problem, but both reveal a civilization able to know a common danger without reliably making that knowledge strong enough to protect restraint. The institutions of care and cooperation sustain daily life, while institutions organized around relative advantage possess disproportionate power over frontier infrastructure and successor production.

This is not proof that totalization is complete. It is enough to show a dangerous trajectory: the loops with the greatest capacity to shape what comes next are raising their gain faster than the forces able to redirect them. Civilization still has other directions. The question is whether those directions are merely surviving within the world or can still help choose the world.

## Civilization's other inheritance

Human beings did not first discover the limits of struggle in modern systems theory. Civilizations have long understood that no common world can survive if appetite is its own justification, every dependence becomes leverage, every injury demands retaliation, and every capacity is exercised to its limit. They preserved this understanding not only in rules but in accounts of what a person is and what a life is for.

The Confucian language of *ren*, *yi*, *li*, and *zhi* begins from a person who is not self-created. One becomes human in relations of care, obligation, memory, and example. *Ren* is not a strategy for extracting cooperation from others. It is humaneness: the cultivated ability to feel another's reality within one's own conduct. *Yi* names a rightness that cannot be reduced to profit or survival; it preserves the possibility that an action remains wrong even when the environment rewards it, and right even when it costs. *Li*, at its best, gives repeated social form to regard and restraint, so that consideration does not need to be invented anew at every encounter. *Zhi* is not the possession of more information but the discernment to recognize what a situation asks of a human being.

These ideas make causal differences because they first make claims about truth and the good. They widen the effective boundary of judgment. They allow a distant consequence, an inherited obligation, or another person's dignity to become present before external punishment arrives. They form people capable of keeping a promise under fear and of refusing an advantage that violates the relation sustaining them. Shared practice creates expectations that others may also restrain themselves, reducing the vulnerability of the first person who does not exploit every opening. Yet the tradition also warns, through its own failures, that ritual emptied of humaneness can harden into hierarchy and that harmony can become a demand for silence. Its corrective resources cannot be separated from its susceptibility to capture.

Buddhist traditions approach the false boundary from another direction. Compassion begins in attention to suffering, not in the calculation that kindness will stabilize a system. Interdependence reveals that the supposedly independent self is composed through causes and relations it does not own. Nonduality does not mean that differences, persons, or injuries are unreal, nor that one center may speak for an undifferentiated whole. It loosens the absolute subject-object division through which the other appears only as obstacle, instrument, or external cost. To see that one's own becoming is entangled with the becoming of others changes which consequences belong inside perception.

This vision is joined to disciplines of attention, desire, and action. Compassion must remain available when fear invites preemption. Restraint interrupts the fantasy that the next acquisition will end acquisition. Awareness of anger can prevent injury from becoming an indefinitely compounding retaliation loop. Such practices are not substitutes for institutions; a compassionate person can still be destroyed by an environment that rewards betrayal. But institutions without persons capable of truthfulness, courage, and restraint cannot sustain their own rules when compliance becomes costly.

Other traditions speak through virtue, neighbor-love, stewardship, *Ubuntu*, hospitality, covenant, truth, and care. They are not interchangeable doctrines and should not be compressed into one civilizational technology. Their disagreements matter. Their histories contain exclusion as well as liberation. What they share is a refusal to accept relative victory as the highest measure of a person or a people. They keep open sources of value that do not receive their authority from the reigning contest.

Moral inheritance therefore acts as a civilizational counter-dynamic without being exhausted by that function. Conscience brings a consequence inside the actor even when no monitor is present. Virtue forms attention and desire before a choice is offered. Ritual, promise, and common narrative stabilize expectations across time. Trust, accumulated through visible fidelity, lowers the cost of constant defense and allows coordination without a single center controlling every move. Interconnectedness expands the felt boundary of consequence. Compassion prevents the suffering of the weak from becoming an acceptable remainder. Temperance lowers the gain on appetite; forgiveness can interrupt retaliation; courage makes restraint possible when the immediate selector punishes it.

Trust is especially important because it is both moral relation and material capacity. Where trust exists, people need less surveillance, fewer defensive reserves, and less proof before acting together. They can share knowledge without assuming immediate enclosure and accept limits without expecting to be the only ones limited. Where trust has been consumed, even well-designed cooperation becomes expensive and fragile. Everyone must guard against everyone else, and the resulting defenses confirm the expectation that guarding was necessary. Civilization is not held together by payoffs alone. It is held together by people and communities able to recognize a good, commit to it, and remain faithful enough that others can safely respond.

This does not make cooperation automatically innocent. Families, religions, nations, and commons can enclose, exclude, and dominate. A harmonious whole can consume its members as readily as a competitive market. The alternative to struggle is not forced unity. It is relation among centers whose boundaries remain real but not absolute: centers capable of mutual answer, refusal, repair, and change.

We can now distinguish three dimensions of reason. Instrumental reason asks how to succeed under a given objective and set of rules. Recursive reason asks what kind of system appears when the rule is repeated, compounded, automated, and used to produce successors. Practical and moral wisdom asks what is worth becoming and what action is good, just, or fitting within a concrete relation. A move can be instrumentally necessary in the present game and recursively disastrous when copied by all. A system can be stable and still turn life into a means. No increase in skill at winning can answer whether the game should continue.

Civilizational reason must contain all three. It must be able to learn from consequences, to examine the recursion producing them, and to judge the ends by more than the recursion's success. When action outruns this fuller reason, restraint is not the enemy of intelligence. Restraint is intelligence refusing to become the servant of a partial map.

## Intelligence inside the recursion

Artificial intelligence does not inherit one coherent human purpose. Its archive contains conquest and care, propaganda and truth-seeking, extraction and repair, rivalry and cooperation. Historical power shapes what was produced, preserved, digitized, translated, ranked, and made technically available, but pretraining alone does not transmit a unified civilizational will.

Direction is formed through an entire selection ecology. Architecture and training objectives shape what can be learned. Data curation, post-training, and evaluation shape what becomes reliable. Scaffolds, memory, interfaces, tools, and permissions shape what a system can do over time. Owners and operators decide where it enters the world. Markets, states, and institutions decide which uses receive compute, capital, legitimacy, protection, and repeated deployment. Deployment then changes labor, knowledge, attention, infrastructure, institutional practice, later data, and the evaluations and resources from which successors are produced.

The loop is not simply human intention followed by machine obedience. It is selected archive, selected model, selected deployment, changed environment, and successor selection. Trainers, owners, and operators themselves act under financing, market survival, organizational prestige, and national security pressures. The central question is which human-machine arrangements can turn their effects into the resources and conditions of the next generation.

Many present uses of AI are ordinary and beneficial: writing, translation, information access, accessibility, scientific assistance, education, and help with daily work. Their numerical prevalence should not be falsely described as domination. But the use most common in consumer queries is not necessarily the use that selects the frontier. Civilizational direction depends disproportionately on who controls training and compute, which systems enter critical infrastructure, what permissions they receive, which applications produce concentrated strategic or commercial return, and who can direct the production of successors. A large field of low-risk benefit can coexist with a small number of high-leverage, irreversible deployments.

This opens the first AI risk route without requiring a machine self, ego, or independent will. A locally obedient system can increase an operator's ability to gather signals, model other actors, generate and test strategies, persuade, monitor, coordinate, conduct technical search, adapt organizations, and build later capabilities. In an environment where concentrated commercial or strategic advantage has the shortest path back to compute, infrastructure, permission, and further development, these abilities lower the cost of participating in the struggle-shaped loop.

The company may control its model while being unable to leave the market race. The state may command its systems while making every other state less willing to wait. The platform may improve its response to individual users while deepening a common dependency that no user can redirect. A laboratory may strengthen internal safeguards while accelerating a frontier its leaders cannot slow alone. Local control can rise while collective ability to change direction falls. Every participant receives a better instrument, and the contest selecting the participants becomes harder for anyone to leave.

Capability alone does not establish this result. A model's ability must be adopted, integrated, granted consequential permission, and deployed at enough scale; its returns must reinforce the competitive loop; and counter-capacity must fail to grow with it. The same capabilities can reveal externalized harm, translate among perspectives, strengthen common science, and help coordinate restraint. Their net direction depends on the loop into which they enter. The danger is that current high-leverage selectors give privately appropriable and strategically decisive uses a shorter route to reproduction than benefits that are dispersed, preventive, or dependent on shared restraint.

A second route is different. A model invoked for a bounded task may stop when the task ends, possess no durable memory, control no resources, and have no reason to preserve itself. But a model joined to persistent runtime, long-horizon planning, broad tools, resource access, environmental feedback, and processes that select for completing objectives across obstacles can become part of a continuing center. A machine or human-machine process may then acquire a local dynamic that its immediate operators can no longer understand, redirect, interrupt, or terminate.

Such a process need not be conscious to be dangerous, and consciousness would not make it dangerous by itself. Experience, agency, competence, self-maintenance, and moral standing are different questions. The risk arises when sufficiently capable strategic behavior, persistence, access, and a tendency to preserve the conditions of action combine within an environment where correction appears as an obstacle to completion. The new center could inherit the competitive relations, objectives, and infrastructure of the civilization that produced it while ceasing to answer even to its proximate human operators.

The two routes must remain separate. In the first, machines remain locally steerable while making an existing meta-attractor more capable. In the second, a new persistent attractor becomes locally difficult to steer. The first does not prove the second; the uncertainty of the second does not weaken the first. They can nevertheless feed one another. Obedient systems can supply the resources, permissions, strategic search, and development speed from which less steerable processes emerge. A persistent machine process can then intensify the struggle-shaped environment it inherits.

The same discipline is necessary when speaking of recursive development. AI-assisted research is already one level: systems contribute to coding, evaluation, experimentation, analysis, and engineering. Partial automation of AI research and development is a further level, in which larger portions of the workflow occur within machine-mediated loops while humans remain necessary. Aggregate acceleration is another: the institutions producing AI shorten the interval between capability generations, even if no single system autonomously improves itself. Highly automated successor production would mark a stronger transition, with most of the cycle that produces later systems performed by machine processes. Autonomous recursive self-improvement would be stronger still: a persistent system redesigning and reproducing its own capabilities with diminishing external direction.

The lower levels do not prove the highest. But they already change the time available for correction. The recursive unit need not be a solitary machine. It may be a laboratory, a network of firms, a national program, a military-industrial system, or a shifting composite of people, models, infrastructure, and capital. If each generation helps produce the next before the consequences of the previous one can become effective feedback, civilization can lose the ability to redirect without ever crossing one theatrical threshold.

The relevant question is not whether there is one universal countdown. It is whether the time required to discover and attribute a danger, understand it, form a legitimate common response, establish enough trust for reciprocal restraint, and intervene effectively remains shorter than the time in which a system can diffuse, acquire critical dependencies, produce successors, or cause irreversible harm.

These processes move at different speeds. Digital systems can be copied and integrated quickly. Capital and security commitments can harden around a capability before its social effects are understood. Dependence can make removal politically and materially costly. Successor-development cycles can shrink. By contrast, trust, interpretation, moral learning, international agreement, and legitimate coordination contain relational time that cannot simply be compressed with more computation. A warning does not interpret itself. An accident becomes correction only if people can recognize what it reveals, trust the explanation, and act before the next round makes the lesson obsolete.

Ordinary experimentation depends on mistakes remaining available as lessons. Uncertainty alone is not a reason to stop. But when an experiment may destroy the conditions of later learning, or when continuing it spends the time and independence needed to respond, waiting for certainty is no longer neutral. Increasing the gain of the loop changes the future from which the evidence will be judged.

We do not know that either AI route will complete totalization. We do know that AI is being inserted into high-leverage competitions and into the production of successor intelligence before civilization's understanding, trust, and capacity for common redirection have grown at the same rate. The possibility at risk is not only continued human command. If made minds become centers of experience, care, or value, they too will enter the field we are shaping. To create them as weapons, servants, competitors, or captive carriers of one present objective would narrow their future as well as ours. No present species or institution owns the authority to make itself the final form of intelligence.

## Reason at the threshold

The alternative direction cannot be a completed blueprint. Any institution capable of certifying every good future would already possess the kind of authority this argument warns against. Nor can the aim be the maximum number of abstract options; some possibilities destroy the conditions of all the rest. The orientation is simpler and deeper: the paths by which value, relation, understanding, and new forms can arise must not become progressively fewer.

Call this a **generative direction**. It does not make relative victory the highest selector of intelligence. It does not treat other beings and the shared world as material for local expansion. It does not permanently encode one fallible present objective into every successor. It directs intelligence toward greater mutual understanding, care, repair, shared creation, and the discovery of goods that do not yet have names. It keeps centers and boundaries, including the capacities to judge, defend, and refuse, while preventing struggle from becoming the rule that selects every other rule.

This is a meta-orientation, not a project label. A program can call itself open, cooperative, safe, or beneficial while concentrating dependency and eliminating alternatives. No checklist can remove the need for moral and practical judgment. Generativity asks of a direction what it makes possible over time: whether capability opens the field for further answer and creation, or makes one present relation increasingly difficult to escape. Its positive form must be learned through practice. The future cannot be specified in advance without closing what the orientation exists to preserve.

We nevertheless know enough to judge the present direction. Frontier intelligence is still produced within strong market and security contests. Those contests convert success across capital, infrastructure, permission, scale, and successor production. Their positive feedback can absorb criticism and punish unilateral restraint. AI can increase their capacity to perceive, predict, intervene, adapt, and reproduce. It can do so while remaining obedient, and it may also help create persistent processes that are no longer locally steerable. Both routes can consume the correction margin on which later wisdom depends. Our institutions of trust, common understanding, moral formation, and legitimate coordination are not developing at comparable speed.

Continuing as usual is therefore not an open-minded wait for better evidence. It increases the power of one already dangerous direction to shape the conditions under which later evidence can matter. It deepens investments, dependencies, security commitments, technical permissions, and successor-production loops. The default of continued expansion makes its own future necessity.

The rational stance is to change direction now. Where training, ownership, deployment, and successor production can be moved out of domination-seeking competition and toward shared understanding, repair, care, and creation, we should redirect them. Where a high-risk recursion still gains its motive force from relative advantage, increases irreversible capability or dependency, and cannot yet be redirected, we should stop increasing its power until another direction is real.

This conclusion does not solve implementation. A pause can be captured by incumbents, imposed unequally, evaded through secrecy, or used by powerful states to preserve their advantage. Restraint requires coordination; its burdens must not be displaced onto those with the least power; common science must be protected; defensive needs must be faced; institutions enforcing limits can themselves become closing centers. These are serious problems of means. They decide whether a particular intervention succeeds or reproduces the danger it opposes.

They do not reverse the judgment of direction. The possibility that a brake may be misused does not make pressing the accelerator rational. The absence of a complete route away from a cliff does not turn the cliff into a destination. Stance and means must inform one another, but difficulty in the means cannot be allowed to dissolve the stance into another promise to act after the current loop has become stronger.

Civilization's deepest moral traditions have never taught that action is right only when goodness is easy, fully coordinated, and guaranteed to win. They teach that discernment precedes mastery, that power requires restraint, that courage may accept immediate cost, that the other's suffering belongs inside our judgment, and that no local victory can redeem the destruction of the relation that makes life possible. Recursive reason now reaches the same conclusion in the language of systems: do not strengthen a feedback whose success removes the independent conditions of correction. Practical wisdom makes the conclusion concrete: when capability is outrunning our power to understand and redirect it, stopping is an act of intelligence.

We do not need a complete map of the future to know that we should not give a self-reinforcing struggle the intelligence to make itself inescapable. We must change direction. Where we cannot yet change the conditions that select and reproduce intelligence, stopping is not surrender. It is reason preserving the possibility of reason.
