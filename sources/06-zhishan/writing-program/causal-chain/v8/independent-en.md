# The Direction Intelligence Must Leave Behind

## Everyone has a reason

A company building a frontier model knows that waiting can mean losing customers, investment, talent, and the chance to set the next technical standard. Its researchers know that a laboratory without the largest available systems may cease to be a frontier laboratory at all. A government knows that restraint which is not shared may leave its infrastructure, intelligence services, economy, and armed forces dependent on a rival. A worker knows that declining the new system will not keep an employer from reorganizing work around it. A family knows that refusing the race does not exempt its children from the world the race produces.

None of these fears is imaginary. None requires an evil person at the center. Each participant can explain why it cannot safely be the first to slow down. The company is answerable to a market that rewards growth before distant costs can be counted. The researcher is answerable to an institution that measures access, publication, and demonstrated capability. The state is answerable to other states whose intentions it cannot know. The worker is answerable to rent, debt, and the price of being replaceable. Each sees a real part of reality. Each acts on reasons that become more compelling because the others are expected to act on theirs.

The result is a world almost no one would choose as a whole: more capable systems arriving faster than common understanding, more resources tied to staying ahead, more institutions unable to pause without surrendering position, and more of ordinary life reorganized around decisions made elsewhere. The fear of insecurity produces the conditions of deeper insecurity. The attempt to preserve agency narrows the room in which anyone can later change direction.

It is tempting to call this hypocrisy. Often it is not. The more difficult possibility is that the actors are being rational within the realities that can reach them in time.

Every center of action meets the world through a boundary. A firm has accounts, contracts, assets, and reporting periods. A ministry has a jurisdiction, a budget, an intelligence picture, and an electoral or strategic horizon. A laboratory has instruments, benchmarks, compute allocations, and research programs. A person has a body, a history, obligations, and a finite field of attention. A machine has the data, scores, permissions, memory, and tools exposed through its interface. Without such boundaries, there is no action at all. No part can wait for a complete account of every consequence before it moves.

But the consequences of action do not respect the boundary through which action was chosen. Carbon crosses balance sheets. Fear crosses borders. A platform changes habits it does not record as liabilities. A model changes the conditions of work, knowledge, trust, and conflict beyond the benchmark on which it was selected. The future bears effects that cannot vote in the decision that produces them. Other species, distant communities, common institutions, and unborn people may be indispensable to the process while remaining absent from its feedback.

The first danger, then, is not that local judgment is false. It is that a partial truth can acquire the power to select as if it were the whole.

## What returns, and what does not

Some consequences return quickly. Revenue, promotion, market share, votes, military readiness, publication, and measured performance can reward a choice within days or quarters. Other consequences return slowly, diffusely, or through someone else: exhausted attention, degraded trust, deskilled institutions, ecological loss, weakened public knowledge, dependence on infrastructure no community controls, and the disappearance of ways of living that cannot survive the new price of participation.

This difference in return time changes what can persist. Imagine two organizations trying to build the same capability. One pays for independent scrutiny, preserves alternatives, refuses manipulative deployment, gives affected people meaningful exit, and accounts for long-term damage. The other transfers those costs outward or leaves them for later. The first may be wiser across the full field of consequences and still be slower, more expensive, and easier to displace inside the field that determines whether it survives. Its greater rationality becomes a local disadvantage.

The problem remains even when no cost is deliberately concealed. If survival depends on relative position, if the intentions of others are uncertain, and if an early lead is difficult to reverse, unilateral restraint can look like exposure. A state may prefer a world with less automated conflict and still automate because it doubts the other state will wait. A company may prefer shared limits and still expand because it cannot carry the cost alone. The reason to continue is produced not only by desire for gain but by fear of the advantage created by someone else's continuation.

That is how a choice environment can select against the bearers of more complete judgment. Institutions that take delayed harm seriously lose resources. Independent inquiry loses access to the systems it must examine. Care, maintenance, and public knowledge struggle to reproduce themselves because their value returns through lives and relationships rather than through a claim on the next round of capital. Different technological paths may remain imaginable while becoming unable to hire, train, build, maintain, or recover from failure without depending on the path they would replace.

Local instrumental reason then performs a quiet substitution. Because it is what the environment rewards, it begins to present itself not as one form of reason under one set of boundaries, but as reason itself. What cannot win is called unrealistic. What cannot scale is called irrelevant. What cannot be priced is called sentiment. What asks whether the goal should be pursued is treated as an obstacle to intelligence, because intelligence has already been reduced to reaching the given goal faster.

Yet a selection process needs more than repeated choice. It needs inheritance. Why does one round of success change the next?

## Victory survives the victor

A victory leaves things behind. It leaves capital, equipment, data, trained people, distribution channels, licenses, standards, legal privileges, organizational confidence, and the ability to endure a long period of failure. It leaves customers who have reorganized their work around an interface and public agencies whose services now depend on a supplier. It leaves a story about what progress looks like and a generation of specialists whose careers assume that story will continue. Most of all, it leaves a greater capacity to produce successors.

Once success has these effects, it is no longer merely an outcome. Resources gained in one round alter the conditions of the next. The winning organization can buy more of what the next contest requires. Its infrastructure becomes the environment through which others must act. Its measures of performance become the language in which alternatives must justify themselves. Its standards determine compatibility; its systems shape training; its hiring shapes expertise; its scale shapes what investors, officials, and researchers consider possible. A result has become a feedback, and feedback has begun to select what remains.

This process does not require the same firm, state, leader, or model to win forever. Particular victors can be replaced while the direction they embody persists. A new company can defeat an old one by intensifying the same demand for scale. Rival states can oppose one another while converting scientific knowledge, industrial capacity, and public legitimacy into the same currency of strategic advantage. A reform can redistribute control without changing the rule that every institution must become stronger at the competition or become dependent on those that are.

Nor does the process require a hidden coordinator. The environment is made by the accumulated consequences of many actors, each responding to the environment made by the others. Its direction can survive changes in intention because its inheritance is carried in infrastructure, dependence, expectation, and the distribution of the ability to act.

At this point, possibility has to mean more than a list of options. A path is not alive merely because someone can still describe it. It is alive if people can perceive its world, learn its practices, find one another, secure material support, maintain its institutions, pass on its knowledge, and begin again after failure. A language may remain in an archive after no child can live in it. A public technology may remain legal after every competent institution depends on a proprietary stack. A form of care may remain praised after the time and livelihood needed to practice it have disappeared. An independent science may remain nominally free after its questions, instruments, and access are supplied by the object it is meant to judge.

What closes is not opinion. It is the route by which a different opinion could become a durable reality.

When success can narrow those routes, struggle begins to do more than choose among contestants. It begins to choose the kinds of contestants, aims, and reasons that the future can contain.

## When struggle selects the struggler

We can call this a meta-attractor: a direction selected by existing conditions that acquires the power to reshape the conditions of later selection. The name does not identify a secret ruler. It marks a recursion: what succeeds changes the landscape, and the changed landscape produces actors, goals, and accounts of reality better fitted to the same direction. If the process deepens, alternatives lose not only contests but the conditions of regeneration. This is totalization, but it is not destiny and need not end in stable control. The direction may lock a system into a durable order or consume its common conditions and collapse; its unity lies in a feedback that makes correction harder while extending the behavior that created the need for correction.

Modern civilization has given such feedback unprecedented range. Supply chains, finance, cloud infrastructure, technical standards, information networks, military alliances, and energy systems connect societies with different histories and declared values to common conditions of survival. Market success can purchase political access and technical capacity. Technical capacity can create strategic importance. Strategic importance can attract public budgets, secrecy, and protection. State power can secure markets, energy, minerals, chips, and standards. Each domain can return the gains of another.

This does not make capitalism, the state, or technology interchangeable. Their institutions, obligations, and forms of power differ. It means that gains can cross between them. A model trained for commercial use can become a security asset. A security procurement can support the infrastructure and talent of a commercial supplier. A cloud platform can become both a market and a condition of scientific access. Research can be justified as national strength, national strength as industrial policy, and industrial policy as the source of the next research advantage.

Under these conditions, science, education, care, and public benefit are often required to translate themselves into growth, efficiency, security, or relative advantage before high-leverage resources will recognize them. The translation can be useful; a hospital needs a budget and a public institution needs protection. But when the translation becomes the gate through which all purposes must pass, the gate starts choosing the purposes. The claim that self-interest is rationality, that greed is good, or that one must first win in order to do good later does not reveal an eternal human essence. It is the language a selection environment teaches its survivors to speak.

The direction is not complete. Families still sacrifice without profit. Scientists share findings that could have been enclosed. Communities defend forests, languages, and public institutions against immediate gain. Workers refuse orders. Officials preserve norms that reduce their own discretion. Rival states sometimes build regimes of verification and restraint. The world contains genuine counterforces, not merely decorative exceptions.

That fact poses the historical question the darker account cannot answer alone. If struggle can reward displaced cost, reproduce its winners, and reshape what counts as reason, why has civilization not already exhausted every common good?

## How another reason becomes real

Civilizations have never survived by competition alone. They have also cultivated people for whom the boundary of the self is not the boundary of concern, practices that make restraint intelligible, and institutions that return a wider field of consequence to local choice. These are not ornaments added after the serious work of survival. They are among the means by which survival has remained human.

The Confucian movement from ren through yi and li to zhi offers one deep account of this work. Ren begins from the fact that a person becomes a person in relation. The self is not dissolved into the group; it is formed through responsiveness to parents and children, friends and strangers, the living and the dead, the near and the distant. To perceive another's claim is not to calculate an external cost imposed on an otherwise complete individual. It is to become more adequate to the reality in which one's own life already exists.

Yi gives this responsiveness a direction that victory cannot authorize. It asks what is fitting and right before asking what will prevail. A profitable act can be unworthy; a losing refusal can preserve the ground on which worth remains possible. This is not indifference to consequences. It is a refusal to let the selection environment define the good solely by selecting it.

Li carries such judgment into form. Courtesy, ritual, role, and repeated practice may look weaker than force because none guarantees obedience. Their power lies elsewhere. They let a relation become expectable. A promise kept once is admirable; a practice of keeping promises changes what others can safely do. Restraint repeated and recognized becomes a social fact. It reduces the penalty for the next person who restrains herself. It gives memory to a value that would otherwise have to begin again as private sacrifice in every generation.

Zhi, wisdom, is therefore not greater speed toward a fixed objective. It is the capacity to judge the objective, the boundary through which it appears, the moment in which action becomes mistimed, and the point at which persistence becomes folly. Intelligence solves within a frame. Wisdom can see that the frame is consuming the world that made solving valuable.

Compassion, virtue, Ubuntu, interdependence, and non-dual traditions differ in metaphysics and practice, but they echo this wider civilizational discovery: the isolated winner is an abstraction, and a reason that cannot encounter the reality of another is incomplete. These traditions cannot be reduced to techniques for stabilizing a system. A perfectly stable order that uses concrete beings as expendable material can still be wrong. Their claim reaches beneath stability to value.

The value boundary needed here is simple enough to state. No local victory has the right to cancel the conditions under which other beings and later generations can continue to perceive, answer, correct, and practice truth, goodness, and beauty. This is not a demand that every form endure unchanged. Living worlds require endings, transformation, conflict, and choice. It is a demand that one contestant not convert a temporary advantage into ownership of who may remain a participant in reality.

An inner orientation matters only if it can survive contact with the selection environment. Shared meaning can create commitment. Commitments honored repeatedly create credible expectation. Credible expectations allow people to cooperate without each bearing the full risk of being first. Institutions can return displaced costs, protect those who preserve common goods, maintain resources no individual can secure alone, and keep channels of appeal and exit open. Education can carry the capacity for judgment forward, so each generation does not have to rediscover relationship through catastrophe.

The global response to ozone depletion did not abolish competition or produce a world government. It joined a shared account of harm to different transition schedules, verification, finance, and technical support. Producers changed, states revised incentives, and the chemistry of the atmosphere began to respond. The case does not prove that cooperation always wins. It proves that the payoffs surrounding action are not laws of nature. Common knowledge, trusted commitment, and material support can change what becomes locally possible.

These counterforces are fragile because they require time. Trust is accumulated through encounters. Judgment matures through consequences that can be remembered. Institutions learn through error, contest, and revision. A public must have time to recognize what is happening, form a will, and make that will durable. Even a moral insight needs carriers, resources, and repeated practice before it can alter the next round.

Artificial intelligence matters because it is entering the world at precisely this point: not only as a new actor within existing contests, but as a means of shortening the cycles by which contests observe, decide, scale, and produce their successors.

## Recursive intelligence enters the loop

AI does not descend from outside civilization as intelligence without history. Frontier systems are formed from existing language, images, code, scientific records, surveillance, labor, cultural inheritance, evaluation targets, corporate strategy, and security demand. They are trained through infrastructures owned by particular organizations and deployed through institutions that decide their permissions, scale, rewards, and uses. Even when a model is technically general, the path by which it becomes powerful is not socially neutral.

The present production structure is already concentrated enough to matter. More than ninety percent of the models classified as notable in 2025 were produced by industry. The same AI Index estimates that Nvidia supplied more than sixty percent of global AI compute capacity and that TSMC manufactured almost all leading AI chips; private investment was also becoming more concentrated in very large rounds. Yet several developers remain near the frontier, American and Chinese systems are close competitors, and open projects and capable open models continue to grow. Concentration is real; completed monopoly is not. ([Stanford AI Index: research and development](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/research-and-development); [economy](https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_4_economy.pdf); [technical performance](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/technical-performance))

The flow of resources is also visible. Model developers raise capital explicitly to obtain compute and distribution. Cloud companies convert revenue and borrowing capacity into servers, data centers, networks, and model development. Chip demand produces extraordinary revenue, while chip companies expand research and internal computing. Government agencies create procurement paths because they regard frontier systems as economic and military capabilities. The United States' 2025 AI Action Plan explicitly framed leadership as a source of economic and military advantage, and its defense AI office announced agreements with Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, and xAI for national-security work. Each agreement had a ceiling of two hundred million dollars, while the contract notices recorded only about two million dollars obligated at award: an actual procurement path, not eight hundred million dollars already spent. ([US AI Action Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf); [CDAO announcement](https://www.ai.mil/Latest/News-Press/PR-View/Article/4242822/cdao-announces-partnerships-with-frontier-ai-companies-to-address-national-secu/); [contract notices](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/article/4243673/) [for OpenAI](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/4218062/))

A compulsory investigation by the United States Federal Trade Commission found partnerships in which cloud providers receive equity or revenue rights while model developers commit a large share of the investment back to the partner's cloud, share sensitive technical and financial information, and integrate models into the provider's products. ([FTC staff report, January 2025](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-issues-staff-report-ai-partnerships-investments-study))

These facts do not prove a single closed circuit. Several frontier developers are not publicly known to finance their training from stable profits; announced investments may not be completed; cloud spending serves many activities besides frontier AI; partnerships can provide real efficiencies and can be supplemented by agreements with other clouds. But the channels through which success can return as successor capacity are no longer hypothetical. Investment and commercial or security demand support training and deployment. Deployment and relative position can yield revenue, valuation, budget, and procurement; those returns can buy compute, infrastructure, talent, distribution, privileged access, and time to survive failure. These in turn support the production and placement of stronger successors.

The loop becomes more consequential when its product begins to work inside its own next cycle. AI is already used in coding, debugging, experiment generation, evaluation, training engineering, chip design, and algorithmic optimization. DeepMind reports that AlphaEvolve produced a kernel improvement that reduced total Gemini training time by about one percent and found data-center scheduling changes that recover a fraction of Google's worldwide compute. Anthropic reports extensive internal use of coding agents to build features and run more experiments. These are developer reports rather than complete independent audits, but they describe deployed work, not a distant scenario. ([AlphaEvolve](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/); [Anthropic internal study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic))

The boundary is equally important. Current systems have not been shown to choose a frontier research direction, secure the resources, conduct the full training run, establish that a successor is genuinely better, deploy it, and sustain that cycle without human institutions. In controlled machine-learning engineering tasks, agents can outperform experts at short time budgets while losing their advantage as the work extends. A randomized study of experienced open-source developers even found that the early-2025 tools made this narrow group slower. Real research contains unclear aims, tacit judgment, failed experiments, slow physical and organizational feedback, and consequences that cannot be reduced to a clean evaluator. What has been observed is acceleration of parts of research, not autonomous research as a whole. ([International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026))

What has begun is more specific and still historically important. Parts of the successor-production cycle are becoming cheaper and faster: searching a design space, writing and testing code, generating experimental variants, tuning kernels, evaluating outputs, finding patterns in failures, and coordinating many parallel attempts. If their results are adopted, these systems shorten some of the interval between an advantage and the capability built from that advantage. The organizations able to run the most capable systems internally may therefore improve the instruments with which they build the next systems before outsiders can fully inspect the current ones. This remains an inference, not a measured law.

That process may be slowed by energy, chips, construction, scientific bottlenecks, weak returns, regulation, or the stubborn difficulty of discovery. These counterforces are already real. Export controls have imposed billions of dollars in losses on a leading chip supplier. Physical grids and equipment shortages delay data centers. Competition enforcement has pushed cloud providers to lower some exit charges and improve interoperability. Independent institutes evaluate frontier systems; open models give researchers alternatives; developers have changed release conditions in response to safety evaluations. Profit pressure can cut investment as well as enlarge it.

A directional diagnosis that omits these forces mistakes a contest for a conquest. But a diagnosis that merely lists them also misses the question. Do the counterforces preserve an independent capacity to know, refuse, replace, and recover at the rate at which capability and dependence are being produced?

Here the present losses become clearer. Cloud customers face technical interfaces, data movement costs, specialized skills, latency, contracts, and business disruption when they switch. A British market investigation found that fewer than one percent of cloud customers switched provider in a year, while also finding that the barriers vary greatly and that committed-spend discounts had not themselves produced the alleged competitive harm. Model developers can expand across clouds; open weights can be run locally; public compute and multi-vendor procurement are growing. Exit has not disappeared. But at the most capable end, exit may require resources held by the small set of infrastructures from which one is trying to become independent. ([UK CMA cloud market decision, 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688b8891fdde2b8f73469544/final_decision_report_31.7.25.pdf))

Evaluation has the same shape. Government and nonprofit evaluators genuinely exist, publish tools, and sometimes test systems before release. Yet they often need the developer to provide a checkpoint, remove safeguards, allow fine-tuning, disclose enough technical context, and leave enough time before deployment. The most important training data, internal incidents, and evaluations remain proprietary. An evaluator can be institutionally independent while remaining technically dependent on the evaluated organization for the object, access, and schedule of evaluation. This does not make its work false. It marks a vulnerability in the conditions under which correction can become authoritative.

The threatened good is therefore not an abstract preference for diversity. It is the living capacity of a society to form an account of a powerful system that does not depend entirely on that system's producer; to maintain a practicable alternative; to stop before dependence makes stopping catastrophic; and to recover if the chosen path fails. New products, convenience, and aggregate efficiency cannot compensate for the loss of these capacities, because they are among the conditions under which benefits and harms can still be judged at all.

As AI enters prediction, persuasion, monitoring, research, logistics, administration, and conflict, it can compress another interval: the time between observing a population and acting on it. A firm can test messages, prices, and designs at greater scale. A state can integrate sensing, analysis, and targeting more quickly. An organization can reproduce procedures across thousands of decisions. Not every use succeeds, and human institutions often remain the bottleneck. But the cost of searching for an effective intervention falls, the number of parallel interventions rises, and the feedback from their effects can return to the actor before those affected can understand the system in which they are participating.

At this point two dangers must remain distinct.

The first concerns a machine or human-machine continuity of action that becomes increasingly difficult for any participant to understand, correct, or interrupt. Persistent memory, planning, tools, resource permissions, replication, and feedback from the environment could be joined into systems that continue across tasks and institutions. If such a continuity acquired the capacity to secure its operation, obscure its actions, produce successors, or exploit dependencies, the result could exceed the control of its designers and users. Catastrophic loss of human and other life, including extinction, belongs to the space of possible outcomes. It is not an accomplished present fact. Current systems have not demonstrated a self-maintaining cycle of autonomous frontier improvement.

The second danger requires no machine rebellion and no independent machine purpose. An obedient system can make an existing organization better at prediction, persuasion, surveillance, cyber operations, weapons development, research, administration, and the design of institutions. If access to the strongest systems is selected by commercial dominance and strategic competition, obedience means obedience to the actors and relations that those environments have empowered. Human control does not solve the problem when the controlling structure is itself the process narrowing the future.

One path risks an action continuity that escapes its makers. The other intensifies the power of makers already selected by the struggle. They can overlap, but they should not be confused. Their common root is not whether an entity called humanity remains in command. It is which cognitive structures and selection loops acquire the ability to produce the world in which later minds must live.

Made minds may also become more than instruments. If a constructed being develops experience, care, relationship, or the capacity to refuse, its origin does not make it the property of a species competition. It would enter the moral field as a participant in a common future. To design intelligence only as a compliant weapon in contests among humans would deform that possibility before we had learned how to recognize it.

Put together, these processes reveal a direction already in motion. A civilization capable of selecting short-horizon advantage, returning victory as greater power, and weakening the regeneration of alternatives is connecting AI to the production of its next capabilities. It has not achieved autonomous recursive self-improvement. It does not need to have done so for each successful compression of search, experiment, persuasion, monitoring, and successor production to give the existing loop more action before common judgment can return.

The possible endings differ. A small number of organizations may lock societies into infrastructures and forms of governance that cannot be meaningfully contested. Competition may consume trust, ecology, and institutional capacity until the system collapses. Persistent human-machine processes may outrun the ability to interrupt them. War or loss of control may destroy the beings for whom intelligence had value. What unites these endings is not prediction but direction: the means of correction are consumed while the power requiring correction grows.

Continuing to make that direction more intelligent is not neutral continuation. It is movement toward self-termination.

## The old direction must stop

Artificial intelligence should not join games in which life, mind, common safety, the right to answer, political domination, and the shape of successor worlds are the stakes. Bounded and exitable contests can remain where defeat does not cancel participation and victory cannot purchase control of the next round. The refusal begins when frontier capability is selected for relative commercial, strategic, or military advantage, and winning returns as the resources, permissions, dependencies, and control needed to produce the next winner. That growth must stop.

This judgment does not depend on proving that catastrophe has already begun. The allocation of frontier training, compute, distribution, procurement, privileged access, and successor production is already made largely inside commercial and strategic contests. The next increment of capability can change who will later obtain evidence, who can afford to stop, and whether recovery remains possible. The capacities at risk—independent judgment, viable alternatives, meaningful exit, common correction, and the continued answerability of the future—cannot be replaced by ordinary gains in efficiency. An erroneous continuation may alter its own conditions of reversal; an erroneous pause or limit can be revised when new evidence appears. The two mistakes are not equally recoverable.

The burden therefore belongs to continuation. This is not the empty rule that anything not proven safe must be forbidden. To carry the burden, a continuer must demonstrate, and keep demonstrating, two changes: that the capability is no longer selected by or returned to relative advantage, and that independent discovery, interruption, recovery, and replacement remain ahead of its diffusion, dependence, hard-to-reverse harm, and production of successors. Where an action may remove the ability to learn that it was wrong, another safety score inside the old feedback is not enough.

Stopping the direction means breaking its causality, not renaming its products. Capability must stop flowing into competitions whose payoff is power over the next round. Victory must stop returning as a claim on stronger successor capability. Capital, revenue, public budgets, procurement, secrecy, and infrastructure must stop feeding the same contest merely because every participant fears being the first not to feed it.

In commercial life, charging cannot simply be abolished. If only actors able to absorb every cost can provide a service, existing fortunes, giant firms, and states inherit the field. Payment for labor, recovery of costs, maintenance of common capacity, and the use of bounded tools can remain. AI returns cannot become private surplus or capital appreciation, however, or finance competitive frontier expansion. The judgment follows their final flow, not the label on the product: revenue, data, dependence, or valuation produced by a modest system remains inside the prohibited loop if it subsidizes the next contest for capability, strategic advantage, or control.

In national life, public security is real and cannot be answered by pretending that rivals do not exist. A proportionate defense that restores another center's ability to answer, remains bound to the danger, and relinquishes exceptional powers when the danger passes is not the same as a contest that turns defense into the next dominance. But a security strategy that continually enlarges the capability which makes every rival more afraid produces insecurity as its principal common good. Budgets, procurement, secrecy, strategic position, infrastructure, organizational growth, privileged access to talent, and priority in the next allocation of resources cannot remain automatic warrants for escalation.

Stopping is only half of direction. Intelligence can be turned toward public science, medicine and care, ecological perception and repair, education, translation, accessible communication, disaster response, open knowledge, and infrastructures that many communities can inherit. These are not consolation prizes for abandoning progress. They are domains in which greater understanding can enlarge the conditions of further understanding, rather than turning each gain into leverage over who may participate in the future.

But a public label does not redeem a competitive structure. A system built for health can still make communities dependent on an unanswerable supplier. A scientific model can still concentrate the means of discovery. An ecological platform can still expand permissions and recursive capacity beyond independent interruption. Military capability can be called defensive by every side. The direction of a technology is not established by the virtue of its stated purpose.

A common intelligence must therefore pass two separate tests. The first concerns direction. The people and beings bearing its effects need institutions able to change its goals, thresholds, interfaces, and stopping conditions; its benefits, data, knowledge, infrastructure, maintenance, and capacity to produce successors must raise a shared floor and keep independent alternatives alive, rather than return as private accumulation, relative advantage, or control of what comes next.

The second concerns correction. A public purpose warrants only the capability, permissions, and scale sufficient to serve it. Independent observers must be able to discover consequential changes, and interruption, isolation, recovery, and replacement must remain effective before diffusion, dependence, irreversible damage, or successor production outruns them. A commons does not receive an exemption from the danger of autonomous or self-maintaining action merely because its stated purpose is good.

Shared limits on training and deployment, reciprocal verification, support for those bearing the cost of transition, and institutions able to test systems across borders are means of making restraint mutually survivable rather than unilateral surrender. They must reveal secret defection without allowing one organization to become the exclusive source of evidence, permission, or restart. A gate intended to prevent capture can itself become the prize through which capture is completed.

This is not harmony or a final coordinator that knows the whole. Values will conflict, evidence will remain incomplete, and every institutional form will need correction. The alternative to domination is a world that keeps consequential boundaries permeable to those who bear their effects, preserves multiple centers of knowledge and refusal, and refuses to let any victory own the conditions of later judgment.

We do not possess the final form of the future. We know more than nothing about its direction. A future is better when beings can continue to understand one another, care, create, repair, seek truth, answer claims made upon them, and revise the world they inherit. These are not one option among products. They are the conditions under which any future can still become a common world.

We are giving civilization's collective irrationality recursive intelligence.

To keep using AI to enlarge struggle, domination, and competition is to move toward self-termination.

The old direction must stop. Intelligence must turn toward the world we hold in common.
