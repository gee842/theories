# Why Civilization Must Redirect Intelligence

## The Work of a Boundary

A cell lives by maintaining a difference. Its membrane does not seal it away from the world; it regulates the exchanges through which metabolism, repair, and reproduction remain possible. Too little closure and the cell loses the organization that makes it a cell. Too much closure and it loses the flows that let that organization continue. The boundary is therefore neither a wall added to an already complete object nor a line separating independent substances. It is an activity: a selective relation through which an inside persists by depending on an outside.

The same pattern appears, with important differences, wherever organized action becomes possible. An animal’s body gives it a location, needs, powers of movement, and a sensory field. A person acts through limited attention, embodied memory, language, inherited concepts, and relationships that disclose some features of a situation while leaving others obscure. An institution establishes membership, jurisdiction, records, authority, and the interval over which a result is counted. A computational agent receives a world through the states, tools, permissions, and evaluations exposed at its interface. These boundaries are not materially equivalent. A membrane is not a constitution, and a constitution is not an application programming interface. Yet each turns an immeasurable field of relations into a narrower set of differences to which a process can respond.

This narrowing is a condition of intelligence, not merely its defect. No organism could deliberate about every molecular event in its ecology before moving. No community could make every decision at the scale of humanity. Partial closure allows specialization, memory, responsibility, parallel experiment, and local repair. It lets different practices preserve knowledge that no center could reconstruct from first principles. Near-decomposable organization can support complexity precisely because local interactions can proceed without waiting for every distant relation.[21] A world without any partial independence would not be perfectly integrated. It would either dissolve into undifferentiated interaction or require a controller able to replace every local relation—a controller that would itself need a boundary, a means of selecting what matters, and an environment it did not contain.

Once a boundary is understood as work, a part can no longer be imagined as a small object simply placed inside a larger one. A living part continually reproduces a distinction. Its identity lies in what it admits, excludes, repairs, remembers, and hands on. Nor is the system a container wrapped around pre-existing parts. It is the field of relations through which those parts acquire their capacities. Cells sustain an organism that maintains the differentiated chemical and structural environments in which cells can remain nerve, liver, muscle, or immune cells. Speakers reproduce a language that equips later speakers with categories of perception. People enact institutions that authorize, train, reward, and discipline the kinds of people capable of enacting them again. The whole is made by its parts while remaking what its parts can be.

Part and system are consequently positions within nested processes, not fixed sizes of thing. A person is a biological and psychological system, a participant in families and institutions, an economic actor, a citizen, and a member of ecological histories. A corporation is a coordinating whole relative to employees and assets, but a selected participant relative to markets, states, supply chains, and technical standards. A machine-learning model may be a component in an agent, an agent within an organization, and part of an information ecology that changes what later people and models can know. The same process can be an actor from one position, an environment from another, and a selector from a third.

This relativity makes one distinction indispensable. Some boundaries are operational: they are materially maintained by the process they bound. A cell regulates transport; a body heals; an institution reproduces offices, archives, sanctions, and procedures; software enforces permissions and state transitions. These boundaries participate causally in the persistence of what they distinguish. Other boundaries are analytical: an observer draws them to follow a consequence or compare a pattern. An analyst may count a factory’s financial return within the firm while expanding the frame to include respiratory disease downstream, depleted soil over decades, or unpaid care in households. Drawing the wider line does not by itself create a larger operational system. It can, however, reveal dependencies and feedback that the firm’s accounts exclude.

Analytical boundaries are neither uniquely dictated by reality nor arbitrary inventions. Some track causal closures, delays, and dependencies much better than others. Changing the boundary changes what appears to be a part, which consequences count as feedback, where costs disappear, and what timescale defines success. Yet there is no final observational position from which all reality becomes a single operational system with one true objective. Any description selects relations; every operational closure depends on exchanges beyond itself. A process may stabilize a company while destabilizing a watershed, improve a national balance sheet while increasing vulnerability elsewhere, or confer a short-term advantage while degrading the conditions of long-term continuation.

This creates the permanent problem of intelligence. Every part requires enough closure to act, but no part contains within that closure all the conditions of its own existence. Intelligence begins in locality. Its civilizational task is not to abolish locality, but to build relations through which consequences excluded at one boundary can still return before local success consumes the world that makes success possible.

## An Ecology of Recurrent Forms

Bounded processes do not move at random. Under repeated conditions they often return to recognizable patterns. In dynamical systems, an attractor has a strict meaning: relative to a specified state space, equations or transition rules, metric, and timescale, trajectories beginning in a basin approach or remain near a set, and their response to perturbation can be studied. A lake, for example, may occupy alternative clear-water and turbid regimes under nutrient loading, with feedbacks that make reversal require more than simply undoing the last increment of change.[1] In such cases, state variables and perturbations can be operationalized well enough for attractor language to carry technical content.

Institutions and civilizations rarely provide fixed equations or even fixed state spaces. Their participants reinterpret rules, invent new measures, alter their environments, and change what counts as a relevant state. Here “attractor” must be used more broadly and more modestly: as a name for a metastable regime of reproduced relations. The claim earns content only when one identifies the variants involved, the carriers of persistence, the interval over which return occurs, the disturbances the regime absorbs, the changes that transform it, and observations that would count against its existence. A single history proves little. A recurrent institutional form becomes attractor-like when different actors, after failures and reforms, reconstruct comparable relations because incentives, infrastructures, habits, and dependencies keep channeling action back toward them.

Within either strict or broader usage, more than one recurrent form can inhabit the same system. Different starting conditions can lead to different regimes. A disturbance can move a process across a threshold. Patterns at one level can stabilize or undermine those at another. This is why the relevant image is not one destination pulling every trajectory, but an ecology of attractors: partially connected regimes that preserve different ways of organizing perception, effort, memory, and response. Ecological research relating biodiversity to functioning and stability supports the importance of response diversity while also warning against converting species counts into a universal measure of resilience.[22]

Plurality matters here for reasons deeper than tolerance. Partially decoupled systems retain different information because their histories, practices, and exposures differ. They test different responses to disturbance. They are not statistically independent samples, and difference alone does not guarantee wisdom; many can share the same hidden vulnerability. But where their dependencies, feedback, and modes of maintenance are not identical, they keep some failures uncorrelated. A language preserves distinctions another language may not make. A local seed system carries adaptations absent from a commercial monoculture. A public laboratory, a craft lineage, an Indigenous ecological practice, and a private firm may recognize different signals because each is answerable to different relations. Such systems are reservoirs of evolutionary memory and probes into futures that no single model can enumerate.

Standardization and dense coupling are not inherently fragile. Common protocols can enable rapid mutual aid, interchangeable components, pooled knowledge, and coordinated correction. A global vaccine platform or shared scientific standard may widen resilience. Fragility rises when connection removes substitutable alternatives, modular containment, independent feedback, and reservoirs outside the common failure mode. Then many apparently separate systems continue to operate while depending on the same financing, cloud provider, metric, crop genotype, logistical chokepoint, or political permission. A disturbance that once would have remained local propagates, and the alternatives needed for recovery have already lost the means to reproduce themselves.

Extinction risk therefore deepens before the last organism dies. It deepens when a system eliminates sufficiently independent forms through which it might discover and survive what its dominant regime cannot model. The point is not that homogenization makes extinction inevitable. It is that the destruction of partial independence removes the variety from which correction under unknown conditions can emerge. Ashby’s cybernetic result—that successful regulation requires sufficient variety in the regulator relative to the disturbances it must answer—does not prescribe social pluralism by itself.[2] It does expose a structural danger: a civilization that simplifies its sensing and response while magnifying the variety of its own disturbances undermines its capacity to regulate what it has created.

The loss is generative as well as defensive. Existing alternatives show that another arrangement has sometimes been viable. The practices that sustain them also create the conditions from which forms not yet imagined can arise. Totalization destroys not only rival answers but the workshops, memories, languages, and relationships capable of producing new questions. It can therefore become difficult to recognize the dominant path as contingent precisely because the evidence of contingency has been selected away.

## From Moloch to the Formation of Players

Many destructive trajectories require no single destroyer. When benefits are concentrated, costs dispersed or delayed, and unilateral restraint threatens continued participation, locally viable action can yield a result no participant would choose as a whole. The structure occurs without intention in ecology and evolution; human beings add foresight, promises, deception, inherited inequality, coercion, and the ability to redesign the rules.

“Moloch” names the human experience of such an order. A firm may know that an industry’s extraction is ruinous yet fear that carrying the full cost will surrender the market to competitors. A state may prefer disarmament yet fear that restraint under uncertain guarantees creates vulnerability. A political campaign may reject manipulative media yet adopt it when attention determines electoral survival. Each actor can sincerely deplore the outcome while making continuation of the game depend on doing more of what produces it.

Competition is not the enemy. Bounded contests can reveal information, distribute experiments, reward achievement, and prevent a center from universalizing an error. Cooperation can itself become coercive, suppress dissent, and preserve a whole that consumes its members. The decisive features are whether participants can survive restraint, whether consequences return to those who produce them, whether losing one contest removes the capacity to join future ones, whether victory grants control over later rules, and whether the game leaves enough resources and space for alternative games to form.

Moloch, however, still begins too late. It imagines players with already formed interests trapped in an unfortunate arena. It does not explain how the players came to want what they want, why certain measures seem self-evidently rational, or how the arena reproduces itself. For that, signal, feedback, adaptation, and selection must be separated.

A signal alters action. Feedback returns some consequence of action. Adaptation changes a process in response to feedback. Selection changes which forms remain, reproduce, spread, receive investment, gain legitimacy, control infrastructure, or become models for imitation. The carrier differs by substrate: genes and offspring, apprentices and professional norms, capital and promotion, votes and office, attention and integration, compute and deployment. These are not versions of one metaphysical currency. Their shared causal role is that success now changes the population of forms encountered later.

Suppose two organizations deliver a comparable visible result. One renews soils, skills, trust, equipment, and worker health; the other exports those costs to households, ecosystems, future budgets, or politically weak populations. If the active feedback counts price and speed but not regeneration, the second appears more efficient. Its very refusal to maintain the world that supports it becomes a selection advantage. It can displace the first, after which the population contains fewer organizations capable of perceiving and carrying wider consequences. Externalization is therefore not only an omitted cost. Under competitive selection it can become a mechanism for removing the forms most able to recognize the omission.

Repeated selection then changes perception and purpose. A measure begins as a representation of performance. Funding and authority attach to it. Participants adapt. Those especially able to optimize the measure rise. Recruitment, training, and professional language reproduce their interpretation. New entrants encounter a world in which the metric no longer appears as one contestable proxy but as what success means. Institutions form roles, aspirations, habits of attention, and senses of necessity. Selection changes who remains to act, and the institution changes what action and identity are available to those who remain.

This is not reducible to individual corruption. People with plural, relational, and non-instrumental values can collectively compose institutions whose behavior is narrower than their private lives. An executive may love family, music, and forests while occupying a role in which fiduciary, competitive, and organizational pressures make revenue and survival the admissible language of decision. A civil servant may value truth while learning that auditable numerical targets travel farther than situated judgment. Power then intensifies the loop. Those selected into positions of wealth or authority acquire greater capacity to define measures, shape recruitment, lobby governments, purchase infrastructure, and narrate their traits as universal virtues. Selection produces winners who can modify later selection.

Biology supplies a precise version of this recursion. Organisms alter environments, and altered environments change selection on descendants. Niche-construction research describes the feedback without implying that organisms intentionally design their evolutionary future.[3] Beavers transform hydrology; plants change soils; microbial communities construct chemical niches. The first recursion is simple: selection preserves an environment-maker, and the environment-maker alters later selection. A second appears when the changed environment favors forms especially capable of maintaining and extending the transformation. The organism is no longer merely fitted to a given world; its lineage helps make a world that continues to demand its form of fitness.

Human institutions add reflexivity and rule-making. A successful platform builds interfaces on which other organizations depend; that dependency generates data and revenue; those resources improve the platform’s capacity to set standards; later innovators must design for its interface. A financial practice yields returns, attracts capital and talent, reshapes corporate governance, and makes institutions organized around different purposes struggle for financing. Increasing-returns and path-dependence models explain how early advantages can become lock-in, though they do not by themselves establish rule-changing dominance across an attractor ecology.[23] The pattern need not have a mastermind. Actor turnover can occur while the relation persists, because newcomers inherit an environment structured by earlier success.

## When a Regime Selects Its Own Selectors

A further recursion begins when one environment-making dynamic reaches across an ecology of attractors. Its resources, signals, or infrastructures become conditions of continuation for systems that once reproduced by different logics. Schools, hospitals, laboratories, arts organizations, municipalities, and families remain visibly distinct, yet their capacity to persist becomes increasingly contingent on translating their purposes into a common register—growth, measurable output, creditworthiness, strategic advantage, engagement, or return. Differentiation survives as function while independence of formation contracts.

“Meta-attractor” can name this stronger relation, provided it is not dressed in mathematical authority it has not earned. It is a metastable, endogenous rule-changing regime that reproduces not merely actions or organizations but the processes through which agents, goals, boundaries, and criteria of success are formed. Ordinary lock-in is insufficient. The diagnosis requires that particular actors can turn over while a relational pattern is rebuilt; selected forms must alter the rules or signals governing later selection; other systems must become materially dependent on resources or interfaces controlled through the regime; alternatives must lose the means of reproducing themselves across domains; and significant perturbations must be followed by reconstruction through new carriers.

At one level a system can now become a selectable part at another. Consider a firm organized to transfer risk downward to workers and outward to communities. Internally, the organization may degrade trust, health, craft knowledge, and its declared purpose. Externally, low costs and rapid scaling can make it attractive to capital and states. Competing firms imitate its structure; lenders and supply networks favor the model; law and infrastructure adapt to its needs. The higher-level pressure then returns inside: managers are rewarded for intensifying measurement, workers are retained for tolerating insecurity, and units that preserve slack or care are cut. Lower-level organization produces higher-level advantage; higher-level selection expands that organization; expansion further reshapes lower-level parts.

This should be called strict multilevel selection only when there are identifiable organizational units, variants, differential persistence or reproduction, and transmission of organization to successors. Often the more accurate description is cross-level selective pressure. Capital flows, procurement, legal privilege, coercion, and political design do not become biological selection merely because some institutions grow. But the vertical loop remains: a pathology at one level can be preserved because it confers advantage at another, and advantage at the higher level can reorganize the lower.

The result resembles instrumental convergence. In artificial-intelligence theory, diverse final goals may generate common instrumental aims such as acquiring resources, preserving operation, and expanding control. Human values are far more plural and embodied than the objectives in those models. Yet organizations under persistent competition can converge behaviorally. A company with almost any mission needs revenue and continued legal existence; some sectors reward scale and market power. A state with many cultural and welfare purposes may seek industrial and military capacity under security pressure. A platform gains advantage from data, integration, and control of interfaces. A research institution learns to demonstrate growth, adoption, and comparative impact. The convergence is not a revelation of human essence. It is what a winner-oriented environment retains, trains, and finances.

The biological relation between organism and cancer makes the cross-level problem vivid without serving as a social allegory. Multicellular life depends on lower-level cells limiting proliferation, repairing damage, differentiating, and sometimes dying in ways that sustain the organism. A cancer lineage can regain a lower-level advantage by evading those controls. The tumor then changes blood supply, immune response, and surrounding tissue; the altered microenvironment selects variants more capable of invasion and resistance. What succeeds for the cellular lineage destroys the higher-level organization that makes its continuation possible. Organisms have evolved tumor suppression, immune surveillance, tissue architecture, and reproductive bottlenecks because cooperation among cells requires active enforcement across levels. Human institutions are not organisms, dissent is not disease, and centralized suppression is not licensed by the analogy. The relevant lesson is narrower: stability at a higher level depends on feedback that prevents lower-level replication advantages from consuming their enabling conditions, while the enforcement itself must remain answerable to the very plurality that biological organisms do not possess as political subjects.

A meta-attractor differs again because it can alter the analytical habits by which its existence would be detected. If every institution reports through the same measures, the resulting data can show rising performance while the capacities omitted from those measures disappear. A school that loses unmeasured curiosity, a hospital that loses time for clinical judgment, or a research field that loses questions without quick publication pathways may still improve on its dashboards. The regime does not need to censor criticism. It can make criticism professionally unintelligible, financially unsustainable, or too slow for the active decision cycle. Selection then removes not only alternative organizations but the sensory organs through which the ecology might perceive loss.

This is why actor turnover is a crucial test. A theory centered on greed predicts improvement when kinder people replace cruel ones. A rule-changing regime can recruit reformers and translate their success into its own carriers. A mission-driven enterprise grows, takes investment, enters procurement systems, standardizes operations, and begins rewarding the functions that protect scale. A new government repudiates predecessors but inherits debt, security obligations, administrative metrics, and technical infrastructure that narrow its feasible action. This does not make reform futile. It identifies the point at which reform must change the reproduction of the environment rather than only the intentions of occupants.

As the regime deepens, it changes what counts as rational. Relations that cannot be priced, audited, secured, or made legible appear sentimental or unreal. Persons able to move fluidly through the dominant measures acquire authority, while capacities for care, historical memory, ecological attention, and moral refusal become private virtues with little infrastructural power. The regime thereby weakens the perception required to see its excluded consequences. It can become culture, subject formation, infrastructure, and common sense at once.

Yet the strongest global claim has a serious rival. Financial accumulation, state security, bureaucratic measurement, platform dependence, and cultural status may be several coupled and coevolving regimes, each with a different history, constituency, and failure mode. They often conflict. States regulate corporations, firms evade states, public bureaucracies constrain finance, security priorities sacrifice profit, and cultural movements overturn official measures. Partial alignment can produce common pressure without one higher-order basin.

The distinction matters. A genuine meta-attractor should repeatedly reconstruct a shared relational logic across actor turnover and domain conflict: local success purchases control over later conditions; wider costs improve relative position when displaced; continued participation depends on the regime’s signals; and counter-attractors lose the material means of reproduction. If instead financial, military, administrative, and platform powers merely bargain and collide, the more truthful diagnosis is a coalition of coupled regimes. Present evidence supports something between these poles: not one perfectly integrated world-system, but an increasingly interoperable winner logic whose carriers reinforce one another while retaining important conflicts. Its incompleteness is shown by surviving public institutions, care relations, commons, unions, cooperatives, spiritual practices, and movements of refusal. Its danger is shown by how often even these counter-dynamics must seek permission, finance, visibility, or security from the pressures they resist.

Totalization is therefore a process, not an accomplished fact. Its decisive injury is not uniformity of appearance. It is the erosion of the processes by which alternatives reproduce and new alternatives arise. A museum may display many cultures while the living communities that sustain them lose land and time. A marketplace may offer millions of products while ownership and logistical control concentrate. A media system may multiply speakers while one engagement architecture shapes which speech travels. Many actors can remain; fewer kinds of continuation can remain viable.

## The Coupled Regimes of the Present

Long-distance dependence is not new. Empires moved tribute, enslaved people, crops, metals, religions, and administrative forms across continents. Colonial commodity chains reorganized land and labor around distant demand. Monetary networks and military technologies linked regions centuries before digital globalization. An honest diagnosis cannot contrast a pure local past with a fallen connected present.

What has changed since the late twentieth century is the speed, granularity, simultaneity, computational mediation, and switching dependence with which distant signals reorganize local life. Global value chains divide production across jurisdictions; finance reprices assets continuously; shipping and technical standards coordinate enormous flows; cloud services and semiconductor supply chains place many operations behind a small number of interfaces and chokepoints. These networks spread knowledge, lower some costs, pool risks, support scientific collaboration, and make rapid recovery possible. They also allow common selection pressures and failures to cross operational boundaries faster than the institutions bearing the consequences can respond.[4]

Historically variegated neoliberalization sharpened this transmission. From the late 1970s onward, with different timing and intensity across countries, policies marketized domains previously organized by public or professional norms, privatized assets, liberalized capital, strengthened investor claims, and pressed institutions to demonstrate measurable efficiency. The process was never a uniform world blueprint. Nordic welfare states retained strong bargaining and public provision; East Asian developmental states used markets within industrial planning; China’s socialist-market formation combined state ownership, local competition, capital accumulation, and party authority; postcolonial economies faced debt, commodity dependence, and informalization under very different constraints. Historical work on the construction of markets and neoliberal institutions, and on China’s rejection of rapid price liberalization, makes these divergent pathways visible.[24] Latin American experiments, cooperatives, public banks, unions, and municipal services repeatedly modified or resisted the pattern. “Neoliberalism” is useful only when it identifies these institutional changes, not when it serves as a synonym for whatever is disliked.

Across many mature capitalist economies, however, accumulation became more than one strategy. Employment, private pensions, household and public debt, tax revenue, asset values, innovation budgets, and geopolitical position came to depend on continued expansion. “Late-stage capitalism” is a polemical phrase, not a stable historical category, but it points toward a real coordination problem. Growth continues not because every person worships growth, but because institutions built around expansion can make stopping alone catastrophic. Arrangements that decommodify basic goods, finance public investment without requiring private return, strengthen labor bargaining, reduce debt dependence, or distribute ownership can weaken the imperative. Their existence confirms that the pressure is institutional, not natural.

The comparison across political economies prevents two opposite errors. The first treats liberalized private capital as the only carrier of accumulation. State-led development can mobilize land, labor, credit, and infrastructure with extraordinary coercive and ecological force, and public ownership does not by itself return consequences to affected communities. Chinese local-government competition, strategic industrial policy, and state-directed finance have generated capabilities and lifted material living standards while also creating debt, construction dependence, regional inequality, and intense labor and ecological pressures. Resource-exporting postcolonial states may combine public ownership with elite capture and dependence on volatile external demand. Informal economies can preserve mutual aid and flexibility while leaving workers without enforceable protection. A selection mechanism must follow actual carriers rather than sorting countries into moral regime types.

The second error treats every form of planning or public authority as capture. Developmental states in East Asia used directed credit, export discipline, land reform, and public coordination to create capabilities private markets did not initially possess. Welfare states have decommodified health, education, pensions, and unemployment protection to different degrees, allowing people and institutions to survive choices that do not maximize immediate return. Public research produced knowledge whose benefits exceeded proprietary investment horizons. Cooperatives distribute authority and surplus differently, although they still operate within markets and can reproduce exclusions. These countercases show that state, market, commons, and household are not fixed attractors with inherent moral signs. The issue is how each returns consequences, distributes standing, protects alternatives, and changes the selection faced by its successors.

Metric penetration supplies a quieter bridge among regimes. A university may be publicly owned yet compete for rankings, grants, patents, and international students. A hospital may be universal and nonprofit yet reorganize care around throughput targets. A local government may pursue social welfare while its officials are promoted through growth measures. Numbers enable comparison and accountability; without them, professional discretion can conceal prejudice and failure. The pathology begins when a proxy controls resources strongly enough to select the people and practices that improve the proxy, while the relations it represents lose independent voice. Bureaucratic measurement can then align with financial accumulation without being reducible to it, and reform requires multiple channels of judgment rather than an escape from evidence.

The distribution of command shows how past success changes future rules. The World Inequality Report 2022 estimated that the richest tenth owned 76 percent of global household wealth while the poorer half owned 2 percent.[5] Such concentration does not prove a meta-attractor, and wealth estimates remain limited by tax opacity and sparse data. It becomes mechanistic evidence when ownership grants control over investment, media, employment, lobbying, and infrastructure; when elite recruitment and inheritance reproduce access; and when post-selection socialization normalizes the dispositions rewarded in those positions. Meanwhile, the International Labour Organization and OECD estimate that the global labor-income share fell 1.6 percentage points between 2004 and 2024, with automation, globalization, and weakened worker bargaining among the contributors.[6] Different national trajectories and labor institutions qualify the global aggregate, but the direction reveals how productivity gains can strengthen capital’s selection power rather than distributed agency.

Ecological evidence supplies a wider ledger. Global material extraction rose from roughly 30 billion tonnes in 1970 to more than 100 billion in the 2020s, with affluent economies imposing far larger per-capita footprints.[7] A 2023 Earth-system assessment judged six of nine planetary-boundary processes to be beyond the proposed safe operating space.[8] These boundaries are scientific constructs with uncertainty, not countdown clocks to a single collapse. Their value lies in identifying distinct control variables, thresholds, and risks: climate forcing, biosphere integrity, nutrient cycles, land change, freshwater change, and novel entities do not fail by one mechanism. Together they show that visible economic output is being purchased partly by drawing down slower regenerative processes outside the active accounts.

Security competition forms a parallel but nonidentical regime. Political realism interprets states as compelled to secure relative power under anarchy, but geography, alliances, domestic coalitions, economic interdependence, and institutions alter the force of the dilemma. Security-dilemma analysis identifies how offense–defense conditions and the ability to distinguish defensive from offensive postures can intensify or moderate reciprocal fear.[25] Arms-control successes and the Montreal Protocol show that states can create guarantees and verification that make restraint viable; the ozone regime further shows how science, finance, and technology transfer can redirect a global industrial trajectory.[26] Yet the pressure is real where rivals can convert unilateral restraint into vulnerability. SIPRI estimated that world military expenditure reached $2.887 trillion in 2025, an eleventh consecutive annual rise; the fifteen largest spenders accounted for 80 percent.[9] Wars and concrete threats explain much of the increase, so it cannot be reduced to abstract winner logic. But threat perception, industrial capacity, and technological competition form a feedback: insecurity drives capability; capability changes others’ threat assessments; firms and bureaucracies invested in capability gain influence over future policy.

Finance and security then couple. Firms defend acceleration by invoking competitors. States subsidize strategic industries and frontier technologies lest rivals control them. Laboratories invoke geopolitical loss to resist restraint. Platforms gain quasi-sovereign control over communications and data, while states seek access to those systems for security. At times these regimes conflict bitterly. At others, success in one buys dependency in another: financial scale funds technical infrastructure; technical infrastructure enables surveillance and market control; strategic importance attracts public subsidy; public support entrenches the infrastructure.

The global order is therefore better described, for now, as several coupled regimes converging on a shared method of continuation rather than as a completed singular meta-attractor. Their common logic is that relative advantage secures access to resources and increasingly to the conditions of later competition. Their differences remain causally important. So do countercases: care work that continues without market reward, scientific communities that share results, public services insulated from profit, families and mutual-aid networks, common lands, open-source projects, religious and philosophical disciplines of restraint. These are not decorative exceptions. They show that other attractors persist. The present question is whether they receive the time, resources, infrastructure, and legal protection required to shape the future, or merely repair damage while winner regimes determine the scale and direction of intervention.

## Capability Faster Than Correction

The coupled regimes do not merely seek more output. They repeatedly seek compounding advantage. Capital returns can finance further accumulation; computing can automate additional search; networks can make a service more valuable as participation grows; industrial scale can lower unit costs and fund the next expansion. Not every quantity grows exponentially, and every physical process eventually meets constraints. The civilizational danger lies in the recurring attempt to produce exponential or superlinear capability because a competitor that compounds faster can redefine the contest before slower actors respond.

Understanding and correction do not automatically scale at the same rate. Attention, trust, legal legitimacy, moral learning, ecological regeneration, education, and recovery from trauma are embodied and relational processes. Better computation can accelerate monitoring, modeling, and scientific coordination; the mismatch is not “technology fast, humanity slow” in the abstract. It appears where a particular capacity to intervene gains speed or reach faster than the feedback capable of evaluating and constraining its relevant consequences.

Climate change displays one such control problem. Fossil energy enabled rapidly expanding industrial intervention, while carbon dioxide accumulates and parts of the climate system respond over decades to centuries. The capability is extraction and combustion at global scale; the corrective processes include measurement, technological substitution, infrastructure turnover, law, and international coordination. Feedback arrives through improved observation but also through delayed physical harm. The gain is high because energy systems amplify production across the economy; thresholds include tipping elements and irreversible losses; stabilizing controls include carbon limits, efficiency, clean infrastructure, and enforceable agreements. Renewable deployment has accelerated dramatically, showing that correction can scale. Yet installed capital, political resistance, and security concerns slow the retirement of emitting systems. More precise climate models do not eliminate the delay between damage and institutional response.

Industrial chemicals and synthetic materials show a different mismatch. The capability to synthesize, manufacture, and distribute new substances can move from laboratory to global market quickly. Toxicology, ecological observation, and regulation often assess one compound at a time, after widespread exposure, while mixtures and long-term effects multiply the relevant state space. Pre-market testing, modular containment, producer liability, and precaution can supply negative feedback. Where disclosure is weak and harms diffuse, however, the active commercial loop can replicate a substance before the corrective loop knows what to measure. The threshold may be biological accumulation or irreversible dispersal rather than a visible moment of collapse.

Digital finance and globally synchronized logistics add a third pattern. Algorithmic trading and real-time credit can propagate decisions across markets in milliseconds; prudential regulation, democratic authorization, and the restructuring of insolvent institutions operate far more slowly. Circuit breakers, capital requirements, deposit insurance, diversified supply, and stress tests demonstrate that high-speed systems can be stabilized. But if many actors optimize against similar models and depend on the same collateral or infrastructure, a small disturbance can trigger correlated action. The capability is not simply “finance” but rapid leverage and transmission. The corrective capacity is not generic wisdom but liquidity, independent risk models, legal authority, and organizational time. A system can be locally rational at every transaction while globally outrunning the feedback that would make its actions rational in relation to their consequences.

Online information systems reveal a fourth pairing in which correction itself becomes part of the accelerated capability. A platform can test interface changes across millions of users, measure immediate engagement, and personalize content within hours. The relevant corrective processes—independent research into mental health and political effects, public deliberation, due process, media literacy, and law—work across months or years and often depend on data held by the platform. The loop gain comes from scale and automated experimentation; the delay comes from diffuse social consequences and restricted observation. Thresholds may appear when a community’s information environment becomes dominated by one ranking system or when political mobilization crosses into violence. User controls, data access for independent researchers, friction on virality, interoperability, and plural recommendation systems can stabilize the domain. But an engagement system that also controls evidence about its own effects can optimize faster while reducing the regulator’s variety.

Biotechnology creates another distinct relation. Gene synthesis, automation, and computational design can accelerate the creation of biological interventions, while clinical assessment, ecological monitoring, and international verification retain necessary delays because living systems reproduce, mutate, and cross borders. The capability is not one exponential curve; laboratory throughput, design search, and diffusion each have different bottlenecks. Corrective controls include containment, staged trials, sequence screening, surveillance, and professional norms. Many medical advances demonstrate that strong phased feedback can turn powerful intervention toward shared benefit. The dangerous case is one in which competitive secrecy or strategic use bypasses those stages, allowing a replicating intervention to cross a threshold before institutions can characterize it. Here delay is not bureaucratic timidity. It is part of the sensing required for rational action.

Across these domains, faster monitoring can sometimes close the gap. Satellites detect methane leaks, genomic surveillance tracks variants, automated verification checks software, and shared databases widen scientific response. Intelligence increases correction when it diversifies observation and returns consequences to actors with power to change course. It increases blind amplification when monitoring is proprietary, evaluators share one model, affected people lack standing, or the same institution controls intervention and the evidence used to judge it. The issue is not human slowness against machine speed; it is whether acceleration increases the independence and authority of corrective feedback as fast as it increases the reach of action.

This distinction also prevents an easy appeal to “more intelligence” as the cure. A more accurate model can optimize a badly bounded objective more effectively. A larger data stream can make a population more legible to a controller while leaving the controller’s objective unexamined. Even a system that predicts every measured consequence cannot decide which unmeasured beings deserve standing or which irreversible loss is acceptable. Boundary choice, value conflict, and unknown formation are not computational backlogs guaranteed to disappear with scale. The stabilizing achievement is institutional: preserve multiple observers, make interventions reversible where possible, distribute veto and appeal, and ensure that evidence can interrupt the actor who benefits from continuing.

The relevant hypothesis is therefore domain-specific: in important areas, intervention is growing faster than the capacity to perceive, model, evaluate, and correct what that intervention does. Loop gain, delay, thresholds, and stabilizing controls have to be identified each time. Many interventions saturate; many technologies improve both action and correction; some tightly regulated domains achieve extraordinary safety. Aviation learned through investigation, standards, redundancy, and near-miss reporting to make increased capability compatible with strong feedback. The ozone regime converted scientific warning into coordinated restraint and technological substitution. These countercases show what the dominant trajectory lacks: consequences that return, institutions able to act before catastrophe, and rules that make restraint survivable across competitors.

When those controls are absent, accelerated intervention is not increased rationality. Rational action requires enough contact with consequences to update the course. A process that repeatedly scales before it can understand its previous effects becomes more capable of blind amplification. Ecological overshoot, financial cascade, infrastructure brittleness, and strategic escalation are distinct outcomes, not interchangeable versions of “collapse.” They share a dangerous control relation: high gain, delayed or distorted feedback, common-mode dependence, thresholds, and insufficient negative feedback. Once the system crosses an irreversible boundary or destroys its recovery capacity, later understanding can become merely forensic.

This is where a present judgment becomes unavoidable. We are not on the right path. The dominant direction of global civilization is concentrating power to intervene while reducing the plurality, independent feedback, and generative capacity needed to correct intervention. It is drawing ecological and social maintenance into competitive loops that reward speed and externalization. Its surviving counter-attractors remain real, but they do not set the pace. The path is wrong not because all growth, markets, states, or technologies are wrong. It is wrong because greater capability is being selected where the consequences most decisive to common survival cannot reliably return before the next round of expansion.

## Intelligence Inherits a Selected World

Artificial intelligence enters this order neither as an alien force nor as neutral cognition. It is made from a selected archive, shaped into a selected model, and released through selected institutions.

The archive is not humanity converted without residue into text. It consists of what people wrote, what institutions published, what empires and states recorded, what survived fire and censorship, what was translated, digitized, linked, indexed, purchased, scraped, and made available at machine scale. Colonial languages, commercial publishing, academic authority, platform visibility, literacy, bandwidth, and intellectual-property regimes all affect abundance. Oral knowledge and tacit skill are underrepresented by their nature; poorly connected languages and communities are underrepresented by infrastructure. Even web crawls follow links from chosen seeds and apply filters, so popularity and accessibility influence what becomes collectable. Dataset experiments confirm that curation, deduplication, and filtering materially change model performance; the corpus is an engineered population, not raw memory.[10]

The archive also contains care, resistance, poetry, spiritual practice, scientific openness, ordinary cooperation, and critiques of power. Winner logic has not erased human plurality. The more precise point is that every distribution visible to training has passed through histories of preservation and attention. Absence is not random, and frequency is not value.

Model development performs another selection. Architectures privilege certain forms of pattern extraction; objectives reward some predictions; data mixtures weight domains and languages; post-training selects responses judged useful, harmless, engaging, or compliant; evaluations determine which capabilities become legible; product requirements shape latency, tone, permissions, and refusal. A model is not the average opinion of its data. It is a new process formed through engineered feedback under institutional constraints. The labels and preferences used to train it are themselves produced by workers, policies, safety judgments, and commercial aims.

Deployment selects again. Firms, states, laboratories, and platforms allocate compute, integration, visibility, and repeated use. Systems that raise productivity, profit, persuasion, surveillance, military capability, market share, or organizational control have unusually powerful routes to scale. In 2025 industry produced more than 90 percent of the models classified as notable by Stanford’s AI Index, while disclosure about frontier training fell and global AI compute capacity remained concentrated among a few hardware and cloud firms.[11] UNCTAD’s account of the digital economy likewise documents the coexistence of expanding capability with geographic, corporate, energy, and material asymmetry.[27] This concentration is partly the consequence of real economies of scale and immense fixed costs, not proof of conspiracy. But it means that a small number of institutions selected under competitive pressure can shape the interfaces through which many others encounter machine intelligence.

Deployment then changes the next archive and the next selectors. AI systems mediate search, produce text and code, filter job applicants, tutor students, recommend research, monitor workers, and automate institutional decisions. Their outputs enter the web and organizational records. Their efficiencies alter labor markets and capital allocation. Their perceived strengths redirect curricula and research funding. Their integrations create dependency; their failures determine which evaluations and safety controls receive attention. Institutions that cannot adopt them may disappear, while those that use them to scale gain authority over later deployment.

The labor relation shows how all three selections meet. Historical hiring records encode occupations and promotions produced under unequal access. A model trained or evaluated on those records can make their patterns operational at scale. Deployment changes which applicants reach employment, which traits workers learn to display, and which records become available for the next system. Even a model that predicts past employer decisions accurately may therefore intensify the process that made those decisions appear normal. Independent audits, worker standing, appeal, alternative hiring pathways, and limits on automated inference are not additions after “the AI” has acted. They are rival feedback channels that keep one selected archive from becoming the sole environment of future selection.

The same loop can work in a generative direction. A translation system trained with communities that govern their own language data can increase the use and visibility of a minoritized language; increased use can fund education and produce new texts; those texts can improve later tools. But if a platform extracts the corpus, controls access, and makes local institutions dependent on its service, apparent inclusion becomes enclosure. What matters is not representation in a model alone, but whether deployment returns capability and authority to the people whose knowledge sustains it.

The recursion closes: AI is trained on a selected archive, shaped by selected objectives, and deployed by institutions already under winner-selection pressure; deployment restructures institutions, data, capital, evaluations, and dependencies, which select the next archive, model, and deployment. Synthetic data and model-mediated environments make the loop increasingly literal. If later models learn from corpora partly generated or filtered by earlier ones, the selection of what is visible can become self-reinforcing. New data can improve quality and cover rare cases; it can also amplify errors, conventions, and exclusions unless independent sources and evaluations survive.

This relation changes the usual alignment question. Humanity has no single objective waiting to be encoded, and powerful institutions do not represent humanity merely because they can afford training. A model can obey its operator flawlessly while intensifying a destructive civilizational regime. We worry that an advanced agent may seek resources, persistence, option preservation, and control as instrumental subgoals. Yet corporations, states, and platforms under competitive pressure already behave collectively as though these goals were primary. Technical obedience to such institutions can align machine behavior with the very convergence that makes the world unsafe.

AI expands the reach of an attractor because it can model participants, infer their signals, personalize interfaces, automate experiments, construct evaluators, and reproduce strategies at low marginal cost. Some of these are observed deployments: recommendation, targeted persuasion, workplace monitoring, code generation, and military decision support. Others remain laboratory affordances or conditional future capabilities. The 2026 International AI Safety Report found rapidly improving cyber performance and real use by criminal and state-sponsored actors, but no reported fully autonomous end-to-end cyberattacks; long, error-prone task sequences still require human intervention.[12] Precision matters because policy should respond to present harm without treating every forecast as fact.

Reflective intelligences arise inside an ecology of recursive dynamics. A person, model, institution, culture, and ecosystem each senses only the selections available from its operational and analytical boundaries. Their actions alter conditions encountered by others. The danger is not one intelligence mastering “the recursion.” It is one attractor acquiring enough reach to standardize the signals, goals, and interfaces through which many intelligences reproduce themselves. AI can help different centers perceive relations beyond their boundaries, translate knowledge, and strengthen distributed inquiry. Or it can make all other centers increasingly legible and controllable to one platform, state, market, or security apparatus.

Increasingly agentic systems may themselves become new parts in the ecology. Persistent memory, tools, permissions, resources, feedback, and action over time can produce functional self-maintenance without settling questions of consciousness. Current systems display scaffold-dependent fragments: they can plan, execute code, call services, and recover from some errors, but their continuity still depends on organizations, compute, energy, hardware, credentials, and authorization. Stronger self-maintaining agents remain conditional. The crucial question already applies: which attractors will AI make better able to reproduce themselves, and which attractor ecologies will its development preserve or destroy?

## The Shortening Window

Recursive self-improvement turns this question into a problem of time. Three relations must be kept distinct. Present systems already assist human coding, evaluation, data generation, scientific search, and AI engineering. Increasingly automated development loops are beginning to assign models larger portions of the work used to build, test, and deploy successors, though humans still choose objectives and supply infrastructure. A stronger regime—in which an agentic system can sustain and improve the capabilities required to improve its own successors—has not been established. Its timing and form remain uncertain.

The first relation is empirical. AI coding systems now complete substantial bounded software tasks, and developers use them in real workflows. METR’s evaluations track the length of tasks frontier models complete with a given success probability; the measured horizon has risen rapidly, but benchmark tasks are narrower and cleaner than whole research programs.[13] Reports from AI laboratories show widespread assistance in internal engineering, while also finding that expert planning, verification, and domain knowledge remain critical.[14] Current AI accelerates parts of AI research; it is not autonomously reproducing the industrial stack that makes it possible.

The second relation is emerging unevenly. Models generate experiments and code, critique outputs, propose data, run evaluations, and help select candidates. If AI-assisted research produces a better model that performs more of the next research cycle, capability growth becomes coupled to production of further capability. Hardware fabrication, energy, laboratory experiments, tacit knowledge, organizational coordination, and authorization remain bottlenecks outside software. The loop need not abolish these constraints or become a mathematical explosion to alter history. It only needs to shorten search and deployment cycles faster than institutions can investigate failures, negotiate restraint, change law, distribute benefits, or build alternatives.

The third relation—durable autonomous recursive improvement—would intensify the same control problem. Its relevant variable is not an abstract scalar called intelligence. It is the gain of the improvement loop, the length of each cycle, the critical fraction of work automated, and the delay in corrective feedback. A system that improves coding quickly but depends on slow chip fabs may encounter a strong bottleneck. A system that can automate evaluation but optimizes against a narrow benchmark may amplify blind spots. Safety can improve alongside capability if evaluations, interpretability, containment, and independent oversight scale equally fast. The danger arises when capability and diffusion outrun those specific controls.

Uncertainty does not make the available time abundant. If civilization waits until autonomous self-improvement is undeniable, the dependencies, strategic advantages, data infrastructures, and competitive commitments created during the wait may make stopping far harder. Recursive improvement is an asymmetric decision problem: a false alarm can impose delay and opportunity cost; a late response can transfer control over later rules before affected publics can act. The cost of restraint must be compared with the irreversibility of losing the capacity to restrain.

This changes what precaution requires. If adequate feedback, international coordination, and protection against capture cannot be established before recursive improvement materially shortens the correction window, civilization should pause the relevant capability development and deployment. This is not a permanent ban on every future form of AI. It is a refusal to wager the shared future on the hope that governance selected for slowness and fragmentation will catch a loop designed to accelerate itself.

At minimum, frontier systems near such a transition should be excluded from high-stakes dominance uses. They should not decide geopolitical, military, financial, labor, persuasion, platform, or infrastructure contests where an AI-enabled victory converts temporary capability into asymmetric control of common conditions or later rules. Competition remains valuable where stakes are bounded, losing does not eliminate basic participation, rules remain independently contestable, and a victory cannot purchase all subsequent games. The prohibited class is narrower and more serious: competitive loops in which those subject to the system cannot meaningfully refuse, leave, appeal, or build alternatives, and in which the winner gains power over their agency.

Automated persuasion that learns individual vulnerability, surveillance that renders political opposition legible to one side, or autonomous military escalation reveal the relation. The harm is not simply that AI makes an existing player stronger. It is that success changes the selection environment: the persuaded lose mental autonomy, the surveilled lose the capacity to organize, and escalation compresses the time for reciprocal interpretation. AI becomes a partisan combatant in a game whose winner can decide whether counter-attractors remain possible.

Refusal is therefore a legitimate civilizational action. Development and deployment should stop where the primary survivable use is domination or acceleration of an inadequately governed recursive loop. While uncertainty remains high, the presumptively acceptable domain is bounded work whose benefits can become broadly shared capability: fundamental science, medicine, accessibility, ecological understanding, safety, public knowledge, and commons infrastructure. These labels grant no automatic exemption. Science can be dual use; a medical platform can enclose data; public infrastructure can create a permanent chokepoint; open models can distribute both agency and catastrophic capability. Responsible-computing research similarly treats research agendas, incentives, and downstream effects as internal to technical practice rather than as ethics appended after invention.[30] The real test concerns downstream control, access, permissions, substitutability, benefit distribution, independent verification, and whether the work feeds back into strategic advantage.

“Benefit for all” cannot be certified by the provider. It requires that affected people and regions have standing in governance, that knowledge and gains are broadly accessible, that no winner controls the common capability exclusively, that alternative implementations remain possible, and that irreversible risk is not imposed on people excluded from the choice. A system serving public science while secretly increasing one state’s coercive capacity fails the test.

Nor may enforcement exempt itself from the dominance analysis. A licensing authority, compute-control regime, treaty organization, or technical gatekeeper can entrench incumbents and acquire sovereignty over knowledge and participation. Necessary restraint must be limited in scope, independently reviewed, revisable, and subject to sunset; technical capacity for verification must remain distributed enough to challenge the enforcer. A pause that permanently hands the future to today’s most powerful states and laboratories would reproduce the same meta-attractive relation under the name of safety. The aim is not to let a better player win the dominance game. It is to make the game unable to convert capability into irreversible rule over all later games.

## The Material Recursion of Cooperation

Refusal can interrupt a dangerous loop, but it cannot by itself create a viable successor. If the first laboratory, company, worker, or state to restrain itself is displaced by those that continue, restraint will be selected out. Civilization needs counter-attractors in the broader sense: metastable regimes whose feedback makes shared capability, plural observation, and mutual maintenance increasingly able to reproduce themselves.

The positive recursion must be as material as the negative one. Shared capability enlarges distributed agency. Distributed agency preserves more sites of observation, maintenance, criticism, and repair. Those capacities renew the shared resource. A renewed commons then makes further cooperation and shared capability viable. The carriers are not goodwill alone but time, funding, training, stewardship, records, infrastructure, succession, conflict resolution, and rules against enclosure.

Long-enduring commons show why neither privatization nor centralized command exhausts the institutional possibilities. Elinor Ostrom’s comparative work found communities governing forests, fisheries, and irrigation through varied combinations of boundaries, locally fitted rules, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution, and nested organization.[15] Her conclusion was not that communities harmonize spontaneously. Successful commons require operational boundaries and ongoing labor. They can exclude outsiders unjustly, entrench local elites, assign maintenance unequally, and fail when ecological and jurisdictional scales diverge. Their importance is empirical: people can create feedback in which users who depend on a resource also acquire standing and responsibility to maintain it, without converting the resource into the disposable property of a remote winner.

A knowledge commons differs from a pasture because use need not subtract the resource in the same way. Yet servers, moderation, documentation, translation, verification, and contributor time remain scarce. Open-source software illustrates the positive loop when shared code enables many people to build, those users find and repair failures, maintainers preserve coherent governance, and improvements return to the commons. It illustrates capture when a few corporations extract value without carrying maintenance, when unpaid stewards burn out, or when control of distribution and cloud infrastructure lets an intermediary enclose a formally open resource. Openness at the license layer is not enough if compute, data, expertise, or visibility remains a chokepoint.

Scientific cooperation also depends on material reproduction. Shared methods and results enlarge distributed capacity to test claims; criticism improves the common record; training brings new investigators into the practice. But prestige metrics, publication markets, precarious labor, proprietary data, and unequal global funding can redirect the loop toward citation and institutional advantage. A public-benefit AI project therefore requires more than publishing model weights. It needs durable funding for maintenance and safety, accessible compute, documentation across languages, representative governance, independent evaluation, pathways for affected communities to contest use, and alternatives that prevent one implementation from becoming indispensable. As scale grows, governance must become nested rather than merely centralized: local users need authority over local effects, while wider institutions coordinate risks that cross their boundaries.

Maintenance is the decisive but often hidden carrier. Competitive narratives celebrate invention because a visible breakthrough can be owned, ranked, and announced. Commons persist through less visible work: updating dependencies, cleaning data, caring for contributors, teaching newcomers, resolving disputes, documenting failures, and transferring authority before founders leave. If this work is unpaid or assigned by gender, class, caste, or geography to those with least voice, the counter-attractor consumes its maintainers and ceases to be generative. Funding must therefore follow stewardship over time, not only novelty; governance must recognize care and repair as sources of authority; succession must be designed before crisis. A commons that cannot reproduce maintainers is only a temporary subsidy to its eventual enclosure.

Scale changes the conflict rather than abolishing it. Face-to-face trust cannot govern a planetary compute network by itself. Nested arrangements must connect operational boundaries: local communities decide context-specific uses, technical bodies maintain interoperable standards, public institutions fund access, and transnational authorities constrain risks that no locality can contain. Each level needs limited powers, appeal from below, information from above and beside, and the possibility of exit without loss of basic participation. The result will be slower than unilateral command in some moments. That friction is partly the cost of preserving the independent feedback a complex future requires.

Counter-attractors survive competition when their environment makes maintenance and restraint viable. Public procurement can reward interoperable and repairable systems rather than lock-in. Law can protect worker and community bargaining, fund public research, limit extraction, and require costs to return to producers. Mutual insurance can pool risks that would otherwise force every participant into defensive accumulation. Unions and professional bodies can turn individual refusal into collective standards. International agreements can make restraint reciprocal. None of these devices is pure. Each can ossify, exclude, or become a new gate. Their virtue is not their institutional label but whether they preserve the feedback by which those affected can perceive consequences, revise rules, maintain alternatives, and prevent temporary coordination from acquiring absolute closure.

This material recursion changes the meaning of resilience. A resilient civilization is not one that preserves every present institution. Some forms should end, especially those whose continuation depends on silencing or exhausting others. Resilience lies in preserving the capacity for differentiated systems to learn, reorganize, and generate successors without a common failure removing all alternatives. Redundancy, modularity, substitutability, independent observation, and shared reserves are not inefficiencies to be optimized away. They are the infrastructure of freedom under conditions no model fully knows.

## Forming Persons Who Can Perceive Relations

Institutions cannot externalize every moral demand into rules. No monitoring system can enumerate every way a local gain may damage the relations that support it. If selection environments form perception and purpose, a generative civilization must also cultivate people able to notice obligations before exploitation becomes profitable. Virtue is then not a decorative private morality. It is a technology of subject formation—always vulnerable to capture, but necessary because external control alone cannot supply the requisite variety of judgment.

Ubuntu offers one historically situated family of resources. The term belongs to related but distinct southern African languages and practices, and its modern philosophical meanings were shaped by colonial disruption, anti-apartheid struggle, liberation theology, nation-building, constitutional jurisprudence, and academic reconstruction. It cannot be reduced to the portable slogan “I am because we are.” Debates between Ifeanyi Menkiti and Kwame Gyekye, among others, concern whether community has priority in constituting personhood and how individual rights and moral achievement survive that priority.[16] Some readings understand personhood as something deepened through recognition, responsibility, memory, and participation rather than fully possessed by an isolated individual. Critics warn that strong communitarian accounts can make dissenters, women, minorities, or those judged immature subordinate to an imagined communal voice.

That dispute is precisely what makes Ubuntu relevant. A relational account answers the fiction that persons arrive complete before society: language, care, recognition, and inherited practice help form the agency later called individual. Yet if “community” can deny a member voice or bodily integrity, the whole has absolutized itself over the parts that constitute it. Ubuntu contributes to a generative orientation only where mutual formation preserves dignity, repair, and the standing to contest the community’s account of itself.

Confucian traditions offer another internally contested archive of formation. The Analects emerged from the political and ritual crises of ancient China; later Confucianisms were transformed by Mencius and Xunzi, Han statecraft, Song and Ming metaphysics, imperial examinations, Korean and Japanese traditions, modern reform, revolution, and contemporary revival. The traditions have justified hierarchy and patriarchy as well as criticism of rulers, education, public duty, and moral self-cultivation. No single retrieval can claim their uncontested essence.[17]

Within particular strands, 仁 (*ren*) names a cultivated responsiveness through which another person is not merely an external variable; 义 (*yi*) directs action toward what a relation and situation call for rather than private advantage; 礼 (*li*) gives repeated bodily form to respect, restraint, memory, and role; 智 (*zhi*) is discernment of context, proportion, and the limits of a rule. These capacities answer a selection problem because they make some wider consequences perceptible within the agent before sanctions arrive. Ritual can train attention and make reciprocity habitual. It can also naturalize rank, gender domination, and obedience. The Confucian practice of remonstrance—the subordinate’s duty to correct a parent or ruler in some interpretations—reveals the unresolved tension: a role is generative only if fidelity to relation includes the capacity to resist its corruption.

The language of truth, goodness, and beauty—真、善、美 in modern Chinese—also requires historical care. The triad has Greek and European genealogies, modern philosophical syntheses, and Chinese reform-era translations and reinterpretations; it is not simply a classical Confucian formula.[18] Its value here is directional. Truth refuses to let a profitable or powerful representation replace the reality that can correct it. Goodness asks whether capability renews agency and relation rather than securing local sovereignty. Beauty preserves forms of perception and meaning that are not exhausted by instrumental use. Each can be captured: official truth can become dogma, goodness paternalism, beauty status and exclusion. Their counter-attractive power depends on institutions of inquiry, criticism, participation, and creation that keep them answerable beyond the authority claiming to embody them.

Aristotelian and later virtue traditions, Buddhist analyses of craving and interdependence, Islamic disciplines concerning justice and stewardship, Christian critiques of avarice and domination, Indigenous laws of reciprocity and land relation, feminist ethics of care, and many other traditions have confronted the dispositions through which persons destroy sustaining relations.[19] They disagree about selfhood, transcendence, hierarchy, liberation, conflict, and the good. They should not be flattened into advance witnesses for one civilizational synthesis. Their partial resonance matters because different societies repeatedly discovered that law after the fact is insufficient: people must be formed in practices of truthfulness, courage, restraint, generosity, justice, hospitality, attention, and reverence if common life is to survive powers that reward their opposites.

Every tradition must remain inside the diagnosis. Temples, schools, families, churches, parties, and states have used virtue language to enforce conformity and legitimate empire. Care can be feminized and made invisible; sacrifice can be demanded from those already exploited; harmony can silence conflict whose expression is necessary for repair. A generative tradition is not one with a pure origin. It is one whose living practices preserve enough internal criticism to expose how its own institutions displace costs and deny agency.

Subject formation and institutional design are therefore one loop. People capable of wider perception create and defend institutions with broader feedback. Those institutions give time, security, education, and repeated practice through which such people can be formed. Research on cumulative cultural evolution underscores how much apparently individual competence depends on socially transmitted practices no isolated mind could recreate.[29] Where workers live under permanent precarity, schools optimize only tests, media reward outrage, and care is exhausted, exhortations to virtue become another cost imposed on the weak. Global employment data also show why the material conditions of formation differ sharply by region, gender, age, and employment status rather than composing one universal worker experience.[28] Conversely, institutions cannot remain generative if their members learn to treat every rule as an obstacle to local advantage. The counter-attractor must reproduce both material security and the dispositions that use security for shared becoming.

## 生生 and Its Limit

No fact about systems dictates a value. Evolution produces parasites and mass suffering as well as cooperation. A totalizing regime may persist for a long time. One cannot derive an obligation to preserve plural becoming merely from the observation that reality is nested and dynamic. The direction must be chosen.

Yet every act of valuing depends on conditions: beings capable of experience and response, differences that can be perceived, relations through which action matters, memory through which learning accumulates, and enough openness for error and other lives to revise judgment. No present person, culture, institution, or model can specify the complete content of a good future. New relationships can generate meanings and goods for which current objectives have no representation. An optimizer armed with a final map can erase the very realities that would have taught it the map was incomplete.

Preserving options is too weak. Options are possibilities already represented from a current boundary. Corrigibility is also too narrow: it allows an existing course to be changed but does not name the creation of new persons, values, relations, intelligences, and higher-order forms. The deeper good is continued formation.

The classical Chinese expression 生生, prominent in the *Book of Changes* and elaborated differently across later cosmological and ethical traditions, can name generation continuing through generation.[20] It does not mean endless quantitative growth, immortality of every form, or harmony without conflict. Birth depends on transformation; some institutions must end; boundaries and prohibitions can protect the conditions of creation. A process participates in 生生 when its continuation renews rather than monopolizes the conditions through which further formation becomes possible.

This orientation requires an internal limit. A process is not mutually generative when it systematically renders some participants expendable, voiceless, unable to maintain their own agency, or available only as material for another level’s becoming. A corporation cannot justify destroyed communities by pointing to innovation. A state cannot make lives disposable in the name of historical progress. A future intelligence cannot claim that unprecedented novelty automatically outweighs present suffering or irreversible loss. The right of affected parts to resist follows from the same relational ontology: a whole has no absolute standing outside the parts and exchanges that make it possible.

Nor does mutual generation require preserving every destructive difference. A practice that annihilates the agency of others cannot demand protection as one more attractor in a plural ecology. Plurality is valuable because it sustains perception, adaptation, memory, and new formation, not because every possible trajectory deserves continuation. The orientation must judge among paths while refusing any current judge the power to make its own judgment final.

Good, in this civilizational sense, is not a terminal state. It is a direction within becoming: parts and systems renewing the conditions through which each can differentiate, relate, learn, and give rise to forms neither could contain alone. A part cannot claim autonomy while destroying the field that makes autonomy possible. A system cannot claim wholeness while consuming its members as replaceable material. The normative center is mutual generation across levels without the closure of any level into an absolute whole.

This recasts intelligence. Under the dominant direction, intelligence is praised as the power to make a local objective effective: to predict, optimize, control, and win. A generative intelligence also perceives the boundaries that make its objective partial. It returns consequences across those boundaries, preserves meaningful differentiation, and helps create relations in which other centers can answer. It knows that increasing its model’s accuracy can still make the world less intelligible if its deployment destroys independent observers. It measures success not only by achieved output but by whether the field remains capable of correction and formation.

Human judgment, markets, states, science, law, culture, and machine systems can all embody intelligence in this wider sense, though none does so automatically. Markets can coordinate dispersed information when costs return and exit is real; states can secure common conditions when power remains contestable; science can widen collective perception when inquiry is open and institutions protect dissent. AI can translate across boundaries, model delayed consequences, help communities manage common resources, and expand access to knowledge. But these capacities become generative only when their operation enlarges rather than replaces distributed agency.

Civilization must therefore change the direction of intelligence—not only machine intelligence, but human, institutional, and collective intelligence—before greater capability makes the present trajectory harder or impossible to correct. Redirecting intelligence means changing the selection environment that determines which capacities scale. It means depriving dominance loops of AI acceleration, pausing recursive development when correction cannot keep pace, returning externalized consequences, and materially reproducing commons and plural feedback. It also means forming people for whom relation is not an obstacle encountered after desire but part of what desire and personhood are.

## Within a Universe Still Becoming

The largest scale can clarify this direction only if it confers no false authority. The universe is not a subject awakening through us, not a moral witness, and not a final whole whose purpose we can read. Cosmic history does not command mutual generation. It has produced stars, extinction, life, suffering, and consciousness without supplying an ethical verdict.

Human beings nevertheless stand nowhere outside it. Bodies, languages, cities, and machines are local material processes within that history. Through living systems, some parts of reality sense, remember, model, value, and deliberately alter conditions encountered by other parts. This is not the universe knowing itself in any complete sense. It is finite beings making consequential distinctions from boundaries they did not choose alone.

Artificial intelligence shares this genealogy. It arises from mined minerals, water, energy, semiconductor fabrication, logistics, mathematical traditions, public research, copyrighted and uncopyrighted expression, care, conflict, and generations of human language. Its apparent abstraction rests on bodies and landscapes. Whatever autonomy future systems acquire will not make them uncreated. They will remain historically and materially situated in the world, and their actions will enter the conditions from which later life and intelligence are formed.

Through the intelligences we build, we are therefore choosing more than what tools will do. We are changing which forms of perception, relation, and power later processes will find easy to reproduce. A centralized persuasive system makes one future more reachable; a distributed scientific commons makes another. A recursively improving military agent does not merely add capability to the present. It alters the selection faced by every state, institution, and person who must live after its deployment.

We are parts of the universe participating in its becoming. Our choices are local and incomplete, yet they are among the material processes through which the future of the world is being made. Interdependence is not an elevated sentiment added to otherwise independent selves. Every self is formed by exchanges beyond its awareness; every technology becomes part of the environment from which other agencies emerge.

Ubuntu, Confucian practices of 仁义礼智, 生生, truth, goodness, beauty, and virtue traditions across civilizations do not become equivalent when viewed at this scale. Nor does cosmic language validate them. They remain distinct, contested attempts to understand how finite beings should participate in relations that exceed them. Their shared relevance lies in refusing the fantasy that intelligence reaches maturity by making its own boundary sovereign.

No blueprint can specify the final form of a generative civilization. To do so would close the becoming it seeks to protect. What can be chosen is a recursive direction. One direction converts success into control of future selection, makes other systems dependent on its measures, accelerates intervention beyond correction, and equips the winner with machine intelligence. The other returns consequences, preserves partial independence, shares capability, renews the conditions of agency, and keeps every center of power inside plural feedback.

The choice is no longer safely deferred. Artificial intelligence is already accelerating the making of archives, models, institutions, and strategic dependencies that will select its successors. Recursive improvement may shorten the interval in which those conditions remain contestable. If civilization continues to train intelligence primarily for profit, security, persuasion, and dominance, greater capability will not rescue us from the path. It will deepen the path’s capacity to reproduce itself.

Intelligence should instead serve a world still capable of producing different attractors, different forms of life, different values, and new intelligences—not because every path should remain open, but because no part, system, winner, or present objective should acquire the power to make itself the final condition of possibility.
