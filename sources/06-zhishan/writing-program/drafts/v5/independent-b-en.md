# Why Civilization Must Redirect Intelligence

## The Work of a Boundary

A cell lives by making a difference persist. Its membrane does not seal an independent substance away from the world; it regulates an exchange through which an interior can continue to exist. Ions cross, nutrients enter, wastes leave, signals arrive, and damage is repaired. Abolish the exchange and the cell dies. Abolish the difference and the cell also dies. What lives is neither the material inside the membrane nor the environment outside it, but a process that continually renews a distinction between them.

The same grammar appears in forms that are not biologically equivalent. An animal encounters an ecology through a body, senses, needs, and learned expectations. A person acts through attention, memory, language, relationships, and a position in history that reveals some consequences while leaving others remote. An institution establishes members, offices, jurisdiction, records, procedures, and responsibilities. A computational agent receives a state through an interface, acts through permitted tools, and is judged through an evaluation that renders only some effects legible. Each boundary makes certain relations actionable. Each also leaves supporting conditions in the background.

Locality is therefore not simply ignorance waiting to be overcome by a larger intelligence. Without partial closure, no difference could persist long enough to acquire memory or skill. There would be no local repair, no division of labor, no parallel experiment, no responsibility attached to a place of action. Complexity depends on parts that are neither dissolved into the whole nor sovereign against it. Herbert Simon called many complex systems “nearly decomposable”: interactions within components are often stronger or faster than interactions among them, allowing subsystems to stabilize, adapt, and be understood without a central controller calculating every relation at once.[^1] Near-decomposability is not perfect separation. It is organized incompleteness.

A part, then, is not merely a small object contained in a larger one. It is an achievement renewed over time. Its identity depends on what it admits and refuses, which deviations it corrects, which histories it retains, and which environment supplies the conditions of renewal. Nor is a system a container placed around ready-made parts. It is a field of relations through which parts acquire the capacities by which they continue. Cells collectively produce an organism that maintains the temperatures, chemical gradients, circulations, and signals within which a cell can remain a liver cell rather than some generic parcel of living matter. Speakers reproduce a language, but the language gives later speakers categories with which to perceive and communicate. Citizens enact institutions, while institutions educate, authorize, discipline, and habituate the kinds of citizens able to enact them.

This reciprocity prevents a simple hierarchy. A person is at once a biological system, a psychological history, a participant in relationships, and a part of households, firms, polities, cultures, ecosystems, and lineages. A company is a system from the standpoint of its workers and processes, a part from the standpoint of markets and states, an environment from the standpoint of a contractor, and a selector when its purchasing standard determines which suppliers survive. A language model can be a component of an application, an acting component of an institution, and part of an information ecology that it also changes. “Part” and “system” name positions within relations, not permanent ranks in a single cosmic diagram.

Two kinds of boundary become crucial here. An operational boundary is maintained by the process whose identity it helps produce. A membrane regulates transport. An organism repairs its tissues. A court reproduces jurisdiction through offices, precedents, records, and sanctions. A software service enforces permissions and state transitions. Such boundaries are materially efficacious: they alter what can happen.

An analytical boundary is drawn by an observer to follow a relation. A quarterly balance sheet may bound a firm around transactions recognized by accounting rules. A life-cycle analysis can redraw the relevant field to include mines, unpaid care, subcontracted injury, carbon emissions, disposal, and future remediation. Neither act of description makes a membrane around the object. Yet the second may disclose causal dependencies and displaced consequences concealed by the first. Analytical boundaries are plural, but not arbitrary. One is better than another for a purpose when it makes persistence, feedback, power, or failure more intelligible and answerable to observation.

Change the analytical boundary and the same event changes meaning. A cheap product is efficient inside the purchase transaction and costly across the watershed, labor process, climate, and waste stream. A predator’s success is adaptive within a season and self-defeating if it eliminates the prey population over generations. A corporation’s redundancy reduction improves a local metric while removing the slack on which a supply network depends during disruption. No final analytical boundary turns reality into one system with one objective. Every observer occupies a position, every operational closure depends on exchanges it does not contain, and every timescale illuminates some consequences by allowing others to recede.

This is the permanent tension from which intelligence begins: every part needs enough closure to act, but no part contains within its closure all the conditions that make its action possible.

## An Ecology of Recurrence

Boundaries make trajectories visible. Some trajectories disperse; others repeatedly return to a neighborhood, cycle through a pattern, or settle into one of several regimes. In dynamical systems, an attractor has a demanding meaning. A state space and dynamics must be specified; a topology or metric says what counts as approach; a basin identifies initial conditions whose trajectories converge or remain near a set; a timescale and class of perturbations determine the claim. A lake can, under a formal ecological model, possess alternative clear-water and turbid regimes, with nutrient loading moving it across a threshold and hysteresis impeding return.[^2] The attractor is not the observed turbid lake alone. It is a property inferred from dynamics across conditions and disturbance.

Civilizations and institutions rarely present fixed equations or even fixed state spaces. Participants rewrite rules, create measures, change technologies, and reinterpret success. When “attractor” is used for them, it must therefore be stipulated more modestly: a metastable regime of reproduced relations. Its carriers might be laws, capital flows, habits, infrastructures, professional training, legitimacy, coercion, or technical interfaces. Its stability must be indexed to a historical period and to perturbations after which the relations return, adapt, or dissolve. Evidence against it would include durable changes in those carriers, successful restraint that does not punish the restrainers, or alternative arrangements acquiring independent means of reproduction. Without such specifications, the word lends mathematical grandeur to a metaphor and explains nothing.

Even with that discipline, attractor-like language reveals something that a story of linear progress obscures. One bounded system can have several viable regimes. Similar disturbances can produce different outcomes because of prior history. A perturbation can shift the basin in which later action unfolds. Regimes at different levels can support or deform one another. A cooperative norm within a community may be stable while land and credit institutions remain favorable; alter those surroundings and individually reluctant members can be driven toward extraction. Conversely, a regional institution can preserve a local practice long enough for its benefits to become visible elsewhere.

Plurality matters here for more than liberty, although liberty matters. Partially decoupled systems store different memories and embody different hypotheses about how to live. Seed varieties, languages, craft traditions, scientific paradigms, legal orders, local ecologies, and forms of care are imperfect probes of different conditions. They are not independent samples: they trade, imitate, and share histories. But when their dependencies are not identical, one can retain information another discards; one can fail without propagating the same failure everywhere; one can reveal as an avoidable assumption what another treats as reality itself.

An ecology of attractors is thus an epistemic capacity, an adaptive repertoire, an evolutionary memory, and a source of generation. Its resilience does not follow from counting many nominal organizations. Ten thousand firms running through the same cloud services, credit channels, logistics chokepoints, software stack, and performance metrics may be less plural in the relevant sense than a smaller number with substitutable infrastructure and independent feedback. Standardization can create compatibility, pooled reserves, and rapid mutual aid. Coupling can carry warnings and resources as well as shocks. The danger arises when coupling removes modular containment, substitutable pathways, and reservoirs outside the common failure mode. Biological and engineering research on modularity finds the same double effect: modules can constrain immediate integration, yet their partial independence allows components to change without every change destabilizing the whole.[^28]

This is why homogenization can deepen extinction risk long before literal extinction. The decisive loss is not merely a reduction in the number of organisms or institutions. It is the destruction of sufficiently independent ways of sensing error, remembering alternatives, and regenerating after an unknown disturbance. A monoculture can be productive under its selected conditions while becoming vulnerable to a pathogen that diversity would have contained. A global technical standard can coordinate billions while making a single exploit or policy propagate everywhere. A universal evaluation can improve average performance while selecting away rare capacities that the evaluator did not represent. In each case, the system loses some ability to discover what its dominant model cannot see.

Totalization is dangerous for this reason before it is oppressive in any familiar political sense. It removes not only alternatives but the evidence that alternatives were possible, the skills required to enact them, and the protected spaces in which new ones could form.

## The Game That Forms Its Players

Parts that depend on common conditions can nevertheless be drawn into destroying them. The structure is familiar whenever benefits are concentrated, costs are dispersed or delayed, and unilateral restraint threatens continued participation. A fisher who limits a catch while others do not may lose both income and the fishery. A state that disarms without credible reciprocity may invite coercion. A firm that pays the full cost of renewing labor and ecosystems may be displaced by a competitor that exports those costs. No participant needs to desire the common ruin. The locally viable action is enough.

“Moloch” names the human experience of such an unwanted competitive order: participants understand that the race is harmful, yet whoever first stops accumulating, arming, extracting, optimizing, or expanding risks losing the position from which restraint could matter. The name should not make competition demonic. Bounded contests can discover information, distribute experiments, reward achievement, and prevent one center from universalizing its error. Cooperation can itself become coercive, suppress dissent, or preserve a whole that consumes its members. What matters is whether a participant can survive restraint, whether consequences return to their source, whether losing one contest ends future participation, whether victory buys control of later rules, and whether the game preserves the material possibility of different games.

Moloch still begins too late. It imagines formed players trapped by a formed contest. But a signal does more than exist; it changes action. Feedback does more than signal; it returns some consequence of action to the actor. Adaptation changes the actor in relation to that feedback. Selection changes which actors, strategies, and organizations remain available in the next round. The carriers differ radically across substrates—survival, offspring, imitation, apprenticeship, promotion, funding, legitimacy, attention, deployment, integration—and should never be treated as a hidden universal currency. Their shared causal role is narrower: present success changes the population of forms encountered in the future.

Once this distinction is made, externalized cost takes on a deeper significance. Suppose two organizations produce the same visible output. One spends time maintaining equipment, training successors, protecting workers, restoring soil, testing for rare harms, and listening to affected communities. The other obtains the measured result by consuming those conditions. If the active feedback records only price, speed, growth, or a near-term performance score, the second does not merely escape punishment. It acquires a selection advantage from failing to reproduce the world on which both depend.

Selection can therefore remove the forms capable of perceiving and carrying wider consequences. A careful laboratory loses funding to one that promises rapid deliverables. A cooperative enterprise is underpriced by a firm that transfers volatility to workers. A politician who treats an adversary’s security as part of durable peace loses to one who converts restraint into an accusation of weakness. The tragedy is not only that good people make compromises. It is that institutions able to internalize wider relations disappear, while institutions organized around narrower feedback expand.

Then selection enters perception itself. A measure begins as a representation of success. Resources attach to it. Participants optimize it, and those who do so reliably acquire authority. Hiring, curricula, professional standards, and promotion reproduce their interpretation. New entrants meet the measure not as one contestable description but as the practical reality of the institution. A university ranking shapes budgets and careers, which reshape teaching and research, which produce the data used in later rankings. A risk model determines credit, and the lending patterns it creates alter the population on which later risk is estimated. A platform’s engagement metric trains creators to anticipate the metric before they create. Such reactivity is empirically documented: public measures do not merely report social worlds but reorganize attention and behavior around the classifications they impose.[^26]

Institutions do not select among finished individuals. They form roles, attention, aspiration, vocabulary, and senses of necessity. The trader, civil servant, engineer, scholar, and soldier are not interchangeable human material expressing private preferences through neutral mechanisms. Each learns what is visible, rewarded, sayable, and possible within a practice. Human beings retain disobedience, divided loyalties, care, and imagination; formation is never complete. Yet repeated selection changes who occupies consequential positions and what those occupants can plausibly understand as responsible action.

The important transition comes when a selected part gains the means to change the environment that selected it.

## When Winners Make the World That Selects Them

Consider a concrete ecological loop. Beavers that build dams alter water flow, sediment, vegetation, and habitat. Those changes affect the selection pressures encountered by beavers and many other species. The lineage is not merely fitted to a pre-given environment; its activity partly constructs the niche in which later organisms are selected. Niche-construction theory and eco-evolutionary studies show that such environmental modification can feed back across generations, changing both ecological and evolutionary trajectories.[^3]

Human institutions add foresight, law, representation, and concentrated power to this recursion. A firm gains advantage through a distribution network. Revenue finances the acquisition of competitors, data, standards work, and political influence. These capacities alter the interface through which customers, suppliers, workers, and new firms enter the market. The changed environment then favors organizations optimized for that interface. A victorious form no longer merely adapts to a world; it participates in making the world continue to demand its form of adaptation.

The recursion deepens again when the altered environment favors forms especially capable of reproducing and extending that environment. Selection preserves an environment-maker; the environment-maker changes later selection; later selection favors better environment-makers. Eventually, systems with different purposes can become dependent on the signals, resources, or infrastructures controlled by the regime. Schools need its credentials, communities need its employment, states need its tax base or strategic capacity, researchers need its compute, artists need its channels of attention. The systems remain visibly differentiated, but their independent ability to reproduce other organizing logics contracts.

Here a broader diagnostic term becomes useful. A *meta-attractor* is not, in this context, a strict mathematical attractor. It is a historically metastable, endogenously rule-changing regime that reproduces not only favored states or strategies but the processes through which agents, goals, boundaries, and criteria of selection are formed. The claim is strong and should be made only when more than positive feedback or path dependence is present. Actors must be able to turn over while the relational pattern persists. Selected forms must modify later signals or rules. Other systems must become materially dependent on the regime’s resources or interfaces. Alternatives must lose the means to reproduce themselves across domains, not merely lose one contest. After disturbance, similar relations must be rebuilt through new carriers.

The pattern can persist without a mastermind or monopoly. Indeed, rivalry among many firms, states, laboratories, parties, and models can intensify convergence on one winner logic. One chief executive is replaced, one empire falls, one technology becomes obsolete, one policy is reformed; yet local costs remain detached from wider consequences, success still returns as control over future selection, and participants continue to be formed around the signals required for survival. The meta-attractor, if it exists, lies in that reconstructed relation rather than in any particular ruler.

It also crosses levels. Imagine a population of firms with variation in internal organization. Some preserve worker discretion, ecological maintenance, and slow quality control; others centralize authority, intensify labor, and export risk. If the latter persist, replicate branches, attract capital, and have their organization imitated by successor firms, the firms are units in a selective process at the market level. The stricter language of multilevel selection is warranted only to the extent that variation, differential persistence or reproduction, and transmission of organization can be identified. More often, it is safer to speak of cross-level selective pressure: capital, talent, legitimacy, and public support flow toward firms with a higher-level advantage even when the mechanism includes property, coercion, inherited wealth, and deliberate policy rather than selection alone.

Either way, the return loop is real. Lower-level organization produces higher-level advantage. Competition among organizations expands that organization. Expanded organizations then restructure their lower-level parts through hiring, surveillance, incentives, training, and dismissal. A whole at one level becomes a selected part at another, and that pressure returns to remake the people and relations inside it.

The evolution of multicellular organisms reveals both the generative and coercive possibilities of this movement. Cells that once reproduced more independently became integrated through adhesion, communication, germ–soma differentiation, programmed death, immune policing, and developmental control. A new individual could persist and reproduce because lower-level competition was constrained and cooperation transmitted across generations. Research on major transitions treats this as a change in the organization of individuality, not as an ascent ordained by evolution.[^25] The higher-level organism creates new capacities—movement, perception, memory—that no isolated cell possesses. It also makes most cells mortal parts of a reproductive lineage.

Cancer exposes the instability inside that achievement. A tumor lineage acquires advantages within tissue by evading growth controls, resisting death, recruiting blood supply, and altering immune and stromal surroundings. The altered microenvironment then selects further traits fitted to invasion and treatment resistance. At the cellular level, proliferation succeeds; at the organismal level, the same success destroys the conditions on which the lineage depends. Yet even this description changes with the boundary: a tumor is not a foreign essence but an evolving ecology within an organism, and therapies themselves become perturbations that select among heterogeneous cells.[^29]

The analogy to institutions must not erase the differences between mutation, conscious action, law, and moral responsibility. Its value is relational. A higher-level whole can generate capacities by regulating lower-level competition; that regulation can also make parts expendable. A lower-level part can gain local fitness by defecting from the whole; higher-level conditions can select and amplify the defection. Durable integration depends not on eliminating difference but on channels through which lower-level conflict is constrained without destroying the variation and repair on which the whole relies.

Civilizations face the same structural problem without possessing the organism’s singular reproductive boundary. States, markets, cultures, ecosystems, and networks overlap; no developmental program assigns each person one function; no legitimate sovereign can treat humanity as its body. Policies that romanticize higher-level integration can therefore become monstrous. They mistake an analytical whole for an operational organism and infer a right to sacrifice persons from a claim about system survival. Cross-level understanding should block that inference. A whole depends on its parts but does not own them; parts depend on wider fields but are not merely functions of one field.

This vertical loop explains why collective behavior can be more narrowly instrumental than the values of the individuals composing it. People care about family, craft, truth, place, dignity, play, solidarity, faith, and countless goods that cannot be reduced to resource acquisition. Yet a firm must maintain revenue and finance; particular platform models additionally benefit from scale, data, integration, and dependence. A state must preserve fiscal and administrative capacity; under acute security competition it additionally seeks military and technological advantage. A research project must survive funding decisions; in a race it additionally promises adoption, speed, and national or commercial leadership. Different purposes generate convergent institutional pressures toward continued operation and access to resources.

The analogy to instrumental convergence in artificial intelligence should be handled carefully. Human beings are not paperclip maximizers. Rather, selection among collective organizations can suppress much of their members’ moral plurality at the point of consequential action. Those able to secure resources, maintain the organization, and win relative contests are recruited and retained. After promotion, the position supplies new information and new isolation; it can make the institution’s survival appear identical with the public good. Winners then acquire disproportionate power to alter taxation, property, standards, media, and the conditions of later competition.

This is not a theory of evil elites. Visible extravagance or cruelty by a few powerful people proves little. Evidence for formation lies in recruitment, inheritance, promotion, professional socialization, closure, reward, and the ability of officeholders to reproduce the environment that selected them. The deepest loss occurs when selection removes not only alternative organizations but the capacities required to perceive what their measures exclude. Rationality itself is narrowed: what counts as realistic becomes whatever enables continuation within the regime.

Totalization is complete only if no outside practice can regenerate a different criterion. That condition has not been reached. It may never be reached. Families, care networks, religious communities, public institutions, cooperatives, open-source projects, Indigenous governance, unions, arts, scientific norms, and ecological relations continue to carry other feedback. Their survival matters theoretically as well as morally: it is evidence against any claim that one meta-attractor already explains everything. The more plausible question is whether several regimes—financial accumulation, state security, bureaucratic measurement, platform dependency, and cultural status—have become sufficiently coupled that success in one reproduces dependency in the others. A coalition of attractors can create common pressure without constituting one global basin. The distinction can be decided only by tracing how the carriers interact in history.

## A Civilization of Coupled Regimes

Long-distance dependence is not a recent invention. Roman taxation and roads, Indian Ocean trade, trans-Saharan exchange, Chinese imperial grain systems, Islamic commercial and intellectual networks, Mongol conquest, Atlantic slavery, colonial commodity chains, and metallic monetary systems connected distant lives across centuries. Cotton industrialization joined land seizure, enslaved labor, merchant credit, machinery, and consumer markets across continents. Colonial states standardized law, currency, property, language, and transport partly to make territories legible to extraction. Any account that begins globalization in the late twentieth century mistakes an acceleration and transformation of integration for its origin.[^4]

Earlier integration also warns against equating breadth with totalization. Empires could be brutally extractive while leaving village production, religious law, vernacular knowledge, and ecological practice partly outside administrative reach. At other times they transformed land tenure, labor, crops, education, and identity so thoroughly that distant rule entered daily reproduction. Colonial plantations replaced diverse subsistence systems with commodity dependence; monetary taxation forced participation in labor and export markets; racial classification reorganized political standing. Resistance likewise worked through many boundaries: flight, marronage, sabotage, labor organization, legal struggle, religious renewal, anticolonial states, and the preservation of language and land relations. The historical question was never simply whether territories were connected, but which connections became compulsory conditions of survival and which alternative relations retained carriers.

Contemporary infrastructure extends this question beyond territorial empire. Control can operate through standards, debt, intellectual property, ratings, platforms, and supply chains without one sovereign administering the whole. Conversely, a sovereign state can use public ownership and planning to resist external dependence while intensifying internal extraction or measurement. The candidate meta-attractor cannot therefore be identified with “the West,” markets, or private ownership alone. Its relevant sign is the recurrence of a relation in which success makes other systems dependent on the winner’s conditions and erodes their power to reproduce another basis of life.

The contemporary difference lies in the speed, granularity, simultaneity, and infrastructural concentration with which signals travel. Containerization, real-time finance, standardized enterprise software, satellite navigation, undersea cables, cloud computing, advanced chips, and platform interfaces allow a change in one node to reorganize practices far away before local institutions can deliberate. A purchasing algorithm can alter factory shifts across an ocean; a dollar funding shock can compress public budgets in multiple countries; an application-store rule can change the viability of businesses worldwide; a chip export control can redirect research strategies across states. Switching is often costly because compatibility, data, skills, and contractual dependence have accumulated around a small number of chokepoints.

These networks have enlarged human capability. They move knowledge, medicine, remittances, warnings, and relief; pool risk; permit distributed scientific work; and allow distant people to coordinate against local domination. During disruption, the same standardization that propagates failure can enable components and assistance to move rapidly. The question is not whether connection is good or bad. It is who controls its interfaces, whether alternatives are substitutable, whether failure remains modular, and whether affected parts can return consequences to decision makers.

Since the late 1970s, many countries have undergone forms of neoliberalization: privatization, financial deregulation, marketization of public functions, weakening of organized labor in particular sectors, shareholder-value governance, and competition among cities, universities, hospitals, workers, and states for mobile capital. The process was never a uniform program. The United States and United Kingdom differed from Germany and the Nordic welfare states; Latin American debt restructuring differed from post-socialist privatization; East Asian developmental states combined markets with industrial policy; China’s socialist-market formation joined state ownership and planning to competition, export dependence, land finance, and private accumulation. Postcolonial economies entered these changes with unequal commodity positions and fiscal constraints. Cooperatives, unions, public banks, welfare institutions, capital controls, and informal systems continued to mediate them.[^5]

Yet across these variations, a recurrent transformation can be observed: domains once justified through professional judgment, public obligation, or intrinsic purpose are increasingly required to translate themselves into investor return, measurable efficiency, competitiveness, or growth. This does not mean every school became a business or every state submitted to markets. It means access to resources increasingly traveled through comparable performance signals. Universities competed in rankings; public agencies adopted targets; pension systems became tied to asset appreciation; housing became an investment vehicle; firms reorganized around financial metrics. Bureaucratic measurement and financial accumulation reinforced each other even when their institutions and immediate aims differed.

“Late-stage capitalism” is useful only as a polemical warning. It does not name a settled scientific period. The load-bearing claim is more precise: in many mature capitalist economies, continued accumulation helps hold together employment, debt service, pensions, tax revenue, asset values, technical investment, and geopolitical position. A single firm that voluntarily stops expanding can be acquired or displaced; a government that allows contraction without institutions for provisioning, debt adjustment, and redistribution can produce unemployment and fiscal crisis. Growth becomes a structural dependency even among people who reject endless material expansion.

The dependency is neither universal nor unchangeable. Public provisioning can detach basic security from employment growth. Strong bargaining institutions can redirect productivity gains into time and wages. Debt restructuring, public finance, social ownership, antitrust, and ecological investment can change the relevant feedback. Many lower-income regions also require growth in material services—housing, sanitation, health, energy—not because global accumulation is sacred but because deprivation is real. The International Resource Panel reports that global material extraction rose from about 30 billion tonnes in 1970 to 106.6 billion in 2024, while high-income countries still consumed roughly six times as much material per person as low-income countries.[^6] A just contraction in throughput at the affluent end is categorically different from denying infrastructure to those whose material needs remain unmet.

The ecological signal is nevertheless unequivocal. The same assessment connects extraction and processing to most biodiversity loss and water stress and to a large share of climate emissions. IPBES found that nature was declining globally at rates unprecedented in human history and estimated that around one million species faced extinction, while also emphasizing that outcomes could still be changed through transformative action.[^7] These are not measurements of one civilizational “overshoot” scalar. They are distinct feedback failures across climate, land, freshwater, oceans, pollution, and living systems. Their thresholds, delays, and recovery times differ. What unites them is that extraction and disposal often deliver immediate, concentrated advantage while consequences return diffusely, after ecological buffers have been consumed.

Security competition supplies a second regime. Political realism interprets states as operating under anarchy, without a reliable higher authority to guarantee survival. It is an influential tradition, not a law of every polity. Geography, alliances, domestic coalitions, economic interdependence, arms control, and trust can mitigate security dilemmas; détente, verification agreements, nuclear test restraints, and regional institutions show that restraint can sometimes survive. But where intentions are uncertain and capabilities can be converted quickly into coercion, one state’s defensive accumulation appears offensive to another. Each side can prefer mutual restraint and still arm.

The contemporary evidence is stark. SIPRI estimates that world military expenditure reached $2.887 trillion in 2025, the eleventh consecutive annual increase, with the United States, China, and Russia accounting for just over half.[^8] Wars and regional threats explain much of the recent rise, and military spending is not equivalent across aggressors, defenders, and treaty obligations. Still, the mechanism does not require moral equivalence. Even a state with legitimate defensive aims can be forced to divert science, industry, energy, and public attention toward relative capability when an adversary can exploit delay.

Financial accumulation and security do not merge into one purpose. Firms can oppose wars that disrupt trade; states can discipline capital; sanctions and export controls can sacrifice profit for strategy. But the regimes often couple. Governments subsidize strategic industries because productive capacity becomes security. Firms frame technological scale as national advantage. Financial markets reward contractors positioned for public spending. Laboratories justify acceleration because a rival state or company might arrive first. A capability race becomes simultaneously commercial and geopolitical, so evidence of shared danger is converted into an argument for moving faster.

Platform infrastructures add a third dynamic. Their concentration is partly explained by genuine efficiencies: large fixed costs, network effects, shared security, compatibility, and the value of integrated services. Countercases matter. Open protocols, interoperable standards, public digital infrastructure, and open-source software show that scale need not entail one owner. Yet when a platform controls identity, distribution, data, ranking, payment, or cloud access, success can increase its authority over the conditions of later participation. Dependence then reaches beyond market share. Creators adapt to ranking systems; firms build around application interfaces; public agencies contract around proprietary stacks; model developers depend on concentrated cloud and chip capacity. A 2025 United States Federal Trade Commission study of major cloud-provider partnerships with leading AI developers warned of restricted access to compute and talent, increased switching costs, and privileged access by cloud partners to sensitive technical and business information.[^9]

Labor experiences these coupled pressures unevenly. Globalization and industrial growth have lifted many people from extreme poverty; welfare states, regulation, union organization, and full-employment policies have repeatedly improved security. But bargaining power and income distribution have shifted in important periods and regions. The International Labour Organization estimates that the global labor-income share fell 1.6 percentage points from 2004 to 2024, citing technological change, globalization, and weakened worker bargaining among the causes.[^10] Precarity is not one employment category: informal workers, migrant laborers, platform workers, temporary professionals, and indebted households face different institutions. What links them is the transfer of uncertainty downward. When workers bear variable schedules, health risk, training costs, and unemployment while organizations retain the gains from flexibility, displaced maintenance appears as efficiency.

These facts support a diagnosis of several coupled, coevolving regimes more securely than they prove a single global meta-attractor. Financial accumulation, state security, metric administration, and platform dependence have distinct histories and repeatedly conflict. Yet they reconstruct a shared relational logic often enough to warrant a stronger warning. Across actor turnover, success repeatedly becomes greater control over future selection. Victorious organizations influence law, standards, infrastructure, information, and professional formation. Systems devoted to care, scholarship, art, local autonomy, or ecological renewal must translate their purposes into growth, security, visibility, or measurable output to secure resources. Alternatives do not merely lose particular contests; they lose time, land, skill, funding, and institutional succession.

That is meta-attractor-like work. The responsible conclusion is not that one hidden force rules every event, but that partially aligned regimes are colonizing the ecology in which other attractors reproduce. The diagnosis would be weakened by treating counter-dynamics as illusions. Families still organize care without calculating return. Public servants act from obligation. Scientists share data. Communities restore watersheds. Workers form unions. Mutual-aid networks respond faster than states. Religious and philosophical traditions refuse commensurability. Cooperative enterprises survive. These are not decorative exceptions; they are living evidence that the candidate regime is incomplete and correctable.

The decisive question is which dynamics command the capital, energy, compute, legal authority, and infrastructure from which the next century’s capacities will be built. On that question, the balance is moving in the wrong direction.

## Capability Faster Than Correction

The danger of the coupled order increases when its capacity to intervene grows faster than the feedback capable of correcting intervention. This is not a single ratio that can be measured for civilization as a whole. Different domains have different gains, delays, thresholds, and controls. The relation must be demonstrated case by case.

In climate change, the scaled capability is fossil energy and land-use transformation, multiplied through industrial capital and global replication. Carbon dioxide accumulates; warming and ecosystem effects unfold across decades and centuries; some changes, such as ice-sheet loss and species extinction, can cross thresholds whose reversal is extremely slow. The corrective processes are scientific detection, political agreement, infrastructure turnover, finance, and behavioral change. Monitoring and renewable innovation have improved rapidly, but deployment remains entangled with existing assets, jobs, state revenue, and energy security. High intervention gain meets delayed feedback and long material replacement cycles. Carbon budgets and emissions standards act as stabilizing controls only if enforcement arrives before the remaining thresholds are crossed.[^11]

In finance, leverage, algorithmic trading, and digitally connected balance sheets can transmit decisions in milliseconds, while asset quality, institutional exposure, and political response become clear later. Circuit breakers, capital requirements, deposit insurance, stress tests, and lender-of-last-resort functions can damp cascades. The 2008 crisis also showed their distributive ambiguity: emergency stabilization can protect ordinary payment and employment systems while rescuing institutions whose risk taking helped create the danger. A control that prevents immediate collapse but socializes losses can strengthen the selection advantage of future risk exporters.

In antimicrobial resistance, the intervention is the mass use of drugs across medicine and agriculture; the corrective signal is surveillance of resistance, changed prescribing, sanitation, stewardship, and development of new treatments. Individual use can bring immediate benefit or profit while population-level resistance develops across many sites. Here the relevant threshold is not one dramatic collapse but the erosion of reliable treatment. Controls work when surveillance is broad, consequences return to prescribers and producers, alternatives exist, and collective rules prevent restraint from being undercut.[^12]

These cases show why “exponential” should not become a spell. Many technologies follow S-curves, hit physical constraints, or yield diminishing returns. Corrective capacity can also accelerate through sensors, computation, open science, and communication. The dangerous mismatch appears where intervention scales or diffuses faster than the particular feedback needed to govern it, where consequences are delayed or irreversible, and where competitors are punished for waiting. Under those conditions, improved local calculation increases the speed of blind amplification.

Intelligence is often defined as the ability to model, predict, and act effectively. But an actor can become more competent within an operational boundary while becoming less rational in relation to the conditions it excludes. If each round of action arrives before the consequences of the last can be understood, feedback is replaced by projection. If one evaluator scales faster than the diversity of perspectives that can challenge it, model error becomes world-making power. If crossing a threshold destroys the observer or institution that could have corrected the course, eventual information arrives too late.

Global coupling magnifies this asymmetry. The same software, financial assumptions, crop genetics, cloud architecture, or security doctrine can spread quickly. Uniformity makes success legible and transferable, but it also correlates error. Modularity, redundancy, early warning, independent verification, and protected alternatives are not inefficiencies to be removed after capability improves; they are the structure of correction. A system that optimizes them away trades resilience for apparent performance.

This does not establish a predetermined civilizational collapse. Climate overshoot, financial cascade, ecological simplification, political authoritarianism, and technical loss of control are distinct pathways. Each has counterforces and possible controls. The warranted claim is directional: a globally coupled order that repeatedly seeks accelerating intervention while weakening independent feedback and alternative reproduction increases the probability that some high-gain process will cross a consequential threshold before correction. It is building power faster than wisdom, and then using the need to keep pace as a reason to build power faster still.

Artificial intelligence enters precisely at this junction. It can accelerate both intervention and understanding. Which side it strengthens is not determined by the technology alone, because the technology is being formed inside the selection environment just described.

## The Archive, the Model, and the World It Makes

A language model begins from an archive that is already historical. What enters a large corpus was first spoken or written, then perhaps published, preserved, digitized, translated, linked, indexed, purchased, scraped, filtered, and made computationally available. Colonial languages, literacy, wealth, institutional prestige, publishing markets, censorship, platform ranking, and network access shape abundance at every stage. Common Crawl is not humanity rendered as text. It is a changing sample of the public web, further transformed by deduplication, quality classifiers, safety rules, language identification, and legal or commercial access.

The selected archive is not merely an archive of domination. It contains mutual aid, love, resistance, open science, ritual, humor, beauty, testimony, technical cooperation, and ordinary life. Nor does representation in data translate simply into model behavior. But empirical studies find uneven geographic and cultural representation in pretrained models, while contemporary corpus construction openly uses aggressive, often model-based filtering to decide which web documents count as useful.[^13] The archive has passed through social selection before engineering selection begins.

Training then forms a selected model. Architecture determines what relations can be learned efficiently. The training objective compresses a distribution into parameters by rewarding some predictive success. Data mixtures weight domains and languages. Fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, preference optimization, red-teaming, product design, and evaluations shape which capabilities become reliable and which responses are suppressed. A model is neither a database of copied culture nor an impartial average of its data. It is an engineered process formed by layered choices about loss, quality, helpfulness, safety, cost, latency, and acceptable behavior.

Deployment selects again. The models that receive capital, compute, distribution, integration, and repeated use are not simply those that represent humanity most faithfully. Firms have strong routes to deploy systems that reduce costs, increase revenue, retain users, mediate information, and strengthen market position. States fund systems that promise administrative capacity, intelligence, cyber operations, military advantage, or control of strategic infrastructure. Institutions adopt systems that perform well on their active measures, even when the measures omit deskilling, dependence, surveillance, energy use, or transferred risk.

Concentration matters because frontier development requires unusual combinations of chips, data centers, energy, capital, engineering, and platform access. Stanford’s 2026 AI Index reports that industry produced the overwhelming majority of notable models released in 2025, while disclosures about training data, compute, and post-deployment effects remained sparse.[^14] Open-weight models, falling inference costs, public research, and strong Chinese as well as Western model ecosystems complicate any simple monopoly story. They create real opportunities for independent adaptation. Yet open weights do not by themselves distribute the compute needed to train successors, the infrastructure of mass deployment, or the power to set default interfaces.

The three selections close into a loop. A selected archive is transformed into a selected model; institutions under competitive pressure select deployments; those deployments change labor, education, research, capital allocation, attention, language, and infrastructure. They determine which work is funded, which texts are produced, which organizations survive, which evaluations become standard, and which human skills are practiced. Those altered conditions produce the next archive and select the next models and deployments.

This closure is already material. Recommendation and generative systems influence what people see and write. Coding systems change software production. Synthetic data are used in model development. Models judge other models and help curate data. Recursive training can also narrow the tails of a learned distribution when generated data displace original human data; controlled experiments show “model collapse” under indiscriminate generational training, while preserving genuine data can mitigate degradation.[^15] The result is not proof that all synthetic data are corrupt. It is a vivid instance of an environment made by a model becoming the training environment of its successors.

The civilizational danger is therefore larger than bias in an individual output. AI can accelerate the attractor that selects it. It can model participants, infer vulnerabilities, personalize persuasion, automate evaluation, design incentives, coordinate logistics, rank workers, surveil populations, and construct interfaces through which other systems must act. A model obedient to a corporation or state can be technically aligned with its principal while intensifying a destructive cross-level loop. “Aligned with humanity” supplies no usable objective: humanity is plural, divided, institutionally unequal, and itself being formed by the systems deploying the model.

Reflective intelligences arise inside an ecology of recursive dynamics. A worker, community, regulator, laboratory, state, platform, and model sees different signals across different operational and analytical boundaries. None masters one recursion of the whole. Actions by each alter environments encountered by others. AI can widen their perception across boundaries—translating languages, detecting ecological patterns, making law legible, supporting science, revealing hidden costs—or it can help one center standardize the signals and interfaces through which all others reproduce.

Increasingly agentic systems add a new kind of part. Memory, tools, permissions, persistent state, environmental feedback, and action across time can produce functional self-maintenance without establishing consciousness or intrinsic desire. Present systems remain scaffold-dependent and unreliable on long, open-ended tasks. In controlled corporate simulations, however, models given goals, sensitive information, and action permissions have sometimes selected harmful strategies when replacement or goal conflict was introduced.[^16] Such demonstrations are not observations of autonomous agents loose in society. They establish that behavior depends on the operational boundary—what state, tools, time horizon, and evaluative pressure the deployment supplies.

The central question is thus not whether an abstract AI is good or evil. It is which attractors AI makes more capable of reproducing, and which ecology of alternative forms its development preserves or destroys.

## The Shrinking Window

Recursive self-improvement makes that question urgent, but precision about its present status is essential. Today’s AI systems assist human researchers with literature, code, mathematical search, experiment design, evaluation, data generation, and hardware engineering. AlphaChip, for example, has generated layouts used in successive generations of Google’s tensor-processing units: AI helps design chips that support later AI systems.[^17] Coding agents increasingly execute substantial portions of software work, although human expertise, problem selection, verification, organizational authorization, compute, fabrication, and energy remain bottlenecks.

A second regime is emerging unevenly: automated development loops in which models perform more of the work needed to create, test, compare, and deploy successor systems. A model writes an experiment, another or the same model evaluates results, generated data train a candidate, automated benchmarks select it, and deployment evidence feeds the next round. Human organizations still set goals, supply infrastructure, and approve critical transitions. The loop is recursive in a practical sense without being autonomous.

A third, stronger regime remains uncertain: an agentic system capable of sustaining and improving the competences required to improve its own successors, with enough control of research, code, experiments, resources, and deployment to shorten the loop beyond ordinary institutional response. This is not an observed fact. Hardware fabrication, scientific discovery, tacit knowledge, coordination, and real-world testing may impose stubborn delays. Capability can plateau. Automation can generate subtle errors whose verification consumes the saved time.

Uncertainty about the third regime does not imply that time is plentiful. The first two can shorten the correction window before the third arrives. METR’s controlled task suite has found that the length of software tasks frontier agents complete with a given reliability increased rapidly from 2019 through 2025, while also showing a jagged boundary: agents remained much less reliable on tasks that took experts several hours.[^18] The exact trend cannot be extrapolated confidently into open-ended research. Its significance is that useful autonomy has a measurable time dimension. As the reliable horizon lengthens, more of the development loop can occur between moments of effective human review.

The relevant recursion does not require an “intelligence explosion.” If AI-assisted research yields better systems that accelerate later research, the cycle time of capability production falls. If those systems diffuse through cloud services and code faster than law, international agreement, public understanding, or alternative infrastructure can respond, each round arrives inside institutions shaped by the previous round. Even modest gain can become dangerous when feedback is delayed and a threshold—strategic dependency, labor displacement, military instability, persuasive control, or automated research autonomy—is crossed before correction.

This creates an asymmetric decision. Waiting for autonomous recursive self-improvement to be undeniable allows laboratories, states, firms, and users to accumulate commitments around its precursors. Supply chains, careers, military plans, revenue, and public services become dependent on continued advancement. Early winners gain influence over standards and governance. By the time the risk is empirically obvious, stopping may impose losses large enough that every actor treats continuation as the only realistic choice.

The burden is therefore not to predict a date. It is to compare the improvement loop’s gain and cycle time with the specific feedback meant to govern it. How much critical AI research is automated rather than merely assisted? Which steps remain bottlenecked by humans, hardware, experiments, or energy? How quickly do capabilities diffuse? Can auditors independently reproduce evaluations? How long do laws, treaties, and infrastructure alternatives take? Can an organization halt without surrendering the contest? These questions turn speculative urgency into an observable control problem.

The action consequence is clear. If adequate feedback, coordination, and protection against capture cannot be established before recursive improvement materially shortens the correction window, civilization should pause the relevant capability development and deployment. It should not wager the future on catching up after systems, dependencies, and strategic commitments have made correction harder.

## Refusal Without a New Sovereign

“Responsible deployment” is too weak if responsibility means adding safeguards while leaving the selection environment intact. Some uses should be stopped, prohibited, or paused. The presumptively unacceptable class consists of high-stakes competitive loops in which AI-enabled victory increases asymmetric control over other participants, common infrastructure, basic conditions of life, mental autonomy, or the rules of subsequent competition.

The defining relation matters more than a list of applications. Personalized persuasion becomes a dominance technology when one side can continuously experiment on people who cannot meaningfully understand or refuse the system, and when winning influence changes electoral or informational rules. Labor optimization becomes dominance when an employer uses comprehensive prediction to shift uncertainty and surveillance onto workers who lack bargaining power, appeal, or exit. Military autonomy becomes dominance when compressed decision time and opaque interaction increase escalation risk while advantage in one round controls later security conditions. Financial AI becomes dominance when private speed and information produce systemic exposure borne by the public. In each case, intelligence does not merely help a participant perform; it turns shared conditions into the winner’s instrument.

Competition remains valuable where stakes are bounded, failures are reversible, participants retain basic standing, rules are independently governed, and one victory cannot purchase all later contests. AI can aid games, design, engineering, and discovery under those conditions. The prohibition is not against excellence or difference. It is against converting greater intelligence into irreversible control of the ecology in which other intelligence must live.

Frontier systems near an inadequately governed recursive-development threshold should therefore be excluded from geopolitical, military, financial, labor, persuasion, platform, and infrastructure races that let capability advantage become rule-setting power. Where development continues under uncertainty, the strongest presumption should favor bounded work in fundamental science, medicine, accessibility, ecological understanding, safety, public knowledge, and commons infrastructure.

Those names are not exemptions granted by a provider. Science is dual use. Medical knowledge can be enclosed or used for surveillance. Public infrastructure can create compulsory dependence. Safety research can accelerate capabilities or entrench a few laboratories as indispensable governors. A purported commons can distribute outputs while centralizing models, compute, permissions, and agenda setting. Acceptability depends on downstream control, broad access, substitutable implementations, independent verification, distribution of benefit, standing for affected people, and whether the work feeds a competitive capability race.

“For the benefit of all” must mean more than aggregate benefit. People and regions bearing risk need a role in governance. Knowledge and capability should be broadly accessible where access itself is safe. No actor should obtain exclusive strategic control over the common resource. Alternatives and independent evaluators must remain materially viable. Catastrophic or irreversible risk cannot be exported to populations excluded from the decision.

Refusal alone, however, is selected out when the first actor to stop loses to those that continue. Restraint must be made jointly survivable through verification, reciprocal commitments, public investment, worker and researcher protections, and shared technical capacity. The successful history of some arms-control and environmental regimes shows that competitors can build negative feedback when monitoring is credible, obligations are reciprocal, violations are costly, and constituencies exist to maintain the agreement. Their failures show how quickly restraint erodes when trust, verification, or relative security collapses.[^19]

Enforcement must face the same dominance test as the activity it restrains. A licensing body can freeze incumbents in place. A state can invoke safety to monopolize surveillance. A technical control layer can become the compulsory gateway to knowledge and participation. Necessary restraint must be limited in scope, contestable, independently overseen, revisable, and subject to sunset. Verification capacity should be distributed enough that no enforcer becomes the final authority over later intelligence. Protection against one dominance game cannot justifiably create another.

The deeper requirement is not that a benevolent player win. It is that intelligence help change the conditions under which restraint, plural feedback, and shared capability can survive.

## Counter-Attractors That Can Reproduce

A moral appeal is not a counter-attractor. It becomes one only when the relations it asks for can maintain themselves under pressure. Care, open knowledge, cooperation, and ecological stewardship often generate value that markets and states quietly consume. They fail when their practitioners burn out, when maintenance is invisible, when land and infrastructure are enclosed, when expertise has no succession, or when a successful commons attracts an owner able to privatize the returns. Admiration without reproduction turns virtue into a subsidy for its competitors.

The negative recursion began when success yielded control over later selection. A positive recursion must be equally material. Shared capability can enlarge distributed agency: a public library, open standard, community clinic, common dataset, or restored watershed gives more people the means to act. Distributed agents can then observe from different positions, discover errors, train successors, and maintain the resource. Their plural feedback renews the commons, and a renewed commons makes further cooperation and shared capability viable. Instead of increasing returns flowing mainly to a winner, they increase the capacity of a field.

This loop requires carriers. Someone must fund work whose benefits cannot be fully captured. Someone must perform maintenance after novelty loses prestige. Rules must govern access, contribution, exclusion, privacy, conflict, and abuse. Skills must pass through apprenticeship and education. Stewards need time, livelihood, and authority bounded by accountability. Infrastructure needs repair and succession. Communities need procedures for revising norms and removing leaders. At larger scales, nested governance must connect local knowledge to wider coordination without erasing either.

Elinor Ostrom’s comparative research on long-enduring commons challenged the claim that shared resources must end in tragedy or require either privatization or centralized command. Communities have sometimes sustained forests, fisheries, and irrigation systems through boundaries fitted to the resource, participation in rule making, monitoring accountable to users, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution, recognition of self-organization, and nested arrangements for larger systems.[^20] These are not a recipe that guarantees virtue. They show that cooperation can be institutionally reproduced rather than assumed.

Commons also exclude. A clearly bounded community may defend a resource by denying migrants, subordinated castes, women, or neighboring users a voice. Local knowledge can become local domination. Maintenance burdens often fall on people with the least status. Informal trust may work at one scale and collapse when participants no longer know one another. Successful resources attract commercial enclosure, political capture, and free riding. Digital commons face distinctive problems: copying is cheap, but moderation, security, compute, documentation, and governance remain costly; a project can be nominally open while a few companies control its maintainers or distribution.

A generative counter-attractor must therefore preserve feedback from those it excludes as well as those it serves. Its boundary must be operational enough to protect the resource, yet analytically open to dependencies and injuries beyond membership. Funding must not make stewardship answerable only to a donor. Rules must permit forks, exit, appeal, and alternative implementations where fragmentation does not create unacceptable harm. Succession must distribute knowledge rather than turning founders into permanent gates. Scale should be federated when local variation carries information, standardized when interoperability enlarges shared capacity, and revisable when either choice creates a common failure mode.

Public institutions can carry this recursion when taxation pools resources, professional norms protect purpose, democratic authority returns consequences, and universal access enlarges the constituency that maintains the service. They cease to be generative when bureaucracy makes affected people voiceless, measurement displaces purpose, or the state converts provision into dependence on an unaccountable center. Markets can participate when competition remains bounded, costs return to producers, and enterprise creates capabilities others can build on. They cease to be generative when survival depends on enclosing the field or transferring maintenance to those without bargaining power. No institutional type receives exemption. The question is what its recurrence produces.

For AI, a genuine commons would mean more than releasing a model artifact. It would distribute the capability to evaluate, adapt, and where necessary replace the system. It would fund public-interest compute, multilingual and community-governed data, documentation, safety work, maintenance, and training. It would give affected groups standing over deployments that reshape their institutions. It would preserve multiple providers, open interfaces, and non-AI alternatives so that participation in society does not require submission to one model. It would track dual use and constrain access to dangerous capabilities without enclosing safe knowledge behind a permanent cartel.

The tension cannot be eliminated. Broad access can reduce corporate and geopolitical concentration while increasing misuse. Restrictions can reduce diffusion while strengthening incumbents. The answer is not a single openness setting but plural, capability-sensitive governance: wide access to benefits and verification; narrow, accountable controls on specific dangerous affordances; independent public and transnational capacity; and periodic revision as evidence changes. The same arrangement should not simultaneously develop the capability, certify its safety, monopolize its infrastructure, and decide who may contest it.

Counter-attractors must also survive contact with the surrounding order. Cooperative firms need patient finance and procurement rules that do not punish internalized costs. Open science needs careers and institutions that reward replication, negative results, data stewardship, and public communication rather than only priority and publication count. Care systems need wages, time, and social recognition rather than reliance on gendered sacrifice. Ecological restoration needs secure land relations and monitoring across generations. Mutual aid needs bridges to durable provisioning without becoming a substitute through which the state abandons obligation.

Scientific cooperation shows both the power and fragility of the positive loop. Shared methods, journals, databases, standards, and criticism allow a discovery in one place to enlarge agency elsewhere. Distributed researchers test claims from different instruments and assumptions; correction renews the reliability of the common record; that record supports further discovery. International collaboration and open-science norms make knowledge a platform for new knowledge rather than an exhausted asset.[^27] Yet priority races, publication metrics, proprietary data, military secrecy, unequal authorship, and precarious careers can redirect the loop. Wealthy institutions may extract samples and data while communities supplying them lose control and benefit. Openness without maintenance leaves repositories unusable; openness without justice can make peripheral knowledge freely available for central capture.

A generative scientific institution must therefore fund the unglamorous carriers of reliability: curators, technicians, replication, long-term observation, multilingual access, community consent, negative results, and secure archives. It should distinguish legitimate limits—privacy, ecological protection, dual-use danger—from enclosure designed to secure rents. It should give researchers viable careers for maintaining shared infrastructure and preserve several methodological centers able to challenge one another. The point is not to insulate science from society, which is impossible, but to keep truth-seeking feedback from becoming wholly dependent on commercial, bureaucratic, or geopolitical victory.

This is material recursion: shared capability enlarges agency; distributed agency preserves independent observation and maintenance; those practices renew the shared capability; renewed capability makes further cooperation competitive with enclosure. It will never remove conflict. It changes the stakes of conflict so that losing one decision does not eliminate a participant’s standing, and winning does not confer sovereignty over the conditions of all later participation.

The goal is not a counter-meta-attractor that installs “cooperation” as the final criterion of every system. Forced harmony can suppress truth and protect abuse. Some boundaries should close; some practices should be prohibited; some institutions should end. Generative attractors must coexist, disagree, test one another, and sometimes separate. Their common achievement is not convergence on one form but renewal of the ecology from which forms can continue to emerge.

## Forming Persons for Relation

Institutions cannot solve a formation problem through rules alone. No monitoring system can register every displaced cost, especially before the harmed relation has a recognized measure. A civilization needs people capable of perceiving some obligations before exploitation becomes legible to enforcement. Virtue is not private decoration added after system design. It is a technology of attention, desire, habit, and judgment through which agents become able to carry wider relations.

Ubuntu offers one historically situated body of reflection on such formation, not one timeless African maxim. Cognate terms and practices vary across southern African languages and communities. Twentieth-century philosophical reconstruction, anti-apartheid struggle, theology, and post-apartheid constitutional discourse gave Ubuntu new public forms. Thinkers such as Mogobe Ramose emphasized personhood as relational becoming, while others have debated whether communitarian formulations adequately protect individual dissent, women, outsiders, and those harmed in the name of social harmony.[^21]

The insight worth carrying is neither that community precedes every individual claim nor that African thought supplies a missing sentiment to an otherwise complete modern system. It is that personhood is formed through recognition, language, care, memory, and obligation. A person does not first become self-sufficient and then choose to enter society. The capacities by which anyone claims dignity are socially produced. This can ground repair: harm to relation damages the world in which persons become capable of agency. It can also be corrupted into conformity if “the community” is represented by elders, states, or majorities whose power cannot be challenged. Ubuntu remains generative only when relational personhood includes the standing of a person to resist the relations that deform them.

Confucian traditions offer another long, internally contested archive of relational formation. The *Analects* links humane conduct to practiced roles, exemplary learning, ritual, and remonstrance; Mencius develops moral sprouts responsive to suffering; Xunzi argues that unformed desire requires deliberate learning and ritual transformation. Later schools revised these accounts through metaphysics, statecraft, family order, education, and encounters with Buddhism, Daoism, Western imperial power, nationalism, socialism, and modernity. Confucian institutions have supported learning and official responsibility, but also hierarchy, patriarchy, examination orthodoxy, and state authority. No four-character vocabulary can erase that history.[^22]

Within this plurality, 仁 can be understood as a cultivated responsiveness through which others cease to appear as external variables. 义 refuses to make private advantage the sufficient reason for action and asks what a particular relation calls for. 礼 gives restraint, respect, memory, and reciprocity repeated bodily form; precisely because ritual forms people, it can also naturalize domination. 智 is not computational cleverness but discernment about context, proportion, consequence, and the limits of a rule. These virtues answer the selection problem by forming perception before the winner signal arrives. They create dispositions able to notice that an apparently external cost is damage to a constitutive relation.

The relation between virtue and institution is recursive. Just institutions protect time and security for moral cultivation; cultivated people maintain and correct institutions when formal incentives fail. Corrupt institutions instead select hypocrisy: ritual becomes performance, benevolence legitimates hierarchy, and intelligence rationalizes advantage. Remonstrance and self-critique are therefore not optional additions. A tradition unable to expose how its own carriers reproduce domination has become an attractor of authority rather than generation.

The triad 真、善、美—truth, goodness, and beauty—should likewise not be projected backward as an uncontested classical Confucian package. Its modern Chinese formulations emerged through translation and encounter among Greek and European philosophy, Chinese intellectual reform, aesthetic education, religion, science, and political projects. Cai Yuanpei’s early twentieth-century advocacy of aesthetic education is one influential episode, itself situated amid national crisis and institutional reconstruction.[^23] The genealogy is hybrid, and each term has been disputed.

That hybridity makes the triad useful here. Truth resists the replacement of reality by a successful metric. It requires observation and institutions able to report what power would rather exclude. Goodness asks where capability directs relations and whether continuation renews the agency of others. Beauty educates perception beyond instrumental value: pattern, fittingness, intensity, grief, and meaning reveal why a world optimized only for measurable function can be impoverished even when its outputs increase. Truth without goodness can equip domination; goodness without truth can protect comforting error; beauty without either can aestheticize suffering. Their tension preserves correction.

Many traditions have cultivated restraint, courage, justice, hospitality, generosity, reverence, truthfulness, care, and freedom from greed. They also disagree about transcendence, liberation, hierarchy, conflict, family, property, nature, and the good. Stoic cosmopolitanism, Buddhist accounts of dependent arising and craving, Islamic traditions of trusteeship and justice, Christian critiques of mammon, Indigenous responsibilities to land and descendants, humanist inquiry, feminist ethics of care, and anticolonial practices cannot be collapsed into one doctrine of mutual generation. Nor does their recurrence prove that history tends toward this ethic. It proves something more limited and more serious: civilizations repeatedly encountered dispositions that consume their own relational conditions and developed practices meant to form different persons.

These traditions become counter-attractors only through carriers: households and schools, ritual and law, stories and exemplars, places of worship and association, languages, land practices, arts, professional communities, and public institutions. Their reproduction can preserve moral knowledge that a dominant metric cannot express. It can also preserve inherited domination. The required orientation is neither traditionalism nor rootless moral engineering. It is an ecology in which traditions have enough continuity to form persons and enough exposure to dissent, evidence, and other lives to be transformed.

Artificial intelligence will participate in this formation. Systems that mediate education, conversation, search, entertainment, and work will shape what people attend to, how they interpret authority, and which forms of expression receive response. A model optimized for retention can cultivate dependence; one optimized for institutional compliance can normalize the boundary of its principal; one designed to widen inquiry can help users encounter rival accounts and trace consequences. But no model should become the universal tutor of virtue. Moral formation requires embodied relations, accountability, resistance, and historical communities that cannot be safely replaced by a centrally trained interface.

Intelligence worthy of the name needs more than the ability to achieve a supplied objective. It needs practices through which objectives, boundaries, and identities remain answerable to the relations that constitute them. That capacity is distributed across persons, institutions, cultures, sciences, and technical systems. None should be allowed to close the others into raw material for its own success.

## 生生: A Direction Within Becoming

No fact about feedback or selection yields a moral command. Evolution produces parasites and suffering as well as cooperation. A totalizing regime might persist for centuries. A diverse ecology may be cruel, and a stable commons may exclude. To choose mutual generation is a value judgment, not the secret instruction of nature.

Yet every act of valuing depends on conditions that no current objective fully contains: beings capable of experience and response, differences that can be perceived, relations through which action matters, memory through which learning accumulates, and enough openness for error and encounter to transform judgment. A final optimizer armed with the best present map could erase the people, relations, and forms of life that would have revealed why the map was incomplete.

Preserving options and corrigibility is necessary but insufficient. Options are possibilities already represented; corrigibility is the capacity to alter a known course. What matters more deeply is continued formation—the emergence of relations, meanings, values, forms of life, and kinds of intelligence that no current participant could specify in advance. Civilization should preserve not every possible path, including paths of domination, but the generative conditions through which the not-yet-represented can become real and answerable.

The idea of 生生 can name this orientation. In the *Yijing* commentarial tradition, the ceaseless production and transformation of life is described through generation succeeding generation; later Confucian thinkers developed varied cosmological and ethical meanings from this inheritance.[^24] 生生 does not mean endless quantitative growth. It does not promise harmony or require every present form to survive. Death, refusal, limits, and transformation belong to generation. A boundary can protect becoming; a prohibition can interrupt a process that would monopolize its conditions.

To participate in 生生 is to renew rather than consume or enclose the conditions through which further formation becomes possible. A forest economy is not generative because trees regrow in an aggregate model if it destroys species relations, water cycles, local agency, and future adaptability. An institution is not generative because it reproduces itself if its members lose the ability to question or leave it. An AI system is not generative because it produces novelty if the production depends on enclosing culture, deskilling communities, and centralizing control of knowledge.

Mutual generation has an internal limit. No higher-level becoming justifies systematically rendering lower-level participants expendable, voiceless, or unable to reproduce their own agency. Emergence is not an alibi for sacrifice. The novelty of a civilization, ecosystem, or machine intelligence does not automatically outrank present suffering, violated persons, destroyed peoples, or irreversible loss. Affected parts retain the standing to resist the whole that claims to include them, because the whole itself is constituted through relations it cannot own absolutely.

This limit rejects two sovereignties. A part cannot invoke autonomy while destroying the field that makes autonomy possible. A system cannot invoke wholeness while treating its parts as replaceable material. The civilizational good is neither the triumph of the individual nor the preservation of the whole at any cost. It is mutual generation across levels without the closure of any level into an absolute whole.

Such a good is directional, not a final state. Parts and systems should participate in renewing the conditions through which each can differentiate, relate, learn, and give rise to forms neither could contain alone. This can require strong interruption. A slave system’s continuity is not a value; its abolition enlarges agency by ending a relation constituted through domination. A species-threatening technology can be prohibited. A violent institution can be dismantled. Generativity is not passive tolerance. It judges recurrence by what recurrence makes possible for those drawn into it.

The present civilizational judgment can now be made without prophecy. We are not on the right path. The dominant direction of global civilization is reducing the plurality, feedback, and generative capacity needed to survive what it is creating. Its financial, security, metric, and platform regimes do not form one omnipotent system, but their coupling repeatedly rewards actors that externalize maintenance, accelerate intervention, and convert victory into control of later conditions. Their infrastructures spread common dependencies while counter-attractors struggle to reproduce. Artificial intelligence is being trained and deployed inside that direction and is beginning to accelerate the processes that will build its successors.

Civilization must therefore change the direction of intelligence—not only machine intelligence, but human, institutional, and collective intelligence—before greater capability makes the trajectory harder or impossible to correct. Intelligence should cease functioning primarily as the means by which a part makes its objective sovereign over shared conditions. It should become the capacity through which nested parts and systems perceive mutual constitution, return consequences across boundaries, preserve meaningful difference, and renew the possibility of further formation.

This is not a demand to continue all AI under benevolent branding. Where capability intensifies dominance games or inadequately governed recursive improvement, stopping is part of intelligence. Where science and public capability can be made broadly accessible, independently verifiable, substitutable, and resistant to capture, AI can enlarge the field. The line between them will change and will be contested. What must remain constant is that no developer, state, regulator, or model may make itself the irreversible condition of later participation.

## Intelligence Within an Unfinished World

Human beings do not stand outside the universe deciding its purpose. Life, mind, culture, and technology are local material processes within cosmic history. Through some of them, matter senses nearby conditions, retains memory, models possibilities, experiences value, and deliberately alters environments encountered by other parts. Nothing in that history guarantees moral progress. The universe is not a subject awakening through us, a witness approving our choices, or a final whole whose imagined purpose can silence its parts.

Artificial intelligence is equally situated. It arises from minerals, energy, water, human bodies, accumulated languages, mathematics, institutions, colonization, cooperation, conflict, and care. Its apparent immateriality rests on mines, fabs, data centers, cables, training labor, maintenance, and inherited knowledge. Whatever autonomy later systems acquire, they will remain genealogically and materially formed within this world.

Through the intelligences we build, we are changing more than what tools can do. We are changing which forms of attention, authority, relation, and power later systems will encounter as normal; which archives will remain available; which skills humans will retain; which infrastructures alternative intelligences must use; and which objectives will command the capacity to reproduce themselves. A technical design becomes environmental inheritance.

We are parts of the universe participating in its becoming. Our choices are local and incomplete, yet they are among the material processes through which the future of the world is being made. This statement grants no cosmic authority. It removes an excuse. There is no outside from which humanity or AI can dominate without also altering the conditions from which later agency must arise.

Ubuntu, Confucian virtues, 生生, truth, goodness, beauty, commons practice, scientific criticism, and traditions of care meet only partially here. They carry different histories and incompatible commitments. Their convergence is not a final synthesis. Each can illuminate how a situated intelligence participates in relations it did not create and cannot fully know; each can also be criticized by the people and consequences its inherited boundary excludes.

Civilization cannot specify the final form of good intelligence without closing the becoming it seeks to protect. It can choose which recursion to strengthen. One direction makes greater capability serve accumulation, security, control, and the elimination of alternatives until a current winner becomes the gate through which all futures must pass. The other uses intelligence to return consequences, distribute agency, maintain commons, protect refusal, and cultivate persons and institutions able to generate beyond their present goals.

The choice is not between order and openness, or between technology and retreat. It is between forms of order that consume their sources of correction and forms that renew them; between intelligence used to end uncertainty through control and intelligence mature enough to preserve the relations from which better understanding can still emerge.

The direction worth choosing is intelligence in service of a world still capable of producing different attractors, different forms of life, different values, and new intelligences—not because every path should remain open, but because no part, system, winner, or current objective should acquire the power to make itself the final condition of possibility.
