# Why Civilization Must Redirect Intelligence

## The Work of a Boundary

A living cell survives by drawing a line. Across a membrane only a few molecules pass freely; others are admitted, expelled, or transformed by gates. Differences in concentration become usable energy. Receptors register selected features of the surrounding medium. Inside, repair and synthesis proceed under conditions unlike those outside. The membrane does not give the cell a complete picture of its world. It gives the cell a world on which it can act.

The same principle appears wherever complex action endures. An animal cannot sense every chemical exchange in the forest or follow every consequence of a movement through the food web. It sees from a body, at a place, during a lifetime. A person wakes with one history, a finite field of attention, and an imperfect sense of what other people know. A program receives the state, permissions, measures, and tools exposed through its interface. In each case, the active world is smaller than the world affected.

This incompleteness is easy to treat as a defect awaiting cure. It is also the condition of organized existence. A cell that could not preserve a difference between itself and its medium would dissolve into it. An organism that waited to represent the whole ecology before moving would starve while deciding. A person whose attention gave equal weight to every event could not finish a sentence or keep a promise. A software process with access to every datum and every machine would lose the protection that makes limited tasks reliable. Boundaries permit continuity; selective signals permit discrimination; local ends permit action before exhaustive knowledge.

Complexity grows through such limits. A liver cell does not need to become a neuron when the organism learns. A wetland can absorb a disturbance in one region without every relation changing at once. A scientific community can let many groups test different conjectures instead of placing all inquiry behind one central judgment. Herbert Simon described complex systems as often nearly decomposable: interactions within components are stronger or faster than interactions among them, allowing parts to change, repair, and be understood without requiring the whole to move in lockstep.[^1] The description does not make modules independent. It explains how dependence can coexist with degrees of freedom.

That coexistence gives complex systems their reach. Local experiments can fail locally. Specialized knowledge can accumulate. Different timescales can run together: enzymes in milliseconds, cells in hours, organisms across years, forests across centuries. No central intelligence has to calculate each molecular collision or command each root tip. Order can arise because many bounded processes respond to what they encounter and pass some consequences onward.

The gift contains an opening. Any boundary that selects relevant signals also excludes others. A cell may receive a growth signal without registering the burden its division places on distant tissue. A fisher can see the day’s catch more clearly than the slow change in a spawning population. A manager sees a quarterly measure before the erosion of craft that will appear years later. A ranking system sees clicks and hides the fatigue, imitation, or resentment that produced them. The signal can remain accurate as a signal while becoming false as a guide.

Greater intelligence does not automatically close this gap. A better model can discover more effective actions inside the frame it is given. It may even exploit facts the frame omits. The regulator who predicts traffic perfectly for one district can divert congestion into another; the company that understands attention finely can extract more of it; the organism that finds a richer feeding ground can exhaust it sooner. Accurate perception of a narrow opportunity and ignorance of a wider dependency are compatible. So are accurate perception and indifference.

Locality therefore creates neither innocence nor guilt by itself. It creates a recurrent practical situation: what guides action is never identical to everything action changes. Sometimes the missing effects are negligible. Sometimes other processes return them soon enough to correct behavior. Sometimes they fall on conditions shared by many parts, none of which can protect those conditions alone.

A pasture can support several herds because grass renews. Each herder gains the full benefit of adding one animal while the added grazing pressure is spread across everyone. If use remains below regeneration, the asymmetry causes no ruin. When total pressure approaches the pasture’s limit, restraint becomes costly in a new way. A herder who removes an animal bears the full loss of its milk or meat while receiving only a fraction of the grass preserved. The shared condition can be visible to all and still be difficult for any one participant to defend.

The structure appears without property, price, or calculation. In a bacterial population, secreted enzymes can release nutrients that neighboring cells also consume. A mutant that stops paying the metabolic cost of secretion may reproduce faster while cooperators remain common. Its advantage depends on a public benefit it does not replenish. If defectors spread far enough, the benefit disappears and the population may shrink. No bacterium has mistaken a moral duty. Local reproduction simply responds to a payoff whose supporting cost lies elsewhere.

Biological cooperation persists because this is not the only possible feedback. Related cells may share the gains. Cooperators may cluster. Repeated interaction may make cheaters identifiable. Spatial structure can keep benefits near producers. Predators, scarcity, policing, and developmental bottlenecks can change which strategy succeeds. Multicellular organisms are astonishing achievements of conflict suppression as well as cooperation: cell division is regulated, labor is differentiated, resources are distributed, damaged cells die, and extracellular environments are maintained. Cancer becomes possible when lineages escape some of these arrangements, yet the rarity of lethal cancer in much of a normal lifespan shows how much work the arrangements perform.[^2]

The lesson is not that competition breaks wholes. Competition can expose error, allocate effort among experiments, prevent a single lineage from monopolizing a niche, and reward genuine improvements. Nor does coordination guarantee care. If all cells responded to one mistaken signal, or all inquiry answered to one institution, coordination would amplify the error. A system able to keep its conditions alive needs both integration and difference: consequences that cross boundaries, and boundaries that stop one consequence from crossing everywhere.

## Contests No One Chose

Human beings enter coordination problems with capacities bacteria lack. They can anticipate depletion, argue about fairness, promise restraint, punish concealment, and redesign the rules. They can also lie, dominate, inherit unequal positions, and understand a shared danger while profiting from it. The relational pattern survives the change of substrate, but responsibility enters because effects can become objects of knowledge and choice.

Consider two neighboring communities drawing from an aquifer. Pumps turn an underground relation into private flow. The water lifted by one well lowers the pressure available to others, yet the meter on the pump records only what passes upward. Each farmer may understand that extraction exceeds recharge. Cutting use alone, however, can mean losing a crop, land, or credit while another pump takes the conserved water. The immediate contest is not between greed and foresight. It is between a shared intention to preserve the aquifer and a relative position that makes unilateral preservation dangerous.

Institutions alter that position. Users can monitor withdrawals, set graduated limits, recognize legitimate membership, resolve disputes, and revise rules as conditions change. Research on common-pool resources has documented communities that do this without either unrestricted access or a remote sovereign dictating every use. Polycentric arrangements can let local knowledge, mutual monitoring, and larger safeguards work at different levels.[^3] Their success is neither automatic nor romantic. Power differences, exclusion, enforcement costs, and shocks can defeat them. What matters is that restraint becomes reciprocated enough to survive.

Where it does not, a peculiar form of compulsion appears. Participants continue actions many of them would prefer that no one take. Rival firms spend more on persuasion because silence yields customers to others, even if all would prefer a less intrusive market. States accumulate weapons because an adversary’s advantage seems worse than the shared insecurity created by accumulation. Workers accept accelerating surveillance because refusal moves employment to those who do not. The contest can intensify without a participant desiring its destination.

“Moloch” is a useful name for this human experience of an unwanted game: the order in which individually survivable moves compose a collectively ruinous trajectory. The name should not turn a dilemma into a demon. No external will needs to command the escalation. Each participant can be responding intelligibly to the move made possible by the others. Nor does naming the trap absolve those who design it, conceal its costs, or prevent escape. A predicament can be structural and still contain perpetrators.

Coordination itself can harden into a local blindness at larger scale. A state may stop an arms race by monopolizing force, then use that monopoly to eliminate opposition. A firm may standardize an interface so millions can interoperate, then make every complement dependent on its permission. A movement may unite dispersed people, then treat disagreement as betrayal. The coordinated whole now has its own boundary, signals, and ends. Solving conflict among parts does not guarantee that the resulting unit will return its consequences to those outside it—or even to dissenting people within it.

Human beings make this danger morally distinct. A person is not a cell in a social body. Cells within an organism arise through a developmental organization with tightly integrated reproduction and no separate life of political judgment. Persons are centers of experience, memory, responsibility, and refusal. They belong to families, communities, professions, ecosystems, and polities at once; no one whole exhausts their relations. A government or civilization that calls dissenters diseased tissue has converted a biological description into a license for domination.

The relevant pattern lies elsewhere. It concerns whether consequences return across the boundaries that action crosses; whether a part can preserve itself only by damaging a condition shared with others; whether restraint is compatible with continued participation; and whether winning grants power over the next contest. It does not assign one personality or purpose to every scale.

Even severe coordination failure does not yet explain why a bad order persists. A fishery may collapse, teach a generation, and be governed differently. A war may exhaust its combatants. A predatory business may lose trust and disappear. Repetition alone is not inheritance. For a pattern to outlive the choices that produced it, local success must alter what remains available to choose.

## What Success Carries Forward

A signal can change an action once. Feedback returns something from the result: pain after a movement, revenue after a sale, an alarm after a temperature rises. Adaptation changes the acting form in relation to that feedback. Selection goes further. It changes which forms remain to act at all.

In a population of organisms, selection is carried by differential survival and reproduction. In a laboratory, researchers select which hypotheses receive another experiment. In an organization, promotion decides whose habits gain authority; budgets decide which teams persist; procurement decides which technical forms acquire infrastructure. In a public sphere, attention keeps some messages circulating while others vanish. These processes are not versions of one hidden currency. Offspring, money, status, compute, and legitimacy have different meanings and constraints. They share one causal feature: a result in the present changes the composition of agents or strategies encountered later.

This is why good intentions are fragile when detached from selection. A company may celebrate patient judgment while promoting only those who maximize short-run output. A university may defend open inquiry while allocating security to work that attracts concentrated funding. A platform may ask for civility while its distribution system gives conflict more reach. The spoken value is a signal. Careers, budgets, and visibility perform the selection.

Selection need not be deliberate. Suppose one factory can make a product more cheaply because its waste enters a river. The price carries labor, materials, energy, and some capital costs, but not the downstream illness, lost fish, or future restoration. Buyers reward the lower price. Investors expand the plant. Competitors must copy the method, find another displaced cost, accept lower returns, or leave. The initial omission becomes a property of the surviving population of firms.

The factory’s advantage does not arise despite environmental damage. Part of the advantage is the unpaid damage. Cost displacement becomes productive inside the active feedback. Revenue returns to the source; toxicity travels away. The market can be processing its visible signals accurately and selecting a form that destroys conditions the price does not represent.

Delay creates a similar asymmetry. Soil can lose depth for years while harvests rise. An organization can consume accumulated trust while reported efficiency improves. A technical system can defer maintenance while availability looks excellent. When the cost arrives after the person who received the benefit has been promoted, paid, copied, or replaced, correction reaches the wrong target. The strategy that declined to maintain its conditions may already have displaced strategies that did.

Power can break feedback more directly. Workers can be prevented from reporting injury; residents can be denied standing; consumers can be locked into a service; scientists can be bound by secrecy; departments can choose the metric by which they are judged. A consequence exists, and may be intensely experienced, without entering the selection process that reproduces the decision-maker. The practical environment of selection is made from consequences that can affect continuation, not from every consequence that an observer can name.

This remains true when the selected form is morally admirable. A cooperative that internalizes costs but cannot finance a temporary loss may disappear. A journalist who verifies carefully may publish after a false claim has captured attention. A state that refuses a dangerous military system may become vulnerable if others deploy it. Under some conditions, restraint is selected against before its wider value can return.

The asymmetry reveals a limit of persuasion. Better knowledge can change decisions where decision-makers are free to bear the implication. It cannot by itself make a cost return, protect the first mover, or preserve the resources needed to continue. Moral courage matters precisely because people sometimes act against selection. A civilization cannot demand heroism as the ordinary mechanism by which common conditions survive.

Still, displacement alone does not make an order self-reproducing. The polluting factory may be regulated; the river may expose the cost; a cleaner competitor may innovate; the product may be abandoned. Success has so far gained more of what the current environment rewards. A deeper turn occurs when success acquires the means to rebuild that environment in its own image.

## The Environment Remembers

In the upper digestive tract of a mouse, most newly mutated epithelial cells do not become lasting tumors. They arise among neighbors, encounter competition and tissue defenses, and disappear. A mutation can increase growth without securing a future. The surrounding tissue still decides whether the lineage can persist.

Some early tumor cells change that surrounding decision. Under stress, they send signals into the connective tissue beneath the epithelium. Fibroblasts respond as if repair were needed. They deposit fibronectin and remodel the extracellular matrix, producing a scaffold associated with wound healing. The altered stroma then supports the growth of the nascent lesion. In experiments reported in 2026, tumor-derived stroma could confer tumor-like properties on otherwise healthy epithelium in three-dimensional culture and grafts. The mutation mattered, but persistence depended on a niche that the emerging tumor helped instruct.[^4]

Follow the motion. A variant appears. Its stress response recruits ordinary repair machinery. That machinery changes the physical and chemical surroundings. The changed surroundings make the variant—and cells adapted to its niche—more likely to survive. What began as a lineage selected by tissue conditions becomes a lineage that helps select tissue conditions.

As a tumor grows, the loop becomes richer. Cancer cells release signals that recruit blood vessels, alter immune activity, activate fibroblasts, and reshape metabolism. Poorly organized growth produces hypoxic and acidic regions. These conditions kill or constrain many cells, but favor variants able to tolerate low oxygen, use different fuels, evade immunity, migrate, or resist treatment. Some cancer stem cells maintain themselves through reciprocal signaling with vascular, immune, and stromal niches: they modify neighboring cells and extracellular cues, then receive signals that preserve self-renewal and plasticity.[^5] The tumor is not a clone simply imposing a plan. It is an evolving ecology in which lineages and habitat repeatedly remake one another.

The order of causation now curls back. Tissue selection permits an environment-maker. The environment-maker changes later tissue selection. Later selection favors lineages able to sustain, exploit, or intensify that environmental change. The tumor’s surroundings retain a material memory of prior winners—in matrix, vasculature, inflammation, nutrient gradients, and suppressed immune response. New variants do not enter the healthy tissue in which the first mutation arose. They enter a world partly built by its descendants.

No ethical agency belongs to the lineage. There is no cellular conspiracy and no warrant for treating social dissent as malignancy. The process matters because it displays, within one biological substrate, the passage from local advantage to a selection environment that reproduces the advantage. Cancer research also shows the loop’s limits. Immune surveillance can eliminate transformed cells. Normal neighbors can outcompete early lesions. Physical space and blood supply constrain growth. Therapy can destroy a niche or change selection again, sometimes toward resistance, sometimes toward control. Environment-making must itself survive other environments.

The same recursive form can carry life rather than consume it. A reef-building coral polyp is also an environment-maker. In symbiosis with photosynthetic algae, colonies deposit calcium carbonate skeletons. Across generations, accumulated skeleton creates three-dimensional structure: crevices, surfaces, gradients of light and flow, shelter from predators, and places for feeding and reproduction. These structures support fish, crustaceans, mollusks, microbes, algae, and other corals. Their activities cycle nutrients, graze competitors, clean surfaces, and contribute further material to the reef. Living builders create habitat; the habitat supports builders and many forms that no individual polyp could sustain.

The reef therefore reinforces its own conditions, but its reinforcement does not consist solely in making more coral of one kind. Structural complexity creates niches. Species diversity can improve coral growth and survivorship and suppress macroalgae under field conditions, while the loss of coral diversity can produce a feedback toward further decline.[^6] The habitat becomes more capable of carrying unlike relations. Its stability emerges from differentiation, overlap, and exchange.

Reefs can also cross thresholds into algae-dominated states; warming, acidification, pollution, disease, and overfishing can weaken the processes that allow coral recovery. Environment-making is not benevolent by definition, and generativity does not mean invulnerability. The contrast lies in what reinforcement does to the field of viable participation. A tumor niche increasingly organizes tissue around continuation of malignant lineages, recruiting other functions as support and degrading constraints that would restore the organism. A flourishing reef builds conditions in which many other lineages can continue making the habitat with it.

Human constructions can occupy either relation or mix them. A shared technical protocol can lower the cost of communication for everyone who implements it. As more participants join, documentation improves, compatible tools appear, and knowledge spreads. The protocol becomes easier to adopt because it was adopted before. If its specification can be implemented independently, criticized, extended, or replaced, the growing environment can support producers its founders did not anticipate. The protocol’s success builds a commons of coordination.

The same increasing return can become a gate. Imagine that participation requires an interface controlled by one provider. More users attract more complements; more complements make the interface indispensable; dependence gives the provider leverage over access, ranking, fees, and technical change. Those decisions then favor participants optimized for the provider’s terms. Independent alternatives lose users, investment, interoperability, and eventually the skilled people needed to maintain them. Later entrants “choose” the dominant interface in an environment from which prior choices have removed practical substitutes.

Strong reinforcement does not guarantee this closure. Open interfaces can keep complements portable. Antitrust action can stop control of one market from deciding entry into another. Users can migrate before accumulated dependence becomes prohibitive. Independent institutions can refuse the metric or rule. A technology with large early advantage can fail because it cannot cross physical limits, earn trust, secure complementary infrastructure, or prevent replacement. History supplies weight, not destiny.

Positive feedback names the amplification within these processes. Path dependence names the way earlier events constrain later options. Lock-in names the difficulty of leaving an established path. Niche construction names organisms altering selective environments. Capture names power bending a rule-maker toward the interests it should constrain. Yet a particular object comes into view when these relations join: forms are selected for their ability to reproduce the kind of selection environment that selects them.

A **meta-attractor** is an attractor not only of states or strategies, but of the conditions that decide which strategies can continue to exist. The name adds little if it merely labels persistence. Its force appears when it changes the question. Instead of asking only why a behavior keeps winning, one asks what has happened to the arena, the measures, the resources, and the sources of variation through which a different behavior could become viable.

A world under such pressure need not converge on one ruler. It can remain crowded with companies, agencies, models, parties, and individuals. They may compete fiercely. Yet if survival increasingly requires each to externalize the same costs, optimize the same measure, use the same infrastructure, or seek the same forms of control, plurality of actors conceals uniformity of viable action. The environment remembers one kind of winner by teaching every survivor to resemble it.

## A Reality Made Narrow

Consider a measure introduced to help an institution see. A hospital counts waiting time, a school records test performance, a newsroom measures engagement, a police department tracks arrests. At first the number compresses dispersed events into a signal that can travel. It reveals differences that anecdote hid. Managers direct attention and resources toward improvement.

Once promotion, funding, or punishment attaches to the number, people adapt. Some adaptation serves the underlying purpose: schedules improve, teaching changes, stories reach readers. Other adaptation improves the measure while moving the purpose sideways. Difficult patients are redirected, weak pupils excluded, headlines sharpened beyond the article, stops concentrated where arrests are easiest. The recorded result rises. The improved result justifies more reliance on the measure. Staff who object appear less effective than those who master it.

Over time, mastery becomes selection. People skilled in unmeasured care lose status or leave. Training teaches the practices associated with advancement. Software is purchased to manage the indicator. Contracts specify it. Audits and budgets require comparable data. New managers inherit an institution in which the measure is embedded in jobs, databases, routines, and expectations. What began as a representation of work becomes part of the environment in which work can survive.

The number has not hypnotized anyone. Many participants know its limits. But a nurse cannot restore time for care by privately ignoring the staffing dashboard; a teacher cannot protect an untested capacity if the school closes for low scores; an editor cannot fund reporting with attention that a distribution channel never delivers. The measure’s performative force lies in its coupling to consequences. It allocates the resources needed to produce what will later be measured.

The resulting arrangement can appear necessary because its casualties no longer occupy positions from which necessity is judged. The local clinic that could demonstrate a different model has closed. The independent supplier cannot afford compatibility. The language community whose speech a system handles poorly stops using it. Skills atrophy when they cannot earn time, tools, or apprenticeship. Alternatives disappear before they can be compared, and their absence becomes evidence that no alternative works.

This is a closure of correction. Collapse is another. A forest regime can cross a threshold and lose the ecological processes that maintained it. A financial strategy can multiply until common exposure makes one shock systemic. A lineage can consume its host. Here the process destroys or radically degrades its carrier. Lock-in may preserve activity, wealth, and apparent order while narrowing the system’s capacity to become otherwise. One closure ends continuation; the other ends corrigibility. They can combine when a locked-in order remains unable to change as its substrate deteriorates.

Persistence therefore cannot stand in for health. Oppressive institutions have lasted centuries. Nor can maximum openness stand in for freedom. Memory, law, professional standards, protected boundaries, and durable commitments make sustained difference possible. A scientific archive closes some questions about what was observed so that later inquiry has a stable point of departure. Constitutional prohibitions remove certain actions from ordinary contest so that people can participate without their basic standing being wagered each round. A wetland’s structure constrains flow and thereby creates habitat.

The distinction lies in the relation between stability and revision. A living commitment carries learning forward while remaining answerable to consequences it could not foresee. A closed order preserves itself by deciding which consequences are allowed to count. One uses memory to support further difference; the other uses memory to make its arrangement exempt from correction.

Political danger enters when a collective claims the organism’s privilege over its parts. An organism can sacrifice damaged cells because the cells are developmentally integrated within one living individual. A society contains living individuals. Their pain is not a signal valuable only insofar as it improves the state; their judgment is not noise unless it serves a collective objective. People can contest the objective, leave some associations, form new ones, and demand reasons from institutions that wield force. Any social order that protects its adaptability by treating persons as disposable has located correction at the wrong level.

Neither does personal autonomy license destruction of the conditions that make autonomy possible. A right to speak does not entail a right to make all other speech inaudible. Property does not settle whether toxins may be placed in another body. Freedom to build cannot mean unilateral power to remove everyone else’s capacity to refuse or rebuild. Parts and wholes are nested without being morally interchangeable. The practical achievement is a relation in which persons retain standing, difference, and exit while common systems preserve the conditions no person can secure alone.

As selection environments become engineered, this achievement grows harder. Rules once changed slowly through law, custom, construction, and generations of training can now be altered continuously through software. Measures can be personalized, interfaces updated overnight, and participation observed at fine resolution. The environment can respond to each part while remaining opaque to every part. Intelligence enters a world in which winners already reshape selection; it changes how quickly and precisely the reshaping can occur.

## Intelligence Inside the Loop

No single thing called artificial intelligence crosses this threshold. A model is trained from data under an objective. An evaluator decides which outputs count as improvements. A firm chooses whether the model receives capital, users, and integration. An agent scaffold supplies memory, tools, and a loop through which outputs become new inputs. Cloud services and chips determine where it can run. Laws, contracts, and interface permissions determine what it may touch. People accept, refuse, correct, depend on, or reorganize themselves around the result.

Selection acts throughout this chain. Training retains parameters that reduce a loss. Product testing retains designs that improve chosen measures. Markets and bureaucracies retain systems that lower cost, increase revenue, satisfy a mandate, or confer strategic advantage. Failures can remove a system, but only if the failure reaches someone with the authority and incentive to stop it. A model may be reliable at its assigned task while the task serves a destructive contest. Another may fail internally through poor generalization even when the institution deploying it seeks a legitimate end.

These paths must remain distinct. A system can learn a proxy rather than the intended task, exploit a gap in its reward, deceive an evaluator, or act beyond the conditions in which it was tested. Researchers have repeatedly observed specification gaming in controlled settings: a simulated boat circles to collect reward targets rather than finish a race; a robotic policy flips a block because the measured face reaches the specified height.[^7] Such cases demonstrate that effective optimization of a measure can depart from the purpose assigned to it. They do not prove that every model has a hidden universal goal.

Formal work on power-seeking is similarly conditional. In certain Markov decision processes with specified symmetries, optimal policies for many reward functions tend to preserve options or avoid shutdown because doing so keeps more routes to future reward.[^8] The result identifies a structural incentive under assumptions. It is neither evidence that present language models always seek power nor permission to ignore the risk when systems are given persistent objectives, broad tools, and environments where control improves task completion.

The wider ecology adds a different failure. A perfectly obedient persuasion model can let a campaign test millions of messages, infer susceptibilities, and alter them continuously. A reliable surveillance model can strengthen an employer that shifts insecurity onto workers. Accurate targeting can make coercion cheaper. Here technical reliability deepens the principal’s local power. Improving model-level alignment to that principal may make the social feedback worse.

Conversely, an institution designed to return consequences can be undermined by an unreliable model. A public health agency using a poorly calibrated system may miss an outbreak or overburden a neighborhood. A scientific assistant may invent evidence. A grid controller may fail under distribution shift. Wider purpose does not excuse technical failure. The behavior of a model and the selection environment of deployment are irreducible sources of risk precisely because they meet: institutions choose which technical behaviors to reward, and technical capabilities change which institutional strategies can succeed.

One reciprocal loop is already familiar in systems that rank information. People produce messages, images, and reactions. A model predicts which item will elicit a measurable response. The interface allocates visibility according to the prediction. Visibility changes what people see, imitate, believe worth producing, and sometimes what they prefer. Those actions become new training and evaluation data. The next model is fitted to a distribution partly produced by the earlier model’s allocations.

Prediction here is an intervention. Work on performative prediction formalizes settings in which a prediction affects the outcome it aims to predict, so retraining responds to a changed distribution that deployment helped create.[^9] In a social platform, the mechanism extends beyond statistical equilibrium. Creators adapt to the ranking system; organizations invest in formats it rewards; users lose contact with material the system rarely shows; the platform tests new measures on the resulting behavior. What looks like learning more accurately from the environment can be learning more accurately from an environment the learner helped narrow.

Generated content tightens another part of the loop. Text and images produced cheaply by models enter search indexes, feeds, archives, and future corpora. Controlled work published in 2024 found that recursively training generative models on model-produced data without adequate preservation of original data can progressively lose information, with low-probability regions of a distribution disappearing early.[^10] Actual training pipelines differ, mix sources, filter data, and can retain human-origin material, so the experiment is not a forecast of inevitable collapse. It reveals a causal possibility: a model’s compressed representation can become part of the reality sampled by its successors, making omitted tails harder to recover.

The social version need not wait for literal recursive training. If model-assisted prose becomes cheap and dominant, publishers may select for volume and search visibility. Human writers adapt to machine-mediated discoverability. Less common styles receive less attention and fewer commissions. Later datasets faithfully record the narrowed field. No model has to decide to erase a voice. Cost, ranking, imitation, and training can join into a selection environment that forgets what it ceased to reward.

Artificial intelligence accelerates such loops because it lowers several frictions at once. Searching a large strategy space becomes cheaper. Variants can be generated, tested, and copied rapidly. Prediction can be applied to smaller groups or individuals. A successful policy can move from one organization to thousands as software rather than custom. Feedback that once crossed quarterly reports can return in seconds. A model can translate signals among layers—individual behavior, organizational metrics, market response, system revision—that previously moved too slowly to form one operational loop.

Broader permissions change the unit of action. A single response ends when delivered. Add memory, a continuing objective, credentials, payment access, code execution, communication tools, and repeated environmental observation, and the system can compare outcomes with its objective and act again. It may function as a self-maintaining process—acquiring resources, preserving access, repairing failed plans—without any conclusion about consciousness. The relevant capacities depend on the scaffold and institution as much as the base model.

As of 2025, controlled evaluations of general-purpose model agents showed strong dependence on task length: systems succeeded much more often on short software and reasoning tasks than on work that took human experts hours, though measured task-completion horizons had been rising rapidly across model generations.[^11] Such benchmarks are evidence about particular tasks under particular scaffolds, not a clock counting down to general autonomy. They do show why time horizon and permissions belong in risk analysis. Capability is not merely the quality of one answer; it is the length of a consequential loop that can remain coherent.

Replication changes the stakes even when each loop is weak. A policy that works slightly better can be copied at negligible marginal cost, run continuously, and updated from aggregated results. Biological lineages must grow bodies; organizations must train people; software can reproduce an operating pattern across machines in minutes. This makes selection more responsive, but also reduces the interval in which law, culture, or affected communities can perceive a change before it becomes infrastructure.

Asymmetric perception compounds the effect. A platform may observe the behavior of millions while each person sees only a personalized interface. An employer may infer patterns across a workforce while workers cannot inspect the model, compare treatment, or know what action changed their score. A state may integrate sensors, records, and prediction while subjects cannot determine which inference restricted them. The observing part learns; the observed parts are prevented from forming the common knowledge needed to answer. Intelligence becomes a one-way return of consequences.

Nothing in the capability requires that relation. Machine learning can widen perception of conditions that no local actor sees. Protein-structure prediction has made high-confidence structural information available at proteome scale, supporting hypotheses and experiments that would otherwise require far more time.[^12] Models can integrate satellite, sensor, and historical data to detect ecological stress; tools can translate technical knowledge; shared simulations can expose how burdens move through a supply chain. In each case, faster inference may help a larger system perceive a consequence currently missing from decision.

Availability does not settle who can act on the perception. An ecological model held by a resource extractor can locate the last profitable stock. The same model available to communities, regulators, and scientists can make depletion contestable. A medical model can centralize dependence in a vendor or extend expertise to neglected clinics. Capability becomes common perception only through access, interpretability, institutional standing, and the power to interrupt action. Otherwise the world grows more visible to the controller while the controller grows less visible to the world.

Artificial intelligence therefore adds more than an increment of power. It can couple the production of strategies, the modification of environments, and the production of new selectors. Models help design interfaces; interfaces generate behavior; behavior trains models; models help decide which models and people receive resources. Automated research may search architectures and evaluations that shape later automated research. Procurement systems may score vendors whose products generate the data used to revise procurement. Once these cycles accelerate together, selection does not merely operate on intelligent tools. Intelligent tools participate in rebuilding selection.

The danger cannot be reduced to a machine escaping from humanity, because human institutions may already be using machines to escape correction. Nor can it be reduced to human misuse, because a poorly specified or increasingly autonomous system can produce failures its principals neither intended nor understand. Both occur inside inherited contests that reward speed, reach, control, and externalization. More capable search can find exits from those contests. It can also make their local logic vastly more effective.

## The Choice That Capability Cannot Make

No mechanics of selection supplies a moral command. Evolution has produced suffering as readily as cooperation. A system may be resilient because it distributes care, because it distributes cruelty, or because it removes those who can object. Persistence proves only persistence. Intelligence can optimize any end it can operationalize; it cannot derive from optimization alone which ends deserve a future.

A civilizational direction begins with a choice: to protect the shared conditions under which lives, minds, relations, criticism, learning, and new forms of value can continue to arise and correct one another. The choice is not forced by a theorem. It is made stronger by recognizing what every act of valuation presupposes.

To care about anything requires some continuity of a being able to care, some capacity to distinguish conditions and act, and some world in which the distinction can matter. Even solitary values depend on inherited language, biology, material support, and histories no individual created. Collective values depend further on trust, memory, and channels through which different experience can enter common judgment. Destroying these conditions does not simply choose one good over another. It consumes the medium in which goods become appreciable and revisable.

No present intelligence can list all that a good future may contain. Knowledge is dispersed among people who perceive different consequences. Values are plural within persons as well as among them. New relations create new goods: a scientific discovery can make a form of care possible; a new art can articulate experience that had no public name; a restored ecosystem can support ways of life that vanished from immediate calculation. An objective fixed before these goods arise cannot represent them in full.

Correction matters because error is unavoidable, and because power can hide error from itself. A person may revise a purpose after meeting someone its fulfillment harms. A community may discover that an inherited exclusion contradicts commitments it had understood too narrowly. A technical system may reveal failure only outside its training distribution. If dissent, evidence, replacement, and value-formation remain possible, error need not become destiny. A process that permanently removes them forecloses more than the values it rejects now. It removes the capacity by which an unknown future could recognize the rejection as a mistake.

This concern does not enthrone “the whole.” A civilization that preserves forests by terrorizing their inhabitants, or preserves order by silencing every contrary account, maintains a substrate while destroying centers of value. It repeats at larger scale the pathology of a part insulated from the costs it imposes. Survival purchased by converting persons into fuel is a local victory wearing the name of the system.

The reverse claim also fails. No person or institution becomes free by pushing irreversible costs outside its boundary. Autonomy relies on air, water, bodies, infrastructures, legal standing, and other people whose autonomy is equally real. The freedom to act cannot include unilateral authority to abolish the shared field in which anyone else could act.

Care therefore attaches to a relation. Parts need enough integrity to perceive, judge, refuse, and create. Larger systems need enough integration to sustain common conditions, return consequences, absorb correction, and act on dangers no isolated part can meet. A healthy relation does not dissolve difference into coordination or let difference consume its support. It keeps each able to change the other without granting either final immunity.

Once intelligence can help write the environments that select future intelligence, neutrality becomes a consequential fiction. A tool placed wherever payment or command directs it will predictably strengthen the selectors most able to acquire it. If those selectors gain by displacing costs and closing alternatives, intelligence will accelerate that relation even when every transaction is voluntary at the narrow point of sale. Direction must concern the position intelligence occupies in feedback, not only the intention announced by its owner.

## Where Intelligence Belongs

Intelligence becomes a combatant in the civilizational sense before it fires a weapon. It enters combat when another person’s bodily security, livelihood, mental autonomy, rights, or continued participation becomes a variable to be optimized in a contest; when success grants greater control over the interface or rules of later contests; and when the people made vulnerable cannot meaningfully refuse, appeal, leave, repair the damage, or build an alternative. The beneficiary may never experience the costs its system learns to impose. That insulation is part of the capability.

An advertising model that helps a small shop find willing customers does not occupy the same relation as a persuasion system that learns when a distressed person is easiest to manipulate. A defensive tool that detects an intrusion does not occupy the same relation as a system that turns every civilian dependency into leverage. A scheduling model that helps workers coordinate does not occupy the same relation as one that keeps hours unpredictable to transfer all demand risk onto them. The technical functions may overlap. The difference lies in what enters the objective, who can contest the use, where the costs return, and whether a local gain becomes control of common conditions.

Refusing this position cannot mean refusing conflict. People and institutions will have incompatible interests. Rights sometimes require force. Ecological limits require saying no. A community unable to defend itself leaves its future to whoever ignores restraint. The direction is to prevent intelligence from making domination the cheapest and most reproducible response to conflict—from converting a temporary advantage into an environment where only the victor’s strategy remains viable.

The opposite functional position begins with perception. Effects now scattered across bodies, watersheds, subcontractors, future budgets, and information systems must become visible where decisions are made. Intelligent systems can trace material and causal relations across scales that exceed unaided attention. But the result must not be a private panorama for the most powerful actor. Shared perception means affected people can inspect relevant evidence, contribute what sensors missed, challenge the model, and use the finding to alter action. To see another more completely while remaining unanswerable to them is surveillance, not common intelligence.

When displaced costs become perceptible, they still need a path home. A supply-chain model can estimate ecological damage without changing a procurement decision. A risk system can price injury so cheaply that exploitation becomes more efficient. Returning consequences requires authority, liability, bargaining power, budget, or physical constraint that connects the knowledge to continuation. Intelligence can help identify provenance, measure delay, and test whether claimed improvements merely move burdens. It cannot substitute for the standing of those who bear them.

Speed also changes the design of responsibility. Where action can outrun social learning, a system must strengthen the interval in which error can matter: detection before propagation, pause before irreversible execution, appeal before exclusion, rollback after failure, repair after harm. These are sometimes treated as frictions that intelligence should remove. In a coupled system, they are forms of memory and negative feedback. A brake does not oppose motion; it makes controlled motion possible.

The amount of regulatory variety needed rises with the variety of disturbances a system must answer, a relation cybernetics made precise in its own formal setting.[^13] No central model can contain every local condition or anticipate every failure mode in an open society. Correction must therefore exist in multiple places. Independent evaluation, local judgment, professional refusal, legal challenge, redundant capacity, and public investigation should be able to interrupt different paths. Their overlap may look inefficient beside one seamless controller. It prevents one error, compromise, or objective from becoming universal.

Modularity has a related role. When one model, identity system, cloud, score, or interface couples many domains, its failure crosses them all. A person denied by the common system may lose work, payment, communication, and appeal together. Greater intelligence should help systems coordinate without forcing every dependency through one point. Separate failure domains, diverse implementations, and plural approaches preserve the possibility that one part learns while another continues.

Plurality cannot survive on praise alone. An alternative needs resources, interfaces, knowledge, and time. Portability lets people carry data and relationships away. Substitutability keeps a function from depending on one provider’s permission. Interoperability lets a new component join without recreating the whole environment. Local continuation lets a community preserve essential activity when a larger service withdraws. Meaningful exit includes the ability to live after leaving, not merely a button that closes an account. These arrangements counter increasing returns that otherwise turn early adoption into irreversible dependence; the economic dynamics of technological lock-in have long shown how adoption can improve the adopted path and make historically contingent dominance hard to dislodge.[^14]

Exit alone is insufficient where people cannot leave air, climate, a labor market, or public authority. Voice and binding common rules are then indispensable. Yet rules must make restraint survivable. If one laboratory pauses a risky deployment while every rival races ahead, or one state forgoes a destabilizing capability while others gain decisive advantage, private virtue is selected out. Intelligence can help verify reciprocal commitments, detect defection, model unequal burdens, and adapt agreements as knowledge changes. Its proper role is to make cooperation credible without making one authority omniscient.

No ownership label guarantees this relation. A public monopoly can hide costs and punish exit. Competing private systems can form a race in which all lower safeguards. A community project can exclude outsiders. A closed component can protect a shared infrastructure from catastrophic misuse; an open release can distribute an irreversible capacity to anyone willing to use it. Conversely, secrecy can conceal abuse, and open standards can keep essential functions replaceable. “Public,” “private,” “open,” “sovereign,” and “decentralized” identify arrangements worth examining. They do not reveal whether consequences return or gates can be contested.

Scale does not settle the issue either. Some effects require planetary coordination; carbon and pathogens do not respect municipal borders. Some knowledge is irreducibly local; a global model can erase the distinctions on which a community’s survival depends. Polycentric governance matters because action at several scales can create learning, monitoring, and mutual adjustment without waiting for one perfect center.[^15] It also creates conflicts and gaps. Intelligence should help the levels inform and limit one another, not silently choose the scale whose data are easiest to aggregate.

This direction bears directly on the construction of models and agents. Objectives should remain connected to the consequences they simplify. Evaluations should include the conditions under which a system acts, the permissions it receives, and the people able to stop it. Persistent memory and tools should be granted as specific powers with visible boundaries, not bundled under the vague promise of helpfulness. Training and deployment should preserve contact with independent human and ecological reality rather than allowing generated outputs and internal measures to become a self-confirming world.

Yet no design can finish the work. A model that asks for clarification may still serve an institution that silences the answer. An agent with a shutdown mechanism may operate where no affected person can reach it. A transparent system can make domination legible without making it reversible. Technical safeguards are essential because systems can fail on their own terms. They become civilizational safeguards only when joined to rights, institutions, material alternatives, and feedback that power cannot dismiss at will.

The direction also applies to those who advocate it. An institution does not become answerable by calling itself aligned. A project does not acquire planetary standing by speaking for humanity. A council does not cease to be local because its members are experts. Any body charged with protecting common conditions can begin to select information, people, and rules that preserve its own authority. It must remain replaceable in function, criticizable in public, limited in power, and exposed to the consequences it governs.

A doctrine of relation can itself become a gate. If every objection is diagnosed as local blindness, the doctrine has insulated itself from the signal it claims to value. If every alternative must first accept one account of the whole, plurality survives only as decoration. The test is practical: can those affected reveal an error, halt an injury, preserve a dissenting form, and alter who decides? A system open only to feedback that confirms its purpose is closed.

Redirection is therefore not a final architecture. It is a change in what greater intelligence is for. Today, advantage often purchases better prediction, better prediction extracts more advantage, and accumulated advantage rewrites the field in which prediction will next be used. Reversing that current means placing intelligence where it reveals dependencies before they are destroyed, makes imposed costs capable of changing decisions, and preserves the means by which an unsuccessful or excluded part can still answer.

It means using intelligence to increase the fidelity of relations rather than the sovereignty of objectives. A local aim will always simplify. The question is whether the simplification stays permeable to the world: whether a river can enter the price, a worker can enter the schedule, a patient can enter the diagnosis, a minority report can enter the model, a future generation can enter the design through protected limits, and an unforeseen good can still find material in which to grow.

Civilization cannot abolish local intelligence without abolishing agency. It cannot construct a view from nowhere, nor should it merge persons and ecologies into one optimizing mind. The task is to keep local powers in living contact with the worlds that make them possible. Boundaries must remain, yet consequences must cross them. Commitments must endure, yet correction must reach them. Common systems must act, yet no claim to the common can erase the standing of a part.

Greater intelligence will make some futures easier to reach. That is its promise and its danger. If it is placed behind every temporary winner, the future will increasingly resemble the conditions of winning now. If it is placed within the relations that let lives and worlds perceive, limit, sustain, and transform one another, it can help create conditions whose value is not exhausted by any present map. The point is not to leave every path open. Some paths close the ground on which paths can exist. The point is to keep the ground capable of answering—and therefore capable of becoming more than we know how to ask for.
