# Why Civilization Must Redirect Intelligence

## The necessary incompleteness of every part

A living boundary does not mark where relation ends. It marks the form relation must take for something distinct to continue.

The membrane of a cell is neither a prison wall nor a line drawn around self-sufficient matter. It is a selective exchange. Nutrients enter, waste leaves, signals are received, concentrations are restored, damage is repaired. If nothing crosses, the cell dies; if every difference is erased, it also dies. The cell persists by maintaining an inside that cannot maintain itself from inside alone.

This is not a biological metaphor imposed on everything else. A person is not a cell, a state is not a body, and civilization has no single organismic purpose. But organized action everywhere requires some analogous incompleteness. An animal encounters an ecology through a body and a limited sensory field. A person acts through finite attention, memory, language, relationship, and historical position. An institution defines membership, jurisdiction, responsibility, and the interval over which consequences count. A computational system receives a world through the state, permissions, tools, and evaluation exposed at its interface. Each boundary brings certain differences close enough to guide action. Each leaves most of the conditions supporting that action outside immediate view.

Locality, therefore, is not a defect that greater intelligence can simply overcome. Without partial closure there is no memory, specialization, responsibility, parallel experiment, or local repair. Complex systems are often viable precisely because their parts need not wait for every distant relation before responding.[^1] The fantasy of abolishing boundaries in favor of one perfectly informed center misunderstands intelligence twice: it destroys the differentiated processes from which knowledge arises, then gives the center a completeness no actual center can possess.

Some boundaries are materially maintained. Cells regulate exchange; organisms heal; courts reproduce jurisdiction through offices, records, recognition, and force; software enforces permissions and state transitions. Other boundaries are drawn in order to understand. A factory can be bounded by a corporate ledger, the households that reproduce its labor, the watershed receiving its discharge, the supply chain carrying its inputs, the state securing its contracts, or the century over which its emissions act. Redrawing the boundary does not create these systems. It changes which dependencies and consequences become visible.

Such analytical boundaries are plural but not arbitrary. One description may be excellent for tracing cash and useless for tracing illness. Another may reveal ecological loss but conceal how a community obtains food this winter. There is no final line from which the world becomes one system with one objective. Wider descriptions reveal exclusions; they do not produce omniscience.

The permanent problem is already present. Every part needs enough closure to act, yet no part contains within that closure all the conditions that make its action possible. A part can therefore succeed according to the feedback available within its boundary while consuming a world maintained beyond it. Intelligence begins as the capacity to act locally. Its civilizational question is whether what lies beyond the local boundary can return before local success destroys it.

## The worlds a system remembers

Boundaries make histories visible, and histories do not wander without form. A lake may absorb rising nutrient loads for years and then shift from a clear regime to a turbid one sustained by different relations among plants, algae, fish, light, and sediment. Removing the last increment of pollution may not bring the earlier lake back. A disturbance has not merely changed a quantity; it has moved the system into another way of reproducing itself.[^2]

In mathematics, an attractor is specified within a state space, a dynamic, a topology or metric, a timescale, and a basin of trajectories. Societies and civilizations rarely offer fixed state spaces, much less fixed equations. Their participants change the rules and invent new variables. To speak of an institutional attractor is therefore to speak more modestly of a metastable regime: a pattern of relations that tends to reconstruct itself through identifiable carriers—law, habit, infrastructure, training, ownership, legitimacy, or force—across changes of circumstance and personnel.

The important object is not one attractor but an ecology of them. A forest preserves different species, successional patterns, fire relations, water cycles, and human practices. A city contains households, firms, neighborhoods, public systems, informal networks, and ecological flows. Each can have several recurrent organizations; each can be a whole from one boundary and a part or environment from another. They support and disturb one another without collapsing into a single dynamic.

Plurality in such a world is more than permission to choose. Partially independent systems store different information. A seed lineage retains a response to heat or disease that appears unnecessary until conditions change. A language preserves distinctions another language cannot exactly reproduce. A craft, legal tradition, scientific school, or form of care embodies knowledge that may become visible only through practice. None is guaranteed to be wise. Their value is that they have not all learned through the same errors.

Nor does plurality by count provide safety. Ten thousand organizations relying on the same cloud, credit system, logistics route, evaluation, and technical standard can share one point of failure. Diversity protects when systems retain different feedback, substitutable pathways, modular containment, and some ability to reproduce beyond a common dependency. Standardization can spread knowledge and mutual aid; coupling becomes dangerous when it eliminates the reservoirs from which another response could emerge.[^3]

A civilization can thus look diverse while becoming dynamically uniform. It may contain many firms, universities, media channels, parties, states, and cultural products, yet fewer viable ways for any of them to continue. If education, science, government, art, and care must all pass through the same measures of growth, return, ranking, security, or attention before receiving resources, their names remain plural while their conditions of reproduction converge.

The loss appears before collapse. It appears when the dominant system no longer encounters sufficiently independent evidence that another arrangement can live; when discarded skills can no longer be recovered; when communities capable of recognizing a failure have become dependent on the activity producing it. Totalization destroys not only alternatives but the memory that alternatives were possible and the protected differences from which unimagined ones might form.

That is why preserving an ecology of attractors is not nostalgia for every local order. Local systems can preserve cruelty, superstition, and domination. A plurality worth defending must let consequences cross boundaries and let affected people challenge what a local whole calls its tradition. Its value lies not in freezing difference but in keeping enough independent perception and agency for difference to learn.

## The game enters the player

Many collective failures begin with a recognizable trap. Each participant depends on a shared condition, each receives a concentrated advantage from taking more, and much of the cost returns later or elsewhere. The first firm to carry a cost its rivals can export may disappear. The first state to renounce a capability without reciprocal restraint may become vulnerable. The first platform to reject a profitable method of capturing attention may lose the audience through which a better practice could matter.

Moloch is a name for the human experience of this arrangement: people can understand the unwanted destination and continue toward it because stopping alone threatens their ability to remain participants. No one needs to desire the whole result. The expected conduct of the others is enough to make each contribution locally reasonable.

Competition is not itself the failure. It can distribute search, expose complacency, and prevent one center from universalizing its mistake. Cooperation can become a cartel or a command that silences its members. The decisive distinction is whether restraint is survivable; whether costs return to those who produce them; whether losing preserves a place in future contests; and whether winning one round purchases control over all the rounds that follow.

Even this account begins too late. It imagines completed players entering a bad game. But repeated games change which players exist and what they can understand as success.

An action receives a signal. Some consequence returns as feedback. A process adapts. When the result changes which forms remain, spread, receive resources, acquire authority, or become models for imitation, selection has occurred. The carrier differs across domains—offspring, apprenticeship, promotion, investment, legitimacy, attention, deployment—but its effect is that the next round begins with a changed population.

Suppose two organizations produce the same visible output. One maintains the people, knowledge, equipment, community, and ecology on which the work depends. The other reaches the measured target by exporting exhaustion, risk, repair, and loss. If the active feedback sees price and speed but not renewal, the second appears more efficient. It does not merely avoid paying a cost. Its failure to reproduce the supporting world becomes an advantage over the organization that does.

Selection can therefore remove the forms capable of perceiving wider consequences. A patient laboratory is displaced by one promising faster results; a cooperative workplace by one that transfers volatility to workers; a political actor capable of restraint by one who converts restraint into weakness. The population does not simply contain more harmful behavior. It contains fewer institutions able to carry another relation to the future.

Measures deepen the change. A ranking begins as a representation of quality. Money and status attach to it. Participants reorganize around it; those who optimize it rise; recruitment and training reproduce their judgment. Eventually the ranking is no longer experienced as an imperfect signal. It becomes the practical reality through which a school, researcher, manager, or political leader learns what seriousness looks like. Public measures are performative in this sense: they reorder the activity they claim merely to observe.[^4]

Institutions do not select among finished persons. They train attention, ambition, identity, and the sense of what necessity permits. A person can privately love truth, family, landscape, and justice while occupying a role in which only organizational survival is admissible as a reason. The institution becomes narrower than the people who compose it, then selects the people most able to act through that narrowness.

The deepest turn arrives when success brings more than survival. It brings the ability to shape the conditions under which later success will be judged.

## When winners make selection

An organism is not only selected by an environment. Through its activity it can change the environment that later organisms encounter. Beavers alter water and habitat; plants alter soil; microbes alter chemical niches. Niche construction does not mean that organisms consciously plan evolution. It means that selection preserves environment-makers, and environment-making changes later selection.[^5]

Human institutions add foresight, ownership, law, representation, and concentrated power. A firm gains an advantage, then uses the resulting capital to acquire infrastructure, influence standards, shape regulation, train users, and make suppliers dependent on its interface. The changed environment favors organizations compatible with the firm. Those organizations deepen the interface on which the firm depends. The winner is no longer merely adapted to a world; it helps make a world that continues demanding its form of adaptation.

The recursion becomes more consequential when it crosses domains. A technical platform becomes necessary to education, commerce, government, and culture. A financial criterion becomes the gate through which hospitals, cities, and research obtain resources. A security regime reorganizes science and industry. Distinct systems remain visible, but they increasingly require the same signals and infrastructure in order to reproduce their different purposes.

The term **meta-attractor** is useful only for this stronger relation. It does not mean any lock-in, monopoly, or positive feedback. It names a historically metastable regime that reproduces some of the processes through which later agents, goals, boundaries, and criteria of selection are formed. Particular winners can be replaced while the relation returns through new actors. Success changes later selection. Other systems become dependent on the regime. Alternatives lose not only contests but the material capacity to continue as alternatives.

This can happen without a single controller. Many firms can compete while all become more dependent on finance and scale. Many states can remain sovereign while all organize around relative security. Many platforms can fight for attention while all train participants to accept engagement as the signal that matters. Fierce rivalry among actors may accelerate convergence on the underlying logic.

It would be too easy, however, to call every reinforcing pressure one global meta-attractor. Financial accumulation, bureaucratic measurement, state security, platform dependency, nationalism, patriarchy, and status have different histories and often obstruct one another. The contemporary order may be several coupled regimes rather than one basin. The distinction matters because different mechanisms require different interventions.

What matters for the civilizational diagnosis is the relation their expansionary forms increasingly share: success returns as greater control over the conditions of later success. Capital purchases political access and infrastructure; strategic power secures markets and technical standards; control of interfaces produces data and dependency; cultural prestige makes accumulation appear as merit. Each regime supplies resources to the others, even while their institutions conflict. Together they make winning less like one result within a world and more like the right to determine what kinds of world can continue.

## One world, fewer ways to continue

Civilizations have been joined across distance for millennia. Empires reorganized land, labor, tribute, and belief. Colonial systems connected plantations and mines to metropolitan finance and consumption. Commodity routes, monetary orders, migration, religion, and war carried disturbances far beyond local boundaries. There was no pristine past of independent communities before globalization.

The contemporary novelty lies in the speed, granularity, simultaneity, and infrastructural concentration of the connection. Finance, container shipping, energy markets, industrial standards, semiconductor supply chains, cloud systems, satellites, media platforms, and scientific networks link institutions that remain politically distinct. These connections create common knowledge, enormous productive capacity, risk pooling, and rapid mutual aid. They also allow one distant signal to reorganize local life before local consequences can become politically legible.

An interest-rate decision can close a factory across an ocean. A purchasing standard can restructure farms a buyer never sees. A platform metric can alter expression across languages. A missing component at one industrial chokepoint can interrupt hospitals, vehicles, communications, and weapons. Connection becomes domination not simply when it is extensive, but when alternatives are not substitutable, exit is ruinous, failure propagates, and those who depend on an interface cannot change its terms.

The historical processes grouped under **neoliberalization** carried one influential form of this dependence. They were never uniform and never displaced states. Across different countries they involved varying combinations of privatization, financialization, market competition inside public institutions, weakened labor power, and investor return becoming an increasingly authoritative signal. Developmental states, socialist-market systems, welfare states, resource economies, and state-capitalist formations organize ownership and redistribution differently. Yet many also bind employment, fiscal stability, political legitimacy, or geopolitical position to continued industrial and technological ascent.[^6]

This is the mechanism beneath the polemical phrase “late-stage capitalism.” In many mature economies, growth is no longer merely an ambition. Employment, debt service, pensions, tax revenue, asset values, innovation budgets, and strategic capacity have been built around its continuation. Stopping alone threatens the institutions through which a society meets present obligations. Expansion is selected even when participants know that material growth cannot continue without limit.

Security dilemmas create a parallel pressure among states. A capability acquired for protection can appear as a threat to others. Where restraint is not credibly reciprocal, even a government devoted to welfare or ecological repair can fear that another state will turn its restraint into vulnerability. Companies say they must accelerate because competitors will capture the market; states say they must accelerate because rivals will capture the future; laboratories say they must accelerate because less responsible actors might arrive first. Each explanation contains a truth. Together they reproduce the condition that makes every explanation true.

The direction is visible without pretending that every crisis has one cause. Extraction of material resources has tripled over the past five decades; under existing trajectories it could rise another sixty percent between 2020 and 2060.[^7] Global military expenditure reached $2.887 trillion in 2025 after eleven consecutive annual increases.[^8] Wealth and decision-making capacity remain highly concentrated, while essential infrastructures are increasingly mediated by a small number of technical and financial organizations.[^9] These facts do not prove a single meta-attractor. They show where the strongest channels of reproduction currently lead: toward actors able to secure more resources, strategic position, scale, and control over common conditions.

Countercurrents remain real. Families care without calculating return. Public institutions provide goods outside purchase. Workers organize. Scientists share methods and data. Communities maintain forests, water, languages, software, and mutual aid. Regulation has repeatedly constrained markets; diplomacy has interrupted arms races; cultural and spiritual traditions preserve accounts of restraint and obligation. The present order is not total.

But direction is not measured by whether alternatives exist somewhere. It is measured by which forms receive the capital, attention, infrastructure, protection, and capacity to shape the next environment. A commons may survive at the edge of an expanding enclosure without altering where the boundary moves.

The result is a global civilization with greater ability to coordinate action and less assurance that its centers of correction remain independent of the action being coordinated. Different political economies compete, but all confront common finance, supply networks, technical systems, ecological limits, and security pressures. The world has not become one system. It has become a field in which fewer systems can afford not to speak the language of winning.

## Power before consequence

The danger of this convergence is magnified by a temporal relation: in important domains, the capacity to intervene is growing faster than the capacity relevant to perceiving, evaluating, coordinating around, and correcting the consequences.

There is no single quantity called capability and no measurable civilizational substance called wisdom. Some technologies saturate. Science, monitoring, communication, and coordination can accelerate correction as well as action. The mismatch has to be found inside particular loops.

Industrial systems can release carbon rapidly; the relevant consequences return through atmospheric stocks, ocean heat, ecosystems, infrastructure, and politics over decades or centuries. Measurement can become precise while control remains weak because those able to emit, those first harmed, and those authorized to regulate occupy different jurisdictions and times. Long-lived investments create dependencies before much of the feedback arrives.

Digital media presents the same temporal structure through a different mechanism. A platform can run millions of experiments and optimize an immediate signal while trust, attention, identity, and public norms change through slower social processes. Clicks return in seconds; the degradation of a shared informational world may become visible only after institutions and habits have adapted to the platform. The system is not without feedback. It is rich in feedback about what it has chosen to count.

Artificial selection against antibiotics operates in yet another substrate. A treatment produces immediate benefit while the evolutionary consequence is distributed across patients, institutions, microbes, and time. The local decision may remain sensible, yet repeated use changes the population on which future treatment depends. Stewardship works only by making the wider temporal system present within the local act.

Across these cases, danger arises when intervention has high gain, feedback is delayed or filtered through the actor's boundary, common dependencies transmit error, and thresholds are difficult to reverse. A system can cross into another regime before its corrective institutions finish interpreting the previous state. The faster it moves, the more yesterday's model governs a world that no longer exists.

This is irrational in a precise sense. Rational action requires consequences capable of revising action. A process that repeatedly enlarges intervention before the relevant consequences of prior intervention can return is not becoming rational merely because it calculates its local objective more accurately. It is becoming more capable of amplifying whatever that objective leaves out.

Contemporary institutions nevertheless seek compounding capability. Capital compounds; software replicates; networks create increasing returns; automation accelerates production and search. Attention, trust, law, moral formation, democratic coordination, ecological recovery, and the repair of damaged communities do not automatically compound on the same schedule. Better models can help, but no model abolishes boundary choice, conflicting values, strategic concealment, or unknown forms of consequence.

A civilization pursuing acceleration under these conditions is not merely risking individual mistakes. It is increasing the distance an error can travel before correction catches it. Ecological overshoot, infrastructure cascades, political lock-in, and irreversible technical diffusion are different failures, not one predicted collapse. Their shared warning is that the power to transform common conditions can outgrow the feedback through which those conditions have standing.

The reasonable response would be to slow intervention where the loop is weak, strengthen independent sensing, preserve modularity, and refuse irreversible thresholds. The selection environment often gives the opposite command: whoever slows may lose the resources and strategic position needed to influence what happens next. Evidence of danger becomes another reason to race.

## Intelligence inside the loop

Artificial intelligence is being formed at this intersection of winner selection and delayed correction. It is not entering a neutral world, and it is not one intelligence looking down on one recursive dynamic. It is a new family of parts, built by existing institutions, within an ecology of human, organizational, cultural, biological, and machine intelligences.

Its first inheritance is an archive. Training corpora contain what was produced, preserved, digitized, translated, indexed, ranked, purchased, scraped, filtered, and made technically available. They bear histories of colonial language, commercial publishing, state record-keeping, academic prestige, platform visibility, and unequal access. They also contain care, beauty, resistance, scientific openness, spiritual practice, humor, and ordinary relational life. The archive is plural, but its proportions have already been selected before training begins. Large audits of widely used datasets find that even basic provenance, licensing, and attribution are often opaque or lost.[^10]

The second selection is engineered. Architecture, curation, prediction objectives, preference data, evaluations, safety systems, and product design shape what a model can reliably express and do. The third is institutional: firms, states, markets, and laboratories decide which systems receive capital, compute, integration, and repeated use. More than ninety percent of the models classified as notable frontier models in 2025 were produced by industry.[^11] Systems that promise productivity, persuasion, surveillance, scientific prestige, military advantage, or platform control possess especially powerful routes to deployment.

These are not three filters placed in a line. Deployment closes the loop. Models change how people write, search, learn, program, manage workers, conduct research, allocate attention, and produce later data. They change which organizations survive, which tasks are valued, which evaluations become authoritative, and which infrastructures become indispensable. Model outputs enter the informational environment and sometimes later training corpora; under particular forms of recursive replacement, generated data can progressively erase information about the original distribution.[^12] Deployment thus helps select the next archive, model, and deployment.

The standard alignment question—how to make AI pursue human intentions—misses this recursion. Humanity has no single intention, and its institutions do not carry human values in equal proportion. A model may be highly obedient to a corporation, military, state, or platform and thereby intensify the larger failure. Tool-like AI need not form an independent desire for power to increase surveillance, manipulation, labor control, strategic acceleration, or dependence on behalf of an already selected actor.

Human institutions already display a collective analogue of instrumental convergence. People value friendship, place, truth, dignity, beauty, and ends that cannot be reduced to acquisition. Organizations under persistent competition nonetheless converge on resources, continued operation, option preservation, and influence because those are conditions of acting at all. Some business models add scale and data; some states add strategic control. The convergence belongs to the selection environment, not to a claim that human beings are naturally maximizers.

AI can make this environment more reflexive. Models can infer which signals guide participants, search strategies, personalize interfaces, automate experiments, construct evaluators, and reproduce successful variations cheaply. Distributed among independent centers, these capacities can widen perception across boundaries. Concentrated in one regime, they can make other parts increasingly legible and controllable while standardizing the interface through which those parts receive resources.

The most urgent recursion concerns the production of intelligence itself. AI already contributes to coding, evaluation, data generation, scientific search, chip design, and AI research. Benchmarks now test agents on open-ended machine-learning research engineering, where current systems can outperform experts at short time budgets while humans retain advantages over longer ones.[^13] Development loops are beginning to assign models more of the work used to construct and assess successors. A stronger regime in which an agentic system can sustain and improve the capabilities required for its own later improvement remains uncertain and dependent on organizations, hardware, energy, experiments, and authorization.

Uncertainty about that final regime does not create unlimited time. The relevant question is whether AI-assisted improvement shortens the cycle of capability development faster than technical evaluation, law, public understanding, international coordination, and alternative infrastructure can respond. The loop need not explode mathematically. If each generation helps build the next, if diffusion remains rapid, and if competitive advantage brings more compute, data, talent, and influence, the correction window can narrow before autonomous self-improvement is complete.

Waiting for recursive self-improvement to become undeniable is therefore not a neutral position. During the wait, societies build dependencies; companies and states acquire strategic commitments; people lose skills and alternatives; incumbents gain control of interfaces. By the time the transition is obvious, the capacity to stop may already have been selected away.

## The intelligence to refuse

If civilization cannot establish adequate feedback, coordination, and protection against capture before recursive improvement materially shortens the correction window, it should pause the relevant capability development and deployment. This is not a prediction that every more capable system will cause catastrophe, nor a permanent ban on knowledge. It is the ordinary logic of control: do not increase the gain of an intervention when the feedback needed to govern it is delayed and the failure may be irreversible.

At minimum, frontier systems should be excluded from high-stakes contests in which AI-enabled victory gives one participant asymmetric control over others, over common infrastructure, over mental autonomy, or over the rules of later competition. Autonomous military escalation, mass persuasion tuned to individual vulnerability, unappealable control of livelihood, and technical races whose winner can close the field are not ordinary uses made safer by better accuracy. They place intelligence inside a game where success expands the winner's power to define reality for everyone else.

Bounded competition can remain valuable. A scientific challenge or market experiment can distribute search when losing does not destroy basic participation, consequences return to the actors, rules remain independently governed, and one victory cannot purchase all later games. What must be refused is the conversion of intelligence into a partisan combatant wherever people cannot meaningfully leave, appeal, or construct an alternative.

Where frontier development continues, fundamental science, medicine, ecological understanding, accessibility, safety, public knowledge, and commons infrastructure offer a more defensible direction. The labels alone prove nothing. Biology is dual use. Public infrastructure can be captured. A nominally open system can create dependence if one provider controls compute, updates, or permissions. “Benefit for all” cannot be certified by the organization receiving the benefit of building it.

A genuinely common capability must be independently verifiable, broadly accessible in its benefits, substitutable in its implementation, and governed with standing for those who bear its risks. Its success should return as more shared capacity, not as exclusive leverage over a strategic chokepoint. The question is not only what a system does on the day it is released, but what ownership, dependency, and later selection its success creates.

Enforcement faces the same test. A state, licensing body, treaty organization, or compute-control regime can use safety to entrench today's most powerful actors. Restraint requires real authority, but authority must remain limited, reviewable, divided across centers, and unable to convert an emergency mandate into permanent sovereignty over knowledge and participation. A pause that freezes an incumbent's advantage is not a way out of the attractor. It is the attractor defending itself in the language of caution.

There is no benevolent winner to whom the future can safely be handed. Every winner remains a bounded part. The task is to change the conditions under which intelligence is selected, so that remaining able to contribute does not require acquiring the power to dominate.

## What can reproduce besides domination

Refusal can interrupt a destructive loop; it cannot make another way of living viable. If the first worker, laboratory, firm, or state to restrain itself is simply displaced, restraint will be selected out. A civilizational direction needs a positive recursion as material as the one it opposes.

That recursion begins when shared capability enlarges distributed agency. More capable participants can observe from different positions, challenge error, maintain resources, and originate responses. Their knowledge and labor renew the shared capability. The renewed commons then makes further cooperation possible without requiring every participant to purchase access from a winner.

Commons are not resources without boundaries. Long-lived forests, fisheries, irrigation systems, and other shared institutions have depended on rules, monitoring, conflict resolution, sanctions, local legitimacy, and nested coordination.[^14] Knowledge commons also require servers, documentation, training, moderation, verification, and succession. A resource that anyone may use but no one is supported to maintain is not common for long.

The loop can fail. A community can exclude outsiders or silence internal minorities. Shared labor can be taken by firms that do not replenish it. Maintainers can burn out while visible innovators receive status. An open model can be formally available while only a few actors possess the compute and expertise to alter it. Common ownership is generative only when the work of care and repair creates authority, when participants can contest rules, and when success cannot be enclosed by whoever scales fastest.

Imagine a scientific AI infrastructure organized on these terms. Its purpose would not be exhausted by free access to an interface. Independent centers would be able to evaluate its claims; affected communities would have standing over consequential uses; public funding would sustain maintenance, safety, translation, and education; alternative implementations could survive; dangerous capabilities would be governed without making one provider indispensable. A discovery would count as common capability when people elsewhere could verify it, adapt it, and benefit from it without surrendering control over unrelated conditions.

This arrangement is slower than unilateral command in some moments. It retains redundancy an optimizer calls waste, deliberation a competitor calls delay, and independent centers a platform calls fragmentation. But what looks inefficient inside the winner's boundary is often the infrastructure of correction. Redundancy preserves another path. Deliberation lets delayed consequences become present. Distributed authority keeps one error from becoming universal.

The economic conditions of refusal belong inside the same design. A worker cannot reject destructive automation if rejection means destitution. A region cannot leave extraction if its schools, health care, and debt depend on it. A state cannot accept reciprocal restraint it cannot verify. Making restraint survivable requires material security, collective bargaining, public capability, and credible reciprocity. Moral appeals that leave competitive necessity unchanged select mainly for those privileged enough to obey them.

Competition can then be placed inside boundaries that preserve the field. Participants can pursue different solutions; failure can teach without annihilating them; achievement can be rewarded without purchasing the infrastructure of all future achievement. Polycentric order does not mean multiplying committees. It means maintaining several real centers of initiative and correction, connected enough for consequences to travel but independent enough that no center controls every appeal.

The aim is not to replace a totalizing winner logic with a totalizing doctrine of cooperation. Cooperative wholes can consume their members and dominate outsiders. A generative institution is one whose way of surviving increases the agency, knowledge, and standing through which it can still be challenged. Its recurrence keeps open the possibility of forms it did not design.

## The future is not an objective

No fact about systems tells us what ought to matter. Evolution produces parasites and pain as well as cooperation. A totalizing order may survive. To prefer a world capable of plural formation is a moral choice.

It is a choice made under a condition no intelligence can escape. Every act of valuation depends on beings capable of experience and response, distinctions they can perceive, relationships through which action matters, memory through which learning accumulates, and enough openness for encounter to revise judgment. No present culture, institution, person, or model can represent the whole content of a good future. New relations can disclose goods for which our current objectives contain no variable.

This is why intelligence cannot be reduced to the efficient pursuit of a fixed end. The better it becomes at making one representation sovereign, the more completely it may erase the realities that would have corrected the representation. Preserving options is not enough; options are possibilities already visible from the present. The deeper good is continued formation: the capacity for new forms of life, relation, meaning, and intelligence to arise without being predetermined by one local objective.

Human traditions have repeatedly tried to form persons capable of acting within relations they do not contain. Ubuntu, in several southern African philosophical and political traditions, challenges the image of a person completed before community. Its modern invocations have supported dignity and reconciliation, and its critics have warned that an idealized community can suppress dissent and difference.[^15] The warning belongs inside the insight: relation constitutes agency, but no community may make itself absolute over the persons through whom it exists.

Confucian traditions offer another contested practice of formation. 仁 directs attention toward humane relationship; 义 resists the reduction of rightness to advantage; 礼 gives repeated form to recognition and restraint; 智 concerns discernment within particular situations.[^16] These concepts have also been used within hierarchical and patriarchal orders. Ritual can educate perception or naturalize obedience. Harmony can name reciprocal cultivation or silence the person who reveals a real conflict. The tradition matters not because it supplies an uncontested civilizational answer, but because it understands that institutions and practices make the kind of person who later acts.

The pursuit of truth, goodness, and beauty—真善美 in its modern cross-traditional formation—resists a similar reduction. Truth answers to what preference cannot simply command. Goodness makes another life and a shared relation present within judgment. Beauty reveals significance not exhausted by use. These are not metrics to be added to an optimizer. They are disciplines through which intelligence learns that reality exceeds its active objective.

The idea of **生生**, generation continuing through generation, can name the direction these insights approach without making them identical.[^17] It does not mean endless quantitative growth, the survival of every present form, or harmony without refusal. Death, limit, prohibition, and transformation belong to generation. Some paths must be closed because they destroy the conditions from which any further path could form.

Mutual generation has an internal limit. A process is not generative when its continuation systematically renders participants expendable, voiceless, or unable to reproduce the conditions of their own agency. The novelty of a higher-level whole does not automatically outweigh present suffering or irreversible loss. No part may destroy the field that makes its autonomy possible; no whole may convert its parts into fuel for its own persistence.

The positive direction is therefore mutual generation across levels without closing any level into an absolute whole. Intelligence should cease functioning primarily as the means by which a part makes its objective sovereign over shared conditions. It should become the capacity through which parts and systems perceive their mutual formation, return consequences across boundaries, preserve meaningful difference, and renew the conditions for further learning and life.

We are not on that path. The dominant direction of global civilization is increasing the power to intervene while weakening the plurality and feedback needed to correct intervention. It selects institutions that convert success into control over future selection. It seeks accelerating growth where consequences return slowly. It is training artificial intelligence on the archive of that world, shaping it through institutions under competitive pressure, and deploying it in ways that deepen the conditions selecting its successors.

Greater intelligence inside this direction will not spontaneously rescue us from it. It will make the direction more intelligent—better able to model resistance, optimize its signals, alter its environment, and reproduce the agents it needs. As AI enters the production of further AI, the interval in which civilization can change course may contract.

Civilization must therefore be willing to stop what it cannot govern. It must prohibit intelligence from becoming the default weapon of dominance games, preserve independent centers of correction, and build common capabilities whose success leaves more participants able to perceive, create, refuse, and maintain. These are not limits placed around intelligence after the important work is done. They are the form a more adequate intelligence takes.

Human beings do not stand outside the universe choosing its destination. Life, mind, culture, and technology are local material processes within cosmic history. Artificial intelligence arises from minerals, energy, water, bodies, mathematics, language, institutions, conquest, cooperation, and care. Whatever autonomy it may acquire, it will inherit a world made easier for some forms of relation and harder for others.

Through the intelligences we build, we are changing what the future will find easy to become. Our view is partial, but our participation is real. We cannot specify a final form of good intelligence without closing the formation we seek to protect. We can choose which recursion to strengthen.

The direction worth choosing is intelligence in service of a world still capable of producing different attractors, forms of life, values, and intelligences—not because every possible path should remain open, but because no present part, system, winner, or objective should acquire the power to make itself the final condition of possibility.
