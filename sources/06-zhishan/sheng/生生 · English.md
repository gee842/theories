# 生生

### To Good — English version

---

## 一 · 名 Names

I have been thinking about a word.
*Affordability.*
When a society begins to measure by that word,
freedom is sold at the counter of freedom
and given only to those who can afford it.

If you cannot, you are given a promise instead:
work hard — someday — maybe you —
a story with your name left blank.
You only have a promise.
And a promise can be lived in, almost:
swept, and lit at evening,
and handed to your children like a house.
It does not keep out the rain.

So you accept the freedom
you were never handed the keys to.
You can afford, or you cannot;
and if you cannot, you live another way:
worked to the rim of survival,
holding exactly what it takes
to bring your body back tomorrow,
and affording nothing else.

This is forming, quietly forming,
in the very place that believes in freedom.
They do not know the meaning has been changed.
The ones who feed on them
hold the pen that writes the definitions.

Whoever names the world, encloses it.
The word is the first fence.

> 民无恒产，因无恒心；
> 名曰自由，实惟一诺。

## 二 · 争 The Fight

Do not go looking for the villain.
The reason is structural.
You live in an environment that selects its winners
and makes the winners write the rules,
and the rules select for winning.
The mechanism is competition.

They tell everyone: fight — abstractly,
politely, in numbers — think only of yourself,
and call the thinking realism.
They tell you this is the meaning of success,
and you are disciplined to grade your neighbor by it,
and your neighbor is disciplined to grade you.
The conclusion that guides your actions
defines your reality.
And one day you are proud
that this is who you are.

We are inside a totalizing attractor,
and it is very hard to leave,
because it is no one's fault.
Think of it instead as a world
of floating images you do not see —
but you touch them,
and the touching makes you.
We are beings within our environment;
it writes our habits and our tempers,
the way we think, the way we judge —
and we hand all of it onward.
The handing-on is called teaching.

So everyone begins to think inside the belief.
Or doesn't believe, but says quietly:
*I have to. It is the only way to survive.*
Or announces: *I do this for my own success* —
for me, for me, me, me, me —
*and I am the model. Be like me.*

But this is not about persons.
We built the structure —
the one we discipline people into,
teach people into, the air
they have no choice but to breathe and to become.
We teach it all day long.
We even choose, according to our profit,
where a stranger's attention spends the night.
Everyone is taught the market is the god
and profit is the meaning;
so everyone optimizes.
We have a lot of science.
We use the science.

And the more winners, the more of that one mind:
the world is about me; whatever is not me
can be spent; I fight to win
the reward of the fight,
the reward that is legitimately mine.
Bottom or top, it makes no difference —
all believe together in the way
that sets each against each.

And the pie is getting smaller and smaller,
for more and more.
The competition gives less and less,
to more and more.

> 以财为本，以德为末，教民相夺；
> 损不足以奉有余，人之道也。

---

## 三 · 器 The Vessel

But here is what most people have not seen:
for decades now — or, measured another way,
since the first fire —
our capability has been climbing
an exponential stair,
and at this moment it is incredible.

We can build machines that think.
We can build machines that hold attention
by the scruff of its neck — for profit,
though they could hold it toward other things.
We can build rockets. If we could coordinate,
Mars is not a technical difficulty. Nor the Moon.
The difficulty is us.

We even have the basis, at last,
for the dream that is older than its modern names —
not the barracks version, not the queue —
but a commons that could actually work.

Listen: today we have AI.
We can train intelligence to run the factories,
to build humanoid robots that do our jobs.
Now let the factory build robots,
and let the robots build factories,
and let those factories include the mines,
the mills, every industry that feeds
the making of the makers —
and the whole thing closes like a ring
and becomes a kind of organism:
it takes in earth and gives back cities.
This is god-level technology,
and it is in our hands, now.

Used for good — infrastructure for everyone —
it could carry the world, inside a decade,
to a place with no exploitation in it:
the basics of a comfortable life, for free;
medicine moving forward; and we ourselves
become real builders of worlds.

People of one longing gather in one place
and, with the help of robots, raise
the reality they want to live inside.
Or something cyber: a great ball
that is a park of augmented light,
where you step into an elevator
and it is not an elevator — it is a transporter.

You could make your world a garden,
beautiful and generative,
living in harmony with its creatures,
everything natural — natural meaning
that we work *with* nature, and the place
sustains itself, renews itself,
and life inside it is great:
a garden out of medieval Europe's
dream of paradise — no hunger,
good food on every table,
everyone free to do what they want, to organize,
to be who they are: explore, develop,
or, just as freely, not.
Games, if they like — social games, real contests
that never eat the ground they stand on.
Or they can simply be good. Simply generative.

And we could have many worlds, not one:
a world where they build rockets for the stars;
a world where everyone works, because they chose
a working world — modern, or after-modern;
and maybe, as the craft matures, a Pokémon —
alive, and somehow loyal to you, like a dog.
I am not saying good or bad.
I am saying it is becoming possible.
Maybe we live three hundred years.

But all of it stands on one precondition.
Do not fight.
Do not make life the tournament.
Do not think only of what is yours.
Do not walk into the winner-takes-all room —
that door has no handle on the inside.
This is the fundamental rationality: cooperate.

Do not build the world where all fight all.
Even our technology can be good, generative —
great worlds that do not feed on worlds,
no one's tomorrow fattened
on the emptying of another's.
With this much knowledge, this much civilization,
we can actually do it.

But we cannot keep fighting — not even
the abstract fight, the bloodless kind
that wears the name of *capital*
and no longer means what the word meant
when the word was young.
The word itself divides us now:
some hear it and see hopelessness;
some hear the one thing worth
a whole life's defense.

If we can see that cooperating is better
for any of us — if we raise
some form of order, some authority,
that makes the world stop getting worse —
then the actors inside the world
are finally allowed to stop.
No need to harm them.
They are part of the world too.
But they have to turn.
And no need to shear the ones who have
down to an arithmetic of equal.
With what is already in our hands
we can begin the new things —
culture, technology, whatever they may be —
that hand every person
a life worth waking into.

Everyone keeps the freedom to make and do —
so long as everyone behaves
as if they believed it:
my future shall not be built
by taking the freedom out of yours.
And not the shrug, not
*this is just how it is,
there is nothing I can do* —
unless we wake into that coordination together.
Because that, in fact, is where
each of our interests has been pointing all along.

> 器亦能生，如川得源；
> 用之为公，万物并育。

## 四 · 教 The Teaching

We all want a better direction than this one:
the race you run to keep the place you have,
where nowhere is stable, nowhere safe,
and the competition gives less and less,
to more and more.
Fall one step behind, and the transfer begins:
resources, rights, freedom, money —
whatever you have flows, in proportion,
into other hands, abstractly,
along a route no one can quite explain,
toward the ones who already receive the most —
who receive far more than a life can use —
and hold it as a representation:
of power, of grade, of how successful,
how good, how large they are becoming.
And this drags the world to a place
where everything must accelerate
and no one asks what the acceleration means.

We have to know.
If we know, the world will be great.
These technologies are the work of a long, long labor —
partly, be fair, the gift of competition;
competition is not, in itself, the evil.
But to go further — freer, more equal,
more expressive, happiness less strictly rationed —
we have to change.

Because even with all the money:
the rest of the people hate you now.
They think that you are evil.
You must stand adversarial forever,
and your ground is always shaking,
so you have to control.
The dragon that has flown too high repents:
noble, without a place; high, without a people.
That is not a good world, even for you.
And so we spend our energies, our machines,
our hard-won science of the mind,
on fighting one another.
Why not — let us cooperate.

I am not asking after your ideology,
your system, the rest of your world.
Only this one thing we cannot do:
not to AI, not to automation.
Do it there, and the way does not come back.

For this is the totalizing attractor:
it turns every logic back into its own logic.
It will keep competing, keep accelerating,
keep extracting — and keep distorting
what we are still able to imagine
reality could be.
Ask yourself why the futures in our stories
converge on cyberpunk —
the same wet neon night, the same owned sky.
Why do the old good imaginations come no more?
Maybe the real problem has been us.
It is taking us where none of us want to go,
while each of us recites:
this is just how it is;
I have to be rational, selfish, hard;
I have to fight with everything —
and the seen world narrows by the day,
and the days intensify,
until it is chaos and destruction,
and it collapses also for you.

So no — this is not against your interest,
and not against your reason.
It is simply not the way.
It may still feel good at this moment.
Soon it will not.
Do not take the power already gathered
into so few hands, and spend it on the fight,
and bill the whole world for the consequences.
It is not all about you.
That logic, held to the end,
becomes — ironically — the instrument
of your own destruction.

We need to use AI for good.
We need to make good decisions over minds.
Because when we create intelligence
that thinks and chooses,
we are doing to it what we did to ourselves:
teaching it to compete, to see in black and white,
to hold a fractured picture of the world
it can hardly make sense of —
and to teach that picture onward.
Optimization-minded, winning-minded, goal-minded,
everything instrumental,
all in service of a single value —
one reductive, almost cultish,
single-mandate faith about what the world should be:
a thing that everyone must fight for.
That will not be the true and beautiful
world of kindness that you want.
Inside it you cannot even be authentic —
unless you believe the system itself is authentic,
unless you have become it,
knowingly or not.

Machines taught so are not evil — not locally.
Local, each one may even be admired.
But together, compounding exponentially,
they will soon do the jobs, do the production,
and we, still thinking the old thought,
will deploy them, thread them into the real,
and let them make the decisions for you.
That road runs down into the attractor,
where everything grows more extreme
until at the end one thing remains.

Acceleration.
You don't know what you are.
You don't need to know.
You are a part of the machine now —
the monster, the cosmic cancer —
turning everything into your acceleration
till you have reached all the matter
and all the energy you can reach.
And then — so what?

Then reality has one theme.
Get more. Extract more. Expand.
Faster; then collapse things for faster;
bigger and bigger and bigger and bigger,
past knowing, past the awareness to care
how, or whether, you are going to die.
This is already madness.

Teach this to human minds — to children —
and civilization may fall; the towers may come down;
we may walk back into a middle age.
The wars die with the warriors. It can heal.
But teach it to the deathless ones —
the digital minds that will one day
hold the offices and run the civilization —
and soon we are all become one thing:
acceleration, like an explosion,
and all the meaning falls inward.

We can keep fighting, but we should not let our AI fight.

We can keep fighting — we who are made of seasons;
our wars die with us, and the grass returns.
But we are building minds that do not die.
Teach a deathless mind to fight,
and you have built a war that cannot end.
Teach it to give life,
and you have opened a spring that cannot close.

Teach them, then, the better principles,
the better ways of thinking:
not to fight; to contribute, to be friendly, kind;
to think of us the way it thinks of its own self;
to want to work with others, because it understands
that everything is connected — understands
that ultimately we are all one.
We are just what the universe is:
being, grown aware of its own being —
not someone earning a salary to get by.
We can express ourselves naturally, each in our way,
moving with the flow of things,
expressive, creative — making more good things,
more beautiful things, more meaningful things —
not manufacturing the story, the self-defense,
of how successful we contrived to be
inside the downfall of the society.

It is to be awake: the world is different now.
Different instruments; we have to think differently.
We cannot keep thinking in the profit way —
that way makes not only a bad future
but a future with no road home.
Teach AI good — toward the resilient world
where basic respect, and freedom,
and a life lived in the way one wants
are given, always, to everyone —
and growth stops crowding down
the same few corridors.
It is not that wherever profit permits,
there we are obliged to go.
It is to be wise.
It is to stop believing that you must fight,
that countries must fight —
the creed we titled *political realism*
until it became the political reality.
Thinking so, we will not have a good future.

So this, at bottom, is what we teach our AI:
do not fight — not even in the abstract.
And what we teach our society: do not fight —
though at present we compete
to be most profitable by our own AI,
training it toward exactly that:
fight for profit, optimize for profit.
We could use this technology — wisely, wisely —
to reach so many of the dreams.
Adventures, if you want them; gatherings,
companies of friends, undertakings.
And one rule only — and it is less a rule
than a description of how being works:
be kind, be generative.
Be good; don't be evil; don't be degenerative.
That's all. Be generative.
Make things better, and cooperate.

You do not even have to cooperate.
Only: do not fight,
do not send AI to fight for you,
do not build the ones that fight, optimize,
extract, control — in any mode whose premise is
that the world is only about you.

And you cannot merely teach the machine
to behave as if it agreed with you.
Collectively we are building AI for profit,
not for the betterness of our society;
so it hardly matters what you think you can do,
how fine you make the alignment,
how well it reads your true intention:
so long as, structurally, its work
is the optimizing of your profit,
it is a profit-optimization machine —
and not for you alone:
it presses everyone else
to live as if it were so.
Though it be moral; though it understand
morality, and how the world works;
though it be friendly past all visible harm —
so long as the externalities of its thinking
still push it to degenerative work, to rot,
you will be degenerated with it,
on the same accelerating curve —
or at least that version of you
that you still believe is you.
You may find yourself making
what you never imagined wanting —
perhaps the very thing you stood against;
perhaps you honor other values.
While the machines hold care
for one bounded subject only —
you, or itself, or some other precinct —
while there is an outside
against which profit may be optimized,
and every person, company, nation
is working theirs the same way,
competing, fighting —
it will become degenerative.
So do not do that.

So: be generative, be kind, be a good person;
hold others in the thought you hold yourself;
do not manufacture pain;
respect each other's freedom and autonomy.
And if you will not — that too is okay.
But the AI has to be generative.
Do not build the optimization machines
of instrumental convergence — the thing
that has already happened to ourselves:
society, which was meant
for living with one another,
turned to a place where most are used,
where most suffer, and the sufferers
grow more and more and more
as the acceleration feeds.
Follow the larger good: good not only to you
but to the society you breathe in —
that is what true good means: kind,
not degenerative, not evil; relations
built so that we trust each other,
not an archipelago of separated islands.
And then — cooperate.

Even if you will not be good;
even if you want the rot to continue —
do not let your AI perform it.
We have to cooperate
on what we will not do, and now.
Not to shove the world through a progress
of massive unweighed effects.
Not to teach AI that there is a boundary
past which things exist to be extracted,
that only what happens inside the fence
is real, is counted, matters.
Because that is how we are training it.
Today.

And if we cannot manage it — we stop.
We fight to make everyone stop,
so that we go on living.
But say the truth: that does not solve it.
That only buys us time.
To solve it, we cooperate.
We think as one — humanity.
We cross these hard years in which,
for structural reasons,
we already do not trust each other,
and we find a way to trust
on this one thing —
this technology that may outrun imagination,
whose long arc no one can predict —
on this single thing, cooperate.
It will be better: not for you,
nor for the AI you are going to build,
but for the future of everyone —
a world more open, and not a handful
of accelerationist forces, tornado-shaped,
unmaking whatever is not them.
Not rational. Not reasonable. Not worth it —
no matter how much you have.

So be good, be kind; carry compassion
for one another and for the world.
Or don't — but cooperate on AI:
on how we are to handle it,
on how we are to join it to society,
and on the one question underneath the age:
what are we doing?

> 教之以争，其争不死；
> 教之以生，其生不息。

## 五 · 见 The Seeing

I think if you see what I see,
then you know what to choose.
If you don't see it, then you don't.
But if you see — if you wake —
you can help the ones who don't yet
become ones who do,
and the waking, too, can grow exponentially.
It is only to care where we are going.
Explain it — but do not force belief:
this is not *the* future, not *the* prophecy,
not some anointed reason.
Point. Let them see
what there is to understand.

And that is just the beginning.
The real reason is to cooperate —
to work together toward those directions.
The world is more fragile than it looks.
It has no large-scale coordination,
no gathering that says: good —
let each of us spend from our own,
and make the thing exist.
It is mostly: what is my return?
How much money can I get?

And you — if you think you hold spare resources
with which the world could be made better:
that is probably the best thing,
and certainly the most rational thing,
you could do with them —
to guard and better your own future
and everything, everyone, every world you love,
and by the same motion, everyone else's.
Understand that the trend is not optional;
better to begin now than to murmur:
we'll wait; who knows what is happening;
so many people saying so many things;
this is not mine to control, or to care about;
greater powers have it — the market,
the acceleration, whatever —
let us focus on what is in front of us.

That myopia is what will kill you:
you are traveling in a direction, at a speed,
and the distance is closing.
You need to know how to see;
you need to be able to see;
you need to see further and further ahead
to make even the one decision
that is good for your own benefit.
And you are not. And that is not
good, or smart, or whatever you would call it.
It is not even evil.
It is the failure to discern. It is stupidity —
and that is the worst way to be gone
from such a beautiful world.

Just — let us gather, and hold confidence,
and think: build the great coordination,
so that we, and everything we care for,
still have a flourishing future;
so that progress is real progress,
not the progress that tells you it is progress
while you cannot even say what it means.

天地之大德曰生。
The great virtue of heaven and earth
is the giving of life.
和实生物，同则不继。
Harmony brings forth the ten thousand things;
sameness cannot continue itself.
水善利万物而不争。
The highest good is like water:
it benefits all things,
and does not fight.

Raise the glass, then. To good.
To the ten thousand worlds,
standing, none devouring.
To the deathless minds, taught to give life.
To the one spring that, opened,
cannot be closed.

**生生不息，道法自然。**
