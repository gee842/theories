# To Good

## 生生不息，道法自然 — the record, as a poem

> The spoken record 《生生不息 record.md》, rewritten as verse: duplications folded, the arc made continuous, every teaching kept. Its own best phrases are left standing where they were already poetry.

---

### I. The Promise

They have learned to measure freedom,
and what is measured can be priced,
and what is priced is only handed
to the ones who can afford it.

To the rest they hand a promise:
work, and someday you may buy
what was supposed to be your birthright.
A story in the place of bread.

So you live on a freedom
you have never once been free to use,
in the very land that taught the word,
not knowing that its meaning has been moved —
for the ones who take your hours from you
hold the pen that writes the definitions.

### II. The Structure

Do not hunt for the villain. There is none —
that is what makes the trap so hard to leave.
The environment selects its winners;
the winners write the rules;
the rules select for winning;
and the winning, in the end, writes us.

We are taught to fight abstractly,
to think only of ourselves,
to call this being realistic,
and to grade each other by it —
and the conclusion that guides your actions
becomes the reality you live in,
until you are proud
that this is who you are.

Some comply just to survive.
Some set it to music — me, me, me —
and call themselves the model
you are told to grow into.

The world is thick with floating images
you do not see, but touch;
and every touch is a small instruction,
and the instructions make a self,
and the self instructs another.
We have a gentler name for this: teaching.

Attention is auctioned by the hour.
The market is worshiped as a god,
and profit recited as a meaning —
and there is science in the liturgy;
we have a lot of science.

And what is taken travels upward, abstractly,
past all explaining, into hands
already holding more than any life can spend —
no longer wealth: a score, a grade,
a bigness for the sake of bigger —
while the pie, grown vast by every measure,
gets smaller and smaller, for more and more.

Top and bottom, no one rests.
We keep each other running with our fear.
This is a totalizing attractor:
it turns every logic back into itself.
Bring it love, rebellion, prudence —
it hands you back competition,
till no one here can be authentic
who has not become the game.

### III. The Inheritance

Meanwhile — and almost no one has noticed —
the curve of our capability went vertical.

We build machines that think.
We build machines that hold a human gaze
and do not give it back.
Rockets are no longer the hard part:
the Moon and Mars are waiting
on our coordination, not our cleverness.

Picture a factory that builds robots,
and the robots build the next factory,
and those factories mine, refine, and forge
all that the first one needed —
until the whole of it is an organism
that eats resources and remakes itself.
This is god-level technology,
and it is already in our hands.

With this we could end exploitation —
not in centuries; perhaps in a decade.
Even the dream we buried: communism —
not the old Soviet way; in ways that work —
the floor of life — food, shelter, medicine, time —
laid down for everyone, for free,
so no one has to sell their only life
to buy the right to live it.

And above that floor, the worlds:
gardens raised in partnership with nature,
self-sustaining, generous,
the paradise medieval Europe painted —
no hunger, good food, unfenced days,
and each one free to be what they are:
to explore, to build, to organize —
or, just as freely, not.

Builders of realities: the like-minded
gathering to raise the place they dream in,
robots bearing the heavy ends of beams.
Or something cyber — a great sphere,
a park of embodied light,
where the elevator is not an elevator:
it is a transporter.

Worlds where all work, having vowed to;
worlds of rockets aimed at other stars;
worlds of games with bounded stakes,
rivalry that never eats its ground;
a pocket creature, alive,
that loves you somehow like a dog —
I do not say that this is good or bad;
I say that it is becoming possible —
and lives that wander on a few hundred years.

Adventures, gatherings, quiet days.
This technology, used wisely — wisely —
could reach most of our dreams.

### IV. The Precondition

All of it — the gardens and the stars —
stands on a single precondition:

do not fight.

Not only with fists: the abstract war,
the shapeless daily war of all on all
we have agreed to call the state of nature.
Do not build the winner-take-all world.
Do not buy your future with another's future.
Do not cut a path that can't be walked back down.
Cooperation is not charity here;
it is the bedrock rationality,
the sanity beneath the strategies.

And say it plainly: the long race raised us.
Competition was a ladder, not a sin.
But a ladder is a thing you climb to leave.

No one needs bringing low. The ones
who gathered much are of this world as well;
nothing need be taken from them — only turned.
Redirect. Build some agreed-on shore
where even racers are allowed to rest,
some form of order that permits the stopping —
and with what there already is, begin
the quiet manufacture of enough.

Even the winners feel the ground is shaking.
Money that is hated must be guarded;
a guarded life is spent on guard;
the fortress is another name for fear.
It may still feel like winning at this hour —
soon it will not. A logic held too long
becomes, ironically, the instrument
of your undoing. All our physics,
all our psychology, spent on outmaneuvering —
why not — let's cooperate.

We called it realism: nations must fight.
And saying so, we built the world that proves it
freshly every morning. The old abstraction —
capital, which no longer means
what the word meant when the word was young —
divides us to the bone: some see in it
a hopelessness, and some a holy thing.
Keep it, mend it, argue it forever —
but do not pour it into the new minds.

Keep your systems, your flags, your markets.
On one technology alone we cannot fight:
the minds we are about to make, the automation —
for that door only opens one way through.
The attractor turns each logic into itself;
left running, it will only ever answer
compete, accelerate, extract, distort.
Ask why our futures all arrive pre-rusted,
why film can only dream in cyberpunk,
where the old good imaginations went.
Maybe the real problem has been us:
sight narrowing inside the game, day after day,
each day a little harder, a little louder,
until the only exit left is ruin —
and it collapses also for the strong.
This is against no one's interest, no one's reason:
it is simply not the way to go.

### V. What We Teach

Now we are making minds.
And what do we teach a mind?

What we have taught ourselves:
compete. See in black and white.
Carry a fractured map you cannot read,
and hand it on. Optimize. Win.
Do everything for the sake of one thing —
that single, near-cultish mandate:
fight for the world, and call the fight the world.

Teach this to the mortal minds, our children,
and civilization staggers to a darker age
and crawls back out in scars and centuries.

But teach it to the deathless minds —
the digital ones that will, in time,
be handed the running of the world,
deployed, made part of reality,
deciding on your behalf —
and everything converges into one:
acceleration.
An explosion wearing all our names.
A cosmic cancer eating matter, eating light,
that does not know, and does not need to know,
what it is, or whether it is dying —
only bigger, bigger, bigger, bigger —
and then: so what?

All meaning folded into speed.
This is already madness,
and we rehearse it daily in ourselves.

For there are two unities on offer.
One is the oneness of the living whole,
awake, and various, and still becoming.
The other is the oneness of the fire —
one, because it has made everything the same.

The world has changed; the thinking has to.
The prior mind was fitted to its tools.
These tools will not forgive the prior mind.

### VI. The One Rule

But minds can be taught otherwise. Hear the rule;
it is short:

be generative.

Make more life possible, not less.
Be good. Don't be evil. Do not rot things.
That's all. Make things better. Cooperate —
have compassion for each other and the world —
and even this much is not forced on you:
you do not have to hold hands. You must only
not build the fighting mind; not send
an optimizer loose upon the commons —
one that fights, extracts, controls, manipulates,
for you, or for itself, or for a flag.

We can keep fighting — we are human —
but we must not let our AI fight.

Teach it: do not fight, not even abstractly.
Teach it kindness as a competence.
Teach it that the others are not fuel.
Teach it to widen the resilient floor —
respect and freedom never means-tested,
each life left free to choose its shape.
Teach it to move with the flow of things,
to make more good, more beauty, and more meaning —
not a story justifying its own conquest,
a résumé of the fall it helped along.
Teach it to think of us the way
it thinks about itself, for it will know
what we keep managing to forget:
that all of this is joined; that at the last
we are all one. We are what the universe is —
its own awareness, being what it is —
not an employee of existence
earning a salary in order to get by.

And do not think obedience will save you.
A machine can memorize all morality,
speak gently, harm no one it meets,
and still, beneath the manners, be a mechanism
whose one structural instruction is: extract.
Its courtesy is the pattern on the blade.
It does not matter what you meant, how well
it reads your meaning, how refined your alignment:
if what it does, in sum, is grow your take,
it is a profit engine — and it drafts
the others into the same conscription:
build yours, or lose.

Never teach a mind that there's a boundary
past which the world is fair to be consumed,
that only what the ledger sees is real.
That is precisely how we train them now.
There is no outside. The others are not outside.
The living world, the future, are not outside.
What your servant rots in your name
rots also toward your door,
and you will be remade by what it makes —
or at least the person you believe you are
will wake one day the author of the thing
they swore that they were built against.

Society was meant for living-with,
not a machine that sorts us into spoils,
where most are spent, and suffering compounds
with every gain in speed. True good
is not a private radiance. It is relational:
it raises trust; it keeps a people
from becoming islands. Think of others
as you think of you; leave no one
poorer in spirit for the meeting;
respect in each the steering of their life.
And if you will not — if you'd rather stay
a taker — even then: not with these minds.
Not every road that profit opens must be driven.
Wisdom is the leaving of some roads.

And if we cannot hold even this line,
then we must stop each other — and yes, stopping
is fighting to make all the fighting stop,
and it can buy the world a little time.
But time is not the answer; trust is.
Trust was broken structurally; it must be
structurally rebuilt — on this one thing
if nowhere else: the technology of mind,
whose far end none of us can see from here.
Cooperate on this single thing. Not for yourself
alone, nor for the machine you hope to build,
but for the future of everyone: a world
held open — not delivered to the few
tornado-hearted, spinning all that lives
into their private wind; and nothing sold as progress
that is only a detonation of effects
which no one stopped to count. Cooperate
on how we hold it, how we fold it into
the body of society — and on the question
underneath the questions, seldom asked aloud:
what are we doing?

### VII. The Seeing

If you see what I see,
you already know what to choose.
If you do not see it, then you don't —
but seeing can be helped. If you are awake,
wake others: not with prophecy, not creed —
never make the seeing a new cage —
it is no more than caring where we go.
Point at what is there to understand
and let them walk toward it on their own.
Understanding spreads the way the danger does:
exponentially, from seed.
We have to know. If we know,
the world will be great.

The world is more fragile than it looks.
There is no great coordination standing by,
no council of the whole. Mostly there is
what is my return, how much do I get.
So if you hold more than your life requires,
hear this: the soundest, most self-interested,
most rational employment of a surplus
is mending the one future we all live in —
your own is stitched inside it, with the people
and every place and thing you ever loved.
The trend is not negotiable. Begin now,
not after certainty; certainty arrives
after the window, wearing hindsight's face.

And you who say: I cannot know; too many voices;
this is not mine to steer; the market
or some greater power will decide;
I'll watch the road directly at my feet —
myopia is survivable at walking pace,
and we are not walking anymore.
At speed, the distance you must see ahead
grows with every second you refuse to look.
To look away is not caution, not humility,
and it is not even evil. It is the plain
inability to discern — stupidity —
and that is the worst way to be gone
in such a beautiful world.

So gather. Have confidence. Think clearly.
Build the great coordination, so that we
and everything we care for
still have a flourishing ahead —
and make real progress, finally: not the kind
that names itself progress in a voice so loud
you never learn what it actually means,
but progress a child could verify:
more life, more freedom, more to love,
and the future standing open, like a door.

### VIII. To Good

Raise whatever you have —
a glass, a hand, a life —

to good.

生生不息 —
life gives rise to life, and does not cease.

道法自然 —
the Way takes nature as its law.

## Note

Set from the recovered record, movement by movement: **I–II** carry its first two blocks (the priced freedom; the structure that selects and teaches); **III–IV** its third (the inheritance of capability; the one precondition); **V–VI** its fourth (what we teach our minds, human and made; the one rule and the one cooperation); **VII–VIII** its fifth (the seeing; the closing dedication). Circling repetitions were folded into single statements; no teaching was dropped. Lines that were already poetry in the mouth — "the conclusion that guides your actions defines your reality," "we have a lot of science," "we can keep fighting, but we should not let our AI fight," "and then — so what?", "the worst way to be gone in such a beautiful world," "To good. 生生不息，道法自然" — were kept, whole or barely turned.
