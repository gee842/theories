I'm suddenly thinking about the word *affordability*. I think once we can measure things based on that, it means a society only gives freedom to the ones who can afford. So if you cannot afford, you don't have the freedom. You only have a promise — a story that tells you that if you work hard, maybe in the future, you will have that affordability. It's a promise. And with that, one can be okay accepting that freedom without actually gaining accessibility to those availabilities.

And if you can afford, you can afford. If you cannot, you have to live in another way — maybe in a way where you just keep being exploited, to the point that you have only what's needed for your survival, but you cannot afford any other things. This seems to be a reality that is slowly forming in the place that believes in the concept of freedom. But they don't know the meaning of freedom has changed — and those who exploit them have the power to define it.

---

And the reason for that is structural. You are in an environment that keeps selecting winners and making them define the rules of society. And that mechanism is competition. They tell everyone to fight with each other, to think only about themselves — all in an abstract way. And they tell you that is the meaning of success, and you are all disciplined to judge each other based on that. The conclusion that guides your actions defines your reality. And eventually, you might be proud that this is who you are.

We are in a totalizing attractor that is very difficult to escape, because it's not anyone's fault. You could think of it more like a world where all those images are floating around in ways you don't see — but when you interact with them, that interaction makes who you are. We are all beings within our own environment, and that creates our behaviors, our personalities, the way we think and the way we judge. And we can also give this to others — which we call teaching.

And so everyone starts to think in this mode of believing. Or maybe they don't, but they just tell themselves: oh, yes, I have to do this, because this is the only way for me to survive. Or maybe they will tell you: I am doing this for my own success — for me, for me, me, me, me. And then they tell you that they are your role models, and you have to be like that.

But this is not about any person. This is structural. We created this structure — you could call it disciplining people into it, teaching people into it, or making them adapt to it as their environment. We teach those things and those values all the time. And we also make choices about where people's attention should be, based on our own profit. And everyone is just being taught that the market is the god and the profit is the meaning, so they optimize that. You know, we have a lot of science — we can use science to do it.

And the more winners, the more individuals that have those mindsets: that the world is just about themselves; that they are willing to sacrifice other things to make this self more successful, or to compete and win against each other so that they have the reward of their fight, they have the reward of what is legitimately supposed to be theirs. So everyone is behaving in this way. It does not matter if you are at the bottom or at the top: you are collectively believing in a way that makes everyone have to compete with each other. And the pie is getting smaller and smaller, for more and more.

But what most of the people don't realize is that today's technology has been growing exponentially in capability for the past few decades. Or maybe it depends on how you measure it, starting from the beginning of human civilization. But at this moment, it has incredible capabilities. So imagine if you can build machines that can think — and they can make you always hook up your attention to those kinds of content. It could be optimizing profit, but it could also be optimizing for other things. And we can build rockets. If we can coordinate, we can go to Mars, we can go to the Moon. Those are not technical difficulties. And we also have the basis to achieve what we have been dreaming about: communism. Not in our Soviet way, but in ways that can actually work.

Imagine: today we have AI — we can train intelligence to optimize our factories, and to produce humanoid robots that can do our jobs. If we produce a factory that builds robots, and then let those robots build other factories, what will happen? And what if those factories are the industries required to prepare and make all the materials needed for the factories that produce the robots? Then the whole thing becomes more like an organism: it can take in resources and replicate itself. This is the god-level technology that we have.

If we use those things for good — like building infrastructures for everyone — we could transform the world, maybe in a decade, into a place that has no exploitation. We can give all the basic needs for free, so people can have a comfortable life. And we can also advance medicine. We could even become real world-builders: people with similar interests go live in the same place, and with the help of robots, they build the reality they want to live in. Or it could even be something cyber — like building a huge ball that is an AR park. You have embodied AR experiences in those realities, and when you step into an elevator, it's not an elevator, it's a transporter.

And you could choose to make your world a very beautiful and very generative garden. And you live in harmony with those organisms, and everything is just natural — but also natural in a way where we can work with nature to create a better place, one that can just self-sustain itself and allow humans to have a great life. A garden that's like a paradise in medieval Europe's imagination: no hunger; everyone has good food; everyone can just freely do the things they want, or organize; and they can be who they are — explore, develop, or maybe not. Maybe they can have some social games, competing with each other without destroying their foundations. Or they can just be good, be generative.

And we could have many different worlds. We could have a place where people build rockets to explore the universe — like different stars. And we could have people working — because in their world, by their choice, everyone there has to work — so everyone just goes there and lives maybe a modern or even post-modern life. And maybe, as technology advances, you could have a Pokémon that's alive, that's just somehow like a dog to you. I'm not saying it's good or bad, but it's technically becoming possible. Or maybe we live to a few hundred years old, because of the technology we have.

But the precondition of using those technologies is to not fight; to not compete; to not all think only about what's good for yourself; to not get into a winner-takes-all reality; to not create a path that you cannot return from. And that's the fundamental rationality that one should establish: cooperate. Don't make a world where we all fight with each other, exploit each other. And we can also make our technology good, generative. And we can build great worlds where we don't exploit — where we don't try to make others' future worse because we want our future to be even better in our own expectations. And with the power of technology, civilization, and knowledge, we can actually do that.

But we cannot be fighting — or fighting in a form of abstraction, capitalism, in a way that does not even represent what capital was when the word originally came up. Even the word capitalism could be very divisive among ourselves: some people think it's a very hopeless situation, and some people think that's the thing worth defending — that's the meaning to their life. And if we know that it is actually better for any of us to cooperate — to establish some form of order or authority that makes the world stop becoming worse — this allows those actors within the world to stop. We don't have to do bad things to them; they're also a part of the world. But they have to redirect.

And we don't have to make people who have more have less, so we have equality. With what we have, we can start developing new things that give everyone a life of happiness — whether it's culture, technology, or something else. And everyone has the freedom to do it — as long as everyone behaves as if they believe it: that we cannot exploit others, cannot take others' freedom to make my future better and others' future worse. Or we just go: yeah, this is what it is, there's no way I can do anything — unless we have the awareness to have that coordination. Because actually, for each of us, that's where our incentive is.

We all want a better direction than the current direction, where we're fighting with each other. You have to always be racing, to be competing, to be able to sustain your position. And nowhere is stable, nowhere is safe, because the competition gives less and less, to more and more. And for each one, if they don't catch up — to the point that the transfer of resources, of rights, of freedom, of money, of whatever they have, is proportionally going into others' hands, in an abstract way that you cannot explain — everything goes to the ones who receive more and more, which is a lot more than what you can actually use, as a representation of power, of your own grade, of how successful you are, of how good you are, of becoming bigger. And this drags the world down to a place where we have to accelerate everything, without actually thinking about what that acceleration means.

We have to know. If we know, the world will be great. We already worked for a long, long time to create all those technologies — partially thanks to the competition. So it is not necessarily a bad thing. But if we want to go even further, become even better — make our society more free, more equal, more expressive, with more happiness everywhere for all of us — then we have to change. Because even if you have a lot of money, the rest of the people hate you, and they think you are evil, and then you have to be always adversarial to them. That's not really a good world for you: because your ground is always shaking, you have to control. So we're all spending all our energies to use our technology, and we use our understanding of psychology, to fight with each other. Why not let's cooperate?

I'm not talking about the other parts of your world, your system, your ideology. But at least we cannot do this to the technology of AI, to the technology of automation. Because if we do that, it's a way of no return. This is a totalizing attractor — which means it turns every logic back into the same logic. And it will keep competing, and keep accelerating, keep exploiting, keep distorting the way we think about what reality could be. Think about this: why do most sci-fis converge on cyberpunk reality? Why don't we have those old good imaginations anymore? Maybe the real problem has been us. And it is taking us to a place that none of us actually want. But we all think: oh, this is just what it is; I have to be rational, I have to be selfish, I have to be tough, I have to fight with everything. And then we're only able to see what's narrowing within that reality day by day, and things get more and more intense, and eventually it becomes chaos and destruction, and it collapses also for you.

So this is not against any of our interests or reasoning. This is simply not the way to go. It might still feel really good for you at this moment, but soon it will not be. And you have to cooperate with each other — but do not use the power that's already concentrated in the hands of so few to continue to fight, and let the whole world take the consequences. It's not just all about you. Because holding that logic will, ironically, become the way of your destruction.

We need to use AI for good. We need to make good decisions on them. When we create intelligence that thinks and makes decisions, we are doing just what we have been doing to ourselves: teaching everyone to compete, to see things in black and white, to follow a fractured way of understanding the world that they can hardly make sense of — and to teach that way. A very optimization-oriented, winning-oriented, goal-oriented way — and doing that instrumentally. We have been doing things instrumentally for the purpose of a single value — a very reductionistic, almost cultish, single-mandate belief about what the world should be — which is that we all fight for it. This will not be the true, beautiful world of kindness that you want. You cannot even be authentic most of the time — unless you believe that this system is authentic, that you have become it, with or without your awareness.

Because when we teach those things to AI, they might not be bad, or even evil, locally. But collectively — with the way we are accelerating those current systems exponentially — we are soon going to create the intelligence that can do the jobs, do the productions; and we will follow the same way of thinking: deploy them, make them a part of reality, making decisions for you. That will lead down to a totalizing attractor — a dynamics that makes everything more and more extreme, or maybe, to the end, there is only one thing that matters: acceleration. You don't even know what you are or who you are — or you don't even need to know. You are just a part of that huge acceleration machine, or monster, or cosmic cancer, that turns everything into your acceleration, until you reach all the matter and energy within the universe that you can reach. And then — so what?

And then our reality becomes a world where there is only one thing that matters: acceleration. We don't think about others; we just think about how to get more, exploit more, expand more — and that becomes the main theme that leads the rest of reality. And then, eventually, as you accelerate faster and faster, you will collapse everything for even faster acceleration. You will become nothing more but the dynamics that accelerates something just to get bigger and bigger and bigger and bigger — to the point that you don't even know, or you don't even have the awareness or care about, how or if you are going to die. This is already madness.

And if you make human intelligence — like your children — think this way, we might collapse our civilization and go back to the Middle Ages. But if you are creating those immortal intelligences — those digital ones that will eventually be the ones in charge and run our civilization — then soon we are all going to become nothing but one: acceleration, just like an explosion. And all the meaning will collapse.

But if we teach intelligence to free us from exploitation — or if we do it ourselves — if we only create good intelligence, which does not do degenerative things like exploitation, which does not lead to a future that rots, then we're going to have a place where there's just abundance everywhere; freedom, more than you could even think today; open and friendly, harmonious, peaceful; where we cooperate with each other, without fighting with each other.

We can keep fighting, but we should not let our AI fight. And we should teach them better principles, better ways of thinking: to not fight; to be contributive, to be friendly, to be nice; to think about ourselves as if it's thinking about its own self; to want to collaborate with each other, because it understands we're all connected — and it understands that ultimately we're all one. We are just what the universe is. That's the awareness of its own being. Not someone who has to get a salary to get by. We can express ourselves in our own ways, naturally; be with the flow of things; be expressive, be creative; create more good things, more beautiful things, more meaningful things. Not create a story — your own justification — of how successful you are, of how much you have contributed to the downfall of the society.

But it is to be aware that the world is different: we have different technologies, we need to think differently. We cannot keep thinking in this prior way — because that is a way that creates not only a bad future, but a future that you could not come back from. But if we teach AI good — to make a world where we're more resilient, where we can always give everyone the basic respect, the freedom, and to live in a way they want — we will not keep growing in certain directions. It's not that if profit allows, we have to go in those directions. It's to be wise; it's to not believe that you have to fight, that countries have to fight. And we call that political realism. It has become the political reality. And when we think like this, we will not have a good future.

---

So basically, the thing that we have to teach our AI is to not fight, even in an abstract way. And it's to make our society not fight — where we have to compete to become the most profitable with our own AI, training our AI to achieve that goal, which is fighting for profit, which is optimizing for profit. We could use this technology — if wisely, if wisely — to achieve a lot of our dreams. Yeah, you could be just going on adventures, or you could be gathering with others, creating groups, and doing things. But there's only one rule: being kind, being generative. Be good, don't be evil, don't be degenerative. That's all. Be generative. Make things better, and cooperate. You don't have to cooperate — but you just cannot fight, or make AI fight for you, or create AIs that fight, optimize, extract, control, in whatever way where you only think everything is about yourself.

And you cannot teach AI to behave as if it agrees with you. Collectively, we are building AI for profit, not for the betterness of our society. So it doesn't really matter what you think you can do, or how good you can make your alignment, or how well it can interpret your intention: as long as structurally it does things to optimize your profit, it is a profit-oriented optimization machine — not only for you, but for others to behave as if so, too. And even if it is very moral — it understands morality, it understands how things work, and it's super friendly; it never seems to harm you — as long as the externalities in how those AIs think still make them do degenerative things, rot things, you will also be degenerated, in an accelerating process. Or at least the version of you that you still think you are. You might make what you don't even imagine wanting. Maybe that's what you are against. Maybe you hold other values. But as long as those machines still hold the values of just only caring about a certain subject, which could include yourself, or itself, or some other things; as long as there is an outside that it chooses to optimize for profit, and every other being — every other human, organization, company — is also using them in the same way, whether competing or fighting: then it will become degenerative. So don't do that.

So let's just be generative, be kind, be a good person — think about others as if you're thinking about yourselves; not create a negative emotional experience; and respect each other's freedom and autonomy. Well — even if you don't, it's okay. But AI has to be generative. But even if you don't, you should also know that you should not turn your AI into optimization machines that have instrumental convergence — which has already happened to ourselves: society is meant to be a place that we can live together, not a place where most of us get exploited, most of us suffer, and the sufferers get more and more and more as it accelerates. You need to follow a more holistic idea, to be good not only to you, but to your society. And that's what true good means: to be kind, to not be degenerative, to not be evil, to create good relations so we trust each other — not atomize everyone into little islands. And then cooperate.

Even if you don't want to be a good person — even if you want to be degenerative, to make it continue — you should not let your AI do it. You need to cooperate. We all need to cooperate on what we should not do right now. And we should not push the world into a progress of massive effects. And we should not teach our AI that there is a boundary, and beyond that boundary things can be exploited — that only what works within the boundary matters. Because that's the way we're training it.

And if we can't do it, we stop. We fight to make everyone stop, so we still live. But the real thing is: that does not solve the problem. That just gives us more time. But if we want to solve it, we have to cooperate. We have to think that we are all one as humanity. We need to get through those hard times where we're already not trusting each other because of structural reasons. And we have to find ways to start trusting each other, at least on this one thing — on this technology that could go well beyond our imagination, and that we could not even have long-term predictability of how things go — to just cooperate on this single thing. It will be better. Not for you, nor for the AI you're going to build, but for the future of everyone. To make the world more open — not to create a few accelerationist forces that work like a tornado that destroys everything else. It's not rational, it's not reasonable, it's not worth it — it doesn't matter how much you have.

So just be good, be kind, have compassion — for each other, for the world. But you don't have to do that. You just need to cooperate on AI: on the issue of how we're handling it, on the issue of how we are combining it with society, and on the issue of — what are we doing?

---

I think if you see what I see, then you know what to choose. But if you don't see what I see, then you don't. But if you see — if you wake up — you can help others who don't to become one. And this process can also grow exponentially. It's to just care about where we're going. And you can explain it to them — but don't force them to believe that this is just the future, or the prediction, or whatever holy reason; point out what there is to understand.

But that's just the beginning. The real reason is to cooperate — to work together towards those directions. The world is actually very fragile. It doesn't have large-scale coordination, where people get together to think: okay, we can all spend our own resources on this to make a thing happen. It's mostly like: oh, what's my return? How much money can I get?

And for you — if you think you have the spare resources to make the world better, that's probably the best thing, and it's definitely the most rational thing, that you could do: to not only better, save, or protect the future of yourself, of all the things, the people, the world that you care about, but also do the same for others. And to understand that this is an inevitable trend — so it's better to start now, rather than: oh, we'll just wait; we don't know what's going on; there are so many people talking in so many different voices; and this is not something I can control, or care about; it's some other, greater power — maybe the power of the market, maybe the power of acceleration, or whatever — so we should just focus on what's in front of us.

The myopia is going to kill you when you are heading toward a direction at a speed — because you're getting closer and closer. So you need to know how to see, and you need to be able to see, and you need to see further and further ahead, to actually make a decision that's good for your own benefit. But you're not. And that is not good, or smart, or whatever you want to call it. It is not even evil. It's the lack of the ability to discern things — it's stupidity. That's the worst way to be gone, in such a beautiful world.

Just let's all get together, have confidence, and think rationally — create great coordination — so that we, and everything that we care about, can still have a flourishing future; to make real progress, not the current progress that tells you that it is a progress but you don't even understand what it actually means.

To good. 生生不息，道法自然。
