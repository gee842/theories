## The new bridge

The [integrated chapter](../../_process/research/2026-10-05-revisable-concern/THEORY.md) makes an exclusion answerable to a stated reason. Under a retained positive anchor and commitment to relevant consistency, a warranted matched case requires extending the reason, supporting a relevant disanalogy, or openly revising the prior commitment. Refusal, suppression, and unresolved uncertainty are distinct outcomes. Direct intake remains necessary for unfamiliar claims with no existing analogy.

A finite Boolean admission procedure extends an existing reason to newly admitted cases, with exact closure and shortest-path delay. A separate averaging implementation gives convergence and finite-time thresholds, including transient dilution when new relations are admitted. These are operations inside a supplied relevance language and procedural commitment; they do not confer standing, equate whole persons’ priorities, or derive new moral concepts from graph connectivity.

The work separates warranted relations, retained certificates, and active delivery. Mere blockage does not refute a reason or erase previously learned concern. Conversely, defeating a false certificate requires inspection of inherited conclusions and surviving alternative support; deleting a communication edge alone does not perform that correction.

## Strategic consequences and verification

For monotone exclusion gains, resistance requires a condition for every nonempty target subset. Two targets can each pass an individual test while joint exclusion remains profitable through a shared channel. An added independent route can change that outcome, but its construction and upkeep are separate from its disabling cost. Strong information channels can also fail behind cheap authority capture or free rule revocation.
