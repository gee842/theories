## Context (AI-authored)

The reply suggested, with reasons:

- **The unit of need.** Count a need in the same unit as the level at each step: when the unit splits into `r` finer units across a step, an unchanged need counts `r` times as many finer units, and a need rises or falls only against that scaled count, as change is read in the unit (`Web.RisesAt`, `Doubling.raw_reading_fails`). Reasons: support compares a formation's need with its condition's level, a relation, so it should not depend on the grain of measurement; with a need fixed in an absolute unit, refining the measure alone could turn an unsupported formation into a supported one. With both sides scaled by the same positive `r`, support is unchanged by the split, so the laws of dependence, reserve and support are expected to transfer to finer units directly. The one condition, to be stated openly, is `r > 0`: no unit splits into nothing.
- **Continuous time.** Build it inside the core as a tower of ever-finer discrete readings linked by the coarse-graining of `Becoming.Sampling`, each law holding at every level of the tower, as `Becoming.Resolution` treats units at every resolution; real-valued time comes later as a correspondence in the periphery. Reasons: the core compiles from Lean's prelude with empty axiom reports, and real numbers from the periphery's library rest on classical axioms; Sampling already shows balance (each relatum listed once), dissipation, closure and running down composing from fine steps to coarse ones; and with a least loss per exchange and a finite stock, a finite stretch can hold only finitely many changes however finely time is divided, which the tower makes provable rather than assumed.
- **Loss per exchange, not per fine step.** The current definition already counts the least loss only when a formation takes (`Flow.DissipatesOn`), so idle fine steps lose nothing and refining the clock creates no loss; counting per fine step would make loss depend on the clock's grain.

The suggested order: first the unit of need and the laws of dependence, reserve and support at finer units; then a tower module on Sampling, with the bound on changes in a finite stretch as its first target.
