## Context (AI-authored)

The explanation said:

- **The theorem.** It is proved with no axiom in `Becoming.Identification` (study §97). In a world whose configurations a list of N holds, two configurations have the same registered future exactly when their first 2N − 1 registered values agree. Moore's bound, proved later with no premise, shortens this to N − 1. So that finite list names a configuration's kind: names are equal exactly where the configurations are alike (`Law.name_makesSense`), and the name carries the law (`Law.name_carries`). With no bound, no finite list suffices. Naming by the whole infinite future would need function extensionality, which Lean proves from the quotient axiom `Quot.sound`.
- **The reading.** The AI had read this as a precise form of 名可名，非常名. The explanation gave three options:
  1. take it as written;
  2. take it as an analogy only: the theorem is "within a bound, kinds have finite names", and 名可名 is an echo, not a claim about the text;
  3. drop it.

  The AI suggested option 2. Its reasons: the reading is a gloss, not a result; it leans on reading 1, "situational = bounded", which Ren did not take, so here the bound is a condition only; and Laozi's line and the theorem do not line up exactly.
- **"Nothing else is pending" was too broad.** No other choice awaited Ren, but proofs remained open:

  - situations: counting per course and the law of large numbers for more than two values, and counting per course for every process;
  - weights that are not natural numbers: amplitudes cancel, so they are not counts of situations.
