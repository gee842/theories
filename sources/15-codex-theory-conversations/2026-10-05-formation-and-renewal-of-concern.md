## What this continuation establishes

The [integrated theory](../../_process/research/2026-10-05-concern-theory/THEORY.md) provides a complete conditional mechanism for a specified class and a broader candidate explanatory architecture. Its learned concern coefficient is the product of acquired valence prediction and acquired recognition, both starting at zero; shared evaluative routing and primitive teaching valence remain explicit premises. Proofs give exact protection and finite-class widening thresholds, memory and succession conditions, and cost-before-return financing. Paid recipient-outcome evidence revises action efficacy; a harmful-action case demonstrates that wiser concern can properly reduce intervention.

The work distinguishes functional mattering, experienced significance, direct concern, and normative standing. Concern is positively oriented toward the recipient, while evaluative relevance alone can also describe exploitation or hostility. The recipient’s standing does not arise from the observer’s recognition, nor depend on familiarity, productive usefulness, or ability to repay. Reflective revision of ethical scope is discussed as an explanatory task, not credited to an efficacy-learning equation.

Identity-gated learning, misleading observer relief, paternalism, forgetting, resource shortfalls, and loss at succession are separately represented. An optional productive-capacity extension shows how care can renew its material support, including the equality case where a finite total deficit can be prefunded. The model does not require every protected recipient to finance their protection. A separate harm ledger makes validation burdens visible; an exact-outcome gate illustrates how correction itself can stop a harmful testing practice after a valid warning, while preserving the initial exposure in the account.
