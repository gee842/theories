**What a description deducts, and what it adds.** There are three of each:

| Deducts | Adds |
|---|---|
| the relation across each cut | freedom the parts do not have |
| differences below its grain | distinctions that never make a difference |
| its own place, including its now | one view where there are only views that meet |

The relation across a cut:

- Describing each part separately gives back the whole exactly when the parts are unrelated (`separation_faithful_iff`).
- The lost relation reappears as surplus (`same_separated`).
- Three parts can be related as a whole while every two of them are free (`whole_not_in_pairs`).

Grain:

- A reading loses nothing relative to what is registered exactly when whatever it lumps has the same registered future.
- A law is not enough. The constant reading carries one, but it is sufficient only where nothing registered ever differs (`const_carries`, `const_sufficient_iff`).

Place:

- From within, everything can be said exactly when there is nothing to tell apart (`complete_iff_no_difference`).
- A saying that keeps what it said and says next what it missed (`Unfolds`) is incomplete at every step, and each gap is new (`gap_new`, `gaps_differ`).
- It never returns (`never_returns`), and no finite stock holds what it misses (`finite_stock`, `unheld`).
- Such a saying exists (`unfolds`, `unfolding_open`).
- What is missed is definite and can be said later, but never all of it: 不是不可说，是说不尽.

One view:

- Specker's three boxes (1960): every opening has a definite showing (`shown_in_context`), and the openings agree where they meet (`views_agree`).
- Yet no single placement fits them all (`not_one_view`).
- Kochen and Specker (1967) proved that quantum mechanics has this shape.
- The core's `Frame` is non-contextual by type, so its claims must be registrations in a context, or claims about the state, rather than values held alone.

**What "make sense" is.** Fix what is registered. A split makes sense when it lumps configurations exactly when everything registered from them on is alike (`MakesSense`).

- Such a split loses nothing (`MakesSense.sufficient`).
- The law comes out of it (`MakesSense.carries`).
- It is unique (`MakesSense.unique`).
- It is the coarsest split that keeps what is registered now and carries a law (`MakesSense.of_law`, `SameFuture.greatest`).

This is the minimal-automaton construction, and the deterministic case of causal states in computational mechanics. Sense is relative to what registers, and given that, the split is not a choice.

Ren's "change(now) -> future" needs only one unfolding and what is registered at each moment. States and the law between them grow out of it (`SameAhead`, `SameAhead.next`).

**What makes a pair a pair.** There are two kinds:

- **Two aspects of one state.** Where the standing changes by the passing and only the standing is registered, the split that makes sense is the pair, and neither part alone makes sense (`pair_canonical`, `pair_makes_sense`, `standing_alone`, `passing_alone`). Where nothing passes, the standing alone makes sense (`still_canonical`). In general, what is registered now makes sense alone exactly where it fixes its own registered future (`MakesSense.now_alone_iff`).
- **Two states of one rhythm.** The liar's unfolding has exactly two states, each defined through the other (`liar_two_states`, `liar_each_through_other`). The readings offered:
  - this has the form of 互根 and 转化;
  - the diagonal behind the third loss, read as a movement and not a value, has the form of 阴阳.

What makes two a pair is relation, in what can go together or in how they go on. Two unrelated things are 二, not 对.
